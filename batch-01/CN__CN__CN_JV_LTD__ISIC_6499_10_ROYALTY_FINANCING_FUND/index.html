Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the 2023 PRC Company Law, no universal statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to ordinary limited liability companies unless the specific industry imposes a higher requirement; no blocked escrow account is required for ISIC 6499 revenue-based financing. Each shareholder must contribute the full subscribed amount within five years from incorporation unless the articles of association set a shorter schedule. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with filings through the unified China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online platform.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese JV: after the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically the Chinese Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer number; the JV must complete tax registration with the competent STA office within 30 days. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese legal form: if it derives China-source income or establishes a permanent establishment, it must file the prescribed tax registration application with the competent STA office at the place of business or income source, submitting the foreign business registration certificate, articles of association, board resolution, power of attorney, legal representative identification, local business premise proof, and financial statements. STA then assigns a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number in the e-Tax system, or, where full registration is not required, the non-resident's home-country TIN is recorded and the Chinese payer must withhold at source. The foreign parent's tax-residency certificate should be submitted to claim treaty benefits.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (five million Renminbi) annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold a taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer (currently 3% or temporary reduced rate) or voluntarily elect general taxpayer status. All enterprises must nevertheless complete basic tax registration after incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is available to VAT general taxpayers for goods that are actually exported from China's customs territory, declared to the General Administration of Customs of China, sold under an export contract, and paid for in foreign exchange that is repatriated in accordance with SAFE rules. Qualifying cross-border services and digital services (including certain R&D, software, information, design, and consulting services consumed wholly outside China) may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt if contracted with a non-resident, payment is in foreign exchange, and no domestic benefit occurs. Royalty and financing income is not automatically zero-rated and must be tested against the STA's published zero-rated/exempt service list.

  • Chinese Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (export declaration issued by China Customs) evidencing the customs export and tariff/commodity classification.
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) or rail/road consignment note proving physical movement of goods out of China.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign-exchange receipt and export collection record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory deadline.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export contract, and any letter of credit or sales order cross-referenced to the customs declaration.
  • For zero-rated cross-border services: signed offshore service contract, service delivery evidence, non-resident payment receipt, and any applicable STA service zero-rating declaration.

China mandates the use of special VAT invoices issued through the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Fully digitalized electronic invoices (all-elements electronic invoices) are used nationally; invoices must carry encrypted digital tax-control stamps and must be transmitted to STA in real time or near-real time. High-volume issuers are expected to integrate via API or approved fiscal e-invoicing interfaces. The anti-fraud regime links invoicing data with customs, banking, and SAMR records.

STA cross-matches export refund claims with customs declarations, bills of lading/airway bills, and bank foreign-exchange receipts through the Golden Tax risk-control system. If the required documentation is missing, inconsistent, fraudulent, or the 180-day FX repatriation deadline is not met, the zero-rating is revoked, output VAT at the applicable domestic rate (13% for goods or 6% for services) is assessed, previously refunded input VAT is clawed back, daily late-payment surcharges accrue (0.05% per day), and administrative penalties of 50%-500% of the tax evaded apply under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export tax-refund claims additionally expose the entity and responsible personnel to criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law, including imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC's managed floating exchange-rate regime, current account items—trade in goods, services, and royalty/financing fees incident to the JV's ISIC 6499 model—are in principle fully convertible: regulated current-account receipts and payments may be settled without a prior SAFE approval, but every transaction must pass authenticity and compliance review at a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital-account convertibility is much more restricted. An inbound FDI capital injection requires a SAFE foreign-investment registration, a licensed bank's foreign-currency capital account and, after settlement, a dedicated 'capital settlement waiting account' that limits the use of the renminbi funds to permitted operating purposes. Profit remittance is current-account in character and normally lawful if the JV has filed its annual foreign investment joint annual report, prepared audited financial statements and tax withholding certificates, and adopted a board distribution resolution. However, Chinese banks will test whether retained earnings are genuinely earned in China, and dividend outflows may be rejected if the audited net profit cannot be reconciled with the FX payment schedule. The capital account remains closed to unfettered same-day RMB pooling and requires a cross-border financing quota for foreign-currency-denominated intercompany loans.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory export surrender, so the statutory conversion ratio is zero. Export receipts from the JV's sale of goods or services must be repatriated into an onshore designated foreign currency bank account and may be retained in FX or voluntarily sold to a licensed bank at any ratio. Under the SAFE trade-verification framework, ordinary post-export proceeds must be received and credited within 90 days from the customs release date; where documentary credit or contract terms require more than 90 days, the JV must file a deferred-collection registration before day 90, and the total proceeds must be received no later than 180 days from customs release. Overdue export proceeds without a valid registration trigger bank reporting, suspension of the exporter's trade verification status and potential SAFE penalties.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low—not viable as a primary treasury or clearing provider. An offshore digital EMI has no legal standing to replace a Chinese licensed bank for the JV's capital account, FDI registration, SAFE filings, or FX settlement. Overseas EMIs that are not licensed by the PBOC as non-bank payment institutions cannot issue domestic CNY account numbers, cannot maintain a capital settlement waiting account, and cannot perform statutory foreign-exchange verification for inbound equity or outbound royalty/disbursement flows. They are also legally barred from offering Chinese-resident entities banking facilities comparable to a Tier-1 commercial bank. The EMI may only be used as a bolt-on collection tool for certain cross-border e-commerce traffic if it holds a PBOC cross-border payment license, and even then the main operational and capital-account treasury must sit with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be wired via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's overseas bank to the JV's dedicated FDI capital account at the licensed Tier-1 bank. The remittance field must state the SAFE-registered FDI establishment code and the foreign-exchange purpose code 'FDI equity capital injection' (foreign-investment enterprise paid-in capital), and must never be commingled with current-account trade payments. On value date, the receiving bank files the capital account payment registration, credits the JV's foreign-currency capital account, and records the amount in the SAFE capital project information system. To convert the foreign currency into renminbi, the JV must follow the 'capital settlement waiting account' regime, which restricts the RMB funds to permitted business outlays and prohibits on-lending, securities investment, or real-estate speculation unless separately approved. A Chinese certified public accountant then performs capital verification, issues a contribution verification report, and files the capital contribution information with the State Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE joint annual reporting system.Outbound settlements should be split by risk and size. Routine royalty/financing disbursements, lender or investor distributions, tax payments, and operational current-account charges should use SWIFT MT103, with each reference stating the true purpose code and attaching invoices, contracts, withholding certificates, and tax payment vouchers. Import of physical goods or large trade-backed disbursements should be routed through an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (L/C) issued by the Chinese bank, especially when beneficiary banks require payment against shipping documents. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) remain permissible for lower-risk, recurring supplier payments where both counterparties have established relationships. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low—not viable as a primary treasury or clearing provider. An offshore digital EMI has no legal standing to replace a Chinese licensed bank for the JV's capital account, FDI registration, SAFE filings, or FX settlement. Overseas EMIs that are not licensed by the PBOC as non-bank payment institutions cannot issue domestic CNY account numbers, cannot maintain a capital settlement waiting account, and cannot perform statutory foreign-exchange verification for inbound equity or outbound royalty/disbursement flows. They are also legally barred from offering Chinese-resident entities banking facilities comparable to a Tier-1 commercial bank. The EMI may only be used as a bolt-on collection tool for certain cross-border e-commerce traffic if it holds a PBOC cross-border payment license, and even then the main operational and capital-account treasury must sit with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 100 million for annual related-party transactions involving tangible property, intangible property, or financial assets, and RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions; mandatory Master File preparation may additionally be triggered by higher aggregate related-party transaction thresholds under SAT Bulletin No. 42 (2016).

Economic substance scope: China has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the manner of certain offshore financial centers, but Chinese tax authorities apply a substantive economic-substance analysis through the beneficial ownership doctrine, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47), and OECD BEPS Action 6 treaty-limitation principles. A royalty/financing JV must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) - including negotiation, credit underwriting, risk management, treasury decision-making, and contractual control over intangibles - are performed by actual employees with real premises and decision-making authority in China. Absent such substance, treaty relief on outbound payments may be denied, intercompany pricing may be disregarded, and deductions may be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Sham Financing Vehicle

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard the entity or recharacterize its financing arrangement under General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47) and the implementation rules; it may disallow all interest/royalty deductions, impose the 10% withholding tax on recharacterized distributions, add daily late interest of 0.05%, and impose an understatement penalty of 50%-500% of the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Chinese Company Law Article 20 where the corporate form is abused to evade taxation or defraud creditors. Directors and shadow directors who approved the sham structure may be personally jointly liable for the company's unpaid taxes and civil damages, subject to travel bans and high-consumption restrictions, and may face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion, with imprisonment from three up to seven years where the amount is huge, plus fines and asset recovery.
Fake Royalty and Interest Stripping via Beneficial-Ownership Sham

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustments under CIT Law Article 41 will restore arm's length results, deny deduction of the excessive payments, recharacterize excess amounts as dividends, and subject the JV to 10% WHT, late interest, and penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law, including 50%-500% understatement penalties and 50%-300% withholding-agent penalties under Article 69.

Director personal liability: Directors who engineered the false royalty/interest scheme may be jointly liable for the JV's debt and unpaid tax under Company Law Article 20; the responsible officers may be blacklisted as serious tax-dishonest persons, restricted from leaving China, and prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion or Article 205 for issuing false invoices/forgery where applicable. Conviction can result in imprisonment of up to seven years for major economic crimes, fines, and confiscation of illicitly transferred assets.
Failure to File / Substantiate Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may perform a deemed adjustment using one-sided profit indicators, deny deductibility of related-party interest or royalty payments, assess back taxes with daily interest of 0.05%, and impose administrative penalties for failure to provide documentation. The underpayment part remains subject to 50%-500% penalties or 50%-300% withholding penalties, and tax evasion attracts criminal fines and imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFOs, and legal representatives who knowingly fail to file or who cause false documentation to be submitted may be held personally liable for civil tax losses under Company Law and debt-piercing principles; they can be added to the tax dishonesty blacklist with restrictions on travel, aircraft/high-speed rail, and asset transfers. Where deliberate falsification of transfer pricing documents is used to evade significant tax, the responsible persons face criminal liability for tax evasion, with potential imprisonment up to seven years and monetary fines.
