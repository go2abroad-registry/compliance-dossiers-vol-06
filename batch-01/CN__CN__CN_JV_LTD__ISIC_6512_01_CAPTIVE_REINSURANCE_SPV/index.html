Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For general limited liability companies in China there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital; however, because this entity is a captive reinsurance SPV subject to the Insurance Law of the People's Republic of China, NFRA imposes a special minimum paid-up registered capital of RMB 200 million (approximately USD 28 million), fully paid-in in cash. The entire capital must be placed in a blocked escrow account with a PRC-licensed commercial bank before the NFRA license application is granted, and no shareholder loans, intangible assets, distributions or capital repatriation are permitted while the escrow condition is in force. The escrow release requires the NFRA written operating license or a specific NFRA authorisation.

Funding & unlock path: Before occupancy, the office must have a current fire safety inspection or recordation certificate issued by the local Fire and Rescue Brigade, with emergency lighting, fire extinguishers, and unimpeded evacuation routes. The employer must register the workplace under the Work Safety Law, submit occupational hazard factors, and arrange emergency drills. No additional civil-defense structural conversion is required for a licensed commercial office building, and standard office load is within the structural permit.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); official electronic portal: National Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax), operated by STA at chinatax.gov.cn. Physical filings are accepted through the in-charge district tax service hall of the local municipal tax bureau.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete enterprise formation through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) foreign investment registration/filing, obtaining the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the definitive taxpayer identification number for domestic-resident entities. Step 2: Within 30 days of the USCC issuance, file the initial tax registration (including banking account details, legal representative, business scope, finance/accounting policy and expected transactions) in the National Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax); no separate VAT ID is issued because the USCC serves as the TIN. Step 3: For non-resident foreign entities that have not established a Chinese branch or entity but receive China-source income, the non-resident must register in the STA tax record through the in-charge withholding agent/e-Tax portal and obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number for withholding tax and VAT purposes. Step 4: Apply for the VAT invoice quota and the fully digital electronic invoice (fapiao) issuance authorization under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Step 5: Maintain a permanent record at the in-charge tax bureau; any change in capital, directors, registered address, business scope or statutory representative must be updated online within the statutory time limit (usually 30 days).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All taxable activities require VAT registration; an entity must become a general VAT taxpayer when its annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 (five million renminbi). A captive reinsurer is required to register from formation because it supplies services and must issue VAT special/professional invoices for B2B premium and commission flows; the normal small-scale threshold does not remove invoicing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Law and its cross-border services catalogue, a domestic supplier may zero-rate an exported supply only if the services are received by an overseas party and are consumed entirely outside mainland China, and the supplier can prove the export through the statutory documentation chain. For captive reinsurance to a non-resident ceding insurer, 0% VAT may apply where the underlying risk is located outside the customs territory, the premium is paid from foreign sources, the contract is contemporaneously signed in foreign currency, and no domestic insurance or reinsurance service is rendered within mainland China. Where the risk is deemed to remain in China, the supply is either taxable at 6% VAT or VAT-exempt under the financial-service exemption; zero-rating is not available. For goods, the goods must be sold by the Chinese entity, physically leave the Chinese customs territory, and be fully documented with export customs clearance.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export customs bill of entry) approved by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, showing the exporter of record as the registered Chinese entity and the goods as physically exiting the customs territory.
  • Original Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document evidencing the named Chinese loading port, foreign destination port, cargo description, and consignee.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt voucher accepted by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, proving that the export sales proceeds were repatriated to China within the statutory window.
  • Official export VAT invoice (digital e-invoice) reflecting the export contract value and, where applicable, the customs and SAFE data-reconciliation batch reference.
  • For offshore services/reinsurance: a signed cross-border service or reinsurance contract stating the non-resident ceding insurer, foreign risk location, non-Chinese consumption, and agreed foreign-currency settlement; plus a confirmation of the underlying risk pool located outside mainland China.

The STA requires fully digital electronic invoices (fapiao) for all taxable supplies; e-invoices are issued through the National Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax) or tax-aware ERP API and are transmitted in real time to the STA's national invoice database. Special VAT invoices are digitally signed with a tax-control seal and are verified online by the buyer through the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Red-letter reversal invoices require an approved reason code and the original invoice verification; the system applies automatic fraud-scoring, invoice quota control and cross-matching with customs and SAFE data. There is no third-party VAT clearance stamp; clearance is achieved through real-time invoice data fiscalisation and VAT return reconciliation.

The STA, together with the General Administration of Customs and SAFE, audits export zero-rating files retrospectively. If a claimed zero-rated export lacks the required customs declaration, FIRC/bank FX receipt, transport document, or beneficial recipient proof, the output VAT is restored at 6% (or the applicable rate) plus late-payment surcharge accruing at 0.05% per day. Tax evasion and fraudulent export refund cases attract an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid or refunded tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection; where criminal thresholds are met, the PRC Criminal Law imposes criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds or evading tax, with imprisonment and confiscation of property. The taxpayer must also repay the clawback refund with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border reinsurance premiums and claim payments, subject to authenticity and compliance verification by designated banks under SAFE's digital monitoring system. However, capital account transactions, such as foreign equity injection, loans, and intercompany financing, require strict prior approval or registration with SAFE. For a captive reinsurance SPV operating under a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture structure, the initial paid-in capital must be declared as foreign direct investment (FDI) and routed through a registered foreign exchange capital account; any subsequent increase needs a new SAFE recordal. Profit remittance of dividends is permitted only upon submission of an audited annual financial statement, corporate income tax payment certificate, and a board resolution, all reviewed by the bank and SAFE. In practice, the remittance process takes one to three weeks. Capital repatriation upon liquidation is also strictly controlled. Practically, the entity cannot access offshore RMB freely, and all cross-border payments must be traced to underlying commercial documents. The 2020 Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration continue to prohibit round-tripping and any circumvention via shell arrangements. Therefore, capital account convertibility remains restricted, while current account flows are administratively routinized.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 reform, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; rather, the captive reinsurance SPV must repatriate all reinsurance premium receipts from non-resident cedants into a designated foreign exchange settlement account in mainland China within 180 days from the date of the service statement or invoice. The full amount may be held in FX or voluntarily converted into RMB at the bank's market rate, but the bank must verify the authenticity of the underlying reinsurance treaty and claim history through the SAFE digital trade monitoring platform.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Infeasible – offshore digital EMI platforms and virtual IBAN providers are legally barred from servicing onshore Chinese legal entities without a physical banking license from PBOC and SAFE. China's banking regulatory framework strictly prohibits non-bank financial institutions from engaging in foreign exchange clearing, fund custody, or cross-border payment processing for domestic corporations. Captive reinsurance SPVs must maintain all transactional accounts with a licensed commercial bank, and every cross-border wire must flow through the SWIFT/CNAPS rail. Therefore, no fintech EMI can be used as a treasury node; at best, a licensed foreign bank may offer limited custody solutions, but not a core operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be registered with SAFE as foreign direct investment (FDI) and must carry a valid SWIFT MT103/MT202 instruction with purpose code 'Investment in capital' (or 'FDI'). The remittance must be paid into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account held at one of the Tier-1 commercial banks listed above. Within three business days of receipt, the bank will issue a 'FDI Capital Verification Permit' (反汇单) to the corporate actuarial department. The certified public accountant must obtain an updated capital verification report after the bank confirms the inflow, and such report must be filed with the State Administration for Market Regulation and the local CBIRC/NFRA supervisory office. All fees related to the capital injection must be routed directly from the parent to the branch, with no intermediary EMIs, and no funding from a third-party unrelated company.For claim payments to overseas ceding insurers or retrocessionaires, the preferred clearing rail is a direct SWIFT MT103 message. Domestic renminbi transfers use China's CNAPS high-value payment system. For a captive reinsurance SPV, letters of credit are economically incompatible with reinsurance claim payments, so Commercial LC (MT700) is only permissible if used as collateral for rated placements under an approved inward reinsurance structure. Documentary collections are permitted only for non-claim-related operational disbursements such as treaty deposits. Every outbound transfer requires the original claims file, the ceding company's formal loss notice, an actuarial reserve calculation sheet, tax clearance certificate for the payment, and the relevant SAFE verification code from the bank's online reporting system.Onshore CNY forward contracts with a licensed designated bank executed on trade-verified reinsurance premium or claim exposure, with a maturity limited to 12 months but extendable under real underlying cash flow.FX swap agreements for management of temporary renminbi liquidity mismatches, subject to SAFE's principle of 'last resort' and not for speculative profit-taking.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for converting long-term foreign-currency reinsurance liabilities into RMB-linked funding structures, but only with SAFE pre-registration.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for onshore licensed banks operating under an approved derivative framework, and only against the SPV's actual foreign-currency income from non-resident cedants, not for synthetic hedging of a different entity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Infeasible – offshore digital EMI platforms and virtual IBAN providers are legally barred from servicing onshore Chinese legal entities without a physical banking license from PBOC and SAFE. China's banking regulatory framework strictly prohibits non-bank financial institutions from engaging in foreign exchange clearing, fund custody, or cross-border payment processing for domestic corporations. Captive reinsurance SPVs must maintain all transactional accounts with a licensed commercial bank, and every cross-border wire must flow through the SWIFT/CNAPS rail. Therefore, no fintech EMI can be used as a treasury node; at best, a licensed foreign bank may offer limited custody solutions, but not a core operating account.

Turnover: The PRC Company Law and the Regulations on Financial Accounting Reports impose a mandatory annual certified audit on every limited liability company, including Sino-foreign equity joint ventures, without any asset, turnover, or headcount exemption. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if annual related-party tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, related-party financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, related-party intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 100 million. Master File is mandatory if aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. These thresholds trigger contemporaneous documentation, disclosure, and the risk assessment annex in the annual related-party transaction reporting package.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not use Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations by name, but the same effect applies through EIT Law Article 47 and the special tax arrangement. A captive reinsurance SPV must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in China, including actual underwriting decisions, pricing and actuarial functions, claims management, reserve ownership, investment policy, and risk governance. If the SPV is determined to be a shell or lacks commercial substance, the tax authority may recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, adjust profits retroactively for up to ten years, and impose interest surcharges of 5 percentage points under the General Anti-Abuse Rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Artificial Profit Shifting and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 and 48 authorize transfer pricing reallocation and special tax adjustments; Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes late payment interest at 0.05% per day and a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax; Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes evasion above RMB 100,000 and 10% of tax due, carrying imprisonment of up to three years and up to seven years for particularly large cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed false tax filings may be jailed, fined, and disqualified from management; the court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and impose civil asset restitution on directors who abused limited liability; serious tax violators are blacklisted and subjected to exit-entry travel bans applicable to their legal representatives.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping - Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and Article 48 permit the tax authority to apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule, recharacterize equity and debt arrangements, deny treaty benefits, and adjust Chinese taxable income retroactively up to ten years with interest; China's DTAs also deny treaty access when the recipient lacks beneficial ownership and exercises no substantive business activity.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who constructed the sham structure are jointly and severally liable under Company Law Article 20 for civil damages and unpaid tax liabilities; the company and its responsible persons are publicly blacklisted, with departures from China blocked under the exit-entry administration rules; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 or Article 175 can follow where the structure is part of fraudulent tax or financing conduct.
Unlicensed Insurance Activity, Reserve Misconduct, and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Article 159 of the Insurance Law of the PRC prohibits unlicensed insurance business and requires cessation, confiscation of illegal gains, and fines of one to five times illegal gains or RMB 200,000 to RMB 1,000,000 where gains cannot be quantified; Anti-Money Laundering Law sanctions include fines on institutions and responsible persons; Criminal Law Article 225 punishes serious illegal business operations with criminal detention and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to regulatory fines, disqualification from financial and insurance management, and civil restitution for losses caused by operating without a license or misusing segregated policyholder reserves; regulatory blacklisting and travel bans may apply; directors who knowingly run the unauthorized reinsurance business face imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 225, with custodial terms escalating in especially serious cases.
