Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary limited liability company under the Companies Law; however, because the entity intends to hold a securities brokerage licence under the CSRC, the Securities Law requires a minimum registered paid-up capital of RMB 50 million, fully paid in cash and verified by bank confirmation before the securities business licence is issued. For incorporation, there is no blocked escrow account requirement; capital contributions must be deposited into the company's verified bank account and documented for each shareholder subscription. The paid-up licensed capital must remain intact and may not be withdrawn until the licensed securities business ceases.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety acceptance inspection and fire prevention certification for the office premises from the local Fire and Rescue Section under the Fire Protection Law; structural load statement from the landlord for the leased commercial space; workplace safety registration with the local Emergency Management Bureau for low-risk office occupancy; and occupational hygiene inspection confirming acceptable indoor environmental conditions for employees.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filings are made through the STA National Electronic Tax Bureau service platform operated by the local competent tax authorities

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-foreign equity joint venture, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) obtained from the State Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation is the statutory Tax Identification Number for all PRC tax purposes. The SAMR transmits incorporation data to the STA through the multi-license integration mechanism; the local tax service bureau automatically completes tax registration and assigns the entity's taxpayer classification. The entity must thereafter activate e-tax access, complete taxpayer type confirmation, register for VAT invoice issuance, and file monthly/quarterly returns. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese permanent establishment, the Chinese payer or withholding agent must complete a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the relevant tax authority and record the non-resident's home-jurisdiction TIN before paying Chinese-source income; a separate PRC USCC is not generally issued to such non-residents.

Entity_Level_Taxation

None applicable to entity classification; the PRC does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. A small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5 million and the taxpayer satisfies the accounting, bookkeeping and filing requirements; this is an indirect-tax status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover measured over any consecutive 12-month period is the mandatory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; entities below this threshold may remain small-scale taxpayers unless they elect otherwise. The joint venture is required to complete VAT registration upon establishment even below the turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT, the 0% rate applies to goods that are physically exported from mainland China under a completed customs export declaration, and to cross-border taxable services that are expressly included in the zero-rating catalogue issued by the STA (covering categories such as international transportation, R&D, design, software, integrated circuit design and certain offshore outsourcing/digital services). The recipient must be a non-resident enterprise or individual, payment must be made by the non-resident in foreign currency or RMB from an offshore account, and the service must be consumed wholly outside mainland China. Retail securities brokerage services and CFD/equity agency services are not automatically included in the zero-rating list; such services are generally taxable financial services at 6% unless they qualify for the cross-border services VAT exemption. To claim zero-rating for any qualifying goods or offshore digital services bundled with the brokerage, the entity must separately document the exported goods/digital service and satisfy all statutory conditions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration form) showing the goods under customs supervision and physical export from mainland China; this is not required for pure brokerage services
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport waybill evidencing the movement of goods to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing full payment received from the non-resident purchaser within the prescribed repatriation period
  • Cross-border service contract and commission agreement, evidence of offshore consumption and performance, the non-resident client's tax-residence documentation, and payment traces for the exported brokerage or digital service

China mandates the use of the full digitalized electronic invoice (all-electronic invoice) service platform of the STA. Invoices must be issued through the e-tax/e-invoice platform or an approved API-integrated billing system, and invoice data is transmitted to the STA's Gold Tax System (Phase IV) in near real time. Each valid invoice must carry a unique nationwide invoice number, digital anti-fraud stamp, QR code and electronic signature. Financial and brokerage invoices must describe the commission or fee service accurately; input VAT credit claims, exemption/zero-rating declarations and invoice data are cross-checked automatically against customs records and foreign-exchange data.

The STA can retrospectively disallow zero-rating or exemption and claw back refunded VAT with statutory late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day on the underpaid amount. Fraudulent or unverified documentation attracts a monetary penalty from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection; serious export-tax fraud cases are referred for criminal liability under the criminal law, and false invoices or forged customs/foreign-exchange documents may lead to prosecution and imprisonment. The STA, customs authority and State Administration of Foreign Exchange share cross-border data systems for automated audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains de facto current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. Trade-related foreign exchange purchases and settlements are processed freely by licensed banks against genuine documentation. Capital-account items, however, including inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, and offshore securities investments, remain subject to explicit SAFE registration, quotas, or case-by-case approval. Export proceeds are no longer subject to any mandatory surrender ratio, but they must be repatriated into mainland China and either retained in a licensed bank foreign exchange account or converted into RMB at the enterprise's discretion. In practice, profit remittance hurdles for a Sino-foreign securities brokerage include full verification of paid-in capital, completion of annual statutory audits, submission of tax clearance certificates, and evidence that all SAFE reporting obligations have been discharged. Dividends declared in foreign currency require audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring the distribution, a foreign exchange payment purpose code validated by the paying bank, and proof of withholding tax payment. The central bank may impose additional documentary scrutiny where the business scope involves derivatives or overseas retail clients, as such typologies raise perceived balance-of-payments and AML risk. Therefore, while the RMB is not freely convertible on capital account, the entity can function with a well-structured onshore banking relationship, active SAFE compliance, and continuous tax and treasury governance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to mainland China within 90 days after the export customs declaration date. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain 100% of export proceeds in a designated foreign exchange account at a licensed bank or convert them into RMB at the prevailing onshore rate. However, for enterprises rated below standard by SAFE, enhanced monitoring may require immediate full settlement and the submission of monthly reconciliation reports.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Line 1 / Box 1 - Total Operating Revenue Report the Chinese JV's gross income from all business sources, including securities brokerage commissions, CFD spreads, derivatives execution fees, account maintenance fees, and custody revenue. Report worldwide income of the Chinese resident JV under Articles 1 and 6 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Do not characterize offshore brokerage fees as nontaxable foreign-source income unless the transaction negotiation, client onboarding, execution, and risk management are genuinely performed outside China and fully documented. If any portion is booked in a non-resident affiliate, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is mandatory. Late filing attracts a statutory fine under Article 62 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration from RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000, increased to RMB 20,000 in serious cases, plus daily late payment interest of 0.05% on unpaid tax under Article 32. Failure to pay tax by intentional concealment or false declaration constitutes tax evasion under Article 63, carrying a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and criminal referral when the amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and is over 10% of the tax payable. Withholding agents that fail to withhold the 10% outbound withholding tax may be penalized under Articles 68 and 69, and the responsible directors may be added to the tax-related credit blacklist, triggering travel bans and asset enforcement.

Only costs supported by qualified invoices or valid vouchers are deductible.
