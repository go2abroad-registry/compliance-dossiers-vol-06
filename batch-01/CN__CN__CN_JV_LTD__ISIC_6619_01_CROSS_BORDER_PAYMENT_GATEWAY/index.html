Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

General rule - None: the Companies Law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company and permits phased subscription under the subscribed-capital regime. Special license overlay - because the Company's ISIC 6619 payment gateway business requires a Non-bank Payment Institution Permit, the PRC Regulations on Supervision and Administration of Non-bank Payment Institutions require a minimum registered capital of RMB 100 million (approximately USD 14 million) in monetary funds, fully paid in cash before the PBOC license application. The foreign shareholder's subscription funds must be remitted through an FDI capital account at an approved bank, verified by a Chinese licensed CPA, and maintained as a blocked/restricted settlement capital balance subject to SAFE use rules and PBOC risk monitoring; withdrawal or pledge of the paid-in capital before licensing will trigger administrative penalty.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), the national unified electronic tax platform operated by STA.

E-government filing portal

1) For the China-established Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); no separate VAT ID is issued. 2) Within 30 days of license issuance, complete tax registration and taxpayer status confirmation on the Electronic Tax Bureau. 3) File for general VAT taxpayer status electronically; registration is compulsory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 and can be elected voluntarily below that threshold. 4) For a foreign non-resident entity with no Chinese establishment, submit non-resident tax registration through the e-Tax Bureau or the competent district STA office using the foreign home-country TIN, the apostilled/legalized entity registration documents, the signed cross-border contract, and the appointed Chinese withholding agent details; the STA will assign a non-resident taxpayer code for withholding and VAT compliance. 5) For the payment gateway licence, complete the People's Bank of China (PBOC) cross-border payment institution filing and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) foreign-exchange registration, then update the tax registration with any licensed revenue items.

None. China does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch entity elections for a Chinese equity joint venture. The only available election is voluntary general VAT taxpayer registration for an otherwise small-scale taxpayer, filed through the e-Tax Bureau.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is mandatory for every incorporated entity within 30 days of business license issuance, irrespective of turnover. Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 are exempt from VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the exporter must be a general VAT taxpayer, goods must be physically cleared beyond China's customs frontier, the customs export declaration must be maintained, and export proceeds must be repatriated and settled through a designated foreign-exchange bank. For qualifying cross-border services, including offshore payment facilitation services consumed entirely outside China, zero-rating is available only where the service falls within the STA's cross-border zero-rating or exemption catalogue under CaiShui [2016] No. 36; the overseas merchant or acquiring bank must be outside China, the service must be performed abroad in substance, and the fee must be invoiced and received in foreign currency. Payment-processing services that do not qualify are subject to the ordinary VAT rate applicable to financial services (generally 6%).

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (customs-cleared, with declaration number)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or cross-border service agreement
  • Bank foreign-exchange settlement receipt for export proceeds (export proceeds collection voucher)
  • Certificate or declaration from the overseas counterparty confirming offshore consumption and receipt of service

China mandates the use of the Golden Tax System Phase IV for all VAT invoice issuance. Full digital electronic invoices (Digital Electronic Invoices) are generated through the STA-controlled National Electronic Invoice Service Platform and are transmitted to the STA in real time. Special VAT invoices must contain the STA-issued unique invoice code and anti-fraud QR code; no third-party digital stamping is recognised, and all invoice reconciliation is performed in the taxpayer's Tax Digital Account on the e-Tax Bureau.

The STA may retrospectively disallow zero-rating and claw back output VAT plus late-payment surcharge if any documentation is missing, inconsistent, forged, or not repatriated within the statutory deadline. Administrative fines range from 50% to 300% of the evaded VAT; export tax rebate qualification may be suspended or revoked; and serious violations are referred for criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law and false special VAT invoice issuance under Article 205. Fraudulent claims are not subject to the ordinary tax assessment limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains selective convertibility under a managed floating exchange rate regime. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, are generally RMB-convertible; the Joint Venture may settle legitimate export receipts and import payments through designated banks under SAFE's goods and services trade verification system. Capital account transfers such as foreign-direct-investment equity contributions, external loans, and cross-border guarantee activation require regulatory registration and are settled only upon presentation of underlying authenticity documentation. For this ISIC 6619 entity, the initial capital injection will be booked into an FDI capital settlement account and converted to RMB at the prevailing market rate; movement of funds beyond the registered business scope or premature repatriation of capital before liquidation is prohibited. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder is treated as a current-account item and is permitted after annual audit, tax clearance, and foreign-exchange declaration; however, cumulative profits cannot be remitted without proof of Chinese corporate income tax payment and SAFE filing. The JV will therefore need robust compliance controls, audited accounts, and a designated relationship bank to ensure convertibility is not blocked at the execution stage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's rules for goods trade, export receipts must be collected and repatriated into a designated domestic foreign-exchange account within 90 calendar days from the date of export; any extension beyond 90 days requires registration with SAFE. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency or sell it at market rate, but every receipt must be matched to a customs declaration and logged in the trade-in-goods monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign exchange regulator, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Mainland China does not licence standalone offshore fintech EMIs for corporate treasury clearing; non-bank payment institutions require both a PBOC payment licence and SAFE cross-border settlement qualification, and even then they are limited to specific e-commerce/travel/shopping transactions rather than general corporate liquidity management. Consequently, offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Stripe Treasury cannot directly service a mainland JV PSP unless they operate through a licensed Chinese partner bank, which still requires the JV to hold direct bank accounts; using them would duplicate KYC, create regulatory exposure, and is not recommended for the Stage 4 treasury platform.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 100 million for related-party intangible property transfers and financial asset transfers, CNY 200 million for related-party tangible asset transfers, CNY 40 million for related-party service transactions, and a Master File is required when total related-party transactions exceed CNY 1 billion in the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) label used in low-tax jurisdictions, but the State Taxation Administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law to disregard entities lacking economic substance and to deny treaty benefits; the cross-border payment gateway entity must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China through local management decisions, operational personnel, payment infrastructure, risk control systems, and the contractual assumption of economic risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes confiscation of illegal income and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid, plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%; Article 201 of the Criminal Law provides criminal punishment for tax evasion, including imprisonment and fines where the evasion is large or the proportion is substantial.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct false financial statements, commingle personal and corporate assets, or cause the JV to file fraudulent returns may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law principles, be ordered to restitute corporate assets, be blacklisted by the tax authority, be placed on exit-control and travel-ban lists, and face imprisonment of up to seven years for serious tax evasion.
Non-Arm's Length Transfer Pricing and Artificial Shell Company Structure

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorize the State Taxation Administration to re-price intercompany transactions, adjust taxable income, apply a 50% to 500% penalty for underdeclared tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, impose daily interest on unpaid tax, and deny treaty benefits for conduit arrangements lacking substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or execute fictitious intercompany contracts can be held personally liable for the economic enrichment diverted from the Chinese entity; they may be subject to civil restitution, asset freezing, exit restrictions and blacklisting, and if the arrangement is found to be fraudulent tax evasion or criminal money laundering, imprisonment for the responsible officers is possible.
AML and Unlicensed Payment Services

Statutory sanction: Enforcement by the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange includes confiscation of illegal income, substantial administrative fines, license revocation, and referral for criminal prosecution; Article 225 of the PRC Criminal Law punishes illegal business operations, and Article 191 punishes money laundering, including financial penalties and imprisonment for the natural persons involved.

Director personal liability: Directors of the JV and the offshore nominee or shell entity may be personally prosecuted as accomplices, have personal assets frozen and forfeited for money laundering, be blacklisted by PBOC and the State Taxation Administration, be subject to travel bans and exit-control measures, and face imprisonment of up to ten years for aggravated money laundering offenses.
