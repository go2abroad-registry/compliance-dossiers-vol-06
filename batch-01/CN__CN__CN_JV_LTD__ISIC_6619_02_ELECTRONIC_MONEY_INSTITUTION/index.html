Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No universal paid-up minimum applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the 2023 Company Law. However, because the entity will operate as an electronic money institution issuing virtual IBANs and managing digital wallet reserves, the PBOC imposes a special capital floor: for a payment institution with nationwide electronic-money and settlement services, minimum paid-up registered capital is RMB 100,000,000 (USD equivalent at the prevailing PBOC mid-rate) and must be fully paid in cash before the payment license is granted. The entire paid-in capital must be deposited in a blocked escrow account maintained with a PBOC-approved commercial bank; the funds may not be withdrawn, pledged, offset, or applied to losses without prior written PBOC approval and a further board resolution following license issuance.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for heavy industrial civil-defense permits, but the registered office must obtain the standard fire-safety inspection or occupancy-fire-safety filing from the local Fire and Rescue Bureau for commercial premises. The technology infrastructure supporting electronic-money issuance must satisfy PBOC cybersecurity requirements and ISO 27001 or equivalent data-security certification. Workplace safety training and emergency response procedures must comply with the Work Safety Law for office-based employees.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is performed on the STA Electronic Tax Administration portal and e-invoice clearance is performed on the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Within 30 days after the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license of the Sino-foreign equity joint venture, the JV must complete taxpayer registration with the competent STA office. Under the integrated market registration system, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier; no separate TIN certificate is issued. The procurement procedure includes: (i) submission of the SAMR business license, joint venture contract, articles of association, registered-address proof, legal representative identification, finance officer identification, basic account-opening certificate from a Chinese bank, and expected business scope; (ii) confirmation of the tax filing period (monthly or quarterly) and taxpayer type (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer); and (iii) activation of the VAT invoice system on the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. A foreign non-resident enterprise with no establishment in mainland China that receives Chinese-source income requiring withholding must apply separately to the competent STA branch for a non-resident taxpayer identification number, submit its incorporation certificate and a copy of the underlying Chinese contract, and appoint a withholding agent; this non-resident TIN is distinct from the resident JV's USCC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box elections, pass-through elections, or hybrid mismatch elections. A Sino-foreign equity joint venture is a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is separately taxed on worldwide taxable income; group consolidation for income tax purposes is not available. For VAT purposes only, the JV may elect small-scale taxpayer status while annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000, or may elect general taxpayer status, but this is an invoicing and credit-collection mechanism rather than an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No value-added tax registration threshold applies to a newly incorporated PRC legal entity; tax registration is mandatory within 30 days of the business license regardless of revenue. The general VAT taxpayer compulsory threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below that threshold the JV may be a small-scale taxpayer and may qualify for a preferential monthly VAT exemption of up to RMB 100,000 (RMB 300,000 per quarter); however, as a licensed electronic money institution generating fee income, the JV should obtain general taxpayer status to be able to issue VAT special invoices and recover input VAT on operating costs. Supplies of financial and payment services are taxable at 6% unless zero-rated as qualified exports.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, export zero-rating is available only where the goods are physically declared to and cleared by Chinese Customs as exported, the exporter receives the sales proceeds from outside China, and the transaction is supported by a valid export contract and commercial invoice. For services, qualifying cross-border services supplied wholly to non-resident recipients and consumed entirely outside mainland China may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt; the JV must demonstrate that the recipient is outside China, the service is not used in China, and the consideration is paid by non-residents through designated foreign-exchange banks. For virtual IBAN issuance and digital wallet payment-processing fees, the zero-rating claim will be accepted only if the STA's place-of-supply rules and the cross-border service zero-rating filing requirements for licensed financial institutions are satisfied; any service that facilitates a domestic Chinese payment or is consumed in China remains taxable at 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration form) issued by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China showing that the goods physically departed China and identifying the JV as the exporter
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, railway waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing delivery to a destination outside mainland China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the designated bank's foreign-exchange income declaration and export-proceeds receipt record confirming that the export consideration was collected in convertible currency from a non-resident payer
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice matching the commodity description, quantity, price, and payment terms
  • VAT invoice and the corresponding export VAT zero-rating/refund declaration schedule filed with the STA

China operates the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform as the mandatory clearing system for e-invoices. Newly established enterprises must use the platform or an STA-certified tax-control device before issuing any VAT invoice; each invoice is assigned a unique invoice code and number, is affixed with an anti-fraud digital stamp, and is transmitted to STA in real time. Fully digitalised electronic invoices carry a structured data file (XML) and are automatically reconciled against the taxpayer's VAT return, customs data, and banking records. High-volume licensed financial institutions may access the STA electronic invoice API to batch create, stamp, and report invoices; offline invoices or private-format fiscal documents are not recognised for VAT input credit or export zero-rating purposes.

The STA, Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange match e-invoice data, customs declaration data, logistics data, and banking receipts before export zero-rating is honoured. If a zero-rating claim is found to be fraudulent or unsupported, the STA will retroactively claw back the refunded VAT, impose a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05% on the amount underpaid, and impose an administrative fine of one to five times the falsely refunded amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. In serious cases, criminal liability for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds may be pursued, with imprisonment and fines for the responsible individuals; the taxpayer's credit rating may be downgraded and its access to e-invoicing and future export refund facilitation may be suspended. Where fraudulent misrepresentation is established, the ordinary three-year assessment limitation does not bar recovery for the periods under investigation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which current account transactions are convertible after authenticity verification of the underlying trade or service, while capital account transactions remain subject to case-by-case SAFE registration, designated bank accounts, and strict post-entry use monitoring. For this Sino-foreign equity joint venture, the registered capital injection must first complete the SAFE foreign direct investment registration before any inbound remittance can be credited to a foreign currency capital account. Once settled, the proceeds enter an RMB capital account and are ring-fenced for genuine operational expenditures within the approved business scope. The electronic-money institution business model additionally triggers PBoC payment oversight because the virtual IBAN and digital wallet float must be segregated from the JV corporate treasury, and client funds cannot be treated as free corporate money. Dividend remittances are permitted after statutory audit and tax clearance, but require board approval, audited financial statements, tax payment proof, and SAFE cross-border payment filing, and the bank will independently verify the economic substance of the profit behind each distribution. In practice, current account convertibility for trade-backed flows such as merchant settlement, platform service fees, and software development costs is manageable, but any attempt to pass e-money client balances through the corporate capital account would be rejected as speculative or commingled. Overall, the treasury must operate with full SAFE/PBoC documentation discipline and treat capital and current account convertibility as two separate regulatory rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign exchange bank account; China no longer imposes a fixed compulsory surrender ratio for general merchandise receipts. A Class-A trade enterprise may retain receipts in foreign currency or convert them to RMB voluntarily at the market exchange rate, while Class-B and Class-C enterprises face heightened authenticity checks and may need to present, on a transaction-by-transaction basis, export customs declarations, logistics documents, invoices, and the banks electronic verification records. Although the historical 90-day export collection verification timeline has been replaced by SAFEs dynamic trade-in-goods monitoring system, banks still require that the foreign currency repatriation occur within commercially reasonable settlement terms and that any overdue receivable be justified with documentary evidence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. Offshore EMIs cannot legally maintain domestic settlement accounts, issue domestic IBANs, or provide RMB clearing services directly to this China-based JV unless they hold a valid Payment Business License issued by the PBoC and complete the relevant SAFE filing for cross-border payments. In practice, offshore EMIs are limited to acting as white-label correspondent front-ends that route instructions to Chinese tier-one banks; their inability to demonstrate local licensing, reserve segregation, and SAFE reporting makes them unsuitable for capital escrow, FX surrender, and operational clearing. The JV treasury should instead contract directly with a licensed domestic bank and, if necessary, establish a wholly-owned licensed non-bank payment subsidiary for e-money reserve management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital must flow only after SAFE FDI registration and the opening of a designated foreign currency capital account at an authorized tier-one bank. The wire should be sent by the named foreign investor from its own regulated account, with SWIFT field 70 clearly identifying the JV name, investor name, and equity injection purpose. The receiving bank then files the receipt through SAFEs foreign exchange receipt and payment declaration system using the direct investment equity capital transaction purpose code, after which funds may be settled into the RMB capital account. All disbursements from the capital account must match the approved business purpose; a PRC licensed CPA must subsequently issue a capital verification report to confirm that the registered capital has been fully paid and recorded, and the report must be kept with the banks compliance file for future repatriation, audit, and deregistration procedures.Outbound trade disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for standard payments to verified overseas suppliers, documentary collections for lower-trust transactions where the JV retains shipping document control, and commercial letters of credit opened by the JVs banker when the counterparty requires bank payment undertaking. Every instruction must carry truthful contract, invoice, customs and logistics references so the bank can complete SAFE goods-trade or services-trade authenticity verification; all RMB cross-border settlements may also clear through CIPS, while foreign currency payments use SWIFT and the domestic banks clearing network. The treasury must maintain audit-ready files for three years to support each clearing rail.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD and CNY/EUR forward contracts with SAFE-authorized tier-one banks, limited to hedge known current-account exposures;Cross-currency interest rate and principal swaps for long-term capital raising and investment in the e-money platform, subject to real-exposure and maturity-matching documentation;Structured FX options such as collars and participating forwards used for cost-effective hedging of forecast trade settlement flows, with no speculative leverage;Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for onshore corporate hedging; hedging must use deliverable onshore products, although an offshore parent may independently enter into NDFs outside China without contaminating the JVs local clearing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for offshore digital EMIs. Offshore EMIs cannot legally maintain domestic settlement accounts, issue domestic IBANs, or provide RMB clearing services directly to this China-based JV unless they hold a valid Payment Business License issued by the PBoC and complete the relevant SAFE filing for cross-border payments. In practice, offshore EMIs are limited to acting as white-label correspondent front-ends that route instructions to Chinese tier-one banks; their inability to demonstrate local licensing, reserve segregation, and SAFE reporting makes them unsuitable for capital escrow, FX surrender, and operational clearing. The JV treasury should instead contract directly with a licensed domestic bank and, if necessary, establish a wholly-owned licensed non-bank payment subsidiary for e-money reserve management.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous local file and master file preparation is triggered when any of the following related-party transaction amounts are exceeded in a fiscal year: tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers including loans, deposits, and virtual wallet reserve placements above RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. Master file is additionally required where the enterprise and its related parties have cross-border related-party revenue exceeding RMB 1 billion or meet the thresholds under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China adopts the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Under the BEPS framework, an electronic money institution must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed in China or in the jurisdiction where profits are reported: strategic management of customer reserves, credit and settlement risk control, compliance and AML decision-making, negotiation and execution of SEPA and central-bank clearing contracts, and treasury management of digital wallet float. If a JV merely holds contracts and accounting records while decisions are made abroad, the tax authority may recharacterize the arrangement, attack the residency of the entity, deny treaty benefits, impose withholding tax on deemed distributions, and levy penalties. A Chinese JV must maintain real employees, physical and digital substance, board autonomy, and auditable evidence of all core activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Artificial Margin Stripping

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the Transfer Pricing Adjustment Rules, assessing additional taxable income, applying the 10% withholding tax on the recharacterized deemed dividend, imposing late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, and issuing a penalty of 50% to 5 times of the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63. If the amount is significant, criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 applies, with a fine and imprisonment of not more than 7 years for especially large amounts.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where the JV is de facto a mere instrument of the foreign shareholder and the directors knowingly executed fraudulent invoices or circular payment schemes. Directors become personally liable for the unpaid tax arrears, are subject to civil restitution of diverted funds, may be placed on the tax-authority blacklist with a travel ban, and may face imprisonment for tax evasion. Criminal fines are imposed on the company and the responsible director individually.
Shell / Conduit Structure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority denies treaty benefits under the Beneficial Ownership rules and General Anti-Avoidance Rule, recharacterizing the intermediate company as a pure conduit. It imposes the full 10% statutory withholding tax, or the higher tax if the immediate shareholder is in a non-treaty jurisdiction, together with late-payment interest and a penalty ranging from 50% to 5 times of the shortfall. The transaction may be recast as a direct distribution to the ultimate shareholder and adjusted accordingly.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the conduit structure and executed the dividend routing can be held personally liable for restitution of the underwithheld amounts. Chinese courts may lift the veil where the conduit is found to be abusive and the directors acted beyond good faith. The individuals are subject to personal asset freezing, travel restrictions, blacklisting from holding board positions in Chinese licensed financial institutions, and criminal prosecution under the Tax Collection Administration Law and Criminal Law if fraud is proven.
Unlicensed Fintech/EMI Operation and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) imposes rectification and revocation of any ancillary license, confiscation of illegal gains, and administrative fines. Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law, serious violations carry fines from RMB 1 million to RMB 10 million and suspension or closure of business. Unlicensed absorption of public funds triggers criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 176 for illegally absorbing public deposits, with imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines. Tax implications include disallowance of all deductions tied to the illegal business, full CIT assessment at 25% on gross profits, and felony-based disqualification.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers are subject to personal asset freezing, travel bans (blacklisting), and prohibition from serving in financial institutions. The corporate veil is pierced when the directors commingle customer reserve funds into operating capital or use the JV as a facade for capital flight. Personal civil restitution is ordered for losses caused to customers and the State. Criminal imprisonment may be imposed for illegal deposit-taking, money laundering, or deliberately orchestrating offshore tax fraud through the unlicensed payment platform.
