Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None at formation under PRC Company Law or Foreign Investment Law: there is no statutory minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. Capital contributions must be made on the schedule agreed in the Articles of Association, and foreign-currency contributions must be remitted through a designated foreign-currency capital account reported to SAFE. Separately, before the payment business can launch, the PBOC non-bank payment institution license will require a material paid-up capital, generally RMB 100 million for nationwide payment business or RMB 30 million under the legacy provincial-scope regime, which must be actually paid in cash and maintained for regulatory solvency, but no escrow-blocked account is imposed by the SAMR formation proceeding.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); taxpayer filings and registrations are executed through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal administered by the STA and local tax offices.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-foreign equity joint venture, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier. Registration with SAMR is followed by automatic transmission of tax registration data to the STA Electronic Tax Bureau; within 30 days of obtaining the Business License, the JV must update its registration with the competent tax authority and register for corporate income tax and VAT. A foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China is not issued a USCC and instead registers for non-resident withholding taxation through the Chinese payer or withholding agent, submitting the required contract and withholding registration to the competent tax authority; a taxpayer reference number is allocated for such non-resident taxation, but a separate VAT ID is not required for purely offshore activities.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover, which triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; a Chinese-established joint venture must complete tax and VAT registration upon issuance of its Business License irrespective of turnover, and may use the small-scale taxpayer simplified method only while eligible below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate is available for goods physically exported from China's customs territory to a non-resident buyer where the customs export declaration is accepted and released by the General Administration of Customs (GACC), the risks and rewards of the goods pass to the foreign purchaser, a commercial invoice and transport documentation support the transaction, and export proceeds are repatriated and recorded through Chinese banks under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) verification. Qualifying offshore services, including eligible technology and information-technology services supplied to non-resident enterprises, may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt where the recipient is outside China, the service is consumed wholly outside China, and payment is received in foreign exchange from outside China; pure card issuing and financial fee income is taxable at 6% unless a specific statutory offshore exemption or zero-rating provision applies.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Customs Export Declaration) with GACC release record and declaration number
  • Bill of Lading (sea or multimodal) or Master Air Waybill evidencing the China export port and foreign destination
  • Foreign exchange receipt record from a Chinese bank evidencing collection of export proceeds and the corresponding SAFE export collection verification data
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Valid tax-controlled invoice issued for the export sale where required under STA invoicing rules

Taxpayers must issue tax-controlled invoices through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the national fully digitalized electronic invoice system. Invoice metadata is transmitted to the STA in near real time through approved API integrations, and each fully digitalized electronic invoice carries a unique digital invoice code and tax-authority electronic seal. Special VAT invoices conferring input VAT credit must be uploaded for authentication and matching in the STA invoice verification system before the credit may be claimed; failure to satisfy the digital stamping and clearance requirements invalidates input VAT recovery and gives rise to late-filing and procedural penalties.

The STA and GACC cross-check customs export declarations, VAT invoices, transport documentation, bank settlement records and SAFE collection data. Export VAT refunds and zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective clawback if goods do not leave China, are diverted to domestic sale, are re-imported, if services are found to be consumed within China, or if export proceeds are not received and verified within the statutory period. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, the tax authority may recover the refunded or zero-rated VAT, add daily late-payment surcharge, and impose a penalty of not less than 50% and up to five times the tax involved; fraudulent export-refund activity can lead to suspension of export refund eligibility, criminal referral, fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate with strict capital account controls; current account items are generally convertible, but every transaction must be documented and processed through a designated commercial bank under SAFE supervision. Capital account entries, such as foreign direct investment, external financing, intercompany loans and portfolio investments, require individual registrations, filings or approvals. For the JV, the inbound equity contribution must be placed in a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and conversion to renminbi can only occur after the bank performs a foreign investment verification against the registered capital schedule. Profit remittances to non-resident shareholders are treated as current account transfers only when supported by audited annual financial statements, corporate income tax payment proof and a board-approved dividend resolution; any missing tax filing or unsettled SAFE record causes a hard remittance hold. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion but must be repatriated and verified through the goods trade verification system. The result is that the treasury must never assume free convertibility; each flow is separately adjudicated and can be refused where documentary evidence is incomplete or where the purpose code does not match the underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within the time period in the trade contract or, if none, within 90 days from customs declaration. The bank will then credit the dedicated goods-trade foreign-exchange settlement account; the entity may choose to retain the foreign currency until required for payments or convert to RMB at the bank's prevailing onshore rate. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for exports; surrender takes effect through repatriation and entry into the settlement account. Failure to repatriate within the prescribed window results in SAFE's export-verification system flagging the discrepancy, blocking future settlement transactions until the overdue payment is explained or returned.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (State Administration of Foreign Exchange, SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Not Viable. Non-bank fintech EMI clearing agents are barred from directly serving mainland Chinese legal entities unless they hold a PBOC payment institution license; no offshore EMI can open an account or act as settlement layer for the JV. The virtual card issuance must therefore be structured with a licensed Chinese commercial bank as principal clearing member, with the fintech JV acting purely as the card-controlling software layer. Any inbound or outbound remittance using an EMI's pooled customer account or unregulated treasury structure would be rejected by domestic banks and falls outside the SAFE registration framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholders execute an SWIFT MT103 from the overseas remitting bank to the JV's designated foreign-exchange capital account at the sponsor tier-1 bank. The wire instruction must include the SAFE purpose code for inbound equity investment, the JV's Chinese corporate registration number, and the reference to the Foreign Investment Registration obtained from SAFE. Upon credit, the bank performs a capital verification process against the approved joint venture contract and the notarized capitalization schedule, and records the inflow in SAFE's cross-border receipt system. Conversion to RMB is only permitted after a valid underlying business contract or statutory project approval is presented; a separate RMB basic deposit account is opened at the same branch to receive the converted funds.Settlement of trade payables to overseas suppliers is conducted via SWIFT MT103 after the JV provides customs declarations, pro-forma invoices and shipping documents. For documentary trade, the sponsor bank may issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit at the JV's request, or the exporter may present a documentary collection after shipment; both routes are compatible with SAFE's goods trade verification regime. For the JV's virtual card disbursements, merchant payments are executed through the Mastercard or Visa network, with batch settlement under the sponsor bank's dual foreign-currency and RMB accounts. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be contemporaneously prepared when annual controlled transactions reach any of the following thresholds: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for related-party intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for related-party financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is additionally required for enterprises with aggregate related-party transactions of RMB 1 billion or above in the financial year.

Economic substance scope: China applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), substance-over-form doctrine, and beneficial-ownership tests. The tax authorities may recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance, deny treaty benefits, and assess a deemed dividend. Although China does not maintain an OECD/ESR-style Economic Substance Regime, the entity must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China, including card scheme contracting, risk management, transaction authorization, BIN sponsorship contracts, and spend-control analytics, to support the permanent establishment and beneficial-ownership status of all related-party arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63, the tax authority orders payment of the unpaid tax and a fine of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; the enterprise's taxpayer credit is downgraded and its license may be suspended for e-payment activities.

Director personal liability: Directors and supervisors who planned or participated in the fraudulent tax filing are jointly liable to restore diverted corporate money; the court may issue a travel ban under the Tax Collection Law social credit system; and under Criminal Law Article 201, responsible persons may be sentenced to up to 7 years' imprisonment, a fine, and confiscation of personal assets in serious cases.
Withholding and Remittance Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Law Article 69, the withholding agent is liable to recover the unwithheld tax; the authority may additionally fine the withholding agent 50% to 3 times the unwithheld amount. The intended tax is collected from the payee if not withheld.

Director personal liability: Directors who instructed or knowingly permitted the wrongful reclassification of a dividend as a loan or service payment to evade withholding become jointly and severally liable for the treasury loss; their personal assets can be attached for restitution; the court may issue a departure ban in cross-border collections; and in deliberate concealment cases, personal criminal liability may follow under criminal tax provisions, including imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Entity / Treaty Abuse / AML Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may re-domicile profits, deny treaty benefits, impose a deemed dividend withholding tax, add interest and 50%-5x GAAR penalties; separately, under the AML law, failure to perform customer due diligence, suspicious-transaction reporting, and actual-bank-ownership checks can attract fines up to RMB 5 million for the institution and RMB 500,000 for compliance officers.

Director personal liability: If the corporate veil is pierced, directors are personally liable for unpaid income tax, social insurance, and creditor debts. Blacklisting by the tax and customs authorities restricts them from leaving China or acting as directors. Criminal exposure includes the new criminal liability provisions for AML violations, with imprisonment up to 10 years for severe laundering of criminal proceeds, and personal confiscation of assets concealed through the shell structure.
