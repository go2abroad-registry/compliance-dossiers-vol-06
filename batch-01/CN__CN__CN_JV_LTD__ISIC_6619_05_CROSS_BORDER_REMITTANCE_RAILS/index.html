Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices or commercial mailboxes are strictly prohibited for a cross-border remittance agency. The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) require a physical, staffed office within the registered jurisdiction, with a dedicated compliance officer and secure record-keeping facilities. The office must be accessible for regulatory inspections and must have a permanent address that is not a P.O. Box. Banks and payment service providers will reject any application that relies on a virtual office, as it fails to meet the 'place of effective management' test under Chinese tax law and the 'physical presence' requirement under the Payment and Clearing Rules.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No nationwide statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to ordinary limited liability companies under the PRC Company Law, but the 2023 Company Law requires shareholders to pay all subscribed capital within five years of establishment unless the Articles of Association prescribe an earlier payment schedule. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture performing consumer remittance and cross-border money transmission as a non-bank payment institution, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) may impose a special paid-in registered capital requirement as a condition for the Payment Business License, generally assessed on the geographic scope and risk profile of the payment activity. The Company Law does not require a blocked escrow account for corporate formation, but if the PBOC license review demands a capital custody account or a committed capital verification certificate, such funds must be held in a dedicated restricted account and cannot be released without PBOC approval.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a digital consumer remittance platform and registered office; however, if the Company opens a physical customer service branch or cash handling center, such premises must receive a fire safety acceptance certificate from the local fire and rescue department under the PRC Fire Protection Law, comply with workplace safety inspections under the Work Safety Law, and maintain functional fire alarms, extinguishers, emergency exits and structural load compliance before employees and cash-handling operations commence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections comparable to OECD systems. A resident enterprise cannot elect pass-through treatment; special deferred tax treatment for qualifying corporate reorganizations is available only under the statutory enterprise income tax rules, not as an entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual VAT-taxable sales is the mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration threshold; enterprises below this threshold are classified as Small-Scale Taxpayers, although all tax-registered enterprises receive a taxpayer identification number through the Unified Social Credit Code regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating applies only where goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory, are declared for export through Chinese Customs, transfer title and risk to a non-resident buyer, and are supported by valid export sales documentation and foreign exchange receipt. For services, zero-rating or VAT exemption applies only to enumerated cross-border taxable services under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement [2016] No. 29 that are supplied to overseas units or individuals and wholly consumed outside China; for the cross-border remittance rails activity, the taxpayer must establish that the payment service is performed entirely outside China, the recipient is non-resident, no PRC real estate or goods are involved, and the service falls within the qualifying cross-border service catalogue. If the remittance service does not fall within the zero-rated or exempt catalogue, the domestic supply remains subject to VAT at 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Customs Export Declaration) filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of goods from PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the customs declaration
  • Export sales contract or order confirmation with the non-resident buyer
  • Bank receipt and SAFE Foreign Exchange Income Declaration evidencing inward remittance of export proceeds, or the export proceeds verification record under the SAFE Trade in Foreign Exchange monitoring system

China mandates issuance of VAT invoices through the National VAT Invoice Management System integrated with Golden Tax Phase IV. Fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices (fully digital e-invoices) must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which supports API/data-interface connectivity for enterprise systems; invoice data are transmitted to the tax authority in near real time for fiscal clearance, and each invoice carries a unique invoice number, QR code, and anti-fraud digital signature. Paper special VAT invoices require licensed tax-control equipment. The platform performs automated risk matching among invoice data, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange collection data.

Export VAT zero-rating and refund claims are subject to automated cross-validation by Golden Tax Phase IV, Chinese Customs export declaration data, and the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system. Under Article 66 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent export refunds are subject to recovery (clawback) of the refunded tax, late-payment interest on the unpaid tax, a fine of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained, and suspension or revocation of export-tax-refund eligibility for the prescribed period. Evidence of intentional fabrication may be referred for criminal investigation under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and the enterprise and responsible persons may face additional customs penalties if false export declarations are used to support the zero-rating claim.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with RMB partially convertible. Current-account transactions are generally convertible under IMF Article VIII once the underlying real trade or service transaction has been verified by the designating bank, but every remittance must be evidenced and filed in the SAFE cross-border receipt/payment monitoring platform. Capital-account items are tightly controlled. Inward foreign direct investment capital subscription proceeds can be credited to a SAFE-registered capital account (Foreign Currency Capital Contribution Account), but unrestricted conversion and deployment require a valid board capital-use plan, a document-by-document payment verification basis, and post-verification by the bank. The Sino-foreign equity JV is also subject to restricted profit remittances: distributable profits can be converted and remitted abroad only after annual audit, tax clearance from the tax authority, and SAFE current-account remittance settlement; insufficient paid-in capital or unfiled tax liabilities will block the outflow. There is no free intragroup loan set-off or netting without SAFE approval. Treasury planning must therefore distinguish current-account flows (remittance service fees, agent commissions, operating expenses) from capital-account flows (registered capital injection, dividends, foreign debt, equity transfers) and maintain a separate paper trail for each category.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Although China has abolished mandatory FX surrender for current-account receipts, all export and service proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into a licensed bank account within 90 days of the invoice or customs-export date unless SAFE has approved overseas retention. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for retained eligible FX receipts, and conversion to RMB is voluntary, but the repatriation ratio is 100% for all amounts above the permitted offshore-retention threshold. The entity must complete the foreign-exchange income declaration and trade/service verification in the SAFE reporting system before the bank processes conversion or payment of the RMB counterparty.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the delegated foreign-exchange control administrator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banks and non-bank fintech payment firms are not legally viable as the primary clearing or treasury counterparty for this mainland-incorporated joint venture. China's payment-services regulations require any entity providing payment or cross-border money transmission services in China to obtain a Payment Business License from PBOC and complete SAFE cross-border payment business filing. Offshore EMI licenses confer no right to open Chinese RMB accounts, maintain capital accounts, or settle client funds with domestic clearing rails. Such providers can only be used as an upstream foreign-currency aggregation or non-mainland corridor reference where they do not hold client money domestically or create unlicensed FX exposure. Legal viability rating: Low for domestic clearing and custody, but Medium for offshore-complementary treasury connectivity.

Conversion of the foreign-currency proceeds into RMB for rental, payroll, and operating expenditure may only occur on a real-need, transaction-document basis, with each drawdown matched to invoices, contracts or tax obligations.
