Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing is administered through the nationwide E-Tax Administration (electronic tax bureau) portal and the E-Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation; the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code is automatically created and serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number for tax filing and VAT purposes. Step 2: Within 30 days after business license issuance, complete taxpayer registration in the local STA E-Tax Administration portal, submitting entity details, registered address, accounting standards, legal representative, and bank settlement account information. Step 3: Confirm VAT taxpayer classification: small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; otherwise the entity may elect small-scale status subject to statutory limits. Step 4: Activate e-invoice entitlement and obtain the tax control number through the STA E-Invoice Service Platform. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment, the Chinese payer or withholding agent must first complete withholding tax registration in the E-Tax portal. If the non-resident entity itself needs to issue Chinese VAT invoices or conducts continuous taxable activities in China, it must obtain a temporary tax registration and a dedicated taxpayer identification number from the local STA office where the activities occur.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and does not grant hybrid mismatch elections. Available statutory elections include: (i) election between small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment and general VAT taxpayer treatment; (ii) election of the simplified levy-rate method for certain VAT-taxable services; and (iii) non-resident election to apply China-foreign income tax treaty protections through the non-resident tax filing procedure. No pass-through entity election is available for a Sino-foreign equity joint venture.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (quarterly RMB 300,000) qualify for small-scale taxpayer VAT exemption. All enterprises are required to obtain VAT registration after business license issuance; there is no turnover exemption for entity registration itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exports, zero-rating applies when goods are physically exported from mainland China and supported by an official customs export declaration. For cross-border services, zero-rating or VAT exemption applies when the services are supplied to an overseas enterprise or individual, are wholly consumed outside mainland China, and fall within the published catalogue of cross-border taxable services. For virtual CFO, bookkeeping, and management consulting services, the substantive criteria are: an overseas service recipient, no direct linkage to Chinese land or goods, a legally valid cross-border service agreement, and foreign-currency consideration received through an authorized bank. If these conditions are not met, the supply is treated as domestic and subject to VAT at 6%.

China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoices through the STA E-Invoice Service Platform. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must carry a unique digital tax-control number and be transmitted in real time to the STA through the Golden Tax Phase IV system. General VAT taxpayers using API integration must generate invoices only after online clearance and digital stamping; issuance of invalid, unauthorized, or manually pre-printed special invoices is prohibited. Any anomaly in invoice quota, sequencing, or declared versus invoiced amounts triggers automatic risk alerts, suspension of invoice quotas, and on-site tax inspection.

The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly verify export declarations, foreign-currency receipts, and invoice history. If zero-rating is claimed without verifiable export, if the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, or if documentation is discovered to be falsified, the tax authority will claw back the VAT rebate or zero-rated refund, impose daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05% on the unpaid tax, and charge an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export tax refund cases may be referred for criminal prosecution under Chinese law, resulting in confiscation, fines, and imprisonment for responsible officers. Unfiled or incorrectly documented export claims also trigger customs penalties and revocation of export rebate qualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility consistent with IMF Article VIII, but the capital account remains subject to comprehensive approval, registration, and reporting controls. A Sino-foreign equity joint venture may accept FDI capital only after the investor and the venture complete SAFE registration, open a foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-designated bank, and submit a genuine-use undertaking. Foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to China and either retained in foreign exchange accounts or converted to RMB only through banks applying the Foreign Investment Negative List and capital-expenditure rules. Profit remittance is not prohibited, but before dividends can be paid abroad the JV must complete annual statutory audit, settle all corporate income tax and withholding tax liabilities, secure board approval, and demonstrate that paid-in registered capital is in place and not damaged by losses. Current-account payments for professional services are normally allowed without SAFE pre-approval if the underlying contracts, invoices, tax records, and business purpose are genuine and consistent. This creates an operating environment where licensed domestic banks remain mandatory and offshore digital EMIs cannot serve as primary treasury rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, 100% of declared export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs declaration or the approved deferred-collection maturity. There is no fixed surrender ratio or forced conversion benchmark: an exporter may keep the repatriated foreign currency in an SAFE-approved foreign exchange settlement account or sell it voluntarily to the bank. The receiving bank must match the wire to the customs export declaration through SAFE's monitoring systems and record the receipt under the exporter's trade item; any mismatch, delayed collection, or offshore retention must be explained and, where applicable, approved by SAFE. Service export receipts follow the same 100% repatriation principle through the service trade and balance-of-payments reporting mechanism.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Overall viability is Low for primary account opening and Low-to-Medium for auxiliary payment orchestration. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not licensed by SAFE or the PBOC as depository institutions in mainland China, cannot open domestic RMB or FX accounts for a China-incorporated JV, cannot process FDI capital injection, cannot submit SAFE declarations, and cannot issue bank capital-verification certificates. They can, at best, operate as a front-end invoicing or payment collection tool where the actual settlement is executed by a Chinese licensed bank, or as a non-resident treasury overlay only after funds have legally left China and are held in a separately licensed offshore account. The JV must maintain its statutory Chinese bank account as the sole account of record for tax, FX, and capital-control purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing for inbound capital is: (i) complete post-incorporation FDI registration with SAFE or its delegated bank and confirm the JV business is outside the Foreign Investment Negative List; (ii) open a foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-qualified domestic bank; (iii) instruct the foreign parent to wire the contribution via SWIFT MT103 with remittance purpose Investment Capital Contribution (FDI) and the registered parent company name; (iv) the Chinese receiving bank verifies the beneficiary, remitter, and registered name, records the payment in the SAFE monitoring system, and credits the FX capital account; (v) convert only for approved operating expenditures using the RMB capital settlement account, requiring a declaration that funds will not be used for prohibited portfolio investment, entrusted lending, or real estate outside the permitted scope; (vi) after the contribution is fully paid, appoint a PRC registered accountant to issue the capital verification report and update SAFE registration. Remittances should not be fragmented to avoid reporting thresholds.For a virtual CFO consultancy, outbound payments are mostly current-account service fees, royalties, and professional fees payable under master service agreements. The cleanest rail is SWIFT MT103 for each service disbursement, supported by the agreed contract, invoice, service delivery records, applicable tax withholding certificate, and bank truthfulness review. The JV may buy FX at the onshore bank's real-time rate and remit subject to current-account documentation. For merchandise trade embedded in a broader group, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections remain available for goods, but the ISIC 6920 entity should not use inventory-based trade finance unless actual goods are transacted; using L/C or documentary collections without underlying goods movement will create AML and export-control red flags. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Overall viability is Low for primary account opening and Low-to-Medium for auxiliary payment orchestration. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not licensed by SAFE or the PBOC as depository institutions in mainland China, cannot open domestic RMB or FX accounts for a China-incorporated JV, cannot process FDI capital injection, cannot submit SAFE declarations, and cannot issue bank capital-verification certificates. They can, at best, operate as a front-end invoicing or payment collection tool where the actual settlement is executed by a Chinese licensed bank, or as a non-resident treasury overlay only after funds have legally left China and are held in a separately licensed offshore account. The JV must maintain its statutory Chinese bank account as the sole account of record for tax, FX, and capital-control purposes.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% effective WHT rate on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise with no Chinese establishment or place of business; the statutory nominal rate of 20% is reduced to 10% under EIT Law Articles 3, 4 and 37 and Implementation Regulations Article 91. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory for a tax year in which annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transfers, and RMB 40 million for provision of services. A Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year exceeded RMB 5.5 billion and the group engaged in related-party transactions; below those thresholds the JV must still file the annual Statement on Related Party Transactions with its EIT return.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but its General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47, Implementation Rules Article 120) allows full recharacterization of arrangements lacking reasonable commercial substance. For an outsourced virtual CFO service JV, the Chinese entity must be the actual entrepreneur exercising functions, owning relevant assets, controlling risks, and employing decision-making staff. A circular structure using an offshore invoicing shell to divert management fees, royalties, or dividend profits will be challenged under substance-over-form, Controlled Foreign Company rules under EIT Law Article 45, beneficial ownership denial, and permanent establishment attribution.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through understated service revenue and false invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law permits the tax authority to recover the unpaid tax, impose late-payment interest of 0.05% per day, and levy a fine of 50%-500% of the tax evaded. Where the evaded tax amount reaches the criminal threshold, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years on the directly responsible officers and accountants, and 3-7 years plus fines for extremely large evasion.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law when the shareholder or director misuses JV legal personality and asset segregation to evade taxes and divert corporate funds to personal accounts, exposing the director to joint and several restitution. The legal representative and actual controller become subject to the tax authority's joint-punishment blacklist: assets of the individual and spouse may be frozen, the director is placed on a social-credit blacklist, departure from Mainland China is blocked, and the individual may lose eligibility to serve as a director of any PRC company for serious tax violations.
Transfer pricing fraud and hollow cross-border CFO service fees

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 requires arm's length pricing and Article 48 authorizes a primary transfer-pricing adjustment plus interest computed at the base RMB central bank lending rate plus 5 percentage points. Article 47 GAAR permits the tax authority to disregard or recharacterize the scheme. Failure to file contemporaneous documentation under SAT Announcement 2016 No.42 attracts a documentation penalty of RMB 2,000-10,000, but the higher exposure is the compensatory adjustment and interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the intercompany agreements and orchestrated the circular fee payments are jointly and severally liable if the court finds abuse of corporate personality to strip Chinese assets; personal bank accounts of the controlling director may be attached for the assessed corporate tax. If the sham affiliate was knowingly used to falsify costs, the director becomes an accomplice in tax evasion, with criminal imprisonment up to 7 years, fines, a SAFE foreign-exchange penalty, and a personal travel ban recorded in the joint punishment system.
Abusive treaty benefit and intentional failure to withhold on disguised dividends

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement 2018 No.9 on Beneficial Owner and the Principal Purpose Test applicable under China's DTAs and certain MLI positions, the loan and royalty are recharacterized as dividends; the tax authority collects the differential WHT, imposes late-payment interest, and may apply a fine of 50%-300% of the amount that should have been withheld under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law. Intentional concealment of the withholding obligation triggers the same tax-evasion penalties as Article 63, including fines of 50%-500% of underpaid tax and criminal liability.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and finance director are personally accountable for the failure to withhold: the tax authority may enforce the fine and, if the director personally received funds disguised as loan repayments, observe the corporate veil for personal asset seizure and restitution. The non-resident director may be placed on the July 2018-style serious tax violator blacklist, barred from high-consumption activities, refused exit from China, disqualified from acting as a director or supervisor of any company in China, and criminally prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 for evading hundreds of thousands of RMB in withholding tax, with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
