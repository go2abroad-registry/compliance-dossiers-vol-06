Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for an ISIC 7020 consulting joint venture. Under PRC Company Law Article 47, shareholders must fully pay subscribed capital within five years of incorporation unless the Articles of Association impose an earlier payment schedule. Paid-in capital may be zero at the time of registration, but the subscribed amount must be commercially reasonable for the consulting undertaking and cannot be used artificially to evade creditors.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial fire permits, structural load inspections or workplace chemicals licensing; the leased office must nevertheless maintain the building's fire acceptance certificate, basic fire extinguishers, emergency evacuation signage and landlord-provided building safety compliance under the Fire Protection Law of the PRC and the PRC Work Safety Law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); administered locally by tax service branches; online electronic portal: National Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax, accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn). VAT-specific clearance is handled through the STA VAT Invoice Integrated Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

For the onshore Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the sole statutory tax identifier is the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation on the business license. The USCC is automatically shared with the STA under the 'one certificate, one code' reform, completing taxpayer registration ex officio. The local tax bureau then classifies the entity for VAT (small-scale or general taxpayer), opens tax registration records, sets electronic invoice quotas, and links the basic tax deposit bank account. The legal representative or authorized agent must authenticate identity through the national e-Tax platform and download the electronic taxpayer certificate. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that supplies taxable services to or derives China-source income from a Chinese counterparty must separately register through the competent tax authority for non-resident tax withholding/contract registration and must obtain a China tax identification (in the SAFE/STA contract filing system) before the first payment; this registration is coordinated with the domestic payer's withholding obligations.

The only material elections are the VAT small-scale taxpayer's election to become a general taxpayer before the compulsory threshold, and, where a non-resident enterprise cannot accurately calculate its taxable income, an election to apply the deemed profit rate method for Enterprise Income Tax withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for all PRC-established entities from incorporation through the USCC-based one-stop registration; compulsory general taxpayer registration is imposed when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, unless the taxpayer elects general status earlier. Below RMB 5,000,000 a taxpayer may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT export zero-rating if they are sold to a non-resident, physically leave China, are cleared by China Customs as export, and the export proceeds are repatriated and verified. Services qualify for 0% zero-rating only if the service is named in the STA's cross-border zero-rated services catalogue (e.g., international transport, certain R&D, design, software, and offshore financial services). ISIC 7020 tax-structuring and management-consulting services provided to overseas recipients are generally treated as VAT-exempt (not zero-rated) when the service is consumed wholly outside China; the JV must verify the latest STA catalogue and obtain an offshore service contract, foreign-recipient documentation, and foreign-currency payment evidence before relying on any preferential treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Customs electronic export manifest/declaration, with customs release endorsement)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill or multimodal transport document evidencing export from China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract / purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or SAFE foreign-exchange collection/receipt record evidencing actual repatriation of export proceeds
  • Foreign Trade Operator Registration / Customs Registration Certificate for exporters
  • For cross-border services: offshore service agreement, foreign recipient business-license or tax-identification evidence, service delivery verification, and overseas payment receipt

The STA mandates issuance through the fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) system for most taxpayers; invoices are generated in the taxpayer's tax digital account, transmitted in real time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system, and validated with a fiscal invoice number and encrypted digital stamp before delivery. Traditional paper Special VAT Invoices require tax-control equipment and encrypted reporting. No-offset / invoice matching and anti-fraud risk control are automated through e-invoice data, cargo/value/flow cross-checks, and real-time e-invoice integration.

If export zero-rating or exemption is claimed without complete customs documents, without verifiable foreign-exchange repatriation, or on the basis of false documentation, the STA will claw back the zero-rated treatment, assess output VAT at the applicable standard rate, collect late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the unpaid tax, and impose administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export-tax-rebate filings additionally attract criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, including imprisonment and criminal fines. SAFE may suspend the exporter's trade FX verification status where repatriation deadlines are breached, and tax authorities will re-open prior refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and a system that is substantially convertible on the current account but tightly controlled on the capital account. The RMB can be freely settled and paid for genuine trade in goods and services, advisory fees, dividend distributions, interest, royalties, and ordinary operational expenses, subject to bank-based documentary screening under SAFE regulations. Capital-account transactions such as foreign direct investment, equity injection, intra-group loans, and cross-border restructuring are not freely convertible and require SAFE registration or approval, a designated domestic bank, and a valid foreign-exchange capital account. For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture, profit and dividend remittance to the foreign investor is permitted only after completion of the annual statutory audit, payment or clearance of enterprise income tax and withholding tax, issuance of a tax payment certificate by the State Taxation Administration, and submission of the audited financial statements, a board resolution approving the dividend, and the required SAFE reporting forms. Funds converted from the capital account enter a special RMB settlement account and may be used only for the stated operating purposes in the SAFE application; reinvestment of those RMB funds into other projects or securities is prohibited unless separately approved. Therefore, the treasury must treat the current account as operationally convertible but must plan all capital movements as regulated events with documentary evidence, purpose codes, and prescribed time windows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not currently enforce a fixed mandatory surrender percentage; exporters may retain up to 100% of eligible foreign-currency export proceeds in an onshore current-account foreign-exchange account or may convert any portion to RMB at the market rate. The statutory duty is that goods-export receipts must be repatriated into the onshore banking system or a SAFE-approved offshore account and reported through the cross-border trade monitoring platform, generally no later than 180 days after the related customs export declaration unless an extension is filed and accepted. If SAFE invokes balance-of-payments stress measures, temporary conversion quotas or mandatory settlement ratios may be reintroduced for specific categories of receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Onshore fintech EMIs are not viable as principal treasury or clearing providers in mainland China. Electronic money institutions in China do not enjoy a licensed corporate-banking charter, cannot access CNAPS or SAFE-regulated foreign-exchange settlement, and are legally barred from establishing the statutory RMB basic deposit account and FDI capital account required for a Sino-foreign equity joint venture. Offshore digital EMIs may support ancillary wallet operations and card payments outside China but cannot clear funds into the JV's onshore tax, payroll, or dividend-repatriation pipeline. The JV must maintain all primary liquidity and FX settlement at a licensed domestic tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must wire funds from its own corporate bank account to the JV's designated capital foreign-exchange account at an approved domestic bank using SWIFT MT103. The wire should state the JV name and SAFE FDI registration number, and the remittance should be categorized under the PRC foreign-exchange purpose code for FDI equity contribution. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the payment against SAFE records, credits the capital FX account, and permits the JV to convert the funds into RMB in a special settlement account. A licensed Chinese CPA must then issue a capital verification report. The RMB proceeds may be used only for the JV's stated operating purposes and cannot be used for securities investments, entrusted loans, or speculative FX trading.For outbound trade and service settlements, the JV should use CIPS and domestic RMB clearing for local expenditures, and SWIFT MT103 for ordinary cross-border service fees, SWIFT MT700 commercial letters of credit for goods transactions, and documentary collections for seller-financed trade flows. Every outbound remittance requires the underlying service or sale contract, commercial invoice, SAFE foreign-exchange payment application, beneficial-ownership declaration, and proof of tax deduction or a valid foreign-income tax clearance certificate where withholding tax applies. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the PRC Company Law, the Foreign Investment Law, and implementing regulations, every company, including all Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Ventures, must prepare annual financial reports and have them independently audited by a registered Chinese CPA firm. There is no turnover, asset, headcount, or shareholder-type exemption for foreign-invested enterprises. The audit mandate is unconditional. Additional compulsory audit layers apply to listed companies, public debt issuers, financial institutions, and entities operating under special licensed sectors (e.g., securities, insurance, banking), as prescribed by the CSRC, PBOC, CBIRC, and MOF regulations.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid by a resident enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder, reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong DTA, 5% under the China-Singapore DTA subject to 25% shareholding and holding period conditions, or other treaty rates if applicable; no domestic withholding tax applies to dividends paid to Chinese residents

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual controlled transaction amount of RMB 20 million for tangible goods transfer; RMB 10 million for financial asset transfer; RMB 10 million for intangible property transfer; RMB 40 million for other related party transactions. Master File: RMB 1 billion in annual related party transaction amount or also if the total group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion but below RMB 1 billion in controlled transactions. CbC Report: group consolidated revenue at least RMB 5.5 billion. Thresholds are cumulative for the reporting calendar year

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine via GAAR under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Circular 118/2009. The Economic Substance Requirements are formalised in the absence of a separate ESR statute but the tax authorities may disregard entities lacking business purpose; for joint ventures engaged in international tax structuring and advisory services, the activity must demonstrate staff, premises, and decision-making in China to support a taxable PE; the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis applies to treaty interpretation and to the attribution of profits to a permanent establishment; the State Taxation Administration may allocate profit to China if the CIGA are performed by the Chinese entity even if legal contracts are with a foreign entity

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Criminal)

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law; penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; criminal prosecution under Article 203 of the Criminal Law; evaded tax exceeding RMB 100,000 and constituting more than 10% of the taxable amount attracts imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine, or 3 to 7 years if the amount is especially large

Director personal liability: Directors who issue false tax declarations or instruct falsification of accounts are personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax plus penalties; assets of the director may be attached; travel ban and exit denial are enforced under the Exit-Entry Administration Law if the director has an outstanding tax liability; criminal liability results in direct imprisonment and confiscation of personal property; a criminal conviction disqualifies the director from serving as a manager of any Chinese company for 5 years
Shell Company and Abuse of Tax Treaty

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Article 47 of Enterprise Income Tax Law) to recharacterize the intermediary and impose the full 10% withholding; an adjustment adds back the avoided tax and a penalty of 50% to 3 times the avoided tax; a 10.5% interest surcharge for a special tax investigation may be applied under Measures for Special Tax Adjustments; the intermediary may be ordered to deregister and its perceived dividend treated as taxable income in China

Director personal liability: Directors of the Chinese joint venture who sign the distribution to the tax haven entity without treaty clearance shall be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax; their personal bank accounts, real estate, and equity holdings may be frozen under the Tax Collection Law; travel bans apply to foreign directors with Chinese tax liabilities; if the transfer pricing adjustment leads to an unfiled amended return, criminal negligence charges may be brought under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for failing to declare taxable income
Money Laundering and Anti-Avoidance Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Violation of Anti-Money Laundering Law Articles 16 and 32; administrative fine of RMB 500,000 to RMB 5 million for the company, with penalties against the responsible persons of RMB 50,000 to RMB 500,000; if the concealment of proceeds exceeds RMB 5 million, criminal liability arises under Article 191 of the Criminal Law (money laundering), resulting in imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and a fine; additionally the tax authority imposes a penalty equal to 50% to 5 times the evaded tax measured on the unreported revenue

Director personal liability: Directors who approve fund transfers to tax havens knowing that the underlying transaction is fictitious are treated as accomplices; they are subject to asset forfeiture and restitution to the company for losses; a court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20 of the Company Law if the corporate form is misused to evade tax, making directors personally accountable for all company tax debts and criminal fines; blacklisting by the State Taxation Administration may result a prohibition on exiting China and on starting or investing in any new Chinese company for a mandatory period of 5 years
