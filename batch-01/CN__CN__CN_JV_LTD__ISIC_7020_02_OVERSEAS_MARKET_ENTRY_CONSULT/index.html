Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered address of a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in overseas market entry consultancy. While certain government-backed business parks offer an approved centralized address or address-hosting arrangement for low-risk consultancies, the local tax authority generally rejects a pure commercial mailbox for tax registration and invoice administration because the company must maintain an actual place of business. Commercial banks likewise refuse to open a basic deposit account if no physical office, personnel, lease documentation, or utility bills can be verified. Accordingly, the JV should procure at least a physical serviced office or a verifiable seat in an approved business incubation center within the registered city; a virtual-only address is not a compliant commercial domicile for this industry.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered or paid-up capital is imposed for a consulting-services Sino-Foreign equity joint venture under the current PRC Companies Law or the Foreign Investment Law. The shareholders must determine a subscribed registered capital in the Articles of Association, and the capital must be fully paid within five years from the establishment date unless a shorter term is agreed in the Articles. Cash contributions must be remitted to the Company capital contribution account in RMB or convertible foreign currency, while non-cash contributions must be appraised in accordance with Article 48 of the Companies Law. There is no government escrow account or blocked capital requirement for a management consulting entity; the contribution schedule, proportion and dividend allocation are governed by the Articles of Association. There is also no maximum foreign ownership restriction for consulting activities outside the Foreign Investment Negative List.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); tax filings, registration, and e-invoice clearance are processed through the STA's Electronic Tax Office (e-Tax Bureau) national online portal, integrated with the Golden Tax Project Phase IV system.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (JV) formed in mainland China, the initial tax registration is integrated with commercial incorporation: the JV obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation, and that 18-character code automatically serves as the enterprise's Tax Identification Number (TIN) for CIT, VAT, and all other national taxes. Within 30 days of the business license approval, the JV must activate its taxpayer record in the Electronic Tax Office and file the following: business license, joint venture contract, articles of association, legal representative identity document, registered office lease or ownership certificate, bank account opening license, and accounting/bookkeeping method declaration. For a foreign non-resident entity that does not establish a Chinese legal entity but performs market-entry consultancy in China or derives China-source income, the entity may be required either to register as a non-resident enterprise with the competent tax authority at the place of service performance or to rely on the Chinese payor's withholding registration and payment; if the non-resident entity opens a branch/PE or creates a fixed base in China, it must complete full USCC-based registration like a resident enterprise and file all applicable taxes. All registrations are performed online through the e-Tax Bureau; no separate physical visit is required except where biometric identity verification is mandated by the local tax authority.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not offer US-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The only material status election is VAT taxpayer classification: an entity with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to become a general taxpayer; once taxable sales in any consecutive 12-month period exceed RMB 5,000,000, general taxpayer registration is compulsory. No other entity-level class elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All incorporated enterprises must complete VAT registration upon receipt of the business license. The statutory mandatory general-taxpayer registration threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of taxable sales in any consecutive 12-month period; below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies with simplified levy collection. The output VAT rate applicable to the JV's overseas market entry consulting services is 6% for general taxpayers, while the national standard headline VAT rate for goods is 13% and certain goods/services use 9% or 6% reduced rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies to exported goods where the goods physically leave China and the export is declared to Customs under the export customs procedure; no domestic consumption occurs in China. For cross-border services, including overseas market entry consultancy, 0% VAT or VAT exemption applies if the service is supplied to an overseas purchaser, the recipient is outside China, the service is actually performed for and consumed wholly outside China, the service is not supplied to a China-resident establishment, and the service falls within the STA-approved list of zero-rated/exempt cross-border taxable services. The JV must maintain written evidence that the consultancy advice is provided for the benefit of a foreign client in a foreign market, that delivery is made offshore, and that invoicing and payment are in foreign exchange or in RMB settled by a cross-border bank transfer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) as generated by the General Administration of Customs single-window system
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export and destination outside China
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export contract
  • Export Proceeds Collection/Repatriation Certificate (bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher under SAFE monitoring)
  • Zero-rated export VAT invoice (Special VAT Invoice or dedicated export ordinary invoice)
  • For cross-border consultancy services: service contract, offshore deliverable evidence, and bank wire or Wise confirmation showing remittance from the overseas client

The JV must issue all invoices through the STA's national digital e-invoice infrastructure integrated with the Golden Tax Project Phase IV anti-fraud tax-control system. Each legally valid 'Special VAT Invoice' or 'Ordinary Invoice' receives a unique fiscal invoice code and must be transmitted to the STA platform in real time at the moment of issuance. Qualifying taxpayers are required or permitted to use 'fully digitalized electronic invoices', under which the STA clears and stamps the invoice data using encryption before the invoice can be delivered to the counterparty. All VAT returns, VAT special invoice verification, export-related invoice data, and customs declaration data must be reconciled electronically; unauthorized invoicing software or manual invoice generation is not a substitute for statutory clearance.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) cross-check export declarations, e-invoice data, port manifests, and foreign exchange receipts. If the zero-rating documentation bundle is incomplete or the FX repatriation deadline is missed, the claimed 0% rate is disallowed and the JV must output VAT at the statutory rate (6% for consultancy services) plus late-payment interest. If the zero-rating or export refund claim is based on false or falsified documentation, the tax authority will claw back the full refunded or exempted VAT and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious cases constitute the criminal offense of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law, exposing controlling persons to imprisonment and confiscation of property, as well as downgrading of the taxpayer's tax credit rating and enhanced exit control supervision.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For current account items, including cross-border service fees, consulting income, and trade receivables, China has accepted Article VIII obligations and permits conversion of foreign currency to Renminbi at market rates provided that the transaction has a real trade or service background and is supported by authentic documentation. The capital account, however, remains highly compartmentalised and subject to administrative licensing. For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture, equity capital injections must be registered with SAFE under the FDI Income and Detail Form regime before the bank can credit or convert funds. Profit repatriation is permitted annually after audit and tax clearance, but requires the submission of an earnings disposition resolution, a certified audit report, and proof that the capital account has been fully verified. In the absence of these documents, the remittance will be QFII-blocked or treated as an unverified capital flow. Moreover, the bank will impose a corporate anti-avoidance review, requiring the consulting company to demonstrate a real headcount and physical office before treating its foreign-currency receipts as current-account revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE rules, all current-account export and service-trade receipts must be repatriated into China within 180 days of receipt by the offshore agent, and must be deposited into a foreign exchange settlement account or converted into CNY. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio to CNY for service-trade receipts, but banks apply a demand-based conversion principle; they may hold a foreign currency position only to the extent of verified future FX commitments. If proceeds remain unconverted for more than 60 days beyond settlement, the bank may require a written explanation and an updated schedule of forecast FX needs.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Wise B2B and other offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised to provide banking services to onshore Chinese entities. The PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border settlement and FX conversion to occur through licensed domestic banks within the state-managed payment infrastructure. Foreign EMIs without a China payment licence cannot access CNAPS or CIPS settlement, and any attempt to substitute an EMI for a domestic bank account would violate Circular 235 (2019) and the Administrative Measures for Foreign Exchange Market. Such EMIs can only serve ancillary purposes for the entity's outbound remittances from China if they partner with a licensed Chinese bank, but the primary operating account must remain with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections (registered USD/CNY equity) must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 from the offshore shareholder's bank to the JV's designated capital account at a licensed domestic bank, using purpose code C-1-1 for Foreign Direct Investment Equity Injection. The transfer must be accompanied by the SAFE FDI Compliance Registration and the bank's Foreign Exchange Capital Account Registration certificate. After the bank receives the funds, a capital verification report must be issued by a PRC CPA firm within 30 days. RMB conversion of capital for operational use is allowed up to the registered capital amount, but not for entrusted loans, prepayments to unrelated third parties, or real estate projects without additional SAFE approval. The bank will reject any credit from third parties not listed in the registered shareholder roster.For a market-entry consultancy (ISIC 7020), typical outbound disbursements are vendor payments, employee salaries, regulatory filing fees, and intra-group management fees. The preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers against an invoice approved by the tax authority. Commercial letters of credit (L/C) and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are permissible but are more relevant to physical goods trade; pure services are usually settled through MT103 with a Service Trade Foreign Exchange Payment Application and a genuine contract. For intra-group service fees, the bank requires a transfer-pricing report, an arm's-length certificate under SAT Circular 16, and proof that the expense is not a disguised dividend distribution.Onshore CNY/USD forward contracts for up to one year, issued by a licensed bank, with a genuine current-account exposure such as expected consulting fee receivables and payable invoices, backed by a margin deposit or authorised credit line.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for long-term foreign-currency debt or capital account liabilities, subject to SAFE registration, a verified underlying exposure, and a tenancy between the notional amount and the audited obligation.Onshore FX swaps for temporary liquidity management between CNY and a convertible foreign currency, with a strict two-day-to-six-month maturity and a fully documented commercial bilateral transaction.No onshore access to offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) or quanto-style derivatives for domestic entities; any NDF transaction would require a special SAFE pilot licence and is not viable for an ISIC 7020 consultancy holding only service trade exposures.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Wise B2B and other offshore electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not authorised to provide banking services to onshore Chinese entities. The PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border settlement and FX conversion to occur through licensed domestic banks within the state-managed payment infrastructure. Foreign EMIs without a China payment licence cannot access CNAPS or CIPS settlement, and any attempt to substitute an EMI for a domestic bank account would violate Circular 235 (2019) and the Administrative Measures for Foreign Exchange Market. Such EMIs can only serve ancillary purposes for the entity's outbound remittances from China if they partner with a licensed Chinese bank, but the primary operating account must remain with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY thresholds: contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report is required for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more in the preceding year.

Economic substance scope: China does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations in the same statutory form, but Chinese tax law embeds a substance-over-form and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Article 47 and related circulars. For the foreign market-entry consultancy JV, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as personnel decision-making, client contract execution, risk assumption, invoice issuance, and strategic management must be genuinely performed in China. A shell company without economic substance will be denied treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as tax fraud or treaty abuse.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: confiscate illegal gains, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax, and recover unpaid tax and surcharge. PRC Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion applies where the evaded amount is RMB 50,000 or more and exceeds 10% of the tax payable; punishments range from up to 3 years imprisonment for relatively large evasion to 3-7 years for huge evasion plus fines.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 23 where a director or controlling shareholder abusing corporate personality causes tax evasion; directors are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of evaded tax, interest and penalties. Personal travel bans and exit restrictions are imposed by the tax authority under the Exit-Entry Administration Law when material tax is due. Directors face criminal imprisonment and personal asset confiscation for organizing offshore accounts or false invoicing.
Artificial Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), SAT Announcement 2012 No. 30 and 2018 No. 9 on beneficial ownership; denial of treaty benefits and recapture of the under-withheld tax at the 10% statutory rate, plus late-payment surcharge, interest, and potential administrative penalty. The scheme may be recharacterized as dividend distribution or taxable income in China.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners who erode the separate corporate form to avoid taxation are personally liable under civil veil-piercing for unpaid withholding taxes and surcharges; they are blacklisted with exit bans and travel restrictions. Criminal liability may arise under Criminal Law Article 191 (money laundering) or Article 201 (tax evasion) where the conduit arrangement involves fraudulent concealment, with imprisonment up to 7 years and asset restitution.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 empowers adjustment to the arm's-length result; CIT Law Article 48 and Tax Collection Administration Law impose interest on the tax underpaid at the benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points. Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation may result in deemed assessment, penalties under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 44 and 62, and a special tax adjustment look-back of up to 10 years under Article 86.

Director personal liability: If directors intentionally engineer transaction prices, false contracts, or sham cost-sharing arrangements to strip Chinese profits, the tax authority can pursue personal liability by lifting the veil; unpaid adjusted tax, interest and penalties trigger blacklisting and an exit ban. Directors who create fictitious cross-border service arrangements may be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years and personal civil restitution for the full tax leakage.
