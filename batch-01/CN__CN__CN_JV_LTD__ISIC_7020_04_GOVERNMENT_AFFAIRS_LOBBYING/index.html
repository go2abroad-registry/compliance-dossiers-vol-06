Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture limited liability company; registered capital is determined freely by the shareholders in the Articles of Association and may be paid in installments within the period specified in the articles, not exceeding the statutory extension period for foreign-invested enterprises unless approved. No blocked escrow capital account is mandated by the Company Law; however, foreign currency capital contributions must be remitted through a designated bank, credited to a foreign exchange capital account, and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the capital account rules.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to industrial or civil-defense-impacting facilities. The registered office must nevertheless maintain a valid building-wide Fire Safety Inspection Certificate issued or accepted by the local Fire and Rescue Division, and the company must observe the Fire Protection Law by keeping emergency exits unblocked and installing portable extinguishers. If the office is within a commercial building, the landlord's fire certificate generally suffices; if the company leases a stand-alone unit exceeding the applicable local threshold, obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Recordation or Opinion from the Fire and Rescue Division. For workplace safety, the employer must complete occupational disease prevention and workplace hazard notifications under the Work Safety Law if employees are exposed to any risks, but for ordinary advisory premises only a general employer safety management policy is required.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture limited liability company; registered capital is determined freely by the shareholders in the Articles of Association and may be paid in installments within the period specified in the articles, not exceeding the statutory extension period for foreign-invested enterprises unless approved. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the local in-charge tax office having jurisdiction over the registered address of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; the online service channel is the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform operated by the STA for taxpayer registration, electronic filing, invoice issuance, and export tax refund applications.

E-government filing portal

A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (EJV) first obtains its legal personality by registering with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receiving a business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). This USCC is the sole statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all Chinese tax purposes, including Corporate Income Tax, VAT, consumption tax, stamp duty, and withholding obligations. Within thirty days after issuance of the business license, the EJV must complete tax registration on the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform, filing the taxpayer registration information record, the charter documents, the foreign partner s investment approval or filing evidence, the board resolution appointing the legal representative and financial officer, the opening bank account information, and the registered address proof. The in-charge tax authority then confirms the fiscal status under which the USCC is activated as the TIN/VAT identity. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no Chinese establishment but derives China-source income or is required to be the withholding agent or taxpayer of record for China-sourced taxable services, the foreign enterprise must apply for a Non-resident Enterprise Tax Registration through the same electronic platform or through its Chinese withholding agent, submitting the foreign enterprise s home-country registration certificate, articles of association, the contract generating China-source income, the appointment of a tax representative or withholding agent, and the foreign bank account details. The tax authority issues a non-resident taxpayer identification number that is used only for the specific China tax file; it does not convert the foreign entity into a Chinese VAT taxpayer unless a taxable establishment or fixed base is found. For the EJV itself, no separate VAT ID is issued because the USCC is used on all VAT invoices, customs declarations, bank receipts, and tax returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a limited liability Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; the EJV is unconditionally classified as a Chinese resident enterprise subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income. There are no hybrid mismatch elections or fiscal transparency elections available under China s general tax law. The only operational status elections concern VAT classification as a small-scale taxpayer versus a general VAT taxpayer and certain simplified methods of tax calculation for designated services, but these do not change the fundamental entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual VAT taxable sales for mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status or is required by the tax authority to register. For foreign non-resident suppliers of taxable services to Chinese purchasers, the Chinese purchaser must self-assess and withhold VAT with no threshold-based registration shelter.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, export zero-rating is available only when the goods are physically exported from the Chinese customs territory and the transaction is evidenced by a valid export customs declaration, a genuine sale between a Chinese seller and an overseas buyer, an export contract, and repatriation of the export proceeds within the prescribed period. For services, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available only where the service falls within the catalogue of cross-border taxable services designated by the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration under the VAT reform rules, especially the cross-border services provisions in Caishui [2016] No. 36 and supplementary STA announcements, and where the recipient is located outside China and the service is wholly consumed outside China. The specific activity of government affairs lobbying and China regulatory advisory under ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541820 is not automatically classified as zero-rated; the EJV must document that the beneficiary is a foreign person, that no Chinese resident receives the benefit of the analysis, that the service is performed in connection with the foreign principal s overseas decision-making, and that the service is expressly included in the applicable offshore service catalogue. If these conditions are not met, the service is treated as standard-rated 6% VAT supply. For substantive export claims, the EJV must also file the ordinary export tax refund declaration through the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform to substantiate a 0% VAT treatment and claim input VAT refunds where applicable.

  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or rail/road consignment note evidencing actual physical departure from the People s Republic of China

China has implemented the national Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under the STA s Golden Tax Phase IV program. VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, ordinary VAT invoices, and export rebate vouchers, must be issued through the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform. The issuance process is real-time and platform-controlled: the STA assigns unique invoice numbers, applies the official anti-fraud digital stamp, records the invoice metadata in the taxpayer s digital tax account, and transmits the invoice data to the purchaser s electronic invoice pool at the moment of issue. Traditional fiscal tax-control devices remain accepted for legacy workflow, but the underlying invoice data must be transmitted electronically. There is no physical clearance stamp; the issuance platform itself performs the fiscal clearance and anti-fraud verification. The EJV is required to keep all FDEI records, report red-letter and void invoices electronically, and ensure that the six-digit VAT service category code selected for the sale matches the actual lobbying and policy advisory services under ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541820.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, State Administration of Foreign Exchange, and banking information systems continuously cross-match export declarations, VAT invoice flows, bill-of-lading databases, cross-border payment records, and enterprise bank statements. Where the export or offshore service is found not to have been genuinely supplied, not to have left China, to have been re-imported, to lack foreign exchange receipt within the 180-day statutory window, or to be a lobbying or advisory service that should have been treated as a domestic taxable supply, the tax authority will revoke the zero-rating retrospectively, assess VAT at the applicable standard rate on the transaction, impose late payment surcharge from the date the tax would otherwise have been due, and apply the tax collection penalty regime of 50% up to five times the underpaid tax for fraud or misrepresentation. In egregious cases the export tax refund qualification may be suspended or annulled, the taxpayer may be placed on the tax-related untrustworthy list, and the responsible legal representative and financial officer may face personal liability. If the arrangement is prosecuted as fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People s Republic of China, the penalties include confiscation of illegal gains, fines, and imprisonment for up to life imprisonment for the responsible persons.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls and a convertible current account. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (JV) engaged in government affairs lobbying and cross-border trade policy advisory, the operational revenue from domestic retainers is in Renminbi (CNY) and fully convertible for current account transactions under the Foreign Exchange Administration Rules. However, the JV's foreign shareholder dividends, profit repatriation, and liquidation proceeds are treated as capital account transactions and require prior SAFE approval, a verified capital account, and audited financial statements confirming after-tax profits. The JV must surrender export proceeds from any cross-border advisory services into CNY within the statutory timeline unless an offshore account exemption is granted. Compulsory conversion applies to export proceeds under the Uniform Rules for Export Proceeds Collection, requiring full conversion into CNY within 30 days of settlement in the designated domestic bank account. Capital injections from the foreign shareholder require certification by a Chinese certified public accountant and must be routed through a capital escrow account at a domestic bank with SAFE-registered, non-deliverable pre-approval. Profit remittances are subject to a 10% withholding tax on dividends, and the JV must demonstrate compliance with tax payment certificates, SAFE audit of the capital verification report, and the absence of any outstanding foreign debt. Practical hurdles include significant documentation burdens, delays in SAFE approval for bonus remittances, and the requirement to source CNY from onshore operations before any conversion application can be entertained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 28 and the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration, all export proceeds from cross-border advisory services or trade-related lobbying contracts must be repatriated to China within 90 days of the actual shipment or provision of service. Once credited to the JV's domestic current account in foreign currency, the JV must surrender the foreign exchange to a designated bank within 30 days, converting 100% of the receipts into CNY. Retained foreign currency in the current account is permitted only up to a SAFE-approved quota, and any excess must be converted or held in a foreign exchange settlement account with a documented holding period not exceeding 30 days.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are legally barred from serving as the primary transactional account for a Chinese onshore entity. The PBOC and SAFE mandate that all cross-border receipts and payments must settle through a domestic licensed bank with a valid Foreign Exchange Business Permit. EMIs cannot provide capital account services, FX surrender capabilities, or export proceeds verification, and any remittance through an EMI would trigger a false declaration and a network suspension by the Clearing Center. Feasibility is therefore 'Unviable for primary banking', although EMIs may be used as supplementary invoicing tools if the JV obtains a special SAFE offshore account authorization; in practice, such authorization is rarely granted. The JV must maintain its entire treasury in a domestic tier-1 bank to ensure compliance with FX regulations and clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder remits equity investment in USD or EUR via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's Capital Escrow Account (资本账户) at a designated Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must include the SAFE-approved Foreign Investment Code, the foreign direct investment (FDI) capital injection purpose code '61', and the reference number of the foreign exchange registration certificate. Within 15 days of receipt, the bank and the JV must complete capital verification through a Chinese CPA who issues an audited capital verification report. The foreign exchange proceeds may then be converted into CNY for operational use after filing with SAFE; urgent outbound remittances of the injected capital are expressly prohibited. If the JV wishes to hold working capital in a foreign currency, the balance must remain within the SAFE-set maximum and cannot exceed 5% of the verified capital unless prior approval is obtained for an offshore foreign currency account.All outbound payments for trade policy consulting, foreign market research, and retainer disbursements must be settled via SWIFT MT103 with the correct SAFE purpose code for cross-border services (code 225 for consulting and policy advice). For settlement of goods-related lobbying support, the JV may use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (LC) only when it purchases physical reference materials; otherwise, the commercial LC route is disintermediated. Documentary collections are permissible but discouraged for services because of their lack of payment certainty. The bank will require the JV to submit the underlying contract, an invoice, a written explanation of the transaction chain, and electronic records for the bank's screening against sanctions lists. Any offshore NDF would be deemed speculative and illegal, exposing the JV to regulatory penalties.Cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible for hedging capital account debt only if the JV has an underlying foreign currency loan; the notional amount cannot exceed the outstanding debt principal, and the swap must be registered with the FX registration system.Options and exotic derivatives are strictly prohibited unless separately approved by SAFE. The JV can hedge its net foreign-currency receivable positions with zero-cost collar strategies, but the maximum loss must be capped at 2% of the notional amount.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex are legally barred from serving as the primary transactional account for a Chinese onshore entity. The PBOC and SAFE mandate that all cross-border receipts and payments must settle through a domestic licensed bank with a valid Foreign Exchange Business Permit. EMIs cannot provide capital account services, FX surrender capabilities, or export proceeds verification, and any remittance through an EMI would trigger a false declaration and a network suspension by the Clearing Center. Feasibility is therefore 'Unviable for primary banking', although EMIs may be used as supplementary invoicing tools if the JV obtains a special SAFE offshore account authorization; in practice, such authorization is rarely granted. The JV must maintain its entire treasury in a domestic tier-1 bank to ensure compliance with FX regulations and clearance.

Turnover: Under PRC Company Law, every Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, being a limited liability company, is unconditionally required to prepare annual financial statements and have them independently audited by an accounting firm licensed by the Ministry of Finance; there is no balance-sheet or turnover exemption for ordinary JV LLCs. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million for services, intangible property, or financial assets and RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; Master File is generally required for groups with annual consolidated turnover above RMB 1 billion or related-party transaction volumes above the SAT threshold.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate separate Economic Substance Regulations, but the tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, beneficial ownership rules, and the OECD-derived functions-assets-risks analysis. For a Sino-Foreign JV engaged in lobbying and government affairs advisory, economic substance must be evidenced by real staff, office premises, decision-making authority, and Core Income-Generating Activities performed in China; a shell or pass-through entity will be recharacterized, denied treaty relief, and taxed on the actual beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Expense Deductions

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection, a short-payment arising from false or fraudulent filing attracts a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, late payment interest, and recovery of the underpaid tax; criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law is punishable by fines and imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: If directors knowingly sign false returns or direct the fabrication of cross-border invoices, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for civil restitution of underpaid tax; directors face asset freezing, travel bans and blacklisting, disqualification from holding office, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Shell Company Abuse and General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the State Taxation Administration's anti-avoidance measures, the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction, disregard the intermediary entity, adjust taxable income, impose interest, and levy a 50% to 500% penalty for underpayment; treaty benefits may be denied and the withholding tax recalculated at 10%.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the shell structure or signed transaction documents may be treated as responsible parties; the veil may be pierced under the Company Law, exposing directors to personal restitution, liability for company tax debts, travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal liability if the conduit arrangement constitutes fraudulent evasion.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unlicensed Lobbying Payments

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and its implementing regulations impose monetary fines, confiscation of illegal gains, license suspension or revocation, and orders to correct the deficiency; under Article 191 of the Criminal Law, money laundering is punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil asset restitution, disgorgement of illicit gains, and administrative removal; they face travel bans and blacklisting, cannot leave China while investigations are pending, and may be convicted and imprisoned for money laundering if they knew or should have known the funds were derived from illegitimate activity.
