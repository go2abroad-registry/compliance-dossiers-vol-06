Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through local STA offices and the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau electronic tax return, invoice, and registration portal

E-government filing portal

A new China incorporated Sino-foreign equity JV automatically receives a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on its business license from the Administration for Market Regulation; this USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, payroll and electronic invoice filing. The JV must complete the national tax information data report within 30 days of business license issuance; under the multi-certificate integration system the market regulator shares the USCC with the STA and the tax registration is activated in the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau. VAT registration and taxpayer type selection (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) are completed electronically on the same platform; for general VAT taxpayer status the JV must have a fixed place of business, adequate accounting system and issue and digitally record VAT invoices through the VAT invoice management system. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment does not register for VAT solely because it holds shares in the JV; where it receives China-source payments, the JV as payer is the withholding agent and the non-resident may submit a simplified tax registration and identification request under the double tax treaty procedure, appoint a tax representative and provide legalised/certified corporate documents to obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number if it needs to claim treaty benefits or file returns. The non-resident must also provide its home jurisdiction tax identification number and corresponding proof of residency certificate when treaty relief is applied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in Chinese tax law. The JV is taxed at entity level as a China resident enterprise on worldwide income under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law. A non-resident enterprise can be treated as a China resident enterprise if its actual place of effective management is in China; this is a fact-driven determination and not a check-the-box election. For VAT, a taxpayer with annual taxable sales below the RMB 5 million small-scale threshold may elect General VAT taxpayer status if it has sound accounting records; small-scale taxpayers are otherwise entitled to use the simplified VAT collection rate. No other statutory entity classification elections apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China imposes mandatory tax registration on all legal entities within 30 days of license issue. For VAT, annual taxable turnover above RMB 5 million compels the enterprise to register as a General VAT taxpayer; below RMB 5 million, the enterprise is normally a small-scale taxpayer but can voluntarily register as a General VAT taxpayer if qualifying. Monthly sales of small-scale taxpayers not exceeding RMB 100,000 are temporarily exempt from VAT as announced by STA policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods from China is VAT zero-rated, provided the exporter is registered in China, the goods actually leave the customs territory, the exporter files the customs export declaration, the title and revenue are documented under an export contract, and the sales proceeds are declared and collected through Chinese designated foreign exchange banks and repatriated. Qualifying offshore services and cross-border services expressly listed in the VAT exemption and zero-rating catalogue can be VAT zero-rated or exempt if the recipient is an overseas or non-resident entity and the services are rendered wholly outside China. M&A advisory services to non-resident clients that do not fall within a listed cross-border service category are taxable at the 6% modern services VAT rate unless a specific STA determination or restrictive tax circular authorises zero rating or exemption.

  • Commercial invoice and packing list

China has mandatory electronic VAT invoice issuance. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are created, transmitted, verified and cleared on the STA national electronic invoice platform; the same platform issues unique invoice codes and anti-counterfeit verification stamps. Taxpayers must report invoice data to the STA and any voided or failed invoice is automatically monitored. Paper special VAT invoices, where permitted for transitional transactions, remain subject to the STA VAT invoice tax-control system and periodic invoice verification and clearance. Digital fiscal stamping is not a separate cash-register requirement, but VAT invoices must carry statutory invoice codes and the national electronic invoice platform real-time verification record.

The STA, General Administration of Customs and State Administration of Foreign Exchange exchange electronic data to verify every export rebate and zero-rate claim. If the declared export is found false, unperformed, non-exported, or foreign exchange proceeds have not been repatriated within the prescribed period without an approved extension, the VAT zero-rating is revoked, input tax credits must be reversed, the refunded VAT plus interest will be clawed back from future rebates or demand for payment, and administrative fines of 50% to five times the tax amount defrauded may be imposed under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent export tax refunds constitute the criminal offence of obtaining export tax rebates by deceptive means under PRC Criminal Law, with penalties escalating from fines to imprisonment and, for very large amounts, up to life imprisonment; the responsible persons and the entity may also be barred from export rebate status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed float for the renminbi under a structured capital account control framework. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, interest, dividends, and legitimate advisory fees, are generally convertible on a real-transaction basis through designated foreign exchange banks; however, every payment must be supported by underlying transaction documentation, tax certificates, and SAFE reporting codes. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity capital contributions into a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, require SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration, an onshore capital account opened at a domestic bank, and subsequent capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is permitted after payment of corporate income tax, dividend withholding tax, and submission of audited financial statements, tax clearance forms, and a resolution of the board of directors. Funds received as equity are subject to authenticity and use constraints: they cannot be borrowed against, used for unapproved equity investments, or routed to prohibited sectors. The practical effect is that the renminbi is convertible for genuine business needs but not freely convertible for speculative or balance-sheet arbitrage transactions. Non-resident investors must navigate the SAFE registration mechanism, the credit reporting system, and the anti-money laundering screening procedures of the receiving bank. Compliance failures can trigger suspension of foreign exchange accounts, fines, and inability to remit dividends abroad. Therefore, treasury structuring must maintain a strict documentary trail from board resolutions, ownership charters, transaction agreements, invoices, tax receipts, and SAFE filings to ensure credible clearance at every settlement point.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exports of goods and services from China require full and timely repatriation of foreign currency proceeds into a domestic account at a designated foreign exchange bank; the statutory benchmark for goods exports is repatriation within 180 days from the Customs declaration date unless SAFE approves an extension. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio into renminbi; qualified enterprises may retain 100% of eligible export proceeds in foreign currency accounts and are free to convert voluntarily, provided the transactions are genuine and documentary evidence is retained.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting under the supervision of the People's Bank of China (PBC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not viable as primary banking or treasury rails for a mainland Chinese JV. Foreign digital EMIs generally lack a Chinese banking license, cannot hold SAFE-regulated foreign exchange accounts, and are legally barred from offering local renminbi clearing or capital account services to resident entities. Such EMIs may only provide auxiliary payment facilitation in offshore corridors where no mainland regulatory license exists; they are unsuitable for capital escrow, export proceeds repatriation, or statutory dividend remittance. The onboarding blueprint should therefore rely exclusively on domestic tier-1 commercial banks with full SAFE delegated foreign exchange administration authority.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed through the SAFE FDI registration process before remittance. The foreign shareholder instructs its overseas bank to wire the contribution to the JV's onshore capital account at a designated domestic bank, using SWIFT MT103 with the external payment reference clearly linked to the SAFE FDI registration certificate. The remittance purpose code should be classified under direct investment, specifically 622011 for foreign direct investment capital injection into a Chinese enterprise. The receiving bank must perform authenticity verification, record the inbound remittance in the foreign exchange monitoring system, and then notify the JV. Immediately upon receipt, the enterprise must engage a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report, confirming the funds have been fully received and earmarked as paid-in registered capital. The capital account proceeds are subject to a negative list: they must be used within the approved business scope, cannot be used for entrusted loans or repayment of unrelated domestic loans, and cannot be deployed for prohibited sectors. Any subsequent conversion into renminbi for operating expenses must be made through the settlement bank with supporting documents such as purchase agreements, payroll schedules, rent contracts, and service invoices.Outbound disbursements for trade settlement and advisory success fees should be executed through a licensed domestic bank under the SAFE proposed-payment filing regime. For cross-border consulting fees, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 wire transfer sourced from the JV's operational CNY account after currency conversion or directly from a foreign exchange account, supported by a service agreement, customs or tax declaration, and an invoice. Where the M&A boutique is settling binding transaction deposits or escrow amounts on behalf of clients, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) or documentary collection may be used if the underlying transaction involves goods or specified commercial deliverables; otherwise, clean remittance is preferred. The treasury must allocate cash between onshore CNY and foreign exchange accounts to ensure daily liquidity, using the bank's internet settlement platform for just-in-time conversion. All outbound wire instructions must carry the appropriate SAFE transaction purpose code, properly identify the beneficiary bank with SWIFT BIC, and be supported by the underlying economic contract. The onboarding blueprint should therefore rely exclusively on domestic tier-1 commercial banks with full SAFE delegated foreign exchange administration authority.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party tangible-asset transfers exceeding RMB 400 million, intangible-property transfers exceeding RMB 200 million, financial-asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million, financing transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, and other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 100 million trigger mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation; a Master File is required where annual related-party transaction volume exceeds RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law, the general anti-avoidance rule, the special tax adjustment rules, and the beneficial-ownership doctrine require the JV to demonstrate real economic substance and performance of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China. For an M&A advisory boutique, CIGA include deal sourcing, negotiation, due-diligence management, strategic advisory, execution, and post-closing integration services. If the JV merely holds contracts and invoices while the foreign parent performs all decision-making and substantive work, the tax authority will reallocate income to the foreign parent, deny treaty relief on outbound payments, impose deemed-dividend treatment, and may apply CFC rules to the JV's profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Thin Capitalization and Excessive Related-Party Interest Deduction

Statutory sanction: Corporate Income Tax Law Article 46, Finance and Taxation [2008] No. 121, and Tax Collection Law Article 63; the disallowed interest is added back to taxable income, additional tax is imposed, a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge accrues, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax is assessed.

Director personal liability: If the directors caused the JV to use the corporate form to conceal loan abuse or evade tax, Chinese courts may pierce the corporate veil under the Company Law and hold directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax debt. Directors may be subject to personal asset seizure, blacklisting on the major-tax-violation public list, restrictions on leaving China, disqualification from serving as directors, and, where the conduct constitutes criminal tax evasion, imprisonment of up to seven years.
False Invoicing and Tax Fraud in Advisory Fees

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Articles 205 and 208, and Tax Collection Law Article 63; issuers and purchasers of false VAT invoices face confiscation of illegal gains, fines of up to five times the illegal amount, fixed-term imprisonment of three to ten years, and up to life imprisonment in particularly serious cases; the tax authority separately imposes fines of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and imposes blacklisting measures.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve, sign, or participate in false invoicing schemes are personally criminally liable and may be sentenced to imprisonment and fines. The corporate veil is pierced where personal and corporate bank accounts are mixed or where the company is used as an instrument of fraud. Directors face civil restitution to the tax authority, loss of professional regulatory licenses, international travel bans, and freezing of personal assets in tax enforcement proceedings.
Shell Company Treaty Shopping and AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Corporate Income Tax Law Article 47 (GAAR), State Taxation Administration Public Notice [2015] No. 7, the Tax Collection Law, and PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law; the tax authority may recharacterize the entire structure, deny DTA benefits, deem the income to be taxable Chinese-source income, impose a 10% withholding tax, and levy penalties of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax plus daily surcharges; AML violations carry administrative fines and criminal prosecution for intentional money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly maintain the shell structure and direct cross-border payments through conduit entities can be blacklisted under the major-tax-violation publication regime, denied exit permits, ordered to personally restore the diverted tax amounts, and held civilly liable to creditors and the tax authority. Criminal exposure includes imprisonment of five to ten years for money laundering, imprisonment for tax evasion, and personal confiscation of proceeds if the JV veil is pierced as a fraudulent vehicle.
