Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for ordinary Sino-foreign equity joint ventures. Under the 2023 PRC Company Law, shareholders must subscribe to the registered capital stated in the Articles of Association and must pay their subscribed contributions in full within five years from establishment, unless the Articles shorten the period. For the CMA inspection/testing licence, the entity must demonstrate independent legal personality, professional staff, test equipment, a quality management system, and a capital base proportionate to the prospective inspection/testing liability; no nationally uniform numeric minimum is imposed.

Funding & unlock path: For office-only pre-shipment inspection QA agency, the company must obtain Fire Safety Filing/Record for the leased office, maintain evacuation routes and fire-fighting equipment, and register safe production compliance under the PRC Work Safety Law; no industrial Civil Defense fire permit is required because the agency itself holds no factory/warehouse operating permit. If the agency installs a testing laboratory, the local fire-rescue authority must approve the fit-out through fire acceptance inspection, and an occupational hazard factors monitoring report for chemical/equipment risk must be filed, including structural load certificates for heavy testing machinery.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, including its online electronic tax portal known as the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax / New Electronic Tax Bureau) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn/

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in China must first obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (NECIPS). Upon issuance, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically serves as the primary taxpayer identification number for tax purposes. The JV must then complete tax registration within 30 days of the business license issue date at the competent in-charge tax authority of the State Taxation Administration (STA), by filing the 'Tax Registration Form for Taxpayers' (Shenshui Dengji Biao) through the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform. The tax authority will validate the taxpayer identity, assign a local taxpayer identification number, activate the electronic tax account, and issue the digital tax registration certificate. The entity must also register for VAT via the same portal, selecting the desired invoicing system, and obtain the electronic fiscal seal and tax UKey / VAT invoice management system credentials for issuing electronic special VAT invoices and general VAT invoices. Where the entity is a contractual joint venture with non-resident partners, each partner is separately identifiable but the JV itself remains a taxpayer for VAT and corporate income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general turnover threshold for VAT registration for companies; all entities are subject to VAT registration upon establishment. Small-scale taxpayer threshold for simplified method is annual taxable turnover of 5,000,000 CNY or below, with a simplified collection rate of 3% (with certain reductions).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of goods by a Chinese VAT-registered seller is eligible for zero-rating at 0% under the 'exemption and refund' (main duty) method, on condition that the goods physically leave the customs territory of mainland China, the sale is made to a non-resident overseas buyer, the export sales are duly declared to customs, and the corresponding input VAT on the export procurement is refunded. Export of offshore-consumed services delivered to overseas entities, such as inspection, testing, and certification services for overseas clients performed on goods intended for export, can also qualify for zero-rating under the zero-rated cross-border service catalogue, provided the services are actually consumed outside China and the receipts are in foreign currency. For the on-site factory pre-shipment quality control and testing business, the VAT treatment depends on whether the service is classified as ancillary to export goods or as a separate cross-border service; when performed within China on goods destined for export, the service may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt with refund, if the service is rendered to a Chinese exporter and the exporter obtains the required export customs declaration and foreign exchange receipt evidence. Where a foreign overseas client directly contracts with the JV and controls the service, the JV may claim zero-rating by proving the service is rendered to an overseas entity and the associated consideration is received in foreign exchange from abroad.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) / Export Customs Declaration Form issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC
  • Bill of Lading (Sea Waybill or Airway Bill) evidencing physical departure from a mainland Chinese customs port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Foreign Exchange Income Verification (reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange SAFE through the Digitally-Controlled Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation System, or bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation)
  • Export VAT Invoice or special electronic fiscal invoice issued through the e-invoice platform
  • Export contract and sales agreement showing the overseas customer and agreed terms
  • Logistics and packing evidence, including customs-observed inspection reports and container tracking records

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice system under the Fully Digitized Electronic Invoices (Shu Dian Piao / Fully Digitalized E-invoice) reform piloted by the State Taxation Administration. Enterprises must issue VAT revenue via the electronic VAT invoice system using the Tax UKey device or the national electronic invoice service platform. Special VAT invoices require real-time fiscal encryption, digital stamping, and transmission to the STA's Golden Tax (Jin San) system, with automated data comparison and anti-fraud anomaly alerts. As of the fully digitalized e-invoice expansion, taxpayers must use the national unified e-invoice service platform for invoice issuance, and the invoice data is recursively checked against VAT returns.

Where a taxable entity claims export VAT zero-rating or refund without fully satisfying the customs declaration, actual removal from customs territory, foreign exchange receipt within 180 days (unless extended by application to the tax authority), or documentary evidence requirements, the STA may reverse the zero-rating, impose an additional VAT charge with late payment interest, recover the unwarranted output tax refund, and impose penalties ranging from 50% to 5 times the amount of false VAT refund obtained under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law in cases of fraudulent export refunds. Export tax fraud can also lead to criminal prosecution, revocation of export tax refund qualification, inclusion in tax dishonesty blacklists, and HMR customs sanctions. For cross-border service zero-rating, clawback is triggered if it is later found that the counterparty was a domestic entity, the services were consumed within China, the foreign exchange receipt is not properly confirmed, or the e-invoicing chain is inconsistent, with full reversion to taxable VAT at the standard rate plus surcharges and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed-floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertibility strictly differentiated between current-account and capital-account transactions. RMB is generally convertible on current-account items such as trade in goods, services, and related remittances, subject to SAFE bank verification of underlying contracts, invoices, and tax payment certificates. The capital account remains heavily regulated: foreign direct investment into a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must be admitted through the FDI foreign-exchange registration system; injected capital must be recorded on the foreign exchange capital account, settled into RMB on a real-purpose basis, and disbursed only within the JV's business scope. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is allowed via the current-account framework but requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the tax authority, and verified bank remittance using the appropriate purpose code. Surrender of export/service proceeds is not mandatory; however, all foreign exchange earnings must be repatriated, retained in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or settled into RMB, and be supported by trade-verification data. At the planning stage, the JV must align capital injection, settlement, and profit remittance with SAFE filings to avoid fines or freezing of accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory physical surrender percentage exists under current PRC rules; export and service trade FX receipts must be channeled to a domestic current-account foreign-exchange settlement account controlled by the JV; receipt must be matched to the electronic trade verification declaration and the relevant SAFE business code, usually within 90 days of the shipment or service completion date; the bank will allow conversion or payment only after seeing the contract, customs declaration for goods, invoice, and completion certificate for inspection services. Surplus FX can be retained or converted at the bank's spot rate, but cannot remain offshore.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. PBOC licensing rules do not treat Wise or other fintech EMIs as banks; they cannot open corporate basic deposit accounts, hold treasury deposits, or support capital-account injections. Cross-border payment institutions may process remittances only through licensed domestic bank partners and are unsuitable for LC operations, SAFE capital registration, FX settlement, or tax payments. At most, Wise could be an ancillary low-value collection channel for overseas client inspection fees through a partner bank, but the JV must execute all statutory banking, FX, and liquidity flows through a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must wire the capital contribution to the JV's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank after the JV completes FDI foreign-exchange registration through the national cross-border FX management system. Each inbound remittance must use a compliant payment purpose code and include the SAFE business number in the reference field. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the remitter matches the registered foreign shareholder, debits the incoming funds, and permits settlement into RMB only against real business uses inside the JV's approved scope. A PRC-certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report, which is filed with the local market-regulatory authority before the JV applies for VAT and operational payments. No inbound capital injection may be credited to a basic operating account outside the capital account framework.The JV should use SWIFT MT103 for high-value cross-border service remittances such as overseas subcontracted inspection fees and profit distributions, with every payment supported by contract, invoice, tax certificate, and SAFE purpose code. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are appropriate for importation of calibration instruments and laboratory equipment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's SAT transfer pricing documentation standards (Announcement [2016] No. 42) require a Local File where annual related-party transactions for tangible property reach CNY 200 million, where intangible property or financial asset transactions reach CNY 100 million, or where other controlled transactions reach CNY 40 million; a Master File is required once the group's annual revenue exceeds CNY 2 billion and cross-border controlled transactions above thresholds exist. For the JV inspection agency, intercompany testing service fees and management charges above these levels trigger contemporaneous documentation obligations.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form doctrine through anti-avoidance rules in the EIT Law (Articles 47 and 48) and through the beneficial ownership test for DTA claims. Although China has no separate law titled like the UAE ESR, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is embedded in the BEPS-relevant PE and treaty benefit analyses. The Chinese JV must demonstrate that personnel, premises, equipment and decision-making for core inspection and QC functions remain in China; if substance is hollow, dividends, interest, royalties and service payments can be recharacterized and treaty relief denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by concealing foreign-currency inspection fees

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection and Article 201 of the Criminal Law; the tax authority is entitled to impose the unpaid tax, a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid amount; criminal sanctions include fines and imprisonment of up to seven years for intentional concealment.

Director personal liability: The court can pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 where the director or shareholder abuses the JV's limited liability, commingles personal funds with corporate receipts, or uses the corporate form to defraud the State's tax claim. Directors are jointly and severally liable to restore the tax shortfall, may be placed on the Exit Clearance blacklist to ban overseas travel, and may be criminally imprisoned after asset seizure.
Shell company lacking economic substance and using treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 47 and 48 permit the tax authority to recharacterize the arrangement, adjust income, deny treaty benefits and impose tax at 25% plus an interest surcharge calculated at China's benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; the absence of substance triggers a penalty of up to five times the tax underpaid under Article 63 of the Collection Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the fraudulent treaty relief forms or knowingly create the shell arrangement personally participate in tax avoidance; the tax authority may pierce the veil to hold them jointly liable, suspend the company's tax registration, issue travel restrictions under the Exit Clearance Program, and refer directors for prosecution where the abuse conceals tax liabilities.
False invoicing and disguised profit-stripping services

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 provides for fines and imprisonment up to 10 years, or life imprisonment for extremely serious cases of false VAT special invoice issuance; Article 64 of the Collection Law permits a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid invoice-linked tax, and the SAT can deny the deductions, recalculate CIT, and impose a daily surcharge of 0.05%.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as principal offenders if they sign, authorize, or oversee false invoices; personal criminal liability includes confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, blacklisting from public credit systems, disqualification from holding corporate office, and imprisonment of up to life in severe VAT invoice fraud, with civil liability in restitution for all unpaid tax and damages to the JV's minority shareholders.
