Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office and commercial mailbox addresses are not permissible as the registered office of a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. SAMR requires a fixed, non-residential physical address evidenced by a property ownership certificate or a lease contract with a valid registration code, and the municipal tax authority will refuse to register the company for VAT invoice issuance if a mailbox address is submitted. Commercial banks performing AML/KYC and foreign exchange account-opening due diligence reject virtual offices because physical business presence is a core requirement for opening RMB basic deposit accounts and capital accounts. For digital advertising media buying, the JV must maintain at the registered address actual personnel, computers, media buying records, accounting books, and server or advertising account access evidence. A virtual office may serve only as a correspondence address ancillary to the physical registered office, and any contrary arrangement creates a substantial risk of business license revocation and bank account closure.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a general Sino-foreign equity joint venture under Company Law Article 26. The shareholders may agree a registered capital amount in the Articles of Association, and the paid-up capital contribution schedule must be completed within five years from establishment, unless the specific industry law prescribes a stricter period. No escrow blocked-account requirement is imposed for the base case, but capital contributions must be remitted to a corporate bank account opened after the business license is obtained, with contribution proof retained for the foreign exchange registration records.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at the industrial level. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered by annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the entity may be registered as a small-scale VAT taxpayer and may benefit from applicable small-scale exemption thresholds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for the zero VAT rate when they are physically exported from China, covered by an accepted customs export declaration, sold to a non-resident purchaser, and the export proceeds are repatriated and settled in foreign exchange within the statutory period. Qualifying offshore services, including digital advertising and media-buying services, may be zero-rated when the recipient is outside China, the service is rendered entirely outside China, and the contract, commercial invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt documentation establish a genuine cross-border supply. If not zero-rated, the domestic VAT rate applicable to advertising/media-buying services is 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Proceeds Collection Voucher

China requires VAT invoices to be issued through the STA-controlled tax-control invoice system. Taxable entities must transmit invoice data to the e-Tax platform in real time using the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) mechanism or the Golden Tax IV electronic invoice service; API-based integration is available for high-volume invoice issuers. Special VAT invoices carry digital tax-control signatures, invoice codes, and anti-fraud encrypted ciphers / QR codes, and must be reconciled with declared output VAT.

If the export zero-rating or VAT refund is not substantiated, the STA will retroactively claw back the VAT refunded, impose daily late-payment surcharges (0.05% per day), and assess administrative penalties of 50% to five times the tax involved. Customs and public security may additionally investigate fictitious exports and foreign-exchange evasion; egregious fraudulent export tax refunds are subject to criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, suspension of export-tax-refund status, and forfeiture of any illegal gains.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible on the current account for legitimate trade and service transactions, but market expectations and financial stability considerations still leave capital-account transactions subject to SAFE/PBOC approvals or registration protocols. Under the Sino-Foreign JV status, inbound FDI must be registered in SAFE's capital information system through the designated domestic bank before the remittance is routed. The capital contribution is credited to a dedicated FDI capital foreign exchange settlement account; conversion into RMB requires bank verification of the JV's approval/information report, the JV contract, the board capital call resolution, and consistency with the applicable negative list. Profit remittances are legally permissible after annual audited financial statements, tax clearance and applicable service-trade withholding payment, and board distribution resolution; the bank applies enhanced look-through checks on ultimate beneficial owners. Export/service proceeds from affiliate media buying, if foreign-currency receipts, must be repatriated into China and can be either retained in a designated foreign exchange current account or converted voluntarily. Detailed evidence of service delivery, campaign performance, and tax filings will be required, because virtual media spend creates heightened transfer-pricing and trade-authenticity scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account rules, export proceeds from service/media transactions must be repatriated to a domestic account within 180 calendar days from the date the service is rendered or invoice issued, or as otherwise certified by the trade item filing. Statutory surrender ratios no longer exist; the JV may hold the foreign exchange in a permitted current-account foreign exchange settlement account or convert it to RMB at a domestic bank after verifying the underlying invoices, contracts, and tax receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated enforcement through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its provincial branches

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must register the FDI with SAFE through the designated domestic bank, using the JV's Foreign-Invested Enterprise information filing and the negative list assessment. Once registered, the remitter wires the capital via SWIFT with the SAFE foreign exchange payment purpose code for FDI capital contribution and the unique JV registration code in the remittance reference. The domestic bank credits the dedicated FDI capital foreign exchange settlement account, verifies the JV contract, board approval, and any required capital verification from a Chinese certified public accountant, and then converts into RMB if required. Foreign EMIs may legally support incoming remittances from overseas affiliates, but the JV's primary disbursement and collection rails must be domestic licensed banks. Using EMIs as a primary treasury venue would be non-compliant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File transfer pricing documentation is triggered when annual related-party transactions reach RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required for Chinese resident ultimate parent entities of MNE groups with prior-year consolidated group revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a separately labeled Economic Substance Regulation regime, but the State Taxation Administration applies substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance, and core income-generating activity principles through EIT Law Article 47, beneficial ownership requirements, and transfer pricing functional analysis. A mainland affiliate media buying JV must employ substantive decision-makers, manage contracts, exercise operational control over traffic quality and campaign optimization, and bear meaningful commercial risk in mainland China. If the local entity is a booking vehicle without real function or control, profits may be reattributed to the foreign parent or related parties and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Articles 63 and 65 impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day plus an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax, alongside collection of the underpaid amount. False issuance, procurement, or use of VAT invoices engages Criminal Law Article 205, which carries fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment that can reach life for serious cases of fraudulent VAT invoice activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instruct, procure, or benefit from phantom traffic, fake invoices, or fraudulent cost deductions face joint and several civil tax restitution liability under PRC Company Law, asset freezing, and confiscation orders. The legal representative and responsible executives may be placed under exit bans and travel restrictions under Tax Collection Law Article 44. Criminal conviction for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment up to seven years; false VAT invoice crimes under Article 205 can result in life imprisonment in severe cases.
Shell company and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 authorizes the tax authority to recharacterize arrangements lacking bona fide commercial purpose and to assess tax on the income that would have arisen had the arrangement been respected on substance. This can result in denial of intercompany deductions, reallocation of taxable profit, denial of DTA relief, assessment of the unpaid tax, a 0.05% daily surcharge, and a 50% to 500% administrative penalty.

Director personal liability: Where directors and controlling shareholders are found to have abused the separate legal personality of the JV and limited liability to evade taxes, the corporate veil may be pierced and personal joint and several liability imposed for unpaid corporate taxes and debts. Directors may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving mainland China under Tax Collection Law Article 44 for significant overdue tax obligations, and may be prosecuted under Criminal Law Articles 201 or 203 if they conceal or transfer assets to prevent tax enforcement.
Anti-money laundering, foreign exchange and payment-rail abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and People's Bank of China implementing rules impose administrative penalties on institutions and responsible individuals for weak AML controls and unreported suspicious transactions. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange can levy penalties for illegal cross-border settlements, false declarations, and unauthorized outward remittances, typically involving confiscation of illegal gains plus fines ranging from a fraction of the illegal amount to quite severe statutory multiples depending on the violation.

Director personal liability: Responsible directors, compliance officers, legal representatives, and beneficial owners face personal administrative fines, asset freezes, cancellation of residence or work permits, and departure restrictions. If the payment structure is used to launder the proceeds of tax evasion or other predicate offenses, the conduct can be prosecuted as money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191, carrying imprisonment up to ten years plus fines. PRC Company Law veil piercing may additionally render directors personally liable for restitution of the full loss caused to the entity and the state.
