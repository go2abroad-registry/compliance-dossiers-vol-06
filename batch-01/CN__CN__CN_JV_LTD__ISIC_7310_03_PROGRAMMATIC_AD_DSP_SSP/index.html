Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed for a foreign-invested advertising-technology service company. The registered capital and shareholder contribution schedule must be set out in the Articles of Association. Unless the Articles provide an earlier date, the shareholders must pay their subscribed capital in full within five years of establishment. There is no blocked escrow account rule under general company law; however, foreign-currency contributions must be received through a licensed bank in China after completion of SAFE foreign-direct-investment registration, and the contributions must be recorded in the company accounts for statutory capital contributions.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portals: Unified National Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-foreign equity JV is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the one-stop market incorporation and tax registration procedure, and the same USCC becomes the entity's TIN, VAT ID and employer registration number. After incorporation, the JV must complete online tax registration in the Unified National Electronic Tax Bureau, activate its electronic tax filing credential, and obtain the tax-control / e-invoice authorization. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment, the non-resident contractor must obtain a non-resident TIN via temporary tax registration at the district STA in charge of the Chinese withholding agent or payor, supplying a certificate of foreign incorporation, foreign tax residence certificate, contract and China-source income explanation; alternatively, the Chinese counterparty may register the non-resident for withholding purposes. No separate VAT ID exists; the USCC is used for VAT and all national tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a standalone hybrid mismatch election. An entity's classification as a resident enterprise is mandatory under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The only elective indirect tax mechanism is voluntary general VAT taxpayer registration for small-scale taxpayers with sound accounting and a fixed place of business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable VAT turnover triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below that, the taxpayer remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC export tax refund (exemption) regime, 0% VAT with input VAT credit applies to physical export of goods and to enumerated cross-border services (including R&D, design, contract energy management, software services, information system services, business process management services and offshore service outsourcing) provided to an overseas recipient and wholly consumed outside China. Pure advertising placement or agency services are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated; however, the programmatic SSP/DSP platform can qualify as a zero-rated software/information system service if the contract, delivery and usage evidence demonstrate that the service is provided to an overseas client and wholly consumed offshore, and the corresponding foreign exchange is repatriated within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form approved by China Customs
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical goods
  • Commercial Invoice / Export Invoice
  • Export Sales Contract and Purchase Order
  • Bank Receipt of Export Proceeds / Foreign Exchange Income Declaration
  • VAT Special Invoices for purchased inputs for refund calculation
  • Cross-border service agreement and offshore consumption certificate for services
  • Geolocation and electronic delivery logs for digital/SSP services
  • Foreign exchange credit advice and payment confirmation

China mandates real-time e-invoicing under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV framework. All VAT invoices, especially special VAT invoices for B2B supplies, must be issued through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform or through ERP software integrated via STA-certified APIs. Invoices carry a unique digital signature, invoice code and QR code; issuance, voiding, red-letter offsetting and reconciliation are reported to the STA in real time. The system performs automated anti-fraud cross-checks against customs declarations, tax returns, bank receipts and non-resident payment records. The applicable service VAT rate for the AdTech/SSP entity is 6%, while the general goods/import VAT rate remains 13%.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are jointly audited by the STA and China Customs. If the required customs, delivery, offshore-consumption or foreign-exchange repatriation documents are missing, false or inconsistent, the STA will deny the 0% treatment, claw back the refunded VAT, impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and assess administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the improper refund. Intentional false declarations constitute the criminal offense of fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, punishable by fines of one to five times the defrauded amount and imprisonment. The taxpayer's credit rating will be downgraded, triggering revocation of paperless and fast-track export-refund facilitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is convertible for current-account transactions, including goods trade, services and ordinary corporate remittances, but capital-account transactions remain tightly controlled by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture, inbound equity capital must enter through an FDI foreign-exchange capital account after SAFE registration, and the foreign currency may be settled into RMB only when the bank is satisfied as to the genuine underlying transaction. Outbound profit remittances are lawful after the annual statutory audit, tax clearance and a board-approved distribution, but the bank will require audited financial statements, booking vouchers, certificates of tax filing and the joint venture's distribution resolution. Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange account or sold to a designated bank on the strength of customs or service-transaction records. No fixed surrender ratio is currently imposed; the true constraint is documentary authenticity and registration. Convertibility risk is concentrated in capital-account items such as early repayment of foreign-shareholder loans, equity transfers and liquidation remittances, each of which demands a separate SAFE filing or approval before funds can leave.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods must be repatriated into China within 180 days after customs export declaration and must be received onshore or into a designated foreign-exchange account; the exporter may retain the full amount in foreign currency or sell part/all to the bank at the prevailing market rate, as no statutory mandatory conversion ratio is in force. For service-export flows, including programmatic advertising, platform or SSP fees, the bank reviews the service contract, the VAT/service invoice and platform settlement statement, and the proceeds should be received within the time limit set by SAFE and bank policy; there is no forced conversion ratio, but every receipt is subject to transaction authenticity and electronic reporting.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW, not viable as a principal account. Foreign fintech EMIs without a PBOC banking license cannot open an RMB or foreign-currency corporate settlement account for a mainland-domiciled JV, cannot receive FDI capital injections, cannot be linked to SAFE tax or VAT filings, and cannot support statutory capital verification. The JV may use PBOC-licensed third-party payment institutions for domestic small-value collections or disbursements, but those are not bank accounts and cannot carry cross-border corporate treasury flows. Any blueprint that relies on an offshore digital EMI as the operating treasury vehicle would fail account opening, SAFE registration and audit requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investors must remit share capital from their own offshore bank accounts into the JV's designated FDI foreign-exchange capital account opened at a SAFE-designated Chinese tier-1 bank. The SWIFT MT103 instruction must reference the JV name, the SAFE FDI registration QR code, and the SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment equity contribution; the remittance must not be annotated as a loan, current-account trade payment or personal transfer. After receipt, the JV may apply to the bank for foreign-exchange settlement into RMB supported by genuine operational documentation, and must obtain a capital contribution verification report from a Chinese certified public accountant before the settled funds are deployed. The capital account is monitored by SAFE, and settled RMB can only be used for lawful registered capital purposes with invoice and contract evidence retained on file.For this AdTech JV, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 for overseas media, vendor and platform settlement payments, and the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for cross-border RMB payments when counterparties accept RMB. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections remain available for ancillary physical trade involving hardware, servers or data-centre equipment, though they are not core to the programmatic advertising flow. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Enterprise Income Tax Law of the PRC, Article 4)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax under EIT Law Article 37 on dividends paid by a Chinese-resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; no withholding applies to dividends distributed to a Chinese-resident corporate shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible assets, RMB 100 million for intangible assets, RMB 100 million for financial assets, or RMB 40 million for other related-party services. Master File is required where the ultimate parent MNE group's previous-year consolidated revenue reaches RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report is triggered at consolidated group revenue of RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not impose a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the tax authorities enforce a substance-over-form doctrine through GAAR under EIT Law Article 47, the DTA beneficial-ownership rule, and special tax adjustment investigations. For a programmatic AdTech JV, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be exercised in China: algorithmic price decision-making, contract negotiation, ad-server operation, risk-bearing, data management, and qualified technical staff. If those functions are performed offshore, treaty relief and arm's-length assumptions may be disregarded and income recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Falsified Ad-Tech Costs and Fabricated Transactions

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63: civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; disallowance of all fictitious costs under EIT Law Articles 6-8; Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion punishable by fine and imprisonment of up to seven years where the evaded amount is large and exceeds the statutory proportion threshold.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law's disregard-of-corporate-personality doctrine, directors who controlled the fraudulent scheme are personally liable for restitution of diverted corporate assets and payment of the evaded tax. The legal representative may be blocked from leaving China through the tax authority's exit-control/dishonest taxpayer list. Willful participation can result in director-level criminal imprisonment and financial penalties.
Shell/Jurisdiction Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and reimposition of the statutory 10% withholding tax; TCAL Article 32 daily late surcharge; EIT Law Article 47 GAAR permits recharacterisation of the conduit arrangement; false beneficial-ownership filings expose the taxpayer to TCAL Article 63 penalties of 50%-500% of the under-withheld tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax residency or beneficial-ownership declarations may be jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax through veil piercing. They may be blacklisted with travel bans and asset attachment; criminal prosecution for tax evasion or issuing false tax invoices can impose imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Failure to Maintain Documentation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 44 authorises the tax authority to estimate taxable income if documentation is not produced; Article 48 requires unilateral adjustment with interest at the PBOC benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points, and the special tax adjustment may be assessed retroactively. TCAL Articles 62-63 add administrative fines and penalties of 50%-500% of any underpaid tax if the mispricing is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors and the financial officer who approved the intercompany contracts can be held personally accountable for the corporate tax shortfall when abuse of corporate personality is shown. Recovery includes personal asset seizure, travel restrictions, and blacklisting; criminal liability for tax evasion attaches if they knowingly structured false transfer pricing for the benefit of an offshore related party.
