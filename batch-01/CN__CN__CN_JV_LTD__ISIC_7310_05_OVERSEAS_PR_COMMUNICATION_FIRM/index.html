Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture limited liability company in China. The shareholders freely fix the subscribed registered capital amount and the contribution schedule in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow account requirement exists under national law, but the foreign shareholder's inbound capital contribution must be received and registered through a designated bank account in compliance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) reporting rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal is the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform operated by STA and its provincial and municipal tax bureaus. Newly incorporated foreign-invested entities complete tax registration through the integrated market entity registration system administered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must be incorporated with SAMR, which issues a Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Under the multi-license integration reform, the USCC is the legal Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the enterprise and no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Step 2: Within 30 days after the Business License is issued, the JV must complete supplemental tax registration information through the e-Tax portal, including legal representative details, accounting standards, banking account information, VAT taxpayer status, and filing frequency. Step 3: The JV must elect or be classified as either a General VAT Taxpayer or a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer; if projected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, General VAT Taxpayer registration is mandatory. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has not incorporated a Chinese entity, a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number must be obtained by application to the competent STA office, either directly or through a withholding agent, before the non-resident may enjoy treaty benefits or execute domestic VAT withholding obligations. Step 5: The taxpayer must configure fully digitalized electronic invoicing through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform, link its bank account for tax payment and refund settlement, and register its export/service-trade foreign exchange receipts with the relevant payment bank and tax filing records for zero-rating or exemption claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a Chinese resident enterprise and is taxed as a separate entity under the Enterprise Income Tax Law on its worldwide income. It is not eligible for pass-through treatment; distributions to foreign shareholders are subject to withholding tax where applicable under domestic law and the relevant double tax treaty.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election, pass-through election, or hybrid mismatch election for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. For non-resident enterprises, an analysis of whether an establishment or place of business exists in China governs profit attribution and income tax exposure, but this is a factual statutory determination rather than an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All incorporated business entities must complete tax registration regardless of turnover. Mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration applies when aggregate annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. A Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000, or quarterly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 300,000, may apply for the applicable VAT exemption, but this does not eliminate the VAT registration obligation. For the subject public relations and communication agency services supplied domestically, the applicable VAT rate is 6% as modern services, while the 13% headline standard rate applies to general goods and the 9% rate applies to certain goods and transportation/construction services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported goods evidenced by a valid customs export declaration, and for enumerated cross-border services supplied to an overseas entity and wholly consumed outside China under Ministry of Finance and STA Circular No. 36 of 2016 and implementing rules. Enumerated qualifying service categories include R&D services, design services, software services, information system services, business process management services, offshore outsourcing, and technical services. Public relations and communication agency services are generally classified as modern services at a domestic VAT rate of 6%; when supplied to an overseas client and fully consumed outside China, they may qualify for VAT exemption rather than automatic zero-rating/refund unless the arrangement falls within the enumerated offshore outsourcing or business process management service categories. To claim zero-rating or exemption, the taxpayer must demonstrate that the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise, is not connected with Chinese real estate or goods or other Chinese services, is not consumed in China, is supported by a cross-border contract, and that the foreign currency consideration has been or will be repatriated into China through a recognised banking channel.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export bill of entry) for physical goods; not applicable to pure service exports
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods across customs boundaries where goods are exported
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued under the relevant export contract
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a Chinese bank evidencing receipt, settlement, or verification of export/service-trade proceeds
  • Cross-border service contract and engagement letter describing the overseas client, scope of work, and offshore consumption
  • Certificate of foreign client status and supporting evidence that the service was performed and consumed entirely outside China
  • Statutory VAT invoice, preferably a fully digitalized electronic invoice, correctly coded and issued for the export transaction
  • Export tax rebate or zero-rating application and service trade foreign exchange receipt filing submitted through the STA electronic tax system

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Since the nationwide rollout of fully digitalized electronic invoices, each invoice is cleared in real time through the Golden Tax System Phase IV and is assigned a unique invoice number and a verified QR code anti-fraud digital stamp. Authorised taxpayers and enterprise ERP systems may use STA-specified APIs to transmit invoice data for real-time authentication. Traditional paper fiscal stamping is replaced by digital anti-fraud validation; STA systems perform automated cross-matching of invoice issuance, VAT filing, payment, and export refund declarations to detect anomalies.

The STA may conduct post-export audits and require production of customs declarations, bank SWIFT receipts, foreign exchange settlement vouchers, service performance records, contracts, and offshore consumption evidence. If a zero-rating or exemption claim is rejected or found to be unsupported, the taxpayer must repay the refunded or exempted VAT, pay late payment surcharges of 0.05% per day on the unpaid amount, and may incur administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the amount of tax involved under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levying. Fraudulent export tax refund or zero-rating claims may be prosecuted as criminal offences under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties including monetary fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for especially serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese yuan (CNY) operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls, while current account transactions are generally convertible subject to documentary review. Foreign-invested enterprises (FIEs) such as the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must obtain Foreign Exchange Registration (FDI) from SAFE before opening a capital account. Capital inflows for registered equity investment may be freely converted into CNY under the capital account convertibility liberalisation, but outbound remittances of profits, dividends, or liquidated funds require verification of audited financials, tax clearance certificates, and compliance with the real-business-transaction principle. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender since the 2012 reform, yet all foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days of customs declaration and reported through the Trade Monitoring System. Profit remittances face additional hurdles: the joint venture must submit an audit report, tax payment proof, and board resolution, and each remittance must be individually authorised by the designated bank. Prudent treasury planning must account for repatriation lead times, the risk of transfer pricing adjustments, and the possibility that SAFE may require supporting documentation for cross-border payments exceeding USD 500,000. In practice, current account convertibility is efficient for genuine trade and service payments, but capital account outflows are slow and highly scrutinised, making early engagement with bank compliance and SAFE essential.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules (as amended), export proceeds collected in foreign currency are not required to be mandatorily surrendered to the central bank. Exporters may retain these funds in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily convert them into CNY. However, all export proceeds from goods exported from mainland China must be repatriated to China within 180 days after the date of export customs declaration. The exporter must register the receipt in the Trade Monitoring System and ensure the payment is consistent with the declared transaction. There is no statutory minimum conversion ratio; enterprises may choose to hold the entirety of their export earnings in foreign currency, subject to the bank's risk classification and SAFE post-verification. Failure to repatriate within the 180-day window can trigger regulatory sanctions, a downgrade of the entity's classification, and mandatory on-site inspections.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore financial technology EMIs (electronic money institutions) are not viable for servicing a China-based entity. The PBOC and SAFE maintain a closed licensing regime for payment institutions and foreign-currency clearing services. Non-bank financial institutions that do not hold a domestic Payment Business License are prohibited from opening deposit accounts, executing FX conversions, or conducting cross-border settlement on behalf of Chinese legal persons. In practice, foreign digital banks and EMIs such as Revolut Business or Wise cannot provide compatible RMB clearing or direct debit/credit rails within mainland China. Any attempt to route treasury flows through an unlicensed EMI would trigger immediate red flags in bank KYC reviews, potentially leading to account closure and regulatory penalties. The only lawful approach is to use a licensed domestic bank for all CNY and FX operations; offshore EMI accounts may be used only for ancillary receivables from overseas clients, provided they strictly comply with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's requirement that all cross-border trade payments be settled through designated domestic banks. Therefore, the strategic treasury blueprint should rely exclusively on Tier-1 Chinese commercial banks for operational liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore financial technology EMIs (electronic money institutions) are not viable for servicing a China-based entity. The PBOC and SAFE maintain a closed licensing regime for payment institutions and foreign-currency clearing services. Non-bank financial institutions that do not hold a domestic Payment Business License are prohibited from opening deposit accounts, executing FX conversions, or conducting cross-border settlement on behalf of Chinese legal persons. In practice, foreign digital banks and EMIs such as Revolut Business or Wise cannot provide compatible RMB clearing or direct debit/credit rails within mainland China. Any attempt to route treasury flows through an unlicensed EMI would trigger immediate red flags in bank KYC reviews, potentially leading to account closure and regulatory penalties. The only lawful approach is to use a licensed domestic bank for all CNY and FX operations; offshore EMI accounts may be used only for ancillary receivables from overseas clients, provided they strictly comply with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's requirement that all cross-border trade payments be settled through designated domestic banks. Therefore, the strategic treasury blueprint should rely exclusively on Tier-1 Chinese commercial banks for operational liquidity management.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY: Contemporaneous Local File/Master File is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed: (i) CNY 200 million for tangible asset transfers; (ii) CNY 100 million for intangible asset transfers; (iii) CNY 100 million for financial asset transfers; (iv) CNY 40 million for other related-party transactions (including service fees; royalty payments; interest). If the JV has any related-party transaction with a low-tax jurisdiction (effective rate below 12.5%), the threshold is suspended and documentation is required for smaller amounts.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the GAAR under Article 47 of the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law and the 'substance over form' doctrine apply. For treaty benefits, the contracting-state resident must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in that state; a Hong Kong or other intermediary with only a mailbox, nominee director, or paper board will be considered a conduit and denied treaty benefits. The Chinese CIT administration uses BEPS Action 3, Action 6, and Action 8 principles to re-characterize transactions without economic substance. The JV must maintain substance evidence for any transaction with its non-resident parent and must not simply re-invoice global PR costs through an offshore entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Tax Collection Law / Criminal Law)

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection: administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the due date until payment, and correction order. If the amount of tax evaded exceeds certain thresholds and is declared a criminal offence under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, the entity is fined and the responsible directors are subject to criminal liability.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs a false tax return or supervises a false invoicing scheme will be personally liable to the extent of unpaid taxes recovered through piercing the corporate veil under Article 20 of the PRC Companies Law. The personal assets of the director may be seized in restitution; the director will be placed of a tax blacklist, get a travel ban and exit-control (出境限制) by the immigration authorities, and can face imprisonment for up to 7 years together with a criminal fine and forfeiture of illegal gains.
Shell Company / DTA Abuse (Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law and Implementing Regulations Article 120 empower the tax authority to disregard the offshore entity in an arrangement lacking reasonable commercial purpose. The taxes will be re-assessed at the statutory 10% rate, plus interest and an administrative penalty under Article 63/64 of the Tax Collection Law. The tax authority can also invoke the OECD BEPS PPT/LOB rule under the DTA, denying treaty benefits and re-characterizing the payment as a taxable dividend.

Director personal liability: Under the circular of State Administration of Taxation on 'Strong exposure and control of high-end tax-reducing arrangements', legal representatives and financial officers who design or execute a treaty-abusive structure can be held personally liable for penalties and interest. The corporate veil may be pierced, and the director's personal assets will be used to satisfy the adjusted tax. Travel restrictions and tax-information 'blacklisting' (重大税收违法失信案件) apply. If the arrangement includes forgery of TRC or financial statements, criminal punishment for tax fraud can be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Related-Party Disclosures (TP non-compliance)

Statutory sanction: Article 62 of the Tax Collection Law imposes a fixed penalty of RMB 10,000 for failure to file related-party declarations; transfer pricing adjustment may be made prospectively and retrospectively, with a special tax adjustment to impute taxable income. If the under-reporting is due to malice or false documentation, the penalty can rise to 50% of the adjusted additional tax, plus 5-year accrual interest at the benchmark rate. The PRC tax authority may also impose a secondary adjustment if the corresponding profits are not repatriated.

Director personal liability: Directors and CFOs who sign incomplete TP documents or sign off on a deliberately ineffective transfer-pricing policy can have the corporate veil pierced under the Company Law. The tax authority may request an administrative investigation of the director's personal bank accounts and assets if intercompany payments disguised as 'expenses' actually benefited the director personally. The responsible individuals are included on the serious tax violation 'blacklist', leading to travel bans, a ban on high-consumption activities, and disqualification from being legal representatives. In cases of fraud, criminal liability under Article 201 applies.
