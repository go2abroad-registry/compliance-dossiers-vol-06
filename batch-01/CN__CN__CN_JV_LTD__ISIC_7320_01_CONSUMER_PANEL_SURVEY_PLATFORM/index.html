Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China (official web portal: www.chinatax.gov.cn) and the STA-managed Electronic Tax Bureau online taxpayer service platform (E-Tax portal)

E-government filing portal

A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a Chinese resident limited company; tax registration is completed through the national one-stop business registration data-sharing mechanism: (1) incorporate/register the JV with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); (2) upon issuance of the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), the USCC automatically becomes the statutory TIN and the STA tax registration record is created through the shared data pipeline; (3) the JV must then complete supplementary registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, including accounting standard, bank account, payroll, VAT taxpayer status, and foreign-exchange reporting particulars; (4) a non-resident foreign shareholder does not require a separate TIN merely to hold equity, but if it earns Chinese-source dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees, the local STA branch performs a non-resident withholding tax registration with the payer acting as withholding agent; the non-resident may also register directly under the non-resident tax registration procedure for refund/treaty claims.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT export treatment is available for qualifying cross-border taxable services; because the platform is structured as a SaaS consumer-panel survey platform, the service should be documented as software services or information system services supplied to a non-resident enterprise, performed and consumed entirely outside the People's Republic of China. Statutory conditions: (i) the customer is a non-resident enterprise/individual with no PRC establishment using the service; (ii) the service does not involve goods/property in China and is not consumed in China; (iii) the platform grants access from outside China with no China-market delivery; (iv) consideration is in foreign currency and repatriated through banking channels or a permitted cross-border payment institution; and (v) the service is classified in the STA catalogue of zero-rated software/information-system services. Standalone offline human-provided market-research reports or polling advisory may be VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, so the commercial contract and delivery evidence must emphasize the automated SaaS nature.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) and corresponding electronic customs declaration return for any physical survey devices, hardware, or printed materials shipped abroad; pure digital SaaS cross-border supply does not require a customs Bill of Entry
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical exports; for pure digital services, retain server-access logs, IP-address evidence, and SaaS usage/consumption logs proving all service delivery and consumption occurred outside China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE bank foreign-exchange settlement voucher, including PayPal Mass Payout and Wise cross-border payout statements, evidencing actual receipt and repatriation of export consideration into the JV's China bank account
  • International service contract or click-wrap SaaS subscription order, in English or bilingual, identifying the non-resident buyer, service scope, SaaS licensing and delivery model, currency, price, and payment terms
  • STA Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice issued as the export invoice, together with the VAT return and export zero-rating/exemption dataset filed through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal

China mandates issuance of VAT e-invoices through the STA Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under the Golden Tax Phase IV data architecture. Invoices must be generated via the Electronic Tax Bureau portal or an STA-approved API connection; each invoice receives a unique electronic invoice number and anti-counterfeiting QR code, and invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA for fiscal clearing, input-output VAT matching, and cross-matching with customs and foreign-exchange data. Offline paper-invoice clearance no longer satisfies the general rule, and a non-compliant invoice may be denied as an input VAT credit.

Upon audit, the taxpayer bears the burden of substantiating every export zero-rating claim. If the STA determines the zero-rating conditions were false, not properly documented, or the service was in fact used/consumed in China, it will claw back any VAT refund or exemption, re-assess output VAT at the applicable rate (6% for services or 13% for goods), charge a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, impose a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and refer the matter for criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law where fraudulent export refund or e-invoice fraud is established. The Golden Tax Phase IV system automatically cross-matches export customs declarations, VAT e-invoices, foreign-exchange receipts, and non-resident status, making unverified or fabricated zero-rating claims prone to immediate flagging and retrospective clawback.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system; the RMB is conditionally convertible on the current account and only selectively convertible on the capital account. Current-account payments and receipts, including service trade and ordinary profit remittances, can be completed through a domestic bank on a real-transaction basis with contracts, invoices, tax forms and transaction-supporting records. Capital-account items such as FDI equity injection, external loans, and foreign-currency securities investment require SAFE registration or approval; conversion is transaction-specific rather than freely available. A Sino-foreign equity JV may open a foreign-exchange capital account and convert paid-in capital into RMB after SAFE registration, but the proceeds must follow the approved business purpose and cannot be diverted to prohibited sectors or portfolio investments. Export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory surrender, and current-account foreign currency may be retained by the enterprise or sold to a bank voluntarily. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permissible once the annual financial statements are audited, taxes including withholding tax are paid, and the bank has verified the funds against the JV's tax and SAFE records. Cross-border data and consumer research services must also satisfy PBOC and cybersecurity data-export compliance before payment can be settled freely.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign-currency receipts; an enterprise may retain 100% of verified current-account export proceeds in a foreign-currency account or sell them for RMB at a licensed bank. However, export related foreign-currency revenue must be repatriated to China by the corresponding due date and generally within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, unless an extension is registered with SAFE. Service-export proceeds must be repatriated by the contractual payment maturity date, also within the 180-day verification window, and the bank will check the commercial contract, invoice, and service-trade declaration before settlement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low and legally restricted for primary banking. Foreign EMI platforms such as Wise and PayPal do not hold PRC depository licences and cannot act as the JV's primary treasury or settlement bank. PayPal and Wise may be used only as downstream collection and payout rails after the JV holds a compliant domestic bank account, and any cross-border flow routed through them must still be declared and settled through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE rules. A China-domiciled entity cannot satisfy tax, capital verification, or SAFE reporting obligations by relying on an offshore EMI, so the onboarding viability of a fintech EMI as a substitute for Tier-1 domestic banking is effectively unusable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally restricted for primary banking. Foreign EMI platforms such as Wise and PayPal do not hold PRC depository licences and cannot act as the JV's primary treasury or settlement bank. PayPal and Wise may be used only as downstream collection and payout rails after the JV holds a compliant domestic bank account, and any cross-border flow routed through them must still be declared and settled through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE rules. A China-domiciled entity cannot satisfy tax, capital verification, or SAFE reporting obligations by relying on an offshore EMI, so the onboarding viability of a fintech EMI as a substitute for Tier-1 domestic banking is effectively unusable.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% under Article 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law) for resident enterprises; non-resident enterprises deriving China-source passive income are subject to 10% withholding unless a treaty rate applies

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% under Article 37 and Article 59 of the EIT Law and its Implementing Regulations: a resident enterprise paying dividends to a non-resident enterprise shareholder is required to withhold 10% on the gross dividend amount, unless an applicable Double Tax Treaty or DTA provides a lower rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is statutorily triggered when related-party annual tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the multinational group's annual related-party transaction value is materially above the State Taxation Administration threshold, and Country-by-Country Reporting is required when the consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion; the Chinese JV should nonetheless prepare arm's length documentation even below the thresholds if any cross-border intercompany transaction exists because China permits the tax authority to scrutinize any related-party arrangement.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code, the State Taxation Administration applies a substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 to disregard a structure that has no substantial commercial justifiability. For DTA benefits, the Chinese tax authority applies a Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) and beneficial-ownership test; an offshore affiliate receiving royalties, dividends or survey-data fees must show management decision-making, employees, risk exposure and real operational activity in its tax-residency jurisdiction. A shell entity interposed solely between the Chinese JV and its end customer will be denied treaty benefits and its payments will be taxed as China-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Cross-Border Revenue Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid tax plus daily interest at 0.05%; PRC Criminal Law Article 201 punses fraudulent failure to file or false filing of tax returns; if the amount of tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable, the criminal penalty is imprisonment of up to three years and a fine, and for very large amounts, imprisonment from three to seven years.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 20, a controlling shareholder or director who abuses the corporate limited-liability form to evade tax is jointly and severally liable to creditors for the unpaid tax and losses. Where the offending director is the actual controller, the competent tax authority may list the individual on the serious-tax-violation information disclosure system, impose exit and travel restrictions under the Administration of Exit and Entry Law, and a criminal conviction triggers mandatory travel bans, asset freezing and confiscation of personal assets used to conceal the fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization Through Offshore Service Fees

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 46, 47 and 48 and the State Taxation Administration's Transfer Pricing Measures authorize the tax authority to re-characterize the payments, deny deductions, impute interest and make a transfer pricing adjustment; the additional tax may be subject to interest computed from the due date plus an added special tax adjustment interest of 5 percentage points where contemporaneous documentation is not provided. Article 102 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law permits priority seizure and collection from bank deposits for the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: If the director or financial controller signs non-arm's length intercompany agreements that cause the Chinese JV to default on statutory tax obligations, Chinese courts can pierce the corporate veil and hold the director personally liable for the adjusted tax, interest and penalties. A foreign director whose personal assets are outside China is nonetheless subject to cross-border civil recognition and enforcement, an Interpol red notice or court-ordered travel restrictions, and if fraudulent falsification of invoices or documentation is established, the director is additionally exposed to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 205 for false invoicing, with imprisonment up to life in serious cases.
Shell Company Status and Abusive DTA Claim / AML Red Flag

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and the Implementation Regulations, the State Taxation Administration may disregard the abusive arrangement and re-source the income to China at a 10% withholding rate or a worse domestic deemed-profit rate. The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and cross-border foreign exchange rules impose substantial fines on financial institutions and payment intermediaries processing disguised transactions, and the relevant regulatory regulator may blacklist the entity; repeated violations trigger revocation of the platform's payment-channel approvals and administrative penalties up to RMB 1 million or higher for institutions.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the fraudulent treaty claim or money transfers are personally exposed to civil claw-back actions for tax shortfalls; the corporate veil is pierced for having used the shell entity as a sham instrumentality, and directors face criminal sanctions for intentional non-payment, forgery of tax residency certificates or participation in tax-related money laundering, including imprisonment ranging from five to ten years for aggravated money laundering under PRC Criminal Law Article 191. The director's travel rights may be suspended by administrative blacklisting until the tax liability and penalties are settled.
