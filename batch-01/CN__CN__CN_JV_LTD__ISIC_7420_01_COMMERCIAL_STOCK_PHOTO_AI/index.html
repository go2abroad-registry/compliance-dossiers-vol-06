Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing and tax registration through the unified National Electronic Tax Bureau, commonly called the Electronic Tax Bureau internet portal (chinatax.gov.cn/etax).

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is first incorporated with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the sole statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and is also used for VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, customs, social insurance, and payroll tax purposes. Within 30 days after the business license issue date, the legal representative or authorized tax agent must complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal by submitting the business license, USCC, articles of association, registered office proof, legal representative identification, capital/bank account information, and intended accounting and tax-filing arrangements. The STA confirms the USCC as the TIN and opens the taxpayer master file, VAT general taxpayer profile, and tax return channels. A foreign non-resident entity without a PRC establishment or place of business does not ordinarily obtain a standalone PRC TIN; where the foreign enterprise is subject to withholding, the PRC payor withholds using the foreign entity's home-country tax reference, while a non-resident with a PRC permanent establishment or otherwise directly taxable in China must apply for non-resident tax registration through the same Electronic Tax Bureau gateway using its foreign TIN and, if applicable, the Chinese establishment USCC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All taxpayers must complete USCC/tax registration before commencing taxable activity; for VAT general taxpayer status, annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (rolling 12 months) is the mandatory compulsory registration threshold, below which small-scale taxpayer treatment applies unless general VAT taxpayer status is voluntarily selected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported from PRC customs territory may be zero-rated under the export goods VAT exemption, credit, and refund method once the goods are actually shipped out of China, the customs export declaration is released, the export sale is properly invoiced, and export proceeds are repatriated in accordance with SAFE foreign exchange rules. Qualifying offshore digital services and copyright/licensing-type transactions supplied to an overseas recipient may be zero-rated or VAT exempt if the service is consumed wholly outside China, the recipient is located outside China, and the contract, consideration, and delivery evidence demonstrate an offshore supply. The stock-photo and AI-generated visual asset marketplace must maintain the customer side, user location, delivery record, payment trail, and licensing terms to prove the cross-border nature of each zero-rated supply.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Export Declaration) bearing the official export declaration number
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or combined transport document evidencing actual export from PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and the export supply contract or digital order agreement with the overseas customer
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Certificate or SAFE export proceeds settlement/foreign exchange repatriation record
  • For zero-rated digital services, supplementary delivery and consumption evidence such as server logs, user-country data, licensing agreements, and payment receipts from outside China

China requires near-real-time invoice clearance through the State Taxation Administration's national invoice control environment under the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform (EISP). Registered taxpayers must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) with STA-issued digital certificates; each invoice is transmitted to STA at issuance, receives a nationally unique invoice number and statutory digital tax control seal, and is crosschecked against VAT return data, bank receipts, and customs records. High-volume sellers are expected to integrate with the EISP through government-approved APIs or certified tax-control software, and no unfiled offline VAT invoice may support input VAT credit or an export refund claim.

The STA, Customs, and SAFE jointly audit zero-rating claims by matching customs declarations, logistics confirmations, VAT invoice chains, corporate income tax filings, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is approved without genuine customs exit, without valid FX repatriation within the statutory deadline, or with forged documentation or circular payments, the taxpayer must repay the refunded VAT plus daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% of the unpaid tax, and is subject to an administrative penalty of between one and five times the tax involved; criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund is pursued where the conduct meets the statutory threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese yuan (CNY/RMB) is only partially convertible. Current account transactions, including trade in services and ordinary cross-border settlement, are broadly liberalised and may be effected through designated domestic banks after legitimate background verification. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment into the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, are heavily regulated by SAFE and require foreign-exchange registration, specified bank account structures, and genuine-transaction evidence. Foreign direct investment equity contributions may be remitted into a SAFE-registered capital account and subsequently settled to RMB for permitted uses, but the proceeds cannot be deployed for prohibited areas such as securities investment, real estate beyond the approved scope, or interbank lending. Profit and dividend remittance to the foreign shareholder is feasible after corporate income tax settlement, statutory reserve allocation, audited financial statements, and a board/shareholder dividend resolution; the remittance itself must pass through a domestic commercial bank with a completed tax payment certificate or withholding tax certificate and the correct SAFE service-trade or income-remittance purpose code. The JV must therefore distinguish carefully between current account flows, which are largely free after verification, and capital account flows, which are subject to quotas, registrations, and post-payment monitoring. Full offshore convertibility is not available; no unregulated fintech channel may substitute for the SAFE-approved banking channel.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic bank account within the statutory time limit, normally 180 days from customs declaration, invoice date, or contractual payment due date unless SAFE has granted an extension for documented cause. The historical universal surrender requirement has been abolished; an exporter may retain eligible foreign exchange in its current-account FX settlement account or sell it to the bank for RMB, subject to SAFE authenticity verification and aggregated account limits. There is no fixed national mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts, but any amount exceeding the approved retention limit must be converted into RMB. The JV must supply the verifying bank with signed export contracts, customs declaration records, commercial invoices, receipts confirmation, logistics documentation, and the relevant balance-of-payments transaction records. Failure to repatriate within the required period or to provide genuine trade documentation will result in SAFE penalties and AML scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Restricted / Auxiliary Only. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe and PayPal are not licensed as commercial banks or designated foreign-exchange banks in mainland China; they cannot operate as the primary cross-border settlement and clearing rail for a PRC-incorporated joint venture. PBOC and SAFE require all foreign-exchange receipts and payments for current and capital account transactions to be processed through designated domestic banks with underlying transaction verification and SAFE reporting. EMIs may be used only as supplementary customer-facing collection interfaces for digital stock-photo subscriptions and royalties, with funds ultimately settled into a domestic bank account in RMB and reconciled against platform merchant statements. Using an offshore EMI to route capital injections, profit distributions, or export-proceeds repatriation would violate SAFE regulations and cannot be recommended as a compliant treasury solution. Core liquidity and FX clearing must remain with tier-1 domestic commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must remit the equity investment capital via SWIFT MT103 with the correct SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contributions (aligned with BOP code 622 for FDI capital injection) and must clearly reference the beneficiary as the joint venture's capital account at the designated domestic commercial bank. Prior to remittance, the JV must complete the SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration and obtain the FX Registration Certificate or electronic registration record from the local SAFE or delegated bank. Upon receipt, the domestic bank will credit the capital account, verify the remittance narrative matches the registration, and record the capital settlement. The JV may then apply for RMB settlement of the foreign currency using the real-need mechanism, providing contracts, invoices, tax declarations, and a board-approved fund-use plan. A PRC-registered certified public accountant must thereafter issue a capital verification report confirming the subscribed capital has been actually paid. All documents must be retained for SAFE post-supervision and annual reporting; profit repatriation later will require an additional tax clearance and dividend remittance purpose code.For outbound disbursements to foreign photographers, AI model licensors, and content contributors, the JV should use SWIFT MT103 payments with the SAFE service-trade purpose code corresponding to royalties, licensing fees, or professional service fees. Each remittance must be supported by the underlying service agreement, invoices, tax withholding certificate, proof of tax payment, and a transaction authenticity statement submitted through the bank's electronic verification system. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not appropriate clearing rails for purely digital stock-photo asset purchases; those instruments should be reserved for physical goods procurement if the JV later acquires hardware, servers, or other tangible items. EMIs may be used only as supplementary customer-facing collection interfaces for digital stock-photo subscriptions and royalties, with funds ultimately settled into a domestic bank account in RMB and reconciled against platform merchant statements. Using an offshore EMI to route capital injections, profit distributions, or export-proceeds repatriation would violate SAFE regulations and cannot be recommended as a compliant treasury solution.
