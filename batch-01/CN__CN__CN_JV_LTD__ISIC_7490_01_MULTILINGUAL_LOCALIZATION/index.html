Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under the 2023 Company Law, there is no countrywide statutory minimum registered capital for a limited liability company or a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. The shareholders determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association. The full subscribed capital must be contributed within five years from the date of establishment, unless the Articles or applicable laws prescribe a shorter period. No blocked escrow account requirement is imposed by statute; instead, the mandatory foreign exchange capital account and the SAFE foreign direct investment registration serve as the capital monitoring mechanism. Contributions made in cash or non-cash property must be supported by evidence and, where required by the Articles, verified by a licensed accountant.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform, accessible through the national tax service portal (chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

After the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license for the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the license constitutes the enterprise's Tax Identification Number (TIN); tax registration information is automatically synchronized to the STA via the SAMR/STA data-sharing protocol. The JV must, within 30 days of license issuance, log onto the e-Tax platform to confirm tax registration details, select VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale), submit the company seal, finance and accounting institution appointment certificate, legal representative identification, lease agreement, and basic bank deposit account information. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a China permanent establishment, a non-resident tax registration may be filed through e-Tax using the tax residence certificate issued by the home jurisdiction and a designated Chinese tax agent; the STA then assigns a non-resident taxpayer code for any mandatory VAT or withholding obligations if a China tax nexus exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration arises when annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan); however, any enterprise with a China business establishment must complete basic tax registration regardless of turnover. Translation and media dubbing services are subject to the 6% VAT service category, while the 13% headline rate applies to goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer may claim 0% VAT on exported goods and qualifying cross-border services. For professional multilingual translation and media dubbing services, zero-rating applies when: (i) the services are supplied to a recipient residing outside mainland China; (ii) the services are performed in China or delivered cross-border to the overseas client; (iii) the consideration is received from overseas in foreign currency or via an eligible cross-border payment rail; and (iv) the invoice, contract, customs (if any), and foreign exchange documentary trail is complete and auditable. The zero-rating claim is filed via the periodic VAT return in e-Tax and is processed under the VAT export rebate/exemption system.

China mandates full digital e-invoicing under the STA's national fiscal control platform (Golden Tax Project). VAT invoices must be generated through the taxpayer's authorised e-invoice API on the e-Tax portal, with invoice data transmitted to the STA database in real time. The fully digital e-invoice embeds an anti-fraud QR code and a fiscal stamp; certain transactions require pre-audit clearance before issuance. Taxpayers must synchronise invoicing data with customs export declarations and bank foreign exchange records to support export zero-rating claims.

The STA performs targeted audits on export zero-rating and refund claims, cross-checking customs declarations, bank foreign exchange receipts, contracts, and the e-invoice database. Failure to substantiate the export within 180 days, or any inconsistency in the foreign exchange repatriation evidence, triggers immediate cancellation of zero-rating, recovery of unpaid VAT, daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05%, a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax underpaid, and potential criminal prosecution for falsified export documentation or tax refund fraud. The taxpayer may also be blacklisted and subjected to enhanced future compliance scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's current account convertibility framework, the RMB is freely convertible for trade and service transactions, subject to documentary verification by banks. Capital account transactions remain strictly controlled, requiring case-by-case approval and registration with SAFE. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (EJV), foreign shareholders' capital injections must be registered under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) system, and funds held in a capital escrow account can only be used for approved business purposes after bank-supervised conversion. Profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and proof of compliance with foreign exchange regulations. The EJV must also comply with the negative list for market access, and any cross-border capital movement must be conducted through designated domestic banks. The real-demand principle underpins all FX transactions, meaning hedging and outward remittances must be supported by genuine commercial or financial exposures. In practice, the mandatory settlement of export proceeds has been abolished since 2012, but foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China and properly reported to SAFE within the statutory time frame. Non-resident shareholders may receive dividends in foreign currency after fulfilling all tax obligations, but the conversion and remittance process involves rigorous compliance verification. Overall, RMB is convertible for operational liquidity but not for speculative capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a statutory surrender ratio on export proceeds. The EJV must repatriate all foreign currency receivables from current account transactions to China within 180 days from the date of shipment or service completion (or earlier if the underlying contract specifies). Once received, funds may be retained in a domestic foreign currency account or converted to RMB at the bank's settlement rate, at the enterprise's discretion, subject to submission of accurate trade background documents to the bank and the SAFE monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated FX regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise and Payoneer do not hold Chinese banking licenses and are legally barred from opening local clearing accounts for onshore entities. While they can be used for receiving cross-border payments from overseas clients into an intermediary account, the EJV's statutory treasury operations must be handled by a licensed domestic commercial bank. Any direct settlement or custody of corporate funds through these EMIs would breach SAFE's foreign exchange regulations and the PBOC's payment institution rules. Therefore, the viability of using fintech EMIs as the primary banking layer is Low; they may serve only as a secondary collection conduit where funds are aggregated and onward transmitted to a domestic bank account via formal bank wires. The EJV must maintain a compliant domestic banking relationship for all regulatory reporting and capital controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 messages to the JV's capital escrow account at a licensed domestic bank, with the payment purpose coded 'Deposit of Foreign Equity Capital' or similar SAFE-recognized code. Prior to the transfer, the JV must complete FDI registration with the local SAFE branch and obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate. The bank will verify the RBI (Registration-Based Information) and issue a payment integration number. Upon receipt, the bank performs capital verification against the authenticated audited financial report. The funds are then credited to the capital escrow account, and conversion to RMB for operational use is settled through the bank's foreign exchange settlement system, subject to a post-transaction authenticity check on the underlying transaction file.For service trade settlements (translation, dubbing, and localization services), the designated clearing rails are telegraphic transfer (SWIFT MT103) through the domestic bank, with the remittance purpose coded as 'International Service Charge' or 'Royalty and License Fee' depending on the nature. Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are generally not used for services, but if the contract warrants, a standby LC (SBLC) may be used as performance guarantee. All outbound payments require the presentation of signed contracts, commercial invoices, proof of service delivery, tax withholding certificates (where applicable), and SAFE's e-tax sharing receipt. When the value exceeds a local threshold (e.g., USD 50,000 equivalent), the bank may escalate for further anti-fraud and AML review. The bank must comply with the PBOC's Regulations on the Foreign Exchange Administration of Service Trade, to ensure authenticity of the transaction.Bank-served RMB/FX forward contracts (FX forwards) under the real-demand principle, with tenors up to 12 months and requiring proof of underlying trade exposure.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for long-term foreign currency liabilities such as loans or bonds, approved on a case-by-case basis by the bank.Plain-vanilla FX options purchased from domestic banks (no short option positions allowed) for protection against adverse RMB rate movements.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for onshore corporate entities and are only available to offshore counterparties; therefore excluded from the JV's permissible list.

Therefore, the viability of using fintech EMIs as the primary banking layer is Low; they may serve only as a secondary collection conduit where funds are aggregated and onward transmitted to a domestic bank account via formal bank wires. The EJV must maintain a compliant domestic banking relationship for all regulatory reporting and capital controls.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold triggered by related-party tangible property transfers above RMB 200 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; intangible property transfers above RMB 100 million; related-party service transactions above RMB 100 million; Master File required when aggregate controlled transactions exceed RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report required when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations but enforces the substance-over-form principle and the statutory General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. For localization services, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be performed by employees and assets in China, including translation management, audio/video editing, quality control, contract negotiation, invoicing and assumption of economic risk. A shell company or conduit interposed solely to claim treaty advantages, shift profit or obtain tax incentives will be recharacterized, ignored for tax purposes and made subject to assessed liabilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Revenue and Cost Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax, levies daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the Criminal Law where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, carrying up to three years imprisonment; amounts above RMB 1 million with a high evasion proportion carry three to seven years and a fine.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders can be personally assessed when the corporate veil is pierced due to commingled assets, false records or fraud. Civil restitution requires directors to repay diverted profits and corporate debts with personal assets. Tax authorities may list the legal representative as an 'serious untrustworthy taxpayer' (blacklisting), impose an exit travel ban, detach the director at border control, and criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and monetary penalties.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration penalizes the withholding agent for failing to withhold or failing to pay the withheld WHT, requiring payment of the tax plus daily surcharge and a fine of 50% to 300% of the WHT understated. Articles 37 and 40 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law confirm the non-resident's direct tax liability if the payer failed to withhold. Repeated non-compliance can escalate into criminal tax evasion charges against responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil liability if the dividend was secretly paid to connected persons and the corporate fund distributions are recharacterized as loans or compensation. The corporate veil is pierced when the JV is treated as the shareholder's alter ego, leaving directors personally liable for the unpaid WHT and civil damages. The legal representative is placed on tax enforcement blacklists, subject to bank account freezes, asset preservation, and a travel ban preventing departure from China until the tax is paid.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / AML-related Pass-Through Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule case law, the tax authority is empowered to disregard the intermediary company, re-characterize the dividend as paid directly to the ultimate non-resident shareholder, impose the full 10% or higher WHT, and assess interest and late-payment surcharge. Transfer pricing special tax adjustments may impose a penalty of up to 100% of the adjusted tax under Article 122 of the EIT Law Implementation Regulations. Unusual cross-border pass-through fund flows without economic substance also trigger anti-money laundering reporting obligations and potential fines under the AML regime.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the JV and the shell company who design or implement the artificial conduit can be jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax understatement and restitution of corporate assets. They face professional and economic sanctions including the corporate tax dishonesty blacklist, restriction of departure from China, freezing of personal bank accounts, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion, fraud, or money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191 with applicable imprisonment terms of five to ten years for serious money-laundering schemes.
