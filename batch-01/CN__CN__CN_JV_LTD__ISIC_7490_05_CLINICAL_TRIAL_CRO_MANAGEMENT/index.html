Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None: PRC law applies a subscribed capital system with no mandatory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary limited liability company. The shareholders must, however, specify a capital contribution schedule in the Articles of Association, and each subscription tranche must be deposited into a dedicated capital contribution settlement account opened at a licensed commercial bank. Such funds may not be used for operating expenses until the statutory contribution verification is completed and the bank has issued the capital contribution receipt. For a clinical-trial contract research organization, no additional paid-up capital floor is imposed under the Drug Administration Law, but the entity must maintain adequate financial and professional capability to satisfy Good Clinical Practice obligations.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no Civil Defense fire permit is required for a pure management office; however, the registered office must hold a valid building occupancy certificate and pass a routine fire safety inspection for office use, and the employer must implement basic occupational safety measures under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China, including fire extinguishers, emergency exits, first-aid kits, and employee safety training records. If the CRO later operates a clinical biospecimen repository, it must obtain the applicable biosecurity, medical waste, and fire protection approvals.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online taxpayer services are administered through the Electronic Tax Bureau (eTax) portal and the China Tax Service (12366) platform.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture automatically receives a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all Chinese tax purposes. The tax registration is completed through the automatic data exchange between the SAMR and the STA, normally within 30 days after issuance of the business license; no separate TIN application is required for a resident enterprise. If a foreign non-resident entity has no permanent establishment in China but derives China-source income, it must undergo non-resident tax registration with the local tax authority at the location of the withholding agent or the place of performance, submitting its articles of incorporation, proof of legal existence, tax residence certificate, and board resolution appointing a local tax contact or withholding agent where necessary. The completed registration yields an electronic taxpayer profile, VAT classification status, and access to the eTax portal for filing and compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete VAT tax registration upon incorporation. Registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed Renminbi (CNY) 5 million; an enterprise below that threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status to claim input VAT credits or to satisfy client invoice requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Chinese-resident taxpayer may apply the 0% VAT rate on exported goods if the goods are physically exported through a licensed customs port and the customs export data is transmitted electronically to the STA. For cross-border services, including clinical trial management and CRO services, zero-rating is available only if the service is supplied to a recipient established outside China, the service is performed wholly or predominantly outside China or is otherwise consumed outside China, no taxable benefit is enjoyed within China, and the payment is received in a permitted foreign currency and repatriated to China through formal banking channels. The specific statutory criteria follow the STA cross-border taxable services zero-rating and exemption rules and the VAT reform pilot measures.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Export Customs Declaration form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank collection record issued under SAFE foreign exchange repatriation rules
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or cross-border service agreement
  • Clinical trial service acceptance or completion certificate issued by the overseas sponsor
  • Evidence of non-resident status of the overseas recipient, including tax residence certificate or registration documents
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform

China operates a real-time VAT invoice clearance environment under the Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform, using the tax control equipment or Tax UKey authorized by the STA. Anti-fraud protection is embedded by means of automatically assigned invoice numbers, encrypted invoice codes, and mandatory transmission of issuance data to the national tax information system in near real time. Buyers must verify and authenticate digital invoices through the national VAT invoice monitoring platform before using them for input VAT credit, and any invoice not cleared through the tax authority cannot be legally deducted.

The STA may initiate retrospective audit review of export zero-rating claims within the legal statute of limitations. If the supporting customs declaration, bill of lading, service completion evidence, or foreign exchange repatriation records are found incomplete, inconsistent, or fabricated, the claimed zero-rate treatment is denied, refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory interest, and a penalty ranging from 50% to five times the tax evaded is imposed. Serious or organized fraud involving false export declarations may lead to suspension of export tax refund eligibility for up to 36 months and criminal referral for tax evasion or fraudulent export refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China follows a managed floating exchange rate regime under PBOC and SAFE. Current account items, including service fee revenue from clinical trial outsourcing, are generally freely convertible once the underlying trade is validated and all tax obligations are settled. Capital account items such as foreign direct investment are subject to pre-registration with SAFE, and the injection must pass through a designated foreign exchange capital account before conversion into RMB. There is no blanket restriction on profit remittance for a Sino-foreign equity joint venture, but dividends can only be remitted after an annual audit, a board resolution declaring distribution, and corporate income tax clearance. The entity is not subject to mandatory surrender of export proceeds; however, all current account receipts must be repatriated to China and properly reconciled. The balance-of-payments verification system monitors each international receipt and payment to ensure no unauthorised capital flight. In practice, banks act as delegated agents for SAFE, requiring underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations where applicable, and tax payment receipts before allowing conversion or outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE reform, the mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds is 0%; the entity may retain foreign currency in a settlement account or convert it to RMB at any licensed domestic bank at the market rate. All service trade receipts, including clinical trial CRO milestone fees, must be repatriated into the mainland banking system and reconciled against the SAFE service trade registry. For physical goods exports, if any are added later, the statutory collection verification window is 90 days from the date of customs declaration; delayed receipts require a concurrent SAFE extended-collection registration before the bank will process the debit.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Overseas digital EMIs are not licensed as domestic commercial banks and are legally barred from servicing a mainland China entity as its primary banking gateway. They cannot hold FDI capital, conduct SAFE capital verification, or support statutory forex filings. Their only permissible use is as a supplementary outbound payment channel for low-value operational expenses, always funded by RMB converted through the licensed domestic bank account, and never used for capital account transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore shareholder must first complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration through a designated bank. The bank then opens a foreign exchange capital account in the JV name. Inbound funding flows via SWIFT MT103 with the remitter's identity and reference exactly matching the FDI registration; the bank assigns the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code 622011 for the capital contribution. Upon arrival, funds are credited to the capital escrow account and cannot be used until a licensed Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report. Only after that report, and after completing the SAFE post-registration filing, can the JV convert the foreign currency to RMB and disburse operational cash. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for a tax year in which the JV's related-party transactions reach any of the following thresholds: tangible-property transfers of RMB 200 million; intangible-property transfer of RMB 50 million; financial-asset transfer of RMB 100 million; related-party services of RMB 100 million; or other intercompany transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File is required where the taxpayer's aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China enforces a robust economic-substance doctrine through EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), SAT Circular 42 (TP documentation), and BEPS minimum standards. For a Sino-foreign JV engaged in ISIC 7490 clinical-trial CRO management, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include protocol design and audit, clinical trial site selection, principal-investigator contracting, patient enrollment and monitoring, medical data analysis, regulatory submission, risk insurance and CRO executive management. All of these CIGA must be actually performed by employees located in China and evidenced by time sheets, trial master files, insurance certificates, monitoring visit reports and board minutes. A mere billing/booking company with no local substance, no qualified staff and no physical site will have its legal form disregarded and its taxable profits re-attributed to the substantive foreign shareholder. Likewise, if a foreign affiliated contract research organization is inserted to disclaim China operational substance, the SAT will re-characterize the arrangement, impose a secondary transfer-pricing adjustment (imputed dividend or interest) and deny treaty benefits on any resulting cash flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via falsified R&D super deduction and inflated CRO expenses

Statutory sanction: Violates EIT Law Article 30 and Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63; the tax authority will disallow the fraudulent deduction, recover the underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late-filing surcharge, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Criminal referral under Criminal Law Article 201 arises where the tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and the evaded amount exceeds 10% of the taxable income; criminal fines range from 1 to 5 times the tax evaded and imprisonment ranges from 3 years to 7 years for serious amounts.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where directors use the company form to evade tax obligations, causing impairment of State revenue: directors are personally and jointly liable for the civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties. The legal representative and finance director will be blacklisted in the SAT's 'Major Tax Violation and Sanction' list, travel bans (exit-denial) are enforced, personal assets freeze, and if the criminal threshold is crossed, the responsible officer personally faces criminal imprisonment and disqualification as a director or senior manager of any Chinese company.
Treaty Shopping and Shell-Corporation Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Violates EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9 (beneficial ownership rules); the tax authority disregards the shell, imposes the full 10% statutory WHT on distributions, exacts a late-payment surcharge from the original dividend date, and adds a penalty up to 5 times the unpaid tax against the withholding agent (either the JV or its responsible person). Under DTA anti-abuse rules and the principal purpose test, the claim for treaty benefits is denied.

Director personal liability: The JV's directors, by signing the dividend resolution without the requisite TRC or beneficial-ownership file, become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties; they are placed on the tax blacklist and denied exit clearance. Where the arrangement is wilful and conceals income of the real shareholder, the de facto controller may be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion with imprisonment up to 7 years and forfeiture of illicit offshore assets; civil liability extends to the payment of restitution out of personal assets for any shortfall in corporate distribution.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Failure to Prove Economic Substance in Clinical Trial CRO

Statutory sanction: Violates EIT Law Articles 41, 43, 44 and 47; the State Tax Administration will perform a primary transfer-pricing adjustment based on an arm's-length functional and economic analysis, impose a secondary adjustment treating the revised profit as a deemed distribution/dividend subject to 10% WHT or deemed interest, add a daily 0.05% late surcharge and impose a 50% to 500% penalty for underpayment; failure to maintain required documentation alone triggers a RMB 10,000 penalty and adverse inference.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false consolidated financial statements or TP endorsements can be held personally liable for the JV's tax deficiency under company law and the piercing doctrine where the company becomes insolvent due to understated income. They are listed on the tax authorities' serious-violation blacklist with travel limitations, and their personal bank accounts, equity, and real estate may be seized for restitution of the additional tax. If the TP fraud is part of a scheme to evade over RMB 50,000 in tax and qualifies as intentional, the responsible director is subject to criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years plus civil disgorgement.
