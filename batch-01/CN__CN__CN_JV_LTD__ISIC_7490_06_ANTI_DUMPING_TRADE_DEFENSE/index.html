Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None; under the PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law, no statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in ISIC 7490 consulting; shareholders determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association and pay it according to an agreed capital contribution schedule. No blocked escrow account is required by law, but contribution funds received in China must be held in a bank account at a commercial bank licensed by the People's Bank of China and reported through the foreign investment information reporting system.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for industrial or hazardous occupancy; the leased office must maintain valid fire safety inspection and fire prevention clearance under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China and comply with workplace safety and occupational health obligations under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China; no Civil Defense industrial fire permit, structural load inspection, or hazardous materials clearance is triggered for pure consulting use.

Paid-in capital rule: None; under the PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law, no statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in ISIC 7490 consulting; shareholders determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association and pay it according to an agreed capital contribution schedule. No blocked escrow account is required by law, but contribution funds received in China must be held in a bank account at a commercial bank licensed by the People's Bank of China and reported through the foreign investment information reporting system.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) with its nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online portal accessible through www.chinatax.gov.cn; domestic administration is handled by the county-level tax service bureaus under the STA via the unified e-Tax platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture formed in China (Mainland), the entity will first complete business registration with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). The AMR issues a business license containing the Uniform Social Credit Code, which is the legal taxpayer identification number. The JV must then complete tax registration through the STA e-Tax portal within 30 days of business registration. The procurement procedure for a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment is separate: the non-resident must apply for a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) with the STA through the withholding agent or directly under the Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration procedure, supplying the enterprise incorporation certificate, foreign legal evidence, and a China contact person or fiscal representative. For the JV, the Unified Social Credit Code will be the sole TIN used for all State Tax Administration filings, VAT registration, export tax refunds, payroll/social insurance contributions, and anti-dumping trade-defense invoice issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box election, partnership pass-through election, or hybrid-mismatch election. Enterprise tax classification is dictated by the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income. The only relevant elections relate to special tax treatment for qualified asset restructuring under STA notices; those are not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (5 million Renminbi) creates mandatory status as a VAT general taxpayer. Below this threshold a VAT taxpayer is treated as a small-scale taxpayer, but may elect to register as a general taxpayer. All enterprises that obtain a business license are automatically tax-registered regardless of the 5-million threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT and the corresponding refund of input tax are available if the goods are customarily declared for export, leave China through a customs supervised area, and the export proceeds are repatriated in accordance with foreign-exchange regulations. For services, the trade-defense and legal-consulting services qualify for 0% VAT only when supplied to an overseas client, the recipient is located outside China, the services are wholly consumed outside China, and full documentation proves the cross-border supply. Anti-dumping trade-defense consulting services involving representation before Chinese authorities may not qualify for zero-rating where the place of supply is deemed domestic; only qualifying cross-border offshore services satisfy STA zero-rating criteria.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration form) containing the export customs supervision number, HS classification, export quantity, value, exporter name, and consignee details
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing the export goods have been loaded and shipped from China to the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt/remittance record proving the export proceeds were received in convertible currency from the overseas buyer through the Chinese banking system
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice showing the parties, terms of trade, pricing, and confirmation that the export sale is not a related-party sham transaction
  • Packing list and verified certificate of origin concerning the exported goods
  • Export goods VAT invoice or the digital electronic invoice issued for the export transaction
  • Customs agent power of attorney and customs release record, when clearance is undertaken via a customs broker

China enforces mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance under the STA's Golden Tax System Phase IV. Invoicing must be done through the e-Tax platform or an authorized third-party electronic invoice service provider integrated by API. VAT invoices are the Full-Digital Electronic Invoice (All-Electronic Invoice) model; issuance sends invoice data in real time to the STA's tax control system, where the invoice is assigned a unique tax-control code, digitally signed, stamped, and QR-monitored. No physical stamped red invoices are permitted for trade-defense consulting and export transactions; digital anti-fraud is achieved by automatic code control, real-time invoice data reconciliation, purchaser verification, and mandatory electronic invoice archive.

Export zero-rating and refund claims face rigorous audit scrutiny by the STA and Customs. If the tax authority determines that zero-rating was wrongly claimed due to insufficient, false, or contradictory documents, or if the export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, the entire VAT output tax deemed zero-rated is clawed back. For fraudulent refund claims, the Tax Administration Law imposes recovery of the unpaid tax plus penalties from 50% to five times the amount involved; late payment interest accrues and tax credits are disallowed. In criminal cases, the Criminal Law punishes export tax refund fraud by imprisonment of up to life plus fines. Administrative penalties also include temporary or permanent loss of export tax refund qualification, third-party liability for tax agents, and reassessment procedures without a limitation period for fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains full convertibility on the current account for trade in goods and services, but the capital account remains administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). RMB is not freely convertible on demand; every inbound and outbound flow must be supported by an underlying real transaction and, where required, a SAFE registration or bank-level verification. A Sino-foreign equity joint venture's injected capital is treated as capital-account income; it must be registered with SAFE under the foreign direct investment (FDI) registration process and may not be credited to any account other than the dedicated foreign-exchange capital account. Once the registered capital is settled into RMB and spent on operations, the JV may not arbitrarily repatriate the funds to offshore investors or redeploy them outside the approved business scope. Profit remittance to non-resident shareholders is classified under the current account as dividend distribution and is permitted only after audited financial statements, board resolution, tax withholding certificate, and tax clearance are submitted to the settlement bank. Service fees from anti-dumping trade-defense consulting are current-account receipts; the JV may retain such FX in its export settlement account or convert it at market rates for working capital. Practical compliance hurdles include SAFE's true-transaction review, bank-level documentation collection, and the absence of unrestricted access to offshore non-deliverable RMB products.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not impose a fixed mandatory surrender percentage or a fixed conversion ratio for export receipts. Under the current total-amount monitoring model, all export service receipts must be repatriated through the domestic banking system; the enterprise may retain 100% of the foreign exchange in its export settlement account or convert at market rates. The statutory verification framework is the SAFE Administrative Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Goods and Services Trade (2012, as amended); in bank practice, the receipt must be matched to the filed service-transaction declaration within the bank's monitoring period, typically 90 days from the contractual payment due date. If the bank flags an unmatched receipt, the entity must supply the original service contract, invoice, evidence of service delivery, and the export-service tax status, or the bank will suspend future cross-border payments. There is no statutory conversion quota for this category, but every settlement must be reflected in SAFE statistics.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign-exchange regulatory agency administered by the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and largely non-viable for onshore entity formation. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including foreign-licensed money-service businesses or e-money institutions, are not permitted to act as the domestic bank or hold the JV's registered capital onshore because the PBOC and SAFE recognize only licensed mainland banking institutions for foreign-exchange and RMB clearing. Domestic clients cannot lawfully maintain their operating account with an unlicensed offshore EMI; foreign-currency collection through such a platform is only permissible if the platform has a licensed mainland payment affiliate and operates under a PBOC payment license, and even then it cannot replace the direct bank relationship needed for SAFE reporting, tax matching, and capital verification. Use tier-1 banks only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 50 million for intercompany loans, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; a Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion; contemporaneous transactional documentation must be filed with the annual corporate income tax return unless exempt by the annual total threshold.

Economic substance scope: China has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations, but the State Taxation Administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine, the beneficial ownership test, and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, through which a transaction lacking reasonable commercial substance may be recharacterized; the JV must perform Core Income-Generating Activities in China, including operational decisions, client contracting, service delivery, and invoicing, and cannot outsource the substance to a shell affiliate in a zero-tax jurisdiction or in a treaty jurisdiction solely to obtain withholding benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Articles 118-120 of its Implementing Regulations authorize upward recharacterization and special tax adjustment; Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; failure to maintain usable transfer pricing documentation may result in a fine of RMB 10,000 plus interest and daily late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Article 20 of the Company Law if abuse of the legal person causes injury to the State's revenue claim; directors may become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; aggravated tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law carries imprisonment of three to seven years, criminal fines, asset confiscation, a judicial travel ban, and blacklisting by the tax authority.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and STA Public Notice 2015 No. 35 authorize denial of treaty benefits and recharacterization of the conduit as an agent of the true beneficial owner; the payer must then withhold at the statutory 10% rate, plus interest and a 50%-500% penalty under the Tax Collection Administration Law; the intermediary may also be administratively dissolved.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations or knowingly approve sham arrangements face civil restitution of the unpaid withholding tax; the sham shell treatment permits piercing of the corporate veil, making directors personally liable; criminal exposure includes tax evasion under Article 201 and money laundering under Article 191 of the Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment of up to ten years, confiscation of personal property, a travel ban, and administrative blacklisting.
AML / False Invoicing / Anti-Dumping Trade Defense Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law treats this as tax evasion and imposes a penalty of 50%-500% of the amount underpaid; Article 205 of the Criminal Law punishes the false issuance of special VAT invoices with severe fines and imprisonment; the Anti-Dumping Regulations and Foreign Trade Law authorize retroactive collection of antidumping duties, fines, and revocation of operating licenses for aiding circumvention.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize, sign, or profit from false invoices are subject to criminal liability under Article 205, with punishments including life imprisonment in the gravest cases, unlimited fines, confiscation of personal assets, and disqualification from managerial positions; piercing the corporate veil permits courts to order directors to restore diverted assets and pay the JV's unpaid tax liabilities, and the authorities may impose a travel ban and blacklist the directors before commencement of criminal proceedings.
