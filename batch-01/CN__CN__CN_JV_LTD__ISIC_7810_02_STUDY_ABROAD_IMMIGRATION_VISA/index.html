Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from China and to qualifying cross-border services supplied by a Chinese resident enterprise to an overseas recipient where the service is consumed entirely outside China. For ISIC 7810 study abroad, immigration and visa advisory services, zero-rating is substantiated when the contractual counterparty is a non-resident client, the service is supplied to and used by a foreign student or foreign applicant in a destination jurisdiction, the consideration is received from abroad, and the advisory activity is documented by overseas university placement records or foreign immigration authority outcomes. The taxpayer must file the zero-rated turnover in the VAT return and submit the contract, foreign exchange receipt, overseas evidence and zero-rated VAT invoice through the e-tax workflow for statutory verification.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form for any physical goods export (a service-only export does not require customs goods declaration but must maintain the equivalent cross-border service documentation)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill where documents or incidental materials are shipped
  • Bank-generated inward remittance certificate / Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation issued by the designated bank, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), showing receipt amount matching the contract and the zero-rated invoice value
  • Signed cross-border service agreement with the overseas commissioning client, including payment and jurisdiction clauses
  • 0% rated VAT invoice (fapiao) issued through the STA digital electronic invoice system with the zero-rate indicator
  • Overseas consumption evidence for study abroad and immigration advisory services, including university offer letters, enrollment confirmations, visa approval letters, destination-country residence permits and immigration case records
  • Residency documentation of the foreign client, such as passport copy, overseas tax residency certificate or a foreign utility/address record, proving non-PRC service consumption

The JV must enroll in the State Taxation Administration Digital Electronic Invoice system; all VAT invoices must be issued under STA-controlled serial numbers, carry the national unified invoice verification code and anti-fraud digital stamp, and be transmitted in real time to the STA Golden Tax monitoring system. General VAT taxpayers issuing special VAT invoices must maintain the prescribed invoice equipment or API clearance connection, and each special invoice must be reported and validated through the e-tax platform before the buyer can claim input VAT credit; fapiao issuance without genuine commercial substance is prohibited as anti-fraud enforcement.

The STA performs data-matching audits between VAT returns, zero-rated invoice ledgers, customs records and bank foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is disallowed due to missing overseas consumption, false documentation, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the statutory period, the tax authority re-characterizes the supply at the standard service rate of 6%, collects the under-declared VAT, imposes daily late-payment interest from the original filing date, and applies an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Tax Administration Law. Fraudulent export zero-rating or invoice chains attract retrospective clawbacks, tax-credit blacklisting, license suspension, and criminal liability for tax fraud, invoice fraud, or foreign exchange evasion; recovery periods extend up to five years in ordinary underpayment cases and are unlimited in fraud or evasion cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account. China satisfies current-account convertibility for bona fide trade, services, and certain transfers, but inbound equity or loan capital can only enter after SAFE registration and through a designated bank account. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must complete Ministry of Commerce filing or approval, obtain the foreign-investment certificate or record, deposit the contributed capital in an FDI capital account, and settle the foreign currency into RMB only after the commercial bank has performed a genuinity check on the underlying contract, transaction purpose, and corporate ownership records. Profit and dividend remittance to foreign shareholders is treated as a current-account item after corporate income tax is settled; the remitting bank will demand audited financial statements, a tax-payment certificate, board resolution, sponsor letter, and an FX payment application with a proper SAFE transaction code. In practice, remittance can be delayed by tax clearance inspection, transfer-pricing documentation, or discrepancy between the visa-advisory billing scope and the actual services rendered. Therefore, medium-term liquidity planning must respect a clearing cycle of 4-8 weeks, and capital repatriation must never be assumed on-demand.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-exchange proceeds received by the JV must be repatriated to China through a designated foreign-exchange bank within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless a SAFE-approved offshore retention facility is in place. Upon collection, the entity may either retain the proceeds in an export-receipts foreign-currency account or voluntarily settle them into RMB; no fixed percentage is currently mandated by statute for conversion, but the bank will verify the export declaration, invoices, packing list, logistics record, and receipt maturity to prevent circular trade or fake-export fraud.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the implementing authority for foreign-exchange regulations

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for onshore Chinese treasury operations. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex lack a PBOC banking licence and cannot lawfully provide CNY settlement, SAFE reporting, FDI capital verification, or statutory audit-ready account statements inside mainland China. The JV may use such platforms only for incidental offshore marketing spend or to pay non-China vendors in a low-value current-account trail, but the primary capital injection, escrow, and profit-repatriation rails must run through a licensed domestic tier-1 commercial bank; otherwise the SAFE and PBOC supervision will deem the transaction unverifiable and the JV could face foreign-exchange violation fines and account freezing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections from the foreign shareholder must be remitted through high-quality SWIFT MT103 messages into the FDI Capital Account of the JV with a designated tier-1 bank; each remittance must carry a SAFE-compliant purpose code for inbound direct-investment equity and reference the SAFE and Ministry of Commerce registration number. After receipt, the bank will confirm the source and perform RMB settlement only on the basis of the JV's approved capital usage plan; a Chinese certified public accountant must then issue a capital verification report. For operating liquidity, the JV should maintain a separate current-account RMB ledger and route any current-account consultancy fee inflow under the cross-border consultancy-services BoP code, never co-mingling with capital settle.Trade settlements and client-client obligations should be cleared by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 direct wires for urgent retainer refunds; by commercial documentary letters of credit for large, authenticated escrow obligations linked to universities or visa providers; and by documentary collections (documents against payment or acceptance) for lower-value, service-completion-based disbursements. Every outbound payment needs an underlying contract and invoice, proof of service delivery, supporting tax-payment or withholding certificate, and a bank-side SAFE export-payment reporting record; the KYC file must remain current before the payment order is released.Onshore RMB/FX plain-vanilla deliverable forwards are permissible only where the JV holds a genuine current-account or capital-account FX exposure evidenced by trade invoices, capital conversion schedules, or an approved foreign-debt contract with a bank-issued ISDA or NAFMII master agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as onshore instruments; they can be traded only in offshore CNH markets and must be segregated from the Chinese JV's onshore treasury books.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps and plain-vanilla FX options with an embedded bona fide exposure are permitted under SAFE risk-need testing, but speculative hedge-only trades without an underlying balance-sheet exposure are prohibited and will be treated as unlicensed cross-border derivatives.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for onshore Chinese treasury operations. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex lack a PBOC banking licence and cannot lawfully provide CNY settlement, SAFE reporting, FDI capital verification, or statutory audit-ready account statements inside mainland China. The JV may use such platforms only for incidental offshore marketing spend or to pay non-China vendors in a low-value current-account trail, but the primary capital injection, escrow, and profit-repatriation rails must run through a licensed domestic tier-1 commercial bank; otherwise the SAFE and PBOC supervision will deem the transaction unverifiable and the JV could face foreign-exchange violation fines and account freezing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders under EIT Law Articles 3 and 37; treaty reduction to 5% is common for a shareholder owning at least 25% of the distributing company under several Chinese DTAs (e.g. Mainland-Hong Kong arrangement, China-Singapore treaty); dividends paid to a resident Chinese corporate shareholder are tax-exempt under EIT Law Article 26.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: service-related party transactions at or above CNY 40,000,000 per year; intangible or financial asset transfers at or above CNY 100,000,000; tangible property transfers at or above CNY 200,000,000. Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions reach CNY 1,000,000,000 or when qualifying cross-border related-party transactions exceed CNY 200,000,000. Country-by-Country Reporting triggers at group consolidated revenue of CNY 5,500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations code, but EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), SAT Public Notice 42 of 2016 and OECD-aligned substance-over-form tests regulate economic substance. For a study-abroad/immigration-visa consultancy JV, Core Income-Generating Activities include client identification and contracting, due diligence on universities/embassies, preparation of visa applications, service delivery, risk assumption and post-placement compliance. Profit must be allocated to the entity performing functions and controlling risks; a shell subsidiary in Hong Kong or Cayman holding invoices without employees, premises, functions or decision-making will be disregarded and the income reattributed to the Chinese JV. Transactions with zero-tax non-resident affiliates are subject to rigorous beneficial ownership testing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Booking of Cross-Border Consulting Revenue

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63: tax authority recovers the underpaid tax and imposes a fine from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Criminal Law Article 201: imprisonment up to 3 years for a relatively large amount (≥ RMB 50,000 and >10% of tax payable), and up to 7 years for a huge amount (≥ RMB 250,000 and >30%). If fake VAT invoices are used, Criminal Law Article 205 imposes penalties up to life imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative and the chief financial officer who order, participate in or knowingly approve the false bookings are personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid taxes and losses; the tax authority lists them in the serious dishonest taxpayer blacklist, triggering travel and exit bans, restrictive asset transfers and banking penalties; criminal conviction may result in confiscation of personal assets and imprisonment.
Withholding Tax and Treaty Abuse on Outbound Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 69: a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is fined 50%-300% of the unwithheld tax; late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day under TCAL Article 32; EIT Law Article 37 imposes the final 10% WHT on outbound dividends. False treaty claims using forged certificates invoke criminal forgery penalties under Criminal Law Article 280.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the non-withholding or the abusive treaty claim become personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties if the JV cannot settle; company assets held by family trusts or relatives may be pierced and recovered; personal travel bans and border-exit control apply; a criminal conviction for tax avoidance or forgery leads to imprisonment and disqualification from holding director positions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Shell Company No-Substance

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 and SAT Notice 42 authorize the tax authority to make an arm's length adjustment; Implementation Regulations Articles 121-122 impose back interest equal to the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points on the additional tax; EIT Law Article 47 GAAR permits recharacterization of shell transactions; if the mispricing is reflected in fraudulent returns, TCAL Article 63 imposes a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve self-dealing related-party payments breach PRC Company Law Article 148 (duty of loyalty and care) and may be sued for personal restitution of company losses; the corporate veil may be pierced where assets were transferred to related parties or to the director's own accounts; personal assets can be frozen to satisfy tax-impairments; travel bans, blacklisting and criminal liability for fraudulent invoice issuance or embezzlement may follow.
