VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the mandatory turnover threshold for registration as a VAT general taxpayer; entities below this threshold may remain small-scale VAT taxpayers, but every entity engaging in taxable activities must complete tax registration upon establishment. For cross-border services paid to non-residents, the China customer acts as withholding agent and no local VAT registration threshold applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui (2016) No. 36 and subsequent STA announcements, a remote BPO call-center service supplied to an overseas client can be brought within the zero-rating or VAT-exemption regime if: (a) the service recipient is an entity or individual outside China; (b) the customer-helpdesk and outbound tele-sales services are performed from China but the service is wholly consumed outside the PRC customs territory; (c) the service is not supplied to a PRC domestic entity or individual; and (d) the JV holds complete service contracts, delivery evidence, call records, revenue recognition schedules, and foreign-exchange settlement vouchers. Where the service is classified as a zero-rated offshore outsourcing/information service, output VAT is 0 percent and related input VAT is refundable; if it is classified only as exempt cross-border service, output VAT is 0 percent but input VAT is not recoverable. The JV must make the cross-border taxable service exemption/zero-rating filing before issuing the zero-rated invoice and must support the claim with the documentary bundle below.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (China Customs export declaration document) - required only if physical goods, media, or equipment accompany the BPO service; pure remote services do not require a goods customs declaration.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - applicable to any physical delivery of pre-recorded call scripts, hardware, or other goods packages supporting the BPO contract.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher, including the SAFE service-trade foreign-exchange payment/income confirmation used to evidence receipt of the export service consideration in foreign exchange.
  • Signed cross-border service agreement, commercial invoice, delivery certificates, call center service logs, and overseas recipient identification performed by the JV to evidence that the service is provided to and consumed by an offshore counterparty.

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system through the STA electronic invoice platform. VAT invoices must be issued in the STA-approved electronic system; full digitalized electronic invoices are generated with a unique nationwide invoice number, transmitted in real time to STA servers, and reconciled through the National VAT Invoice Authentication Platform. There is no physical fiscal stamping; instead, invoice data is cleared through the STA API-based electronic invoice service platform, and large taxpayers may integrate their billing systems with the STA via standard API or file-based interfaces.

If the STA determines that the 0-percent VAT rate or exemption was applied without meeting the cross-border service conditions, it will reassess output VAT at the standard 6 percent rate, disallow input VAT credits, and charge late payment surcharge at 0.05 percent per day. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, the penalty can be 50 percent to 5 times the underpaid tax for false documentation; any fraudulent refund or false export VAT refund claim may trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, including fines and imprisonment. Foreign-exchange repatriation failures or false FIRCs may also trigger State Administration of Foreign Exchange penalties and a retrospective clawback of the zero-rating benefit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, reflecting China's adoption of IMF Article VIII obligations, but the capital account remains subject to a nexus of licensing, registration, negative-list, and prior-approval controls administered by SAFE and PBOC. A Sino-Foreign Equity JV is required to obtain an FDI registration certificate and to open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account through a designated domestic bank before any inbound equity contribution can be credited. Upon receipt, the foreign currency capital is placed into the capital settlement framework, under which RMB conversion is permitted only for genuine business expenses such as salary, rental, equipment, software, telecom bandwidth, and working-capital needs; conversion for speculative securities, entrusted loans, real estate beyond the negative list, and unauthorized intra-group lending is prohibited. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder is available as a current-account transaction once the annual corporate income tax return is settled, audited financial statements and a board resolution approving the dividend are produced, and the bank confirms that no unpaid SAFE or tax liabilities remain. For the BPO call-center exporter, service receipts are fully current-account eligible and may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB at market rates; however, the data-processing nature of the business triggers PIPL and Data Security Law compliance before cross-border settlement will be cleared by any lawful bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods export proceeds must be repatriated to China through a designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days of customs declaration; service-export proceeds from BPO/call-center activities have no fixed 180-day customs window but must be collected through a licensed bank and reported under SAFE's Current Account Trade Monitoring System. Since SAFE's 2012 reform, the mandatory surrender ratio is zero: the JV may retain 100% of export/service receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account, or convert to RMB at the market rate at its own discretion. There is no legal requirement to convert current-account FX receipts into RMB, nor to surrender a specified percentage.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, exercising delegated authority under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Council

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low for primary banking; Medium only as ancillary non-China payment subledger. A foreign fintech EMI cannot hold a domestic CNAPS RMB account for a mainland-resident JV, cannot open a SAFE-regulated foreign exchange capital account, and is not authorized under PBOC payment regulations to provide cross-border RMB settlement. Wise B2B or similar EMIs may be used by the foreign parent to disburse consultancy or subcontractor fees outside China, but all Chinese-source revenue must be repatriated to a licensed onshore bank and all capital injections must pass through the SAFE registration process. Using an EMI to bypass the designated FX bank would expose the JV to penalties under the Administration of Foreign Exchange Regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must remit the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's dedicated SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated bank such as Bank of China or ICBC. The remitter field must match the approved foreign shareholder exactly, and field 70 should state FDI Equity Contribution under the SAFE registration number. The bank will apply the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection in China. After the inward remittance is credited, a licensed Chinese CPA firm must issue a capital verification report using the bank confirmation and SAFE database check. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party tangible goods transactions exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible property or service transactions exceeding RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million in a single year trigger contemporaneous Local File preparation. A Master File is required when annual aggregate related-party intercompany transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or when the enterprise qualifies for exceptional documentation treatment under SAT Announcement No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China enforces economic substance and substance-over-form principles through the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Article 47 and the framework of beneficial ownership. For a remote BPO call center, the Chinese JV must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) within China, including locally managed human resources, employee training, IT infrastructure, telephony systems, compliance supervision, and strategic decisions. The foreign offshore affiliate should not be a mere booking vehicle passing revenues through without physical offices, personnel, and risk-bearing capacity. Chinese tax authorities may reallocate profits based on functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed, and may disregard unreal transactions that lack commercial rationality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Fabricated Intercompany Service Fees

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, the taxpayer must pay the underpaid tax and a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and be fined between 50% and 500% of the evaded tax. Article 41 of the CIT Law authorizes the tax authority to re-determine taxable income by ignoring the non-arm's-length arrangements. The foreign affiliate's receipts may be recharacterized as constructive royalties or dividends subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: The director of the Chinese JV who signed the fabricated service contracts may face personal joint and several liability under PRC Company Law Article 20 for abusing corporate personality, harming creditors and the tax authority. In serious cases, the director is subject to criminal liability under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law for tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines, plus seizure of personal assets, travel bans, and entry into the enforcement blacklist.
Shell-Company Dividend Stripping and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty benefits and apply the statutory 10% withholding rate on the entire gross distribution, impose late surcharges, and penalize the Chinese withholding agent 50%-300% of the under-withheld tax. The offshore shell may be deemed a conduit under the Limitation-on-Benefits clauses and the general anti-abuse rule, triggering a de facto 10% (or 20% if treated as arbitrary) WHT plus penalties for abuse.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling shareholders of the Chinese joint venture can be personally liable for malicious tax withholding failure, under PRC tax enforcement rules for serious violations. For egregious treaty abuse characterized as tax evasion, the person responsible can be imprisoned for up to 3 years or a greater term if involving a huge amount, and the blacklist measures restrict exit, business registration, and banking services.
Failure to File the Annual Return and Outbound Remittance Withholding

Statutory sanction: Late recovery demands: unpaid tax plus daily surcharge at 0.05%; fine under Article 62 of the Tax Collection Administration Law up to RMB 10,000 for failure to file, increasing to RMB 200,000 in serious cases. Under-collection of WHT triggers a fine of 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount; fraudulent failure to file is treated as concealment of taxable income under Article 63, creating criminal liability. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may additionally penalize illegal outward remittance with fines from RMB 200,000 up to RMB 500,000 or 5%-30% of the illegal amount.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Chinese entity, as directly responsible personnel in a unit crime, face criminal liability under Article 31 of the PRC Criminal Law and Article 201 if the under-declared amount is substantial; personal assets can be attached to satisfy the tax claims, the individual can be prohibited from leaving China under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and civil liability extends to restitution for the corporation's creditors under Company Law Article 20. The joint venture partner may also exercise a contractual right to remove and indemnify the defaulting director.
