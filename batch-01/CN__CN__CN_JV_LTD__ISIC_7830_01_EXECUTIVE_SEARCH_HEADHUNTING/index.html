Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No nationwide statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the 2023 Company Law. However, Article 47 requires the subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Association to be fully paid within five years from establishment. No blocked escrow account is mandated; subscribed capital must be credited to the JV's corporate bank account and may then be used for rent, payroll, statutory fees and administrative license costs strictly subject to board approval and lawful business purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the unified Electronic Tax Bureau platform (e-Tax) and local competent tax offices

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the enterprise obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation, and that USCC automatically constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID) for China. The mandatory registration sequence is: (1) complete the business-license registration with SAMR; (2) within 30 days present the licensed charter, articles of association, legal representative identification, registered-address proof and banking details to the local STA office through the e-Tax portal for tax registration confirmation; (3) receive the tax registration record and the Electronic Taxpayer identification number; (4) if the foreign non-resident investor or any non-resident counterparty has no Chinese permanent establishment, apply for a non-resident tax registration file via the e-Tax platform or at the local tax service hall, and provide a foreign tax residence certificate plus the foreign jurisdiction's TIN when seeking treaty withholding benefits; (5) for VAT purposes the non-resident is registered only where it makes taxable supplies through a Chinese VAT nexus; in other cases the Chinese JV or other buyer is required to withhold VAT and Enterprise Income Tax at source.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT taxable sales. Every enterprise must complete basic tax registration; annual taxable sales exceeding 5,000,000 RMB triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. Below that threshold the enterprise may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to the simplified levy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT implementing rules, cross-border taxable services provided by a general VAT taxpayer to an overseas entity or individual are zero-rated or VAT-exempt when the service is performed for and consumed entirely outside China. The 0% rate is expressly reserved for published categories such as international transportation, research and development, software, design and certain information technology services. Executive search and headhunting services are not automatically included in that zero-rating catalogue; they may nevertheless be eligible for zero-rated or exempt treatment where the contract demonstrates that the service recipient is non-resident, the search candidates are recruited globally, no performance or benefit occurs within mainland China, and the exporter supports the claim with a service contract, commercial invoice, proof of delivery/render of services, and bank foreign exchange receipt records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (PRC Customs export goods declaration, containing item description, classification and value)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment where goods are involved
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign exchange receipt certificate recorded in the SAFE monitoring system
  • Signed cross-border service agreement or sales contract
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the exporter

Under the Digital Electronic Invoice (DEI) reform, virtually all VAT invoices must be issued through the STA e-Tax invoice system. General taxpayers issue Special VAT Invoices under the VAT invoice management subsystem, and invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA. The tax authority performs automated system-side validation and data matching between issued invoices, VAT filings and counterparty registrations. A statutory electronic signature is embedded in the DEI; no separate legacy anti-fraud fiscal stamping device is required.

Any export zero-rating or refund is subject to ex-post STA audit and customs/SAFE data cross-checks. If substantiation is inadequate or documentation is found to be false, the STA will claw back the refunded VAT plus statutory interest and impose a fine of one to five times the amount of export tax refund fraudulently obtained. Criminal liability may also arise under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, including imprisonment and forfeiture of property. The enterprise may additionally be downgraded or disqualified from export-tax refund processing for a compliance period, and supporting records must be retained for at least five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a dual-tier exchange system. The renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in services such as cross-border executive search retainers, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and documented. However, capital account items remain strictly controlled under China's negative-list approval and registration framework. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (JV), inbound share capital injection must be registered with SAFE under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) regime, and the foreign currency must be parked in a dedicated capital account before being settled into renminbi for enumerated 'positive list' expenditures. Profit remittances to the foreign investor are permitted after annual statutory audits, a tax clearance certificate, and board approval, but there is no free convertibility or automatic repatriation absent documentary proof. Export proceeds and service fee revenues earned in foreign currency must be repatriated to China via a designated bank and either retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted to RMB under the 'receipt and settlement' verification system; mandatory full surrender was abolished years ago. In practice, SAFE real-time monitoring, bank-level authenticity review, and anti-avoidance rules on round-trip investment create meaningful compliance friction. The JV must maintain a clean BOP reporting trail, preserve original contracts, invoices, and customs declarations, and cannot rely on offshore structures to circumvent settlement or surrender obligations. The overall convertibility regime is therefore current-account-liberalized but capital-account-rationed, with profit remittance requiring audited accounts and full tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds and outbound service income must be repatriated into China by the JV through a designated bank within 180 days from the date of export declaration or completion of service, as recorded in the SAFE monitoring system. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; the JV may maintain the foreign currency in its export proceeds settlement account or voluntarily convert to CNY, subject to underlying documentary evidence such as customs declaration, commercial invoices, and service contracts. For service-related income in executive headhunting, the JV must submit a 'Foreign Exchange Income Receipt Verification' application supported by the master agreement and blue-ink invoice before settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Low for the Chinese JV. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise or Revolut hold no PRC banking or cross-border payment institution license, and PBOC circulars require all enterprises to maintain primary transaction accounts at licensed domestic banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions. A mainland-incorporated JV cannot legally rely on an offshore EMI as its principal operating account; such EMIs can only be used for supplementary outward remittances if they partner with a licensed PRC clearing bank and the underlying purpose code is fully documented. Otherwise, SAFE and PBOC will treat the flow as an unregistered offshore settlement channel, subject to penalties and forced closure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound share capital injection must follow strict SAFE-managed protocols: execute SAFE FDI registration and obtain the FDI business record form; open a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the Chinese bank where the JV maintains its primary relationship; receive the MT103 remittance from the overseas investor's bank using payment particulars 'FDI Share Capital Subscription' and no current-account commodity code; instruct the Chinese bank to perform capital account confirmation on SAFE's ASOne system before any RMB conversion; and after each contribution obtain a certified registered capital verification report from a PRC CPA or licensed firm. The settled RMB funds may be used only for permitted positive-list purposes, including office rental, employees' salaries, domestic procurement, and taxes, but never for equity investment into other PRC entities unless separately approved. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File (Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation) must be prepared and maintained when the aggregate related-party transactions in the prior fiscal year exceed any of the following thresholds: (i) tangible asset ownership transfers greater than or equal to RMB 200 million; (ii) intangible asset ownership transfers greater than or equal to RMB 100 million; (iii) financial asset transfers and intercompany loans greater than or equal to RMB 100 million; (iv) services (including management fees, technical services, royalties, and headhunting support services) greater than or equal to RMB 40 million; or (v) any other related-party transactions greater than or equal to RMB 40 million. A Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EITL Article 47. Although China does not have a separate 'ESR' regime, the tax authorities will examine whether a Chinese JV earns its profits through substantive functions, assets, and risks. For executive headhunting, the JV must demonstrate that it employs the key recruiters, conducts client outreach, negotiates fee structures, and bears financial risk. If a foreign parent merely invoices for 'candidate referrals' without real personnel or activities abroad, the Chinese JV may be denied a deduction for those payments. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is used in TP audits to allocate profits from services. In the absence of economic substance, the tax authorities may re-characterize the transaction and exercise special tax adjustment powers, including imputation of profits to the Chinese entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fictitious Service Expenses

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (TCAL) Article 63, the taxpayer shall be ordered to pay the additional taxes and late-payment interest within a prescribed time and shall be subject to a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. If the evasion involves a large amount (over RMB 50,000 for an individual or RMB 100,000 for a unit) and constitutes a crime, criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, with a fine and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: If the directors personally directed the fraudulent invoicing scheme, the corporate veil may be pierced under the PRC Company Law (Article 20) where the abuse of corporate independence seriously harms creditors' interests. The directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the underpaid taxes and penalties, with personal bank accounts frozen and assets seized. They will be blacklisted as 'major tax evaders' and subjected to a travel ban (exit ban) and restrictions on high-consumption acts under the joint punishment mechanism. Criminal conviction for tax evasion may lead to imprisonment of 3 to 7 years, plus fines, disqualification from corporate directorship for up to 5 years.
Failure to Withhold WHT on Outbound Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 69, the withholding agent (the JV) is liable for a penalty equal to 50% to 300% of the amount of tax it failed to withhold. Additionally, the tax authority may collect the unpaid tax directly from the withholding agent if the non-resident cannot be taxed within a 90-day period. If intentional criminal conduct occurs, the director may be prosecuted for tax evasion by a withholding agent under Criminal Law Article 201, exposing the company to a fine and the responsible individuals to imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly decided to forgo withholding may have personal assets attached to satisfy the uncollected taxes and fines. They will be subject to exit restrictions (travel bans) and inclusion on the tax blacklist. For severe violations, criminal prosecution may result in personal imprisonment of 3 to 7 years, a criminal fine, and prohibition from serving as senior officers of any company for a statutory period.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under EITL Article 41 and Article 48 of the EITL Implementation Regulations, tax authorities may make a special tax adjustment, recomputing taxable income at arm's length. The adjusted tax must be paid, along with interest computed at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points per annum. If the TP underpayment was intentional and concealed, a penalty of 50% to 200% of the additional tax may be imposed under TCAL Article 63. CbCR and documentation penalties apply if the JV fails to maintain required records (up to RMB 50,000 for each document, and up to RMB 100,000 for severe cases).

Director personal liability: If directors deliberately orchestrated the sham pricing to shift profits, they may be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax and interest as economic guarantors; the People's Courts may attach their personal assets. Under China's anti-tax-evasion information sharing, the directors will be blacklisted and subject to a travel ban (exit embargo). If the adjustment reveals an intentional intention to evade tax, criminal conviction for evasion may be pursued, leading to imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and fines. Additionally, directors may be disqualified from holding management positions for up to 5 years upon conviction.
