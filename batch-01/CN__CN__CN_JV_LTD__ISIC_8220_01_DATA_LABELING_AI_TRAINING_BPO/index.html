Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. The registered office must be a physical address within the jurisdiction where the JV is incorporated, and it must be the actual place of business. Tax authorities and banks require a physical presence for verification, and the JV must have a lease agreement or property ownership certificate. The use of a registered agent for service of process is allowed, but the registered office itself must be a physical location. Failure to maintain a physical office may result in deregistration and penalties.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement apply to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in ISIC 8220 AI data labeling and BPO. Under the PRC Company Law, shareholders determine the registered capital in the articles of association, must pay the full subscribed amount within five years after incorporation, and may contribute in cash or lawful non-cash assets. The subscribed capital is not subject to a government escrow, but the contribution must be paid into the company's own bank account and recorded in the company's capital contribution ledger. Any regulatory minimum would arise only if the company later adds a licensed physical activity.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as an industrial Civil Defense permit. Routine commercial fire-safety inspection of the office premises under the PRC Fire Protection Law and standard occupational safety obligations under the Work Safety Law for office and IT workstations apply; these are normal lease and employer clearances, not a project-specific operating license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online taxpayer portal accessible via the STA official website and provincial STA e-taxation portals.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: After SAMR incorporation, the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is issued an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code on its PRC business license; this code is the statutory taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, VAT, and other national taxes. Step 2: Within 30 days of business license issuance, the JV completes taxpayer registration with the competent local STA office through the e-Tax Bureau, submitting the business license, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identity documents, and board resolution appointing finance/tax personnel. Step 3: The JV completes indirect tax status registration: it must register for VAT, choose between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer status (general status is compulsory when rolling 12-month taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000), open an electronic invoice account, obtain tax-control digital credentials, and bind the account to the STA e-invoice platform. Step 4: The JV registers for payroll individual income tax withholding and social insurance. Step 5: For any foreign non-resident enterprise that is the overseas parent or beneficial owner of China-source income (dividends, interest, royalties, fees) but maintains no PRC establishment, the JV as statutory withholding agent completes non-resident withholding tax registration through the e-Tax Bureau; the non-resident uses its home-country TIN where available, or is assigned an STA-issued temporary tax identification number for Chinese withholding filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification: the PRC taxes resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Ventures at entity level as a domestic enterprise; there is no check-the-box, limited-liability look-through, or hybrid-mismatch election. Administrative elections available are VAT-related: the small-scale/general taxpayer status election before the RMB 5,000,000 mandatory threshold, election of simplified VAT accounting for eligible modern services where permitted, and the zero-rating/exemption classification of cross-border services through the STA cross-border taxable services catalogue.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No annual turnover threshold for basic tax registration; every PRC-incorporated enterprise must register for tax within 30 days of business license issuance. For VAT general taxpayer status, mandatory registration is triggered when taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that amount the JV may remain a small-scale taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on export of goods where the goods are physically exported from the PRC, the GACC export customs declaration is filed, the exporter retains title/possession evidence, and the export proceeds are collected from the overseas buyer. For cross-border services, a PRC supplier may claim zero-rating or VAT exemption only if the service falls within an STA-recognised cross-border taxable service category (including qualifying data-processing, information technology, software and business-process/BPO services) and the recipient is located outside the PRC, the service is wholly consumed outside the PRC, and the supplier holds contemporaneous contracts, delivery/acceptance evidence, e-invoices, and bank foreign-exchange collection records. In the absence of qualifying export treatment, domestic data-labeling/modern services are generally subject to VAT at the 6% rate.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form issued by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (for goods exports; for cross-border services, the formal export service contract and service delivery/acceptance record serve as the export evidence)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing cross-border movement of the goods
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt issued by a designated PRC forex bank (the PRC statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate) or the State Administration of Foreign Exchange cross-border receipt/payment record confirming repatriation of the export price from the overseas buyer

China mandates real-time digitalized VAT invoicing under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) platform. The JV must issue special VAT invoices or fully digitalized e-invoices through the STA e-invoice service platform using tax-control digital credentials; high-volume issuers may integrate via API to the platform. Each fully digitalized e-invoice is transmitted to the STA in real time, carries a unique invoice number, a dynamic anti-counterfeiting QR code, and a digital signature; no physical stamping is required and invoice data are cleared by the tax authority at issuance.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by STA against the e-invoice data, customs export declarations, transport files, and bank foreign-exchange records. If documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, the STA will claw back the VAT refund or zero-rating benefit and re-instate output VAT, add daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, impose administrative fines of 50% to five times the amount evaded, and may conduct retrospective assessments without statutory limitation where fraud is established. Criminal referral may also be made under the PRC Criminal Law for export-rebate fraud, carrying imprisonment and confiscation of property. Goods or services for which export payment is neither repatriated within the 180-day FX window nor formally registered as deferred collection are treated as non-export sales and assessed VAT with the same penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility for trade and service payments under IMF Article VIII, but imposes strict capital-account controls. This Sino-foreign equity JV must register all inward direct investment with SAFE through the designated domestic bank before receiving equity capital. Shareholder loans, repatriation of profits, and liquidation proceeds are capital-account items requiring verified documentation and tax clearance; any capital conversion from USD to CNY is settled through the onshore interbank FX market within the daily band. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, corporate income tax return records, a board profit-distribution resolution, and FIE annual report compliance. No de facto free convertibility exists for speculative or unregistered flows, and all FX transactions require underlying true-exposure documentation. Current-account service fees from overseas clients can be received and settled more freely after bank collection verification, but the bank must retain invoices and contractual evidence. This classification means the JV treasury must maintain a segregated capital account for registered equity and separate settlement accounts for operating revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters in China must repatriate overseas current-account proceeds into domestically licensed banks within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, subject to SAFE verification. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio today; after collection, the JV may choose to retain the foreign currency in its settlement account or convert to CNY at the market rate. For services and data-labeling export revenue, the SAFE service trade digital declaration and the applicable service-trade transaction code must be supported by the underlying contract and invoice.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Viability is structurally low for primary treasury use. Wise, PayPal Mass Payout and similar offshore EMI or payment-institution rails are not licensed in China as deposit-taking commercial banks and cannot open corporate settlement accounts that satisfy PBOC account-management rules or SAFE capital-flow reporting. They may serve only as ancillary employee or contractor disbursement conduits through licensed Chinese payment institutions, and must not be used for equity injections, trade-settlement outflows, or repatriation of JV revenue. All compliant clearing must route through domestic banks with direct SAFE reporting and documentation retention.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Local File must be prepared where annual related-party transactions exceed the following thresholds in RMB: tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. A Master File is also required where the MNE group's audited consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year is RMB 5 billion or more, and Country-by-Country Reporting is required at the same group-revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: The STA applies the economic substance doctrine through Article 41 and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42. A shell Chinese JV that books profits without performing core income-generating activities, maintaining premises, employees, or risk-management functions in China will be recharacterized as a conduit. In the AI data-labeling context, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) consist of actual annotation labor, quality-control management, model-training and human-feedback systems, and active servicing contracts. The JV must derive profits in the jurisdiction where the personnel and systems performing those activities are located and cannot be a pure booking company for the foreign partner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell-Company Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and Tax Administration Law Article 52, the tax authorities may recharacterize the entity and deny the preferential rate, assess the underpaid CIT at 25%, impose a 0.05% daily surcharge, and levy a fine between 50% and 300% of the underpaid tax under Tax Administration Law Article 63. SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 additionally requires the filing of a special adjustment report, and the JV may be stripped of its HNTE certificate.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the false related-party disclosure or approve the intercompany allocation will be jointly and severally liable for the CIT underpayment in civil restitution. The legal representative may be placed on the SAT blacklist and banned from leaving China until the tax debt is settled. If the JV's false filings are used to evade tax, directors can face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to three years, and three to seven years for an especially huge amount.
Intercompany Transfer-Pricing Fraud and False TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax bureau may make a transfer-pricing adjustment under EIT Law Article 41, add an interest adjustment on the under-declared tax, and impose a fraudulent-return penalty of 50% to 300% under Tax Administration Law Article 63. The omission or false filing of related-party transaction schedules is a serious non-filing for which the maximum RMB 10,000 fine under Article 62 is aggravated by the daily late-payment charge.

Director personal liability: Directors who prepare or sign the false TP documentation are personally liable for the tax adjustment to the extent they individually benefitted from the profit shift. A court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20, order asset restitution, enforce a travel ban and exit-control order, and impose imprisonment for tax evasion if the adjusted CIT exceeds the criminal threshold and the intent to evade is proven.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Fraudulent DTA Relief on Outbound Dividends

Statutory sanction: The failure to withhold the full 10% statutory tax is a tax-collection violation. Under EIT Law Articles 37 and 38, the JV as withholding agent must pay the uncollected tax plus interest. Under Tax Administration Law Article 69, the JV is fined between 50% and 300% of the amount that should have been withheld. General anti-abuse rules allow the tax bureau to disregard the treaty claim and impose the full 10% rate, or the applicable rate on the actual recipient, plus criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct the CFO to release dividends with a false reduced WHT certificate are jointly liable for the withholding shortfall, as the JV's corporate veil can be pierced for fraudulent under-withholding. The legal representative's personal assets may be used for restitution, the SAT blacklist and travel ban will apply, and under Criminal Law Article 201 the director can face imprisonment of up to seven years and fines if the aggregate fraudulent under-withholding exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents more than 10% of the tax assessed.
