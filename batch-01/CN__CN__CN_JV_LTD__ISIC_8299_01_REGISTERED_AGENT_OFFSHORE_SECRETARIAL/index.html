Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the Foreign Investment Law; however, the registered capital must be fully subscribed within the time limit specified in the Articles of Association (typically 30% within 6 months and the remainder within 5 years). Capital contributions must be paid into a designated capital contribution account at a licensed commercial bank in China and verified by a certified public accountant. No escrow account is required, but foreign exchange regulations mandate that contributions be made in accordance with the Foreign Exchange Administration Rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; tax registrations, filings and invoice activations are processed online through the STA's unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portals and the national electronic invoice service platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture established in China: (1) complete company name pre-approval and establishment application with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); (2) submit the foreign investment information report required under the Foreign Investment Law; (3) obtain the SAMR business license, which contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that functions as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes; (4) when the business license is issued, SAMR automatically transmits registration information to the competent local STA office, and the entity must perfect tax registration, financial accounting system filing, bank account reporting, and payroll withholding registration through the e-Tax portal within 30 days; (5) apply for VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), invoice quota, and activation of fully digitalized electronic invoicing. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China, the non-resident entity must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number by registering at the competent STA tax service hall or through its PRC withholding agent, supplying the foreign certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing a tax representative, passport/identity documentation of the responsible officer, and any tax treaty residency certificate required for reduced withholding rates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Sino-foreign equity joint venture is treated as a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income; foreign corporate shareholders are separately subject to withholding tax on dividends under domestic law and applicable double tax treaties.

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification elections; classification is statutory under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the VAT Law, and the corporate tax anti-avoidance framework including controlled-foreign-company rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; any entity that obtains a business license is VAT-registered automatically upon incorporation, regardless of turnover, and may remain a small-scale simplified taxpayer only while taxable turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate and input VAT refund when the goods are physically exported and the export customs declaration is matched with logistics and foreign exchange receipt evidence. Cross-border services supplied to overseas recipients qualify for 0% zero-rating only if the service is listed in the zero-rated cross-border services catalogue, the recipient is located outside mainland China, and the service is not consumed within China. For the JV's corporate secretarial and registered agent services provided wholly to offshore clients, zero-rating may be available only if the service qualifies under the published catalogue and the entity retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of offshore use and payment; otherwise standard VAT rates apply.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Bill of Entry) bearing the customs release number and export declaration number.
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or international multimodal transport document evidencing shipment to an overseas consignee.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the export declaration and transport documents.
  • Export sales contract or overseas purchase order with all amendments.
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or foreign exchange income declaration certificate, the FIRC-equivalent, proving receipt of export proceeds through a designated foreign exchange bank.
  • For zero-rated services: written contract with the overseas recipient, detailed service description, proof that the recipient and the service use are outside China, and cross-border payment receipt.

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) framework integrated with Golden Tax System Phase IV. Each invoice carries a 20-digit electronic invoice number, an automatic digital tax-control signature/anti-fraud stamp, and a QR code, and issuance data is transmitted to STA in near real time. Input VAT credit is available only for valid special VAT invoices whose data has been matched and verified on the national invoice verification service platform.

If export zero-rating or VAT refund substantiation is incomplete, false, or the required customs and foreign exchange repatriation documents are not obtained within the 180-day repatriation deadline, the STA will claw back refunded VAT, charge daily late-payment surcharges from the original refund date, and impose administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Intentional fraudulent export refund arrangements may additionally trigger criminal liability for tax fraud, customs penalties, revocation of export tax refund filing status, and inclusion in the tax dishonesty enforcement and blacklist supervision system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese foreign exchange regime operates under a dual-track system. The RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions (trade, services, and remittances of dividends), but capital account transactions remain heavily regulated and require case-by-case approval or filing with SAFE. Foreign investors can establish a domestic foreign currency capital account (benefits from the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) process) to inject equity, but all capital settlement must follow the 'payment settlement' rule, meaning RMB converted from foreign currency must be used for genuine business purposes and cannot be redirected to speculative use. Profit remittance to overseas shareholders is allowed after annual audit, tax clearance (including withholding tax), and submission of the tax payment certificate and audited financials, but the process involves substantial documentary review by the designated bank. For a corporate secretarial and registered agent entity with limited domestic sales revenue, the practical convertibility hurdle is the stringent proof of economic substance for every RMB outflow, especially for cross-border service fees or management fees, which trigger anti-avoidance scrutiny. The central bank's banking acceptance of foreign currency from clients is subject to anti-money laundering (AML) verification and requires the entity to have a valid operating license, business scope, and tax registration. Additionally, China's foreign exchange management bans the circumvention of capital controls through 'in-transit' transshipment or round-tripping structures. The 2020 edition of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and enhanced data reporting via the 'Cross-Border Clearing Information System' require real-time reconciliation of every currency exchange operation, thereby limiting de facto convertibility even where legal rules allow. Overall, current account convertibility is effectively open for trade-linked payments, but capital account settlement, profit repatriation, and cross-border payments are conditional on rigorous compliance, with a de facto requirement of bank-level 'know-your-business' verification. Foreign exchange rates are market-determined within the PBOC's daily fixing band, but operational convertibility remains constrained by mandatory reporting, purpose authenticity, and the ability of the bank to refuse suspicious transactions without full documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Pursuant to the SAFE measures for trade in goods (Circular No. 14 of 2020 and subsequent notices), all export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration (or the contractually agreed collection date) and credited to the exporter's designated foreign exchange settlement account. The exporter must file a trade declaration through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's monitoring system. Although China abolished the mandatory 'surrender and exchange' requirement, the physical receipt of foreign currency is compulsory; after receipt, the company may retain the currency in USD/EUR or convert it into RMB, but the proportion of retained foreign currency is subject to a default limit of 50% of the total operating balance (unless upgraded to 'A' status, which permits full retention). In practice, for a corporate services company, export proceeds are rare, but if the entity invoices offshore clients for secretarial fees, the full amount must be repatriated and declared under code '122010' (services income) before any conversion or disbursement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. The People's Bank of China and China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission do not recognize purely offshore digital EMIs as licensed deposit-taking institutions. For a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, all corporate banking services, including inward capital transfer, outward payments, and trade settlement, must operate through supervised commercial banks after obtaining a valid Foreign Exchange Business License. Offshore fintech platforms (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, or TransferWise) are not permitted to hold or transit funds for entities without a local payment institution license under the 'Administrative Measures for Payment Services Provided by Non-Financial Institutions.' Additionally, the cross-border guarantee of liquidity through an EMI would violate the direct supervision framework of SAFE. The only permissible hybrid route is to use an EMI as a payment instruction interface, but the ultimate clearing and custody must remain within a bank account; therefore, the viability for acting as a primary treasurer rail is disallowed.

The foreign investor must first obtain the Foreign Investment Approval or filing certificate from the Ministry of Commerce (or local counterpart), then open a foreign exchange capital account at a designated Tier-1 bank (ICBC/BOC) using the FDI registration form from SAFE. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. The People's Bank of China and China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission do not recognize purely offshore digital EMIs as licensed deposit-taking institutions. For a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, all corporate banking services, including inward capital transfer, outward payments, and trade settlement, must operate through supervised commercial banks after obtaining a valid Foreign Exchange Business License. Offshore fintech platforms (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, or TransferWise) are not permitted to hold or transit funds for entities without a local payment institution license under the 'Administrative Measures for Payment Services Provided by Non-Financial Institutions.' Additionally, the cross-border guarantee of liquidity through an EMI would violate the direct supervision framework of SAFE. The only permissible hybrid route is to use an EMI as a payment instruction interface, but the ultimate clearing and custody must remain within a bank account; therefore, the viability for acting as a primary treasurer rail is disallowed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other types of related-party transactions. Master File must be prepared by the Chinese group if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion; Country-by-Country reporting is required for ultimate parent groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: In addition, tax authorities may apply a six-year special tax investigation period and disregard transactions whose sole purpose is to avoid Chinese tax. The entity's business model as an ISIC 8299 corporate secretarial office must be evidenced by client contracts and professional-licence documentation; any arrangement designed to shift profits to tax-haven affiliates without true CIGA is an invitation to GAAR adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, the tax authority will recover the underpaid tax, impose a daily surcharge of 0.05% and levy a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid amount. Criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for tax evasion, carrying imprisonment of up to three years for substantial amounts and three to seven years for huge amounts; Article 205 for false issuance of special VAT invoices carries penalty of up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Upon repeated evasion or abandonment of the corporate form, courts pierce the corporate veil under the PRC Company Law (2023 revision) Article 21, making directors and persons in control jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes. The tax authority may also impose an exit ban under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, place the director on the social-credit and tax blacklist, freeze personal bank accounts, and obtain criminal convictions that result in imprisonment of the responsible directors.
Shell Company without Economic Substance / Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and the SAT's substantive requirements, the tax authority may disregard the shell structure, reallocate income to the real profit-earning entrepreneurs, deny treaty relief and impose a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. The corporate-secretarial licence may be revoked by the State Administration for Market Regulation for failing the statutory substance and licensed-practice requirements; directors may be disqualified from forming or administering companies for up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who abuse limited-liability protection are personally liable under Company Law Article 21 for all losses caused to the state in unpaid taxes and penalties. Personal assets may be seized in civil restitution; a travel ban will prevent exit from China pending full settlement; the director is blacklisted under the Joint Punishment Memorandum for tax dishonesty and may face criminal charges under Articles 201 or 203 of the Criminal Law for refused or fraudulent tax payments.
AML Compliance Failure / Concealment of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the revised PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, a non-compliant institution may be fined up to RMB 5 million for serious AML violations, with licence revocation and a public blacklisting order. Criminal Law Article 191 prescribes imprisonment of up to seven years (or ten years for serious) for laundering criminal proceeds. The administrative regulator may also impose an administrative penalty of up to RMB 1 million for professional-service entities used to create hidden ownership.

Director personal liability: Compliance officers and directors who approve or conceal unlawful ownership structures will be subject to personal fines (RMB 50,000-500,000), prohibition from serving as a director or supervisor for a fixed number of years, asset freezing and travel bans. Under the AML law and Company Law veil-piercing rules, the court can order personal restitution of diverted proceeds and impose criminal imprisonment on those who intentionally orchestrated the money-laundering architecture.
