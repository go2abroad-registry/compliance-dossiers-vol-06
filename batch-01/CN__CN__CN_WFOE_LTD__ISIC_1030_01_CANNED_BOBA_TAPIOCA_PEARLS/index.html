None. The PRC imposes no statutory minimum registered capital for an ordinary food-manufacturing wholly foreign-owned enterprise. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA); exclusive online portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), operating as the national unified electronic tax filing and payment platform.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned WFOE, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation. The foreign shareholders must first form the WFOE under the PRC Company Law, submitting notarised and legalised foreign investor incorporation documents, board resolutions, shareholder passports, articles of association, registered address proof, lease for business premises, and capital commitment. After approval, the business license bearing the USCC is issued, and the USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the multi-license integration reform. The local tax office in the registration district records the taxpayer in the Taxation Administration Information System without a separate TIN application. The WFOE must then access the Electronic Tax Bureau, complete real-name authentication of the legal representative, file its financial and accounting system rules, report bank account details, elect VAT taxpayer status as small-scale or general VAT taxpayer, and if exporting, complete Customs unit registration and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange trade foreign exchange status filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover from taxable sales in China is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold; all taxable entities must register for tax and VAT upon commencement of business, and entities below this threshold may elect small-scale taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China by a VAT-registered exporter qualify for the 0% export VAT rate, namely exemption and refund of input VAT, provided the goods are physically exported under an approved Customs export declaration, leave the customs territory of China, and the export foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to China in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules. Refundable zero-rating is generally available to VAT general taxpayers; small-scale exporters may use the exemption-without-refund method. Canned boba tapioca pearl products qualify for zero-rating upon satisfying physical export, documentary, and repatriation requirements.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form accepted by the China Customs Declaration System)
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent transport document such as airway bill, rail waybill, or road waybill, depending on exit mode)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, for China, the bank-issued export foreign exchange receipt certificate and the SAFE-recognised export collection verification record

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved digital invoice systems and cleared against the tax authority in real time. Fully digitalised VAT invoices carry a unique nationwide invoice code, an anti-fraud QR code, and a digital signature, and are transmitted instantly to the Golden Tax Phase IV tax control system. Unvalidated invoices are invalid and cannot support input VAT credits or export refund claims. Golden Tax Phase IV cross-matches invoice data with customs declarations, logistics, foreign exchange receipts, and contract flows for anti-fraud supervision.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are audited ex post by the STA and Customs under Golden Tax Phase IV through cross-matching of customs declarations, invoices, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If documents are missing, inconsistent, counterfeit, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the zero-rating is revoked retroactively. The exporter must repay the VAT refunded plus daily late payment interest at 0.05%, and administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax amount may be imposed under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Intentional fabrication of export declarations, forged transport documents, or use of fake foreign exchange receipts to obtain export VAT refunds constitutes the crime of fraudulent export tax refunds under the Criminal Law, punishable by fines, confiscation of assets, and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for especially large fraudulent amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated foreign exchange regime. The current account is broadly convertible under bilateral agreement with the IMF Article VIII, meaning trade in goods and services can be settled in foreign currency with minimal documentary friction, provided the legitimacy of the underlying transaction is demonstrated through the banking system. The capital account, however, remains subject to strict registration, quota, and approval mechanisms. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), inbound shareholder equity injections must be recorded with SAFE and routed through a designated domestic bank account after receiving a valid foreign exchange settlement permit. Convertibility is not automatic; each transaction is carefully vetted. Export receipts are automatically deemed to have passed the surrender requirement once repatriated and either retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted as instructed by the enterprise. Profit remittance, i.e. dividend distribution, is allowed on an annual basis after tax and audit, but the auditor's report and proper filing with the SAFE must substantiate the source of distributable profits. In practice, the WFOE must present tax payment certificates, audited financial statements, board resolutions, and a fine-grained capital flow passbook showing the lawful provenance of RMB funds obtained from prior conversions. The most critical hindrance is the need to prove economic substance and the true business purpose of each capital transaction, which can create delays when wires are subject to enhanced review. Currency convertibility is therefore technically high for trade flows but practically constrained for account balances, investment injections, and profit repatriations—all of which require layered compliance documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs declaration. Once repatriated, the WFOE may hold the proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or convert them into RMB at the prevailing market rate through a designated bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under normal conditions; the enterprise can retain up to 100% of approved export receipts in foreign currency. However, if the balance exceeds the SAFE-defined annual quota and the enterprise fails to provide evidence of pending commercial obligations, the bank will compel conversion through the spot market. During acute balance-of-payment stress, PBOC may invoke contingency surrender requirements, but this is exceptional and only through central bank circular.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) - State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer) have no legal licence to service a China-domiciled WFOE as a full treasury counterparty. They cannot open a domestic settlement account, accept export proceeds on behalf of the entity, or issue a compliant RMB account. Their viability is therefore low. At most, an overseas group treasury may use an offshore EMI to pre-aggregate funds before wiring into the WFOE's domestic account, but direct EMI usage for local payroll, tax payments, and LC issuance is not permissible under PBOC regulations. Any such usage would be a violation of SAFE and PBOC requirements on licensed payment institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound capital injections, the WFOE must instruct the non-resident parent to wire funds to the capital-account foreign currency settlement account at the designated bank using SAFE purpose code 1220 (overseas investment in domestic enterprises). The bank will require the case-by-case registration certificate issued by SAFE, the business license of the WFOE, and the shareholder board resolution. Once the foreign currency wire is credited, the WFOE may convert the proceeds into RMB at the onshore spot price only after completing a capital verification with a locally registered CPA. The bank then opens a qui gīn RMB capital account with a distinct sub-account code for each shareholder injection to ensure traceability in the foreign exchange case. Subsequent contributions must be coordinated with SAFE renewals and new registration updates.For outbound trade disbursements related to canned boba tapioca pearls, the WFOE may release payments through standard SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for spot purchases of raw materials or via an irrevocable documentary letter of credit (MT700) when settling with overseas suppliers under UCP600 rules. Domestic payments to local suppliers are executed through the domestic clearing system (CNAPS) and interbank settlement. For international transactions that require redundancy, the bank may route through both SWIFT and the cross-border interbank payment system (CIPS) for RMB-denominated transactions. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are permissible for low-risk counterparties with clean shipping documents. The bank must verify that each outbound payment carries a proper SAFE transaction purpose code, e.g., 100112 for import goods or 9961 for service-related payments, and that the WFOE maintains full compliance with the single-fine ledger matching of invoices, customs declarations, and funds flows.Onshore RMB forward contracts with a trade-background documentary requirement, settled at delivery with the same designated bankCross-currency swaps for current-account operational cash flow, capped by audited exposure and SAFE-registeredNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted domestically; enterprises may access offshore NDF arrangements solely for hedging the parallel non-RMB balance sheet exposures through licensed offshore parent treasury centres, but such NDF overlays must not create deliverable RMB swaps in mainland

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, when intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or when related-party services exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required at the consolidated group level when the aggregate related-party transaction threshold is met, and Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion or the equivalent treaty threshold.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a Gulf-state style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule impose a reasonable-commercial-purpose and substance-over-form test. Profits from the tapioca manufacturing operation, the aseptic production process, brand exploitation, and raw-material procurement must be allocated according to the core income-generating activities actually performed by the WFOE. A hollow WFOE that lacks DEMPE functions with respect to intangibles, or a contract manufacturer that has no control over business risks and strategic decision-making, will not be allowed to retain manufacturing residual profits and may be recharacterized as a royalty payer, agent, or service provider.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent corporate income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the SAT will recover the underpaid tax plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and impose a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Where the amount is large and accounts for more than 10% or 30% of the tax payable, criminal referral under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 may result in fines and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors who order, design, or knowingly participate in the evasion may be criminally named individually, banned from leaving China through the SAT blacklisting and exit-ban system, and subject to asset freezing and judicial collection. Where the WFOE is found to be a mere alter ego or sham, the PRC Company Law veil-piercing doctrine imposes joint and several personal liability on directors and shareholders for the company's unpaid tax, penalties, and interest.
False VAT invoice abuse connected with cross-border payments

Statutory sanction: False VAT-invoice conduct violates the PRC Invoice Management Measures and PRC Criminal Law Article 205; the sanctions include confiscation of illegal gains, administrative fines, cancellation of invoice and tax qualifications, and criminal penalties including imprisonment up to life and property confiscation depending on the amount and severity.

Director personal liability: Directors who arranged or authorized the false invoicing can be prosecuted as primary or accessory offenders and face substantial personal fines and imprisonment. The corporate veil may be pierced to hold controlling directors personally liable for the unpaid taxes and civil compensation; the SAT will normally blacklist the legal representative and actual controller, preventing exit from China until the amounts are settled.
Disguised profit repatriation and withholding-tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69, the WFOE as withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax and a fine of 50% to 300% of the withheld-but-unpaid amount. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose separate administrative fines for unlawful cross-border outward remittances, and the arrangement may trigger special anti-avoidance adjustments, deemed-dividend recharacterization, and criminal examination for money laundering where the proceeds are moved through unauthorized channels.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax-payment declarations, issue cross-border payment instructions, or cooperate with treasury-route shell companies can be held jointly liable for the unpaid WHT, penalties, and interest. The legal representative and actual controller face travel bans and blacklisting until the tax debt is cleared; if the funds are laundered through underground banks or third-party entities, the responsible directors may be prosecuted criminally and sentenced to imprisonment, with personal assets subject to confiscation and civil restitution.
