Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None: the PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law do not impose a minimum registered capital for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The Articles of Association must specify the registered capital and the subscription schedule; under the Company Law (2023 Revision, effective 1 July 2024), the sole shareholder must fully contribute the subscribed capital within five years from the date of incorporation. No blocked escrow account or capital freeze is required unless the sole shareholder adopts a later contribution schedule that is inconsistent with law. Paid-in capital must be truthfully reflected in the annual enterprise information report.

Funding & unlock path: Fire protection design review and completion acceptance certificate from the Fire and Rescue Brigade; civil air defense structural pre-approval for new industrial workshops with basements or shelters above the local threshold; structural occupancy load inspection certificate from the construction authority; special equipment use registration and annual inspection for boilers, pressure vessels, autoclaves and aseptic filling machines; workplace safety and occupational hazard notification, employee health files and safety training records under the Work Safety Law and the Occupational Disease Prevention and Control Law.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the nationwide Electronic Tax Office / e-Tax portal operated by STA and local provincial tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory Chinese Tax Identification Number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the SAMR Business License. The procedure is: (1) the foreign investor files notarised and legalised corporate documents and the WFOE's incorporation dossier with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) jurisdiction where the WFOE is domiciled; after approval SAMR issues the Business License containing the USCC, which automatically constitutes the national tax registration number under the multi-permit integration reform. (2) Within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, the WFOE's legal representative and financial officer complete the tax registration data confirmation through the Electronic Tax Office (e-Tax) using the USCC and real-name authentication, declaring registered address, business scope, legal representative, banking account, and shareholder details. (3) The competent tax authority accepts the registration and assigns the WFOE a taxpayer status and tax filing calendar; no separate TIN certificate is issued because the USCC is the uniform tax identifier. (4) The WFOE must separately determine its VAT taxpayer status by reporting estimated annual taxable sales; if exceeding RMB 5,000,000, general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory; if below, it is a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless voluntary general taxpayer registration is approved. (5) The WFOE obtains a tax-control digital U-Key / CA certificate through the electronic tax platform and activates electronic invoice (all-electronic invoice) capability, including VAT special invoice quotas, with submission of the legal representative's identity and company chop/seal registration. (6) For export processing, the WFOE completes the enterprise export registration with Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and updates its tax registration information for export tax refund/zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election for a WFOE; the WFOE is taxable as a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The only relevant statutory election is VAT: a taxpayer with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer, and registration is mandatory when taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the threshold above which registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory. All incorporated entities, including WFOEs, must perform tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance regardless of turnover; below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold the entity is classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless voluntary general taxpayer registration is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating of manufactured goods, including aseptic barista-format hydrolysed oat milk, applies where the WFOE exports goods from the People's Republic of China customs territory in an export customs clearance mode, transfers ownership and control to a customer outside China pursuant to a genuine export sales contract, and obtains payment in a permitted foreign currency. The goods must actually leave China and the export customs declaration must correspond to the goods description, quantity, weight, value, and consignee. The WFOE must be a registered general VAT taxpayer or eligible small-scale taxpayer under the export tax rebate administration rules and must file the VAT declaration, export sales data, and any required export-rebate application with the STA. For cross-border services and offshore digital supplies, zero-rating or exemption may be available only where the service is rendered entirely outside China and the recipient is outside China; for the WFOE's physical goods export, the zero rate is substantiated by the export customs declaration and export receipt evidence.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Export Customs Declaration) issued by China Customs and cleared through the customs clearance system
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement from China to the foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Sales Contract signed with the overseas buyer
  • Packing List and transport documentation matching the customs declaration
  • Export proceeds collection confirmation/foreign exchange receipt record issued by the settlement bank and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), showing repatriation of the sales proceeds in foreign currency
  • VAT invoice (VAT special invoice or export invoice, as applicable) issued under the all-electronic invoice platform and linked to the export sales

The STA operates the nationwide all-electronic invoice (fully digitalised e-invoice) system and the Golden Tax Phase IV administrative control system. VAT special invoices and all-electronic invoices must be issued through the STA-approved invoice management software connected to the Electronic Tax Office; no offline or third-party unsanctioned invoicing is permitted. Each invoice is transmitted to the national tax-control platform in near real time, authenticated with a digital tax-control U-Key/CA certificate, and validated against mandatory invoice fields (e.g., tax classification code, VAT rate, amount, buyer and seller USCC, project description) before a compliant invoice number is returned. Anti-fraud digital stamping and real-time fiscal clearance automatically block inputs that fail validation; all-electronic invoices carry an XML data file with electronic signatures in place of paper copies. The taxpayer must maintain an invoice ledger and reconcile source data with the customs export declaration, underlying contracts, and foreign-exchange receipts for export zero-rating purposes.

The STA and Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims through cross-matching of customs declarations, electronic invoices, freight documents, foreign-exchange receipt records, and enterprise tax filings. Where export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day foreign-exchange collection window without an approved extension, or where the claimed export transaction is found to be fictitious, inflated, or materially inconsistent, the tax authority may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate, treat the export as domestic taxable sales at 13%, assess overdue tax and interest, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. More serious conduct, including false export declarations for the purpose of obtaining refunds, may constitute fraudulent export tax rebate under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which carries custodial penalties, fines, confiscation of property, and loss of export tax rebate qualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the PBOC and SAFE. The Renminbi is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including export trade receipts, service payments, and genuine profit or dividend remittances, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. Capital-account transactions, however, are not freely convertible and require underlying regulatory registration: foreign direct investment capital injections must be registered with SAFE and deposited through a designated bank account, while cross-border loans, external debt, equity conversions, and liquidation distributions require separate approvals or confirmations. A WFOE may use registered capital after the bank validates the SAFE FDI registration and capital verification, but the funds cannot be converted into Renminbi for prohibited domestic uses or re-remitted offshore except through permitted channels such as audited profit distribution, lawful equity transfer, repayment of genuine external debt, or liquidation surplus. Operational profit remittance requires annual corporate income tax settlement, audited financial statements, a dutiable profits calculation, and the submission of the relevant foreign exchange payment application. The system creates practical speed and documentary friction through the genuine transaction principle, balance-of-payments reporting codes, bank pre-review, and post facto SAFE monitoring. Liquidity fungibility between trade proceeds and capital-account funds is strictly prohibited, and each rail must be traced to its own legal and documentary basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Category A exporters, there is no statutory mandatory foreign currency surrender ratio in mainland China: export proceeds may be retained 100% in a current-account foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily converted into Renminbi at the market rate through a designated bank. Under SAFE's trade-in-goods total-volume administration, export proceeds must be repatriated to China through a bank account held by the WFOE and reconciled against customs declarations in the Trade-in-Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. In practice, a bank must identify and match the receipt against the corresponding customs declaration within 30 calendar days of the funds entering the account; unreconciled or mismatched receipts can trigger further verification, classification downgrade, or a forced settlement instruction for B/C-category enterprises. For B/C-rated entities, SAFE may require on-site inspection, installment settlement, or mandatory conversion upon receipt, but no fixed national percentage applies.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) via the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE, N18 Huancheng North Road, Xicheng District, Beijing)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, RevolutBusiness, and similar non-bank financial technology companies are not legally viable as primary onshore banking or clearing rails for the WFOE. The PBOC and SAFE require all foreign exchange receipts, registered capital, and Renminbi settlement to flow through financial institutions that are licensed deposit-taking banks in mainland China with direct CNAPS and SWIFT connectivity. Offshore EMIs do not hold a Chinese banking license, cannot access CNAPS, cannot submit SAFE-balance-of-payments declarative codes, and cannot perform foreign investment capital verification. Their practical use is limited to ancillary, low-value foreign currency receivables for overseas SaaS or e-commerce settlements, but even then they fail to satisfy the corporate treasury requirements for statutory financial statements and audit trails. The onboarding viability rating is therefore Low for regulated WFOE cash management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment must be routed through a SAFE-registered capital account at a designated licensed bank. The offshore parent wires registered capital via SWIFT MT103 with the transaction purpose code consistent with foreign direct investment capital injection (commonly reported under balance-of-payments code 622011 for FDI capital injection). The receiving Chinese bank reconciles the wire against the SAFE FDI registration record and the foreign investment information filing certificate. After receipt, the bank performs capital verification, confirms that the remitter matches the registered shareholder, and either credits the foreign currency capital account or converts the funds into Renminbi through the capital settlement account regime. The funds may then be used for genuine operating expenses, fixed asset purchases, payroll, and inventory within the business scope. The WFOE must retain all underlying equity transfer, investment agreement, and source-of-funds evidence because SAFE can request it during post-verification within the subsequent six months. For plant construction and UHT packaging machinery imports, a separate equipment purchase escrow can be created, but the governing principle is that capital-account monies cannot be used for cross-border trade-account netting without full documentary support.Outbound trade disbursements for the oat milk plant will primarily settle through SWIFT MT103 for open-account raw material purchases and spare parts, irrevocable commercial letters of credit for high-value aseptic processing and packaging lines supplied by overseas machinery manufacturers, and documentary collections for medium-value repeat transactions where documentary risk is acceptable. The WFOE must present import contracts, shipping documents, customs declarations, and commercial invoices to the issuing bank so that payments can be classified under the correct SAFE current-account trade purpose code. For equipment imports, the Chinese bank may issue a usance or at-sight commercial LC backed by the company's collateral or bank guarantee after full KYC and credit assessment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China requires Local File contemporaneous documentation when the annual related-party tangible property transfer amount exceeds RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfer exceeds RMB 100 million, financial asset transfer exceeds RMB 100 million, related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million, or related-party interest-bearing financing exceeds RMB 50 million. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the group annual consolidated revenue in the prior year reaches RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not enact a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the substance-over-form principle applies through CIT Article 47, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and the beneficial ownership doctrine. A WFOE with a physical UHT plant, oat milk production employees, actual packaging lines, risk management, and decision-making functions in China demonstrates core income-generating activities; a shell entity without employees, premises, or operational control will be disregarded, re-characterized, denied treaty relief, or taxed as having a permanent establishment in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 1 et seq. and Tax Collection Law Article 63: recovery of evaded tax, 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and a penalty from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Criminal Law Article 203 triggers imprisonment or criminal detention of up to three years where the evasion exceeds RMB 100,000 and exceeds 10% of tax due, rising to three to seven years plus fines where the amount is especially large and exceeds 30% of tax due.

Director personal liability: If the director signs a false return, authorizes falsified invoices, or misuses the corporate limited liability as a fraud vehicle, PRC Company Law veil-piercing rules make the director civilly liable for restitution of the underpaid tax and losses to creditors; the director is placed on the tax authority's Serious Dishonesty Taxpayer blacklist, subjected to exit control, flight and high-speed rail travel restrictions, and may face criminal imprisonment for tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Abusive Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 gives the tax authority power to re-determine taxable income by comparison with independent enterprises. SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes documentation and filing obligations; failure to file triggers penalties under Tax Collection Law Articles 44 and 62 up to RMB 10,000 plus reconstruction of income. Transfer pricing adjustments carry an additional interest charge over the benchmark RMB lending rate, and if the mispricing is intentional, the 50%-500% evasion penalty applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve distorted transfer prices, conceal related-party contracts, or certify false documentation are personally liable for the resulting tax shortage through veil-piercing and fraudulent insolvency principles; they may face compulsory civil compensation, confiscation of gains, blacklisting, travel bans, and criminal prosecution where the transfer pricing distortion acts as a disguised dividend diversion or tax evasion.
Shell Company Treaty-Shopping and DTA Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and General Anti-Avoidance Rule permit the tax authority to disregard the conduit arrangement, deny treaty benefits, impose the statutory 10% withholding tax plus additional interest, and levy penalties where false beneficial ownership declarations are submitted. A wrongful claim to a treaty benefit with forged TRC or sham agreement may be prosecuted as tax fraud under Tax Collection Law Article 63, with fines reaching 50%-500% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Chinese WFOE and the offshore shell who execute sham agreements and sign beneficial-ownership declarations face joint civil liability for the avoided withholding tax, clawback of dividends paid into the shell, blacklisting on the China Taxpayer Serious Dishonesty List, court-imposed exit and travel bans, and personal criminal liability for fraud, including imprisonment where the unlawful treaty-shopping arrangement is proven to be a criminal tax evasion scheme.
