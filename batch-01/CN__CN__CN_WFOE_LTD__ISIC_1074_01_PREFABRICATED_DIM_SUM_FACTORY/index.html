Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to this ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise under the current Company Law. The registered capital is the amount stated in the Articles of Association and is subscribed by the sole shareholder. No blocked escrow account is required by the corporate registry. Paid-in capital may be contributed according to a schedule set in the Articles, generally within five years from establishment, unless an applicable industrial license authority requires proof of financial capacity. Any inbound capital contribution must be routed through a licensed bank and, when converted into RMB, must follow foreign exchange administration rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Acceptance Voucher or fire filing certificate from the Fire and Rescue Department is mandatory before occupancy; structural load inspections and building completion acceptance are required for factory floors and cold storage; special equipment including pressure vessels and ammonia refrigeration systems must be inspected and registered before use; occupational hazard identification, workplace safety facilities and emergency plans must satisfy the Work Safety Law Three Simultaneous requirement; the local Emergency Management Bureau may require filing of emergency response plans and registration of the factory as a major workplace safety risk unit where attributable.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with primary electronic filing through the China Electronic Taxpayer Portal (Electronic Tax Bureau / e-Tax system) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon incorporation functions as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 calendar days after the business license is issued, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local competent STA office, either through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal or in person at the tax service hall. The tax registration is synchronized with SAMR corporate registration data and activates the WFOE in the VAT invoice system, corporate income tax filing modules, and export tax rebate system. A foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China may separately complete non-resident tax registration with the local STA to obtain a withholding tax identification code; however, a China-resident WFOE is registered directly under the USCC and requires no separate TIN certificate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported manufactured goods, including industrially quick-frozen steamed dim sum and dumplings, are eligible for the 0% VAT export zero-rating and input VAT refund treatment when the WFOE is registered as a General VAT taxpayer, the goods physically leave China customs territory, the export sale is made to a non-resident buyer under a genuine commercial contract, the export proceeds are repatriated to China through licensed banking channels within the applicable foreign exchange period, and complete export declaration and rebate documents are filed with STA through the Electronic Tax Bureau.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (China Customs export declaration number and Bill of Entry for export clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment
  • Export Special VAT Invoice or export commercial invoice issued by the WFOE
  • Foreign trade contract, packing list, and customs valuation documents
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export foreign exchange collection/verification record proving repatriation of export proceeds

China operates mandatory tax-controlled electronic invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the national Electronic Tax Bureau. Fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices are issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform; each invoice carries a unique tax control number and is transmitted to STA in near-real-time for authentication, reconciliation, and anti-fraud verification. Traditional paper Special VAT Invoices remain integrated through tax control U-keys and are likewise reported to the STA invoice management system upon issuance. All sales invoices must be generated through this state-supervised clearance architecture; any private offline invoicing is non-compliant and incapable of supporting input VAT deductions or export VAT refunds.

STA and China Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims through documentary matching, customs export data verification, and banking foreign exchange records. If the export is found to be fictitious, the goods never exited China, customs declarations are false, or export proceeds are not repatriated as required, STA will deny or rescind the 0% VAT rate, claw back all VAT refunds previously granted, assess daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05%, impose administrative penalties of 100% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained VAT refund under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, and may suspend the WFOE's export tax rebate qualification. Serious cases constitute the criminal offense of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, punishable by imprisonment up to life imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime for the RMB under which current account transactions are broadly convertible, but capital account items remain heavily regulated. The current account allows trade-related foreign exchange receipts and payments to be settled through designated banks after genuine transaction verification. The capital account, however, operates under a negative list and requires underlying approvals, registrations, or filings. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the frozen prepared food manufacturing sector, inbound registered capital injections are classified as foreign direct investment (FDI) and must pass through a foreign exchange capital account opened with a designated commercial bank. The funds must be registered with SAFE via the Foreign Exchange Business Registration system, typically obtained through the local market regulation authority's incorporation procedures. Profit remittances and dividend distributions are permitted on a current account basis but require tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements, board resolutions, and a record of foreign debt or capital verification where applicable. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer compulsory on a fixed ratio; enterprises may retain foreign currency revenue in a settlement account or convert it to RMB at the prevailing market rate. Enterprises must still repatriate export earnings into China within statutory timelines and present customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, and bank statements during trade verification. Overall, the regime is liberalized for operating and trade flows but commands strict documentary discipline for capital inflows, equity changes, and overseas remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless an extension or special reconciliation is approved by SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the full amount in foreign currency or voluntarily sell it to the banking system at market rates. All receipts must be matched against customs declarations and verified through the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail. Mainland China's foreign exchange regulatory architecture does not recognise offshore fintech EMIs as settlement account providers for corporate entities. SAFE-designated domestic commercial banks exclusively control the opening and operation of foreign exchange capital accounts and current account FX settlement accounts. An offshore digital EMI cannot hold statutory corporate accounts, process FDI capital verification, issue LCs, or act as the executing bank for SAFE-regulated outward remittances. Such fintech platforms may serve only as ancillary payment initiation tools in limited cross-border e-commerce contexts, but they are legally barred from replacing the onshore banking layer for this WFOE structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must execute a telegraphic transfer in a freely convertible currency, normally USD or HKD, to the WFOE's designated SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at an onshore commercial bank. The remittance must reference the FDI purpose code and the approved business registration. Upon receipt, the bank performs capital account supervision, records the inflow in the SAFE cross-border banking system, and requires the WFOE to apply for capital settlement into RMB using the 'foreign exchange capital settlement payment use letter'. Settlement proceeds must not be used for activities outside the approved business scope, and large disbursements require supporting vouchers such as construction contracts, equipment purchase agreements, payroll records, and lease contracts.Outbound trade settlements for raw ingredients, packaging, or overseas distribution are executed via SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct payments, commercial letters of credit through MT700/MT707 messages for secured supplier financing, and documentary collections using DP/DA terms. For cross-border RMB settlements, the WFOE may use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or CHATS connections through its onshore bank.
