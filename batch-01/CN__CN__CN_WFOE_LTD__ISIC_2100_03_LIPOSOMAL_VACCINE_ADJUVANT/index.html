Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required under current PRC law for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in the form of a limited liability company. Shareholders must specify the registered capital and a realistic capital contribution schedule in the Articles of Association, generally subscribed within five years. No blocked escrow account is required by law for registration, but for pharmaceutical production the registered capital must be sufficient to fund GMP-compliant facilities and the funds for any foreign exchange capital injection must be remitted through a designated SAFE capital account.

Funding & unlock path: Mandatory fire safety design and completion review by the local Fire and Rescue Department, issuing a Fire Protection Acceptance Inspection Opinion before operation; structural load and construction acceptance inspection by the local housing and urban-rural development bureau; workplace safety evaluation and occupational disease hazard pre-evaluation under the Work Safety Law; special equipment registration for autoclaves, pressure vessels, and clean-room HVAC systems.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, supported by the official e-Tax electronic tax filing and payment portal operated by each provincial STA bureau.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) organized as a Chinese limited liability company, the entity is first registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax and administrative purposes. The tax registration is automatically synchronized with SAMR. Within 30 days after the business license issuance, the WFOE must complete the tax registration record filing at the competent tax authority by logging into the e-Tax platform and submitting the following documentary package: (a) the corporate business license with uniform social credit code, (b) Articles of Association and shareholders' resolution appointing the legal representative and financial officer, (c) lease, ownership, or land use certificate evidencing the registered tax address, (d) identity documents of the legal representative and tax agent, (e) biometric or identity proof of foreign shareholders or ultimate beneficial owners, and (f) banking account opening permit or bank verification. Foreign non-resident entities that do not establish a Chinese entity but derive taxable income from China are required to undergo withholding tax registration via the STA withholding registration process to obtain a payroll or withholding TIN, which is distinct from a resident taxpayer registration. No generic federal form is used; the national electronic tax filing system handles the application and issuance of the taxpayer status certificate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (Five Million Renminbi) for sales of goods or taxable services triggers mandatory registration as a General VAT taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayer status applies to entities with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000; such entities may elect voluntarily to register as General VAT taxpayers if they meet the audit and accounting standards, but once the threshold is exceeded the tax authority will compulsorily upgrade the registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under Chinese VAT applies to exported goods physically shipped out of China and subject to customs supervision; qualifying offshore services performed for foreign recipients and used entirely outside China; and international transportation services. To claim the 0% rate, the WFOE must be a General VAT taxpayer, issue the export sales invoice correctly, obtain a customs export declaration with an approved export customs code, and show that the sales price is received in foreign currency from overseas. For cross-border services, the contract must clearly identify the foreign buyer, the services must not be rendered for taxable Chinese entities, and supporting evidence must demonstrate that the benefit of the service is consumed wholly outside China. The issuance of an Electronic Export Certificate and linkage of the export invoice to the customs electronic clearance record are prerequisites for the refund monthly declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (出口货物报关单) as evidenced by the electronic customs record generated at the port of departure
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing actual physical movement of goods from China to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List reflecting the exact export transaction
  • Foreign exchange settlement record in the bank's SAFE-monitored export collection verification system, demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds in freely convertible currency
  • Export Trade Authorization and any export license or health approval required for the liposomal vaccine adjuvant product class under PRC Customs commodity inspection rules

China mandates the use of the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax Phase IV system, which integrates VAT invoice issuance, customs trade data, and banking payment flows. All General VAT taxpayers must issue official VAT invoices (special and ordinary VAT fapiao) through the STA-approved Tax Control Device or the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform, and the invoice data must be transmitted in real time to the STA for regulatory approval. Special VAT invoices, which separately state the VAT amount eligible for input credit, are subject to strict pre-clearance anti-counterfeit stamping and cannot be manually issued without an STA-issued digital certificate. Real-time electronic invoice reporting to the STA's Golden Tax database is mandatory, and businesses must synchronize invoice records with general ledger and customs export declarations to prevent artificial mismatches.

Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims expose the WFOE to recovery of all input VAT previously refunded on the exported goods, together with interest computed from the date of the refund. Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, the taxpayer is subject to an additional late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day of the unpaid or incorrectly refunded tax, and a penalty of 50% up to 500% of the tax evaded for false export refund claims. Where the export declaration, bill of lading, or foreign exchange settlement records are fabricated, criminal liability for export VAT fraud or smuggling may apply under the PRC Criminal Law, including fines equal to one to five times the evaded tax and imprisonment for persons deemed responsible. The STA and General Administration of Customs conduct joint analysis of export invoice, logistics, and banking data to trigger automated red flags when zero-rating was not substantiated by actual cross-border movement or when the WFOE fails to repatriate the foreign exchange within the statutory 180-day window.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account RMB convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but capital-account transactions remain heavily regulated by SAFE. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the lipid nanoparticle vaccine adjuvant manufacturing sector must therefore distinguish between current-account flows such as trade settlement, service fees and after-tax profit remittance and capital-account flows such as foreign equity injection, shareholder loans and liquidation proceeds. Equity injections from non-resident parents are permitted only after completion of foreign direct investment (FDI) information registration with SAFE and the relevant market regulation authority; foreign currency must enter a designated capital account and may be converted into RMB only for approved genuine operational purposes. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is allowed after a statutory audit, tax clearance confirmation and board resolution, but requires documentary proof that the profit is after-tax and compliant with the applicable foreign exchange receipt declaration framework. In practice, banks apply both the transaction-purpose declaration regime and the genuine-need test, so the WFOE must maintain a complete documentary trail covering contracts, invoices, customs declarations, tax filings, payroll and supplier payment records. Payments for imported lipids, excipients, microfluidic equipment, cleanroom consumables and commissioning services are current-account items and can be processed with a trade declaration, invoice, contract and customs bill, but banks will require matching goods declarations under the SAFE goods-trade monitoring system. There is no unrestricted free convertibility for speculative capital flows; the system favours operational liquidity and punishes round-tripping, invoice mismatches and arbitrage-driven treasury manoeuvres. Any foreign debt or cross-border financing will require additional SAFE debt registration and real-risk verification before disbursement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE goods-trade foreign exchange monitoring regime, export foreign exchange proceeds from the WFOE's manufacture of liposomal vaccine adjuvants and lipid nanoparticle intermediates must be repatriated to China and booked through a licensed domestic bank account. The general statutory monitoring deadline is within 180 days of the export customs declaration date; enterprises failing to repatriate receivables must register the outstanding amount in SAFE's trade-item monitoring system and face documentary verification on export receipts versus customs declarations. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage applicable in the modern regime: export proceeds may be retained by the exporter in foreign currency at 100% or voluntarily converted into RMB at the enterprise's discretion. The binding statutory obligations are truthful declaration of the proceeds under the correct foreign exchange receipt code, preservation of the underlying export contract, invoice and customs declaration, and consistency between the receipt amount, the counterparty and the declared trade purpose. Banks may also require proof of the physical goods by presenting bills of lading or air waybills and, where applicable, an entry of trading data in the SAFE goods-trade monitoring platform.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and effectively non-viable as a treasury account substitute. Non-bank electronic money institutions operating offshore are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the PBOC and NFRA framework, cannot receive SAFE-designated capital account wires, cannot process export proceeds booking or surrendering requirements, and cannot clear RMB through CNAPS. Using such an EMI for the WFOE's own operating treasury would be treated as an unlawful settlement channel and would trigger PBOC and SAFE penalties. Their permissible role is limited to ancillary payment analytics, expense workflows or invoicing support; the regulated account layer for capital injections, trade settlement and FX hedging must remain with a licensed Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE should first complete SAFE foreign direct investment information registration and open a foreign exchange capital account at a licensed domestic bank. The offshore parent remits the equity injection via SWIFT MT103, or MT202 where a correspondent bank is used for the attributable bulk credit, using the Chinese foreign exchange receipt purpose code for direct investment equity contribution. After receipt, the WFOE applies to the bank for RMB conversion under the capital settlement payment mechanism; each RMB disbursement from converted capital must be consistent with the declared operational purpose, pass the bank's transaction authenticity and ratio review, and be reconciled with the capital verification report issued by a competent PRC certified public accountant. Shareholder loans require separate SAFE foreign debt registration, loan agreement filing and genuine-need assessment. For recurring smaller disbursements, direct SWIFT MT103 outbound payments are acceptable after the bank verifies the contract, commercial invoice, customs registration and goods-trade declaration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and effectively non-viable as a treasury account substitute. Non-bank electronic money institutions operating offshore are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under the PBOC and NFRA framework, cannot receive SAFE-designated capital account wires, cannot process export proceeds booking or surrendering requirements, and cannot clear RMB through CNAPS. Using such an EMI for the WFOE's own operating treasury would be treated as an unlawful settlement channel and would trigger PBOC and SAFE penalties. Their permissible role is limited to ancillary payment analytics, expense workflows or invoicing support; the regulated account layer for capital injections, trade settlement and FX hedging must remain with a licensed Chinese commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible property transfers in a fiscal year; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible property transfers, financial asset transfers, or service provision; RMB 40 million for all other related-party transactions; Master File is required where aggregate related-party transaction turnover reaches the RMB 1 billion statutory threshold or where a transfer pricing audit adjustment in prior three years equals or exceeds RMB 5 million.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a separate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the general anti-abuse rule under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form doctrine require that a treaty claimant be the true beneficial owner with real management, employees, premises, decision-making, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) in the claimed jurisdiction; the State Taxation Administration Notice No. 9 (2018) sets out the beneficial ownership factors, including whether the recipient company actively conducts business, bears risk, and does not simply act as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fictitious VAT invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes restitution of the underpaid tax plus a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; PRC Criminal Law Article 205 for fictitious special VAT invoices carries imprisonment up to life sentence in the most serious cases; Article 201 of the Criminal Law for tax evasion carries up to three years for ordinary amounts and three to seven years for substantial evasion exceeding such thresholds.

Director personal liability: Under the PRC Company Law Article 20 and its 2023 revised Article 23, courts may pierce the corporate veil where the WFOE was used to perpetrate fraud, causing directors and the actual controlling shareholder to bear joint and several liability for all unpaid tax, customs duty, VAT, penalties, and interest; the tax authority may simultaneously apply exit restriction blacklisting under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law prohibiting directors from leaving China; criminal conviction inserts personal imprisonment penalties directly upon the officer who signed the fraudulent VAT invoices and false tax returns.
Shell company treaty abuse and dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the General Anti-Abuse Rule of EITL Article 47 and the Beneficial Ownership test of State Taxation Administration Notice No. 9 (2018), re-characterizing the recipient as a conduit; the shortfall withholding tax, late interest, and penalties of 50% to 500% of the under-withheld amount are assessed under Tax Collection Law Article 63; if the arrangement is criminal in nature, Criminal Law Article 201 applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the WFOE to sign and submit beneficial ownership declarations known to be false become jointly and severally liable for the WHT shortfall through corporate veil piercing; the authorities may freeze the directors' personal assets, execute travel bans, and revoke visas and exit rights; criminal exposure includes imprisonment for up to seven years where the under-paid withholding tax is large, and additional fraud charges if the TRC or board minutes were falsified.
Transfer pricing mispricing and non-disclosure of offshore profit shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC EITL Article 41 empowers the tax authority to make an arm's length transfer pricing adjustment; Article 48 requires the taxpayer to add interest for underpaid tax; Article 44 allows deemed income determination where records are absent; under Tax Collection Law Article 64, failure to disclose and under-declare taxable income triggers a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax in addition to the tax adjustment; the related-party disclosure fine ranges from RMB 2,000 to RMB 50,000 under administrative rules.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the inaccurate transfer pricing documentation and the related-party disclosure suit may be held civilly liable to the company for its losses and personally responsible for the tax shortfall if the corporate entity is an instrument of fraud; the tax authority may blacklist the director as an exit-restricted person and seize personal assets located in China; where the profit shift is intentionally fraudulent and the underpaid tax exceeds the Criminal Law threshold, the director faces criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to seven years for serious tax evasion.
