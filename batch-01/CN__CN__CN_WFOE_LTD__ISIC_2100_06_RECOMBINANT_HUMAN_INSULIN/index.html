Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) as the statutory online portal for tax registration, filing, invoice validation, and export refund filings; local State Taxation Administration branches execute delegated administration and audit.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China, the WFOE obtains a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the license carries an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which automatically becomes the entity's taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID). The SAMR incorporation data is transferred through the Foreign Investment Single Window and the integrated government service platform to the competent local STA, completing tax registration without a separate TIN application. The legal representative then accesses the National Electronic Tax Bureau to activate the tax file, set fiscal calendars, and file the use of the USCC for VAT special invoice issuance. If the WFOE is a non-resident enterprise without permanent establishment, the foreign enterprise itself may separately register for VAT and CIT obligations via the electronic portal, but this does not apply to the CN_WFOE_LTD resident manufacturing entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The industrial manufacture of recombinant human insulin qualifies for the VAT zero rate and export rebate only when the goods leave China through a recognized customs port, are identified as exported in the electronic customs declaration, and are supported by commercial evidence and foreign-exchange receipt. The exporter must be a registered VAT general taxpayer; the VAT output tax on exported goods is zeroed and the corresponding input VAT becomes refundable if the export is substantiated. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied by the same WFOE are zero-rated only where place of supply, cross-border delivery, and use outside China are proven to the tax authority.

  • Commercial invoice

The WFOE must issue Full-Digital Electronic Invoices (fully digitized e-invoices) through the National Electronic Tax Bureau under the Golden Tax Phase IV architecture. All special VAT invoices are generated using tax-control software and are digitally encrypted, allocated a unique invoice number, QR-coded, and transmitted in real time to the STA for fiscal clearance before legal issuance. Any invoice not cleared and stamped by the tax system is invalid for input VAT deduction by the purchaser. Enterprises integrate accounting or enterprise-resource-planning systems with the electronic tax bureau for pre-issuance validation and post-issuance reporting.

The STA, in conjunction with China Customs and SAFE, may retrospectively examine export zero-rating files. If a zero-rated export is unsubstantiated or proceeds have not been repatriated within the statutory window, the previously zero-rated output VAT is clawed back, daily late-payment interest at 0.05% per day accrues from the original due date, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid may be assessed under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Collection and Administration; false declarations with intent are subject to Customs penalties and criminal referral for fraud or tax-evasion offenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed float with stringent foreign-exchange administration. Under the current-account framework, trade in goods and services is broadly convertible: a WFOE may transact through designated banks after authenticity and consistency checks, retain foreign currency in a current account, or convert receipts to CNY at the prevailing market rate. The capital account is not freely convertible: the WFOE's paid-in registered capital must be admitted through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account, converted only against actual operational expenditure, and monitored by the bank to prevent diversion into prohibited uses such as securities purchases or real estate outside the approved scope. Profit distribution to overseas shareholders is remitted as current-account income, but only after the annual statutory audit, corporate income tax final settlement, and a tax bureau certificate confirming no outstanding tax liabilities; the bank also requires a board resolution and withholding tax payment voucher. Practical hurdles include documentation-intensive FX purpose codes, strict trade matching, and the requirement to prove the genuine source and use of funds for every remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the post-2012 goods trade FX administration framework, there is no mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts; the exporter may retain up to 100% in a foreign exchange current account or settle up to 100% into CNY at enterprise discretion. However, export proceeds from goods trade must generally be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of export declaration unless SAFE has approved overseas retention, and each receipt must be verified against the matching customs export declaration in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), as the delegated foreign exchange administration authority under PBOC, supervises all onshore FX conversion, capital flow registration, trade verification, and profit remittance controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex are not licensed as commercial banks in China and are legally barred from maintaining domestic settlement accounts, performing SAFE-regulated FX conversion, or acting as the principal clearing institution for a WFOE. They may be used only as ancillary overseas payment visualization tools after the WFOE has funded them back-to-back from a licensed domestic bank; they cannot replace the onshore corporate banking relationship, FX purpose-code review, or SAFE trade verification. Domestic third-party payment institutions would require a PBOC payment institution license and still cannot perform bank-grade FX authenticity checks for goods trade. Therefore the legal viability is nil for principal cross-border clearing and only medium for offshore-side supplementary treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore investor must first complete SAFE inbound direct investment registration and then remit subscribed registered capital by SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code for investment in the registered capital of the WFOE. The funds are credited to the SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at a designated domestic tier-1 bank; the bank will debit this foreign-currency capital account and convert to CNY only when the WFOE presents real operating expenditures such as payroll, lease payments, raw material purchases, or equipment costs. The converted RMB is placed in the RMB capital account and strictly ring-fenced from prohibited uses such as securities investment or real estate acquisition. A CPA capital verification report is recommended for updating the registered capital record and for supporting later profit remittance or capital reduction compliance.Outbound settlement to overseas suppliers and technology partners should run through SWIFT MT103 against an underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and SAFE trade data matching; large capital equipment purchases should be supported by commercial import letters of credit to protect both the WFOE and the foreign beneficiary; documentary collections such as D/P or D/A are reserved for low-risk recurring suppliers with established trade history.
