Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic tax registration has zero monetary threshold and is mandatory from the date of incorporation. For general VAT taxpayer status, the mandatory turnover threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; taxpayers below this threshold may voluntarily register as general VAT taxpayers if they have fixed places of business, reliable accounting records, and proper VAT invoice control. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualification for 0% VAT export treatment requires the WFOE to be a VAT general taxpayer; the goods, including printed circuit board assembly (PCBA) manufactured under contract, must physically leave mainland China and be entered into the China Customs single-window export declaration system; title and risk in the goods must pass to an overseas non-resident buyer; export proceeds must be received in foreign currency and repatriated into China through a licensed bank; and the transaction must be compliant with the export tax rebate product classification and not disqualified as a deemed export. Contract manufacturing of PCBA qualifies where the assembled goods are exported and the input VAT on domestic components and processing services is legally claimable through the STA export rebate system. Qualifying cross-border offshore services and certain digitally delivered services to overseas recipients may also be zero-rated or exempt where statutory conditions are met and remuneration is received in foreign currency.

STA requires all VAT invoices to be issued exclusively through the State Taxation Administration's VAT Invoice System, including the Physical Tax-Control Invoice System under the Golden Tax Project IV and the Digital Electronic Invoice platform. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which current-account transactions are largely freely convertible, while capital-account transactions remain subject to registration, real-use, and quota controls. For this WFOE engaged in PCBA contract manufacturing, the full amount of the registered capital can be remitted in by foreign shareholders after the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) control filing and SAFE registration are completed through a domestic designated bank. The registered capital foreign exchange funds must first enter a capital escrow or capital settlement account and may be converted into Renminbi after authenticity checking of the intended use. Profits may be remitted offshore after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax final settlement, and submission of the tax payment certificates, the audited financial statements, the board profit-distribution resolution, and the FDI-related registration details. Repatriation is therefore affected by documentary friction rather than blanket unavailability. Although SAFE has relaxed settlement-for-actual-use rules, funds cannot be used beyond the approved business scope or in prohibited sectors of the Foreign Investment Negative List. Export proceeds from goods trade are current-account receipts and may be received in RMB or foreign currency, retained or settled, as long as the underlying trade transaction is truthfully declared and reconciled with customs and SAFE systems. However, offshore-holdings and intercompany royalty or service-payment remittances will be scrutinized for transfer pricing, thin capitalization, and economic substance; any suspected round-tripping funds can be blocked at the bank payment-screening layer. Overall, the currency is not freely convertible for speculative purposes, but genuine trade and investment flows can be cleared if the full evidentiary chain is maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds under this PCBA contract-manufacturing scope must be repatriated to a SAFE-designated domestic foreign-exchange account and declared as goods trade income in the Trade Administration Digital Monitoring System. Advance receipts are reportable within 30 days of fund arrival; for ordinary post-shipment collections, payment must be received by the commercial due date and reconciled against the customs export declaration. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% - China currently imposes no fixed percentage surrender requirement; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in its export proceeds account or convert into RMB at the onshore mid-price based rate, subject to bank verification of the export invoice, customs declaration, bill of lading, and contract.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legally not viable as a primary account platform for this China WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual account providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot issue RMB account numbers, cannot access CNAPS or SWIFT-clearing as domestic participants, and cannot perform SAFE FX settlement or capital-account filing obligations. Such providers may be used only as ancillary payment tools if they work through a licensed onshore bank as the actual settlement agent; however, the treasury backbone must remain with a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. Recommendation: avoid fintech EMI dependency for corporate liquidity and use only the domestic bank negotiable or quick-transfer channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File thresholds under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42: (1) Annual related-party goods transactions > CNY 200 million; (2) annual related-party financial transactions > CNY 100 million; (3) annual related-party services > CNY 40 million; (4) annual related-party intangible property transfers > CNY 40 million; (5) total related-party transactions > CNY 40 million and any one related-party transaction category exceeding CNY 20 million. Additionally, all enterprises must file the annual related-party transaction reporting forms (G100000-G114030) regardless of transaction value, and a Contemporaneous Documentation File must be prepared by May 31 of the following year unless the total related-party transaction value is below the de minimis threshold of CNY 20 million for a single type and CNY 40 million for all types.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, but has anti-avoidance rules requiring substantial business presence. Under CIT Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) in Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations, and the SAT's 'Beneficial Ownership' requirements (Announcement 2018 No. 9), an entity claiming treaty benefits or transfer pricing margins must demonstrate real business operations. For a PCBA contract manufacturer, the Chinese WFOE must show that it performs core manufacturing functions (PCB assembly, SMT, testing, quality control) and bears corresponding risks (inventory, warranty, equipment risk). It must not be a 'shell company' or a low-risk commissionaire. The economic substance is evidenced by physical manufacturing premises, personnel, production equipment, and entrepreneurial risk. CIGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) is relevant under OECD BEPS, and Chinese tax authorities may recharacterize the WFOE's income if it does not perform the core functions or lacks the capacity to control risks. Additionally, the 'Management Fees' and 'Cost Contribution Agreements' must reflect actual services rendered at arm's length. The State Administration of Taxation may apply the 'deemed profit' method (核定利润率) if the taxpayer fails to provide TP documentation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent Under-declaration of Profit and Related-Party Output

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Article 45; Tax Administration Law Article 63 (Tax Evasion): imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; additional daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day accrues from the original due date to the date of payment. The tax authority may also adjust taxable income using the deemed profit method under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 if documentation is missing. The amount of tax underpaid plus applicable penalties must be paid. If the evasion exceeds CNY 100,000 and constitutes 10% or more of the tax due, Criminal Law Article 201 applies, imposing up to 7 years' imprisonment and a criminal fine of up to double the tax amount for the responsible individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior management who designed or approved the fraudulent transfer pricing scheme will be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, interest, and penalties (Tax Administration Law Article 83). Chinese courts have pierced the corporate veil to hold directors personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax. Additionally, the SAT may impose a 'personal obstruction penalty' of up to CNY 50,000 per individual under TAL Article 70 if they destroy evidence or falsely simulate accounting records. Directors may face a travel ban (blacklisting) under the 'Joint Punishment for Serious Tax Violations' (重大税收违法失信案件) provisions, restricting flights and high-speed rail travel, and disbarring them from concurrent directorship in any enterprise. Criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment for 3-7 years and fines.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance - Misuse of Treaty to Avoid Chinese Tax

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 (Beneficial Ownership) and CIT Law Article 3(3) and Article 37 (imposition of withholding tax on non-residents without a PE). The tax authority will deny treaty relief and recharacterize the conduit company as not the beneficial owner, imposing full 10% withholding tax plus late surcharge. Under the GAAR (CIT Law Article 47), the tax authority may disregard the shell entity and reallocate income to the Chinese WFOE, resulting in an additional 10% corporate surcharge (if the reallocation increases the Chinese taxable income) and a penalty of up to 50% of the tax underpaid (TAL Article 63). The foreign entity will be subject to a 'not applicable for treaty' official determination.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Chinese WFOE who knowingly approved dividend flow-through to the conduit without verifying beneficial ownership may be deemed to have committed fraudulent tax evasion. They could be personally assessed for the unpaid withholding tax under TAL Article 86 (joint and several liability for illegal acts). The directors' personal assets may be traced and seized. Travel bans and visa denial apply under the 'rahvastiku talitluse' blacklist. Under Criminal Law Article 201, if the use of the shell entity resulted in tax evasion exceeding the criminal threshold, directors can be sentenced to up to 7 years' imprisonment and held jointly liable for the restitution amount.
Money Laundering / Bribery and False Invoicing (VAT and Criminal Liability)

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Articles 205 and 206 (VAT invoice fraud): issuing or using fraudulent VAT invoices to defraud tax can lead to imprisonment up to 7 years for serious cases; if the amount involved exceeds CNY 5 million, imprisonment can be up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property. Article 205 imposes fines of up to 5 times the defrauded tax amount, with the person directly in charge (director, CFO) criminally liable. Additionally, a false VAT invoice claim violates the Tax Administration Law Article 63, resulting in a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. The use of forged invoices is also a AML red flag under Article 25 of the Anti-Money Laundering Law, leading to administrative fines on the entity of up to CNY 5 million and on key responsible persons of up to CNY 500,000 each.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize the purchase of fake invoices are personally criminally liable, not shieldable by corporate limited liability. Under Criminal Law Article 205, the 'unit crime' doctrine makes the company and the directors jointly liable: directors face up to 7 years' imprisonment for issuing or using false special VAT invoices; if the fraud is deemed particularly serious (e.g., evading tax of several million RMB), life imprisonment can be imposed. Personal bank accounts of the directors and their immediate family members can be frozen, and they may be blacklisted from international travel. Additionally, under AML laws, directors who negligently fail to adopt internal controls face direct personal fines and prohibition from serving as directors in financial or trading companies. Restitution of the VAT and tax claimed, plus criminal fines, must be made from personal assets if the company cannot pay (piercing the corporate veil under the Company Law).
