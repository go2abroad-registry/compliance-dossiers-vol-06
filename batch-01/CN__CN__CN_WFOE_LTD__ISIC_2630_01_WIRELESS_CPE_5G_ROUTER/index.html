Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an ordinary WFOE under the Company Law or Foreign Investment Law. The registered capital is fully subscribed by the foreign shareholder and is payable in cash or non-cash in accordance with the capital contribution schedule in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow account is required by SAMR for a standard WFOE; however, when foreign currency share capital is remitted into China, the capital entering through a bank-designated capital account is subject to State Administration of Foreign Exchange verification, and any later decrease or increase of capital must be filed and published. For radio equipment manufacturing under ISIC 2630, no higher minimum capital applies, provided the subscribed capital is reasonably commensurate with the production scale and operating cost commitments.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety acceptance certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade must be obtained before occupancy of the production hall; the premises must have fire separation, emergency exits, automatic fire alarm and extinguishing system as required, with a certified annual fire protection maintenance contract. Structural load inspection for the machinery, racks, and warehouse area must be approved by a licensed structural engineer or the local construction quality authority. Occupational health approval requires a workplace hazard assessment covering soldering fumes, noise, electromagnetic radio frequency exposure, and manual handling, plus personal protective equipment plans and medical surveillance where exposure limits under Chinese occupational safety standards may be reached.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an ordinary WFOE under the Company Law or Foreign Investment Law. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through its local tax service bureaus and the STA online Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal, which is the single electronic gateway for taxpayer registration, VAT filing, issuance of digital VAT invoices, customs-related tax procedures, and export rebate claims.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE organized as a limited liability company, the company is first registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which assigns the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is recognized by the STA as the statutory taxpayer identification number for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, and all other national taxes. Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA tax service bureau through the e-Tax portal, declaring the registered address, legal representative, financial officer, bank settlement account, corporate shareholders, paid-in capital, and actual business scope. The WFOE then receives its electronic tax registration profile and is classified for VAT purposes. For a foreign non-resident parent without a permanent establishment in China, a separate parent TIN is not required for Chinese-source income because the WFOE acts as the statutory withholding agent; nevertheless, before remitting dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees to the parent, the WFOE must obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration/code in the STA withholding module and report the relevant contract through the e-Tax portal. The USCC is the master key for all filing, invoicing, and export reimbursement infrastructure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under the PRC tax system; China does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election. A WFOE is taxed as a resident enterprise on its worldwide income at the Enterprise Income Tax rate of 25%. The only relevant statutory elections are the small-scale VAT taxpayer simplification election (available only while annual taxable sales do not exceed CNY 5,000,000) and the special enterprise restructuring tax treatment under the Notice on Enterprise Income Tax Treatment of Enterprise Restructuring (Caishui [2009] 59) for qualifying reorganizations. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; China applies the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), transfer pricing rules, thin capitalization rules, and controlled foreign corporation rules as mandatory enforcement provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; all taxable persons below the threshold are registered as small-scale VAT taxpayers and must still obtain VAT registration upon commencing taxable activities, with no registration exemption for a WFOE limited liability company.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC Value-Added Tax Law and its implementing rules, exported goods are zero-rated when the goods are physically exported through Chinese customs under supervision, the exporter has foreign trade operation qualification and customs registration, the sale is made to a non-resident buyer, the customs declaration is truthful and matches the shipping and payment records, and the export proceeds are collected from outside China within the statutory timeframe. For the 5G wireless CPE router, zero-rating applies to the physical goods supply, not to any associated software or digital services; the WFOE must evidence that the goods departed the customs territory and that the input VAT embedded in the router units is supported by procurement VAT special invoices. Cross-border digital services are separately treated under the STA cross-border service VAT zero-rating and exemption measures and are not relevant to the physical export of the router inventory.

  • Customs export declaration record filed through the China International Trade Single Window, containing the HS tariff classification, declared value, quantity, destination country, and exporter information
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list issued by the WFOE matching the customs declaration
  • Bill of lading or multimodal transport document (or air waybill where applicable) showing export shipment from China to the foreign buyer
  • Foreign exchange collection voucher issued by a Chinese designated foreign-exchange bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the overseas buyer through the SWIFT or telegraphic transfer rails
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the non-resident customer setting out the payment terms, Incoterms, and product specification for the 5G wireless CPE router
  • Procurement VAT special invoices for component and finished-goods purchases supporting the export input VAT rebate claim
  • Chinese Customs electronic exit/release record generated upon actual departure of the goods from the customs territory
  • Export freight and insurance documentation, where applicable, to substantiate the recognized export cost and customs valuation

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice platform and its VAT invoice management system. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time through state-standard application programming interfaces (APIs) or directly through the official web portal. The platform applies digital anti-counterfeit stamps, encrypted invoice digest codes, automated bill verification, and the Golden Tax Phase IV risk-monitoring system to prevent tampering and false invoicing. No form of non-fiscal handwriting or override is permitted; every VAT invoice must be created, transmitted, numbered, and reconciled within the STA-authorized system before it is legally effective.

The STA and Customs jointly audit zero-rated export transactions using the Golden Tax Phase IV export-rebate risk engine. If the required customs documentation or foreign exchange collection record is missing, inconsistent, or unverified, the export zero-rating is disallowed, the sale is recharacterized as domestic sales, output VAT at 13% must be paid, and late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day accrues from the original VAT filing date. For fraudulent export refunds involving false declarations, forged customs documents, or circular cross-border transactions, the tax authority must recover the refunded amount, impose a penalty of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China where the amount is material. The collection period for clawbacks is three years for ordinary miscalculation, five years for specified computational errors, and unlimited for tax evasion or fraudulent refund acts. The WFOE can also be downgraded in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange A/B/C trade classification, losing simplified payment and rebate processing privileges.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividend remittances, and interest payments, subject to genuine transaction verification by designated foreign exchange banks under SAFE's 'purpose-based' documentation regime. Capital account transactions remain heavily regulated and are not freely convertible; all inbound direct investment, cross-border loans, securities investment, and foreign debt must be registered or approved by SAFE. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), registered capital paid-in via the capital account must be deposited in a RMB capital account or a foreign currency capital account, and any conversion to RMB for business operations must comply with the 'FDI Foreign Exchange Business Operation Guidelines' under a 'positive list' approach. Profit remittance and dividend outbound transfers are permitted on the current account once the enterprise provides audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and board resolutions confirming distributable profits. However, funds converted from capital accounts may not be used for equity investment, real estate speculation, or securities trading outside the approved business scope. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory in the traditional sense, but enterprises must repatriate export proceeds to China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, after which they may either retain the foreign currency in a settlement account or proactively settle it into RMB at any licensed bank. The effective convertibility is therefore asymmetrical: current account flows are liberalized with documentary oversight, while capital account flows require pre-approval, post-verification, and strict adherence to the actual-use principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods shipped from China must be repatriated to the mainland by the exporter within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration (per SAFE Circular [2019] No. 17 and the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations). The exporter may retain the receipts in a foreign exchange settlement account with a licensed bank or convert them into RMB voluntarily; there is currently no mandatory conversion ratio. However, for customs declaration values exceeding USD 50,000 equivalent, the exporter must provide the customs declaration and customs clearance data to the bank for electronic verification, and any delay beyond 180 days requires written justification and potential SAFE penalty.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Compulsory for every limited liability enterprise in China, including WFOEs, regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount. Article 164 of the Chinese Company Law (2018 revision) provides that all companies must prepare financial accounting reports at the end of each fiscal year and such reports must be audited by a legally established PRC accounting firm. The quantitative thresholds for the WFOE under ISIC 2630 (wireless router/5G CPE manufacturing) are therefore zero: any balance sheet, turnover, or employee headcount of more than zero triggers the statutory audit. Listed companies, financial institutions, and large state-owned enterprises face additional cumulative thresholds under the Securities Law and Ministry of Finance regulations, but those are not applicable to the standard WFOE limited liability structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200,000,000 for related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for related-party intangible property transfers, financial asset transfers, or service transactions; RMB 400,000,000 aggregate related-party transaction volume triggers contemporaneous Local File preparation; the Master File threshold is RMB 1,000,000,000 annual related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Although mainland China does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, the tax administration applies equivalent substance-over-form doctrines under CIT Law Article 45, Article 47, and the State Taxation Administration GAAR implementation rules. A WFOE manufacturing 5G routers must demonstrate core income-generating activities such as local procurement, manufacturing execution, inventory risk, credit and accounts receivable management, pricing support, and R&D decision-making; a shell or pure booking entity whose directors reside offshore and whose management is exercised by the parent will be disregarded and its income reattributed to the true economic owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Invoicing and Cost Inflation

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax, imposes daily late interest, and levies a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; Criminal Law Article 201 provides imprisonment of up to three years for relatively serious evasion and three to seven years for large evasion; Article 205 punishes false issue of special VAT invoices with up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property in the most serious cases.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil where a director or controlling shareholder mixes company funds with personal accounts, uses corporate revenue for private expenses, or deliberately destroys accounting books; under Company Law Article 20(3) the director is jointly and severally liable to restore company assets and compensate creditor losses. The legal representative faces an exit ban and travel restriction until the tax debt is paid; a criminal conviction exposes the responsible director to imprisonment and monetary fines, and civil restitution orders may attach personal bank accounts, real estate, and equity holdings.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Article 44, the tax authority may make a deemed taxable income adjustment; Article 121 and the implementation rules impose special tax adjustment interest on underpaid tax; Article 122 permits a five-year lookback period. Administrative fines under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 70 apply for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation, with aggravated fines for repeated refusal, and the adjusted profit is treated as unpaid CIT plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inaccurate related-party transaction reports or direct the accounting team to destroy transfer pricing evidence may be held personally liable in a civil compensatory action by the company or a creditor; in serious cases the authority may blacklist the legal representative and restrict departure; if the arrangement conceals criminal tax evasion, the director can be prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 for evasion and Article 162 for unlawful destruction of accounting books, with imprisonment exposure up to seven years and confiscation of hidden assets.
Treaty Shopping and Shell Entity Abuse of DTA Dividend Reduction

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty relief under State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 9 of 2018 on beneficial ownership and under CIT Law Article 47, recharacterize the transaction as a direct dividend to the ultimate beneficial owner, impose the full 10% dividend WHT, add late interest, and impose a penalty of 50% to 100% of the unpaid withholding tax under Article 64 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law; serious evasion escalates to the Article 63 penalty of 50% to 500% and may trigger criminal liability.

Director personal liability: If a director instructs the withholding agent to apply the WHT exemption knowing the foreign entity is a conduit, the director becomes a joint withholding-violation participant; the tax authority may order the withholding agent and its responsible personnel to pay the outstanding WHT personally where collection from the foreign shareholder fails. Civil veil-piercing principles authorize seizure of the director's personal assets for intentional abuse of corporate personality, and the director may face criminal prosecution for falsification of tax documents or fraudulent withholding declarations, with imprisonment terms up to seven years and an exit ban until the tax liability is settled.
