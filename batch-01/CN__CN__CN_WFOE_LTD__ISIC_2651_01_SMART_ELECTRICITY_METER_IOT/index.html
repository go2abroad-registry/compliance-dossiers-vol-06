Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. For an ordinary wholly foreign-owned limited liability company, the Company Law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up registered capital. The Articles of Association must specify a subscribed capital amount adequate for the planned smart-meter manufacturing and IoT operations. The foreign shareholder may agree a capital contribution schedule within the company's stated operational period, generally within 30 years. No blocked escrow account is required by law. However, contributions must be actually paid by cash transfer, appraisal of in-kind assets, or transfer of intangible assets, and each contribution must be recorded in the company's books and reported through the Ministry of Commerce foreign direct investment reporting system.

Funding & unlock path: Fire protection design review and completion acceptance certificate issued by the Fire and Rescue Section of the Ministry of Emergency Management under the Fire Protection Law; annual fire safety inspection certificate and evacuation plan approval; building structural load inspection certificate for the factory floor and storage racks; workplace safety approval under the Work Safety Law with the three simultaneous requirements covering design, construction, and acceptance of safety facilities; electrical work safety certificates for employees engaged in high-voltage metering assembly; occupational hazard assessment for small process areas involving soldering fumes and noise.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the electronic tax portal 'Xin Ban Dian Zi Shui Wu Ju' (新版电子税务局) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident enterprise establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in China, the legal representative must first obtain a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the enterprise registration system. The USCC automatically functions as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax and value-added tax purposes. After formation and issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete taxpayer status establishment (税务登记) with the local tax authority, which is automatically completed via data sharing between SAMR and STA. The tax authority will issue a tax registration certificate and assign tax-related information including the tax payer category, VAT general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer status, and filing frequency. The entity must then verify its tax registration through the electronic tax bureau, set up digital certificates (e.g., e-tax USB key or mobile authentication), and complete the pay-by-agreement (三方协议) bank account linkage for tax payments. Supporting documents include the foreign investor's Certificate of Incorporation or equivalent, the WFOE's Articles of Association, the business license, the legal representative's passport/ID, and the registered address lease or ownership proof.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For enterprise registration, all WFOEs are automatically registered for VAT upon business registration. For non-resident sellers without an establishment in China, the threshold for VAT registration and collection is RMB 1,000,000 per taxable period (or RMB 100,000 per month) under the VAT law's small-scale taxpayer standard for non-resident entities. However, a WFOE is a resident taxpayer and must register for VAT from inception regardless of revenue volume; small-scale taxpayer status applies when annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000, and general taxpayer status is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. Additional thresholds exist: RMB 500,000 for goods sold by small-scale taxpayers, and RMB 300,000 for services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT Law and the State Taxation Administration's Measures for Administration of Export Tax Refund (Exemption), zero-rating applies to goods exported by WFOEs that are legally exported out of China customs territory, provided the export goods have completed customs declaration, the sales proceeds are received in foreign exchange and repatriated into China, the export goods are physically shipped and evidenced by customs declarations, and the WFOE is a registered VAT general taxpayer with proper accounting. For qualifying exported goods, VAT is zero-rated and the corresponding input VAT on purchases of materials and services can be refunded through the export tax refund procedure (出口退税). Smart electricity meters classified under HS Chapter 90 (or relevant chapters for electrical measuring instruments) qualify if they are actually exported and satisfy the customs declaration rules. Certain software and technology services exported offshore may also be zero-rated, but the scope here is goods manufacturing. The taxpayer must apply for export tax refund/exemption using the electronic export tax refund system and meet the documentation requirements below.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (出口货物报关单) with verified 'Export' status and HS classification code
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical shipment out of China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate (出口收汇核销单) or bank system records evidencing foreign currency proceeds collected from overseas buyer
  • Export Sales Invoice in the statutory format matching the customs declaration value
  • Export Contract and Overseas Purchase Order demonstrating commercial substance and pricing
  • Packing list and shipping documents matching the commercial invoice and customs declaration
  • Proof of input VAT paid on materials used in production, such as special VAT invoices and customs import VAT payment vouchers
  • Foreign exchange receipt reconciliation statement from the designated foreign exchange bank
  • Customs declaration verification record from China Customs (中国国际贸易单一窗口)

China operates a nationwide digital invoicing system under the fully digital electronic invoice (数电票) reform. All VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the State Taxation Administration's 'Electronic Invoice Platform' (电子发票服务平台). Fully digital invoices carry a single 20-digit invoice number, require real-time tax control number (税控码) from STA's fiscal clearance server, and are transmitted via API or web interface. The platform validates the invoice against the enterprise's tax identification and automatically passes the invoice data to both the seller and buyer's tax files. Anti-fraud measures include mandatory real-time upload of invoice metadata to STA before issuance is completed, automatic comparison against declared VAT returns, and business process verification for special VAT invoices used for input deduction. Non-compliant invoices are rejected by the system. Physical block-chain based invoices have not been required since the full digital invoice rollout, but for certain industries legacy 'special VAT invoices' (增值税专用发票) may still be required to be printed via approved tax-control equipment and processed through the VAT Invoice Check Platform (增值税发票查验平台).

Under the PRC Export Tax Refund Administration Rules, fraudulent export refund claims are punished by recovery of the refunded VAT plus interest, a fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the illegally obtained refund under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, criminal liability for tax evasion under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export rebates, and suspension or revocation of export tax refund qualification. If proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline (normally 210 days; may be extended to 210 from the date of export declaration plus an allowable extension under specific conditions), the export is treated as a domestic sale subject to VAT at the applicable rate, and the exporter must pay VAT plus late fee penalties. For failure to repatriate foreign currency within 180 days, the tax authority may impose an additional levy equal to the VAT refunded and treat the refund as false export. Scrutiny is enhanced for hardware exports involving IoT devices given digital monitoring capabilities; the STA and General Administration of Customs may cross-validate customs data, foreign exchange receipts, and logistics information. Any false declarations, inflated price manipulation, or overclaimed input VAT refunds trigger retrospective clawback for a period of up to 10 years.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's current legal framework, the RMB is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, and royalties, subject to verifiable documentation and bank authenticity checks under SAFE's Know Your Customer requirements. The capital account remains administratively restricted: foreign direct investment into a WFOE must be registered through SAFE or an authorized bank, the business scope must comply with the Foreign Investment Negative List, and each capital injection, conversion, reinvestment, or liquidation remittance requires separate regulatory coding and documentary support. Export proceeds must be repatriated into China and included in the trade monitoring system, but since 2012 there is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio; the exporter can retain eligible foreign currency or settle it into RMB at a domestic bank. Repatriation of profits requires audited Chinese financial statements, corporate income tax final settlement clearance, a shareholder resolution on dividend distribution, and bank verification of the underlying paid-in capital and retained earnings. Practical hurdles include longer processing times for cross-border RMB, rigid bank internal compliance reviews, and the requirement that all documentation be consistent across customs, taxation, and foreign exchange data systems.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into China within the statutory period as monitored by SAFE's cross-border trade monitoring system; the default time frame for trade-in-goods is 180 days from the date of customs declaration unless amended by the bank under documented justifiable delays. There is no mandatory percentage surrender requirement; enterprises may retain eligible export receipts in a foreign currency settlement account or convert them to RMB at any authorized bank at market rate, subject to accuracy, consistency, and complete declaration in the trade in goods and services system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with SAFE as the specialized foreign exchange administration regulator under the State Council

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for the domestic CNY treasury spine. A China WFOE holds its onshore operating and capital accounts with licensed banks; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex are not authorized deposit-taking institutions or foreign exchange banks in mainland China. They cannot issue Chinese bank account numbers, clear RMB through CNAPS or SWIFT CNY rails, or act as the receiving or settlement bank for FDI capital verification. Their only permissible role is as a limited ancillary payment tool outside China; even then, all inbound and outbound flows must be supported by the same SAFE documentation and the WFOE must maintain its bank-controlled ledger for tax and audit.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment is routed as a SWIFT MT103 from the offshore parent to the WFOE's capital foreign exchange settlement account at a designated tier-1 bank. The remitter must be the registered shareholder; the payment instruction must reference the SAFE FDI registration number, the approved investment code, and the purpose code for inward direct investment capital, which is the foreign-invested enterprise capital injection category. After arrival, the bank, acting through or on behalf of SAFE, verifies the remittance and completes the capital account settlement into RMB. The WFOE must then obtain a capital verification report from a qualified Chinese CPA within the statutory filing window after settlement, and that report becomes the foundation for tax filings, contract performance, and eventual dividend repatriation.Trade disbursements should be processed via SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign currency settlement, with commercial invoices and customs declarations linked. For larger contracts, use irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by a Chinese bank, with presentation of transport, invoice, and inspection documents, or documentary collections on D/P or D/A basis when open-account risk is acceptable. Bank verification uses China's cross-border trade single window to ensure that the underlying transaction matches export or import customs records, preventing phantom trade or invoice manipulation.Onshore CNY/USD deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts with a licensed bank, limited to hedge genuine current-account or foreign-currency-denominated capital-account exposure, with documentary evidence of the underlying trade or investment flowCross-currency interest rate swaps and FX swaps allowed for managing foreign-currency debt or investment positions, subject to SAFE authenticity and necessity checks and a prohibition on speculative tradingNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore for a China WFOE as a speculative instrument; hedging must be conducted through deliverable forwards, currency options with genuine underlying exposure, and strictly documented current or capital account transactions

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for the domestic CNY treasury spine. A China WFOE holds its onshore operating and capital accounts with licensed banks; offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex are not authorized deposit-taking institutions or foreign exchange banks in mainland China. They cannot issue Chinese bank account numbers, clear RMB through CNAPS or SWIFT CNY rails, or act as the receiving or settlement bank for FDI capital verification. Their only permissible role is as a limited ancillary payment tool outside China; even then, all inbound and outbound flows must be supported by the same SAFE documentation and the WFOE must maintain its bank-controlled ledger for tax and audit.

Turnover: China does not impose a single universal balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold that exempts a WFOE from audit; instead, a WFOE is required to have an independent Chinese CPA audit because the annual Foreign Investment Information Report, profit remittance, liquidation, equity transfer, and SAFE-related filings must be supported by an audited financial report.
