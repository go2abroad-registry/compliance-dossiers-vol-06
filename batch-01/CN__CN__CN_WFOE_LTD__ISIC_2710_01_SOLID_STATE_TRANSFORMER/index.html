Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE under PRC law; however, the registered capital must be fully subscribed and paid in accordance with the schedule in the Articles of Association (typically within 30 years for manufacturing). Capital contributions must be injected via a foreign exchange capital account (FDI) and verified by the designated bank. No escrow account is required, but the capital must be paid in within the agreed timeline to avoid penalties.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department (Civil Defense) after structural inspection, including fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and fire alarm systems. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; principal compliance portal is the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, supplemented by the China International Trade Single Window for customs and export procedures.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Name pre-approval and incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the National Enterprise Registration Service Platform; no minimum capital is required for a WFOE limited-liability company. Step 2: SAMR issues the Business License and assigns the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for the WFOE; no separate TIN certificate is issued. Step 3: Complete tax registration and taxpayer confirmation in the STA Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of the Business License date; the incorporation record is automatically shared with STA. Tax details include the competent tax authority, legal representative, tax supervisor, accounting standards, and payroll declarations. Step 4: Activate VAT invoice quota and Fully Digitalized E-Invoice (FDEI) issuance capacity; submit the VAT general taxpayer election if annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 or if the WFOE elects general taxpayer status to claim input-VAT credits. Step 5: Report bank deposit accounts through the Electronic Tax Bureau and complete social insurance registrations. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment, the STA issues a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number or permits the Chinese payer or withholding agent to register and withhold on its behalf; this does not apply to the WFOE, which is a resident-taxable entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply the 0% export VAT rate to manufactured solid-state transformers where: (1) the goods physically leave PRC customs territory under an export customs declaration; (2) title and risk transfer to a non-resident buyer; (3) the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer with a complete input-VAT credit chain; (4) export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC-designated bank and reported under SAFE trade-in-foreign-exchange monitoring; and (5) the customs declaration, export invoice, bill of lading, contract, and bank receipt are mutually consistent. Production enterprises satisfying the criteria use the export exemption-credit-refund method, under which output VAT is zero-rated and residual input VAT is refunded.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (Customs Manifest / Bill of Export) generated through the China International Trade Single Window and bearing the 18-digit customs declaration number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE in the name of the foreign buyer
  • Packing List
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Voucher / inward remittance advice from a PRC-designated bank
  • SAFE Electronic Export Proceeds Verification Record (the Chinese statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate; China does not issue FIRC)
  • Export ordinary VAT invoice or Fully Digitalized E-Invoice for the export sale
  • Container loading report or customs seal record where applicable

China mandates issuance through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, using Fully Digitalized E-Invoices (FDEI) with near-real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA tax-control system. Digital invoices carry an STA anti-fraud digital stamp and QR code, enabling automatic verification and clearance. Large and high-volume taxpayers integrate through STA-approved API solutions (Enterprise Direct Link) for automated issuance and reporting. Physical tax-control-device stamping is being phased out in favor of the electronic fiscal platform. For export sales, the WFOE must issue an export ordinary VAT invoice or FDEI and link it to the customs declaration and foreign-exchange receipt through the China International Trade Single Window and SAFE systems.

The STA audits export VAT refunds by cross-matching data from China Customs and SAFE, including customs declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, invoice data, and payment rails. If zero-rating or refund eligibility cannot be substantiated, the STA claws back the refund with late-payment interest and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export-rebate claims additionally trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties including imprisonment up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property. The WFOE may also be downgraded in tax-credit rating, suspended from export-rebate processes, placed on a customs risk-monitoring blacklist, and subjected to administrative fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility for goods, services, profit remittances and dividends under IMF Article VIII, but the capital account remains firmly managed by SAFE. A WFOE may convert registered equity capital into RMB only after obtaining an FDI registration recordal, opening a designated foreign-exchange capital account with a domestic Tier-1 bank, and demonstrating a genuine underlying transaction through invoices, contracts and customs records. Profit remittances are permitted only after annual statutory audit, tax filing, tax clearance and a board resolution; the overseas parent then receives USD or EUR through a bank's standard investment-profits remittance procedure. In practice, operational liquidity is freely convertible, but structural capital transfers, derivative hedging and cross-border financing remain subject to pre-approval or bank recordal. Any round-tripping, speculative currency trading, or use of injected capital for off-scope investments will be blocked or unwound, and the bank must report suspicious use to SAFE under the trade-background principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the manufacture of high-voltage power transformers must be repatriated to mainland China via a SAFE-monitored domestic bank and entered into the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within the contractually agreed payment terms, generally no later than 180 days after the date of customs export declaration unless a SAFE extension is granted beforehand. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain the proceeds in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert them to RMB at the market rate. All foreign exchange receipts must be matched to genuine customs declarations, commercial invoices, packing lists and transport documents before settlement is accepted. Stand-alone surrender requirements no longer apply, but the full amount must be repatriated, not retained offshore.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) is the delegated foreign-exchange regulator enforcing capital controls and trade administration rules.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally restricted. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business and other neobanks are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to retain RMB deposits, open domestic corporate accounts, or participate in CIPS and China Financial Gateway clearing for a mainland WFOE. They cannot receive FDI capital as a receiving bank, cannot issue Chinese account numbers, and cannot act as settlement banks in cross-border LCs or trade collections. Their permissible role is limited to front-end foreign-exchange quotation and user interface, with the actual clearing executed by a licensed mainland bank or an offshore correspondent. For this WFOE, fintech EMI onboarding is therefore not a viable substitute for the Tier-1 bank program and is not recommended.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent remits equity injection via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated SAFE-recorded Foreign Exchange Capital Account at Bank of China Limited, with field 72 or purpose declaration stating FDI registered capital and equity contribution and the corresponding SAFE BOP purpose code 622. The receiving bank verifies the FDI registration code, entity name in the bank account, and the exact currency and amount against the recorded registered capital. Upon receipt, the WFOE may retain USD or EUR in the foreign-currency capital account or settle into RMB at the prevailing spot rate; settlement must be supported by invoices, purchase contracts, payroll schedules and other genuine transaction documents. A Chinese registered CPA then performs capital verification and issues a formal report, and the bank files the transaction with SAFE within the prescribed reporting window. Funds are ring-fenced for transformer manufacturing working capital and cannot be used for equity investment, entrusted loans, land purchase, or wealth management without SAFE permission.For imports of silicon steel, copper winding wire, cooling radiators, insulating materials and electrical switchgear components, the WFOE can execute open-account outward remittances via SWIFT MT103 to the overseas supplier after customs clearance, or use a SWIFT MT202 for bank-to-bank cover. For high-value capital equipment purchases, the preferred rail is an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit issued under UCP600 through the Tier-1 bank, with commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and certificate of origin presented; documentary collections D/P and D/A are acceptable only for repeat low-risk counterparties. Where the supplier accepts RMB, settlement should be routed through CIPS using the supplier's CIPS membership identifier, with the WFOE's customs declaration number and trade invoice identifier included in the payment instruction. All outbound payments require matching customs declarations, contract copies, and transport documentation before the compliance release.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD and RMB/EUR forward contracts with genuine trade-backed cash-flow documentation; maturity must match the underlying commercial contract and the hedge cannot exceed the contracted amount.FX swaps between spot and forward legs for temporary RMB/USD liquidity management during annual profit remittance cycles, limited to operating treasury needs and not for speculative carry trades.Cross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term USD or EUR capital-structure hedging, approved by the domestic bank under SAFE derivative eligibility rules and marked-to-market through the onshore interbank system.Zero-cost collar structures combining FX options for current-account hedging, purchased only from a licensed onshore bank; offshore non-deliverable forwards are not permissible for mainland corporate treasuries and are unenforceable in China.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally restricted. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business and other neobanks are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to retain RMB deposits, open domestic corporate accounts, or participate in CIPS and China Financial Gateway clearing for a mainland WFOE. They cannot receive FDI capital as a receiving bank, cannot issue Chinese account numbers, and cannot act as settlement banks in cross-border LCs or trade collections. Their permissible role is limited to front-end foreign-exchange quotation and user interface, with the actual clearing executed by a licensed mainland bank or an offshore correspondent. For this WFOE, fintech EMI onboarding is therefore not a viable substitute for the Tier-1 bank program and is not recommended.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: The legal threshold is effectively zero: under the PRC Company Law, every limited liability company, including a WFOE with zero minimum capital, must prepare annual financial statements and have them independently audited by a licensed CPA firm, regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover or headcount. Thus any positive balance-sheet asset balance, any operating turnover, or any employee headcount triggers the audit mandate; there is no small-enterprise audit exemption for a WFOE. Additional quantitative audit triggers arise only in regulated overlay categories such as securities listing, where CSRC and stock-exchange rules impose minimum net profit, revenue and asset thresholds for mandatory independent audits, and in financial-institution supervision, where balance-sheet and turnover thresholds determine the audit regime.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million total related-party tangible asset transactions; RMB 100 million related-party intangible asset or financial asset transactions; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions in aggregate (per SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42).

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in offshore hubs; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and SAT Bulletin 2012 No. 7 allow recharacterization of transactions lacking commercial substance. A WFOE must have actual employees, premises, equipment, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) within China for manufacturing; otherwise, tax benefits may be denied and withholding taxes imposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting Income / Inflating Costs

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law imposes recovery of unpaid tax plus a penalty of 50%–500% of the tax evaded; failure to pay may trigger a tax lien and asset seizure.

Director personal liability: If the evasion is committed by the company, the courts may pierce the corporate veil where the director personally directed or actively participated. Under Article 20 of PRC Company Law and tax case precedent, directors can be jointly liable for restitution of the tax loss. Travel bans and exit restrictions are imposed under Article 12 of the Exit-Entry Administration Law; criminal conviction under Article 201 of the Criminal Law may lead to up to 7 years' imprisonment and fines.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement – Failure to Maintain or Submit Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 and 45 of the EIT Law, tax authorities may make transfer pricing adjustments, impute interest on the resulting adjustment, and impose a 5% per annum penalty (surcharge) on the underpaid tax. The related party adjustment rules in Bulletins 2016 No. 42 and 2017 No. 6 also provide for a 50%-500% penalty for incomplete or false documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the TP documentation or knowingly implement pricing without arm's length analysis may be personally assessed for the secondary liability. In egregious cases, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 47) allows recharacterization, and the director's personal assets may be vulnerable to satisfaction of tax claims if the company's structure is deemed a sham. Criminal exposure for tax evasion may arise if the adjustment leads to tax underpayment exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the declared tax.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance Facilitating Cross-Border Abuse

Statutory sanction: GAAR under Article 47 EIT Law and SAT Bulletin 2017 No. 6 allow disregarding the legal form and recharacterizing income. Unilateral anti-avoidance provisions in the tax treaty (e.g., Limitation on Benefits provisions) may deny treaty benefits. Penalties under Article 123 TAL for false reporting; the tax authority can adjust taxable income and impose a 50%-500% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity risk personal liability for abuse of corporate form; Chinese courts may apply 'piercing the corporate veil' (Article 20 Company Law) when the WFOE's separate existence is used to escape tax obligations. Personal fines and criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent tax filings. Moreover, under the Foreign Investment Law and associated regulations, the enterprise could be blacklisted for serious tax violations, restricting the director's ability to travel into and out of China and managing other enterprises.
