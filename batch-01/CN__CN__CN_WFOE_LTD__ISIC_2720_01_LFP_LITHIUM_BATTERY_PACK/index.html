Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No national statutory minimum paid-up capital for a wholly foreign-owned LLC under the revised Company Law. Registered capital is determined by the sole shareholder and stated in the Articles of Association; it must reflect an amount commensurate with the fixed-asset investment and at least the initial three months of operating costs. Contributions may be denominated in RMB or agreed foreign currency, and the shareholder must fund the enterprise through a foreign exchange capital account established with a bank under SAFE's FDI system. There is no blocked escrow requirement at the state level; local development zones may condition incentives on staged capital deployment or an escrow guarantee.

Funding & unlock path: Must obtain the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade after completion of the building fire-protection design review and pre-acceptance test; the building within the manufacturing plot must pass structural-load acceptance for the weight of battery racks and charging systems. The worksite must satisfy the Law of the People's Republic of China on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases, including occupational hazard evaluation, monitoring for welding fume, dust and noise, fit-for-work medical certificates and employee training, and a safety production clearance under the Work Safety Law; storage of LiFePO4 battery packs must be separated into temperature-controlled charging and tooling zones to prevent thermal runaway.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing through the provincial STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portals, supported by the national Golden Tax System Phase IV and the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) tax registration database.

E-government filing portal

For a PRC WFOE, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license. After incorporation with the Administration for Market Regulation, the registration information is transmitted to the STA through the government one-stop data exchange, completing the information-based tax registration. The WFOE must then confirm its tax registration through the local e-Tax portal within 30 days, file bank account and accounting system records, elect or confirm its VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general taxpayer), and obtain invoice issuance entitlements. No separate TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is the single legal tax ID for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, and other taxes. A non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment would be addressed through withholding, but the CN_WFOE_LTD is a resident enterprise and holds direct tax registration under its USCC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic VAT tax registration; every PRC enterprise is registered for VAT upon incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales reach RMB 5,000,000. Below that threshold the entity is a small-scale taxpayer, and a monthly sales exemption of RMB 100,000 (or RMB 300,000 per quarter) may apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported LiFePO4 battery packs qualify for the 0% VAT rate with input VAT refund under the PRC export tax rebate regime when the goods are actually declared to customs and physically leave PRC customs territory, the exporter is the PRC WFOE that sells to an overseas buyer, and the transaction is supported by customs export declaration, transport document, commercial invoice/contract, and foreign exchange collection. As a production enterprise, the WFOE applies the exempt-credit-refund method for exported manufactured goods. Zero-rating is not available for mere domestic bonded-zone transfers without actual export, nor for goods whose sales proceeds are not repatriated in accordance with SAFE rules.

  • Official Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods (Customs Export Declaration) with customs release stamp, export classification and HS code for the LiFePO4 battery pack
  • Marine Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Export sales contract and international commercial invoice
  • Packing list and, where required, lithium battery transport/certification records for dangerous goods
  • Bank export proceeds collection voucher issued by the settlement bank upon receipt through SWIFT LC or TT wire, which functions as the Chinese equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); China does not issue a separate FIRC document
  • Complete VAT export rebate declaration package filed through the STA e-Tax portal, including export declaration data, VAT invoice details, and the export proceeds collection status report

China operates the National Electronic Invoice Platform and the Golden Tax System Phase IV. General VAT taxpayers issue Special VAT Invoices through the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform; each invoice carries a unique tax control number, QR code, cryptographic fiscal stamp, and is uploaded to the STA in near real time. The STA performs automated cross-checks against the USCC, invoice database, customs data, and the taxpayer's VAT returns. Input VAT on Special VAT Invoices is denied unless the supplier invoice has been matched and verified in the national invoice database. Fully digital electronic invoices are the default for most registered enterprises.

The STA, General Administration of Customs and SAFE jointly match export declarations, export VAT rebate filings, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If the WFOE cannot substantiate zero-rating or fails to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days (or an approved statutory exception), the STA will claw back the refunded VAT with daily late-payment surcharge from the date of refund, require the export to be reclassified as a domestic taxable sale, and may impose an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax/refund amount involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and its Implementing Rules. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may trigger suspension or revocation of export rebate qualification, red-list credit risk consequences, and criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud, including imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current account items (goods, services, earnings) are generally convertible under the IMF Article VIII, provided there is a genuine trade background, verified documentation, and compliance with the Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures. Capital account items are heavily regulated: all inbound direct investment must be registered with SAFE, and opening a foreign exchange capital account requires a certified Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration code. Profit remittances are permitted only after annual statutory audit, full tax settlement, board resolution, and audited financial statements filed with the onshore bank, and surpluses must be evidenced by foreign currency receivable account statements. Renminbi is not freely deliverable offshore; offshore non-deliverable market (NDF) pricing persists because onshore spot and forward markets are concentric on actual trade or capital flows. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory; companies may retain foreign exchange in conventional accounts but must repatriate proceeds into China, and capital account conversions are limited to purpose-specific sales. In practice, mismatches between the capital injection schedule, business scale, and transaction pattern trigger enhanced due diligence and potential suspension of settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The export settlement and verification system mandates that non-invoiced foreign exchange proceeds from physical exports be repatriated into China within 90 days from the date of customs clearance. With the 2012 SAFE reform, the mandatory surrender (cover) requirement was abolished; exporters may either retain proceeds in foreign exchange settlement accounts or convert into RMB at their FX bank, provided that the transaction is captured in the ASOne system, supported by customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and collection documentation. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; however, the full amount must be credited to the exporter's account in China. For advance collections, funds must be registered on the trade monitoring platform within 30 days, and discrepancies exceeding a 5% threshold trigger documentary review by the bank.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBoC) acting in concert with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) do not possess a legal Persons License from PBoC and are therefore legally barred from providing account-held deposits, clearing, or foreign exchange settlement services to China-resident enterprises. A WFOE cannot use an offshore EMI as a primary treasury account; such platforms may only serve as a pass-through payment processor if they have a licensed onshore partner (i.e., via a licensed third-party payment institution), but funds must be held at a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank in China. Inbound capital injections and outbound trade settlements must be routed through a SAFE-registered domestic bank account. Any off-balance-sheet arrangement with an unlicensed EMI would create a compliance and money-laundering vulnerability, violate SAFE's administered-flow rules, and expose the WFOE to penalties and freezing of assets. Hence, feasibility is rated Low for primary banking and Medium only as a non-custodial payment gateway with an onshore licensed PSP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Forced FDI equity injection routing: a shareholder resident outside mainland China must wire funds from its own bank account into the WFOE's Foreign Currency Capital Settlement Account at a Tier-1 domestic bank using SWIFT MT103/MT202. The remittance must be labelled with a SAFE-recognised transaction purpose code - typically '622' for a non-resident shareholder's equity injection for establishment of a foreign-invested enterprise, and the beneficiary account name must match the FDI Registration Code. The receiving bank will require the Foreign-Invested Enterprise's Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, a capital verification report from a Chinese CPA, and an internal account-opening file. Once funds are credited, the enterprise can use the funds for actual business expenses after converting to RMB via the Capital Account Settlement and Sale form, submitting invoices 15 working days in advance to the bank's compliance desk. Shareholder loans must be separately registered under the Foreign Debt Registration system.Trade disbursements can be made via (1) SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers against soft-copy invoices for low-risk open account transactions; (2) commercial documentary L/Cs issued at a Tier-1 domestic bank under UCP600 for import of lithium cathode active material, conductive agents, and battery management system panels, with documents negotiated via the beneficiary's bank; (3) documentary collections (CAD/DP) for moderate-risk relationships where the exporter forwards shipping documents through the negotiating bank with the WFOE paying upon sight. All payments must carry SAFE trade purpose codes (e.g., 1102 for imports of finished goods / raw materials under non-resident trade), and match against the customs declaration within 90 days; discrepancies beyond $10,000 per item or 5% trigger SAFE verification and possible freezing. For services such as software licenses (for battery management algorithms), use purpose code 2280 with a contract and invoice.Deliverable CNY/USD foreign exchange forward contracts (also known as trade settlement forwards) are permissible for current account hedges; a WFOE must document a genuine trade exposure (underlying import/export contract, invoice, and customs declaration) and transact with its Tier-1 bank under the SAFE Real Demand principle, standard tenors from one week to one year, with up to 20% notional variation permitted via sourcing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for a WFOE; only offshore non-resident entities may trade NDFs on CNY, and any offshore NDF must not be used to hedge an onshore entity's risk without violating SAFE's rules on deliverability and offshore-onshore linkage. Therefore, treat NDFs as off-limits for the WFOE treasury.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) are permissible only where the WFOE has a legitimate cross-border loan or trade debt, duly registered with SAFE; the bank will allow a plain-vanilla CNY/USD swap to convert coupons and notional, but the notional cannot exceed the underlying registered external debt, and settlement must be physical rather than netted.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex) do not possess a legal Persons License from PBoC and are therefore legally barred from providing account-held deposits, clearing, or foreign exchange settlement services to China-resident enterprises. A WFOE cannot use an offshore EMI as a primary treasury account; such platforms may only serve as a pass-through payment processor if they have a licensed onshore partner (i.e., via a licensed third-party payment institution), but funds must be held at a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank in China. Inbound capital injections and outbound trade settlements must be routed through a SAFE-registered domestic bank account. Any off-balance-sheet arrangement with an unlicensed EMI would create a compliance and money-laundering vulnerability, violate SAFE's administered-flow rules, and expose the WFOE to penalties and freezing of assets. Hence, feasibility is rated Low for primary banking and Medium only as a non-custodial payment gateway with an onshore licensed PSP.
