Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None for an ordinary WFOE under PRC law. The revised Company Law adopts subscribed capital with no statutory minimum. The WFOE must, however, state a registered capital that is reasonably matched to the total investment under the local Foreign Investment guidance and must specify a contribution period not exceeding the legal ceiling (commonly up to 30 years) in the Articles of Association. No statutory backend escrow account is mandated; the sole shareholder may make in-kind or cash contributions and, where requested by banks or counterparties, obtain a capital verification report from a licensed PRC certified public accountant.

Funding & unlock path: The factory must obtain a Fire Control Design Review and a Fire Control Completion Acceptance Inspection Certificate from the Fire and Rescue Department of the Ministry of Emergency Management before operation. The fire safety plan must address sodium-ion cell fire hazards, including Class D extinguishing agents, dry powder systems, battery thermal runaway containment, emergency egress and smoke management. Structural load inspections of battery racks and storage areas must be certified by a licensed structural engineer. In addition, the WFOE must complete an occupational-disease hazard project filing, a workplace safety pre-evaluation, and receive a work safety inspection under the Law on Work Safety and the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (STA e-Tax platform) and the China International Trade Single Window for customs-related tax filings

E-government filing portal

Upon business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is automatically assigned a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes. Within 30 days after obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent STA tax authority through the STA e-Tax portal, submitting the articles of association, SAMR business license, legal representative identity documents, registered address proof, bank account information, business scope details, foreign investor qualification/certificates, and appointment documents for the legal representative and financial officer. The STA will finalize the taxpayer registration record, determine the VAT taxpayer type (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), and activate the entity for VAT invoice issuance. The USCC is thereafter used as the VAT registration number and corporate income tax identifier for all filings, assessments, and refund procedures.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (Chinese resident enterprise; the WFOE limited liability company is taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment to foreign shareholders) — Upon business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is automatically assigned a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes. Within 30 days after obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent STA tax authority through the STA e-Tax portal, submitting the articles of association, SAMR business license, legal representative identity documents, registered address proof, bank account information, business scope details, foreign investor qualification/certificates, and appointment documents for the legal representative and financial officer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Chinese resident enterprise; the WFOE limited liability company is taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment to foreign shareholders)

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or elective hybrid mismatch classification. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese resident enterprise for corporate income tax purposes. Controlled foreign corporation rules and general anti-avoidance rules under the Enterprise Income Tax Law may recharacterize income or impose additional adjustments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory immediately upon business registration regardless of turnover. For compulsory general VAT taxpayer status, the statutory turnover threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the WFOE may be registered as a small-scale taxpayer using the simplified 3% levy method or may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of finished sodium-ion battery cells is eligible for VAT zero-rating, applied as exemption from output VAT plus refund of input VAT (export tax rebate), provided the goods are physically exported, customs-declared, sold to an overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated into China within the statutory foreign exchange deadline. The WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer with export business filing and must submit a correct and truthful export declaration through the China International Trade Single Window. Zero-rating may also apply to qualifying offshore services or digital services where the services are rendered entirely outside China and the supporting contract, payment evidence, and service delivery documentation demonstrate overseas consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration / Bill of Entry) generated through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical exportation of the battery cells from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation issued by a designated Chinese bank
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order between the WFOE and the overseas buyer
  • VAT invoice issued in accordance with export VAT regulations

The WFOE must issue VAT special invoices or VAT ordinary invoices through the STA-controlled electronic invoice system. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are cleared in near real time through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with centrally allocated invoice numbers and direct digital transmission of invoice data to the tax authority under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. API-based direct e-invoicing is available for integrated enterprise resource planning systems. Paper VAT invoices, where still used, require tax authority invoice number allocation and the use of an approved fiscal control device. Anti-fraud controls include automatic invoice verification, exception screening, risk-based red flags, and mandatory blacklist reporting for abnormal invoices.

Export zero-rating and VAT refund claims are subject to post-clearance risk assessment, documentary verification, and tax audit. If the required customs, transport, invoice, or foreign exchange repatriation documentation is missing, false, or inconsistent, the export zero-rating is denied and the transaction is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale, triggering VAT at 13%, recovery of any refunded input VAT, statutory late-payment surcharges, and administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Collection. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims may constitute criminal fraud in obtaining export tax rebates under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, carrying fines, confiscation, and imprisonment; retrospective clawback may extend to all related input VAT credits, export rebate years, and affiliated party documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for ordinary current-account trade and services, but convertibility is conditional on documentary verification through SAFE's electronic monitoring platform. Capital-account transactions, including FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border guarantees, require registration, approval, or post-event reporting. The WFOE must complete an FDI registration certificate before opening a dedicated capital settlement account and is subject to the negative-list 'willing settlement' regime for converting foreign currency capital into RMB. Profit remittance to the overseas shareholder is allowed only after local statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax settlement, and a formal board resolution; the bank verifies the SAFE annual FDI census and the genuineness of the remittance request. Practical hurdles include five-year retention of underlying trade documents, total-amount matching of export receipts and import payments with customs declarations, and the prohibition on using settled capital for entrusted loans, equity investments, or real-estate purchases outside the approved business scope. With a disciplined documentary pipeline, the WFOE can operate a compliant treasury within this controlled convertibility environment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China currently imposes zero mandatory surrender. The WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or sell them voluntarily at market rates. The statutory control mechanism is SAFE's goods-trade total-amount verification system; receipts must be credited, reported, and reconciled against export customs declarations. Advance collections or delayed collections differing from the customs declaration date by more than 90 days must be reported through the bank under SAFE's trade credit and debit reporting rules. Persistent mismatches can degrade the company from A-class to B/C-class and trigger per-transaction verification, documentary scrutiny, and reduced clearing flexibility.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

RATING: LOW / VOID FOR THE MAINLAND ENTITY. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed banks in China and cannot legally hold RMB deposits, settle domestic invoices, or clear cross-border trade and capital payments for the WFOE on a principal basis. PBOC, CBIRC, and SAFE regulations require all domestic receivables and payables to be settled through licensed Chinese commercial banks with qualified FX business. Offshore EMIs may be used only in the offshore holding company's treasury as payment utilities, not as the WFOE's bank. Any attempt to route trade proceeds or injected equity through a non-bank EMI into the mainland entity will create bank sanctions risk, FX-control violations, and PBOC enforcement exposure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The equity injection must be remitted from the direct overseas shareholder to the WFOE's dedicated FDI capital settlement account at a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank branch. The wire should identify the WFOE full legal name, address, account number, and reference the SAFE FDI registration certificate and the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code for inward direct investment capital contribution. Third-party remitters are prohibited. After credit, the bank logs the FDI inflow, obtains the current-account or capital-account verification, and the WFOE instructs a licensed Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report. The WFOE may then apply for voluntary settlement of foreign-currency capital into RMB subject to the SAFE negative list. All documents, including the FDI registration certificate, board resolution, capital-use plan, bank advices, and CPA report, must be archived for inspection.For import of raw materials, components, and machinery, settlement rails include bank-issued commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT701) with documents-against-acceptance or documents-against-payment terms, direct outward MT103 remittances against validated customs declarations and invoices, and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) processed through the bank's trade-services department. For export sales of sodium-ion battery cells, the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 receipt on the company's collection account, supported by export customs declarations and sales contracts. Standby letters of credit and documentary collections may be used for open-account and cross-border trade risk mitigation. Cross-border RMB trade can alternatively be cleared through CIPS while SWIFT remains the messaging layer for non-RMB transactions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: RATING: LOW / VOID FOR THE MAINLAND ENTITY. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed banks in China and cannot legally hold RMB deposits, settle domestic invoices, or clear cross-border trade and capital payments for the WFOE on a principal basis. PBOC, CBIRC, and SAFE regulations require all domestic receivables and payables to be settled through licensed Chinese commercial banks with qualified FX business. Offshore EMIs may be used only in the offshore holding company's treasury as payment utilities, not as the WFOE's bank. Any attempt to route trade proceeds or injected equity through a non-bank EMI into the mainland entity will create bank sanctions risk, FX-control violations, and PBOC enforcement exposure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where prior-year related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200,000,000 for tangible property transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for intangible property transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40,000,000 for other related-party transactions (services, loans, royalties, and commissions). Master File preparation is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1,000,000,000; contemporaneous documentation must be filed with the STA within 15 days of the annual return deadline, and any related-party transaction can be challenged regardless of thresholds where pricing is not at arm's length.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form doctrine operates through EIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rules), Article 45 (Controlled Foreign Corporation rules), SAT Announcement [2015] No. 60 (beneficial ownership anti-abuse), and judicial anti-abuse principles. For a sodium-ion battery WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include procurement of raw materials, production scheduling, cell chemistry R&D, quality control, inventory and logistics management, product pricing, warranty risk management, and strategic management exercised by directors and executives physically present in China. A WFOE without employees, assets, factory, operational expenditure, and decision-making functions will be treated as a shell, and its profits and treaty benefits will be recharacterized or denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 and 48 authorize the STA to recharacterize the related-party transaction, adjust taxable income, impose secondary transfer pricing adjustments, and charge 5% annual interest on the tax adjustment; TCAL Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50%-300% of the evaded tax where the conduct is intentional or fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns, issue false intercompany invoices, or knowingly direct the underpricing scheme may be jointly and severally liable for the tax adjustment out of personal assets; exit bans and travel blacklisting may be imposed on directors with unsatisfied tax obligations; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years, plus fines and confiscation.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied under STA Announcement [2015] No. 60 and EIT Law Article 47; the full 10% statutory WHT becomes immediately payable, plus daily 0.05% late-payment interest and a 50%-300% penalty under TCAL Article 63 for false beneficial-ownership declarations; the non-resident entity is blacklisted in tax-integrity records and may lose all treaty access for subsequent years.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false beneficial-ownership forms, sign sham dividend resolutions, or authorize dividend payments to a conduit without substance face personal civil liability for the under-withheld tax; travel bans are automatically issued for directors responsible for unpaid withholding liabilities; criminal liability for fraudulent tax avoidance and concealment under Criminal Law Article 201 can lead to imprisonment for 3-7 years.
False Deduction, Invoice Fraud, and Illegal Cross-Border Remittance

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 32 imposes daily late-interest charges; Article 63 imposes a 50%-300% penalty on evaded tax; Criminal Law Article 205 imposes severe criminal penalties, including imprisonment up to 10 years and fines for false invoicing; SAFE may impose penalties of 30%-100% of the illegal outward remittance under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, and the AML risk classification will be escalated to enhanced due diligence.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in the false invoice or remittance scheme are jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts when the WFOE cannot satisfy the assessment; the responsible persons are placed on the tax non-payment blacklist with exit bans and travel restrictions; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205 can result in imprisonment for up to 10 years, personal fines, and disqualification from serving as directors under PRC Company Law.
