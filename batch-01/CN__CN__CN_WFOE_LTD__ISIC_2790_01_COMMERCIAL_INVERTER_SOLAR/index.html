Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online compliance runs through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal with the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system used for VAT invoice reconciliation and electronic fiscal clearance.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporation: the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is incorporated under the Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), and the issued business license contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which operates as the mandatory taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax, VAT, and other consolidated taxes. 2) Tax registration completion: within the statutory period after the business license, the WFOE files tax registration information confirmation through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, submitting legal representative identity, registered address, capital structure, business scope, bank account details, and accounting/fiscal system information to the competent STA office. 3) Taxpayer activation: the STA activates the entity's resident enterprise status and the applicable tax returns/filing obligations, including enterprise income tax, VAT, individual income tax withholding on employees, stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax, and education surcharges. 4) VAT status: the entity applies for general VAT taxpayer status (mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, or voluntarily if accounting records are complete) and receives a VAT invoice quota in the Golden Tax system. 5) E-invoice setup: the entity registers for the fully digital electronic invoice platform and, where required, configures the STA-approved electronic invoice data interface/API for ERP-based issuance. 6) Non-resident alternative: a foreign non-resident enterprise with China-source income and no permanent establishment must complete non-resident tax registration through the e-Tax portal or in-person with the competent authority, obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number/certificate, and rely on payer withholding obligations; a WFOE is not a non-resident taxpayer for national tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election for income tax. A WFOE limited liability company is compulsorily classified as a resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income. VAT status (general vs. small-scale taxpayer) is an administrative election/registration choice, not an entity classification election; BEPS Action 2-style hybrid outcomes are countered by the general anti-avoidance rule and controlled foreign corporation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is automatic from the issuance of the business license; there is no minimum turnover threshold for registration. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, while entities at or below that threshold may remain small-scale VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods export zero-rating applies only where the solar inverter/equipment is physically loaded and exported from China's customs territory; the exporter must be a general VAT taxpayer; the export must be evidenced by an electronic Export Goods Customs Declaration; the goods must not be prohibited or restricted from export; sales proceeds must be received from an overseas buyer in convertible foreign exchange and repatriated/settled within the statutory foreign-exchange window; and the exporter must file the export sale in the VAT 'exemption, credit, and refund' system to claim the 0% export rate and refund of residual input VAT. Qualifying cross-border services, including certain technology and after-sales digital services consumed entirely outside mainland China, may also be zero-rated when the overseas recipient, overseas consumption, service contract, and foreign-exchange receipt are documented.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration; the export-equivalent bill of entry)
  • Export sales contract or purchase order signed with the offshore buyer
  • Commercial invoice issued to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill (original/electronic negotiable transport document evidencing physical exit)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank/SAFE export-proceeds collection and settlement certificate
  • VAT invoice for the export sale as required in the export-rebate filing package
  • Export tax refund declaration form (exemption and refund/exemption-only) and the electronic data record transmitted through the STA-Customs export information system

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled electronic VAT invoice management system (Golden Tax Phase IV and the fully digital electronic invoice platform). Special VAT invoices carry unique encrypted invoice numbers and are uploaded in real time to the tax authority; issuance, red-letter reversal, purchase-side confirmation, and tax-rate validation are performed electronically. Anti-fraud controls include cryptographic signatures, automatic line-item transmission, statutory invoice serialization, and mandatory digital stamping/sealing; ERP/accounting system issuance is only valid when connected through the STA-approved official electronic invoice interface/API.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to retrospective STA and China Customs cross-reconciliation. If any customs declaration, transport document, foreign-exchange receipt, or commercial trade document is false, incomplete, or uncorroborated, the claimed 0% rate is revoked, the 13% output VAT is assessed, late-payment surcharge is imposed at 0.05% per day, and administrative penalties of 50% to five times the tax evaded apply under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export-rebate applications also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds, with fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment in aggravated cases; tax authorities may additionally suspend or revoke the exporter's export-rebate filing qualification and general VAT taxpayer status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the RMB under a current-account convertible framework. Current-account items, including trade in goods, services, and operational profit remittances, are conditionally convertible: banks verify the genuineness of the underlying transaction through contracts, commercial invoices, customs declarations, and tax certificates. Capital-account items remain highly regulated. FDI equity injections, cross-border loans, and repatriation of invested capital require SAFE registration or approval and are subject to the Foreign Investment Access Negative List. There are no statutory surrender quotas for export proceeds; exporters may retain foreign currency in domestic accounts or sell it on the interbank market at market-determined rates. Profit remittance is practical but requires corporate income tax clearance, dividend withholding tax payment, audited financial statements, and the bank's compliance review. Because the solar-inverter business generates genuine trade flows and the WFOE has no statutory minimum registered capital, the treasury should maintain separate CNY and FCY accounts, register every cross-border capital movement with SAFE, and maintain a full audit trail to satisfy the 'real demand' standard for current-account convertibility, especially for trade settlement and profit distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds to the central bank. Under China's current-account total-volume monitoring framework, export goods proceeds must be repatriated to the exporting WFOE's domestic account through a designated licensed bank; the inward remittance must be matched against customs export declarations and should normally be collected within 90 days from the date of export declaration for general trade. The WFOE may choose to retain export receipts in a current-account foreign-exchange deposit account or convert them to CNY at market rates. Any overdue, unreconciled, or artificially inflated export proceeds will trigger SAFE administrative scrutiny, potentially lower the entity's trade facilitation classification, and require supplemental documentary evidence.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated authority for foreign-exchange administration in China (Mainland)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking relationship in Mainland China. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise, MultiPass, and Currenxie are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic RMB settlement accounts, issue Chinese corporate account numbers, or clear CNY through CIPS. They cannot legally replace a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank for trade settlement, VAT/tax payment, payroll, or SAFE-mandated FX transaction filings. At most, an offshore EMI may be used by the parent or a Hong Kong treasury vehicle as a concentration wallet for non-CNH currency collections, but the WFOE must maintain its onshore treasury and all cross-border trade flows through a licensed Chinese bank. Any attempt to use an EMI as an orchestrating account for the PRC entity will be rejected at onboarding and will trigger AML red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the overseas parent's capital injection via a SWIFT MT103 wire to the WFOE's designated FDI capital account at a licensed Chinese bank. In Field 70, reference the SAFE FDI registration number and state 'FDI REGISTERED CAPITAL INJECTION'. Use the SAFE-designated Balance of Payments purpose code for inbound direct investment equity injection; the receiving bank will map this in its local BOP reporting system under the inward direct-investment capital category. The wire amount must match the amount recorded in the SAFE FDI information system; funds are posted to the capital account and cannot be released to the CNY operating account unless the negative-list compliance review passes. After receipt, complete a statutory capital verification with a licensed CPA firm if required, and record the contribution in the company's registry and accounting books. All subsequent conversion of capital funds into CNY must be for permitted operating expenses within the WFOE's actual business scope.For genuine trade settlements, use standard SWIFT MT103 wires for operational payments and for payments evidenced by customs declarations and commercial contracts. For large procurements of solar inverter components, open commercial letters of credit using SWIFT MT700 issuance and MT707 amendment messages, with the beneficiary receiving payment against conforming shipping documents. Documentary collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) are acceptable for established counterparties and lower-risk periodic transactions, with the bank acting only as courier and remittance processor. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary banking relationship in Mainland China. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Wise, MultiPass, and Currenxie are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic RMB settlement accounts, issue Chinese corporate account numbers, or clear CNY through CIPS. They cannot legally replace a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank for trade settlement, VAT/tax payment, payroll, or SAFE-mandated FX transaction filings. At most, an offshore EMI may be used by the parent or a Hong Kong treasury vehicle as a concentration wallet for non-CNH currency collections, but the WFOE must maintain its onshore treasury and all cross-border trade flows through a licensed Chinese bank. Any attempt to use an EMI as an orchestrating account for the PRC entity will be rejected at onboarding and will trigger AML red flags.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No size-based safe harbor applies: a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise limited liability company in China is mandatorily required by Article 164 of the PRC Company Law to have its annual financial report audited every fiscal year by an independent Chinese certified public accountant firm licensed by the Ministry of Finance, regardless of balance-sheet size. For quantitative classification and filing purposes: (a) industrial enterprises with annual operating revenue equal to or above RMB 20 million are classified as enterprises above the designated size by the National Bureau of Statistics and are subject to full audited financial disclosure duties; (b) enterprises with total assets above RMB 50 million, annual turnover above RMB 30 million, or employees above 300 are treated as medium or large enterprises and cannot adopt the simplified Small Enterprise Accounting Standards; (c) a WFOE meeting the NBS medium-enterprise threshold of RMB 20 million revenue and 300 employees must include related-party transaction reporting, transfer-pricing documentation and intercompany account verification in the audit scope; (d) even a WFOE using simplified Small Enterprise Accounting Standards remains subject to a mandatory independent CPA audit because foreign-invested enterprise status carries no audit exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Yes, the threshold is RMB 200 million or more of aggregate related-party transactions for the year, requiring a contemporaneous Local File and Master File under Announcement 2016 No. 42; additionally, the Annual Related Party Transaction Report Form (A100000) must be filed by May 31, and a Transfer Pricing Special Tax Investigation adjustment risk attaches to transactions exceeding RMB 40 million for a single controlled transaction even below the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under EIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Article 120, requiring commercial substance and reasonable business purpose for intercompany arrangements. While China is not a member of the OECD Inclusive Framework's mandatory ESR in the same manner as zero-tax jurisdictions, Chinese tax authorities assess Core Income-Generating Activities ('CIGA-equivalent economic substance') for intangible transfers and offshore treasury structures; the WFOE must prove actual personnel, decision-making, risk assumption, and corporate assets supporting solar inverter manufacturing. The value of core functions (manufacturing, risk control over product liability, and quality assurance) must be adequately compensated; a 'cash-box' or 'risk-stripped' WFOE with minimal Chinese substance is subject to recharacterization of profits under OECD BEPS Action 8-10 and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent EIT Filing

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 49 and Tax Collection Administration Law (TAL) Articles 63: a penalty of 50% up to 500% of the underpaid tax is imposed; if the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and accounts for more than 10% of the tax payable, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 applies. Late-payment interest at 0.05% per day runs from the unpaid due date.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for fraud: directors, the financial officer, and the actual controller may be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax, interest and penalties where they ordered or actively participated in the act. They face travel bans (exit restriction notices under TAL Article 44), asset freezes and seizure, blacklisting as 'dishonest judgment debtors', confiscation, and up to 7 years' imprisonment in egregious evasion, plus a criminal fine. A person who has been criminally punishable for tax evasion may be barred from serving as a director for a period under the Company Law.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance (General Anti-Avoidance and Abuse of Corporate Form)

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Article 123: the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction, disregard the WFOE's separate existence for tax purposes where it lacks business substance, and assess tax on the true economic owner. A special tax adjustment may carry a 5-year look-back period and an interest surcharge of 5 percentage points over the benchmark loan rate under Implementation Regulation Article 122. Fines of up to RMB 50,000 may be imposed for false books/records under TAL Article 24/35, and the administrative license or business registration may be revoked under Company Law if the WFOE acts as a shell in violation of public interest.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the veil when the shareholder uses the WFOE to evade tax or harm a creditor and the corporate form is abused under Company Law Article 20. Directors who signed false resoluti,ans, failed to supervise substance, or allowed asset commingling and severe capital inadequacy are personally liable to make restitution to the tax authority and creditors. A travel ban is routinely issued against the legal representative, and the director may be detained administratively or, if fraud is proven, imprisoned for up to 7 years for tax evasion or up to 3 years for issuing false invoices (Criminal Law Articles 205-206, severely if falsely issuing special VAT invoices).
AML / False Export & Foreign Exchange Fraud (Money Laundering and Under-Invoicing of Export Receipts)

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 66 for fraudulent export tax rebates: a penalty of 1 to 5 times the fraudulently obtained refund and criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 204 for those who obtain tax refunds by false declaration; fines of the same magnitude and imprisonment of up to 10 years for serious amounts. False declaration to customs triggers Customs Law penalties and potential forfeiture/confiscation of the invoice value of goods. SAFE may impose a fine of 5%–30% of the foreign exchange amount unlawfully retained/paid under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations.

Director personal liability: Under Criminal Law Article 191 (money laundering) and Article 201 (tax evasion), the directors who controlled the invoice manipulation, offshore collection and conversion face criminal liability as principal offenders. The corporate veil is pierced for the dominant shareholder to recover the tax and customs duties plus fines and lose personal assets in execution proceedings. In addition to imprisonment up to 10 years (money laundering) and 7 years (tax evasion), the legal representative will be placed on the exit-restriction/blacklist list, denied credit facilities, and may be prohibited from re-entering foreign C-suite roles in Chinese companies. The company's VAT export rebate qualification can be revoked, and the director's personal bank accounts linked to the unlawful money flows may be investigated and frozen under AML rules.
