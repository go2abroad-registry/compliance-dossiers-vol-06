Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise engaged in a permitted manufacturing sector outside the Foreign Investment Negative List. The registered capital and the contribution schedule are set out in the Articles of Association. The sole shareholder must fully pay the subscribed capital within five years from the date of incorporation under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law. No blocked escrow account or bank certificate is required as a precondition to incorporation; the registered capital may be paid into a local bank settlement account opened immediately after issuance of the Business License. Where banking or other regulated licenses trigger a specific capital calibration, the competent regulator's minimum capital rules will supersede.

Funding & unlock path: The WFOE must obtain a fire protection design review and fire safety acceptance certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Division under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. A Civil Defense Fire Permit is required before the plant is occupied and energized. The owner must also secure structural load inspection and certification for the building supporting CNC machines, broaching units and high-pressure hydraulic test benches, including vibration and anchoring calculations. Occupational safety approval from the local Emergency Management Bureau must verify machine guarding, lockout/tagout procedures, noise exposure control, ventilation for coolant mist, PPE requirements, emergency exits and first aid facilities. Production may not commence until all industrial, environmental, civil defense and occupational safety licenses are endorsed in force under the unified regulatory inspection window.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); statutory electronic filing is conducted through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal (China Electronic Tax Administration platform) and the Integrated Tax Service System.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE first registers with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receives a business license carrying its 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which constitutes the legal Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and is also used as the VAT registration number. Within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA office using the e-Tax portal or in-person service, submitting the business license, foreign investor corporate/personal identification documents, notarized constitutional documents, articles of association, registered office lease or property certificate, legal representative identification, and bank account opening notice. After acceptance, the STA finalizes the tax registration record, and the Unified Social Credit Code is used for all enterprise income tax, VAT invoicing, customs-related tax processes, monthly/quarterly filings, and export tax rebate applications. A foreign non-resident shareholder without a Chinese establishment normally does not receive a separate TIN unless it registers as a non-resident taxpayer with the STA for treaty or withholding purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise taxpayer for corporate income tax and as a VAT taxpayer. The only available election is the choice between qualifying as a small-scale VAT taxpayer and registering as a general VAT taxpayer, subject to statutory turnover thresholds and administrative approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic tax registration: every WFOE must register upon incorporation. However, a mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold of RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales applies to manufacturing and goods-trading businesses; below this threshold an entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the reduced rate unless general VAT taxpayer status is voluntarily elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim a 0% VAT rate on exported goods only where the goods physically leave mainland China under a genuine export sale to an overseas buyer, the export is declared in the WFOE's name through China Customs, the sale price reflects arm's-length value, and the corresponding foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to China through banks under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade foreign exchange monitoring system. Such exports are processed through the exemption, credit and refund method: the export sale is zero-rated and eligible purchase-side input VAT is refunded under the export tax rebate system after the submission and verification of statutory documentation.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Customs Export Declaration / Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document (air waybill for airfreight)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Foreign trade contract or order confirmation with the overseas buyer
  • VAT invoice issued for the export transaction and the export tax rebate declaration
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt certificate or SAFE trade foreign exchange monitoring system collection record evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

China mandates Golden Tax System Phase IV compliance and use of the national electronic invoice service platform. All VAT invoices must be issued using STA-approved tax control equipment (tax UKey) or the e-invoice platform, with mandatory real-time transmission and fiscal clearance to STA systems, automated invoice numbering, anti-counterfeit digital stamping, strict batch and interval controls, and automatic verification before input VAT credits are recognized.

The STA performs post-export audits by cross-matching Golden Tax IV data, customs export declarations, logistics manifests, and SAFE foreign exchange collection records. Any unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating is reversed, the VAT refunded is recovered in full with statutory late-payment surcharges, and an administrative fine ranging from 50 percent to five times the improperly refunded tax may be imposed under the Tax Administration and Collection Law of the People's Republic of China. Deliberate fabrication of customs documents or foreign exchange records to obtain an export tax refund is a criminal offense and may additionally result in unlimited clawback of state funds, confiscation of proceeds, and personal criminal liability for responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial convertibility. Current account foreign exchange transactions are generally convertible after satisfying real-trade background verification, documentary review, and standard SAFE reporting requirements. Capital account transactions, including injection of equity capital by foreign shareholders, cross-border loans, and direct investment repatriation, remain subject to strict SAFE registration, approval, quota, and post-event filing controls. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must complete FDI foreign exchange registration before receiving inbound shareholder remittances, and the funds must be paid into a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a qualified Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. Fund conversion from USD, EUR, or Hong Kong dollars into RMB is permitted on the strength of the business operating purpose, but must be executed through the SAFE direct investment information system with appropriate payment application codes. Since SAFE Circular No. 5 of 2012 abolished mandatory foreign exchange surrender, export proceeds can be retained in foreign currency current accounts or freely converted into RMB when used for legitimate operating expenditures. Profit remittances to the non-resident parent require audited statutory financial statements, corporate income tax declaration and proof of tax settlement, board resolution approving dividend distribution, and a SAFE-declared remittance application; after these current account conditions are satisfied, conversion and remittance are generally permitted without an additional quota. Overall convertibility is therefore current-account open but capital-account constrained, with the regulatory emphasis placed on underlying transaction authenticity, anti-money-laundering screening, and cross-border balance reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days of the export customs declaration date, unless SAFE-approved extensions are obtained for advance-payment or open-account trade arrangements. Mandatory conversion ratio: none, because the historical mandatory surrender system was abolished; exporters may lawfully retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in eligible current account foreign exchange settlement accounts or convert them into RMB through banks at their own discretion, subject to the submission of customs declarations, contracts, invoices, and shipping documents supporting the genuineness of the trade.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Very Low and legally non-viable for onshore corporate treasury purposes. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and Airwallex do not hold PRC commercial banking licenses and are not permitted by SAFE or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to open domestic RMB or foreign exchange operating accounts for Chinese enterprises. Such platforms may be used only as settlement service providers for inbound collections from marketplace sales, but they cannot serve as the primary banking rail for WFOE equity injections, capital verification, export proceeds receipt, SWIFT MT103 clearing, or SAFE reporting. Attempting to route statutory capital through an unlicensed EMI would create a fatal compliance gap in the bank KYC file and expose the entity to capital account penalties; the treasury blueprint must therefore rely exclusively on PBoC-supervised Tier-1 licensed commercial banks for onshore clearing and foreign exchange execution.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opacity of the offshore parent and ultimate beneficial ownership chain above the WFOE, particularly when the parent resides in a multi-tier trust or holding structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection must be executed via the global SWIFT payment network from the non-resident parent financial institution to the beneficiary account at a qualified Tier-1 Chinese bank using MT103 messaging. The remittance must reference the official entity name, registered capital amount, and SAFE FDI registration identifier. The receiving Chinese bank validates the transaction through the SAFE Foreign Exchange Direct Investment Information System, applies the correct foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment capital contribution, credits the Foreign Exchange Capital Account, and requires the WFOE to submit the capital verification report prepared by a licensed Chinese certified public accountant once the funds are actually received. Conversion into RMB and payment of local expenses must follow the SAFE 'direct investment foreign exchange settlement and payment' positive-list mechanism, meaning each RMB disbursement is processed against a real business purpose such as payroll, rent, domestic raw materials, utilities, taxes, or equipment purchases; all invoices and contracts must be retained on file for the post-event SAFE and bank compliance review.The primary clearing rails are bank guarantee-backed SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for commercial open-account payment terms, irrevocable confirmed commercial letters of credit (L/C) for high-value or high-risk international trade settlements, and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for established repeat counterparties. For RMB-denominated settlement with counterparties eligible to use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS), the WFOE may instruct its Chinese bank to route through CIPS to eliminate correspondent bank costs; for USD, EUR, HKD, and JPY settlements, Chinese banks will operate through their New York, London, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Taiwan correspondent network, with full MT103 message fields including bank identifier codes, reconciliation references, and underlying contract numbers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very Low and legally non-viable for onshore corporate treasury purposes. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, Payoneer, and Airwallex do not hold PRC commercial banking licenses and are not permitted by SAFE or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to open domestic RMB or foreign exchange operating accounts for Chinese enterprises. Such platforms may be used only as settlement service providers for inbound collections from marketplace sales, but they cannot serve as the primary banking rail for WFOE equity injections, capital verification, export proceeds receipt, SWIFT MT103 clearing, or SAFE reporting. Attempting to route statutory capital through an unlicensed EMI would create a fatal compliance gap in the bank KYC file and expose the entity to capital account penalties; the treasury blueprint must therefore rely exclusively on PBoC-supervised Tier-1 licensed commercial banks for onshore clearing and foreign exchange execution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File and Master File preparation if related-party transactions exceed RMB 20 million (annual aggregate for tangible property; RMB 10 million for financial transactions; RMB 5 million for intangibles or service transactions). Additionally, a Country-by-Country Report threshold applies for a Chinese group ultimate parent with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions; however, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form principle under Articles 6 and 122 of the Tax Collection Law are applied. The Chinese tax authority may recharacterize transactions that lack commercial substance. The 'beneficial ownership' doctrine applies for treaty claims. For manufacturing WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China include actual production, procurement, and R&D functions. If the WFOE is treated as a 'shell' or low-risk entity, the STA may reallocate profits to the foreign parent under the 'profit attribution to permanent establishment' rules or use economic substance analysis to deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Invoicing (关联发票虚开 / VAT and CIT fraud)

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 63 (understatement of tax) imposes a penalty of 50%-500% of underpaid tax plus daily surcharge of 0.05% on late payment. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 205: fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for VAT fraud where amount exceeds RMB 500,000; Article 201 for tax evasion carries imprisonment up to 7 years for amounts exceeding 30% of total tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors, especially the legal representative and CFO, may be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax and fines if corporate assets are insufficient (piercing the corporate veil). Courts may issue travel bans (黑名单 / blacklisting) and asset freezes on directors. Criminal imprisonment terms range from 3 to 10 years for artificially inflating deductible expenses; life imprisonment for egregious VAT fraud. Directors cannot escape liability by resignation if the fraudulent conduct occurred during their tenure.
Thin Capitalization and Cross-Border Interest Stripping (资本弱化 and 间接转让避税)

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 46 and Implementation Regulations Article 119 disallow the excess interest deduction and reclassify it as non-deductible. The tax authority may apply the general anti-avoidance rule (Article 47) and impose additional CIT of 25% on the disallowed amount plus penalty of 50%-500% of the avoided tax under Article 63. Interest paid to non-residents may also be subject to sub-optimal WHT rates if the treaty claim is denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the loan structure may be held jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall plus fines if the company is strike-off or cannot pay. Courts may levy personal assets for unpaid tax under the Tax Collection Law Article 49. Travel bans may be imposed under the Exit Administration Law. In severe cases, criminal liability for 'tax evasion through deceptive means' may extend to the CFO and legal representative, with imprisonment up to 7 years when the amount exceeds RMB 500,000.
Shell Entity and Lack of Economic Substance for Treaty Shopping (导管公司：无实际经营的海啸公司)

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and Notice 698/2009 (as amended by Notice 7/2015) permit the tax authority to adjust income and impose tax on the indirect transfer of shares; the intermediary entity is considered a 'conduit', and dividends paid by the WFOE are subject to a withholding rate of 20% (or 10% as per domestic law) on the gross amount, plus penalties under Article 63 for failure to withhold (withholding agent must pay 100% of unpaid tax). Statutory fine: 50% – 500% of the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or maintained the shell structure may face personal liability for the unpaid WHT and penalties when the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20. Civil restitution of assets transferred to related parties can be clawed back. Chinese courts may issue 'blacklisting' restricting the director's exit from China and access to bank accounts. Criminal exposure under Article 201 (tax evasion) may be pursued if the structure evades a large tax amount (≥ RMB 50,000), leading to imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines. In cross-border cases, the director may also be subject to extradition and mutual legal assistance requests.
