Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up registered capital is imposed on a manufacturing WFOE under the PRC Company Law as revised in 2024; however, the registered capital must be specified in the Articles of Association and fully contributed within the period stated therein, which may not exceed five years unless a special industry rule applies. Foreign investment capital must be remitted through the offshore investor to the Company's designated foreign direct investment bank settlement account opened under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. There is no general blocked escrow account requirement, but contributed funds must remain on deposit and cannot be withdrawn until the Company's books reflect the subscribed capital and all statutory filings, including tax and foreign exchange records, are completed. In-kind contributions of machinery or equipment must be appraised by an authorized valuation institution and, if imported, cleared through customs with proof of title transfer to the Company.

Funding & unlock path: The facility must pass fire protection design review and completion inspection before production, and must obtain the Fire Safety Check Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade. The factory layout must comply with the PRC Code for Fire Protection Design of Buildings, including separation of cryogenic pump test cells from combustible materials, flameproof electrical equipment, exhaust ducting, and emergency egress. A structural load assessment for mezzanines and heavy pump testing foundations must be submitted to the local construction authority together with the building occupancy certificate. Workplace safety approvals under the Law on Work Safety require that occupational hazard prevention facilities are designed, built, and put into operation at the same time as the principal project, and that noise, cold stress, high pressure, and electrical safety controls are documented. E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise. Upon incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the business license carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is legally the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for tax purposes; tax registration is completed automatically through the integrated market registration and tax registration procedure. The WFOE must then access the local competent tax service bureau through E-Tax, supplement its registration record with the bank accounts, accounting basis, legal representative, financial officer, shareholder details and customs exporter registration data, apply for the appropriate VAT taxpayer status (general taxpayer registration is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, while below that threshold small-scale VAT status applies unless an election for general status is approved), apply for invoice quota and full digital electronic invoice eligibility, and complete the export tax rebate (exemption) filing with the competent tax authority. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of goods, including cryogenic liquid pumps manufactured and exported by the WFOE, are zero-rated under VAT when the goods are physically exported from the customs territory of China and the sale is made to an overseas buyer. The WFOE must be registered as a general VAT taxpayer, hold a valid export customs declaration, actually ship the goods, receive export proceeds, and file the export tax refund (exemption) declaration with the competent tax authority. Qualifying cross-border services can also be zero-rated or exempt only if they appear on the statutory offshore services list; cryogenic pump export transactions are goods transactions and therefore rely on customs export and freight documents rather than the offshore services rules. Domestic sales inside China remain subject to 13% VAT.

All VAT invoices are subject to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV VAT invoice management system. Fully digital electronic invoices are issued through the national unified e-invoice service platform in E-Tax; invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority upon issuance, and each invoice carries a unique national invoice number and digital verification code that can be verified through the National VAT Invoice Inspection Platform. Legacy tax-controlled VAT special invoices remain subject to tax-control equipment and anti-fraud anti-counterfeiting controls. ERP and accounting systems must use the approved interfaces and APIs to transmit invoice and declaration data in the prescribed electronic format. The system automatically cross-matches invoice data, customs declaration data, logistics data and foreign-exchange receipt data to prevent fake invoices and improper export refund claims.

The State Taxation Administration, the General Administration of Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit export zero-rating claims, cross-matching customs release data, transport documents, VAT invoice data, export rebate declarations and actual foreign-exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is claimed without a genuine export, with falsified documentation, or without timely receipt and reconciliation of export proceeds, the tax authority will deny or retroactively claw back the refunded VAT, disallow input VAT credits, impose late-payment surcharges, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax involved under the Law on Tax Administration and Collection. Customs false-export penalties may be imposed under the Customs Law; foreign-exchange violations may be sanctioned by SAFE; and fraudulent export tax refund conduct is punishable as a criminal tax crime under Criminal Law Article 204, with fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment in serious cases. Audit records and supporting documentation should be retained in accordance with statutory tax record-keeping requirements and longer where fraud or evasion is suspected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII while enforcing a carefully managed capital account regime. For a cryogenic liquid pump manufacturing WFOE, foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injections are subject to SAFE's positive-list registration, requiring a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate (FDI registration) before a foreign currency capital account can be opened. Once registered, the capital injection can be remitted via bank wire, converted to RMB, or held in foreign currency, but the proceeds must be applied within the approved business scope and cannot be diverted to equity investment or real estate without special clearance. Profit remittance is permitted after annual tax audits and is evidenced by tax payment certificates, audited financial statements, and a board resolution declaring dividends; the remittance itself is a current account transaction and can be processed at a designated bank under a straight-through examination if the documentation is complete. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China; however, the mandatory conversion ratio has been abolished, and enterprises may retain earnings in a foreign exchange settlement account (FESA) subject to real-trade-background verification by the settlement bank. The two-pronged control of SAFE's Trade Monitoring System and the PBOC's cross-border financing macroprudential regulation ensures that all material FX flows are subject to documentary scrutiny, creating an operational environment where compliance timelines, rather than convertibility restrictions, are the primary hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 360 days from the date of the relevant customs declaration and must be received into the exporter's foreign exchange settlement account (FESA) held with a designated FX bank. Since the 2012 SAFE liberalization, there is no mandatory conversion ratio rule; the exporter may hold the full amount in foreign currency, voluntarily sell it to a bank at the prevailing market rate, or use it directly for permitted outbound payments. However, all such proceeds remain subject to the authenticity and compliance review by the settlement bank, and the enterprise must submit the underlying trade documents (i.e., contract, customs declaration, invoice, and shipping documents) while maintaining consistent records in SAFE's Trade Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the delegated foreign exchange control authority under the PBOC mandate

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for onshore WFOE operations. Under the PBOC's Administrative Measures for Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions and SAFE's Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, non-bank payment institutions—even those holding a Chinese payment license—are prohibited from providing corporate foreign exchange clearing, foreign currency deposit-taking, or trade settlement services that constitute the statutory monopoly of designated foreign exchange banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, or intergalactic platforms) may offer client onboarding, multi-currency wallets, and payment collection outside mainland China, but they cannot satisfy the legal requirements for corporate bank account, tax clearing, or the SAFE capital verification threshold required for a WFOE in the cryogenic pump manufacturing sector. Any attempt to route onshore treasury flows through an EMI would breach PBOC Payment Rules, trigger automated bank account freezing, and permanently impair the WFOE's trade finance credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete Foreign Direct Investment registration with SAFE and open a Special Foreign Exchange Account for Capital (also known as the FDI capital settlement account) at a licensed commercial bank. The overseas investor wires the equity contribution using a SWIFT MT103 message with Field 70 (Remittance Information) clearly stating 'Equity subscription capital for CN_WFOE_LTD' and the SAFE-registered investment purpose code (e.g., FDI-Investment in Equity). Upon receipt, the bank must be notified to convert or settle the funds into RMB; the RMB can only be credited to the company's RMB basic account and disbursed after a capital verification report is issued by a Chinese certified public accountant (CPA). The bank reports the entire flow to SAFE's Capital Account Information System. For additional liquidity, the WFOE may raise a cross-border financing facility under the PBOC macroprudential framework, subject to external debt registration and the 1/4 leverage cap on operating income.Outbound payments for imported materials, equipment, and service fees must be executed through the onshore bank using SWIFT MT103 (direct credit) or MT202 (interbank funding) for transfers between the exporter and the WFOE. For the LNG cryogenic pump sector, the recommended clearing rail is a commercial irrevocable letter of credit (L/C) at sight, confirmed by the beneficiary's bank, which provides both payment assurance and document verification. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for lower-value, repeated transactions. The bank validates each payment against the valid trade contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, and bill of lading, and it reports the transaction to SAFE under the appropriate current account examination code (e.g., code 1101 for goods trade or 1210 for services). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for onshore WFOE operations. Under the PBOC's Administrative Measures for Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions and SAFE's Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, non-bank payment institutions—even those holding a Chinese payment license—are prohibited from providing corporate foreign exchange clearing, foreign currency deposit-taking, or trade settlement services that constitute the statutory monopoly of designated foreign exchange banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud, or intergalactic platforms) may offer client onboarding, multi-currency wallets, and payment collection outside mainland China, but they cannot satisfy the legal requirements for corporate bank account, tax clearing, or the SAFE capital verification threshold required for a WFOE in the cryogenic pump manufacturing sector. Any attempt to route onshore treasury flows through an EMI would breach PBOC Payment Rules, trigger automated bank account freezing, and permanently impair the WFOE's trade finance credibility.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual related-party tangible asset transactions; RMB 100 million annual related-party intangible property transactions; RMB 100 million related-party financial asset transactions; RMB 100 million other related-party transactions; Master File required when the group's consolidated annual revenue reaches RMB 4 billion or above.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a regime formally labeled Economic Substance Regulation, but it applies a powerful substance-over-form doctrine, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, and the beneficial ownership test under SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9. A manufacturing WFOE must maintain in China its core income-generating activities, including production management, inventory, procurement, personnel, and strategic decision-making for cryogenic pump manufacturing. A passive royalty collector or intermediary distributor with no employees, premises, decision-making authority, or business risk in China will fail the anti-abuse test, be denied treaty benefits, and risk recharacterization as a Chinese tax resident.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company with no economic substance and sham intermediary structure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the GAAR implementing rules allow the tax authority to disregard the intermediary entity, reallocate profits to the Chinese manufacturing entity, apply interest adjustment, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-declared tax. The non-resident will be denied treaty benefits and may be reclassified as a PRC tax resident for its China-source income.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally exposed to exit-restriction travel bans under tax enforcement laws when tax liabilities exceed RMB 100,000 and remain unpaid. Under PRC Company Law Article 20, shareholders and controlling persons who abuse the corporate form to evade tax liabilities are jointly and severally liable for the tax and civil restitution. If the sham structure constitutes intentional tax evasion, criminal liability attaches under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to seven years plus fines and asset confiscation.
Transfer pricing manipulation and profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and impose interest and penalties. Under Tax Administration Law Article 63, deliberately false books or documents supporting transfer pricing positions may be classified as tax evasion, attracting 50% to 500% penalties, public listing as a major tax violation subject to restricted government services, and criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements or knowingly approve inaccurate transfer pricing documentation are not protected by the corporate veil. Courts may pierce the veil under Company Law Article 20 to impose personal liability for the restored tax and penalties. The director's personal assets can be used to satisfy restitution; a criminal conviction under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in three to seven years' imprisonment, criminal fines, and disqualification from serving as a director of any PRC company.
Export VAT fraud and fraudulent customs declarations

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent export tax refund claims are punishable under Criminal Law Article 204 with fines and imprisonment; Article 205, concerning the issuance or use of false special VAT invoices, carries penalties including life imprisonment in extremely serious cases. The tax authority will recover all refunds, impose a 50% to 500% penalty, and forward the case to the Public Security Bureau for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: All directors, financial executives, and actual controllers who conceived or executed the false documentation face personal liability for the State's lost revenue, civil asset restitution, travel bans and blacklisting as serious tax violators, and criminal prosecution. Under PRC Company Law Article 20, the corporate veil is pierced; individual criminal exposure ranges from three years' imprisonment to life imprisonment for especially large amounts, together with confiscation of property and fines.
