Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to an ordinary wholly foreign-owned limited liability company under current Chinese law. The registered capital amount and contribution schedule must be stated in the Articles of Association and the capital must be genuinely subscribed and paid within the agreed period. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Direct export of manufactured port container gantry cranes and hoisting gear qualifies for the 0% VAT export rate and corresponding export tax rebate when the goods physically leave the customs territory of China, are declared under customs supervision through China International Trade Single Window, and the export proceeds are repatriated and verified with the customs and foreign exchange administrations.

  • Customs Export Declaration generated through China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document
  • Bank receipt or foreign exchange settlement certificate evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Special VAT Invoice or ordinary export invoice for the exported goods
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Packing list and weight list

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated foreign exchange regulatory framework. The current account, which covers trade in goods, services, and routine profit remittances, is largely convertible, subject to genuine transaction verification and documentary compliance under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the SAFE counterparty verification system. By contrast, the capital account remains heavily controlled, requiring case-by-case approval, registration, or filing with SAFE and its local branches. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must register its foreign direct investment (FDI) equity injection under the Foreign Investment Information Report and obtain a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate for FDI, after which the injected capital is credited to a Capital Account (FDI Capability Account). Surrender requirements apply to export receipts: under the 2019 SAFE circular on optimizing trade foreign exchange administration, export proceeds in goods may be retained offshore or repatriated but must be repatriated to China within the statutory time limit if the exporter opts for onshore settlement, and banks supervise the authenticity and consistency of trade background documents. Profit remittance hurdles include the requirement of prior year audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board resolution on dividend distribution, and proof that registered capital has been fully injected if required by the articles of association. A WFOE may distribute profits only from after-tax earnings under Chinese GAAP, and the remittance of dividends is treated as a current account transaction once the capital verification and SAFE filing are complete, although banks still apply a strict know-your-customer review on the ultimate beneficial owner and the underlying business rationale. The overall assessment is that the Chinese yuan is not freely convertible on the capital account; current account convertibility is real but administratively intensive, and any cross-border capital movement requires a clear regulatory paper trail through SAFE-approved banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For general trade exports, repatriation of goods trade foreign exchange receipts is required within 180 days from the date of export declaration unless a prior SAFE extension or offshore retention registration is obtained; after repatriation, the exporter may keep the proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account with a domestic bank or convert into CNY voluntarily, and there is no mandatory conversion ratio for most ordinary manufacturing exporters under the current SAFE administration, except where the bank flags false trade or the exporter is on a special monitoring list requiring full surrender and transaction-level verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions and digital banking platforms are generally not viable as primary treasury conduits for a China mainland WFOE. Chinese foreign exchange regulations require that cross-border receipts and payments be routed through banks that have obtained the relevant foreign exchange settlement and sale license from SAFE, and that the counterparty bank be a licensed financial institution in the jurisdiction of the transaction. Pure-play offshore EMIs that do not maintain a physical branch or licensed presence in China and that are not qualified foreign exchange designated banks cannot execute SAFE reporting, capital account registration verification, or export proceeds surrender reconciliation on behalf of a WFOE. Their utility is limited to auxiliary functions such as pre-onboarding account aggregation, payment tracking dashboards, or treasury visibility, but they must not displace domestic bank settlement rails. Legal constraints under the Administrative Measures for the Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions and PBOC regulations on payment providers further prohibit unlicensed entities from engaging in currency conversion and cross-border settlement services for resident onshore entities. The feasible approach is to use a licensed domestic tier-1 bank as the settlement anchor and, if required, integrate an offshore EMI only as a non-account information aggregator for group-level cash visibility outside China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound capital injection into a WFOE, the routing protocol begins with the sponsor filing the Foreign Investment Information Report with the local Administration for Market Regulation or through the Ministry of Commerce reporting system, followed by opening a Capital Account (FDI Capability Account) at a SAFE-designated domestic bank in the WFOE's name. The overseas parent then wires the equity funds via SWIFT MT103 with the transaction purpose code designated for foreign direct investment, typically codes under the International Transaction Reporting System such as 'FDI equity contribution' or 'foreign exchange registration for direct investment', and the remittance instruction must include the SAFE registration number and the WFOE's name exactly as registered. The receiving bank will verify the capital account registration information against the SAFE system, confirm the transaction can only be credited to the Capital Account and not to a current account, and process the funds as pending for the purpose of a post-arrival capital verification by a licensed CPA firm. The CPA issues a capital verification report confirming the contribution is fully paid after the funds are received and the bank provides a Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation. The proceeds in the Capital Account can be used only for actual business purposes in compliance with the negative list, after which they may be transferred to the operational current account for permissible working capital expenditure if supported by invoices, contracts, and tax invoices from third parties. Any subsequent increase or decrease in registered capital requires an updated Foreign Investment Information Report and SAFE filing.Outbound trade disbursements for the manufacture and sale of port container gantry cranes should be settled primarily through commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, and SWIFT MT103 wire transfers, all subject to the bank's trade authenticity review and SAFE's overall trade monitoring framework. For import of key components, a WFOE can open an irrevocable usance LC at its domestic bank with a SWIFT MT700 issuance, with the documentary package comprising a commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance policy, packing list, and a certificate of quality; the bank will release payment against compliant presentation under UCP 600. For export sales to overseas port operators, the WFOE as beneficiary should receive LCs from the buyer's overseas bank, or if the buyer pays by documentary collections, the WFOE's bank acts as presenting bank under the Incoterms agreed in the contract, typically CIF or CIP for heavy machinery with marine cargo insurance. Where direct wire payment is used for spare parts or pre-shipment advances, the remittance purpose code for export trade should be selected so that the receipts can be matched with the customs export declaration in the SAFE monitoring system, thereby satisfying the 'goods, funds, and declaration consistency' principle. Disbursement of project escrow funds, as required by the entity's target payment rails, should be executed through a domestic bank's project clearing desk, using an escrow agreement and milestone-based inspection certificates to release funds under an onshore trust or escrow account, ensuring each disbursement is backed by valid VAT special invoices and customs clearance records.Onshore RMB forward contracts (FX forwards) with a licensed domestic bank, available only for genuine hedging of documented trade exposures and current-account transactions, requiring underlying contracts and business licences for each transaction.Over-the-counter currency swaps between CNY and major convertible currencies for approved current-account and capital-project cash-flow matching, executed through SAFE-registered domestic banks with real demand and risk exposure verification.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore CNH markets may be considered only for group-level management of RMB volatility outside China, but cannot be settled onshore and do not replace the legally recognized onshore CNY hedging market; onshore hedging must be executed via the China Foreign Exchange Trade System with SAFE approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech electronic money institutions and digital banking platforms are generally not viable as primary treasury conduits for a China mainland WFOE. Chinese foreign exchange regulations require that cross-border receipts and payments be routed through banks that have obtained the relevant foreign exchange settlement and sale license from SAFE, and that the counterparty bank be a licensed financial institution in the jurisdiction of the transaction. Pure-play offshore EMIs that do not maintain a physical branch or licensed presence in China and that are not qualified foreign exchange designated banks cannot execute SAFE reporting, capital account registration verification, or export proceeds surrender reconciliation on behalf of a WFOE. Their utility is limited to auxiliary functions such as pre-onboarding account aggregation, payment tracking dashboards, or treasury visibility, but they must not displace domestic bank settlement rails. Legal constraints under the Administrative Measures for the Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions and PBOC regulations on payment providers further prohibit unlicensed entities from engaging in currency conversion and cross-border settlement services for resident onshore entities. The feasible approach is to use a licensed domestic tier-1 bank as the settlement anchor and, if required, integrate an offshore EMI only as a non-account information aggregator for group-level cash visibility outside China.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: PRC Company Law imposes an unconditional statutory audit on every limited liability company, including a WFOE, at the end of each fiscal year; there is no exemption based on balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount. The entity-based trigger is registration as a company under PRC Company Law, not a quantitative metric. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation must be prepared when the WFOE's intercompany transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial transactions, and RMB 40 million for other intercompany transactions in a single fiscal year. A Master File must be prepared where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or the multinational group meets the Country-by-Country reporting revenue threshold of approximately RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate a standard OECD Economic Substance Regulations registry, but tax authorities apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, substance-over-form, and beneficial-ownership doctrines. For a port crane WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are procurement of steel and components, crane design adaptation, component assembly, quality assurance, shipping, and after-sales project support; those functions and risks must be performed and managed by substantive employees in mainland China. If intangible development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation are artificially split from the WFOE without actual decision-making in the Chinese entity, profits can be reallocated and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Fraud / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41, the tax authority makes a transfer pricing adjustment and recomputes taxable income. Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a daily 0.05% overdue surcharge and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-paid tax; the adjustment is not limited by the rate reduction and can be assessed retroactively where fraud is proven.

Director personal liability: The courts may pierce the WFOE's limited liability shield if directors personally signed false invoices, fake service contracts, or misleading transfer pricing documentation. Directors become jointly and severally liable for the recovered tax and penalties, their personal assets may be frozen, they are subject to cross-border exit bans under the dishonest-judgment-debtor blacklisting system, and they can be prosecuted for tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, leading to imprisonment for up to seven years where the evaded amount is large.
Treaty Abuse / Conduit Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes the beneficial-ownership rule and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 to deny the treaty claim, reclassifies the payment as a taxable dividend, recovers the 10% statutory withholding tax, and adds surcharges and fines of 50% to 500% of the under-withheld amount under the Tax Collection Law. The conduit entity may also be deemed to have a permanent establishment in China.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and of the intermediary who knowingly sign false beneficial-ownership declarations or misrepresent the foreign entity's substance face personal civil restitution for the unpaid withholding tax, attachment of personal assets, travel bans, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax avoidance. If the sham scheme is used to evade taxes, the statutory criminal punishment can include imprisonment and forfeiture of proceeds.
Withholding Agent Failure and Non-Filing of Dividend Remittance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority orders the WFOE to remit the unwithheld tax, with a daily late-interest rate of 0.05%; under Tax Collection Law Article 62, late filing penalties of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 apply, and, where the failure to withhold is deliberate, a penalty of 50% to 300% of the tax that was not withheld. If the non-filing is part of an intentional tax-evasion plan, Article 63 penalties of 50% to 500% of evaded tax and criminal prosecution apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the dividend distribution without a tax withholding certificate can be held personally liable for the missing tax because their act contravenes the mandatory withholding obligation. The tax authority may obtain personal asset preservation orders, impose exit controls until the tax is settled, and pursue criminal charges for tax evasion where the directors intentionally concealed the non-resident dividend to defraud the State, with a maximum imprisonment of seven years.
