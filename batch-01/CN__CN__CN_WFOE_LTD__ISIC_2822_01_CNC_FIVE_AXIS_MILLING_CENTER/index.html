Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: STA Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (E-Tax Bureau) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing the WFOE first completes company name registration and incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued business license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the single statutory legal-person identifier and taxpayer identification number for tax purposes. Tax registration is integrated into the business license and the local competent STA branch automatically creates the taxpayer master file after the SAMR data is pushed to the tax system. The WFOE must then activate its account on the E-Tax Bureau platform, complete real-name authentication of the legal representative, confirm the registered address, industry (manufacture of machine tools), bank account information, and tax filing frequency, and can apply for general VAT taxpayer status on the same portal. For export refund purposes, the WFOE must separately complete the export tax refund registration declaration through the E-Tax Bureau. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment or business office generally does not obtain a full WFOE tax registration; instead, the payer must perform withholding tax registration, or the non-resident can complete a simplified non-resident taxpayer registration with the competent STA office when needed for domestic contract or property formalities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections; a WFOE is taxed as a resident entity under the Enterprise Income Tax Law on its worldwide income, and no pass-through or territorial election is available at the entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (exclusive of VAT) trigger mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the entity is a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily applies for general taxpayer status. A WFOE manufacturing and exporting CNC machine tools will generally register as a general VAT taxpayer to obtain input VAT credits and export VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of physical goods by the WFOE qualifies for VAT zero-rating under the export tax rebate and exemption regime if the goods are actually exported from China customs territory, the export customs declaration is electronically matched with the tax record, the export sales invoice is issued, the sales revenue is booked, the export proceeds are received within the required foreign exchange period, and the CNC five-axis milling center is not on the export tax rebate prohibition or restriction list. As a manufacturing enterprise, the WFOE applies the Exemption, Credit, and Refund method: the output VAT on export sales is exempt, the relevant input VAT is credited against domestic output VAT, and any remaining creditable input VAT is refunded after tax authority review. For qualifying offshore digital services, eligibility would require a separately enumerated cross-border service classification, which is outside the goods export regime applicable to the CNC machine tool scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (electronic release record with export entry serial number)
  • Ocean/Multimodal Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued by the carrier
  • Original export sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales VAT invoice
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, under the current PRC practice, the receiving bank's export proceeds collection record and exact State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) cross-border receipt data evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Export tax rebate declaration file with the invoice, customs, and payment matching schedule
  • End-User Certificate, final destination certificate, and export license when the five-axis CNC machine is classified as a controlled dual-use or strategic export item
  • Freight and insurance documents, plus certificate of origin, where required for customs valuation or the importing country's clearance

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved digital invoice system, including the fully digitalized e-invoice platform or traditional tax-UKey based invoice control equipment; every invoice is allocated a unique invoice serial number and anti-counterfeit QR code from the national tax invoice service platform, and issuance data are transmitted to the STA in near real time. Enterprise resource planning systems may connect through the official API provided by the electronic tax bureau; no third-party fiscal stamping or private clearance may be used.

The tax authorities can retrospectively claw back export VAT refunds that were erroneously or fraudulently obtained, impose late-payment surcharges of 0.05% per day from the date of refund, and assess administrative fines of between one and five times the amount of tax fraudulently refunded under the Tax Collection Administration Law. The WFOE may also lose or be temporarily suspended from exercising export tax refund qualifications, and individuals involved may incur criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law. Customs may penalize false or inaccurate export declarations, and SAFE may impose fines or monitoring measures for failure to collect export proceeds within the statutory repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed current-account convertibility model. For genuine goods and services trade, the RMB is convertible once the underlying transaction has passed bank authentication and SAFE system screening. Capital account items, by contrast, remain heavily controlled and require registration, approval, or quota allocation. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may lawfully receive foreign currency export proceeds, retain them in an approved current-account foreign exchange settlement account, and convert any surplus or operational requirement into RMB at a designated bank. The foreign shareholder's equity capital injection is regulated as Foreign Direct Investment: SAFE registration must be completed before the inward remittance, and the capital contribution must enter a dedicated FDI capital account. Capital funds settled into RMB are subject to the payment-verification rule, meaning each post-settlement disbursement must be matched against genuine business documents and prohibited uses such as equity investment in domestic securities or speculative real estate. Dividend remittances to the overseas shareholder are permitted only after a statutory financial audit, final tax settlement with the local tax authority, and SAFE-compliant profit remittance declaration. The practical consequence for a CNC five-axis milling center WFOE is full trade-related FX convertibility, but no free capital account mobility; all outbound funds must substantiate real trade, tax, or investment purpose, and any anomaly detected by the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System will trigger documentary investigation and potential freezing of the bank relationship.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE trade-in-goods framework, there is no fixed statutory percentage of export proceeds that must be compulsorily sold to banks. The WFOE may retain eligible export receipts in a current-account foreign exchange settlement account up to the bank-approved retention limit, and the surplus above that limit must be converted into RMB. Export proceeds must nevertheless be repatriated to China and credited to a domestic designated FX bank account within the monitored collection period; for regular goods exports, the receiving and reporting cycle is expected to be completed without unjustified delay, generally within 180 days from the shipment or customs declaration date unless a legitimate extension is filed with the bank and SAFE records. The bank reports the receipt under the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System using the applicable Balance of Payments trade-in-goods code. Any overdue, withdrawn, or structurally unusual collection will be subject to a document-based explanation, including the export contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and payment receipt from the overseas buyer.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions and digital cross-border EMIs are not licensed by the PBC or SAFE to operate as depository institutions, maintain local RMB settlement accounts, accept FDI capital, or submit statutory trade FX declarations on behalf of a Chinese WFOE. A China-domiciled WFOE must use a licensed designated FX bank as its legal clearing counterparty for capital injections, export proceeds, import disbursements, tax payments, payroll, and profit remittances. Such EMIs may be useful only as ancillary collection tools in overseas markets where they hold legitimate licenses, but they cannot replace the onshore bank rails and must not be presented as the primary treasury infrastructure for the Chinese entity. Any attempt to route WFOE capital, export collections, or operating funds through an unlicensed EMI will create AML, SAFE, and tax reporting failures and will likely trigger banking termination.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when total related-party transactions in a tax year exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 50 million for services, interest, or other related-party transactions; Master File mandatory when aggregate related-party revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion and one of the specific transaction thresholds is met; Country-by-Country Report notification required for groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: PRC imposes Economic Substance Doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), Special Tax Adjustment provisions, and anti-conduit rules: a WFOE manufacturing CNC five-axis milling centers must show substantive assets, personnel, and business risks in China; benefits from tax treaties are denied to shell or low-substance Hong Kong/Singapore intermediate companies; Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as R&D, design, precision machining, assembly, and technical support should reside in mainland China to support the WFOE's residual profit allocation; the place of effective management, key asset ownership (patents, software, trade intangibles), and contractual risk allocation must align with value creation, otherwise the STA can recharacterize transactions under Article 47 EIT Law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure, Shell Company Substance Abuse, and DTA Fraud

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41-48: tax authority may adjust taxable income and impose interest on the underpaid tax from the transaction year at benchmark rate + 5 percentage points; Article 44 permits deemed taxable income if no documentation; Tax Administration Law Article 64: 50%–500% penalty on underpayment if concealment; Government Procurement and tax credit restriction can be imposed; CRS and AML referrals for offshore conduit structures.

Director personal liability: Civil liability: courts may pierce the corporate veil where the WFOE is a mere instrumentality or fraud is shown (PRC Company Law Article 20); directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties if they signed false declarations or caused deliberate underpayment; tax authorities can blacklist directors, impose exit bans prohibiting travel abroad, freeze personal assets; criminal liability risk under Criminal Law Article 201 (tax evasion) with imprisonment from 3 to 7 years plus heavy fines for cumulative evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of tax due; for DTA fraud, Criminal Law Article 201 and customs/exchange control violations carry additional penalties.
Export Tax Refund Fraud and VAT Reconciliation Failure

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 204 (fraudulent export tax refund): imprisonment up to 10 years for serious amounts, life imprisonment for extremely serious amounts, and monetary penalties; Tax Administration Law Article 63: 50%–500% of evaded tax plus 0.05% daily surcharge; revocation of export tax refund qualification, VAT general taxpayer status downgrade, customs penalties under Customs Law; blacklisting by State Administration of Taxation and Customs (lost trust enterprise), joint punishment led by NDRC.

Director personal liability: The tax authority can pierce the corporate veil and impose personal joint liability on directors who control the fraudulent refund scheme, especially when corporate funds are commingled with personal accounts or sham export structures exist; directors face personal asset seizure, travel bans, exit denial, and prohibition on serving as senior management; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets; foreign directors may be extradited under mutual legal assistance if resident abroad.
Non-Resident Dividend Repatriation Without WHT Compliance and Customs/Technology License Evasion

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent failure: Tax Administration Law Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50%–300% of the unwithheld tax and a daily 0.05% surcharge; the WFOE remains jointly liable to pay the tax; foreign exchange remittance can be suspended; unreported technology import contracts lose deductibility of royalty expense, and both VAT WHT and CIT WHT are assessed; Customs Law penalties for undervaluation of imported spare parts or misdeclared technical license fees, with fines up to 30% of undeclared dutiable value plus additional import VAT.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally fail to withhold, arrange alternative non-reported payment channels, or sign false technology licensing agreements are personally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties; personal bank accounts are exposed to freezing orders; exit bans and blacklisting are imposed during tax enforcement; Chinese criminal law imposes personal criminal liability for intentional withholding failure under Criminal Law Article 201 and for illegal transfer of foreign exchange under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations (imprisonment up to 10 years); directors’ personal assets can be traced and confiscated if corporate veil piercing is established under Company Law Article 20.
