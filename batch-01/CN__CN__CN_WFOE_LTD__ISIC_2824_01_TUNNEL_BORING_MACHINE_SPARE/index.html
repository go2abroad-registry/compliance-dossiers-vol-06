Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox arrangement is generally impermissible for a WFOE engaged in heavy manufacturing (ISIC 2824 / NAICS 333120) without a physical production or service facility. The local SAMR will require a fixed lease or ownership certificate for the registered address, and the tax authority will demand a physical location for inspection and invoice administration. Banks providing project finance, letters of credit, and trade finance will require unannounced site visits, utility bills in the company's name, and a physical server/equipment/subcontractor foot print. Use of a virtual office address increases bank de-risking risk, triggers enhanced EDD, and may cause a rejection of the WFOE license application. It is permitted only in a very narrow service-industry context, not for a heavy equipment spare parts manufacturer. A physical registered office with clear signage, a dedicated mailbox, and accessible production records is mandatory; using a virtual office as a secondary mail-forwarding address is not acceptable.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for WFOE under current law; registered capital must be subscribed as per the Articles of Association (typically within 30 years). Capital must be paid into the company's bank account in China; no escrow account is required, but a capital verification report may be requested by the local SAMR.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, including local tax service bureaus, operating through the national Electronic Tax Bureau portal (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the STA electronic invoice service platform

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete foreign investment incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the WFOE receives a business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the single statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) under the one-license-one-code reform. Step 2: Tax registration is automatically integrated with market registration; the WFOE must activate its taxpayer account in the Electronic Tax Bureau using the USCC, complete the taxpayer supplemental information and financial accounting particulars, and appoint a tax and finance representative. Step 3: If annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, file VAT general taxpayer registration electronically; otherwise small-scale VAT taxpayer status is automatic and may be upgraded voluntarily if accounting and fixed premises satisfy the law. Step 4: Apply for customs registration with the General Administration of Customs to obtain a customs operating registration code for export declaration, and register in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-in-goods foreign exchange directory so that export proceeds and customs declarations can be matched for export zero-rating and tax refund. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no Chinese permanent establishment but with PRC-source income, a separate non-resident enterprise tax registration and withholding registration must be filed with the competent tax authority, which will issue a non-resident TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; China does not permit check-the-box classification and applies anti-hybrid mismatch rules automatically. VAT-specific elections are available: a qualifying taxpayer may elect between small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment and general VAT taxpayer treatment, and a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment may elect the deemed-profit method for Enterprise Income Tax withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All taxpayers must be tax-registered from business commencement; mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered by annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below this threshold, the taxpayer is classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer, with a small-scale monthly tax-exempt sales threshold of RMB 100,000 and quarterly threshold of RMB 300,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export goods are eligible for the 0% VAT rate where the exporter is a registered VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from China, the export is made under a lawful customs export declaration, the export sales are bona fide arm's-length transactions, and the corresponding foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory period. A manufacturing WFOE exporting tunnel boring machine spare parts uses the exemption-credit-refund method, meaning output VAT on the export sale is zero-rated and eligible input VAT on directly attributable domestic purchases is credited and refunded. The zero rate does not apply where the goods are not actually exported or where the exporter cannot substantiate the outbound shipment and receipt of sale proceeds.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (also referenced as the export Bill of Entry) evidencing export clearance, commodity code, quantity, value, and the electronic customs record transmitted to the tax authority
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure from China and arrival at the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or the functionally equivalent bank receipt for export proceeds in foreign exchange, including the bank foreign exchange collection notice and the PRC foreign exchange income declaration
  • Export tax rebate declaration and the accompanying electronic matching records from the STA electronic tax system, customs, and the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Platform; fully digital electronic invoices are the statutory norm and are transmitted in real time to the national Golden Tax IV system. Tax authorities perform digital clearance by automatically cross-matching the unique invoice number, seller and buyer identity, invoice amount, VAT output tax, customs declarations, logistics documents, payment rails and foreign exchange receipts. There is no legacy pre-printing fiscal stamp approval, but anti-fraud digital controls, mandatory invoice data reporting, QR-verification, and centralized Golden Tax IV risk analytics are legally required.

The STA, General Administration of Customs and SAFE jointly scrutinize export declarations, VAT invoices, logistics waybills, and export foreign exchange receipts under Golden Tax IV data-matching. If the zero rating is disallowed because the documentation bundle is incomplete, fraudulent, or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the export is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale and VAT at 13% is assessed on the export value, any refunded input VAT is clawed back, daily late-payment surcharges accrue, and administrative penalties apply. For tax evasion, penalties range from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; for fraudulent export tax refunds, the refund is clawed back with a fine up to 100% of the refunded amount and suspension from export tax refund procedures. Criminal exposure may arise under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties including imprisonment up to life imprisonment, fines, and forfeiture of property for especially large fraudulent export refund amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and partial capital-account convertibility. Current-account transactions, including trade receivables for exported tunnel boring machine spare parts, are generally convertible and may be settled freely through designated foreign-exchange banks, subject to authenticity review under SAFE regulations. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity investment from the offshore parent, cross-border financing, and repatriation of invested capital, remain controlled and require registration, filing, or approval. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may retain export earnings in approved foreign-currency accounts or convert them into RMB, but every transaction must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, tax receipts, and trade-monitoring data. Dividend and profit remittance to the overseas shareholder is permitted only after completion of a statutory annual audit, full tax settlement, proof of paid-in capital, confirmation that all foreign-exchange registration obligations are current, and a board resolution declaring distributable reserves. There is no compulsory surrender percentage for export proceeds under the current regulatory framework; however, all export receipts must be repatriated into China within applicable statutory timelines, and the bank must reconcile each receipt with the customs-trade monitoring system. RMB is convertible for trade and routine current-account purposes, while capital-driven conversion and outbound remittances beyond ordinary profits remain subject to documentation, authenticity review, quota limits, or case-by-case SAFE scrutiny. This creates a strict segregation between operational liquidity, which flows under the current account, and strategic investment capital, which must be managed under the capital account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from tunnel boring machine spare parts must be repatriated into China through a designated foreign-exchange bank within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, or within the extended contractual payment period if a longer settlement term is justified and documented. There is no statutory fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE regime; the WFOE may choose to retain the foreign currency in an approved export-proceeds foreign-currency account or sell it to the designated bank for RMB at the prevailing market rate. The bank is required to perform pre-payment and post-payment verification against customs declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and contract terms, and any overdue collection must be explained through the enterprise trade monitoring system with supporting proof of delay or commercial dispute.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility for the WFOE as a primary treasury solution. Foreign fintech electronic-money institutions and offshore digital banks are not recognized as licensed settlement banks under Chinese banking regulations and cannot independently open domestic RMB or foreign-currency accounts for an onshore entity. The PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border receipts, capital injections, export proceeds, and trade payments to flow through banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions operating under Chinese payment licenses. A foreign EMI may only act as an ancillary beneficiary-collection channel for low-value receivables, and those funds must ultimately be withdrawn to a licensed domestic bank account in the name of the WFOE. The capital account, LC issuance, export verification, and FX hedging for a heavy-machinery manufacturing business must remain exclusively with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted from the parent's own bank account directly to the WFOE's designated FDI capital account at a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The transmitted wire should be an SWIFT MT103 with the payment narrative clearly stating 'Equity injection into Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise for tunnel boring machine spare parts manufacturing'. The receiving bank will verify the SAFE FDI registration certificate, the investment contract, the share-subscription agreement, the capital-injection declaration, and the authenticity of the parent's identity. Funds received under the capital account are subject to strict use controls; they may be settled into RMB only for permitted business expenditures such as purchase of equipment, raw materials, salaries, taxes, utilities, and intangible-asset costs. The WFOE cannot on-lend such funds, invest in securities, or make secondary equity investments without additional SAFE approval. After receipt and settlement, a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital-verification report for subsequent business licensing, tax filing, and dividend-distribution purposes.Outbound trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments under purchase contracts, commercial documentary letters of credit for large-value imports of steel castings, hydraulic motors, bearings, and electronic control systems, and documentary collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) for recurring counterparties with a clean trade-performance record. RMB cross-border trade payments should be routed through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) while USD and other major-currency disbursements should be cleared through SWIFT and correspondent-banking corridors. All outbound payments must be matched with customs declarations, invoices, tax payment records, and the enterprise trade-monitoring system filing. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by a Chinese WFOE to a non-resident corporate shareholder, unless a lower treaty rate is available and the beneficial ownership test is satisfied; typical treaty reduction is 5% for a corporate shareholder holding at least 25% capital, subject to the specific Double Tax Agreement and domestic anti-abuse rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is mandatory when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. A WFOE manufacturing TBM spare parts with intercompany raw material purchases, technology royalties, and export sales to foreign affiliates must test all transactions against these thresholds annually.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation modeled on Caribbean ESR regimes, but the GAAR, substance-over-form doctrine, and beneficial ownership rules under SAT Notice 2018 No. 9 impose equivalent requirements. The WFOE must maintain real manufacturing activity, employees, assets, procurement, risk control, and decision-making in China; a foreign parent or intermediary claiming DTA benefits must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) and substantive business in its claimed residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Value-Added Tax Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Criminal Law Article 205, the issuance or procurement of false special VAT invoices carries imprisonment up to life, fines, and confiscation of property; under Criminal Law Article 201, tax evasion exceeding RMB 100,000 and more than 10% of the tax payable is punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years; the Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax plus daily surcharges and unlimited recapture in fraud cases.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the WFOE is a façade; directors who sign false returns, authorize false invoices, or conceal offshore revenue are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the underpaid tax and damages; tax authorities routinely impose exit bans and blacklisting on legal representatives; criminal conviction results in imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion and up to life for false VAT invoice offenses, with asset freezing and travel restrictions applied to the individuals.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41, 44, and 48, the tax authority may recharacterize the transactions, deem a taxable adjustment, impose interest on incremental tax, and deny deductions for missing documentation; under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 64, failure to file truthful information may attract penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; serious arrangements intentionally disguised as arm's-length transactions are treated as tax evasion and prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed where the transfer pricing scheme is fraudulent: the corporate veil may be pierced, exposing personal assets to civil restitution of the adjusted tax and interest; tax authorities impose personal asset preservation, travel bans, and blacklisting on responsible board members; knowing concealment of profits triggers criminal liability with imprisonment up to 7 years; foreign directors with criminal exposure face extradition or Interpol red notices.
Shell Company / DTA Abuse / Anti-Money Laundering Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Notice 2018 No. 9, the intermediary is denied Beneficial Owner status, and the statutory 10% withholding tax, or 20% for individuals, is reasserted with late surcharges; under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, false treaty claims and concealed ownership constitute fraud subject to penalties of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded; under the Anti-Money Laundering Law, concealment of beneficial ownership and use of shell accounts attracts fines and freezing of assets; Criminal Law Article 191 imposes imprisonment up to 10 years for laundering criminal proceeds through shell structures.

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners who knowingly construct shell arrangements, sign false declarations, or move funds through conduit entities will be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution; courts will pierce the corporate veil, impose asset seizures, travel bans, and blacklisting; criminal liability for tax fraud reaches 7 years, while money laundering through shell structures can reach 10 years; personal bank accounts and offshore assets are subject to preservation and confiscation orders.
