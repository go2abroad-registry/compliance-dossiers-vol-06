Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online registration and filing through the STA national Electronic Tax Bureau portal ('E-Tax'), with cross-system data integration to the General Administration of Customs of the PRC (GACC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor forms the WFOE as a limited liability company with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR); the AMR business license automatically bears the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the sole statutory TIN and VAT registration number for the WFOE. Step 2: The AMR one-stop establishment/data-sharing mechanism completes the tax-registration recordal with STA within the business-license issuance process; no separate TIN application is required for the WFOE. Step 3: The WFOE legal representative activates the E-Tax account using the National Tax Service digital certificate (electronic identity) and completes supplementary taxpayer registration data, including accounting/financial system details, bank account information, expected export modes, and applicable VAT taxpayer status. Step 4: For VAT, the WFOE registers as a general taxpayer by election or mandatory threshold; after approval, the STA enables issuance of VAT special invoices using tax-UKey or digital e-invoice cryptographic identification. Step 5: For a non-resident foreign shareholder without a Chinese establishment, no separate Chinese TIN is required for ordinary dividend/interest/royalty withholding; the WFOE, as withholding agent, reports and pays on behalf of the non-resident under the non-resident withholding regime through the E-Tax portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification. PRC law does not provide check-the-box, partnership pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch elections for a WFOE. The only relevant elections are VAT classification elections (voluntary general taxpayer registration by small-scale taxpayers satisfying accounting and internal-control conditions) and corporate income tax incentive elections (e.g., high-and-new technology enterprise, software enterprise, small low-profit enterprise), none of which alter the WFOE's entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) in annual taxable sales: the statutory compulsory turnover threshold for VAT general taxpayer registration. A WFOE is nevertheless VAT-registered from establishment under its USCC even below this threshold; below RMB 5 million it may remain a small-scale taxpayer or elect general taxpayer status. Exported goods qualifying for zero-rating are excluded from present assessment of the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods manufactured/sold by the WFOE are zero-rated under PRC VAT law when (i) the goods physically leave the PRC customs territory; (ii) the sale is to a non-resident customer and title/risk of loss pass to the buyer; (iii) the goods are covered by a properly filed customs export declaration (export release) in the name of the WFOE; (iv) export sales revenue is collected in foreign currency from the buyer through designated FX banks under SAFE trade monitoring (or qualifies for a statutory no-remittance exception); and (v) the WFOE applies for export tax refund/exemption in the monthly/periodic export-tax-rebate declaration and substantiates the refundable input VAT attributable to the exported production. The WFOE, as a manufacturing exporter, uses the export-refund method to exempt export sales from output VAT and to refund/credit the remaining purchase-input VAT after reconciliation with customs export data.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry; GACC export goods declaration) with export declaration number, commodity code, quantity, FOB/CIF value, and customs release record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing sea/air shipment from a PRC port to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in the name of the WFOE/seller and the foreign buyer
  • Export sales contract or purchase order, with payment terms (SWIFT LC or TT) and Incoterms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt/collection confirmation together with the SWIFT payment message, proving export-proceeds repatriation through a designated foreign-exchange bank
  • Export tax refund declaration package and electronic data submitted through the STA E-Tax/export tax-refund system, including the input VAT special invoices and customs release matching data
  • Certificate of origin where the importing country requires preferential tariff evidence (supporting but not a VAT zero-rating condition)
  • Bank/warehouse loading records or terminal cargo-release evidence if required by customs audit

All VAT invoices are issued through the STA VAT invoice administration system, currently including the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform. Golden Tax IV architecture requires real-time transmission of invoice issuer, invoice number, buyer tax ID, amount, tax rate, and tax amount to the STA; tax-control UKey or the digital e-invoice platform performs cryptographic anti-fraud stamping in lieu of physical fiscal cash registers. B2B exporters must integrate their enterprise resource planning systems with the electronic invoice service for high-volume issuance and must obtain STA authorization for 'red-letter' credit invoices or issue-correction invoices. VAT special invoices require completion of the taxpayer identification data before issuance; foreign invoices/export invoices are separately recorded in the export tax rebate declaration system and are cross-matched with customs export declaration data and SAFE trade foreign exchange data.

The STA automatically cross-matches the export tax-refund declaration with GACC customs-release declarations, SAFE trade-monitoring foreign-exchange receipt data, logistics/transport documents, and VAT invoice data. Export refunds for which export proceeds are not collected within 180 days of export (without an approved extension) are denied or clawed back; the zero-rated treatment is revoked and output VAT at the statutory goods rate becomes payable. Late payment interest accrues at 0.05% per day under the Tax Administration Law. False or fraudulent export-rebate claims are punishable by recovery of the rebate plus a fine from 50% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained refund, suspension of export-tax-rebate qualification, inclusion in the STA tax-credit black-list, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China; under the Tax Administration Law, fraudulent underpayment or unlawful refund assessments may be recovered without a statute of limitations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility regime: foreign exchange for trade in goods and services, normal current transfers, and repayment of trade-related foreign loans can be procured from authorized banks after verification of genuine underlying documents. The capital account, however, remains tightly controlled. Direct investment requires prior FDI registration through the bank; cross-border financing is subject to macro-prudential rules relating to foreign debt quotas; and portfolio flows into onshore RMB assets are largely channelled through regulated schemes such as QFII/RQFII and Stock Connect. For a WFOE, profit remittance is permissible after completion of the annual statutory audit, settlement of PRC corporate income tax, and allocation of legal reserves; the bank must see the board resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment proof, and the foreign exchange registration record. Capital proceeds beyond the registered equity capital cannot be freely repatriated without a local exit approval. There is no mandatory foreign-exchange surrender requirement for export receipts, but all proceeds must be repatriated to China and reported into the SAFE trade monitoring system; voluntary conversion to CNY at the onshore market rate is standard. Practical hurdles include documentary strictness, beneficial-ownership disclosure, and real transaction background checks performed by commercial banks as gatekeepers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current law, there is no mandatory surrender ratio and no fixed conversion deadline for export foreign currency receipts. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and credited to a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or operating account, and the receipt must be reported into the SAFE Trade Monitoring System against the corresponding customs declaration. SAFE monitors outstanding export receivables; if payment is not received within the commercial timeframe, typically by 90 days after customs clearance for open-account or documentary-collection trade, the bank will mark the transaction as overdue and require a written explanation. The exporter may retain the full amount in foreign currency or may voluntarily convert all or part of the receipt into CNY at the onshore spot market rate.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the foreign-exchange administrative authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and structurally not viable as a direct account provider. PRC banking and foreign-exchange rules do not authorize foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) to open or maintain settlement accounts for a domestic WFOE, and no unlicensed EMI may act as the principal in cross-border CNY clearing or foreign-exchange conversion. SAFE requires all underlying trade and capital transactions to be reflected in the bank's reporting systems, with the licensed onshore bank retaining AML/CFT responsibility. A foreign EMI may only operate as a technology front-end or customer-facing channel where the actual KYC, clearing, and SAFE reporting remain inside a licensed Tier-1 bank. The WFOE should therefore rely on a direct commercial banking relationship with a domestically licensed bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions by a Chinese-resident WFOE to a non-resident corporate shareholder; reduced to 5% under many DTAs where the beneficial owner has directly held at least 25% of the WFOE's capital throughout a 12-month period.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File threshold: group-wide consolidated revenue above RMB 5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the OECD ESR as a separate statute, but requires substance over form under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47) and Chief Tax Authority rulings. For a WFOE manufacturing high-speed aseptic liquid packaging automated lines, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include: product design and engineering, procurement of stainless steel and electronic components, assembly and testing, quality management, and export order/after-sales support. The entity must maintain a physical factory, employees, board meetings and decision-making in China; a letterbox operation claiming treaty benefits will be disregarded and recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Article 41 (arm's length) and Article 48 (interest on adjustments); Tax Administration Law Article 63: fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% surcharge; SAT can use indirect sub-sector profitability benchmarks to re-determine taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable under Company Law Article 20 for abusing corporate personality to evade tax; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 on tax evasion may trigger asset seizure, travel blacklisting by the border authority, and imprisonment up to 7 years for large amounts.
Withholding Tax and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 69: payor is liable for the unpaid withheld tax and is subject to a fine of 50%-300% of the amount that should have been withheld; interest on late payment runs from the due date; treaty benefits can be denied retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in under-withholding can be personally assessed for restitution; under Criminal Law Article 201, intentional failure to pay non-resident tax constitutes tax evasion, leading to prison terms up to 7 years, personal asset freezing, and exit control/blacklisting.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 42 (2016) Special Tax Adjustment rules, the SAT may disregard the entity or recharacterize the transaction, disallow all tax treaty benefits, and assess tax at 25% on a deemed taxable income; penalties up to 500% and interest.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 makes the controlling shareholders/directors personally liable for the total tax assessment; criminal liability for fraudulent tax behavior can arise, with directors blacklisted from exiting China and imprisoned for up to 7 years in serious cases.
