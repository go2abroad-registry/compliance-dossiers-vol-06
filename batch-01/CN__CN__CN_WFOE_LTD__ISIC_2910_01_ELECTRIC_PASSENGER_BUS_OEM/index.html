Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company under the Companies Law (2023 Revision); the sole shareholder must subscribe for the entire registered capital and determine the contribution schedule in the Articles of Association. Paid-in capital may be contributed within the agreed term, generally not exceeding five years, and no blocked escrow account is required by SAMR for ordinary manufacturing projects. The registered capital must nevertheless be commensurate with the scale of the electric passenger bus manufacturing investment, and a capital contribution schedule must be filed with SAMR and supported by bank deposit or in-kind valuation evidence if required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety: obtain the Fire Protection Design Review Certificate and Fire Protection Acceptance Inspection Opinion from the local fire rescue authority before use, in accordance with GB 50016-2014, including fire-resistance rated walls, smoke extraction, emergency lighting, hydrant coverage, protected steel structures and evacuation routes. Structural Load: secure a Structural Load and Seismic Safety Inspection Report from the local Housing and Urban-Rural Development Bureau, particularly for battery assembly mezzanines, overhead cranes and painting booths. Occupational: complete an Occupational Hazard Evaluation with the provincial occupational health authority, register noise, welding fume and paint vapor hazards, arrange pre-employment and annual medical examinations, and obtain the Work Safety Production License if hazardous chemical storage exceeds threshold limits.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing, registration, and invoicing are performed through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn and the provincial e-Tax Bureau platforms, supported by the Golden Tax System Phase IV.

E-government filing portal

Under Chinese tax administration, a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) in its business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), and that USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for Corporate Income Tax, VAT, and all local indirect taxes. Step 1: The foreign investor submits formation documents through SAMR's National Enterprise Registration Information System, including notarized and legalized or apostilled investor identity documents, Articles of Association, certified English translations, and registered capital proof; SAMR issues the business license bearing the USCC. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE completes tax registration by logging into the competent provincial e-Tax Bureau portal or by visiting the competent tax office; the STA automatically registers the USCC as the TIN for all tax types. Step 3: The tax authority establishes the taxpayer profile, determines applicable taxes, and issues electronic tax registration credentials; no separate VAT ID application is required because the USCC is the VAT registration number. Step 4: If the WFOE's annual VAT taxable sales will exceed RMB 5,000,000, it must apply to become a VAT General Taxpayer through the e-Tax Bureau; if below that threshold it may remain a Small-Scale Taxpayer or elect General Taxpayer status. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese branch that earns China-source income, the Chinese payer or withholding agent must complete non-resident tax registration on the e-Tax Bureau and the non-resident enterprise may be assigned a TIN after submitting contract documentation, proof of foreign residence, and a Tax Residency Certificate, with withholding reported under the Non-Resident Enterprise Withholding Module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime for WFOEs; a WFOE is mandatorily treated as a Chinese resident enterprise for Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. No hybrid mismatch election is available; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through general anti-avoidance rules. The only relevant elective mechanism is the VAT status election between Small-Scale Taxpayer and VAT General Taxpayer, which is an indirect tax registration status rather than an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration itself is mandatory for a WFOE regardless of turnover; the USCC-based VAT registration is completed within 30 days of issuance of the business license. Mandatory VAT General Taxpayer registration arises when annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; taxpayers with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may remain classified as Small-Scale Taxpayers, but may elect VAT General Taxpayer status by filing through the e-Tax Bureau.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods exports, including pure electric passenger buses, the exporter must be a registered VAT General Taxpayer, must complete a genuine customs export declaration for goods that physically depart China under customs supervision, must hold an enforceable cross-border sale to an overseas buyer, and must receive the export price in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB settlement within the statutory collection period. Such export sales are zero-rated at 0% VAT, and the WFOE may claim an export VAT rebate for input VAT attributable to the exported buses up to the applicable export rebate rate published by the State Taxation Administration and the Ministry of Finance; for electric passenger buses this rebate rate is ordinarily aligned with the 13% headline VAT rate. Qualifying offshore digital services require the recipient to be outside China, the service to be wholly consumed outside China, and the service to fall within the STA's cross-border services zero-rating/exemption catalogue; those services are secondary to this goods-export manufacturing mandate.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Goods Declaration) issued through the General Administration of Customs electronic clearance system
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer
  • Packing List covering the exported electric passenger bus units
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage
  • Export Sales Contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Bank receipt and cross-border payment declaration evidencing receipt of export proceeds, monitored by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)
  • Export Tax Rebate Declaration File generated through the STA Export Tax Rebate Information System
  • VAT special invoices or other VAT-creditable invoices showing input VAT attributable to the exported buses

The STA mandates the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) platform. VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the e-Tax Bureau electronic invoice module or an STA-approved API-integrated fiscal software system. Each fully digitalized electronic invoice is cleared in real time by the tax authority's central information system at issuance and is stamped with a unique digital signature, invoice verification code, and anti-forgery two-dimensional code. The system performs real-time invoice acceptance verification, cross-matching of VAT invoices with customs export data and purchase/sales declarations, and automated quota monitoring to prevent duplicate issuance and fraudulent input VAT offsetting. Paper invoices, where still permitted, must be verified through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform before input VAT credit can be claimed; offline issuance without the real-time electronic clearance is non-compliant and may result in suspension of invoice quotas and penalties.

Export VAT zero-rating and rebate claims are subject to automated alerts, desk review, field audits, and data-matching with customs and SAFE foreign exchange records. If the mandatory documentation bundle is incomplete, proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, goods do not physically leave China, or the sales are found not to be genuine, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating, treat the sale as a domestic taxable supply at the 13% VAT rate, collect the unpaid VAT, impose late payment surcharge interest of 0.05% per day, and may impose late filing penalties. Under Article 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Administration of Tax Collection, obtaining an export tax rebate through fraudulent declaration, forged customs documents, false bills of lading, or circular transactions results in recovery of the entire clawed-back refund plus a fine of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained, and suspension of export rebate eligibility. Criminal liability may arise under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export tax refunds. Tax authorities may retrospectively examine and claw back erroneous or fraudulent refunds across open statutory assessment periods, including prior-year refunds if the underlying documentation fails audit verification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system under which the Renminbi is convertible for current-account transactions such as trade in goods, services, dividends, interest, royalties, and employee compensation, provided that the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by authentic documentation. Capital-account convertibility, however, remains controlled and subject to registration, licensing, quota, and post-verification mechanisms. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must complete Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration through the relevant bank or SAFE before receiving inbound equity capital. Inbound equity injections are credited to a Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Account and, once settled into Renminbi, must be used within the business scope registered by the WFOE and cannot be freely diverted into prohibited sectors such as securities or property investment without additional approval. Profit remittances to the overseas shareholder are permitted as current-account outward remittances, provided the enterprise produces audited financial statements, board-approved profit appropriation resolution, corporate income tax filing and payment proof, foreign exchange registration details, and a bank-based authenticity review. Dividends can be declared only after statutory reserves have been allocated and the enterprise has met its tax obligations. SAFE continues to monitor trade flows, suspicious cross-border payments, and large USD-denominated settlement patterns through the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and anti-money-laundering reporting channels. In practice, convertibility for a WFOE engaged in EV bus manufacturing is workable but requires disciplined regulatory documentation, full tax history, and transparent ultimate beneficial ownership.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished the mandatory forex surrender system, so there is no statutory minimum conversion ratio and a WFOE may retain 100 percent of its eligible export foreign currency receipts in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert them to Renminbi at market rates through a licensed bank. Export proceeds must nevertheless be repatriated into China and deposited in the enterprise's account with a locally licensed bank, and the receipts must be registered and reconciled under the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within the applicable statutory reporting window. Standard compliance practice requires the exporter to complete declaration and verification no later than 30 calendar days after the funds are credited to the designated foreign-currency settlement account, and the regulatory expectation is that export proceeds are received within 180 days from the customs clearance date unless SAFE-approved extension or special trade-finance arrangements apply. Failure to repatriate or properly report export proceeds can trigger administrative penalties, loss of trade facilitation status, and enhanced due diligence in future cross-border settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its provincial/branch offices, with commercial banks as designated foreign exchange designated banks for settlement and sale

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal feasibility is low for treating offshore digital EMI providers as the primary banking or settlement rail for a mainland China WFOE. Only domestically licensed Chinese banks and qualified foreign-funded banks operating in China may hold corporate accounts, receive export proceeds, conduct CNY conversion, and execute RMB payments. Offshore EMI platforms using electronic money licenses in Singapore, the United Kingdom, or European jurisdictions generally have no legal capability in mainland China to act as a receiving bank for FDI capital, to obtain a SAFE capital-account registration, or to clear CNY trade payments through CIPS. Such platforms may be used only as ancillary treasury visibility, expense tracking, or reporting tools, not as the statutory local treasury account. The WFOE must open the regulated accounts with Chinese financial institutions; fintech EMIs can offer API connectivity or currency overlay in other jurisdictions but cannot replace the compliance obligations imposed by PBOC/SAFE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the overseas shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 or equivalent high-value wire to the WFOE's designated Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Account opened at a licensed Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must clearly reference the SAFE FDI registration number issued for this WFOE, the investment purpose code corresponding to cross-border equity contribution in an operating entity, and the name of the bank branch holding the capital account. The receiving bank will conduct an authenticity and compliance review against the FDI verification form, the foreign investment information filing from the Administration for Market Regulation, shareholder resolution, and the capital contribution schedule. Once the funds are credited, they are settled into Renminbi under the bank's capital settlement system; the WFOE must then complete capital verification procedures with a Chinese certified public accountant and file the capital verification report with the corporate registry and relevant regulators. The settled RMB funds may only be used for operating expenses, procurement of raw materials, salaries, rent, and other registered business purposes within the approved scope. For electric bus OEM, sub-accounts may separate equity injection from operating working capital, and banks may require advance reporting if funds will be used for fixed-asset purchases such as machinery, production lines, or EV battery assembly equipment.Outbound trade disbursements for components, battery systems, imported chassis, and supplier materials can be executed through three primary clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 single customer credit transfer is appropriate for high-value, time-critical supplier payments and must be supported by trade contracts, pro-forma invoices, customs declaration forms, and written payment instructions; banks verify under the current-account regime and require genuine trade background before conversion from RMB. Second, commercial letters of credit are recommended for procurement of expensive EV subsystems where shipment dates are long and contractual certainty is required; the Chinese bank will issue an irrevocable letter of credit through SWIFT MT700, and settlement is achieved through presentation of shipping documents, bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, certificate of origin, and inspection certificates under ICC UCP 600 rules. Third, documentary collections using D/P or D/A instructions are acceptable for lower-risk repetitive purchases and standard parts; documents such as invoices, transport documents, and quality certifications are transmitted through the correspondent banking network, and payment is made upon acceptance or presentation according to the collection instruction. All outward trade disbursements remain subject to bank-side authenticity verification, transaction purpose coding, anti-money-laundering screening, and the record-keeping standards established by SAFE and the PBOC.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts with a licensed Chinese bank, supported by genuine trade-in-goods or FDI-related exposure and board-approved treasury policyPlain-vanilla CNY/USD options bought from a qualified bank for hedging conversion risk on export receipts and imported-component payments, subject to SAFE real-need complianceCross-currency swaps limited to qualified cross-border funding or project financing transactions with approved SAFE documentation and verified underlying investment or trade flowsCNY forward settlement transactions using the onshore fix published by the China Foreign Exchange Trade System, prohibited from being used for speculative or uncovered positionsOffshore nondeliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as an onshore treasury instrument for a mainland WFOE; any use of CNH NDF is outside the legal onshore hedging boundary and should be avoided

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility is low for treating offshore digital EMI providers as the primary banking or settlement rail for a mainland China WFOE. Only domestically licensed Chinese banks and qualified foreign-funded banks operating in China may hold corporate accounts, receive export proceeds, conduct CNY conversion, and execute RMB payments. Offshore EMI platforms using electronic money licenses in Singapore, the United Kingdom, or European jurisdictions generally have no legal capability in mainland China to act as a receiving bank for FDI capital, to obtain a SAFE capital-account registration, or to clear CNY trade payments through CIPS. Such platforms may be used only as ancillary treasury visibility, expense tracking, or reporting tools, not as the statutory local treasury account. The WFOE must open the regulated accounts with Chinese financial institutions; fintech EMIs can offer API connectivity or currency overlay in other jurisdictions but cannot replace the compliance obligations imposed by PBOC/SAFE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual related-party tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; and related-party service transactions above RMB 40 million trigger mandatory Local File contemporaneous documentation. Master File is required for high-risk taxpayers or covered groups, and Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered where consolidated group revenue is RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China does not use a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the PRC EITL General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, and OECD BEPS standards require a substantive analysis. The WFOE must have actual employees, business premises, independent decision-making, real risks, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China related to the design, manufacturing, assembly, testing, and after-sales support of electric buses. A letterbox company, shell invoicing, or contract manufacturing without real functions and risks will be recharacterized and may lose treaty benefits or face transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False or Understated Annual CIT Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, the tax authority will recover the underpaid tax and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax amount plus daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05%. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law can result in imprisonment of up to 3 years for relatively large evasion, or 3 to 7 years where the amount is huge.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly approves or participates in the false filing is personally liable as a criminal co-principal and may face asset confiscation, travel bans and exit blacklisting under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, a personal ban on serving as a company director, and civil joint and several liability for company losses under PRC Civil Code and Company Law if the corporate veil is pierced.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance for DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Under EITL Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules, the tax authority may recharacterize the structure, deny treaty benefits, adjust taxable income or withholding tax, impose daily late-payment interest and penalties under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and apply punitive fines of 50% to 500% of the adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and directors risk personal travel restrictions, passport cancellation, and blacklisting where unpaid tax is assessed. Directors who knowingly use a sham WFOE to avoid Chinese tax face criminal enforcement as accomplices, civil restitution obligations, disqualification from board positions, and unlimited personal liability if the corporate veil is pierced.
False VAT Invoices and Fraudulent Export Declarations for Electric Bus Components

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under Article 205 of the Criminal Law: for false special VAT invoice issuance, individuals can face detention, fines, confiscation of property and imprisonment ranging from up to 3 years for relatively small amounts to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts; the tax authority may revoke tax registration, recover the VAT fraudulently refunded, and impose fines of 50% to 500% of the tax involved.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or benefit from false fapiao transactions are criminally liable with no corporate limited-liability shield. They face personal criminal imprisonment, assets freezing, civil damages to the Chinese tax administration, permanent or long-term blacklisting, revocation of passports, and prohibition traveling abroad; payment demands may be enforced against their personal estates where the court determines the company was a mere instrumentality of the fraud.
