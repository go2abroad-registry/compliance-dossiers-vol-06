Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); primary electronic administration portal: the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax@China) via the national unified online tax service platform at www.chinatax.gov.cn. Supplementary statutory administrations for the export regime: General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC) through the China International Trade Single Window, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for export-proceeds repatriation monitoring.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued Business License carries the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally functions as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax matters. Step 2: the SAMR automatically pushes the formation record to the State Taxation Administration (STA); the WFOE's tax registration is thereby established and an in-charge tax authority is assigned by registered address. Step 3: the legal representative and the financial officer authenticate corporate digital identity on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, enabling corporate income tax, VAT and withholding filings. Step 4: elect VAT identity; annual taxable sales at or above RMB 5,000,000 obligate general VAT taxpayer status, which is mandatory for input VAT crediting and for the export VAT rebate; below the threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer but should voluntarily elect general status before exporting. Step 5: for export activity, register with GACC as a registered customs enterprise through the China International Trade Single Window to obtain the 10-digit enterprise customs registration code; this code must appear on every Export Customs Declaration Form and is a statutory precondition for zero-rated export benefit. Step 6: complete the foreign-exchange record filing with SAFE through the WFOE's designated bank and open a foreign exchange settlement account within the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; each inward export remittance is recorded by the bank as an Export-Proceeds Receipt Voucher. Step 7: enroll in the STA Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform and obtain the special VAT invoice issuance quota; paper-based fiscal printers are not accepted for new general taxpayers. Step 8: if foreign non-resident shareholders or cross-border service providers earn PRC-source income, the WFOE completes withholding-agent registration to obtain the statutory withholding certificate; no separate non-resident TIN is required for the foreign party to be compliant via withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not administer a check-the-box election or any entity-classification election analogous to hybrid mismatch regimes; every Chinese WFOE is mandatorily classified as a resident enterprise taxed at entity level at the 25% standard corporate income tax rate. Statutory elections that remain available are limited to: (a) voluntary general-VAT-taxpayer registration for taxpayers below the RMB 5,000,000 annual sales threshold, (b) the corporate income tax foreign tax credit comprehensive method election, and (c) elective accelerated depreciation or incentive treatments confined to designated zones only; hybrid mismatch outcomes are corrected by the mandatory Special Tax Adjustment rules of the Enterprise Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic-turnover trigger for initial VAT registration: every incorporated WFOE is a VAT taxpayer from the date the Business License is issued. The statutory turnover threshold that compels standard (general) VAT taxpayer status is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales; a manufacturing WFOE with export ambitions should register as a general taxpayer voluntarily even below the threshold to access input VAT credits and the export VAT rebate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A manufacturing WFOE exporting high-power DC ultra-fast EV charger hardware qualifies for the PRC export VAT zero-rated treatment under the export exemption-credit-refund mechanism where all of the following statutory criteria are met: (1) the goods physically depart the PRC customs territory through a registered customs port to the overseas buyer; (2) the export movement is declared by the WFOE to GACC through the China International Trade Single Window under a valid Export Customs Declaration Form; (3) the underlying export transaction is genuine, arm's length and supported by an unbroken special VAT invoice purchase chain for the inputs; (4) the export turnover is filed in the Electronic Tax Bureau within the statutory VAT return period; and (5) the export proceeds are collected and repatriated into the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange account within the monitored collection period. When satisfied, output VAT on the export is levied at 0%, input VAT attributable to the export is first credited against domestic output VAT and the remaining credit is refunded by the STA; the export-rebate declaration must be lodged no later than April 30 of the year following the export calendar year.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls while offering current account convertibility for trade-related transactions. The Renminbi (CNY) is freely convertible for current account items such as trade in goods and services, but capital account items including foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and portfolio investments require case-by-case approval from SAFE. A WFOE established for manufacturing DC fast EV chargers will be subject to the Foreign Investment Law and the Catalogue of Industries for Encouraging Foreign Investment; the business scope being manufacturing places it in the encouraged category, which simplifies initial capital injection via the Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement System. However, profit remittances to foreign shareholders require a three-step verification: audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the State Taxation Administration, and a board resolution declaring dividends. Additionally, the PBOC/SAFE requires all material cross-border transactions to be reported in the SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment verification system. Since 2015, the Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Policy has allowed WFOEs to convert registered capital into CNY and use it for routine operating expenses, but not for equity investments or securities speculation. For outbound capital repatriation, a WFOE must provide evidence of the underlying transaction, and the funds must flow within the scope of the foreign exchange registration certificate issued by SAFE. Persisting hurdles include separate registration for each commercial bank account, mandatory reporting of every balance change above USD 50,000, and the risk-based scrutiny of outbound remittances for high-value technology hardware exports. While the current account is de jure convertible, de facto operational obstacles include the 'know your customer' inspection of the end-beneficiary in cross-border trade and the requirement to present customs declarations for every physical shipment. In summary, China's FX regime is controllable and transparent, but non-compliance with the administrative review process can freeze capital movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE regulations, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign exchange account or sold to a domestic bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to CNY for export receipts; enterprises may hold up to the approved quota in foreign currency. However, they must lodge a 'receipt and payment confirmation' with the bank within 5 business days of settlement, and the entire export proceeds must be brought onshore within 180 days from the date the goods are declared at customs, unless extended by SAFE for exceptional supply-chain reasons. For a WFOE manufacturing DC fast chargers with cross-border e-commerce sales, the export proceeds must be matched with electronic customs declarations in the China International Trade Single Window, and any deviation exceeding USD 100,000 per transaction triggers an automatic audit alert.
