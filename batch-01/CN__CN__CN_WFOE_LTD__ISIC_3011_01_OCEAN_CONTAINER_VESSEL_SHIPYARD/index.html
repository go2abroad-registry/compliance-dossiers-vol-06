A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address is generally not permissible as the sole registered address for a WFOE engaged in commercial shipbuilding under ISIC 3011 because the Enterprise Registration Regulations and the Company Law require a fixed, verifiable business premises that matches the registered address, and the local taxation authority and the market regulatory authority may reject addresses classified as virtual offices, especially for manufacturing entities with statutory licensing requirements. The China (Shanghai) Pilot Free Trade Zone permits address clustering for certain services and headquarters-type enterprises, but industrial manufacturing and shipbuilding are not within the ordinary scope of virtual-office relief. Banking AML/KYC under PBOC Order No. 3 will also refuse a virtual mailbox for a shipyard because the compliance department is expected to verify physical presence through site visits, interviews, and equipment inspection. For the WFOE, the applicant must enter into a lease agreement (at least one year) or a property-rights certificate for a dry-dock, production facility, or industrial estate unit that can accommodate vessel construction, block assembly, and weld-out fitting; the lease must be registered with the local housing and urban-rural development authority if required. A satellite business-office address may be permitted as an additional seat for administrative staff, but cannot substitute for the production operating site.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE limited liability company in China; the registered capital is contractually subscribed in the Articles of Association by the sole foreign shareholder and must be fully paid up within five years from the date of establishment. There is no blocked escrow account mechanism for standard shipbuilding formation, but all foreign direct investment capital contributions must be remitted through a bank-designated capital account and registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain fire protection design review approval and fire protection completion acceptance certificate from the local fire and rescue authority for shipyard buildings, painting booths, paint stores and emergency exits; register dangerous chemical storage sites and obtain the hazardous chemicals safe storage approval for coating and thinning agents; obtain the local civil air defense or premises defense clearance or a documented exemption before occupancy of administrative, workshop and warehouse buildings; complete occupational disease hazard project registration, workplace occupational hazard pre-evaluation and control effect evaluation, work-related injury insurance enrollment, special equipment use registration, and issue confined-space, welding, lifting and electrical work permits for shipyard personnel.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic tax registration and filing are administered through the STA e-tax service platform (Electronic Tax Bureau) and the competent local STA office where the WFOE is registered.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) formed in mainland China first obtains a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The assigned Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier. Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must activate tax registration in the STA e-tax portal and complete VAT registration at the competent local STA, filing the business license, articles of association, registered office proof, legal representative identity documents, bank information and corporate seal. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a branch or permanent establishment in China applies to the STA for non-resident taxpayer registration/TIN through the e-tax platform using its certificate of incorporation, board resolution appointing a tax agent, POA, tax residency certificate and the relevant China-source income contract; if it creates a permanent establishment, the PE's place of business must be separately registered with the local STA.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All established entities must register for VAT with the STA; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, with voluntary general registration available below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of physical goods, including ocean-going container vessels built by the WFOE shipyard, is eligible for VAT zero-rating with input tax refund under the domestic export tax rebate (exemption, offset and refund) regime if the goods are physically exported out of PRC customs territory, covered by a valid customs export declaration and bill of lading, sold to an overseas purchaser, paid in foreign exchange remitted through a designated domestic bank, and accompanied by compliant VAT special invoices and export refund applications.

  • Export Customs Declaration Form with customs release data electronically transmitted to the STA
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill showing shipment to an overseas destination outside the PRC
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export contract or letter-of-credit payment terms
  • Export VAT refund (exemption, offset and refund) declaration submitted through the STA e-tax portal and matched with customs data
  • Bank foreign exchange collection receipt and Cross-Border Receipts Declaration Form evidencing actual foreign exchange repatriation

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA tax-control system and the fully digitalized e-invoice service platform; invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time, matched through the China International Trade Single Window with customs export data, and protected by an anti-counterfeit QR code, digital signature and automatic risk verification before export VAT refund clearance.

The STA will reconstruct the export transaction by matching customs declarations, VAT special invoices, bills of lading and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If any document is false, incomplete, or foreign exchange is not repatriated and evidenced within the 180-day monitoring window, the zero-rated treatment is reversed, VAT becomes payable from the original export date with late-payment interest, and the taxpayer is subject to administrative penalties of 50 percent to five times the amount of tax due under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection; deliberate export tax fraud also carries criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including imprisonment and confiscation of assets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's FX control regime operates on two tracks: the current account is practically convertible for genuine trade and service payments, while the capital account remains administratively controlled and is opened only through case-by-case SAFE registration, licensing, or negative-list approvals. A CN WFOE engaged in ISIC 3011 shipbuilding may receive equity injection and shareholder loans under the Foreign Investment Negative List after completing FDI currency registration. Capital account RMB conversion is permitted only when the funds enter the designated capital foreign exchange account and the bank verifies the declared use; conversion proceeds are then credited to a supervised capital settlement account with ongoing expenditure supervision. Profit remittance is permitted through the basic account if audited accounts demonstrate distributable profits derived from business, but the remittance must be accompanied by tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and bank/SAFE verification. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory: WFOEs retain 100% of export proceeds in domestic foreign exchange settlement accounts and may voluntarily settle with banks. However, the bank duty to police authenticity remains strong. Thus convertibility is not absolute; any unusual equity injections, capital account refinancing, or advance royalty payments will trigger enhanced review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%, meaning there is no forced conversion of export foreign currency receipts into RMB. All export proceeds must nevertheless be repatriated to a domestic PRC bank settlement account and entered into the SAFE goods trade monitoring system. The statutory expectation is collection under the underlying contract credit terms; if export proceeds are unreceived or unrepatriated by 90 calendar days after the customs export declaration, the bank and SAFE monitoring system will flag delayed collection and may downgrade an A-class enterprise status. For B-class and C-class enterprises, all trade receipts require pre-verification of documents and per-transaction reconciliation; no mandatory percentage surrender applies, but banks must confirm customs declarations, transport documents, invoices, and contract terms before releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated foreign exchange administration authority vested in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - not viable for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital EMI providers such as international quasi-banks are legally barred from servicing a mainland PRC WFOE as a settlement institution; they are not PBOC-licensed banks and are not PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institutions under the Payment Services Regulations. A PRC WFOE must maintain its corporate accounts at a PBOC-licensed commercial bank, and all cross-border payments, export receipts, and FX conversions must be routed through the licensed banking system or a SAFE-approved cross-border RMB settlement bank. Offshore EMIs can at most serve as auxiliary petty cash tools outside China and must never be used for inbound capital injection, export proceeds collection, or domestic RMB disbursements. Any attempt to use an offshore EMI for mainland settlement is legally unsound and may trigger bank account termination, SAFE penalties, or AML adverse classification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions must be routed through the PRC's FDI regime: first, complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for the WFOE through the designated bank; second, open a Capital Account - Foreign Exchange Settlement Account; third, ensure the overseas sponsor sends a SWIFT wire referencing investment or equity contribution and the official FDI registration number; fourth, after credit confirmation, the licensed bank converts the foreign currency into RMB and transfers the funds to a supervised Capital Settlement Account; fifth, a licensed Chinese CPA issues the capital verification report and the bank uploads the transaction to the SAFE information system; and sixth, subsequent RMB disbursements must be used solely for approved business purposes such as shipyard construction, equipment procurement, payroll, and operational expenses. Any material change in the use of funds or additional foreign debt must be filed separately with SAFE.For procurement of steel, ship machinery, and imported shipbuilding equipment, use SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers carrying the correct trade purpose code and underlying contract evidence. For cross-border receivables in the sale of ocean container vessels, the preferred clearing rail is an irrevocable installment letter of credit issued in favor of the WFOE, with each milestone presentation supported by stage certificates, invoices, and shipping documents. For ancillary marine equipment and services, documentary collections under D/P or D/A terms are acceptable. For advance payments and warranty obligations, standby letters of credit and counter-guarantees under URDG should be used. Each outbound disbursement must be documented with purchase orders, delivery confirmations, customs declarations, transport documents, and invoice-level matching to prevent trade-based money laundering.Onshore CNY/USD deliverable FX forward contracts executed with a licensed PRC bank, supported by genuine current account or capital account exposure and non-speculative underlying documentationCNY/USD foreign exchange swaps and cross-currency swaps used only to match long-term shipbuilding financing flows or capital account liabilitiesOTC FX options and option combinations approved by bank treasury on the basis of real exposure, with clear risk management objectives and loss documentationOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for an onshore PRC WFOE; all hedging must be booked onshore and any hedge must not exceed the verified underlying exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - not viable for primary treasury operations. Offshore digital EMI providers such as international quasi-banks are legally barred from servicing a mainland PRC WFOE as a settlement institution; they are not PBOC-licensed banks and are not PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institutions under the Payment Services Regulations. A PRC WFOE must maintain its corporate accounts at a PBOC-licensed commercial bank, and all cross-border payments, export receipts, and FX conversions must be routed through the licensed banking system or a SAFE-approved cross-border RMB settlement bank. Offshore EMIs can at most serve as auxiliary petty cash tools outside China and must never be used for inbound capital injection, export proceeds collection, or domestic RMB disbursements. Any attempt to use an offshore EMI for mainland settlement is legally unsound and may trigger bank account termination, SAFE penalties, or AML adverse classification.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% statutory Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate under Art. 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law) of the PRC

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% definitive statutory dividend withholding tax payable on outbound dividends distributed by the WFOE to its non-resident ultimate parent or shareholder under EIT Law Arts. 3 & 37 and Art. 4 of the Implementing Regulations, unless reduced to 5% or 0% under an applicable bilateral Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) such as the PRC-Singapore DTA (5%) or the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA) where the Hong Kong shareholder holds at least 25% of the registered capital and satisfies the Beneficial Ownership conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory preparation of the Local File when the total value of related-party tangible property transfers exceeds RMB 200 million, or when intangible assets, financial assets, or lending/borrowing of funds to related parties exceed RMB 100 million, or when other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million during a single taxable year (STA Public Notice 2016 No. 42). The Master File must be prepared when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 4 billion, or when the ultimate parent prepares a Master File with group-wide cross-border transaction exposure. A Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is obligatory when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and triggers Chinese filing under the CbC reporting notification regulations.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation as seen in offshore jurisdictions but enforces the substance-over-form doctrine pursuant to the General Anti-Avoidance Rule of Art. 47, EIT Law; the Implementation Rules (Arts. 120-123) and judicial precedents permit the tax authority to recharacterize a transaction or disregard a WFOE lacking commercial substance. The 'Beneficial Ownership' requirement of STA Announcement 2018 No. 9 confirms for treaty purposes that a recipient of dividends, interest, or royalties must perform actual management, bear the economic risk, and conduct a material portion of its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in its residence state. For the CN_WFOE_LTD shipyard, genuine CIGA must take place in China: dry dock operations, marine fabrication, shipyard asset custody, engineering/technical BU processing, payroll, and board decision-making. Undertaking these activities in mainland China qualifies as economic substance; if the entity delegates all CIGA to a foreign shipyard subcontractor without Chinese operational substance, the claim to domestic tax incentives, export rebates, and even DTA benefits will be collapsed, and the full 25% tax plus penalties will apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Invoicing & Cost Inflation

Statutory sanction: Under Art. 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, taxpayers who falsely declare, under-declare, or fabricate invoices are liable to a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus a daily late surcharge of 0.05%; where the use of forged VAT invoices leads to fraudulent export rebate claims, Art. 205 of the Criminal Law imposes a term of imprisonment of up to ten years or life imprisonment for especially large amounts. The shipbuilding enterprise may also be deprived of the VAT export rebate (KaoDui) status and forced to pay back the rebate amount plus an equivalent fine.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under Company Law Art. 20 if the director's conduct abuses corporate independence; the directors, the chief financial officer, and the legal representative become jointly and severally liable for the entire underpaid tax, surcharges, and criminal fines. Personal wireless effects include seizure of personal bank accounts and equity stakes, a six-month to three-year vehicle or property sale ban, a travel ban issued by the STA (blacklisting), restrictions to leave China, and criminal conviction under the anti-money laundering and tax evasion statutes with possible imprisonment of up to 7 years for the legal of the vessel and up to 15 years in aggravated fraud cases involving maritime export VAT fraud.
Transfer Pricing / Thin-Capitalization Violation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority issues a transfer pricing adjustment under EIT Law Arts. 41-47, adding the adjusted profit back to the taxable income and imposing a supplementary tax of 25%; the underpaid tax is charged at a 10% interest rate compounded monthly from the due date to the date of adjustment. Additionally, a 50% to 500% accuracy-related penalty applies for the negligence or concealment of related-party transaction data, and the taxpayer will lose any reduced treaty benefits on the recharacterized dividend/royalty income, causing 10% additional WHT.

Director personal liability: Where the transfer pricing adjustment results from intentional submission of forged documents or fraudulent concealment, the director and CFO are personally liable for the resulting deficiency where they signed a non-compliant declaration, and the imposition of a travel/blacklisting order prevents them from leaving the country. Under the Company Law Art. 5 and Art. 20, the court may order personal restitution of the unpaid corporate tax from their personal assets, and in severe evasion cases where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 1 million, criminal liability attaches under Criminal Law Art. 201, with imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Shell Company Abuse / AML & Maritime Sanctions Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may classify the entity as a 'lack of substance trust' and deny all treaty relief and export VAT rebates; customs may seize the export cargo and impose a fine of up to RMB 10 million and revoke the customs outbound registration; the People's Bank of China imposes AML corrective orders and asset freezing on the WFOE and its liquidity bank accounts; the Ministry of Transport may revoke the shipyard license for manufacturing harbor or ocean-going vessels.

Director personal liability: Under Criminal Law Arts. 191 (money laundering), 120 and 138, and the Anti-Money Laundering Law, directors involved in transactions with sanctioned entities are subject to personal criminal fines and seizure of personal assets; travel bans (blacklisting) are issued by the immigration authority and the STA, and the director is personally barred from holding directorships in any Chinese enterprise for a period of 5 years. The court will pierce the corporate veil for the purpose of confiscating personal in kind assets fabricated through the shell entity, and imprisonment of 5 to 15 years applies to executive boards that funnel payments through non-resident shell accounts for the purchase or sale of maritime steel hulls linked to trade-based money laundering.
