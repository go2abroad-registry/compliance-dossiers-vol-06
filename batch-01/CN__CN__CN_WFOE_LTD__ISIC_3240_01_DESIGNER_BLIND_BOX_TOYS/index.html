Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the PRC Company Law, no statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a WFOE limited liability company. Registered capital shall be fully subscribed in the Articles of Association and the shareholder may determine the contribution schedule within the statutory period. Current rules require the subscribed capital to be paid in within five years from establishment unless a special industry rule or schedule prescribed in the Articles allows a longer or shorter period. No blocked escrow account or bank-issued capital proof is required for incorporation, but after formation the bank account must be opened and the paid-in capital contribution may be remitted and registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange or a designated bank for inbound remittance.

Funding & unlock path: The factory and registered office must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate of Fire Protection Inspection from the local Fire and Rescue Department after completing fire-protection design review and final acceptance inspection, including compliance with building fire-resistance and occupancy-load rules under GB 50016-2014. A workplace-safety approval from the local Emergency Management Bureau is required under the Work Safety Law, covering mechanical equipment guards, ventilation systems for solvent vapors, personal protective equipment, emergency rescue plan, employee safety training and occupational health monitoring; the structure must also pass verified static load inspection for molding machinery and warehouse racking before production begins.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal is the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Taxation), also referred to as the Electronic Tax Bureau, operated by the STA through national and provincial systems.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is a PRC resident enterprise. The statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at incorporation. Under the integrated multi-certificate registration system, the USCC simultaneously serves as the VAT taxpayer identification and tax registration identity. Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must access the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and complete taxpayer information confirmation, including articles of association, legal representative identity, registered address, operational address, capital contribution schedule, bank account details, authorized financial and tax officers, accounting method, business scope, and ISIC/NAICS activity mapping. The STA then activates the taxpayer master file for VAT, Corporate Income Tax, and local surcharges. No separate VAT ID is issued. A WFOE with estimated annual taxable turnover above CNY 5,000,000 must complete General VAT taxpayer registration; a lower-turnover WFOE with sound accounting may voluntarily elect General VAT taxpayer status. Foreign non-resident shareholders do not require a PRC TIN solely as investors; dividend withholding is handled by the WFOE as statutory withholding agent. A foreign non-resident entity deriving PRC-source income without establishing a WFOE or permanent establishment must obtain a non-resident taxpayer reference or Temporary TIN through the withholding registration process at the local STA office in the income-source jurisdiction before tax is withheld and remitted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide U.S.-style check-the-box elections, partnership pass-through elections, or hybrid mismatch elections for a WFOE. In the indirect tax sphere, the only available election is that a qualifying small-scale VAT taxpayer with annual taxable sales not exceeding CNY 5,000,000 may voluntarily elect General VAT taxpayer status; a General VAT taxpayer cannot reverte to small-scale status without statutory grounds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic VAT registration is mandatory for all enterprises conducting taxable sales regardless of turnover. General VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000; taxpayers below this threshold are classified as small-scale VAT taxpayers and may voluntarily elect General VAT taxpayer status if accounting is adequate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical designer blind box toys exported by the WFOE from China to overseas buyers qualify for VAT zero-rating at 0% output VAT, with refund of the attributable input VAT, provided the goods are genuinely declared at customs and depart PRC customs territory; title and risk pass to a foreign purchaser; export proceeds are repatriated and credited through a licensed bank within the applicable State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-in-goods monitoring window; and the exporter maintains a complete corroborating documentary trail. For a production enterprise, the standard statutory method is 'exemption, credit, and refund' under the general export tax refund mechanism: export output VAT is exempted, input VAT first offsets domestic output VAT, and any residual input credit is refunded by the STA. Cross-border e-commerce retail and B2B export channels may use approved simplified declaration and refund procedures when the exporter is registered with customs and the STA. Pure physical goods export is the primary zero-rating basis for this business; qualifying offshore digital services, if any, are subject to separate VAT and tax-treaty rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export bill of entry with customs declaration number) issued by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading (original negotiable or electronic B/L evidencing transport out of PRC customs territory)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Proceeds Receipt Certificate issued by a licensed bank under SAFE trade-in-goods administration
  • Export sales contract and commercial export invoice
  • Packing list and cargo weight/volume manifest
  • Special VAT Invoice issued by the WFOE for the export sale where required
  • Customs release receipt and, where applicable, cross-border e-commerce export declaration list

VAT invoices are controlled by the STA's electronic invoice system. General taxpayers must issue Special VAT Invoices or Ordinary VAT Invoices through the State Taxation Administration Electronic Invoice Service Platform, or through approved tax-control equipment integrated with the STA network. Since the national rollout of fully digitalized electronic invoices, invoice data is uploaded in real time to the STA, and each fully digital e-invoice carries an anti-fraud QR code, digital signature/stamp, and unique invoice number. The STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system performs centralized reconciliation of invoice issuance, VAT returns, customs export declarations, and treasury receipts. Taxpayers with ERP systems may use officially supported application programming interfaces (APIs) of the e-invoice service platform for automated issuance and invoice metadata exchange. There is no private third-party fiscal clearance stamp; the STA platform itself performs validation and anti-fraud digital stamping of each electronic invoice.

The STA and China Customs share export data and may conduct documentary audits, physical inspections, and post-clearance review of export declarations, transport route evidence, insurance documents, bank receipts, and final overseas customer records. If zero-rating is claimed without actual customs exit, genuine foreign exchange inflow, or a legitimate commercial transaction, the STA may revoke the refund, recharacterize the sale as taxable at 13% VAT, and assess urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and late-payment interest of 0.05% per day. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the PRC, penalties range from 50% up to five times the tax underpaid, and the taxpayer may lose future export rebate facilitation. Fraudulent export refund and zero-rating schemes involving false customs declarations or false invoicing may be prosecuted under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud, with potential imprisonment and monetary fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility for legitimate trade and service transactions, while capital-account items remain subject to registration, approval, or post-event filing. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may lawfully receive inbound direct investment capital only after the local bank completes SAFE's FDI registration and issues a capital account registration certificate. The injected foreign currency is credited to an FDI capital account and can be converted into RMB under the enterprise's self-declared operational use, but the proceeds may not be deployed into equity investments, entrusted loans, securities or speculative real estate without special allowance. Current-account profit remittances to overseas shareholders are lawful, but the remitting bank will require annual audited financial statements, a board resolution, tax payment receipts and proof that wages, rents and domestic liabilities have been settled. There is no full capital and financial account liberalization; the WFOE must therefore build a compliance architecture matching each receipt and payment to an underlying business event, and cannot rely on blanket currency convertibility for speculative cross-border flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory conversion ratio applies to export foreign-currency receipts; enterprises may retain foreign exchange in domestic settlement accounts or sell it to designated banks at their discretion. However, proceeds must be repatriated into China and booked through a domestic bank, with the bank matching the SWIFT receipt against the corresponding customs declaration and sales contract. SAFE's total quantity monitoring normally expects collection within the trade payment terms; A-class enterprises enjoy broad flexibility, while B-class and C-class counterparties face registration, documentary review and restricted settlement. If collection lag exceeds 90 days without documented open-account terms, the bank may require the export contract, bill of lading and collection ageing explanation to reclassify the payment.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the statutory foreign-exchange regulator administratively subordinated to PBOC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary account provider. Offshore digital EMI platforms do not hold PBOC payment institution licences and cannot open resident current or capital accounts for a mainland China WFOE, cannot access CIPS settlement, and cannot perform China-specific foreign-exchange filing. Such platforms may be used only as auxiliary customer-facing payment channels after they have entered a licensed cooperation with a Chinese bank or a SAFE-approved cross-border payment institution; the corporate treasury core must remain with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE first completes SAFE foreign-direct-investment registration through the domestic bank, obtains the FDI registration certificate, and opens a foreign-currency capital account. The overseas parent then sends a SWIFT MT103 wire denominated in USD, EUR, HKD or CNY to the receiving bank, with beneficiary being the WFOE's capital account and the payment reference stating the FDI registration number and 'injection of share capital'. The receiving bank performs a capital-project authenticity check, credits the funds, and the WFOE may settle into RMB for permitted operating uses. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 50 million for financial transactions, and RMB 40 million for other transactions (SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, Art. 13). Master file mandatory if the group aggregates related-party transactions of RMB 10 billion or more (Art. 7).

Economic substance scope: China does not enact a standalone Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) comparable to the UAE; however, the general anti-avoidance rule in CIT Law Art. 47 and the substance-over-form rules under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 require that the WFOE performs actual manufacturing, IP management, and entrepreneurial risk-taking analogous to Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA). A shell entity lacking staff, premises, and real decision-making authority can be re-characterized and denied treaty benefits under beneficial ownership and GAAR. For designer blind-box toy manufacturing, the core income-generating activity is rotocasting, injection molding, and airbrush finishing; transfer pricing analysis must recognize the WFOE's routine manufacturing return and allocate residual profits only to entities performing substantive R&D or marketing functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Understatement of Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law (TAL) Art. 63: recovery of underpaid tax plus late payment interest; penalty of 50%-500% of the tax short-paid; criminal referral under Criminal Law Art. 201 for tax evasion: up to 3 years imprisonment plus fines if underpaid tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the taxable amount; 3-7 years imprisonment for huge amounts.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Art. 20 and related judicial interpretations, the corporate veil may be pierced where the director caused the company to evade tax for personal gain; personal assets are subject to restitution and fines. The tax authority will impose travel bans and exit restriction orders under TAL Art. 12 on the legal representative once tax arrears exceed RMB 10,000; egregious cases trigger criminal arrest and personal imprisonment of responsible directors.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation - Excessive Royalty Drain

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Arts. 41 and 47; SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 allows the tax bureau to adjust taxable income, re-deny royalty deductions, and apply a 5-percentage-point annual retrospective interest mark-up under Art. 48; penalties of 50%-250% of the tax shortfall for inaccurate documentation or a closed investigation; failure to file contemporaneous TP documentation triggers TAL Art. 44 fines of RMB 2,000-10,000.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Civil Code Art. 83 and Company Law Art. 20, directors who deliberately set up sham IP licensing to strip profits are jointly liable for the company's tax debts and can be disqualified as directors; the exit-ban/blacklist applies to the legal representative; criminal liability for tax evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201 can lead to imprisonment; beneficial owners may be personally liable for the adjusted CIT under thin-capitalization and substance-over-form re-characterization.
Wilful Failure to Deduct Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: TAL Art. 69: the payer is fined 50%-300% of the withheld tax that was not deducted, plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the unpaid withheld tax; under Criminal Law Art. 201, such conduct can be prosecuted as tax evasion if wilful and significant.

Director personal liability: The legal representative or CFO who signs off on unreported outward remittances becomes directly responsible; personal bank accounts and outbound travel may be restricted; if financial statement concealment is proven, judicial arrest and imprisonment of up to 7 years can be imposed. The corporate veil is pierced where the dividend was declared after falsifying profit accounts, making the shareholder and director jointly liable for treaty relief fraud.
