Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital for an ordinary WFOE under PRC Company Law. The sole shareholder must determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association and contribute it within the agreed term. No blocked escrow account is statutorily required for this non-restricted industry; however, the local SAMR and commercial bank may insist that capital be injected through a licensed foreign exchange settlement account and identified by share subscription reference before corporate bank accounts may be used for operating payments.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense Fire Acceptance Inspection and Fire Safety Check must be completed before production starts; building structural load inspection and approval by the Housing and Urban-Rural Development Bureau is mandatory; workplace occupational hazard registration and safety production clearance must be obtained from the Emergency Management Bureau; the facility must install fire alarm and suppression equipment, emergency exits, emergency lighting, and appoint a designated fire safety warden; all workers handling paints, plastics, or chemical agents must participate in occupational health surveillance and receive material safety data sheets. No manufacturing or warehousing activity may begin until the fire certificate and workplace safety approvals are physically posted at the site.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic administration through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax portal) and the local State Taxation Administration office for the registered WFOE address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor files notarised and legalised or apostilled corporate documents with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to establish the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE). Step 2: SAMR issues the business licence bearing an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number for the enterprise. Step 3: Tax registration is completed automatically through the multi-licence one-stop integration between SAMR and the State Taxation Administration, and the WFOE activates its China Electronic Tax Bureau account. Step 4: Using the e-Tax portal, the WFOE completes tax-registration particulars, appoints the legal representative and financial officer, determines VAT status (small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer above the statutory threshold), and registers export-tax-rebate eligibility before commencing export sales. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without permanent establishment, the Chinese withholding payer handles non-resident tax registration through the e-Tax withholding procedure; the tax authority issues a non-resident taxpayer registration identifier used for information matching and withholding compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China on worldwide income, with no pass-through/partnership classification.

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity-classification elections for a limited-liability WFOE; the resident-enterprise classification is fixed by statute. VAT small-scale versus general taxpayer status is an administrative status governed by turnover and election thresholds, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration for VAT is mandatory for all WFOEs from establishment with no turnover threshold; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed Renminbi (RMB) 5,000,000; below that amount the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer and may voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The manufacturing WFOE may zero-rate export sales of physical action-figure/mecha model kits if: the goods are actually exported out of China customs territory; the sale is made to an overseas buyer with title passing at export; the export is covered by a China Customs export goods declaration; the WFOE is VAT-registered and export-tax-rebate registered; the export sales, customs values and input VAT trail are declared in the VAT return and export-tax-rebate declaration; and export proceeds are received in a licensed bank foreign-exchange account and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange within the statutory repatriation window, ordinarily 180 days from export declaration. Qualifying offshore digital services are outside this ISIC goods manufacturing scope.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (export goods customs declaration) with electronic release record
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal/air transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / licensed bank export-proceeds receipt and foreign-exchange settlement confirmation
  • Export VAT invoice or ordinary digital invoice issued for the export sale
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract
  • Packing list, weight manifest and shipping instructions
  • Certificate of Origin or other export eligibility documents where required

China operates a fully digitalised electronic invoice mandate. The WFOE must issue fully digitalised electronic invoices through the China Electronic Tax Bureau and the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform of the State Taxation Administration; invoice data is transmitted electronically to the tax authority in real time for authentication before the invoice may be used, and special VAT invoices must complete digital authentication in the national VAT invoice system before input VAT credit is available. Anti-fraud controls include unique digital invoice numbers, QR codes, cryptographic tax-control signatures, and automatic cross-matching with General Administration of Customs export declarations and State Administration of Foreign Exchange export-proceeds data for export zero-rating substantiation.

The State Taxation Administration, General Administration of Customs and State Administration of Foreign Exchange run automated data matching on customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading and export-proceeds receipts. Unverified or fraudulent zero-rating/export-rebate claims are rejected; any VAT refund already paid is clawed back with the statutory VAT underpaid, plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the outstanding amount and, under the Tax Collection Administration Law, fines of 50% to 5 times the tax involved. False customs declarations expose the WFOE to Customs Law penalties. Fraud intended to obtain export tax refunds is a criminal offence under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, punishable by imprisonment and forfeiture of assets. Ordinary understatements are generally assessable for three years, extendable to five years for larger omissions, while fraudulent evasion may be pursued without time limit. Compliance failures can also lead to restriction or removal of VAT invoice issuance rights, delisting from the export tax rebate system, inclusion in the public list of serious tax dishonesty, and exit restrictions on the legal representative and responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under which current account transactions are broadly convertible after submission of genuine trade documentation, while capital account transactions remain highly controlled and require specific registration, approvals, and bank-level verification. Under the harmonized foreign investment rules, a WFOE is permitted to receive inbound foreign currency equity into an FDI capital settlement account and to convert those funds into RMB under SAFE's capital account settlement framework, but the scope of use is restricted by the foreign investment negative list and prohibited from flowing into regulated sectors without additional licensing. Profit remittances are permitted only after annual statutory audits and completion of corporate income tax settlement, and the central bank authority retains the right to examine the authenticity of the underlying earnings and the bank must verify that the remittance is supported by audited financial statements, foreign exchange registration information, and a board resolution. In addition, all cross-border RMB and foreign exchange receipts and payments must be reported through the bank's SAFE system, and any mismatch between the declared business purpose and the actual transaction documents is treated as a compliance violation. Registered capital can be repatriated only after a legal liquidation procedure; advance dividend remittances, disguised loans, or round-tripping structures are closely scrutinized and often blocked. Therefore, the WFOE must treat the onshore banking relationship as the central treasury anchor and maintain a disciplined documentation trail for every capital and current account flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China within the statutory collection period of 90 days for general trade with authorized banks, extendable to 180 days where genuine trade justification is accepted. The old mandatory surrender system has been abolished, so there is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; exporters may retain receipts in foreign currency in their designated foreign exchange settlement accounts or voluntarily convert to RMB through the settlement bank. All export receipts must be declared under the SAFE cross-border receipts and payments reporting system with matching customs and trade documents, and failure to repatriate or misdeclaration triggers administrative penalties, reduced bank quotas, and enhanced AML review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / Non-Viable as the primary treasury rail for this WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC as commercial banks, cannot hold a domestic RMB settlement account, cannot clear through CNAPS, IBPS, or CIPS, and are not recognized counterparties under the SAFE foreign exchange registration workflow. They can only function as a marginal front-end collection tool through licensed Chinese payment integrators for e-commerce pocket transactions, and they are legally barred from servicing the WFOE's capital injection, statutory export surrender, trade settlement, or FX hedging operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign currency equity must be wired to the WFOE's FDI capital settlement account at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank using SWIFT MT103, with the remittance reference clearly cross-referencing the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for inward direct investment equity injection and the company's FDI registration number. The bank will verify the shareholder's prior SAFE registration, the authenticity of the commercial substance, and the compliance of the sender account before crediting the funds. After receipt, a licensed PRC CPA issues a capital verification report confirming the paid-in registered capital, and any conversion of foreign currency into RMB must be executed through the bank's capital account settlement system within the negative-list permitted scope. The WFOE must maintain a documented trail of the board resolution, subscription agreement, bank receipt, and SAFE reporting confirmation for subsequent auditor and regulator review.The WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 for direct payments to international suppliers when invoices, packing lists, contracts, and customs declarations are fully matched, while larger or higher-risk trade flows will be settled through Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the Chinese bank via SWIFT MT700 with cover payments routed by MT202 or MT103. Documentary Collections are available for established and lower-risk counterparties where the bank only transmits drafts and title documents. For cross-border RMB transactions with counterparties accepting CNY, CIPS is the preferred clearing rail, reducing the settlement risk and fees associated with USD correspondent switching. Every outbound trade payment must fit the entity's SAFE trade registration, and the bank will review underlying trade documents, invoice currency, and goods descriptions before releasing funds.Deliverable CNY foreign exchange forwards with licensed onshore banks, permitted only for genuine current account trade receivables and payables with real documentation and maturity aligned to the underlying cash flowCross-currency swaps for onshore capital account flows, such as converting foreign currency capital proceeds into RMB while preserving the ability to fund future USD trade payments, only to hedge a real and documented exposureOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as a primary onshore hedging instrument for the WFOE itself; the parent may use NDFs outside China only if settlement gains and losses are separately ring-fenced and governed by an approved board treasury policy

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the PRC Company Law all limited liability companies, including wholly foreign-owned enterprises, are obliged to have their annual financial statements audited by a lawfully licensed independent Chinese accounting firm; no turnover or asset exemption removes this statutory audit for a WFOE. The Ministry of Finance classification of enterprise size uses quantitative indicators such as total assets above RMB 50 million, annual business revenue above RMB 30 million, or headcount above 200 employees as elevated review and large-enterprise reporting thresholds, but these thresholds only intensify documentation and audit obligations and do not serve as an exemption from mandatory CPA auditing for small WFOEs. Furthermore, banks, tax bureaus, and the foreign exchange authorities effectively require a certified audit report for any WFOE engaged in cross-border payment, foreign debt, purchase, sale, or annual foreign investment reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party tangible-property transfers (e.g. finished mecha model kits, plastic resins, and steel molds) exceed RMB 200,000,000, or where intangible-property transactions, financial asset transfers, or services with related parties exceed RMB 100,000,000; the Master File is due when the consolidated group-wide related-party turnover reaches RMB 1,000,000,000 or when the tax authority formally requests it. All WFOEs must file the Related Party Transactions Annual Report regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations, but it enforces equivalent substance and anti-avoidance rules through EIT Law Articles 41, 46 and 47 and the OECD BEPS Action Plans. The WFOE must demonstrate actual economic substance in China by retaining employees, management control, manufacturing equipment, production execution, inventory and warranty risk, and substantive decision-making over pricing, procurement and product development. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for injection molding and model kit assembly are performed in China; profits must be allocated to the Chinese entity in proportion to its functions, assets and risks, and any offshore contract-manufacturing or I.P. shell arrangement that strips profits to a low-tax jurisdiction will be recharacterized, resulting in transfer pricing adjustments, deemed dividends, interest charges and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax return

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 63, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax, imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount, and charges a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%. Under Criminal Law Article 201, tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 in one year and constituting more than 10% of taxable income attracts fines of 1 to 5 times the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors use the WFOE as an instrumentality; directors are jointly and severally liable out of personal assets for the underpaid tax, penalties and interest, face bank account freezes, asset seizure, and are placed on the blacklist of dishonest taxpayers with travel bans and exit restrictions; the responsible legal representative and finance director may be criminally prosecuted and sentenced to imprisonment for up to seven years.
Transfer pricing abuse and profit stripping

Statutory sanction: Special tax adjustment under EIT Law Articles 41 and 114 recharacterizes the transactions and adjusts taxable income to the arm's length price. The tax authority imposes an additional levy equal to 10% of the underpaid tax for each year of underpayment, plus daily surcharge, and a penalty of up to 50% of the adjusted tax amount. Failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation triggers a RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 administrative penalty, a formal TP risk warning, and possible deemed dividend treatment.

Director personal liability: If the profit stripping is found to be a sham, courts pierce the WFOE veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 and hold directors personally liable for restitution of the diverted profits; personal bank accounts and real estate of directors may be attached to collect the tax shortfall; the directors face credit blacklisting, travel bans, prohibition on leaving China, and criminal liability for tax evasion where intentional conduct is proven.
Shell company, treaty abuse and money laundering

Statutory sanction: The treaty benefit is denied under SAT Public Notice No. 9 (2018) and GAAR under EIT Law Article 47; the WFOE must withhold the full 10% dividend WHT plus a 50% to 500% penalty. The AML Law and Criminal Law Article 191 impose fines on the entity of up to RMB 5 million, forfeiture of the transferred funds, suspension or revocation of the business license, and imprisonment of responsible personnel of up to ten years for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the treaty relief forms, order the dividend transfer, and conceal the beneficial owner are personally liable for the unwithheld tax and face asset restitution; the individuals are blacklisted as untrustworthy persons, banned from leaving China, disqualified from serving as directors, and may be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion and money laundering with imprisonment of up to ten years plus fines.
