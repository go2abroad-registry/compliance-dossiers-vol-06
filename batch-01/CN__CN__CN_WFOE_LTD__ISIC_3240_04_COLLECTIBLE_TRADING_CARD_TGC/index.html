Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); official online portal: the Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax) integrated online service platform, accessible through the STA official website at www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident shareholder first incorporates the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation and receives the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE's legal representative or authorized tax agent must complete national tax registration through the local STA Electronic Taxation Bureau by submitting the incorporation certificate, articles of association, registered-address proof, shareholder identity documents, legal representative appointment deed, financial officer identity information, and bank account opening certificate. The STA assigns the taxpayer identity keyed to the USCC, confirms resident enterprise status, VAT taxpayer category, payroll withholding duties, and activates the VAT invoice management system. For export activities, the WFOE must additionally register with the General Administration of Customs to obtain a 10-digit customs registration code and link its tax identity to the China International Trade Single Window export declaration platform. A foreign non-resident seller without a Chinese permanent establishment follows only non-resident withholding registration; a WFOE, however, is a Chinese resident entity and is registered directly as the taxable person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the WFOE is a Chinese tax resident limited liability company taxed on worldwide income at entity level; no pass-through or tax-transparent status is available).

Available election forms

None. Chinese corporate income tax law does not provide a check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or entity-disregard regime for a WFOE; the entity is automatically classified as a resident enterprise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all incorporated WFOEs must complete tax registration within 30 days of incorporation regardless of turnover. Export-oriented security-printing and trading-card manufacturers generally register as general VAT taxpayers in order to access export zero-rating and input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT applies to collectible trading cards physically exported from China where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, the export is evidenced by a China Customs export declaration, and the foreign-currency sales proceeds are repatriated through an approved banking channel. The goods must not be consumed domestically in China; domestic sales of the same security-printed cards remain taxable at 13% VAT.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window / e-Customs platform
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal/air waybill for non-sea shipments)
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank foreign-currency receipt voucher / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) equivalent issued by the settlement bank through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange cross-border receipts system
  • VAT special invoice or fully digital electronic invoice

The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the STA-controlled VAT invoice management system integrated with the Electronic Taxation Bureau platform. Special VAT invoices and fully digital electronic invoices carry an STA-issued tax-control code, encrypted invoice signature, and QR anti-counterfeit stamp; invoice data are transmitted to the STA in near-real-time via the prescribed tax-control interface/API and matched with customs export declarations. Unauthorized off-line invoice generation is prohibited, and export VAT refund or zero-rating treatment requires the invoice data to have been successfully reported and matched.

The STA, customs, and foreign exchange administration jointly cross-check export declarations, VAT invoice data, transport documents, and bank receipt records. If export zero-rating is claimed without verified physical export or without retention of the full statutory documentation bundle, the STA disallows the 0% rate, requires the supply to be reclassified as taxable at 13% VAT, recovers any input VAT refund or credit already granted, and imposes daily late-payment surcharge. Where false documentation is knowingly used to obtain an export VAT refund, administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times of the false refund amount apply, and serious cases are prosecuted as criminal fraud under the Criminal Law provision on fraudulent export tax rebates, with penalties including imprisonment or life imprisonment and fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a bifurcated convertibility system. The current account, which includes trade in goods, services and current intra-company transfers, is fully convertible in accordance with IMF Article VIII. Exporters and importers may freely convert RMB to foreign currency and vice versa for current-account transactions upon submission of underlying commercial documentation to a designated bank. However, the capital account remains heavily controlled by SAFE. Inbound direct investment, including registered capital of a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, requires an FDI foreign exchange registration and must enter through a dedicated capital account. Repatriation of equity investment and liquidation proceeds requires a capital verification report, tax clearance, and SAFE reporting. Profit remittance is permitted after annual audited financial statements are filed and corporate income tax has been settled, with conversion at prevailing market rates and a bank-required tax payment certificate. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation, but there is no mandatory surrender ratio: the WFOE may hold foreign currency proceeds in a licensed foreign exchange account or convert them into RMB at its discretion. Short-term outbound capital flows and financial derivative transactions without real business background remain restricted by SAFE regulations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-in-goods total-value screening, export proceeds must be repatriated to mainland China within the general statutory default period of 90 days from the date of export customs declaration unless an extension has been approved by the bank or SAFE for legitimate commercial reasons. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio: the exporting WFOE may retain 100% of repatriated foreign exchange in a designated foreign-currency cash account or convert it into RMB at the prevailing market rate offered by the bank, subject to reconciliation with customs declarations and accurate reporting of payment purpose. Failure to repatriate within the applicable deadline triggers SAFE monitoring alerts, data matching discrepancies, and potential administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) through designated foreign exchange banks

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex, Payoneer and other similar payment service providers are not licensed by SAFE as designated foreign exchange banks and therefore cannot serve as the primary deposit-taking treasury hub for a mainland China WFOE. Their legal use is limited to acting as cross-border payment aggregators or collection rails that funnel settlement proceeds into a licensed Chinese bank account. The WFOE may use them for small-ticket e-commerce collections from platforms such as Stripe and Authorize.Net, but final RMB clearing, FX conversion, and all capital-account transactions must be booked with a licensed onshore bank. Practical viability is low for working-capital banking and corporate bank accounts, medium as an ancillary collection and reconciliation service, and legally prohibited for holding corporate deposits or executing onshore FX settlement.

All inbound registered capital must be remitted from the foreign investor's account to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank. Before funds are accepted, the WFOE must complete FDI Foreign Exchange Registration and obtain the bank reference and business registration code. The wire instruction must be sent as a SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary capital account, quoting the SAFE BOP payment purpose code for inbound direct investment equity contribution and the FDI registration number, with the remitter name exactly matching the registered foreign investor. Upon receipt, the designated bank performs a compliance check, issues an FX settlement notification, and permits RMB conversion under the payment-matching procedure for approved capital uses, supported by a self-formed Statement of Use of Capital for post-verification by SAFE. A Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming the statutory capital contribution schedule before the funds can be deployed into operational accounts.Outbound payments for imported raw materials, security holographic foils, and IP royalties are executed through SWIFT MT103 for ordinary commercial payments, referencing the import contract, customs declaration for goods, or deemed sales contract for services. Commercial letters of credit, at sight or usance, are recommended for first-time supplier relationships involving high-value orders of collectible card substrate and specialized printing materials; the issuing bank in China will advise through a correspondent bank. Documentary collections, including documents against payment and documents against acceptance, are acceptable for trusted counterparties and short-term trade finance. For RMB-denominated cross-border trade, the WFOE may use CIPS through its Chinese clearing bank; for USD and EUR, CHIPS or local RTGS clearing is accessed via the designated bank's foreign currency clearing account. All disbursements must be supported by contract, invoice, transport documents, and, where applicable, tax withholding evidence for royalties.Onshore deliverable FX forwards permissible for hedging anticipated current-account receipts and payments under genuine trade exposure; forward maturity must match the underlying transaction and require physical delivery of the foreign currency or RMB.FX swaps permissible for temporary liquidity management between RMB and USD, EUR, or other major currencies, subject to SAFE registration and a real trade or financing background, with no speculative use permitted.Cross-currency swaps and interest-rate swaps permissible for long-term foreign-currency financing or capital-account funding, subject to SAFE approval and matching to underlying borrowing and repayment flows.Plain-vanilla bought FX options and option collars permissible for hedging contingent export revenues from collectible cards, with sold options generally restricted for onshore non-financial corporate entities.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) not permissible onshore; NDFs are offshore RMB hedging instruments available only in the CNH market and are outside the legal perimeter for a mainland China WFOE.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex, Payoneer and other similar payment service providers are not licensed by SAFE as designated foreign exchange banks and therefore cannot serve as the primary deposit-taking treasury hub for a mainland China WFOE. Their legal use is limited to acting as cross-border payment aggregators or collection rails that funnel settlement proceeds into a licensed Chinese bank account. The WFOE may use them for small-ticket e-commerce collections from platforms such as Stripe and Authorize.Net, but final RMB clearing, FX conversion, and all capital-account transactions must be booked with a licensed onshore bank. Practical viability is low for working-capital banking and corporate bank accounts, medium as an ancillary collection and reconciliation service, and legally prohibited for holding corporate deposits or executing onshore FX settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation thresholds: Local File required where annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed CNY 200,000,000; related-party financial asset transfers exceed CNY 100,000,000; related-party intangible asset transfers exceed CNY 100,000,000; or other related-party transactions (services, interest, royalties, management fees) exceed CNY 40,000,000. Master File is required where the aggregate annual related-party transactions reach or exceed CNY 1,000,000,000. Country-by-Country Report is required for a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of at least CNY 5,500,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year, subject to the group parent's filing and treaty requirements.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations code, but the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, the General Anti-Abuse Rule (EIT Law Article 47), and the State Taxation Administration's beneficial-ownership administration apply a substance-over-form test. A WFOE engaged in trading-card holographic cold foil printing must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China: actual personnel, physical premises, manufacturing equipment, strategic risk-taking, invoicing, treasury decisions, and international distribution contracts controlled from China. If the WFOE is a shell or conduit, the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize transactions, disregard the intermediate entity, and re-attribute profit to the Chinese enterprise. Foreign shareholders claiming treaty benefits must also prove the same substantive activity in their residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing documentation failure and TP manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 44 and its Implementing Regulations, the tax authorities may apply deemed pricing methods and adjust taxable income. Under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 63 and 64, the underpaid tax is assessed together with a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. In cases involving deliberate evasion, criminal prosecution may follow; the enterprise may also be disqualified from tax preferential treatment and lose export VAT refund status.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who signed false documentation are exposed to personal civil liability for damages suffered by the company or creditors, including the tax authority's restitution order, under PRC Company Law Articles 147 and 149. In severe cases the court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 when directors abuse the limited-liability structure, causing tax losses. Personal consequences include travel bans and blacklisting through the tax irregularity public announcement system; criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment up to 7 years and fines for those who have organized or facilitated tax evasion.
Shell WFOE and treaty shopping without economic substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny DTA benefits under the State Taxation Administration's beneficial-ownership and treaty-abuse rules, and may apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule under EIT Law Articles 47 and 48 to recharacterize the transaction. As a result, the payment is treated as China-source dividend/interest/royalty subject to 10% WHT, plus late surcharges and a 50%-500% penalty. The intermediate company may be disregarded, and income reallocated to the Chinese operating entity. The WFOE may be added to the tax blacklist, its export registration may be suspended, and Customs/foreign exchange penalties may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Veil piercing is available when the company is undercapitalized, assets are commingled with shareholder assets, or the entity is a sham. Under Company Law Article 20 and company law judicial interpretations, directors, de facto controllers, and nominee shareholders may be ordered to assume unlimited joint liability for unpaid corporate tax and third-party debts. Directors who participate in fabricated board minutes, backdated agreements, or false beneficial-ownership declarations may receive administrative blacklisting, travel restrictions, and enforceable asset seizure; criminal fraud or tax evasion charges under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 224 may result in imprisonment up to 10 years and personal fine or confiscation.
Cross-border revenue concealment through offshore payment rails and undisclosed export proceeds

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, this constitutes tax evasion and requires payment of underpaid tax, daily surcharge, and fines of 50%-500% of the evaded amount. Under the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration, undeclared cross-border receipts and misreported settlement expose the entity to foreign-exchange penalties, confiscation of unlawful proceeds, and prohibition from registering new foreign-invested enterprises. The e-commerce sales records of foreign payment processors may be cross-matched by Chinese tax authorities, and the failure to declare VAT and EIT revenue can also trigger criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who controlled offshore bank accounts and concealed revenue may be held liable personally when the WFOE cannot satisfy the tax order, especially if assets were distributed or transferred to shareholders. Under Company Law Article 20 and Tax Collection Law Article 49, the court can lift the corporate veil, restrain exit, freeze personal assets, and impose restitution. Directors face criminal exposure under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion, Article 203 for failure to pay or maliciously transferring assets to evade execution, or Article 225 for illegal foreign exchange operations in aggravated cases; imprisonment ranges from 3 to 10 years with fines, and the criminal penalty follows even if the company is later liquidated.
