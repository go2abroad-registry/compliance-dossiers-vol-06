Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (electronic tax administration platform) accessible via the official STA portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn; local STA offices administer registrations within the unified national system.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) formed as a limited liability company in China, the statutory tax identification process starts with registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the sole tax identification number for the entity. Under the national multi-certification-one-account reform, tax registration is completed concurrently with market entity registration: SAMR transmits the registration record to the STA, and the WFOE must then activate its tax administration account on the Electronic Tax Bureau using the USCC, the legal representative identity verification, and corporate digital credentials. The WFOE must then complete VAT taxpayer classification registration, electing or being classified as a general VAT taxpayer when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or when voluntary general taxpayer status is sought, and must file the appropriate tax registration information including business scope, bank account details, accounting standards, and legal representative data. A foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment but making taxable supplies from outside China must instead obtain a tax identity through the withholding registration procedure at the STA service office where the withholding agent is registered, receiving a contract-based tax code for that transaction; however, the WFOE itself is resident and obtains the USCC-based tax number through the standard SAMR/STA synchronization pathway.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election or hybrid mismatch election. The WFOE is automatically treated as a resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax and VAT. Statutory election mechanisms are limited to VAT taxpayer status elections: the simplified levy method for small-scale taxpayers versus the general VAT taxpayer input-output credit method, voluntary registration as a general taxpayer where turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, and the general taxpayer election as required for export zero-rating eligibility. Anti-hybrid and controlled foreign company rules under the Enterprise Income Tax Law apply independently of any tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All VAT taxpayers in China are required to register; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Entities below this threshold may remain small-scale VAT taxpayers, subject to the simplified levy rate of 3% (with temporary preferential reduction to 1% currently in effect) and no input VAT crediting. A WFOE intending to export goods may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer regardless of turnover in order to claim 0% export VAT treatment and associated input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A WFOE may claim 0% VAT export zero-rating on resin statue goods only if it is a general VAT taxpayer and the goods are actually exported from mainland China under customs supervision. The statutory criteria include: the existence of a valid written export sales contract; lodging of a formal customs export declaration; physical departure of the goods through a designated customs port with customs release records; delivery of the commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading or air waybill evidencing transport to an overseas destination; receipt of the sales proceeds in foreign currency through a licensed Chinese bank in accordance with foreign exchange control regulations; and completion of all export tax refund qualification filings with the STA. Certain qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas customers may also qualify for zero-rating or exemption under the cross-border services VAT rules, but the goods-based resin statue supply must follow the export goods regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs export bill of entry with final customs release endorsement)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill evidencing international carriage of the resin statues outside China)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign exchange repatriation certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds

China has implemented mandatory nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoices (known as digital e-invoices) issued through the national unified electronic invoice service platform on the Electronic Tax Bureau. VAT invoices are generated, validated, and uploaded to tax authorities in real time, with each invoice receiving a unique system-assigned invoice number and tax-control authentication. Issuance is integrated into the taxpayer's business systems through API-based interfaces with the tax administration platform; the traditional paper invoice stamping and separate tax-control disk clearance for ordinary invoices is being superseded by the digital invoice mandate. All sales data, including exports, must be reflected through the electronic invoice and customs data exchange channels to support VAT compliance and export rebate verification.

The STA and customs authorities jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims through cross-verification of customs declarations, electronic invoices, logistics records, financial receipts, and foreign exchange data. Any false declaration, fabricated invoice, doctored bill of lading, or unrepatriated export proceeds will invalidate the 0% VAT treatment. The tax authority will recover the refunded VAT, assess late payment surcharges from the due date at 0.05% per day, impose a penalty of between 50% and five times the tax amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, and may impose fines for export control violations. Cases involving intentional fraudulent export tax refund schemes may be referred for criminal prosecution for the crime of defrauding export tax refunds, resulting in forfeiture of illegally obtained refunds, imprisonment, and fines. The enterprise may also be suspended from export tax refund registration and placed on a high-risk audit list for at least three years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under the RMB. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are generally freely convertible for WFOEs, subject to genuine transaction verification and SAFE filing. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled; inbound direct investment requires registration with SAFE via a domestic bank, and outbound capital flows such as dividend remittances, capital reduction, and liquidation proceeds require verification of audited financials, tax clearance, and bank-level compliance review. Profit remittance is permitted once annual audit and tax returns are filed, but the bank must validate that the enterprise has fulfilled its foreign exchange registration, actual capital verification, and any outstanding SAFE reporting obligations. Surrender of export proceeds is not mandatory in the traditional sense; the current rule (effective from 2020 onward under Notice 14) requires that export foreign currency receipts be collected into a domestic account, but enterprises may retain or use such proceeds within allowed current account purposes. However, a 1% random and non-random post-verification ratio applies to trade payments. For a resin statue manufacturing WFOE selling cross-border through e-commerce, the bulk of revenue will arrive as inbound payments via Stripe/PayPal cash collection gateways. These must be settled through a licensed domestic bank account, with underlying transaction documentation (sales orders, customs declaration, e-commerce platform settlement statements) to support settlement and avoid future SAFE penalties. The key hurdle is not convertibility per se but documentary integrity: SAFE requires each settlement to have a matching customs record or digital trade certificate. Because this WFOE is engaged in high-end resin statues with potential licensing of intellectual property, the bank will classify royalty payments as current account services, requiring a technology import contract registration with the local branch of SAFE if the license fees exceed a certain threshold. Overall, RMB is convertible on current account with robust compliance, but capital account flows remain strictly license-based and time-consuming.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 2020, China no longer imposes a mandatory surrender ratio on export proceeds. WFOEs must repatriate exports into a domestic foreign currency or RMB account within the following statutory timeline: (a) if the export collection period is 90 days or less, the proceeds must be settled (or retained) within 30 days after the prescribed collection date; (b) if the enterprise applies for an extension beyond 90 days, the proceeds must enter the domestic account within the extended period as approved by the bank, not exceeding 180 days from the date of export declaration, unless a special SAFE deferral is granted. The conversion to RMB is voluntary; the enterprise may retain 100% of foreign currency in its current account or convert to RMB at market rate. However, all trade-related receipts must be matched with the China Customs export declaration data and e-commerce platform transaction records through the bank's trade digitalization system. Discrepancies exceeding USD 5,000 or 5% of the declared value will trigger a manual compliance review and may delay liquidity deployment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability. Legislation in mainland China requires all cross-border transaction settlements to be performed through a licensed domestic commercial bank with a physical branch registered with PBOC/SAFE. Fintech EMI entities, including offshore digital banks such as Revolut, N26, or Hong Kong virtual banks without a Chinese banking license, are legally barred from providing destination account settlement services for Chinese-resident WFOEs. They may be used as upstream payouts for overseas supply chain subsidiaries but cannot serve as the primary treasury account for the Chinese entity. Even for foreign-currency collection via Stripe or PayPal, the final settlement must be routed to a domestic bank account under the WFOE's name with a valid SAFE trade code. Some licensed fintech payment institutions in China (e.g., PingPong, LianLian Global) may assist with e-commerce collection, but these are regulated as payment facilitators and must clear through an underlying Tier-1 licensed bank. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must establish Tier-1 domestic bank settlement rails as the sole liquidity backbone; no independent EMI can satisfy capital verification, export proceeds registration, or dividend remittance requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. Legislation in mainland China requires all cross-border transaction settlements to be performed through a licensed domestic commercial bank with a physical branch registered with PBOC/SAFE. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File/ Master File documentation is required if the related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 100,000,000 (for combined transactions) or RMB 20,000,000 for a single type of intercompany transaction (e.g., sale of resin statues to a foreign parent). In practice, the STA's thresholds are: total related-party sales, purchases, or services exceeding RMB 100 million annually; or the combined amount of all related-party transactions exceeding RMB 200 million. In addition, a Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required only for MNEs with annual consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more. The WFOE should maintain contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation (Chapter 114, Implementation Rules) if its transactions reach these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must demonstrate substantive manufacturing and commercial functions. Under the PRC Circular 7 (2015) and the implementation of the BEPS Action 6 & 13, the State Taxation Administration applies economic substance requirements to deny treaty benefits to shell companies lacking genuine business activities. For high-value resin statue production, the WFOE must show: (a) physical plant and equipment in China, (b) core R&D and design staff for the product, (c) independent decision-making regarding pricing, marketing, and raw-material sourcing, and (d) bearing of operational risks. Since the entity is a manufacturing WFOE, the economic substance doctrine is used to allocate profit to the Chinese manufacturing functions. If the foreign parent licenses IP to the WFOE, the WFOE must prove it is not a mere contract manufacturer with artificial profit stripping. The General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the EIT Law may re-characterize transactions that lack commercial purpose.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Artificial Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the EIT Law and Circular 6/2016, the STA may adjust the taxable income upward and impose a 5% to 10% additional TP penalty on the understated tax if transfer pricing adjustments exceed a threshold, plus a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. If the adjustment is due to missing contemporaneous documentation, the penalty can be up to 10% of the underpaid tax. The tax underpayment is subject to interest at the benchmark lending rate, which may be compounded.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documents or deliberately understate revenue may be held personally liable for the unpaid CIT to the extent that the corporate assets are insufficient to satisfy the tax claim. Under Article 20 of the Company Law, a court may pierce the corporate veil if the director uses the company as a vehicle for tax evasion, causing creditors or the State to be defrauded. The director may face a travel ban (出境限制) issued by the STA for unpaid tax liabilities exceeding RMB 10,000, preventing departure from China. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law (Tax Evasion) can lead to up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine, if the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax due, as well as seizure of personal assets.
Dividend WHT Evasion / Fraudulent DTA Relief Claim

Statutory sanction: Under Article 23 of the EIT Law and Announcement 2019 No. 35, the STA will deny treaty benefits and issue a reassessment for the additional 5% WHT, plus a late payment surcharge of 0.05% daily and a penalty ranging from 50% to 5 times the amount of the undeclared tax (Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law). If the 'loan' is recharacterized as a deemed dividend, the same penalty applies. The WFOE, as the withholding agent, is jointly liable for the full amount if it fails to verify the beneficiary's economic substance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the unpaid WHT if they knowingly arranged the dividend through a conduit and failed to withhold. A director can be ordered to pay restitution of the withheld taxes from personal assets under the veil-piercing doctrine. The PRC Exit-Entry Administration Law permits the tax authority to impose a travel ban on the legal representative and the responsible director until the outstanding tax and penalties are paid. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion (Article 201) can result in 3 to 7 years in prison and criminal fines. Additionally, banks and payment processors may be notified, freezing the WFOE's bank accounts, which affects PayPal and Stripe cash flows.
Underreporting E-Commerce Sales / Use of Unreported Offshore Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, underreporting or non-reporting of taxable revenue is classified as 'concealment' (偷税). The STA will demand payment of the omitted CIT (25%), VAT (13%), and any late payment surcharges, and impose a fine of 50% to 5 times the amount of tax evaded. In addition, the WFOE may be blacklisted under the 'Serious Tax Illegal Acts Joint Punishment Memorandum', which suspends VAT invoicing, blocks export refunds, restricts government procurement, and revokes business licenses. If the underreported amount is significant (typically exceeding RMB 50,000 in tax), criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law applies.

Director personal liability: The directors and the financial controller who designed or tacitly approved the off-book payment structure can be held personally liable for the full tax liability plus penalties if the company's assets are insufficient. Under the personal liability rule (the 2009 Supreme Court interpretation on the application of tax evasion), directors may be named in criminal indictments as the persons directly responsible. The consequences include: (1) personal asset seizure and restitution; (2) entry into the tax blacklist (失信名单) leading to travel bans, business flight bans, and restrictions on taking high-speed trains/air travel; (3) criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion, or up to 3 years for failure to file if the value is above certain threshold. Additionally, directors may be disqualified from serving as company officers in China for up to 5 years, and any attempt to dissolve or transfer assets is nullified.
