Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local competent State Tax Bureau of the WFOE's place of incorporation, with online filing and registration through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (electronic tax bureau portal: etax.chinatax.gov.cn; principal STA portal: chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Administration for Market Regulation issues an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the sole statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number for the WFOE. No separate VAT ID is issued. The market-registration information is automatically shared with the STA, and the WFOE must complete the tax registration information confirmation in the Electronic Tax Bureau within the prescribed post-incorporation period (generally within 30 days of obtaining the business license), declaring taxpayer type, financial-accounting standards, bank account, legal representative and business scope. The local STA then confirms VAT taxpayer status, invoice capability, and records the export tax rebate (exemption) registration for the WFOE before export zero-rating can be claimed. For physical exports, the WFOE must separately obtain the GACC customs registration code as an import/export goods consignee/consignor through the China International Trade Single Window. For foreign non-resident entities that have no Chinese permanent establishment but receive Chinese-source payments from the WFOE, the WFOE as withholding agent must register each non-resident enterprise with the STA in the e-tax system and obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration number for withholding purposes; this non-resident registration does not change the WFOE's own entity-level tax classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim the 0% export VAT rate, the WFOE must be a PRC VAT general taxpayer with valid export tax rebate (exemption) registration. The wooden puzzles must be physically exported from the customs territory of China to an overseas destination, declared to the General Administration of Customs (GACC) under the applicable export commodity code (HS 9503.00 for puzzles), and cleared by customs. The WFOE must retain an accepted Customs Export Declaration, transport documents proving physical departure, the commercial invoice and contract with an overseas buyer, and bank-verified evidence of repatriation of the export sales proceeds in foreign currency through a SAFE-designated bank. For cross-border e-commerce sales, platform order data, Amazon Pay or Stripe settlement statements, delivery records and customs export data must be consistent. The export zero-rating operates through the exemption-and-refund mechanism: output VAT on the export is zero and attributable input VAT is refunded. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only where the statutory cross-border services list is met and delivery/use occurs wholly outside China; the WFOE's core scope here is physical goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) accepted by the General Administration of Customs, declaring the export commodity code HS 9503.00 and the applicable customs supervision/export trade mode (e.g., 9610, 9710 or 9810 for eligible cross-border e-commerce export models)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal/air transport document proving the goods physically departed China from a Chinese customs port to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export sales contract or overseas purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange income voucher issued by the PRC settlement bank under SAFE, evidencing receipt and repatriation of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Export tax refund (exemption) declaration data filed in the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the export sales invoice referencing the corresponding customs export declaration number

VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved electronic tax invoice systems; general taxpayers issue Special VAT Invoices or fully digitalized electronic invoices, with invoice data transmitted in near real time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system. Each invoice carries a unique invoice number, the taxpayer's USCC, item description, rate, amount and an anti-counterfeit two-dimensional code. Input VAT deduction requires the recipient to verify and confirm the invoice online in the VAT invoice selection platform before crediting input VAT. The fully digitalized electronic invoice reform permits qualifying taxpayers to operate without physical Tax Ukey devices, but the digital invoices must still be issued through the national unified electronic invoice service platform and cleared electronically in real time. For exports, an export sales invoice referencing the customs export declaration number must be retained and filed in the electronic tax bureau export rebate system.

The STA, GACC and SAFE cross-check export declaration data, invoice data, logistics events, platform settlement data and bank foreign-exchange receipts under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. If a claimed export is found to be false, not physically exported, misdeclared in value, supported by forged documentation, or lacking bank-verified foreign-exchange repatriation within the applicable deadline (generally 180 days unless a lawful extension has been filed before the deadline), the export zero-rating is disallowed and any VAT refund already paid is clawed back. The taxpayer may also be subject to a daily late-payment surcharge on underpaid tax, an administrative fine of 50% to five times the tax or refund improperly obtained, suspension or revocation of export tax rebate qualification, and criminal liability under PRC tax criminal provisions for fraudulent export tax refunds or false issuance of special VAT invoices, with penalties ranging from fines and imprisonment to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a hybrid convertibility regime. The current account is broadly convertible for trade in goods and services, allowing a qualifying WFOE to receive export payments and settle invoices in RMB subject to transaction authenticity reviews by designated FX banks and SAFE's Trade in Goods Monitoring System. The capital account remains heavily controlled: a WFOE's paid-in registered capital arriving from foreign investors must be registered with SAFE through the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration system, and each inbound remittance must carry a clear purpose code and be credited to a capital settlement account after completing FDI registration. Conversion of that capital into RMB is permitted only for genuine operating purposes such as procurement, salaries, rent, and VAT payment; prohibited activities include domestic equity investment, securities investments in RMB, and real estate purchases beyond the company's actual premises. Dividend and profit remittance abroad is lawful for after-tax profits but requires annual audit financial statements, corporate income tax settlement documents, withholding tax payment certificates for non-resident shareholders, and bank verification that funds originated from registered capital or accumulated operating profits and not from domestic borrowing. Surplus conversion proceeds are monitored and must be used within the approved business scope. Therefore, fund repatriation, intercompany loans, and capital injections require multi-party documentary evidence and cannot be executed via unlicensed digital cross-border platforms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from wooden puzzle goods must be repatriated to China within 180 days of the date of Customs export declaration; the statutory repatriation ratio is 100% of the invoice value net of bank charges. Once credited to the domestic current account foreign exchange settlement account, the WFOE may retain the foreign currency or sell it to the bank for RMB. No mandatory conversion ratio applies under the current post-2012 framework, but SAFE and the designated bank require full supporting documentation under the goods trade verification system within 30 days of receipt, including customs declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and marketplace payout statements if sold through cross-border e-commerce.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability for primary treasury use. China's foreign exchange law does not recognize offshore EMI or neobank e-money licenses as valid FX settlement institutions; only banks designated by SAFE and licensed domestic non-bank payment institutions may handle cross-border trade receipts. Offshore fintech EMIs cannot provide domestic RMB clearing, SAFE FDI registration, or capital account verification. They may support collection payouts from Amazon and Stripe into an intermediate USD account, but final net proceeds must be routed to a domestic designated FX bank via SWIFT MT103 with the correct SAFE FX purpose code. Thus, an offshore EMI is acceptable only as a conduit collection tool, not as a corporate bank account or liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent must transfer USD from its home bank via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated capital settlement account at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial transactions, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is compulsory where the enterprise's total annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion. In all cases, a Related Party Transaction Reporting Form must be filed with the annual corporate income tax return.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Article 47 of the EITL and Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations. A WFOE engaged in laser cutting and assembly of wooden puzzles must demonstrate substantial manufacturing operations in China, including customs records, payroll, production equipment, inventory, and warehouse premises, to support the performance of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) and to resist treaty-shopping and GAAR recharacterization. The absence of real commercial substance may lead to denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of related-party transactions, deemed dividends, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fictitious Export and False VAT Export Rebates

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the TCAL imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; Article 204 of the Criminal Law (Export Rebate Fraud) prescribes imprisonment from 5 years up to life for serious fraud, plus confiscation of property. The STA will also revoke the export tax rebate qualification and require refund of all fraudulently received rebates with daily surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false declarations may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of fraudulent rebates and taxes. Travel bans may be imposed through the tax blacklisting system, suspending passport issuance and exit for non-resident directors. Criminal conviction can lead to 5 years to life imprisonment for the legal representative and finance director under the Criminal Law, with disqualification from management positions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Non-Disclosure of Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under EITL Articles 41-48 and STA Public Notice 42 (2016), the tax authority may adjust income, disallow inflated deductions, and assess additional interest on the transfer pricing adjustment (benchmark lending rate plus up to 5 percentage points). Failure to file related-party returns attracts statutory fines under TCAL Article 62; deliberate adjustment procedures include a 10-year lookback and a gross-margin audit of the whole profitable chain.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally ordered to cover the corporate underpaid tax if assets were commingled or funds were extracted without lawful distribution, piercing the corporate veil under Article 20 of the Chinese Company Law. Directorial criminal liability arises where the conduct is part of a scheme to evade tax, leading to criminal prosecution for the planning accountant and CFO. The foreign parent may be blacklisted as a serious tax-violation enterprise, and non-resident directors may be prohibited from re-entering China.
Shell Company with No Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may apply the substance-over-form principle, recharacterize the WFOE as having a permanent establishment of the overseas parent, impute profits to the Chinese operation, deny the DTA dividend reduction under the Principal Purpose Test (BEPS Action 6), and assess additional tax, interest and penalties of up to 500% of the unpaid tax. The WFOE may also face administrative deregistration and cancellation of the foreign shareholder's treaty rights.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate the shell structure risk personal civil liability for all underpaid taxes and customs duties under the Company Law's veil-piercing provisions; the STA and Customs jointly issue blacklisting decisions that freeze bank accounts, impose travel bans, and invalidate export approvals. In criminal cases, the courts may impose up to 7 years of imprisonment for tax-related fraud where economic losses are substantial, and the directors will be jointly liable to compensate the State for the full amount of taxes evaded.
