Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every enterprise with VAT taxable activities must complete VAT registration; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Below this threshold, the entity remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer, with conversion to general taxpayer status compulsory upon crossing the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

General-taxpayer sales of goods that are declared to Chinese customs, physically leave the customs territory, and are sold to an overseas buyer qualify for the 0% VAT export rate under the tax exemption, offset and refund regime, with input VAT refundable. To claim zero-rating, the exporter must be the actual seller, hold a valid export customs declaration, issue an export invoice, receive the export proceeds in foreign currency, and submit the VAT rebate/exemption declaration within the statutory filing window. Prohibited, restricted, or fictitiously exported goods do not qualify.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration filed with the General Administration of Customs through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or other international transport document (sea waybill, air waybill or international rail waybill) proving actual cross-border shipment
  • Bank Export Proceeds Receipt/Collection Notification issued by a designated foreign-exchange bank and reported through SAFE's Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration System (the FIRC-equivalent for China)
  • VAT export invoice issued under the electronic VAT invoice system
  • Export contract or sales order with the overseas buyer
  • Export tax rebate/exemption declaration data submitted to the STA Electronic Taxpayer Portal

VAT invoices are controlled under the Golden Tax System Phase IV and must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Fully digitalized electronic invoices carry a unique nationwide invoice number and are transmitted in real time to the tax authority for fiscal clearance; conventional tax-controlled invoices are generated with approved tax control equipment whose data is uploaded automatically. Export sales must be electronically reconciled with customs declarations and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts through the integrated e-tax/customs data platform.

Tax authorities automatically compare export VAT claims with Chinese customs export data and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the refunded or exempted VAT is clawed back with 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and administrative penalties from 50% to 500% of the tax amount may be imposed. Repeat or intentional fraud can trigger enhanced customs inspection, downgrading of the exporter's foreign-exchange credibility classification, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under Chinese criminal law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial capital account convertibility. The RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods, transportation, insurance, and tourism, provided that genuine underlying documentation is submitted through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, however, remain subject to case-by-case SAFE approval, registration, or filing. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the die-cast scale automotive model manufacturing sector, inbound foreign direct investment must be registered under the SAFE FDI system before the capital injection is remitted. The WFOE may open a foreign currency capital account, receive the offshore equity contribution, and then convert the funds into RMB for factory construction, equipment procurement, payroll, and operating expenses. Profit repatriation to the overseas parent is permissible but requires audited annual financial statements, Chinese tax payment certificates, a board resolution approving the dividend distribution, completion of the annual foreign investment information report, and settlement through a domestic bank under a capital account purpose code. There is no general prohibition on paying royalties or management fees to overseas affiliates, but transfer pricing and treaty benefit documentation may be requested. In practice, current account payments are facilitated quickly, while capital account movements such as equity outbound investment, external guarantees, and cross-border financing are more heavily policed. Currency convertibility risk therefore centres on documentary compliance, not administrative availability of FX. The WFOE must maintain robust trade records, customs declarations, tax filings, and SAFE reporting to avoid freezing, penalties, or retroactive classification of receipts as illegal cross-border funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account in the name of the WFOE and must be logged in the SAFE export collection and verification system. The statutory benchmark is that export goods delivered under customs declaration must have corresponding foreign exchange receipts verified within 180 days from the date of export declaration, unless an extension is granted by SAFE for documented legitimate commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the old forced surrender regime has been abolished, so the effective ratio is 0% mandatory surrender and 100% optional conversion or retention in foreign currency. The WFOE may sell all eligible export proceeds to a domestic designated foreign exchange bank at the prevailing market rate, retain the foreign currency in a current-account settlement account up to its announced aggregate limit, or use those funds directly for approved current account outgoings. The bank will require the export declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and any customs-verification matching report before admitting the receipt into the settlement account.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange control and enforcement authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Offshore digital banks and FinTech EMI providers are not legally viable as the primary treasury or clearing infrastructure for a mainland China WFOE. Such entities generally lack a licensed banking presence in China and are not permitted to maintain domestic CNY clearing accounts, issue RMB settlement instructions to CIPS, or provide SAFE-compliant foreign exchange conversion services. A WFOE must use a branch or subsidiary of a locally licensed bank or a foreign bank with a locally licensed China branch for onshore treasury operations. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for cross-border marketplace receipts, such as PayPal settlement from overseas collectors, but those funds must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign currency account through compliant export verification channels. Relying on an offshore EMI as the main account is operationally dangerous and may be treated as an unauthorised cross-border payment arrangement. The overall feasibility rating for FinTech EMI dependency is Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent must first complete SAFE FDI registration using the China Comprehensive Online Service Platform for Foreign Investment, obtaining the unique FDI registration code. After execution of the equity subscription agreement and receipt of the SAFE-registered approval number, the parent should instruct an SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire to the WFOE's designated foreign exchange capital account at a licensed domestic bank. The remittance instructions must cite the SAFE transaction purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment capital injection, together with the parent company name and the WFOE's registration number. Upon receipt, the bank will perform a capital account verification, confirm the authenticity and completeness of the underlying documents, and allow the funds to be credited or converted to RMB. A Chinese CPA must then issue a capital verification report confirming the injected funds have been fully paid into the WFOE's bank account in accordance with the articles of association. The converted RMB capital may be used only for approved operational expenditures within the WFOE's registered business scope and may not be used for unapproved equity investment, lending, or securities transactions unless separately authorised under prevailing SAFE pilot policies.For trade payments, the WFOE should use standard international clearing rails through its domestic bank: SWIFT MT103 for direct commercial remittances and small-value open-account transactions; SWIFT MT700 with an accompanying commercial letter of credit to give the overseas supplier payment certainty against presentation of compliant shipping documents; and documentary collections through bank remittance channels when open-account settlement is not acceptable but a full letter of credit is too costly. For RMB-denominated cross-border settlements, the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) offers direct CNY clearing with shorter settlement chains and SAFE-compatible reporting. Each outbound payment must be supported by a valid import payment purpose code, a purchase contract, commercial invoice, transport document, customs declaration where applicable, and proof of title such as bill of lading or airway bill. The WFOE must also maintain an electronic trade single-window trail to demonstrate that the outward flow is genuinely trade-related and that the collection cycle is consistent with the physical movement of die-cast die tooling, raw materials, packaging, and finished goods.Onshore outright CNY forward contracts with SAFE-designated domestic banks, hedging genuine current account or capital account exposures with supporting trade documentation.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for rolling short-term liquidity and long-term capital flow hedging, subject to the WFOE having real underlying exposure and obtaining the bank's annual hedging authority approval.European-style FX options and simple option combinations for corporate treasury risk management, permitted under SAFE rules when the bank has confirmed the suitability and the premium is clearly documented.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible for the mainland WFOE itself as they fall outside the domestic hedging regime; any NDF strategy must be implemented through an offshore affiliate and must not interfere with the onshore account settlement trail.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital banks and FinTech EMI providers are not legally viable as the primary treasury or clearing infrastructure for a mainland China WFOE. A WFOE must use a branch or subsidiary of a locally licensed bank or a foreign bank with a locally licensed China branch for onshore treasury operations. Offshore EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection tools for cross-border marketplace receipts, such as PayPal settlement from overseas collectors, but those funds must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign currency account through compliant export verification channels. Relying on an offshore EMI as the main account is operationally dangerous and may be treated as an unauthorised cross-border payment arrangement. The overall feasibility rating for FinTech EMI dependency is Low.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under STA Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Local File must be prepared if any category of related-party transaction exceeds the threshold: tangible property transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million in the fiscal year. A Master File is required for large groups that maintain or should maintain a group master file and whose annual related-party transaction volume exceeds RMB 1 billion; annual related-party transaction reporting starts at lower de minimis thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China's anti-avoidance regime adopts substance-over-form under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and the Special Tax Adjustment rules require an entity performing Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China to actually carry out manufacturing, sales, and strategic risk management in China. The WFOE must have a physical die-casting and tampo printing facility, qualified employees, and decision-making capacity. A WFOE with no substantive manufacturing activity and only contractual or IP holding functions will be disregarded as a conduit for treaty benefits, and Chinese-source profits will be recaptured through deemed dividends or royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing evasion and artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 48 authorize the STA to adjust taxable income on the arm's length principle, with a 10-year lookback for special tax adjustments. The adjustment is subject to 25% enterprise income tax plus daily interest at the prescribed rate and may attract the TCAL Article 63 penalty of 50-500% of underpaid tax where false books were prepared.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause false invoices, false service contracts, or deliberately mispriced related-party agreements are exposed to personal tax liability under TCAL Article 64 where the company's assets are insufficient, to civil restitution through veil piercing under Company Law Article 23, to exit/travel bans and blacklisting under the joint punishment mechanism for serious tax dishonesty, and to criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion.
Failure to withhold withholding tax on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37 and 38 make the WFOE the statutory withholding agent. TCAL Article 69 provides that a withholding agent that fails to withhold shall be fined 50-300% of the amount that should have been withheld, and must pay the overdue tax with 0.05% daily surcharge if the tax cannot be recovered from the non-resident taxpayer.

Director personal liability: A director who intentionally orders the distribution without withholding and then transfers assets abroad may be personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties; the tax authority may invoke Company Law Article 23 to disregard the corporate entity and hold the director personally liable. The director may also face a travel ban under TCAL Article 44, blacklisting for serious tax violations, and criminal liability for fraudulent remittance or tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201.
Conduit shell company and abusive DTA benefit claim

Statutory sanction: The STA will deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership and principal-purpose tests in SAT Public Announcement 2015 No. 60, impose the statutory 10% WHT on all outbound flows, and apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 with Article 48 special tax adjustment, 10-year lookback, and interest. False declarations in the treaty-benefit filing expose the entity to fines for fraudulent tax treatment and potential criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors of the conduit WFOE who knowingly make false beneficial-ownership statements or arrange sham contracts may have the corporate veil pierced for civil restitution, face blacklisting as de facto controllers of a serious tax-violation enterprise, be prohibited from leaving China while tax liabilities are outstanding, and be criminally prosecuted for tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 201 if the avoided tax exceeds the criminal threshold.
