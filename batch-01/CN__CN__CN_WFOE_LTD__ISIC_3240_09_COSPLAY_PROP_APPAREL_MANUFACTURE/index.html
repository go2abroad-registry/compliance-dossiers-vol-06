Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the general foreign investment framework; the PRC Company Law (2023) does not impose a statutory minimum amount of paid-up capital for an ordinary WFOE manufacturing apparel. However, the shareholders must pay the subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of establishment, unless the Articles of Association provide for a shorter period. The capital contribution plan must be recorded in the Articles of Association, and capital may not be withdrawn prior to lawful reduction or dissolution. No blocked escrow account is required because this WFOE scope is under the permitted foreign investment category and not subject to sector-specific minimum capital thresholds.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety design review and completion acceptance or filing with the local Fire and Rescue Section; fire extinguishing system annual inspection; structural load inspection certificate for production mezzanines, racks and equipment anchors; production safety precondition approval; occupational hazard monitoring for noise, VOC and dust; and employee emergency-rescue drill records. No production may commence before the fire inspection and EIA acceptance are issued.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic access via the national Electronic Tax Bureau portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must first obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation during incorporation. Within 30 calendar days of receiving the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent local STA office for its registered address. The registration submission includes the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, registered office proof, and shareholder documentation. The STA issues the tax identification number as part of the USCC and enrols the WFOE for Corporate Income Tax, Value Added Tax, withholding obligations, and export tax rebate eligibility. The WFOE must then activate digital invoicing and the export-tax-rebate module on the e-Tax portal.

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All business entities must register for tax upon incorporation with no turnover exemption. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification applies where annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status may apply unless the entity elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate when the WFOE holds a valid export customs registration, the goods physically exit China through customs supervision, a verified export customs declaration is made, the export proceeds are repatriated into China in foreign currency, and the STA export tax rebate declaration is submitted within the statutory period. Qualifying offshore digital services require a cross-border taxable services contract, evidence that the service is consumed outside China, and proof of inbound foreign currency receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange collection record
  • Commercial invoice
  • Packing list
  • Export sales contract
  • Export tax rebate declaration and STA electronic clearance record

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA controlled e-invoice system. The fully digitalized electronic invoice platform requires real-time submission of invoice metadata, unique national invoice numbers, digital tax-control signatures, and anti-fraud digital stamps. Export zero-rating transactions must be matched electronically through the Export Tax Rebate module of the e-Tax portal. API integration is available for enterprise systems; legacy tax-control equipment is being phased out.

Export zero-rating claims are audited against customs, logistics, invoice, and foreign exchange data. Unverified or fraudulent claims are clawed back with late-payment interest and a penalty of 50% to five times the refunded or evaded tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious false export refund or VAT invoice fraud is prosecuted under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with imprisonment, criminal fines, and forfeiture of illegal gains.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a restricted convertibility framework. Current account transactions are generally convertible under IMF Article VIII, subject to 'real transaction' verification by SAFE-designated banks. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled and require item-by-item approval, registration, or filing. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the registered capital and shareholder loan investment are categorized as foreign direct investment under the capital account. Injections are permitted after inbound FDI registration with SAFE or a delegated bank; remittances must pass through a foreign exchange capital account and be used only for the WFOE's legitimate business scope. Conversion to RMB is permitted under the 'capital settlement' regime but the resulting RMB funds are subject to a negative list: they cannot be used for fixed-asset investment outside the business scope, on-lending to non-financial enterprises, or equity investment unless specifically allowed. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are treated as current account dividends, but require audited annual financial statements, tax completion certificates, and a board resolution declaring dividends. Repatriation of surplus registered capital upon liquidation is likewise capital account and requires SAFE approval. Export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory surrender, but must be repatriated into China within statutory deadlines and may be retained in foreign exchange accounts or settled into RMB. In practice, banks act as first-line compliance filters, applying know-your-client and transaction-authenticity checks. Offshore private equity, fintech EMI rails, or back-to-back lending for capital injection are generally impermissible for inbound capital formation; the route must be shareholder equity or shareholder loan documented through official SAFE channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may choose to settle into RMB or retain foreign currency in approved accounts. Export proceeds in foreign exchange must be repatriated to China within 180 days of the customs export declaration date (or within the period prescribed by the relevant foreign exchange registration, unless a prior SAFE extension is granted). The full amount of goods and service export receipts is subject to repatriation and authenticity verification via the digital cross-border monitoring system and customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the monetary authority, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange regulatory and enforcement agency for current and capital account transactions.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

In-depth feasibility rating and legal constraints: Non-bank fintech EMIs (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currenxie) have no licensed branch or payment-institution qualification to open domestic RMB corporate settlement accounts for a WFOE under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Payment Services. Cross-border digital EMIs may process outbound disbursements from an offshore entity, but they are legally barred from being the primary clearing rail for a Mainland WFOE's inbound registered capital or regulated capital account transactions. These platforms cannot satisfy SAFE's authenticity-based verification, capital account monitoring, or customs reporting integration. They are viable only as secondary ancillary rails for low-value export trade fees or third-party marketplace payouts (e.g., Shopify/Stripe) if routed through an official onshore bank settlement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party transactions of tangible assets exceed CNY 200 million, intangible property, financial asset, or services transactions exceed CNY 100 million each, or other related-party transactions exceed CNY 40 million; Master File is mandatory when the annual related-party transactions of the group reach CNY 1 billion or the multinational group meets the consolidated revenue threshold of around CNY 5.5 billion requiring Country-by-Country Reporting.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime for cosplay manufacturing, but substantive economic activity is the legal foundation for treaty benefits, beneficial ownership, and transfer pricing outcomes. The State Taxation Administration may refuse treaty access where a Hong Kong or offshore intermediate entity has no real office, employees, decision-making, or risk-bearing and the only purpose is treaty shopping. For manufacturing WFOEs, Core Income-Generating Activities in China include procurement of raw materials, production planning, cutting and tailoring, wig assembly, quality control, inventory management, logistics coordination, and risk management of sales functions. Under the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, arrangements with no reasonable commercial purpose that create tax benefits will be recharacterised; a letterbox WFOE unable to demonstrate CIGA can lose reduced treaty rates and may face full taxation on deemed undisclosed profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Concealment of Offshore E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Administration and Tax Collection, the tax authority must recover the underpaid tax, add daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose a penalty of 50% up to five times the evaded amount. If the concealed tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable, Criminal Law Article 201 can be invoked, exposing the WFOE and its responsible officers to criminal fines and imprisonment; huge evasions can attract imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers who sign false returns, approve sham payment-flow arrangements, or mix company revenue with personal accounts can be subjected to civil liability and piercing of the corporate veil under PRC Company Law principles, including personal asset restitution to the extent corporate assets were misappropriated; tax blacklisting and exit travel bans can be imposed on responsible persons; involvement in the evasion scheme can result in criminal custody, with the court ordering the director to pay restitution, costs, and criminal fines.
Transfer Pricing / Base Erosion and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law empowers the tax authority to make special tax adjustments and re-determine taxable income by arm's length comparables under the approved methods; under the Implementation Regulations and SAT special tax adjustment measures, adjusted tax liabilities attract interest on the underpayment from original due date to settlement, and where documentation is deliberately false or records are destroyed, the adjustment can be treated as tax evasion under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law with 50% to 5x penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inflated service agreements, issue false intercompany invoices, or cause the WFOE to fail to withhold tax on cross-border royalties and interest may be held personally responsible as withholding-agreement signatories and controlling officers; civil restitution may be ordered if the inflated payments flowed to personal or family-controlled accounts, and because the acts constitute intentional tax evasion, directors can face criminal imprisonment, travel bans, and judicial blacklisting alongside the company.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership doctrine reflected in SAT Announcements 2009 No. 601 and 2018 No. 9, and under Principal Purpose Test provisions in treaties and the MLI; it will apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law to recharacterise the entire arrangement, impose full 25% corporate income tax on deemed taxable income, assess 10% statutory WHT without treaty reduction, and if false documentation was submitted, levy Article 63 tax evasion penalties and criminal liability where the understated tax is substantial.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors who establish and operate the sham structure can have the corporate veil pierced if the shell entity's assets are intermingled with shareholder funds or if the structure is found to be a mere instrumentality; personal assets become reachable for unpaid taxes and withholding deficiencies; they can be placed on the dishonest taxpayer blacklist and restricted from leaving China; deliberate submission of false residency certificates or fictitious substance records gives rise to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 and possible imprisonment of three to seven years for aggravated evasion.
