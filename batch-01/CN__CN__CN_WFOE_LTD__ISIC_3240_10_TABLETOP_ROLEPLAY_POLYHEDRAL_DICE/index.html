Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox without a physical presence is generally not permissible as the sole registered office for a WFOE in China. The Market Entity Registration Regulations and the Company Law require a fixed and legitimate business address that is actually owned or leased by the enterprise, verifiable by the local SAMR, tax authority, and banks. Municipal tax authorities and banks routinely reject virtual offices because the WFOE must maintain substantive business premises for VAT invoice verification, tax inspection, labor contract filing, customs registration, and financial audit. For the manufacture of handmade sharp-edge epoxy resin liquid core RPG polyhedral dice, a physical production and warehousing facility is indispensable; a virtual office is operationally untenable and would expose the company to cancellation of registration, tax abnormality designation, bank account restrictions, and legal liability. A mail-forwarding service may be used only as an auxiliary address, never as the sole registered address. The registered address must match the business license, tax registration, and bank account records, and any relocation must be filed before the municipal SAMR and tax authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the electronic tax filing portal known as the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with the China International Trade Single Window used for export clearance integration.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company in mainland China, the tax identification procedure is as follows: (1) Incorporate the company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code, which serves as the de facto Tax Identification Number for tax registration; (2) Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, complete tax registration at the local competent tax authority office of the STA by filing the taxpayer registration questionnaire, legal representative identity documents, articles of association, registered address proof, and bank account information; (3) Receive the official tax registration certificate and complete the taxpayer classification determination; (4) Apply for general taxpayer status or remain a small-scale taxpayer depending on projected annual turnover; (5) Activate the electronic tax filing account on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal and link the Unified Social Credit Code; (6) For VAT and export-related purposes, apply for the export tax refund and exemption registration through the electronic portal; and (7) Obtain any required digital certificates (U-key or e-tax seal) for encrypted electronic filing and digitalized electronic invoice issuance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tabletop roleplay polyhedral dice manufactured by the WFOE qualify for zero rating of VAT under Chapter 5 of the PRC VAT Implementing Regulations, provided the goods actually leave mainland China through an approved customs clearance point and the seller is a registered general VAT taxpayer that has completed export tax exemption and refund filing. The export sale must be properly declared in the export customs declaration, with all goods physically shipped abroad, and the sales proceeds must be received in foreign currency and repatriated to the enterprise's domestic bank account through the official cross-border foreign exchange settlement system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs clearance declaration submitted through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the exporting WFOE
  • Special VAT invoice for export (if applicable) or export-invoice documentation supporting zero-rated treatment
  • Original Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing shipment to the overseas consignee
  • Export proceeds receipt record and bank foreign exchange collection confirmation from the approved settlement bank
  • Export contract or sales order and any packing list or certificate of origin required by the importing jurisdiction

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (digital e-invoice) clearance mandate. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled e-invoice platform, with real-time encrypted reporting and automatic one-click upload to the tax authority's central database. Special VAT invoices must include the digital anti-fraud tax control code, and all voided or corrected invoices must be reported under the same system. Full digitalized electronic invoices are integrated with the e-Tax Bureau and require API-based or portal-based issuance with real-time validation before the invoice is legally recognized for input VAT crediting and customs export rebate purposes.

The State Taxation Administration has full audit authority to verify export zero-rating claims, using cross-referencing of customs export data, electronic invoicing data, and bank foreign exchange settlement records. If any export transaction is found to be fraudulent, unsubstantiated, or not accompanied by genuine foreign exchange repatriation, the tax authority will claw back the entire VAT input credit or export rebate previously granted, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day of the underpaid tax, and may impose an administrative fine of 50% to five times the evaded tax amount. In severe cases of deliberate false export declarations involving forged bills of lading or fabricated export contracts, the matter is referred to criminal authorities under the PRC Criminal Law for smuggling and tax fraud penalties, including imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a regulated hybrid convertibility system. Current-account transactions, including trade-related import/export payments, service payments, and post-tax profit remittances by a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), are substantially convertible provided that the underlying transaction has a genuine commercial basis, supporting documents are presented, and the domestic settlement bank reports the transaction to SAFE. Capital-account transactions remain tightly controlled; inbound equity subscriptions, foreign-currency loans, and liquidation proceeds require SAFE registration, pre-approval, or post-event filing, with banks refusing payments that lack a valid foreign-exchange purpose code and matching economic reality. For a WFOE manufacturing tabletop gaming dice, an important surrender-related concern is that export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into a licensed domestic bank account and either retained or sold for RMB within prescribed trade-monitoring windows; funds cannot remain indefinitely offshore. Profit remittance is treated on the current side, but the bank will require audited financial statements, the annual corporate income tax return, the China tax clearance certificate, a board resolution approving the dividend, and evidence that the RMB funds were lawfully converted before the outbound remittance. Overall, the RMB is not freely convertible in the capital sense, but operationally the WFOE can circulate cash between its CNY and foreign-currency accounts through licensed banks once every transaction is documented and reported.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE trade monitoring framework, China no longer imposes a mechanical fixed-percentage forced surrender of export proceeds. Export receipts from the sale of RPG dice and gaming accessories must be repatriated to a licensed domestic bank account, generally within 180 days from the applicable export declaration date unless the bank approves a longer verified commercial payment term. Once received, the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in its current-account foreign-exchange settlement account up to the bank-approved cap or may voluntarily sell it to the bank for RMB at the prevailing onshore rate. The account cap is established by the bank based on the enterprise's verified annual trading volume, and any retention beyond the cap must be converted or removed through a valid underlying transaction. All export proceeds, including funds collected through PayPal and Stripe platform aggregators before settlement into the corporate bank account, must be traceable to genuine sales orders, customs declarations, logistics documentation, and platform settlement reports.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neobanks and non-licensed fintech payment companies are legally barred from serving as the primary treasury account holder for a mainland Chinese WFOE because China requires all cross-border trade and capital-account settlements to flow through PBOC-licensed banks integrated with SAFE reporting. Foreign fintech EMIs cannot issue mainland account numbers, cannot receive RMB settlement, and cannot satisfy SAFE regulations on trade verification, anti-money-laundering, and capital controls. Third-party service providers such as Stripe, PayPal, and cross-border payment gateways may only be used as downstream collection channels for B2C consumer payments; their aggregated proceeds must then be remitted to the WFOE's licensed domestic corporate bank account, accompanied by transaction-level settlement and e-commerce documentation. No unlicensed digital institution can replace a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank for capital escrow, foreign-exchange settlement, or corporate liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be wired from the foreign shareholder's own overseas bank account to the WFOE's designated capital account at a licensed domestic bank using SWIFT MT103 or fast-payment cross-border messaging. The remittance instruction must cite a SAFE-recognized purpose code in the Direct Investment category for Inbound Foreign Direct Investment Equity Capital Injection. The WFOE must first complete the SAFE basic information registration under the Foreign Direct Investment system and obtain an FDI number, after which the bank deposits the funds into a foreign-currency capital escrow account. For every inward remittance, the corporate treasury must preserve the capital injection notice, the board resolution authorizing the funding amount, the foreign investor's certificate of incorporation, and the source-of-funds evidence. Upon receipt, the bank conducts currency convertibility by allowing the enterprise to sell the incoming foreign currency for RMB at the onshore rate or to retain it within the capital account subject to SAFE conditions. The WFOE then engages a registered Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in registered capital, and the same evidence is used to update SAFE and the market regulation authority. All RMB conversion from the capital account is separately reported under SAFE's capital settlement system and must be applied only to lawful operating expenditures within the WFOE's business scope.Outbound trade payments for imported raw materials, molds, packaging, licensing fees, and overseas operational services should be routed through the WFOE's current-account settlement bank using SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments up to approved trade limits. For higher-value or recurring procurement contracts, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued via SWIFT MT700 provides secure settlement by matching bank documents against sales contracts and shipping evidence. Documentary Collections remain available for fixed business partners where the WFOE and the overseas supplier agree on bank-mediated documents against payment or acceptance; the bank will require invoices, purchase orders, bills of lading/airway bills, customs declarations, and evidence that the foreign beneficiary is a genuine operating counterparty. When the WFOE repays overseas shareholder loans or distributes dividends, separate board resolutions, tax clearance certificates, audited financial statements, and form-based SAFE declarations are mandatory. All outbound payments must be made from the lawful foreign-currency or RMB funds in the corporate account; payments cannot be executed against unverified third-party instructions, and the bank applies economic-sanctions screening against UN, EU, US OFAC, and Chinese sanctions lists before each transfer.Onshore deliverable RMB/FX forward contracts concluded with the licensed Chinese commercial bank, with tenors generally from T+1 to 12 months, supported by verified current-account net exposure or approved capital-account cash-flow exposure, including rolling window forwards for recurring trade settlements.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for a mainland WFOE are generally impermissible as an onshore instrument; Chinese residents cannot access the offshore NDF market, so treasury hedging is limited to deliverable CNY forwards, with any NDF-type risk layering allowed only through the ultimate foreign parent operating outside mainland China.RMB/FX currency swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps are available through licensed domestic banks for matched principal and interest flows, but only where the swap is tied to an actual trade or investment liability, the maturity aligns with the underlying exposure, and the transaction carries no speculative or speculative arbitrage motive.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neobanks and non-licensed fintech payment companies are legally barred from serving as the primary treasury account holder for a mainland Chinese WFOE because China requires all cross-border trade and capital-account settlements to flow through PBOC-licensed banks integrated with SAFE reporting. Foreign fintech EMIs cannot issue mainland account numbers, cannot receive RMB settlement, and cannot satisfy SAFE regulations on trade verification, anti-money-laundering, and capital controls. Third-party service providers such as Stripe, PayPal, and cross-border payment gateways may only be used as downstream collection channels for B2C consumer payments; their aggregated proceeds must then be remitted to the WFOE's licensed domestic corporate bank account, accompanied by transaction-level settlement and e-commerce documentation. No unlicensed digital institution can replace a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank for capital escrow, foreign-exchange settlement, or corporate liquidity management.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File contemporaneous documentation is required when related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million in a single tax year, or when related-party transactions involving financial assets or intangible property exceed RMB 100 million in a single tax year. A Master File is required where the ultimate parent of the multinational group has annual consolidated revenue exceeding RMB 3 billion and the WFOE is participating in cross-border related-party transactions, as stipulated by SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a named Economic Substance Regulation in the style of offshore financial centres, but the PRC corporate income tax system mandates economic substance under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 45, and 47. A WFOE manufacturing polyhedral dice must show genuine factory premises, production equipment, operational staff, managerial decision-making, and core income-generating activities in China. If the WFOE is a mere letterbox entity invoicing through the group, the general anti-avoidance rule allows the tax authority to recharacterize transactions, reallocate profit to the Chinese manufacturing site, impute a dividend to the foreign shareholder, and impose withholding taxes plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 (transfer pricing adjustment), Article 44 (presumptive assessment), and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction, impose additional corporate income tax at 25%, charge a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Where the evasion is intentional and exceeds the criminal threshold, PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for fraudulent tax reporting.

Director personal liability: If the director or legal representative instructed the underpricing to a related foreign entity, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 (successor Article 23 of the 2023 Company Law), making the director personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and civil restitution. The director may be placed on the State Administration of Taxation blacklist, banned from leaving China, prohibited from high-consumption travel, and, upon criminal conviction, sentenced to up to 3 years imprisonment for ordinary tax evasion or 3 to 7 years where the amount of tax evaded is especially enormous.
Withholding Tax Evasion / Disguised Profit Distribution

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3, 37, 41, and 47; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69: a withholding agent that fails to withhold is subject to a penalty equal to 50% to 300% of the tax not withheld. The payments will be recharacterized as dividends sourced in China and taxed at 10% gross, in addition to the penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the fictitious licensing documents face personal civil liability for the withheld tax, interest, and penalties. Where the tribunal finds intentional evasion of withholding, the director may be subject to criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, asset freezing, exit bans, and disqualification from acting as a company director for a specified period.
Shell Company / GAAR and Falsified Export Mechanics

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 (GAAR), Article 44 (deemed assessment), Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 34, 63, and 66, and PRC Criminal Law Articles 204 and 205: false export refunds and fictitious VAT invoices are punishable by severe financial penalties, confiscation of illegal gains, forced refund, interest, and fines up to 5 times the tax involved; the criminal maximum for particularly large fraudulent VAT or export rebate cases may be life imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The court may disregard the corporate limited liability and pierce the veil under PRC Company Law Article 20/23 where the corporation is used as a vehicle for fraud. Directors are personally liable for the restitution of all refunded taxes and public funds; the Public Security Bureau may impose travel bans and blacklist the legal representative; the person may also be barred from leaving China under the exit-restriction notice system and may face criminal detention or imprisonment, in serious cases exceeding 10 years or life imprisonment, with personal assets subject to confiscation.
