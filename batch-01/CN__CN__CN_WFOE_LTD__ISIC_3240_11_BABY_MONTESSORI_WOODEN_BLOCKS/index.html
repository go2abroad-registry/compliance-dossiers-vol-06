Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. China abolished the minimum paid-in capital for general foreign-invested WFOEs. The sole shareholder must subscribe the registered capital within the term specified in the Articles of Association, not exceeding 10 years, and may register with zero paid-in capital subject to authenticity and transparency. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China. Electronic filing is performed through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) portal maintained by the STA and operated through provincial e-Tax Bureau sub-portals.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a Chinese resident limited liability company and must obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) through the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) at incorporation. Procedure: (i) file the articles of association, shareholder identity documentation, registered capital, business scope (ISIC 3240 toy manufacturing and export), and proposed directors with the competent AMR via the government service online portal; (ii) receive the Business License bearing the USCC, which is the sole statutory tax identification number for both enterprise income tax and VAT; (iii) rely on the automatic data synchronization between AMR and STA under the multi-license integration reform to complete the initial taxpayer registration; (iv) within 30 days of license issuance, sign into the Electronic Tax Bureau with real-name authentication to complete supplementary taxpayer registration—legal representative, accounting details, bank account, invoice-issuing personnel, expected turnover, and export-tax-refund (export VAT rebate) status; and (v) for foreign non-resident entities without a Chinese establishment, no separate USCC is issued; the Chinese counterparty or withholding agent registers the non-resident in the VAT/enterprise income tax withholding module and obtains a non-resident taxpayer identification number for treaty and withholding purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide US-style check-the-box elections, pass-through entity elections, or hybrid entity classifications. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed as an entity-level resident enterprise taxpayer; anti-hybrid mismatch rules and tax treaty provisions apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete tax registration regardless of turnover. The VAT general taxpayer classification threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales (measured over any consecutive 12-month period); if the threshold is exceeded, mandatory registration as a VAT general taxpayer applies. Below that threshold, the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, and small-scale taxpayers enjoy an exemption for monthly taxable sales up to RMB 100,000 (quarterly RMB 300,000).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered VAT general taxpayer may apply 0% VAT to tangible goods that are actually exported from China customs territory and sold to a non-resident buyer, provided the export customs declaration is completed, the goods physically leave China, the exporter holds valid VAT input invoices on the purchased goods, and the corresponding export proceeds are repatriated to China through approved banking channels within the statutory deadline. Qualifying cross-border services or offshore digital services are zero-rated only when rendered to non-resident enterprises, consumed wholly outside China, and evidenced by the prescribed service export documentary package.

  • Electronic Customs Export Goods Declaration (export manifest declaration number from the China International Trade Single Window);
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing goods release from China;
  • Commercial invoice and packing list on the WFOE letterhead;
  • Export VAT rebate declaration together with upstream VAT special invoices proving domestic input VAT;
  • Bank-issued export proceeds receipt / Inward Remittance Certificate (or equivalent Foreign-Currency Income Declaration and settlement water note) demonstrating foreign-exchange repatriation.

China mandates real-time issuance and reporting of electronic VAT invoices through the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. The WFOE must issue Full Digital Electronic Invoices (FDEI) with an anti-fraud digital tax-control stamp or, for affected legacy scenarios, tax-controlled special/ordinary VAT invoices through approved e-invoicing software. Invoice data must be transmitted to STA servers in real-time or near-real-time for fiscal clearance, verification, and matching; ERP or billing-system integration is performed through STA-certified APIs. Buyers and tax authorities validate invoices using the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform; any mismatch, duplicate, or abnormal invoice is automatically flagged for anti-fraud inspection.

Export zero-rating is subject to retrospective STA audit. If the STA finds a false, incomplete, or unsupported export declaration, missing foreign-exchange repatriation, counterfeit invoices, round-tripping, or fictitious cross-border transactions, it will claw back the claimed VAT rebate, impose a daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% of the unpaid tax, and levy a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export-tax-rebate claims also constitute a criminal offense under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment up to life plus confiscation of property. Additional administrative consequences include downgrade of the tax credit rating, suspension of export rebate eligibility, mandatory full-documentary on-site inspection, and inclusion on the joint disciplinary 'serious tax dishonesty' blacklist; the coercive collection period is unlimited in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed float regime under the People's Bank of China and SAFE with strict bifurcation. Current account transactions—export/import payments, fees, royalties, and arm's-length service payments—are in principle freely convertible, subject to transactional authenticity documents and anti-fraud review by designated banks. Capital account transactions, including the original injection of equity capital into a WFOE, the conversion of foreign exchange for registered capital, domestic reinvestment, and ultimate profit remittance to overseas shareholders, remain controlled. A WFOE must register its FDI capital with SAFE through a designated bank, maintain a dedicated capital escrow account, and expend funds within the approved negative-list business scope. Profit remittance requires annual audited financial statements, an underlying board resolution approving distribution, a Chinese mainland tax clearance demonstration (corporate income tax withholding certificate), and bank documentation proving funds originate from after-tax retained earnings. Although current-account payments enjoy no mandatory surrender ratio, the principle of 'real demand, not speculation' dominates, so a WFOE must present contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and customs manifests to convert currency in either direction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE's current framework sets no mandatory conversion percentage. A WFOE must repatriate export receipts generated by cross-border trade through a designated FX bank within 180 days after export declaration or the relevant collection due date; any delay requires a justification and extension filing. Once the USD, EUR, or other proceeds arrive in the WFOE's foreign exchange settlement account, the company may voluntarily convert 100% to CNY at market rates or retain 100% in foreign currency, subject to authenticity review and trade monitoring system reporting. Non-trade service export proceeds follow contract/service completion evidence and do not require customs declaration. Failure to timely repatriate or report may trigger SAFE penalties and removal of the batch-verification convenience.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China (SAFE), under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Fintech-as-a-bank providers have no bank license from the PBOC/CBIRC and cannot open virtual current accounts for mainland WFOEs, issue CN domiciliation codes, or perform CNY clearing. Amazon Pay and Stripe can only operate as offshore collection rails into which the WFOE's overseas sales are drawn, but the proceeds must ultimately be remitted to a SAFE-monitored account through licensed cross-border payment institutions or a commercial bank. A WFOE cannot rely on these EMIs as the financial institution of record for statutory tax clearance, capital verification, or export proceeds surrender. Viability: very low as a primary banking solution; permissible only as front-end e-commerce collection aggregators, and even then the aggregator must hold a PBOC cross-border payment clearance license or be serviced by a licensed Chinese bank.

Initial FDI capital should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the registered foreign investor's corporate bank account to the WFOE's dedicated capital escrow account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must include the correct foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection and reference the SAFE FDI registration/QR code. The bank validates authenticity, converts funds at market rate or retains foreign currency per the capital account cap, records the capital verification via a local CPA certificate, and updates the SAFE digital system. No payment can be made from the capital account for non-business purposes or before negative-list review. For multiple injections, the investor must remit within the registered capital schedule and obtain a renewed capital verification and SAFE update.Outbound payments for imported raw materials and operating expenses must clear through the WFOE's ordinary current account at a licensed bank. The preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 with mandatory supporting documents (import declaration, invoice, contract, transport documents). For significant trade flows, Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the buyer's bank can be advised and confirmed through the domestic bank; Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) may be used where a clean LC is not available. RMB-denominated counterparties in strategic jurisdictions should be routed through CIPS with SWIFT-compatible messaging. The same bank must perform authenticity reviews, report to SAFE, and maintain export/import monitoring.Onshore deliverable forward FX contracts with a licensed bank under SAFE's real-demand rule, limited to actual trade or capital-exposure amounts.Currency swaps for hedging of foreign-currency debt, equity capital conversion exposure, and cross-currency operational cash flows over a notional equal to the underlying commitment.FX options on CNY/foreign pairs for hedging, subject to the absence of net speculative positioning and documentary submission of underlying trade contracts.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not legally available to a mainland WFOE as a direct customer; a compliant alternative is to manage residual FX risk through the onshore forward market or structural pricing in export contracts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Fintech-as-a-bank providers have no bank license from the PBOC/CBIRC and cannot open virtual current accounts for mainland WFOEs, issue CN domiciliation codes, or perform CNY clearing. Amazon Pay and Stripe can only operate as offshore collection rails into which the WFOE's overseas sales are drawn, but the proceeds must ultimately be remitted to a SAFE-monitored account through licensed cross-border payment institutions or a commercial bank. A WFOE cannot rely on these EMIs as the financial institution of record for statutory tax clearance, capital verification, or export proceeds surrender. Viability: very low as a primary banking solution; permissible only as front-end e-commerce collection aggregators, and even then the aggregator must hold a PBOC cross-border payment clearance license or be serviced by a licensed Chinese bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory when a single-year related-party transaction exceeds: RMB 200 million for tangible property; RMB 100 million for intangible property; RMB 100 million for financial assets; RMB 40 million for services; Master File mandatory when the aggregate cross-border related-party transaction exceeds RMB 1 billion. In all cases the annual related-party transaction schedule must be submitted regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation regime like offshore financing centers, but the State Taxation Administration applies a substance-over-form doctrine, the Beneficial Ownership Standard, and the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and its Implementation Rules. For a WFOE manufacturing toy blocks, the substantive test requires actual personnel, factory premises, production equipment, inventory control, sales functions, and decision-making in China; a non-resident parent that merely invoices through a Hong Kong or tax-haven entity with no local staff violates the economic substance standard, and Chinese-source profits may be recharacterized, treaty relief denied, and a 10% withholding applied to the deemed dividends.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Enterprise Abusing DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Denial of the DTA benefit; imposition of the 10% domestic withholding rate on the entire accumulated dividend amount; interest under Article 32 and penalty of 50%-5 times of the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law; the GAAR (EIT Law Article 47) authorizes tax authorities to aggregate and recharacterize the arrangement.

Director personal liability: Personal civil liability for the unpaid withheld tax and penalties if the director knowingly implemented the sham structure; an exit ban (blacklisting) and asset freeze under the Tax Collection and Administration Law provisions for non-compliant legal representatives; imprisonment up to 7 years under the Criminal Law Article 201 if the tax evasion attributable to the scheme exceeds the criminal threshold.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Fictitious Inter company Pricing

Statutory sanction: Special Tax Investigation adjustment under income tax law with a 10% net-margin floor for reallocated profit; late-interest compounded daily; tax deficiency penalty from 50% to 5 times the evicted CIT; a 25% base-rate adjustment multiplier in serious cases; denial of double-taxation relief.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing statements may be personally liable to repay the underpaid tax plus interest; the responsible legal representative is subject to exit prohibition, personal asset preservation, and civil compensation claims by the Chinese tax collector; criminal liability is prosecuted under the Criminal Law Article 201 and Article 205 (fictitious invoices), carrying a maximum sentence of 7 years in prison.
Off-Book E-commerce Revenue and Payment-Rail Concealment

Statutory sanction: Civil tax fraud penalty of 50%-5 times the unpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law; the tax authority may also impose a late fee of 0.05% daily on the whole concealed period; where the concealed tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and comprises more than 10% of the payable tax, criminal tax evasion is a felony.

Director personal liability: Legal representatives and responsible finance officers are exposed to severe personal liability: exit bans and blacklisting, freezing of personal accounts, civil restitution for the tax losses, and criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201 for up to 7 years, together with fines and confiscation of property derived from concealment.
