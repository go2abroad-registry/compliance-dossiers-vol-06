Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under the PRC Company Law and the Foreign Investment Law, a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) may determine its registered capital in its Articles of Association, and there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for this silicone tactile fidget manufacturing enterprise. No blocked escrow account is required by the national company registry. The sole shareholder must subscribe the full registered capital according to the capital contribution schedule stated in the Articles, and the full subscription period may not exceed the five-year statutory maximum applicable to limited liability companies. The first instalment must be received before the company commences manufacturing operations, and a capital contribution ledger and bank payment records must be maintained for foreign exchange filing, tax reporting and annual credit information reporting.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); administered locally by the in-charge district or city Tax Bureau of the registered address, accessible through the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online portal

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Pre-incorporation: verify that the proposed WFOE activity is not on the Foreign Investment Negative List and is permitted for foreign investment; if permitted, proceed with name clearance and incorporation through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) online enterprise registration system. Step 2 - Incorporate the WFOE as a limited liability company with SAMR, submitting the articles of association, shareholder identification documents, registered address proof, lease agreement, appointed legal representative details, and capital contribution declarations. Upon approval, obtain the Business License which contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code. Step 3 - Post-incorporation formalities: complete the company seal filing, register the foreign investment and capital account details with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and open the RMB and foreign currency bank accounts. Step 4 - Tax registration: because SAMR and the tax authorities share registration data, the WFOE's 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code functions as its statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID). Within 30 days after the Business License issuance, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration information through the E-Tax portal, including enterprise type, accounting standards, bank account details, legal representative identification, registered operating address, and estimated taxable turnover. Step 5 - Taxpayer status election: apply for general VAT taxpayer registration through the E-Tax portal if the WFOE intends to claim input VAT credits and export VAT zero-rating; if no election is made and annual turnover is below the statutory threshold, the tax authority will classify the entity as a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not maintain a check-the-box entity classification election for WFOEs; a WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax. The available election is VAT taxpayer status: a taxpayer with annual taxable sales below CNY 5,000,000 may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer, while a taxpayer exceeding that threshold must register as a general VAT taxpayer. No hybrid mismatch election exists; anti-hybrid rules are applied under the Enterprise Income Tax Law to attribute mismatches to the Chinese payer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual aggregate taxable turnover (sales excluding VAT) exceeds CNY 5,000,000 within any consecutive 12-month period; below this threshold an exporter may elect general VAT taxpayer registration voluntarily, but crossing the threshold makes general taxpayer registration mandatory. Small-scale taxpayers are subject to simplified levy rates and cannot claim export refunds under the general export-rebate mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under China's VAT framework, goods actually exported from mainland China are eligible for a 0% VAT rate, with the input VAT incurred on domestic purchases being refunded under the export tax rebate (exemption) mechanism. The WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer, hold a valid customs registration, and demonstrate that the silicone stress-relief fidget toys were physically shipped out of Chinese customs territory to an overseas purchaser under an arms-length commercial sale. The exported goods must not be on the State Council restricted or prohibited export list, and the stated ISIC 3240 / NAICS 339930 toy product does not require a separate statutory export license. The WFOE must submit an electronic customs export declaration, procure transport documentation, invoice the foreign buyer in foreign exchange, and repatriate the export sales proceeds to China. For a production enterprise, the exemption-credit-refund mechanism applies: output VAT on exports is zero-rated, relevant input VAT credits are used to offset domestic output VAT, and any residual credit is refundable. The export VAT rebate declaration must be submitted through the STA E-Tax module within the statutory application period after the export customs date. For qualifying offshore digital services supplied to overseas recipients, zero-rating would require the service to be consumed entirely outside mainland China and to be supported by an export service declaration; this is not the primary scope for the physical goods WFOE.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (electronic customs clearance manifest accepted by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China and synchronised to the STA export tax rebate system)
  • Original Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / multimodal transport document) evidencing the physical export of the consignment from a mainland China port to the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)-equivalent bank receipt: the bank-issued foreign exchange collection or settlement notification demonstrating that the export sales proceeds have been repatriated to the WFOE's domestic bank account
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the export declaration value, description, quantity, and consignee
  • International sales contract or confirmed purchase order signed with the foreign buyer
  • Export VAT Rebate (Exemption) Declaration dossier submitted through the STA E-Tax export tax rebate module, including the electronic export declaration data and zero-rated invoice lines
  • Optional supporting transport, insurance, and logistics warehouse documents to confirm actual export movement and bona fide trade transaction

All VAT invoices must be issued through either the tax-control digital invoicing system (Golden Tax Phase IV) or the Full Digital Electronic Invoice platform operated by the State Taxation Administration. Every invoice is transmitted in real time to the STA centralised fiscal clearing system and must carry a unique electronic invoice number, the seller's taxpayer identification code, and an anti-counterfeiting tax-control digital stamp. Enterprises must activate electronic invoicing credentials, bind their taxpayer information and bank accounts, and use the STA-approved web portal or API interface before issuing any VAT special or ordinary invoice. Large-volume or batch invoice issuance is subject to real-time validation against the enterprise's declared turnover and prior tax filings. For export-oriented manufacturing operations, export sales and zero-rated supplies must be electronically coded in the invoice system and aligned with the customs export declaration data synchronised from the General Administration of Customs into the STA export tax rebate platform.

If the STA, the in-charge tax authority, or customs finds that an export declaration, invoice, or foreign exchange receipt is false, inflated, unsubstantiated, or missing, the claimed export zero-rating or refund will be reversed and treated as domestic taxable supply, requiring the WFOE to pay the applicable 13% VAT plus statutory late-payment surcharge. The tax authority may retrospectively claw back refunded amounts for prior periods, impose monetary penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China ranging from 50% to five times the tax amount evaded for fraudulent conduct, and transfer the case to public security if criminal tax fraud is suspected. Fraudulent export tax rebate obtained by false customs declarations, fake invoices, or fictitious overseas buyers may constitute an offence under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, carrying terms of imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of assets. Additionally, the WFOE may be downgraded in taxpayer credit rating, suspended from online VAT invoice issuance, barred from future export rebate processing, and subjected to joint enforcement measures with customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed FX system. Current account items enjoy de jure convertibility under IMF Article VIII, but in practice every RMB or foreign currency payment requires genuine trade, service, dividend or profit background documentation verified by the receiving bank. Capital account items remain strictly controlled. Inbound foreign direct investment is admitted only after completing SAFE FDI registration and opening a designated foreign exchange capital account in a licensed onshore commercial bank. The WFOE may convert registered paid-in capital into RMB for permitted operating expenditures, but capital account proceeds cannot be deployed into areas on the Foreign Investment Negative List and cannot be used for unregistered offshore lending. Dividend remittance is treated as a current account transaction but still requires the WFOE to produce audited financial statements, a board shareholder distribution resolution, and a tax certificate proving that mainland withholding taxes on the dividend have been cleared; in the absence of these evidentiary dossiers the designated bank will reject the outward remittance. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer a true fixed-percentage forced conversion; however, all export-related foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into the WFOE's onshore foreign exchange settlement account, and the exporter may elect to retain the foreign currency or convert it into RMB at market rate. The overall convertibility outcome is asymmetric: RMB is freely usable onshore with verified purpose, while outward capital and hedging flows require strong exposure evidence, tax compliance, and bank-level approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods exports must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic bank account in China within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration unless the WFOE has registered an extension with SAFE for trade payment terms longer than 90 days. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds is effectively 0%; the exporter may retain 100% of repatriated foreign currency in its export settlement account or convert to RMB voluntarily through the designated bank. Nonetheless, the WFOE must lodge the export verification record with the customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading and bank receipt confirmation, and any diversion of export proceeds into non-operating channels will constitute a capital control violation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated authority responsible for foreign exchange administration and entity-level FX controls.

Administrative blacklisting (tax dishonesty list) applies for evasions over RMB 100,000 or where a tax inspector has been obstructed; blacklisted entities are subject to travel bans on directors and restrictions on bank financing, government procurement, and exit permits.
Line 31-34 (实际应纳所得税额) – Actual Income Tax Payable Final tax liability after all credits, offset with provisional payments made during the year. Reconcile quarterly prepayments; any shortfall attracts interest at the benchmark RMB lending rate plus surcharges. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared if the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceeds RMB 40 million (for asset transfers, intangible property transfers, and other transactions) or the total consolidated related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 400 million. More specifically: (a) tangible asset transfers (purchase/sale with related parties) exceeding RMB 200 million; (b) financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (c) intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (d) other related-party transaction amounts exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File is mandatory when annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion (10 billion RMB), or when cost-sharing arrangements are executed. Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not have full Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like offshore jurisdictions, the concept of economic substance is embedded in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 3 rules. A WFOE must itself own or employ the rights, assets, risks, and personnel that generate income; foreign shareholders with no substance cannot claim DTA benefits on dividends if the WFOE is a mere conduit. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China include the principal manufacturing operations, procurement, inventory management, quality control, and the decision-making for the silicone fidget production. The tax authority may recharacterize the WFOE if it bears no economic substance or if its intercompany contracts allocate risks artificially. The WFOE must maintain local substance: actual offices, employees, managerial decisions, and accounting records. For intercompany services, a markup on costs must be applied; a mere pass-through without substance fails the benefit test under Circular 16 of 2013 and results in non-deductibility.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Intentional Under-Reporting of Offshore Sales

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law (PRC Presidential Order No. 49): The tax authority shall pursue the underpaid tax and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid amount, plus daily 0.05% late surcharge. If the evasion amount exceeds RMB 50,000 (for individuals) or RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of taxable amount, it is a criminal offense under Article 201 of the Criminal Law: up to 3 years’ imprisonment and/or a fine, increasing to 3–7 years if the amount is huge.

Director personal liability: Under the Company Law (2018 revision) and the Tax Administration Law, directors who intentionally instruct or approve under-reporting are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax liabilities. The tax authority may freeze the directors' personal bank accounts, seize assets, impose a personal fine equivalent to 50% of the underpaid taxes, and apply a travel ban (exit restriction) preventing the director from leaving China (Article 44 of the Tax Administration Law). If the case is transferred to criminal justice, the director may face personal criminal detention, criminal fines, and imprisonment up to 7 years. Civil liability extends to restitution of misappropriated assets if the evasion caused loss to the shareholders or creditors (piercing the corporate veil under Article 20 of the Company Law).
Transfer Pricing Pricing Misstatement and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law, failure to provide documentation demands a fine of RMB 10,000 to RMB 100,000, plus additional penalty of 5% of the adjustment amount if the adjustment exceeds 10% of reported income. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law applies if transfer pricing manipulation is considered deliberate tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the inaccurate intercompany pricing agreement is personally liable for the tax underpayment if he or she acted in bad faith. The tax authority may pierce the corporate veil to collect the adjusted tax from the director's assets. Directors face an exit ban (Article 44 of the Tax Administration Law) until the tax debt is paid, and may be barred from serving as director/manager in any Chinese company for up to 5 years. Criminal imprisonment of 3–7 years can be imposed for intentional misstatement. In civil cases, the parent company or creditors may sue the director for breach of fiduciary duty, requiring compensation for losses.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping – Abuse of DTA on Outbound Dividends

Statutory sanction: Under Article 48 of the Tax Collection Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law), the tax authority shall deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the dividend as taxable income, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid WHT, and levy daily 0.05% surcharge. Criminal sanction for evasion of the withholding obligation applies under Article 201 of the Criminal Law if the evaded WHT exceeds the criminal threshold (imprisonment up to 7 years).

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE who fail to withhold WHT while aware that the recipient is not the beneficial owner are personally liable for the withheld tax under Article 65 of the Tax Collection Law; they may be held jointly and severally with the company for the full WHT amount and penalties. The tax authority will impose a travel ban (exit restriction) on the responsible directors and may have their personal assets attached through asset preservation orders under Article 55 of the Tax Administration Law. If the WFOE is a shell company with no genuine business activity, courts may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20 of the Company Law, exposing the director to personal liability for all corporate debts, including unpaid taxes. Criminal consequences for intentional failure to withhold can reach imprisonment for 3 to 7 years, plus heavy fines.
