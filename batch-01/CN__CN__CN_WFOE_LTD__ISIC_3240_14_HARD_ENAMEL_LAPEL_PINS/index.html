Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital for a general Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise limited liability company under PRC law. The registered capital amount is set in the Articles of Association by the sole shareholder, and there is no mandatory blocked escrow account. Under the Company Law 2023, each shareholder must contribute the full subscribed amount within five years after the date of establishment (or sooner if stated in the Articles), and the capital contribution may be made in cash or non-cash assets, documented by a capital contribution verification report and deposited into the company's bank account once opened.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and administration through the national Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) portal operated by provincial STA branches, with invoice issuance and real-time validation through the State Taxation Administration Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number; no separate VAT certificate is issued if the USCC is registered. Within thirty (30) days after the business license date, the WFOE must complete initial tax registration with the competent local tax office of the State Taxation Administration (STA), including enterprise income tax, VAT, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, payroll tax type and individual income tax withholding status. The STA opens the taxpayer file and activates e-invoice privileges; the WFOE then completes customs registration under the same USCC for export declarations. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no fixed establishment in China, the competent STA office of the Chinese payer or the withholding agent creates a non-resident taxpayer file and issues a non-resident taxpayer identification number; the withholding agent is responsible for VAT and enterprise income tax (including deemed profit) withholding, filing and payment under the PRC tax laws. The foreign investor of the WFOE does not itself require a PRC TIN for the WFOE's ordinary business, but must provide its own foreign tax residency certificate for treaty benefit claims where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the PRC-incorporated WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no pass-through or partnership treatment is available for VAT or enterprise income tax purposes.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under PRC law. For VAT, a WFOE with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale taxpayer status (simplified VAT at 3%, temporarily reduced to 1% under current relief), or may elect general taxpayer status if it maintains proper accounting records; once elected general taxpayer, it generally remains subject to general-taxpayer rules for at least twelve (12) months. A general taxpayer election is required for export VAT zero-rating with input-VAT refund entitlement.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every registered WFOE must complete VAT registration at incorporation without any turnover threshold. The mandatory general-taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the enterprise may remain a small-scale taxpayer. The current small-scale taxpayer exemption threshold is monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 150,000 (or quarterly sales not exceeding RMB 450,000), subject to the policy in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of manufactured goods (including die-struck brass cloisonné enamel lapel pins) from the customs territory of the People's Republic of China to an overseas buyer qualifies for the zero VAT rate where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are actually cleared by Customs and leave China, the exports are sold to a non-resident party, and the export proceeds are eventually received through a licensed bank. General taxpayer exporters may also claim a VAT export refund (input-VAT rebate). Small-scale taxpayers may apply the export exemption but are not entitled to input-VAT refund. Qualifying offshore services supplied to overseas consumers may also be zero-rated if the services are consumed entirely outside China and payment is received in foreign exchange; however, the principal zero-rating claim for this WFOE is the physical export of goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) as accepted and released by the Customs of the People's Republic of China, including export customs clearance data showing exit from China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the WFOE to the foreign buyer
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, international courier consignment note, or post-office proof of export evidencing outbound shipment
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the non-resident buyer
  • Bank receipt of export proceeds (the bank-issued export collection confirmation or inward remittance certificate for export receipts; under the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, a physical FIRC is no longer mandatory but the bank receipt and monitoring-system record must evidence repatriation)
  • Electrical or paper record of the export VAT rebate declaration package filed with STA, including allocation of input VAT invoices for material purchases used in the exported goods
  • Certificate of origin or any customs preference certificate only if required by the destination customs authority or buyer; it is not a VAT zero-rating condition

China's VAT system requires all registered taxpayers to issue VAT invoices electronically. The State Taxation Administration has implemented fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEIs), issued through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform; the invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time and assimilated into the Golden Tax System Phase IV anti-fraud control architecture. Issuers must obtain invoice quotas and, for VAT special invoices, apply for special invoice authorization; large enterprises may use STA-published APIs and interfaces to integrate e-invoicing with ERPs. There is no private third-party fiscal stamping; the STA performs digital authorization, anti-counterfeit watermarking, and cross-matching of invoice issuance with VAT returns, customs declarations, and treasury collection data.

The STA and Customs jointly verify export zero-rating through the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the General Administration of Customs data ledger, cross-matched with SAFE foreign-exchange receipt records. If the WFOE cannot substantiate physical export, receipt of proceeds within the applicable repatriation period (normally 180 days from customs clearance unless an extension is approved under SAFE/STA rules), or truthful declaration, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating, charge VAT at the standard rate of 13% on the export turnover, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date, and order recovery (clawback) of any input-VAT refund already paid. Fraudulent or falsely documented export-rebate claims may attract an administrative penalty of one to five times the amount of the tax deficiency or refund fraudulently obtained, and if the facts constitute export tax rebate fraud, handling under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China may lead to imprisonment, fines, and disqualification from future export-tax-rebate procedures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an administered float with strict foreign exchange supervision. The RMB is convertible on current account transactions with genuine trade and service backgrounds, including export proceeds and import payments, while capital account transactions remain highly regulated. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) therefore enjoys substantial current account flexibility, but inbound equity capital, loans, profit repatriation, and foreign debt all require registration, documentary verification, and post-event reporting. Export proceeds earned by the WFOE must be repatriated into China through a licensed bank and either settled into RMB or retained in a designated foreign exchange account, subject to SAFE total-volume trade monitoring. The capital account for a WFOE is opened only after Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) filing and SAFE registration, and the bank must verify the authenticity and intended use of every capital settlement. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder requires a statutory audit, tax clearance, board resolution, and bank confirmation that the distributable profits are real and not funded by unresolved capital mis-matching. There is no general non-remittable conversion restriction for trade receipts, but any delayed collection, offshore retention, invoicing mismatch, or suspicious aggregation from marketplace payouts will trigger enhanced review. Sovereign capital controls therefore do not block legitimate pin manufacturing and export operations, but they demand disciplined documentary consistency across the customs declaration, bank receipt, settlement, and tax filing records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the 2012 SAFE reform of trade in goods administration, the mandatory surrender ratio and fixed conversion requirement were abolished. An A-class WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign exchange receipts in an approved FX account or settle 100% into RMB at its own discretion. Export proceeds must nevertheless be repatriated into China through a licensed bank promptly after receipt, and if collection occurs more than 180 days after the relevant customs export declaration date, the WFOE must file a deferred collection report in the SAFE goods-trade monitoring system. B- and C-class enterprises face stricter verification, enhanced documentary review, and possible restrictions on settlement until transaction authenticity is proven.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs, including aggregators such as Wise, Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe, and Etsy Direct Pay, are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to serve as the WFOE's domestic settlement bank, and they cannot legally open onshore RMB clearing accounts on the WFOE's behalf. Their permissible role is limited to upstream e-commerce payment aggregation and collection of card-settled sales outside mainland China. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's transfer pricing documentation triggers: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million; related-party intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; related-party financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; related-party services exceed RMB 50 million; or 'other' related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File must be prepared if total aggregate related-party revenue (domestic + cross-border) in the previous year exceeds RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report notification is required when the ultimate Chinese-held group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Art. 47) and the Special Tax Collection/GAAR measures require a real business purpose and economic substance. For treaty relief and preferential regimes, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a WFOE manufacturing enamel pins include physically developing the die-struck brass base, executing the cloisonné enamel application, curing/firing, polishing, quality control, final packing, and inventory/warehousing management. If those CIGA shift to a Hong Kong or tax-haven affiliate while the Chinese WFOE retains only paper commissions, the tax authority will recharacterize the structure and adjust taxable profits. Foreign related parties receiving royalties, shared services fees, or interest must show they perform actual decision-making and operational functions outside China to be considered beneficial owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Diversion

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Art. 41 and Art. 48 empower the tax authority to make a transfer-pricing adjustment and add 5 years of look-back interest; LATC Art. 63 imposes fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax and forfeiture of illicit profits; the adjustment plus surcharges can easily exceed the nominal understatement.

Director personal liability: Where a director knowingly approves the underpricing, the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law fiduciary principles for restitution of lost taxes. The tax authority may impose a travel ban (出境限制/blacklisting) under LATC Art. 44, freeze personal bank assets, and if the shortfall is criminally material, the director is exposed to Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion): up to 7 years imprisonment plus criminal fines for serious evasion; additionally Art. 211 applies to entities.
Abusive Treaty Claim / Shell Shareholder

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement [2018] No. 9 — beneficial owner test — denies the treaty benefits and re-taxes the dividend at the 10% statutory withholding rate; additionally EIT Law Art. 47 GAAR treats the HK structure and asserts full 25% CIT on the deemed dividend income if the entity is recharacterized; the competent tax bureau may assess late surcharge from the original distribution date and penalize the withholding agent 50% to 3 times the unpaid withheld tax under LATC Art. 69.

Director personal liability: The WFOE directors who cause the misrepresentation are personally liable for the withholding tax deficiency and penalty. They face blacklisting (travel restriction), civil asset writs, and criminal liability for producing false TRC or business-substance documents (Criminal Law Art. 280 for forgery, Art. 201 for aiding tax evasion) — imprisonment up to 7 years where repeated or where large foreign-exchange concealment is involved.
AML/Cross-Border Proceeds Concealment

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Art. 39: penalty of 5% to 30% of the illegal amount and mandatory re-settlement; the unreported revenue is added to taxable income and assessed as evasion under LATC Art. 63 (50% - 5 times of evaded tax); the Anti-Money Laundering Law requires an RMB 50,000/foreign-exchange equivalent threshold to trigger beneficial-owner disclosure and suspicious-transaction reporting.

Director personal liability: Directors who control the offshore payment route bear personal liability for the foreign-exchange clawback and tax shortfall. Under Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering) and Art. 225 (illegal business operation), they face asset freezing, blacklisting/travel bans, company disqualification, and imprisonment from 5 to 10 years in serious cases involving large amounts of undisclosed cross-border settlement funds on behalf of the WFOE.
