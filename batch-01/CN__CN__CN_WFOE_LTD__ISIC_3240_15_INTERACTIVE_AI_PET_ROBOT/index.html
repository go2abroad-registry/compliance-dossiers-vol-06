Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company in China. There is no blocked escrow account requirement. The registered capital is determined by the Articles of Association and the founding sole shareholder must complete the capital contribution within five years from date of establishment unless an exemption set by law or administrative regulation applies. Paid-in capital must be traced into the company's bank capital contribution account, and foreign-currency contributions must be converted in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange capital account rules. Contributions may be cash or in-kind, but in-kind contributions require licensed asset valuation. The registered capital must not be evacuated or reduced except through statutory procedures.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax platform (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) under the STA's Golden Tax Project (Jinshui) system.

E-government filing portal

Because the CN_WFOE_LTD is a wholly foreign-owned enterprise incorporated in Mainland China, it is a PRC domestic resident entity and must be registered for tax before conducting business. Step 1: Foreign investor documents (articles of incorporation, director/passport identities, bank reference) are notarized/apostilled and filed with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the National Enterprise Registration Service to obtain a business license. Step 2: SAMR's one-window establishment mechanism automatically assigns the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number under the STA's 'one enterprise, one code' rule. Step 3: The SAMR establishment record is electronically pushed to the local STA office; on the e-Tax portal, the WFOE completes the initial tax registration particulars (legal representative, accounting basis, fiscal year, VAT taxpayer type) and receives an electronic taxpayer registration certificate. Step 4: If the WFOE expects annual taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000, it files a general VAT taxpayer registration via e-Tax; otherwise it can register as a small-scale taxpayer or voluntarily elect general taxpayer status. The USCC is sufficient for invoicing, customs export declarations, and export tax refund applications; no separate non-resident TIN is issued to the WFOE.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification; PRC does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. A WFOE is mandatorily a resident enterprise subject to enterprise income tax on worldwide income. Indirect tax election available: voluntary VAT general taxpayer registration for enterprises below RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; no other statutory indirect tax elections apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary threshold for basic VAT registration; any Chinese enterprise with taxable sales must register from the day of incorporation. The mandatory economic turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer classification is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 in any 12-month period (manufacturing/sales of goods). Below this threshold the entity is a small-scale taxpayer taxed at 3% (with temporary reduced rates when enacted); voluntary general taxpayer registration is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods outside the customs territory of Mainland China is zero-rated (0% VAT) under the export tax rebate/exemption framework of the Ministry of Finance and STA when: (1) the WFOE is a Chinese VAT general taxpayer with foreign trade operating rights; (2) goods are actually exported and customs-cleared through a full Export Goods Customs Declaration Form; (3) title passes to an overseas buyer and the supply occurs outside China; (4) foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated and settled in accordance with SAFE rules; and (5) the export is supported by complete audit documentation. Qualifying cross-border services consumed wholly outside China can also be zero-rated under the cross-border taxable services list.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (customs-cleared export entry record)
  • Commercial invoice or export invoice expressly stating 0% VAT
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent international transport document showing physical export
  • Sales contract and overseas purchase order
  • Bank documentary proof of foreign exchange repatriation (e.g., inward remittance receipt, export proceeds settlement/verification record)
  • Customs export release/loading list printed from the E-Port (electronic port) system
  • Electronic export tax refund declaration filed in the STA e-Tax portal

The STA mandates issuance of fully digitalized electronic invoices (all-electronic invoices) for new taxpayers. Invoices are generated in the STA-approved platform through API or SFTP integration with the taxpayer's ERP/billing system; each invoice receives a nationwide unique invoice number, tax-control ciphertext, anti-counterfeit QR code, and is transmitted in near-real-time to the Golden Tax Phase IV (Jinshui IV) central database. For high-value or high-risk sales, pre-issuance risk clearance automatically compares TIN, commodity/service classification code, amount, and payment information before the invoice is made available to the buyer. Paper invoices, when still permitted for certain legacy taxpayers, require tax-control equipment and an anti-counterfeit stamp. Enterprises must retain original electronic invoice files and supporting transaction data for audit.

STA and Customs jointly scrutinize zero-rating positions. Failure to substantiate physical export or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day statutory deadline triggers retrospective clawback of any VAT rebate or zero-rated treatment, plus late payment surcharge (0.05% per day) and administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the tax underpaid under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. False or fabricated export documentation, sham export transactions, or fraudulent rebate claims constitute export tax fraud under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, leading to criminal fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment; repeated violations also disqualify the exporter from future export tax rebate and simplified customs clearance programs.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls but de facto full convertibility for current account transactions. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, ordinary trade-related FX receipts, payments for imports, service fees, royalties and dividends are processed through designated domestic banks under SAFE's records-based administration, without case-by-case approval. Capital account items, including initial FDI capital injection, foreign loans, equity conversion and capital reduction, require prior FDI registration with SAFE or a delegated bank, plus a domestic FX capital account for escrow of the paid-in capital. The central bank and SAFE do not impose a mandatory conversion surrender ratio on export proceeds; after verifying the goods customs declaration and invoice, the bank may convert export earnings to CNY or retain them in a foreign-currency account. Profit remittance is legally permitted after tax settlement and is handled via bank verification of audited financial statements, board resolution on dividend distribution, and proof of prior tax payments; such dividends are considered current account items and are not blocked subject to compliance screening. The practical constraints are documentation heavy rather than capital prohibitions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated into a designated domestic foreign-exchange account through a SAFE-registered bank and should be collected within 180 days from the customs export declaration date; no mandatory conversion ratio applies, and the WFOE may retain all export proceeds in FX or voluntarily convert to CNY within the current-account operating framework.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a full-service treasury solution. Foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking banks in China and cannot open or maintain a WFOE's primary corporate bank account, capital escrow account or FX settlement account. Cross-border fintech operators may only cooperate with licensed domestic banks or PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institutions to collect payments or provide e-wallet top-ups; they are legally barred from replacing the supervisory rails controlled by PBOC and SAFE. Their role should be limited to customer-side payment collection, not corporate treasury settlement.

The legal representative must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration with the designated bank or SAFE and open a domestic foreign-exchange capital escrow account. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: PRC Company Law applies a legal-form threshold rather than a size threshold: every WFOE limited liability company, irrespective of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount, must engage a PRC-licensed accounting firm and undergo an annual independent audit of its financial statements. There is no statutory exemption based on total assets, revenue, or employee count for a private WFOE manufacturing entity under ISIC 3240. The quantitative threshold is effectively zero: any positive or zero balance-sheet total, any turnover amount, and any headcount still require the audit because the obligation attaches to the corporate form. If the company were to become a listed or public-interest company, additional quantitative triggers would apply under securities regulatory rules, such as listed issuers with total assets above RMB 100 million or annual revenue above RMB 300 million being required to submit enhanced audited disclosure, but the WFOE's mandatory audit obligation is absolute from the first year of registration.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party transactions of RMB 40 million or more in tangible property (together) in a single fiscal year trigger mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; RMB 20 million for intangible property; RMB 10 million for financial assets; and RMB 10 million for related-party services (or when total other transactions meet the threshold). Contemporaneous documentation is submitted with the annual CIT return and requires benchmarking studies.

Economic substance scope: China does not adopt an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime on a national level, but substantive economic activity is required through the substance-over-form doctrine and the GAAR in CIT Law Article 47. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of a WFOE must be performed by its own employees, assets and premises in mainland China; a WFOE that merely contracts manufacturing to its own related party abroad without controlling the risk, using shell offices and missing payroll records, may be recharacterized and denied treaty benefits. The State Administration for Taxation can analogously apply the Principal Purpose Test and deny treaty benefits if the arrangement's principal purpose was treaty-shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the unpaid or underpaid tax and recovery of unpaid taxes and surcharges. Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, tax evasion with a tax underpayment exceeding RMB 50,000 and representing more than 10% of the tax payable is punishable by up to 3 years imprisonment; where the amount is massive and the proportion extremely high, imprisonment is from 3 to 7 years, plus fines.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, CFO and directors who knowingly signed false accounts or manipulated invoices can be pierced under the principle of abuse of corporate form; where the company's assets are insufficient, courts can order restitution from personal assets. The tax authority may also request an exit-entry control (travel ban) to blacklist non-compliant directors. Criminal liability attaches to natural persons who organised or participated in the fraud, not merely the entity.
Failure to Withhold Outbound Dividend WHT / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 37 of the CIT Law requires the payer to act as withholding agent; Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes on the withholding agent a penalty of 50% to 3 times the amount of tax that should have been withheld unless the tax has already been collected. The WFOE itself is assessed for the unreleased tax, with daily surcharges and potential blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally abandon withholding obligations are personally liable for civil damages suffered by the company or foreign shareholder under general tort law; in egregious cases, the veil is pierced and personal bank accounts are frozen. Tax criminal offences include evasion of withholding obligations, and the legal representative can be subjected to an exit ban and addition to the tax discipline black-list. Criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years may apply where the false treaty claim is treated as fraudulent tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Missing TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41-48 of the CIT Law and the Special Tax Adjustment Implementation Rules, the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction, make a primary adjustment of income, impose interest at the base loan rate plus 5 percentage points, and, where fraud is involved, impose a penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid tax. The absence of contemporaneous TP documentation removes any fraud 'reasonable cause' defence and accelerates the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or authorised the mispriced intercompany contracts face civil liability for the company's TP adjustment and additional tax, because their personal assets were used to move value abroad. Where intentional mispricing shields Chinese profits in a low-tax jurisdiction, the court can pierce the veil to recover the tax from the directors' personal accounts; the tax bureau can place them on the travel-ban list and procure criminal conviction under the tax evasion provisions for deliberate concealment of Chinese-source profits.
