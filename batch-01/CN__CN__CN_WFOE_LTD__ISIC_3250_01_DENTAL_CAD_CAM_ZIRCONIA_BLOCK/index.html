Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE under PRC law; however, the 2023 Companies Law requires each shareholder's subscribed capital to be paid in full within five years unless a shorter period is specified in the Articles. For this dental ceramic block manufacturing WFOE, the subscribed capital must be economically consistent with the facility lease, production equipment, cleanroom or controlled environment requirements, and initial working capital. No blocked escrow account requirement applies at the incorporation filing stage; contributed funds must be remitted through a SAFE-approved capital account and maintained in the company's bank account before production commences if the Articles or investors require capital verification.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety: the company must obtain fire protection design review, fire protection acceptance record and, where required, a Fire Safety Inspection Permit from the local Fire Rescue Brigade before staff occupation and production; the facility must maintain fire hydrants, extinguishers, automatic alarm or manual alarm systems, emergency exit signage and an evacuation drill log. Occupational safety: the company must register with the Emergency Management Bureau, complete occupational hazard pre-evaluation and control effect evaluation for dust exposure related to zirconia and ceramic powder, install local exhaust ventilation and dust extraction, provide respiratory and eye protection, calibrate monitoring instruments, prepare a workplace safety production system, and comply with the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China and the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) and its provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), the National Unified Electronic Tax Administration Portal operated by the STA.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE, as a Chinese resident enterprise with foreign investment, obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) upon incorporation. The USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number for all tax filings. Within 30 days of receiving the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA tax service bureau through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal. The procedure comprises: (1) submit the AMR business license, articles of association, registered office lease or ownership certificate, legal representative identity documentation, and shareholders' identification; (2) file the Basic Tax Information Registration Form and receive the tax registration record tied to the USCC; (3) elect VAT taxpayer status, either as a small-scale taxpayer or, if annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or upon voluntary application with adequate accounting capability, as a VAT general taxpayer; (4) register for the issuance of VAT invoices through the national e-invoice platform; and (5) open a designated foreign exchange receipt account and file export-related banking details with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) via the designated bank. Foreign non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in China may separately obtain a Non-Resident Tax Registration Code for withholding tax purposes, but this WFOE operates as a resident taxpayer using its USCC.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China imposes mandatory entity-level taxation on a WFOE under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 generally mandate registration as a VAT general taxpayer; newly established WFOEs may voluntarily register as general taxpayers from the date of tax registration, and the small-scale taxpayer threshold applies below that amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer exporting manufactured goods from Mainland China to overseas territory is eligible for the export zero-rating and VAT rebate regime. The dental zirconia blocks produced by the WFOE qualify as export goods under ISIC 3250 when: (1) the goods are actually shipped out of China under a valid customs export declaration; (2) a written export contract exists; (3) sales proceeds are repatriated into China through a designated foreign exchange bank within the statutory period; (4) an export VAT rebate declaration is submitted within the relevant tax filing cycle; and (5) all supporting customs, transportation, and payment documentation is retained and consistent with STA and customs electronic data.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (China Customs Export Bill of Entry)
  • Goods Release Notification / Customs Export Cargo List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, as applicable
  • Commercial Invoice issued for the export sale
  • Packing List
  • Export Contract
  • Export VAT Rebate Declaration, submitted electronically via the e-Tax portal to the STA
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Voucher from the designated bank (bank SWIFT credit advice or export collection confirmation)
  • Export Inspection or clearance certificates required for medical-grade dental materials, where applicable

VAT invoices are governed under the STA VAT invoice management system (Golden Tax Project Phase IV). All VAT special invoices and fully digital electronic invoices (FDEI) must be issued through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Platform with real-time transmission to the STA tax control system. Invoices require a unified tax identification code, digital signature encryption, anti-counterfeiting QR stamping, and are subject to system-to-system APIdata exchange through the e-Tax portal. Special VAT invoices cannot be issued for export zero-rated sales unless specifically permitted; export sales are documented through regular export invoices or electronic receipts in conformity with customs clearance data.

The STA and China Customs jointly cross-match the customs export declaration, VAT invoice data, electronic logistics records, and the foreign exchange collection voucher. Where the zero-rated export declaration is false, lacks physical export movement, or fails to verify repatriated proceeds, the refunded VAT is clawed back with interest and the enterprise is subject to administrative penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law and the Regulations on Export Tax Rebates. The penalty may range from 50% up to five times the amount of the fraudulently obtained tax rebate. Deliberate fraudulent export rebate claims also constitute criminal offense under the Criminal Law, subjecting responsible persons to imprisonment and the entity to fines, with no statutory limitation period where fraud or concealment is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is convertible on current account items, and a WFOE engaged in dental ceramic block manufacturing can freely process trade-related payments and receipts through designated banks if the underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations and tax documents are genuine. Capital account items are not freely convertible: inbound equity contributions, cross-border loans, and outward investments must be registered or approved by SAFE. The current legal framework imposes no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; exporters may either retain foreign exchange in a domestic current-account FX account or sell it to a designated bank at the market rate. Profit and dividend remittances to the non-resident shareholder are permitted through the bank after verification of audited financial statements, tax settlement certificates, and board resolutions, with withholding tax paid on dividends. However, any intra-group capital increase, shareholder loan, or foreign debt must be separately registered. The WFOE must also demonstrate that all capital account-funded expenditure has a real business background, as banks are obliged to reject suspicious or speculative flows. Cross-border hedging is limited to genuine exposure and cannot be booked offshore via NDF, making the operating environment workable for real manufacturing trade but not for speculative treasury management.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration framework, there is presently no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; all goods-trade foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China and either retained in a domestic foreign-exchange current account or converted into RMB through a licensed bank. Post-shipment export receipts are expected to be collected within 90 days of export, and advance receipts should be matched by exports and customs declaration within 30 days. Longer collection periods require trade credit registration with SAFE and additional bank underwriting documentation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Mainland China does not permit unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs to open onshore corporate accounts for a WFOE or to act as the bank of record for SAFE reporting, FX settlement, or capital account registration. Foreign EMI platforms may only be used as ancillary collection/payment tools outside China, and any RMB clearing must ultimately flow through a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE is therefore legally required to maintain its core CNY and FX treasury accounts with a domestic bank; a fintech EMI cannot fulfill the statutory KYC, sanctions screening, and SAFE regulatory reporting duties in China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first complete the SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration through the designated domestic bank, which opens an FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account for the WFOE. The injection wire must be sent by the registered investor only, using the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code for foreign direct investment equity contribution (typically code 622011 for a newly established WFOE) and a payment reference that exactly matches the investment agreement and founding registration. The bank will conduct receipt verification, apply the capital settlement into RMB if required, and monitor the use of settled funds against the company's actual operating expenses and the negative list. Where required by the investor's home jurisdiction or the bank's internal policy, a Chinese certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report after the credit entry is confirmed.For import of zirconia powders, milling tools and other production inputs, the WFOE may use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for routine high-frequency payments, commercial letters of credit for large or contract-critical equipment purchases, and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) where the counterparty and trade terms are well established. The bank will verify the import contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, customs import declaration and tax records before releasing each payment. Export transactions may be settled by inbound SWIFT/TT payments into the WFOE's goods-trade foreign exchange account, followed by optional RMB settlement. For renminbi-denominated trade, CIPS and SWIFT-CIPS bridging arrangements can be used through the licensed bank, but SWIFT remains the standard messaging rail for cross-border disbursements.Onshore RMB FX forwards with licensed Chinese banks for genuine trade- and cash-flow-backed current account exposureRMB FX swaps and cross-currency swaps between the WFOE and a licensed domestic bank for real working-capital and balance-sheet hedgingPlain-vanilla option structures and option combinations for hedging goods-trade receivables and payables with a clear underlying transactionOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore WFOE and cannot be booked through a domestic commercial bank; the offshore parent may only use NDFs in its own jurisdiction outside SAFE-regulated entity books

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Mainland China does not permit unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs to open onshore corporate accounts for a WFOE or to act as the bank of record for SAFE reporting, FX settlement, or capital account registration. Foreign EMI platforms may only be used as ancillary collection/payment tools outside China, and any RMB clearing must ultimately flow through a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE is therefore legally required to maintain its core CNY and FX treasury accounts with a domestic bank; a fintech EMI cannot fulfill the statutory KYC, sanctions screening, and SAFE regulatory reporting duties in China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million intangible/equity transfers; RMB 40 million other related-party transactions; and RMB 1 billion total related-party transactions for a Master File. The local file threshold for these amounts is assessed on the enterprise’s annual related-party dealings with all related parties.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a standalone ESR, but economic substance is central to the beneficial ownership doctrine under the DTA relief and to the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. For a manufacturing WFOE, it must maintain real premises, manufacturing equipment, staff and decision-making functions in China. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is applied when assessing beneficial ownership of royalties and dividends (Circular 2018 No. 9); the absence of substance may cause the tax authority to disregard the separate legal personality of the WFOE or the intermediary, recharacterise the income, levy 10% WHT and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law and Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, the tax authority shall recover the unpaid tax and late payment surcharge and impose a fine of 50% to five times the amount of the underpaid tax. Criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment of up to three years for ordinary evasion, and seven years where the amount is enormous and involves major fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate or instruct the evasion may be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax and fines; they may be subjected to asset freezing, travel bans, and criminal imprisonment for complicity in tax evasion.
Failure to withhold non-resident deposit

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law, the withholding agent is fined 50% to three times the amount of tax that should have been withheld but was not withheld. The tax authority may also collect the tax from the non-resident payee. In a serious criminal context, directors involved in concealment may be prosecuted under the Criminal Law for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors are held civilly liable for the uncollected tax and fines if they knowingly authorised the payment. Personal travel bans and impairment of personal credit under the ‘dishonest taxpayer’ blacklist mechanism may be imposed; in grave cases, criminal imprisonment up to seven years may arise under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil when the entity is abused.
Transfer pricing non-disclosure and violation of anti-avoidance rules

Statutory sanction: Under the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (Announcement No. 42 of 2016) and Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may adjust the taxable income, assess additional CIT at 25%, add late payment surcharge from the date the tax was due, and may also impose an interest surcharge up to 5 percentage points higher. Failure to furnish documentation may produce a RMB 2,000 fine plus additional penalties and reverse the burden of proof in an audit.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they knowingly designed or condoned the artificial pricing structure to evade tax. They may be required to personally reimburse the extra tax and penalties; under GAAR the corporate veil may be pierced, exposing directors’ personal assets. Blacklisting and travel bans are typical, and criminal liability for evasion can result in imprisonment when the authorities prove deliberate intent.
