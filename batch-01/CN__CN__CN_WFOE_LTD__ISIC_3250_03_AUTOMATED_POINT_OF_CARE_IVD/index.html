Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision) and the Foreign Investment Law, there is no universal statutory minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account rule for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in medical device manufacturing. The sole shareholder must subscribe to the full registered capital in the Articles of Association and must complete the contribution within the period stated in the Articles, with a statutory convention of no more than five years from incorporation for fully subscribed capital. Although no escrow account is required, the foreign investor must open a bank-designated foreign exchange capital contribution account with a licensed mainland commercial bank and convert funds into RMB only after the bank has completed the SAFE-related registration and the enterprise has filed the corresponding foreign investment information. For the provincial Medical Device Production License, the manufacturing authority will expect the enterprise to demonstrate adequate paid-in operational capital, premises, equipment, and quality management resources; a prudent minimum initial paid-in capital of RMB 1,000,000 or equivalent is strongly recommended for the planned automated point-of-care IVD manufacturing scope.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the electronic tax bureau portal 'Gong Shang Shui Wu Wang' (Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform, etax.chinatax.gov.cn). For foreign-invested enterprises, the local State Taxation Administration branch where the WFOE is registered handles issuance of the Tax Registration Certificate (now unified with the business license as the 'Five-in-One' integrated certificate).

E-government filing portal

As a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE, limited liability) incorporated in mainland China, the entity must complete the following steps before tax registration: (1) Obtain the Articles of Association and establish a registered address; (2) Apply to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) for the business license (营业执照) with a unified social credit code, which automatically embeds the tax registration number (Taxpayer Identification Number, TIN); (3) Complete the post-license tax information registration within 30 days at the competent STA branch by submitting the business license, legal representative identification (scan or passport copy), financial/accounting system rules, bank account information, and the foreign investor's corporate registration evidence; (4) Activate the electronic tax bureau account and obtain the tax-specific password and digital certificate (CA/USB-key) through the local STA service hall or online verification; (5) Register for Value-Added Tax (VAT) general taxpayer status if expected annual turnover exceeds the threshold, or opt for small-scale taxpayer status otherwise; (6) File additional registrations with the local customs authority for import/export registration, and obtain a customs registration number (Customs Information Registration) that links to the unified social credit code. The TIN for a WFOE is the 18-digit USCC; for a non-resident foreign entity without a PE in China, a separate non-resident TIN is issued by STA upon withholding registration, but this WFOE, as a domestic Chinese entity, holds a domestic TIN.

It is not a pass-through entity; no check-the-box fiction is available to render it fiscally transparent in China. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Chinese WFOE limited liability company is a resident enterprise (Chinese tax resident) subject to Chinese Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on its worldwide income, and is a VAT taxpayer for domestic and cross-border supplies. It is not a pass-through entity; no check-the-box fiction is available to render it fiscally transparent in China. Its branches are not separately taxable for CIT (consolidated head office filing) but each branch may separately register for VAT when applicable.

Available election forms

None on general tax residency classification. Available elections: (1) VAT general taxpayer versus small-scale taxpayer election, with general taxpayer mandatory once annual turnover exceeds RMB 5 million; (2) simplified CIT expensing policies and preferential rate elections (e.g., small low-profit enterprise concessions under Notice No. 21, 2022) if the WFOE qualifies; (3) an accounting/tax base election for fixed assets accelerated depreciation; (4) the 'foreign-invested enterprise reinvestment temporary non-collection' election for distribution of profits from a foreign investor into an encouraged sector; (5) for cross-border digital services or royalties, WFOE may elect the deemed profit method for paid remittance when acting as withholding agent, but this is not a hybrid mismatch election. China applies dual-consistency rules for hybrid mismatch arrangements under BEPS Action 2 but does not offer check-the-box entity classification; no explicit hybrid mismatch election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For manufacturers and goods suppliers: RMB 5,000,000 annual VAT taxable turnover (12 consecutive months) mandates registration as a VAT general taxpayer. Certain services/immaterial supplies have a RMB 5,000,000 threshold as well. Below this threshold, an entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer with a 3% tax rate (reduced to 1% during preferential windows) but must still obtain tax registration via the unified social credit code; VAT registration itself is automatic upon business issuance, and a separate voluntary general taxpayer election is allowed below the threshold if accounting records are adequate and turnover is RMB 500,000 or more (effective 2023). For export purposes, general taxpayer status is generally required to claim full input VAT credits and zero-rating refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE, as a manufacturer/exporter of IVD goods, may claim VAT zero-rating (0% VAT) for exported goods under Article 15 of the VAT Interim Regulations and the STA/Customs export tax-refund (rebate) regulations. Eligibility requires: (1) the goods physically leave China through a customs port under a formal customs export declaration; (2) export sales are made to an overseas buyer and consideration is received in foreign currency (or RMB converted foreign exchange); (3) the taxpayer holds VAT general taxpayer status; (4) the goods are not in the catalogue of commodities prohibited from export rebate; (5) proceeds are repatriated to China and matched to the corresponding export declaration; (6) the exporter handles the export customs declaration or engages a qualified forwarder under its own name; and (7) for certain medical devices, no separate export license contradiction exists. The zero-rating may be applied through the 'exempt and credit' method (出口免抵退) versus 'exempt and refund' method; medical devices are typically under the 'exempt, credit, refund' (免抵退) general goods policy with a tax refund rate (export rebate rate) set by the STA. Services do not qualify here except for the R&D or consulting fees that are ancillary; the principal supply is goods. To claim zero-rating, the e-invoice system must issue the export-invoice record, and VAT filing must include the export sales in the tax return and remit the export declaration data through the electronic tax bureau's export tax refund module.

China operates a mandatory nationwide electronic invoice (e-Fapiao) system through the State Taxation Administration. All registered VAT taxpayers must use the official tax-control software/Smart Taxation platform (Jin Shui IV and the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform) to issue special VAT invoices (增值税专用发票) and ordinary VAT invoices. The system uses real-time fiscal clearance: digitally issued invoices are serialized, encrypted, and transmitted in real-time to the STA database; auto stamping with the tax authority's electronic signature occurs upon issuance. Since 2023, the STA's 'full electronic invoices' (全电发票) are the default in pilot jurisdictions and progressively mandatory nationwide; they require API or web-service integration with the national e-invoice platform via the taxpayer's digital certificate (CA/USB-key), with no need for physical fiscal seal stamps. Anti-fraud controls include the special invoice anti-counterfeit verification system, automatic cross-matching of invoice data (fapiao) against input/output VAT returns, the 'one machine for one enterprise' ordinance, blacklist risk controls, and the 'golden tax IV' system which monitors abnormal VAT chain. There is no separate physical fiscal real-time clearance device for goods manufacturers; instead, the electronic tax bureau's cloud clearance is mandatory before a VAT special invoice is legally valid for deduction by a customer.

The STA and customs jointly verify export refund claims through the Golden Tax IV and digital risk management systems, matching customs declaration data, foreign exchange receipt data, invoice data, and transportation documents. Exporters must present the complete package on audit request (usually retained for at least five years). If information inconsistencies or documentation insufficiency appear, the STA may suspend the refund, request supplementary evidence, or conduct a site inspection. If a false export declaration, fake invoices, or fraudulent refund is found, the taxpayer faces: (1) denial of zero-rating and mandatory repayment (clawback) of any VAT refund received with interest; (2) a penalty of 50% to 5 times the fraudulent tax refund amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection; (3) customs penalties for false declarations under the Customs Law, including confiscation of illegal gains and a fine of up to 10 times; (4) a stricter unfair penalty under the Interim Regulation on Export Tax Refund (withholding of refunds for 1-3 years); (5) referral to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 204 if the fraud amount exceeds RMB 50,000, with sentences up to life imprisonment in severe cases; and (6) downgrade to a 'high-risk exporter' (Class D) with special audit review and stricter documentation requirements. Failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days (unless an extension is granted by SAFE for legitimate commercial reasons) generally disqualifies the export sale from zero-rating and subjects the export VAT to a deemed domestic sale, requiring output VAT at 13% with a corresponding input VAT recapture and a late-payment surcharge.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Regulations, the RMB is convertible on the current account for trade and service transactions once the underlying business is genuine and documentation is verified. Capital account transactions remain restricted; foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and repatriation of equity proceeds require filing or registration with SAFE or its delegated banks. The WFOE's inbound share capital must be booked into a foreign exchange capital settlement account and each conversion to RMB must satisfy an actual-use review, with proceeds held in a separate RMB settlement-supervision account to ensure traceability. Dividends can be remitted offshore after audited financial statements, tax clearance, and SAFE verification. Profit distribution requires evidence of accumulated retained earnings, corporate income tax payment certificates, and the absence of unresolved capital-repayment restrictions. This regime creates an administrative hurdle matrix: corporate treasury must pre-file every cross-border flow, submit contracts and invoices, and reconcile with customs data. For IVD manufacturing, recurring import of reagents and export of analyzers will be subject to trade-in-foreign-exchange verification, customs valuation, and transfer pricing review, making full current account convertibility conditional on documentary perfection from the first transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE foreign exchange reform, export proceeds are no longer subject to a mandatory conversion ratio into RMB; exporters may retain foreign currency in current-account settlement accounts or sell it to the bank. However, all goods-trade foreign exchange earnings must be repatriated to China and credited to a domestic account, and the collecting bank must verify the underlying customs export declaration through the SAFE trade monitoring system. For standard open-account terms, receipt of export proceeds is benchmarked at no later than 90 days after customs declaration; delays beyond that require a written explanation and SAFE registration before the bank can clear the foreign exchange. Advance receipts must also be declared and supported by a valid contract to prevent trade-based money laundering.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally unavailable for onshore entity treasury. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open Chinese enterprise bank accounts, execute RMB settlement, or access CIPS directly. The WFOE must use a Chinese licensed bank for all domestic and cross-border clearing. Offshore EMIs may only function as collection agents for overseas retail customers, but they cannot serve as the treasury bank for the Chinese entity without creating unlicensed payment business and significant AML risk. The recommended strategy is to avoid any EMI dependency and use SWIFT MT103 and CIPS rails through Tier-1 domestic banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when related-party tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, related-party intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, related-party financing exceeds RMB 100 million, or other related-party services exceed RMB 40 million in a single year; Master File preparation is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the GAAR in CIT Law Article 47 and the beneficial ownership tests in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 require substantive economic activity, real business purpose, and CIGA location in the jurisdiction claiming treaty benefits; a resident WFOE must maintain actual manufacturing, R&D, distribution, and management substance in China to avoid recharacterization of its Chinese-source income or denial of DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority recovers the evaded tax, imposes a late-payment surcharge, and levies a fine from 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; repeated or large-scale evasion converts to criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, punishable by imprisonment for up to seven years plus confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who approve or participate in the concealment are personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties; the State Administration of Taxation lists the enterprise and its directors on the serious tax-violation blacklist, triggering exit-tax restrictions, travel bans, disqualification from corporate office, and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 requires a comparative arm-length adjustment and reallocation of profit by the tax authority; failure to maintain required TP documentation leads to penalties under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 62 and 63 from RMB 2,000 to RMB 50,000; an intentional transfer-pricing adjustment may additionally be treated as tax evasion with a 50%-500% fine and 0.05% daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute artificial pricing knowing it shifts China-sourced profits abroad can be ordered to make civil restitution for the underpaid tax; serious egregious schemes involving forged invoices or concealed bank accounts subject the directors to personal criminal prosecution, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 permit the tax authority to disregard the structure, deny treaty benefits, impose the full 10% statutory withholding rate, and assess additional tax plus late-payment surcharges; the denial can trigger anti-fraud penalties of 50%-500% of the under-withheld tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who create or operate a sham foreign holding chain face civil asset restitution for the additional withholding tax, personal blacklisting with exit bans and travel restrictions, and criminal liability for tax evasion or money laundering when the disguised dividends are diverted through multiple shell jurisdictions.
