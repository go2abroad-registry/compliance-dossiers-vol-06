Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under PRC national law. Under Article 47 of the PRC Company Law, shareholders of a limited liability company must pay their subscribed capital in full within five years after establishment unless otherwise provided by a special law, but no statutory minimum amount is prescribed for a WFOE engaged in medical device manufacturing. The Articles of Association must state a registered capital and contribution schedule, funds must be remitted to a bank account in the Company's own name, and foreign-exchange-funded contributions must be recorded with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through the designated bank. There is no blocked escrow account requirement for ordinary WFOE incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: The manufacturing plant must obtain a Construction Project Fire Protection Design Review and Fire Protection Completion Acceptance Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Authority before operation. The building must satisfy fire resistance rating, occupancy load, exit width, automatic fire suppression, and smoke extraction requirements for a Class III metal-processing and cleanroom facility. Separate storage must be provided for argon cylinders and titanium powder, with inert gas detection and explosion protection per GB 50016 building code. Occupational health clearance is required under the PRC Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases, including a pre-evaluation and control-effect evaluation covering metal dust, noise, electron-beam-generated X-ray radiation, argon asphyxiation, and manual handling. The Company must register any pressure vessel, autoclave or lifting machine with the local special equipment safety authority and obtain current inspection certificates, and must appoint a workplace safety manager and complete hazardous environment monitoring prior to the first production run.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: Electron Tax Authority (e-Tax) website and local STA e-Tax terminals

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a unified social credit code. The WFOE must then complete tax registration with the local competent tax authority within 30 days of obtaining the business license, using the unified social credit code as the primary Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings. The WFOE must register for value-added tax (VAT) and corporate income tax (CIT) at the same time. For a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment, tax registration is generally not required; instead, the withholding agent registers the non-resident for withholding tax purposes. For cross-border e-commerce export sellers, the WFOE must also register for export tax refund (export VAT zero-rating) status through the tax authority's export tax rebate system, and may need customs registration as a customs declaration unit.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration threshold for manufacturing enterprises; any entity engaged in taxable sales of goods or services in China must register for VAT regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers may have simplified rates, but manufacturing WFOEs are generally general VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim zero-rated VAT treatment for exports of goods if it is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are actually exported from China, the export customs declaration has been completed, and the sales proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory time limit. The exported goods must be physically shipped out of Chinese customs territory, and the export must be verified by customs. For qualifying offshore services or digital services supplied to overseas clients, zero-rating or VAT exemption applies under the cross-border service tax rules if the services are consumed entirely outside China and are listed in the 'zero-rated/免税' scope of current tax regulations. For orthopedic titanium implants, export zero-rating eligibility requires a valid export contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration, proof of receipt of foreign exchange, and transport documents evidencing cross-border movement.

China operates a national electronic invoice (e-invoice) system integrated with the State Taxation Administration's tax control console. General VAT taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the VAT invoice management system, with mandatory real-time reporting of invoice data to the STA. Full digital electronic invoices (fully digitalized e-invoices) have been progressively rolled out, requiring qualified taxpayers to issue e-invoices through the electronic invoice platform, with no paper invoice requirement. The system uses anti-fraud digital stamping and unified tax control device coding to ensure authenticity. For exports, electronic export declarations are transmitted through the customs single window and matched with tax authority data. Since 2026, all taxpayers are generally required to use fully digitalized e-invoices, with batch issuing and API connectivity allowed under the electronic invoice service platform.

China's tax authority conducts post-clearance audit and risk control on export tax rebates and zero-rating claims. If an exporter falsely declares exports, fabricates export documentation, or fails to repatriate foreign exchange within the statutory period without reasonable justification, the tax authority will deny the zero-rating claim, recover any VAT refund received, impose a late payment surcharge, and may levy penalties ranging from 50% to five times the amount of tax underpaid or fraudulently obtained. In serious cases, export tax rebate fraud may constitute a crime under the Criminal Law (e.g., smuggling or tax fraud), leading to criminal liability for the responsible persons, suspension of export tax rebate qualifications, and downgrading of customs credit status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese Renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including payment of import charges, service fees, and dividends after tax, subject to commercial bank verification and reporting. Capital account convertibility remains heavily restricted. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must register foreign debt and foreign direct investment (FDI) capital contributions with SAFE, either through the local SAFE branch or its delegated bank. Capital may be injected via a capital account in the name of the WFOE, and subsequent settlement into RMB is permitted only for genuine operating expenses, verified against contracts, invoices, and customs declarations. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders requires audited financial statements, tax compliance certificates, and a board resolution, and must be processed through a bank under the current account framework. Surrender requirements exist for export proceeds: the exporter must repatriate foreign currency earnings to China within 180 days after customs declaration, but mandatory conversion ratios have been removed, allowing retention in a foreign currency current account or voluntary conversion into RMB. Any conversion is recorded in the bank's monitoring system and reviewed for consistency with the entity's import and export volume. For a titanium orthopedic implant manufacturer, royalties, raw material imports, and clinical testing service fees will be scrutinized for verifiable invoices and customs documentation. Banks also require proof that the WFOE's registered capital is fully paid and that the business scope matches the actual use of funds. Because China does not allow free portfolio capital flows or offshore RMB loans without prior approval, multinational treasurers must structure intercompany funding strictly through SAFE-registered FDI or short-term foreign debt quotas, with every transaction traceable and supported by underlying commercial documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate all export proceeds to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; proceeds may be retained in a domestic foreign currency current account or voluntarily settled into RMB. Banks must verify the export customs form and receipt matching through the 'Proceeds Collection and Verification System' before releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) have no legal standing to provide banking or payment services to a registered China WFOE. Under PBoC and SAFE regulations, only licensed Chinese-incorporated banks and non-bank payment institutions with a 'Payment Business License' may handle RMB clearing, foreign currency conversion, and cross-border trade settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut or Wise cannot open local RMB or foreign currency accounts for a mainland entity, and their settlement rails cannot be used for customs declarations or SAFE reporting. At best, the WFOE may use an offshore EMI for non-mainland ancillary services after the bank account is established, but any payment that crosses the Chinese border must flow through the domestic licensed bank. The feasibility rating is therefore 'Low' for primary treasury operations, and no reliance on EMIs is recommended for CAPEX, payroll, or supplier payments within China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a WFOE, all inbound capital injections must be routed through a SAFE-registered foreign currency capital account opened at a Chinese commercial bank. The offshore investor will wire funds via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's capital account, referencing the foreign investment code 'FI - Foreign Direct Investment' and the SAFE registration number. After receipt, the bank verifies the transaction against the electronic FDI registration record in SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administrative Information System. Once verified, the funds can be settled into RMB only for approved expenditures: salary payments, rent, raw materials, equipment imports, and domestic taxes. Each disposal must be backed by original expense contracts, invoices, and customs forms. For medical device production, an injection used for purchasing electron-beam 3D printing equipment must be accompanied by a customs declaration and valuation report. No separate capital verification by an accounting firm is required after the 2014 reform; the bank's settlement record and the company's audited financial statements suffice for future capital falsification. Any funds retained in foreign currency may be kept in an operating foreign currency account, but should be used within 90 days for bona fide current transactions.Outbound trade settlements for the orthopedic implant business will use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments of service fees, royalties, and intercompany cost allocations, with the purpose code '122010' for services and '121010' for goods. For large equipment import or export transactions under a commercial contract, the bank will issue a Letter of Credit (L/C) in favor of the foreign supplier, with the timeframe typically 30 to 60 days after sight. Documentary collections are accepted when the exporter and importer have established trust and the transaction value is lower than USD 100,000. All outbound payments must be matched with the corresponding import customs declaration or contract, and the bank will verify that the beneficial owner is not on a sanctions list. The enterprise must retain a complete file of trade documents for at least 5 years for future audit by SAFE and the State Taxation Administration.Onshore FX forwards with licensed Chinese banks for trades with a genuine current-account underlying exposure, maximum tenor of 1 year, using the standard order-driven or negotiated forward contract under 'Current Account Foreign Exchange Settlement and Sale'.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term foreign-currency debt or intercompany loans, subject to SAFE approval and the real debt contract, used to hedge currency mismatches on the WFOE's capital account.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore for resident WFOEs; offshore investors may use NDFs in Hong Kong SAR or London, but the mainland entity cannot access those markets. Basic FX swaps between RMB and USD can be used to cover operational liquidity if a single-purpose trade on the bank's proprietary trading book is available.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) have no legal standing to provide banking or payment services to a registered China WFOE. Under PBoC and SAFE regulations, only licensed Chinese-incorporated banks and non-bank payment institutions with a 'Payment Business License' may handle RMB clearing, foreign currency conversion, and cross-border trade settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut or Wise cannot open local RMB or foreign currency accounts for a mainland entity, and their settlement rails cannot be used for customs declarations or SAFE reporting. At best, the WFOE may use an offshore EMI for non-mainland ancillary services after the bank account is established, but any payment that crosses the Chinese border must flow through the domestic licensed bank. The feasibility rating is therefore 'Low' for primary treasury operations, and no reliance on EMIs is recommended for CAPEX, payroll, or supplier payments within China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016), the Local File is mandatory once annual related-party transaction amounts exceed the statutory thresholds: approximately RMB 200 million for tangible property transactions, RMB 50 million for intangible property transactions, RMB 40 million for service transactions, and RMB 100 million for financial transactions; the Master File is required for large multinational groups meeting the global consolidated related-party reporting thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a separately labelled Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are embedded in the beneficial-ownership test, permanent-establishment attribution, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47. The WFOE claiming preferential rates or treaty benefits must maintain actual personnel, premises, decision-making, risk control, and value-adding functions in China; a paper shareholder or parent in a treaty jurisdiction with no CIGA will be disregarded and profit reattributed to the entity performing the economically relevant activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing mispricing on the cross-border titanium implant supply chain

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 and 48 permit the tax authority to redetermine profit under the arm's length principle and apply a special tax adjustment through the standard assessment process; interest is charged from the underpayment date at the central-bank benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; fraudulent transfer pricing documentation may also be penalized under Tax Collection Law Article 64.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false related-party schedule and leaves the WFOE unable to pay the adjusted tax may be jointly liable under Company Law Article 20 where the corporate veil is pierced for abuse of limited liability or asset commingling; intentional tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 exposes the director to personal criminal liability of up to 3 years' imprisonment, or 3-7 years for a huge amount, plus fines, blacklisting, travel bans, and disqualification from serving as a director in China.
Unlawful withholding avoidance and abusive treaty relief on dividend repatriation

Statutory sanction: As withholding agent, the WFOE is liable for the uncollected tax under EIT Law Article 37 and Tax Collection Law Article 69; the underpaid WHT is recovered from the withholding agent with a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 where fraud or false declarations exist, plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge; treaty relief is denied where the recipient is not the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors who order the remittance without withholding or who procure a false Tax Residency Certificate or beneficial-ownership statement may be criminally liable for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201; courts may impose fines and imprisonment, order restitution from personal assets, impose an exit ban under the seriously dishonest taxpayer blacklisting system, and restrict high-consumption activities and foreign travel.
Shell-company economic-substance abuse and fraudulent low-tax profit shifting

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 allows the tax authority to disregard the artificial structure and recharacterize the arrangement by reference to commercial substance; the special tax adjustment is backdated, interest is charged at the benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points, and where artificial transactions are booked in tax returns, the fraud penalties of Tax Collection Law Articles 63-64 apply; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 can follow where the tax evasion threshold is met.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve the sham shell structure with no CIGA can be treated as responsible persons for the company's tax fraud; civil piercing under Company Law Article 20 exposes personal assets where corporate assets have been shifted beyond creditor reach; criminal tax fraud liability can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years, travel-ban measures, permanent personal credit entries, and disqualification from serving as a director or officer of any PRC company.
