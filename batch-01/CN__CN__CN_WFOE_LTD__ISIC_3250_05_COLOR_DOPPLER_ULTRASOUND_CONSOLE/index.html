Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital or paid-up capital is imposed for an ordinary WFOE manufacturing medical devices under current PRC law; the sole shareholder determines the registered capital in the Articles of Association. However, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, all subscribers must pay their subscribed capital in full within five years after establishment unless a shorter period is specified in the articles or applicable industry rules. There is no blocked escrow account requirement; capital is remitted from the foreign parent to the company's foreign currency capital account after completion of FDI foreign exchange information registration through an authorized bank, and settlement of capital into RMB is regulated by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and the bank's capital account controls.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A WFOE organized as a limited liability company is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income at the statutory rate of 25% and is not eligible for pass-through treatment. The WFOE is simultaneously the registered VAT taxpayer and the customs exporting entity for the medical device manufacturing and export business.

Available election forms

None for check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections in China. No passthrough election exists for a limited liability WFOE. Under VAT, an enterprise below the general taxpayer compulsory threshold may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer if it satisfies accounting and invoicing conditions, and a small-scale taxpayer may apply to switch to simplified taxation; these are invoicing and collection-rule elections, not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to an incorporated WFOE; VAT registration is mandatory upon incorporation. For a non-established foreign supplier, VAT registration is required whenever the supplier makes taxable goods or services supplies in China. The annual VAT taxable turnover of RMB 5,000,000 is the statutory threshold that makes registration as a general VAT taxpayer compulsory for enterprises; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer simplified rules apply unless a voluntary general taxpayer election is approved.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods physically leaving the customs territory of mainland China qualify for 0% VAT and refund of input VAT when: the exporter is a registered VAT taxpayer (general taxpayer for refund eligibility); the goods have been declared and cleared by Chinese Customs; the goods are actually shipped abroad and the export declaration is filed; export proceeds are received and repatriated through an approved bank or foreign exchange channel; and all statutory invoices, contracts, transport documents and medical-device export license records are submitted and match the customs and tax databases. A Color Doppler ultrasound console manufactured by the WFOE is eligible under this goods-export zero-rating regime when classified under the applicable medical-device customs commodity code and accompanied by the required export licensing documents.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (bill-of-entry equivalent generated through the International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment
  • Export VAT invoice and commercial invoice for the Color Doppler ultrasound console
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Packing list
  • Medical device export license or statutory certificate for the Color Doppler ultrasound console
  • Foreign exchange receipt record generated from the SAFE Trade in Goods Monitoring System (in lieu of an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

China mandates issuance of fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration's Comprehensive Digitalized E-invoice Platform. Invoices must carry a unique tax-control invoice code, anti-fraud QR code, taxpayer digital identity and digital stamp, and invoice data is transmitted in near real time to STA central systems. Buyers and sellers authenticate invoices through the Official VAT Invoice Verification Platform. Enterprise systems must integrate via STA-approved APIs or use the STA-provided electronic fiscal service portal. For export sales, the export VAT invoice data must match customs declaration data and SAFE foreign exchange receipt data in the export tax rebate automated verification engine.

The STA, China Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly match customs export declarations, VAT export invoices, transport documents, license records, and repatriated foreign exchange receipts. Unsupported, false, or fraudulent export zero-rating or rebate claims are rejected, and any refund already paid is clawed back with statutory late-payment surcharge accruing at 0.05% per day. The enterprise may also be fined between 50% and 500% of the tax shortfall under the Law on Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. Conduct constituting export tax refund fraud gives rise to criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including imprisonment and confiscation of property, and the enterprise may be placed under higher-risk inspection, required to post tax payment guarantees, and subjected to retrospective audit review beyond the normal limitation period in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime anchored by the PBOC and administered by SAFE. Current account transactions are broadly convertible as required under IMF Article VIII: export/import trade, wage remittances, and service payments may be settled through designated foreign exchange banks after genuine trade verification. Capital account convertibility remains heavily controlled, and every inward foreign direct investment remittance must be registered with SAFE via the local bank, opened to the FDI foreign-exchange capital account, and then settled into RMB for domestic operating purposes only. Funds cannot be freely transferred between capital accounts and current accounts without evidentiary documents. Profit/dividend remittance to the overseas shareholder is conditionally permitted after annual audited accounts are filed, income tax is settled, and SAFE registration is updated; banks scrutinize retained-earnings calculations and require proof that settlement of capital was performed through a qualified RMB settlement account. Surrender requirements have been substantially liberalized: exporters are no longer forced to convert a fixed percentage of receipts, but foreign exchange earnings legally resident in China must be repatriated, verified against customs declarations, and monitored under A/B/C classification frameworks. Overall convertibility is operational for genuine trade/investment flows, but not free-market parity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under applicable SAFE rules, exporters are required to repatriate export-generated foreign exchange into China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, except for approved offshore receivable arrangements. Once repatriated, the proceeds may be retained 100% in the enterprise's foreign exchange settlement account or converted at the market rate; there is no mandatory conversion ratio and the old fixed surrender ratios have been repealed. For Category B/C enterprises, additional verification requirements and classification-based restrictions apply before funds are eligible for unrestricted use.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as principal treasury account; Medium only as an ancillary cross-border collection channel. A CN WFOE is legally required to maintain its primary settlement relationship with a SAFE-licensed bank; offshore fintech EMIs such as non-licensed digital banks cannot open domestic RMB accounts, cannot provide FDI capital-account clearance, and cannot perform statutory SAFE registration. An overseas EMI may provide foreign-currency receivable collection through a licensed Chinese payment institution if it has obtained PBOC/SAFE cross-border payment filings, but even then all funds must ultimately be repatriated through the WFOE's bank account and verified against customs/trade documentation. Therefore, EMIs cannot substitute for the domestic Tier-1 bank treasury structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent bank must transmit SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for cover) to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Escrow Account at the Chinese Tier-1 bank, using a SAFE-approved payment purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection and including the WFOE's FDI registration reference number. After receipt, the beneficiary bank verifies the capital account notice, performs authenticity checks, and obtains a capital account settlement order before converting into RMB; the settled RMB is restricted to permitted operating expenditures and cannot be used for certain prohibited investments. A PRC-certified public accountant must then issue a capital verification report, which is filed with SAFE and used to update the business license/registered capital records. The transaction must remain within the aggregate FDI quota recorded in the SAFE system.The WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 for open-account trade payments and service fees where contracts and tax payment vouchers are available; commercial letters of credit (sight or usance) for high-value medical component imports and overseas distributor shipment security; and documentary collections (documents against payment/acceptance) for established low-risk counterparties. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory enterprise income tax withholding on dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to a non-resident beneficial owner under PRC EIT Law Articles 3, 37 and 38. No income tax is withheld when a Chinese resident enterprise pays dividends to another Chinese resident enterprise. Treaty rates commonly reduce dividend withholding to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the WFOE's paid-in capital and satisfies limitation-on-benefits and principal-purpose tests.

Failure to file the annual EIT return by May 31: the tax authority may order correction, impose a fine up to RMB 10,000 (or RMB 2,000-RMB 10,000 for serious cases), and levy a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the overdue tax for each day of delay. Fraudulent underpayment under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 incurs a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax plus the daily surcharge. If the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, criminal tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 applies: up to three years' imprisonment and/or criminal fine for relatively large evasion; three to seven years' imprisonment and a criminal fine for huge evasion. Failure to prepare transfer-pricing local file or master file can also trigger RMB 10,000-100,000 penalties, and the tax authority may disregard the taxpayer's documentation and estimate taxable income.
Box 21 Off-return declaration for outbound dividend withholding: not an EIT line but a mandatory withholding event associated with the annual return and profit appropriation resolution. When the WFOE declares a dividend to its non-resident shareholder, it must withhold 10% EIT on the gross dividend (or treaty-reduced rate) and remit it to the state tax authority within 15 days. Dividend, interest, and royalty remittances are reviewed by the bank and tax bureau simultaneously; absence of a withholding receipt may block the foreign-exchange payment. Failure to file the annual EIT return by May 31: the tax authority may order correction, impose a fine up to RMB 10,000 (or RMB 2,000-RMB 10,000 for serious cases), and levy a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the overdue tax for each day of delay. Fraudulent underpayment under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 incurs a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax plus the daily surcharge. If the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, criminal tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 applies: up to three years' imprisonment and/or criminal fine for relatively large evasion; three to seven years' imprisonment and a criminal fine for huge evasion. Failure to prepare transfer-pricing local file or master file can also trigger RMB 10,000-100,000 penalties, and the tax authority may disregard the taxpayer's documentation and estimate taxable income.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation must be prepared by a taxpayer whose annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required where the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in the fiscal year reaches RMB 1 billion. A Country-by-Country Report is required where the China-related enterprise is part of an MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not use the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations name, but the same substantive doctrine is enforced through Article 47 of the EIT Law, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules, and the beneficial-ownership rules of SAT Announcements. The medical-imaging WFOE must have CIGA in China: physical manufacturing infrastructure, qualified ultrasound engineering personnel, regulatory certificates for domestic/export medical devices, actual warehousing, order-taking, risk control, and substantive service and financing decisions. If an intermediate Hong Kong, Singapore or Bermuda holding company is interposed solely to obtain a lower DTA rate on Chinese dividends or royalties, the Chinese tax authority may apply the principal-purpose test, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the transaction, and impose 25% EIT on the deemed China-source income plus penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Expense Deduction and Understatement of Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 orders recovery of the evaded tax and imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded amount plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes failure to file true returns, resulting in imprisonment up to three years plus criminal fines for relatively large evasion, and three to seven years' imprisonment if the amount is huge and the evasion proportion exceeds 30%.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law where directors abuse the corporate form to evade taxes or harm creditors. The directing shareholders and finance directors may be jointly and severally liable for the WFOE's unpaid taxes from personal assets. Chinese tax authorities may place the legal representative on the non-compliant taxpayer blacklist, trigger exit-restricted measures, and prohibit the director from leaving China until the tax, penalty, and surcharge are cleared. Where the falsification is proven, the responsible officers can be sentenced to criminal detention or fixed-term imprisonment and disqualified from serving as directors.
False Issuance and Use of Special VAT Invoices for Export Rebate Fraud

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 205 punishes the false issuance of special VAT invoices with criminal fines and imprisonment. If the falsely invoiced tax amount is relatively large, the responsible individuals can be sentenced to up to three years; if the amount is huge, three to ten years; and if particularly huge, the sentence is more than ten years or life imprisonment, with confiscation of property. The tax authority also voids the input credit, disallows the expense, recovers the fraudulent export rebate, imposes a 50%-500% penalty for false declarations, and revokes the WFOE's tax-payer rating and export-rebate status.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and financial officers who approve or order the false invoicing cannot hide behind the limited-liability entity. Under Company Law Article 23 (one-person company) and Article 192 (actual controller/director liability), the court may order direct personal liability, asset attachment, restitution of evaded tax, and a ban on serving as directors of any Chinese enterprise. Criminal conviction exposes them to fixed-term imprisonment or life imprisonment for especially large false VAT amounts, alongside the corporate fine; after the conviction, the director is subject to blacklisting, exit controls, and asset execution.
Treaty Abuse, Shell-Company Conduit, and Lack of Economic Substance in Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority applies Article 47 of the EIT Law, the general anti-avoidance rules, and the principal-purpose test under the MLI/Arrangement to deny treaty benefits, recharacterizing the Hong Kong entity as a conduit. The full statutory 10% WHT is reinstated on the entire dividend or royalty, plus interest at 0.05% per day and a penalty of 50% to five times the under-withheld tax. In egregious cases the entire arrangement is re-taxed in China at 25% EIT on deemed China-source income, and administrative blacklisting is issued.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false beneficial-ownership declarations and approved the dividend flow under the shell structure face joint personal liability under the Company Law for the additional taxes and damages caused to the WFOE and the state. Tax authorities may use inter-agency joint punishment mechanisms to freeze personal accounts, forbid the director from leaving China, cancel visas or residence permits for foreign directors, and impose a global-travel ban. If the profit-shifting scheme is found to involve concealment with criminal intent, directors can be prosecuted under Article 201 for tax evasion, with imprisonment up to seven years and criminal fines, while the blacklisted director cannot rehabilitate unless the tax loss, penalties and surcharge are fully paid.
