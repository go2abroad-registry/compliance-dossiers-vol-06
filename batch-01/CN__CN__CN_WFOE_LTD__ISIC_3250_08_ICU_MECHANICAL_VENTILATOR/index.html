Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law adopts a subscribed capital regime for limited liability companies; no minimum paid-up capital and no statutory escrow or blocked account rule applies to a WFOE manufacturing medical ventilators. The registered capital amount and contribution schedule are fixed in the Articles of Association and must be subscribed by the sole shareholder. Foreign funds are injected through a foreign exchange capital account maintained with a bank authorized by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange; a statutory capital verification certificate is not required unless a particular licensing authority demands one.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the unified electronic tax portal known as the China Electronic Tax Bureau (etTax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the 12366 national tax service hotline.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is first established through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) one-stop business registration process. The SAMR issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the legal and accounting tax identification number for all tax filings; no separate TIN issuance is required for a resident enterprise. The USCC is automatically synchronized to the State Taxation Administration (STA), and the WFOE must complete tax registration recordal on the local STA or through etTax immediately after incorporation. For foreign non-resident enterprises that generate China-source income or supply taxable goods or services into China, the obligation is primarily satisfied by the China payment-withholding agent; if the non-resident seller has no withholding agent, it must separately apply for tax registration with the competent STA office and obtain the required tax identification record before filing returns on etTax. VAT registration details for the WFOE are completed by selecting general or small-scale VAT taxpayer status, registering the VAT invoice system, and obtaining the invoice administrator and tax-control issuance authorization. The WFOE must also complete withholding registrations for employees' individual income tax and social insurance contributions, but these are separate obligations and do not replace the USCC-based tax identification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Law; no pass-through or transparent classification is available for a WFOE limited liability company)

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification election. The WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise-level taxpayer. The only relevant statutory elections are turnover-based VAT taxpayer status selection (small-scale versus general VAT taxpayer) and ordinary CIT accounting method elections; neither changes the entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when taxable turnover from goods sales exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. A taxpayer below this threshold is automatically treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily registers as a general VAT taxpayer to claim input VAT credits. Because the WFOE manufactures physical goods (ICU mechanical ventilators) under ISIC 3250, the standard goods rate of 13% applies to domestic sales; export sales are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside the customs territory of China, including turbine-driven critical care ICU mechanical ventilators manufactured by the WFOE, are eligible for the VAT zero-rating and export tax rebate regime if the goods are physically exported, the exporter holds the export contract and customs clearance evidence, the goods are named in the export declaration as the selling WFOE's products, tax risk and title are transferred to an overseas buyer, the sale proceeds are received in foreign exchange (or lawfully settled in RMB only for qualifying cross-border transactions), and the export sale is correctly declared in the VAT return. The goods must fall within the statutory export goods rebate catalogue; medical device registration and export compliance approvals do not replace the VAT zero-rating conditions. Qualifying outward sales under Government letters of credit and SWIFT wire payment rails are eligible provided all supporting documentary and foreign exchange receipt conditions are met.

China operates a centrally controlled electronic VAT invoice system through the State Taxation Administration. Since the nationwide roll-out of fully digitalized electronic invoices, all VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved electronic invoice service platforms and uploaded in near-real time. Every invoice carries a nationally compiled unique invoice number, an anti-counterfeit QR barcode, and a tax-control stamp. The platform performs automatic fiscal clearance by matching invoice issuer identity, taxpayer qualification, invoice amount, VAT rate, and invoice sequence with the taxpayer's archived registrations. Enterprises using self-built ERP systems must interface with the STA e-invoice service platform through approved APIs to transmit invoice metadata; offline manual invoicing is restricted and subject to prior authorization. The Golden Tax Phase IV supervision system reconciles invoice data against customs export declarations, foreign exchange settlement, bank accounts, VAT returns, corporate income tax filings, and business license data. There is no private-sector fiscal voucher clearance operator in China; the STA platform itself is the sole legal clearance and digital stamping authority. For export sales, the digital electronic export VAT invoice must be issued before the VAT refund and export rebate application is accepted.

Export zero-rating is subject to coordinated post-clearance audits by the State Taxation Administration, China Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. If the required export declaration, freight document, foreign exchange receipt, payment evidence, or VAT invoice is missing, inconsistent, or fabricated, the STA may disallow zero-rating, reinstate output VAT at 13% on the export sale, claw back input VAT previously refunded, and charge a daily late payment surcharge on the underpaid VAT. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Collection and Administration, false export refund claims or fraudulent zero-rating may attract penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refund obtained, together with administrative forfeiture of export tax rebate qualifications. Serious cases involving false export documents, fraudulent VAT invoices, or export rebate fraud may be transferred under the Criminal Law for tax evasion and export rebate fraud, exposing responsible persons to fines and imprisonment. The exporter must retain the complete documentation bundle for the statutory tax records retention period and produce original electronic customs and foreign exchange records on audit demand.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current-account items such as trade in goods and services, but capital-account transactions remain heavily managed, making full free convertibility unavailable for a WFOE. For inbound FDI into the WFOE, the foreign investor must first complete the SAFE FDI registration and open a dedicated foreign-currency capital account in a domestic licensed bank. Inward remittances must be supported by a Board resolution, the investment agreement, the business license and the FDI registration certificate. The bank will debit the capital account and convert the foreign currency into RMB under the SAFE positive-list settlement regime, restricting use to operating expenses, salaries, rent, and permitted procurement of materials. Conversely, conversion from RMB back to foreign currency and outbound profit remittance requires the WFOE to provide audited annual financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the local tax authority, a board resolution on dividend distribution, and evidence that the profit is sourced from genuine operations. Current-account receipts from exports are fully verifiable and settle quickly, but any capital-account outflow, including equity transfer proceeds, capital reduction, liquidation proceeds, and repayment of cross-border loans, must be separately approved or registered with SAFE. The overlay of tax-clearing and anti-money-laundering reviews therefore imposes substantial friction on profit repatriation, and banks may delay conversion if any prior information is incomplete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a WFOE exporting ventilators under ISIC 3250, the exporter must repatriate export proceeds to China within the statutory verification window of 90 days after the export customs declaration. There is no fixed percentage surrender ratio; however, 100% of proceeds must be received into a designated domestic foreign exchange settlement account and included in the SAFE goods-trade verification system. The bank will match the incoming SWIFT remittance to the export customs declaration, e-invoice, and transport documents. If the payment is not received within 90 days or differs by more than 25% from the declared value, the enterprise must file a goods trade foreign exchange exception report to SAFE before the bank will release the funds. Any retention of export proceeds in offshore bank accounts is prohibited absent specific SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. Mainland China does not issue EMI licenses to foreign digital banks or allow unlicensed fintech EMIs to service onshore WFOEs. PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border payments and RMB conversions to be performed by licensed banks via the cross-border payment management system. Foreign fintech EMIs may only clear via correspondent banking and cannot open resident settlement accounts. Therefore the viability rating is 0/10 and legal constraints make them unusable as primary liquidity rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital wires must be denominated in USD, EUR or HKD and addressed to the WFOE's FDI capital escrow account at the domestic Tier-1 bank, with the SWIFT MT103 clearly quoting the investor name, WFOE name, SAFE FDI registration number, and the FX purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection. The receiving bank must complete the foreign exchange capital settlement procedure for conversion to RMB and allocate funds strictly in line with the SAFE positive-list rules. No payment may be released to goods or service vendors until the FDI registration and capital verification by an approved accounting firm have been completed; the capital verification report should be filed with the bank within the statutory period. For automated clearing, use the bank's straight-through processing module and never route through a non-bank EMI.For import and export trade settlements, use domestic Tier-1 bank branches with SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers, or issue commercial letters of credit in favor of overseas suppliers for ventilators and raw materials, and use documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-risk consignments. All outbound disbursements must match an underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration and transport document; payments above a threshold are reported to SAFE in the cross-border payments and income system. For medical device components from sensitive countries, use confirmed irrevocable letters of credit with bank-to-bank SWIFT messages to secure transaction integrity and ensure a clean packing list.Onshore CNY/USD deliverable forwards under the SAFE real-demand rule, used for export proceeds and import payables, with a maturity aligned to the underlying contractual payment date.Onshore foreign-exchange swaps and cross-currency swaps for the notional value of FDI capital conversion and dividend remittance, booked with a licensed domestic Tier-1 bank.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible on the WFOE's own books; the offshore shareholder outside mainland China may maintain an NDF hedge, but the WFOE cannot take, clear, or expense such contracts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable. Mainland China does not issue EMI licenses to foreign digital banks or allow unlicensed fintech EMIs to service onshore WFOEs. PBOC and SAFE require all cross-border payments and RMB conversions to be performed by licensed banks via the cross-border payment management system. Foreign fintech EMIs may only clear via correspondent banking and cannot open resident settlement accounts. Therefore the viability rating is 0/10 and legal constraints make them unusable as primary liquidity rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is generally required for related-party tangible property transactions exceeding RMB 200 million, intangible property transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, financial asset transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, and other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File documentation is required for a Chinese entity within a group whose total related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or whose group consolidated financial statements meet the State Taxation Administration filing thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations framework, but a WFOE must demonstrate substantive business purpose, real management, personnel, premises, decision-making authority, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) within China for CIT and DTA purposes. A paper entity without substance will lose treaty relief, face withholding tax recharacterization, and be exposed to anti-avoidance adjustments under the General Anti-Abuse Rule and the beneficial ownership doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company abuse and treaty shopping for profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief; withholding tax re-characterized at the statutory 10% rate; additional income tax imposed under PRC CIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Abuse Rule) and the Special Tax Adjustment measures; late payment interest at 0.05% per day; penalties ranging from 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the treaty-shopping structure may be jointly liable for the unpaid corporate tax if they abused the corporate form; civil restitution can extend to personal assets. The legal representative may be placed on exit-banishment / travel blacklist, preventing departure from China until the outstanding tax is settled; criminal liability may arise for fraudulent tax declaration under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years for significant evasion.
Transfer pricing manipulation through intercompany ICU ventilator sales

Statutory sanction: Tax authority makes a transfer pricing adjustment under PRC CIT Law Article 41 and the State Taxation Administration Special Tax Adjustments Implementation Measures; taxable income is recalculated to an arm's length result; additional CIT is payable; late payment interest accrues; the adjustment may include secondary adjustment and penalty up to 5 times the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed the manipulated pricing policy can be held personally liable for a resulting shortfall when the corporate veil is pierced under the Civil Code and Company Law. The director's travel may be restricted through the blacklisting mechanism for unpaid tax liabilities; if the manipulation amounts to fraudulent tax evasion, criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years can be imposed on the responsible officers.
Unlicensed medical device manufacturing and export fraud

Statutory sanction: Medical device manufacturing without the legally required license violates PRC Regulations on the Administration of Medical Devices and can attract administrative fines of RMB 100,000 to RMB 500,000 plus confiscation of illegal products and income. Fraudulent export tax refund claims trigger criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 204, with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years or life imprisonment for especially large amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed where they ordered or recklessly allowed unlicensed manufacturing and export refund fraud: civil asset restitution can be enforced against directors, exit bans are applied for outstanding tax and customs liabilities, and criminal sanctions include imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion or up to life imprisonment for export tax refund fraud where the amount is extremely large.
