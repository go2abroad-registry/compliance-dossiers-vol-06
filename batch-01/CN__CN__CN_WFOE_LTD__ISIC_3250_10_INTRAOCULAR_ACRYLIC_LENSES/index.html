Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; operational online portal: the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-taxation platform) of the STA, accessible through provincial-level STA e-tax service portals and integrated with the General Administration of Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The WFOE is incorporated under the Administration for Market Regulation and obtains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license. Under China's multi-certificate integration regime, the USCC is the sole statutory tax identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number for the enterprise. Step 2: Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration verification through the Electronic Tax Bureau by filing the legal representative, financial responsible person, tax handler, bank deposit account ledger, and accounting system information. Step 3: The competent STA office activates the tax record and assigns the VAT invoice quota, invoice inventory, and filing cycles; the WFOE must apply for general VAT taxpayer registration if annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, and may voluntarily apply if below the threshold to enable input VAT credit and export zero-rating. Step 4: For foreign non-resident entities with no Chinese establishment, the Chinese WFOE or domestic payer acts as a withholding agent and must file the non-resident registration particulars with the competent STA office to obtain a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number (NR-TIN) for each non-resident recipient. The NR-TIN is used for treaty relief applications, for enterprise income tax withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, and for VAT withholding on cross-border services. Step 5: If the non-resident has a permanent establishment in China, the PE itself must complete standard tax registration using the USCC if established as a branch; otherwise the NR-TIN procedure applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Chinese WFOE is a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxed on worldwide income at the statutory 25% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through treatment is available for limited liability companies in Mainland China. The WFOE is also a general VAT taxpayer and is eligible for export VAT zero-rating as a producing exporter. This entity does not sit in a special tax-exempt zone; the standard mainland tax jurisdiction applies.

Available election forms

China does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or hybrid mismatch elections. Available elections include: (i) voluntary general VAT taxpayer registration for taxpayers below the CNY 5,000,000 mandatory threshold; (ii) election between the exemption-offset-refund export method and the buy-and-sell export method for export tax rebate purposes; (iii) election to apply double taxation agreement (treaty) benefits for non-resident withholding rates through the non-resident tax treatment application; (iv) election to use the accrual method of accounting for Corporate Income Tax; and (v) election of tax-filing frequency, generally quarterly for CIT and monthly/quarterly for VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese enterprises must obtain VAT taxpayer identification upon registration; there is no exemption threshold for an incorporated WFOE. A small-scale taxpayer with annual taxable sales of CNY 5,000,000 or below may apply the simplified VAT collection rate (currently 3%, temporarily 1% through 2027), while annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 make general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory. Manufacturing exporters such as the intraocular acrylic lens WFOE normally register as general VAT taxpayers to claim input VAT credits and the export zero-rating mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating is statutorily available for tangible goods produced in Mainland China and physically exported outside the customs territory. The WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer, hold a genuine cross-border sales contract, complete the customs export declaration, cause the goods to be shipped through a Chinese customs-supervised port, and receive the export sales proceeds in foreign currency (or approved RMB settlement) through licensed banks. Intraocular acrylic lens exports are eligible under the manufacturer's exemption-offset-refund method, which allows the refund of remaining input VAT after offset against output VAT on domestic sales.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form bearing the digital customs release record (export Bill of Entry equivalent)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract/purchase order matching the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical export from China
  • Bank inward remittance certificate / export proceeds collection declaration (foreign exchange receipt record and SAFE trade collection data)
  • Non-collection or deferred-collection registration certificate, if the export proceeds have not yet been collected within the statutory deadline due to approved commercial terms
  • Medical device export documentary attestation or certificate of origin when required by customs or the destination country
  • Any applicable export VAT refund application and exemption/offset/refund tax calculation statement filed with the STA

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices exclusively through the STA-approved Electronic Tax Bureau digital e-invoice service platform, using fully digitalized electronic invoices. Authorized accounting/ERP integrations may connect via STA-approved APIs for invoice issuance, red-letter reversal, and reporting. Invoice issuance data are transmitted in real time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system, which performs automated risk screening, invoice inventory controls, counterparty matching, and anti-fraud analysis; no separate physical fiscal stamp is applied, but each digital invoice carries a unique invoice code and number assigned by the national e-invoice system.

The STA cross-references the Golden Tax IV invoice database, customs export declaration data, bank collection records, and SAFE trade foreign-exchange monitoring. If a sales transaction lacks actual physical export or the export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory deadline (unless a SAFE-approved extension is obtained), the STA will refuse or claw back the zero-rated refund, charge late payment interest on the unpaid VAT, require repayment of the refunded amount, and impose an administrative fine of up to five times the amount of tax involved for fraudulent claims. Criminal liability for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds includes imprisonment, criminal fines, and confiscation of assets. The enterprise may also be downgraded in the export tax refund credit classification, suspended from receiving future refunds, placed on the serious tax violation blacklist, and subjected to joint disciplinary restrictions in banking, customs clearance, and public procurement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB operates under a managed-floating exchange-rate regime with conditional convertibility. Current-account payments (imports, service fees, royalties, and dividends) are largely convertible after authentic transactions are verified by designated banks under SAFE's goods-trade and services-trade verification protocols. Capital-account items such as inbound equity investment, cross-border financing, outward FDI, and intragroup lending require SAFE filing or registration, post-verification, and are subject to the Negative List for Foreign Investment; no unrestricted free capital movement exists. A WFOE must establish an FDI foreign-exchange capital registration with the local SAFE branch through its bank before receiving shareholder capital. The RMB can be settled from foreign exchange into RMB for permitted working-capital purposes under the Negative List, but converting capital into speculative investments, wealth-management products, or real estate outside the business scope is prohibited. Surpluses and profit remittances are allowed on a current-account basis after audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board resolutions are presented. The absence of full capital-account convertibility means the treasurer must maintain an expenditure cash-flow calendar, pre-register equity injections, and align disbursements with real trade documentation to avoid blocked funds or regulatory penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through a designated bank receiving account within 90 days from the export declaration date or bill of lading date, or within 180 days where an extension is registered with SAFE under trade-credit terms. There is no blanket mandatory conversion ratio for verified current-account export proceeds in the current regulatory environment; a Category A exporter may retain the foreign currency in its FX current account or voluntarily settle it into RMB through a SAFE-licensed bank after authentic declaration matching in the goods-trade administration system. Category B and C exporters face stricter monitoring, and banks may require accelerated settlement or documentary proof of historical transactions when irregularities are detected.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated authority exercising regulatory and enforcement control over all foreign-exchange transactions in China (Mainland).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally restricted as a primary banking layer. Cross-border digital EMI platforms (including Wise, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, and Airwallex) are not licensed by the PBOC/SAFE as Chinese domestic banks and cannot host the WFOE's statutory SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account or RMB settlement account. They are not substitutes for onshore banking because all foreign-exchange receipts and payments originating from or receivable by a mainland WFOE must be channelled through banks that hold a valid foreign-exchange business licence. Overseas EMIs may only be used outside mainland China as parent-company side treasury interfaces or ancillary supply-chain payment tools, but their balances cannot flow directly into the WFOE treasury without an onshore receiving bank. Relying on overseas fintech EMIs creates KYC/AML gaps and would violate SAFE's centralized transaction-monitoring and reporting rules, so domestic Tier-1 commercial banks remain mandatory.

After incorporation, the WFOE must complete FDI foreign-exchange registration at the bank under SAFE's Foreign Direct Investment system. The parent company wires foreign currency from its own corporate bank account to the WFOE's newly opened FDI capital account at a designated bank using SWIFT MT103, with remittance information field 70 stating 'Equity investment in Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise for intraocular lens manufacturing' and applying the SAFE-specified direct-investment remittance code for capital contributions, namely the category for inbound equity investment. The bank verifies the remittance against the establishment registration dossier, capital amount, investor identity, and sectoral negative-list status. Foreign currency may be kept as foreign exchange or settled into RMB through a capital-account foreign-exchange settlement instruction, but only for permitted working-capital uses within the approved business scope and Negative List boundaries. Received funds must remain in the FDI capital account until the bank performs authenticity and compliance review; they cannot be transferred to offshore cash pools without SAFE approval. Documentary collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) are suitable for mid-risk suppliers with an established track record, but they lack the bank's payment undertaking. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally restricted as a primary banking layer. Cross-border digital EMI platforms (including Wise, Revolut Business, Currencycloud, and Airwallex) are not licensed by the PBOC/SAFE as Chinese domestic banks and cannot host the WFOE's statutory SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account or RMB settlement account. They are not substitutes for onshore banking because all foreign-exchange receipts and payments originating from or receivable by a mainland WFOE must be channelled through banks that hold a valid foreign-exchange business licence. Overseas EMIs may only be used outside mainland China as parent-company side treasury interfaces or ancillary supply-chain payment tools, but their balances cannot flow directly into the WFOE treasury without an onshore receiving bank. Relying on overseas fintech EMIs creates KYC/AML gaps and would violate SAFE's centralized transaction-monitoring and reporting rules, so domestic Tier-1 commercial banks remain mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Local File must be prepared if annual related-party transactions exceed CNY 200 million for tangible property transfers, CNY 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transfers, or CNY 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is additionally required for a group whose aggregate annual related-party transactions equal or exceed CNY 2 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although the formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) of offshore jurisdictions do not directly apply to mainland China, the PRC CIT Law contains a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47, and the tax authorities apply substance requirements through the Beneficial Ownership test for treaty relief and the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis for transfer pricing. A WFOE must demonstrate real premises, active employees, genuine management decisions, and commercial substance in China. Merely registering a China entity while the actual managerial and risk-bearing functions reside offshore will trigger recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and penalty exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47), SAT Notice No. 2 (2009), and Tax Administration Law (TAL) Article 36: the tax authority may recharacterize the transactions, disallow deductions, deny treaty benefits, impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and charge daily late surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly orchestrate paper-entity arrangements may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20, making them personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and creditor damages. Departure block (travel ban) may be imposed under Tax Administration Law Article 44 for unpaid tax in excess of routine thresholds. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 205 for false VAT invoicing or Article 201 for tax evasion may result in imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years and a criminal fine.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Articles 41-44 and TAL Articles 36-37, the tax authority is empowered to make a transfer pricing adjustment by applying the arm's length principle, adjusting the taxable income, imposing a 25% CIT rate on the uplift, and levying a penalty equal to 50%-500% of the underpaid tax plus interest. A special anti-avoidance adjustment may also arise if the arrangement is considered lacking commercial substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or direct the underpricing are exposed to personal liability: as responsible officers they may be fined personally under TAL; civil liability for the corporation's tax debt if they gave fraudulent guarantees; a travel ban is available if the WFOE fails to pay after adjustment; and deliberate tax evasion exceeding the criminal threshold triggers Criminal Law Article 201 imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 69: the WFOE (as withholding agent) is liable to pay the under-withheld amount, an administrative fine of 50%-300% of the amount not withheld, and, if the amount is not paid after demand, the tax authority may attach bank deposits or enforce against assets. The foreign recipient may also be reassessed directly if the withholding agent fails.

Director personal liability: Failure to withhold is a strict statutory liability of the legal representative and financial officer if they participated in or consented to the distribution. Director personal assets may be seized if corporate veil piercing applies; repeated or willful non-withholding is classified as a criminal act under Criminal Law Article 201 (tax evasion) and Article 211 (unit crime), subjecting directors to imprisonment of up to 7 years, criminal fines, and a personal exit ban until the tax is fully settled.
