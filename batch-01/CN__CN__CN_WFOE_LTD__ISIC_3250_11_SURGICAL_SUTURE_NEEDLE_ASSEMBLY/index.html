Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the online electronic portal is the China Electronic Tax Administration (China e-Tax) platform operated by STA, accessible through the official tax authority website and local State Tax Service bureaus, with all taxpayer registration, filing, VAT invoice, and export refund procedures conducted through the e-Tax portal and the national electronic invoice service platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Chinese Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) assigned by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at company incorporation; no separate VAT ID is issued. The procedure is as follows: (1) incorporate the WFOE with SAMR and obtain the business license bearing the USCC; (2) within 30 days of incorporation, complete taxpayer registration through the China e-Tax portal by matching the USCC, legal representative identity, articles of association, registered address, business scope, banking details, and finance officer information; (3) after successful registration, the entity is automatically classified as an enterprise income taxpayer, VAT taxpayer, and potential export VAT refund claimant; (4) for a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that derives China-source income, apply separately to the competent tax authority for non-resident enterprise tax registration using the foreign non-resident enterprise registration procedure, obtaining a taxpayer identification number or social credit code as required for withholding, VAT filing, and treaty relief purposes; (5) all subsequent VAT invoices must display the USCC as the VAT taxpayer identification number; (6) maintain updated tax registration files with STA for any changes in legal representative, address, bank account, business scope, or cross-border trade status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a China resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; shareholders are not pass-through entitled, and China does not apply a territorial scope exemption for the WFOE's global income except as specifically exempted by law or treaty; the WFOE is not located in a tax-exempt free port or special tax-exempt zone for income tax purposes, although certain regional preferential policies may apply where lawfully introduced.

Available election forms

None; China does not operate a US-style check-the-box entity classification election nor does it provide a statutory hybrid mismatch election for WFOEs. The WFOE's tax classification is determined by law as a resident enterprise. For VAT purposes, an eligible small-scale taxpayer may elect to be treated as a general VAT taxpayer if statutory accounting and registration conditions are satisfied, and taxpayers may claim applicable tax treaty benefits or preferential tax treatments through formal filing with STA, but no entity-level classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the statutory threshold above which mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is required; any entity engaged in taxable business activity in China must complete tax registration regardless of turnover, and businesses with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 are generally categorized as small-scale VAT taxpayers subject to simplified VAT collection rates (currently 3% standard reduced to 1% under temporary policy where available); export-oriented manufacturing WFOEs with export goods are ordinarily required or entitled to register as general VAT taxpayers to claim input VAT refunds on exported goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply 0% VAT export zero-rating on surgical suture needle assembly goods if the following statutory criteria are met: (1) the enterprise is a general VAT taxpayer; (2) the goods are actually sold to a non-resident overseas buyer; (3) the goods are physically exported out of China customs territory under an approved export customs declaration; (4) the goods are legal for export and, where applicable, satisfy medical device export licensing and product registration requirements; (5) export sales proceeds are received from abroad through designated Chinese banks and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for export proceeds verification; (6) the export price, quantity, product code, and destination are accurately declared; and (7) all input VAT attributable to the exported goods is accurately allocated and substantiated for refund or exemption. Qualifying offshore digital services, if separately supplied by the WFOE, must also meet China's cross-border service zero-rating rules requiring non-resident recipients, genuine offshore benefits, and foreign exchange or equivalent consideration collected in accordance with SAFE rules.

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the national electronic invoice service platform, under which VAT invoices must be issued in real time through STA-approved channels. General VAT taxpayers must transmit invoice data electronically to STA upon issuance through the China e-Tax portal or integrated enterprise resource planning APIs; fully digitalized electronic invoices are mandatory for eligible taxpayers and carry a unique invoice number and electronic fiscal seal confirming STA clearance; paper VAT invoices must be issued through fiscal tax-control equipment integrated with the STA network. The system enforces anti-fraud controls through automated matching of invoice data with customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, and payment flows; each invoice must be digitally signed and accepted by STA before delivery to the buyer, and non-compliant issuance can trigger invoice blocking, red-flag audit, and suspension of export refund rights.

STA, customs, and SAFE may jointly audit the entire export transaction chain, including production capacity, raw material procurement, VAT input invoices, customs declaration values, logistics documents, insurance, payment flows, and foreign exchange repatriation timing. If zero-rated export treatment cannot be substantiated with the required documentation or if any declaration is found false, the tax authority will claw back any VAT refund or exemption already granted, assess the goods as domestic taxable sales at the standard VAT rate (currently 13%), impose late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day from the due date, and may apply fines under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection; fraudulent export tax refund claims are punishable by recovery of the fraudulently obtained refund and a fine of up to two times the refund amount, with criminal liability under Chinese law for serious cases; the enterprise may additionally lose its status as an export VAT refund filer, be downgraded in tax credit rating, and face customs compliance penalties, suspension of export privileges, and inclusion in cross-border tax risk monitoring systems.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated convertibility framework. The CNY is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, provided genuine documentation such as contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax payment records underpin the FX application. Capital account transactions remain subject to case-by-case approval, registration, and filing through SAFE. A WFOE may convert injected foreign currency registered capital into RMB only after verified filing of the SAFE Foreign Currency Capital Account; conversions must comply with actual-use rules, the negative-list screening under the Foreign Investment Law, and the enterprise's approved business scope. Profit remittance is permitted after the annual statutory audit, issuance of an audit opinion, confirmation of tax clearance, and compliance with statutory surplus reserve requirements, conditioned on audited financial statements, board-approved dividend distribution, and tax settlement documentation. There is no forced surrender ratio for export proceeds at the point of conversion; enterprises may retain export FX in their domestic Foreign Currency Settlement Account, but all transactions must be reconciled through SAFE's information-monitoring platform. The operative impediment is not current-account convertibility itself but the administrative documentation burden and bank scrutiny around capital-account validation, especially when the ultimate shareholder is an offshore holding entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The People's Bank of China and SAFE impose no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; however, all export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic Foreign Currency Settlement Account within 180 days of the due collection date unless otherwise approved. The exporter may either retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert them into CNY at any time, subject to presentation of truthful trade documents including customs declarations, shipping documents, invoice and contract to the settlement bank through SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), as delegated by the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms are not a legally viable primary clearing rail for an onshore Chinese WFOE. SAFE and PBOC authorise only licensed domestic commercial banks and their cross-border banking subsidiaries to execute capital-account injections, FX conversion, and trade settlement for Chinese resident enterprises. EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business may only support auxiliary treasury activities outside China, but cannot hold the WFOE's onshore CNY accounts, cannot receive registered capital injections under FDI rules, and cannot perform SAFE filing or capital verification. Using an offshore EMI as the main operating account would constitute unlicensed cross-border settlement and expose the WFOE to regulatory sanctions, facility freeze, and loss of foreign-exchange credibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE's registered capital injection must be wired from the overseas investing entity's bank account in the exact corporate name of the investor to the SAFE-designated Foreign Currency Capital Account held at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. Every inbound SWIFT wire must include the unique SAFE FDI registration number, the stated purpose codes aligned with the Foreign Investment Information Report, and the remitter's relationship to the WFOE. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the purpose, the registered capital schedule as filed with the market regulator, and the absence of cross-border financing restrictions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms are not a legally viable primary clearing rail for an onshore Chinese WFOE. SAFE and PBOC authorise only licensed domestic commercial banks and their cross-border banking subsidiaries to execute capital-account injections, FX conversion, and trade settlement for Chinese resident enterprises. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when any one of the following related-party annual transaction thresholds is exceeded: RMB 200 million for tangible property, RMB 10 million for intangible property, RMB 100 million for financial assets, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. The annual Related-Party Transactions Disclosure Form must be attached to the CIT return whenever any related-party transaction exists during the year. A Country-by-Country Report threshold applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion, approximately EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations filing system of the type used in the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the beneficial-ownership test in SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 9 impose an equivalent economic-substance doctrine. For a manufacturing WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities include employee hiring and payroll in mainland China, operating the suture and needle assembly production lines, performing quality-assurance testing, managing inventory, negotiating contracts and making the strategic decisions that generate profit. If these activities are performed abroad and the WFOE acts only as an invoice-issuing shell, the tax authority may disregard the entity, deny treaty benefits and attribute the corresponding income to the non-resident enterprise.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Falsification of Books and Invoices

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law: the tax authority recovers the unpaid tax and late-payment interest and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Article 201 of the Criminal Law: where the evasion amount is large and exceeds 10% of taxable income, the responsible persons face criminal fines, imprisonment of up to three years, or three to seven years for especially large amounts.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, chief financial officer and directors who approved the false filing are personally liable for restitution of the tax loss. The court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 23 of the PRC Company Law and attach personal bank accounts and property. The tax authority also places the legal representative on the serious-tax-violation blacklist, triggering a travel ban, denial of exit from China and disqualification from acting as a director of any new company.
Abusive Treaty Shopping / Shell WFOE / Lack of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and its implementation rules authorise a special tax adjustment that disregards the shell structure. SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 9 denies treaty benefits where the recipient is not the beneficial owner; the full statutory 10% withholding tax is reimposed on the original payment, with late-payment interest and surcharges from the date the tax was due.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the artificial conduit structure are jointly and severally liable with the WFOE for the unpaid withholding tax if the WFOE's assets are insufficient, through Article 23 of the PRC Company Law. Criminal liability may arise under Article 201 of the Criminal Law where the under-withholding was intentional. The foreign shareholder and directors face blacklisting from future market entry, wage garnishment, travel bans and civil asset-restoration proceedings.
Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance and Deficient TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law requires arm's-length pricing and allows the tax authority to reallocate profit. The absence of contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer and exposes the WFOE to documentation penalties plus special interest adjustments. Where deliberate false pricing is proven, Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law applies with 50% to 500% penalties.

Director personal liability: If the transfer-pricing adjustment creates a tax shortfall that the WFOE cannot pay, the tax authority may hold the controlling shareholders and directors jointly liable when they personally participated in and profited from the pricing decisions. Personal assets can be reached through veil piercing, and the responsible finance director may face criminal tax-evasion liability for intentional fraudulent pricing. Persistent non-payment triggers the travel blacklist and exit ban on the legal representative.
