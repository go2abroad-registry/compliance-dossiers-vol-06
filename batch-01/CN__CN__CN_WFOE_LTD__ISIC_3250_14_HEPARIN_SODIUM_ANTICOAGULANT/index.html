Competent tax authority

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered general VAT taxpayer may claim the zero rate for physically exported goods where the goods are customs-supervised and declared for export out of China, the exporter holds valid export registration, the export goods are not subject to export restrictions, and foreign-currency export proceeds are repatriated within the required time frame. Qualifying cross-border digital or offshore services may also be zero-rated where the service is consumed wholly outside China, the recipient is a non-resident enterprise, and contemporaneous documents prove the offshore supply and foreign-currency settlement.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (export declaration submitted through the China Customs Integrated Clearance System)
  • Commercial invoice and statutory VAT invoice for the exported goods
  • Packing list
  • Ocean/marine bill of lading, air waybill, or international consignment note evidencing export movement
  • Export sales contract/agreement
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher or export-proceeds reconciliation record/SAFE monitoring record evidencing inward repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export tax rebate declaration and supporting input-VAT credit documents (where an export VAT refund is claimed)

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA e-invoice platform or authorised tax control devices. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are transmitted in near-real-time to the national VAT electronic invoice service platform; special VAT invoices carry an anti-counterfeiting tax-control code and are reconciled through the State Taxation Administration's invoice verification/clearance system. Taxpayers using enterprise invoicing systems must interface through STA-approved APIs or channels.

Export zero-rating and refund positions are audited using customs data, logistics traces, bank receipts, and invoice-chain analytics. Where the export declaration or foreign-exchange repatriation does not substantiate the zero-rated claim, the competent STA will claw back the refunded/zero-rated VAT with late payment interest, impose administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the tax amount involved under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, suspend export tax-rebate eligibility, and may initiate criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. For fraudulent or intentionally concealed claims, there is no statutory limitation period for recovery.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an inorganic current-account convertibility system under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE Operational Guidelines, while maintaining strict capital-account controls. The CNY is convertible for trade-related payments, service fees, dividends, and intra-group royalties supported by verified underlying contracts and customs declarations; however, all cross-border receipts and payments must be validated through the National Foreign Exchange Administration Information System and banks must apply the 'truthfulness and consistency' review standard. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in heparin sodium active pharmaceutical ingredient fractionation, export proceeds from overseas buyers must be collected in foreign currency or RMB under cross-border trade settlement rules, routed into a domestic foreign-currency current account, and may be retained or converted only in accordance with SAFE guidance; conversion for non-trade or working-capital purposes is restricted. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is permissible in the form of declared dividends, but the WFOE must first complete annual statutory audit, file audited financial statements, settle CIT at the entity level, distribute dividends in accordance with the company law, and provide foreign-exchange registration forms, board resolutions, tax payment certificates, and auditor reports to the settlement bank under SAFE Circular 3 and related operational notices. Free capital mobility is not available at the capital-account level; foreign direct investment capital must be injected via the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system, deposited into a capital account at a designated bank, and used only for approved business purposes in accordance with the Negative List and the 'capital-account income use positive list' mechanism. Continued regulatory monitoring applies to early repatriation of capital, shareholder loans, secondary capital increases, and the conversion of retained earnings into distributable reserves, all of which are handled under SAFE or PBOC filing obligations. In practice, dividend remittance, import settlement, service fee payments, and trade financing are operationally convertible if documentation is complete, while portfolio inflows, offshore borrowing beyond quota, and speculative FX transactions require additional approvals or filing with SAFE and PBOC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished the mandatory forced surrender of export proceeds; however, under SAFE Entity Periodic Reporting rules, an exporter must monitor and verify settlement of export receivables within the customs declaration period and complete trade foreign-exchange receipt verification in the SAFE system. For goods exports, the receipt should generally occur within 90 days or within the applicable customs-conferred payment term; where the cross-border receipt date exceeds the listed customs declaration interval, the WFOE must submit a deferred receipt registration and an underlying trade justification to SAFE. There is no fixed statutory percentage requiring conversion of export foreign currency into CNY; the entity may retain the full export proceeds in a foreign-currency settlement account. Nevertheless, banks will assess the WFOE's currency pattern, liquidity needs, and the validity of current-account payment requests; any sale of foreign exchange must be completed through a licensed bank using a genuine trade background document (contract, invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, and verification filing). For RMB-denominated cross-border trade settlement under the Cross-Border RMB Settlement framework, no conversion is applicable, but the same document verification and filing obligations apply to the transaction record.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary banking rail for a China-domiciled WFOE. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Neat can support ancillary collection and vendor payment flows in certain markets, but they are not licensed to provide local domestic CNY accounts, RMB clearing, or statutory FA/SAFE filing services within mainland China. The WFOE’s core treasury and cross-border payments must be handled by a Chinese bank licensed by PBOC/SAFE. Furthermore, pharmaceutical API manufacturing and trading operations require an entity with a valid Pharmaceutical Manufacturing License, GMP-compliant facilities, and a documented supply-chain trail; using a non-bank fintech as the primary deposit-taker would degrade bank KYC transparency and trigger reversal of approvals at the time of annual inspection. Accordingly, any EMI should be used merely as a supplementary collection tool for low-value, non-regulated overseas invoicing or for payment to international vendors where Chinese bank rails are less efficient, and never as a substitute for the statutory settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must remit registered capital from its own overseas bank account directly to the WFOE’s FDI capital-account at a SAFE-designated bank, using the SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent cross-border electronic transfer. The remittance purpose should be described as 'Registered capital injection of a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise under the Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China', and the beneficiary bank must match the FX registration certificate or the Foreign Investment Information Reporting record. The bank will post the receipt to the capital account and file the transaction in SAFE’s capital-account information system; if the paid-in capital is denominated in USD, the bank uses the onshore reference rate to record the RMB equivalent, but no automatic conversion is required. The WFOE must within a prescribed window (typically one month but depending on local SAFE branch) complete the Foreign Investment Information Reporting update and, if applicable, obtain a capital-account confirmation letter. The company can then convert capital into RMB only in line with the positive-list usage principles, providing a payment purpose statement for each conversion (e.g., purchase of equipment, procurement of raw heparin crude, employee wages, lease, or royalties). For a production WFOE, SAFE may require the bank to verify the authenticity of the underlying business contract before releasing the RMB conversion. The L/C should be issued by a top-tier overseas bank and advised through the WFOE’s Chinese settlement bank, with documents comprising commercial invoice, bill of lading, phytosanitary or health certificate if required, certificate of analysis, packing list, and the export customs declaration. In the ordinary course, the WFOE will instruct its bank to negotiate the documents and receive payment in USD or EUR under the L/C, or through a SWIFT MT103 transfer from the buyer on open-account terms. For cross-border supplier payments, especially for imported crude heparin, filter aids, chemicals, and laboratory consumables, the WFOE may use an MT103, an outward documentary collection, or a standby L/C as guarantee. All outbound payments require the bank to check the goods classification code and the business documentation; for services, a service trade tax certificate and valid contract must be presented. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary banking rail for a China-domiciled WFOE. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Neat can support ancillary collection and vendor payment flows in certain markets, but they are not licensed to provide local domestic CNY accounts, RMB clearing, or statutory FA/SAFE filing services within mainland China. The WFOE’s core treasury and cross-border payments must be handled by a Chinese bank licensed by PBOC/SAFE. Furthermore, pharmaceutical API manufacturing and trading operations require an entity with a valid Pharmaceutical Manufacturing License, GMP-compliant facilities, and a documented supply-chain trail; using a non-bank fintech as the primary deposit-taker would degrade bank KYC transparency and trigger reversal of approvals at the time of annual inspection. Accordingly, any EMI should be used merely as a supplementary collection tool for low-value, non-regulated overseas invoicing or for payment to international vendors where Chinese bank rails are less efficient, and never as a substitute for the statutory settlement bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party transaction disclosure (Form A000000/AD200) is mandatory when the aggregate amount of related-party transactions exceeds ¥20 million (tangible assets) or ¥10 million (financial assets) or ¥10 million (intangible assets) or ¥40 million (other transactions). Local File must be prepared if any one threshold is exceeded; Master File is required when the global consolidated revenue of the multinational group exceeds ¥5 billion, or other specified conditions are met.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form principle; transactions lacking genuine commercial or economic substance may be re-characterized. The PRC CFC rules under Article 45 of the EIT Law tax non-resident entities without reasonable business purposes. For the heparin manufacturing WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include procurement, fractionation, quality control, R&D, and logistics management. The WFOE must maintain real operational staff, plant, and decision-making in China to avoid profit attribution to a low-tax offshore entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Art. 49 and Tax Collection Law Art. 63, the taxpayer must repay underpaid tax, pay daily interest (0.05% per day), and incur a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid amount; VAT fraud also triggers Criminal Law Art. 205 with penalty up to life imprisonment for issuing false special VAT invoices.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors may be jointly and severally liable to compensate for corporate tax losses if they directed or approved the fraudulent invoicing; personal assets may be frozen; travel bans (exit restriction) under Tax Collection Law Art. 44; criminal directors face up to 7 years imprisonment for tax evasion (Criminal Law Art. 201) and potentially up to 10 years for money laundering if proceeds were repatriated.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Art. 41 and the State Council Implementation Regulation on Special Tax Adjustments (Order No. 649), the tax authority may reassess taxable income, impose interest with a 5% annual surcharge, and levy a penalty of 50% up to 5 times the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Law Art. 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly concert in underpricing can be held personally liable for the additional tax and penalties under corporate veil-piercing doctrine (Company Law Art. 20); personal assets may be traced, exit bans imposed, and criminal charges brought under Criminal Law Art. 201 (up to 7 years imprisonment) if the transfer pricing scheme constitutes deliberate tax evasion.
Abusive Profit Repatriation through Shell Companies / False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Art. 45 (CFC rules), the passive income may be included in the Chinese shareholder's taxable income; DTA relief will be denied and underpaid tax recovered with 50%-5× penalty under Tax Collection Law Art. 63. Foreign exchange violations trigger SAFE penalties up to 30% of the illegal remittance amount (max RMB 1 million) under Regulations on Foreign Exchange Control; severe instances trigger Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering) with confiscation and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors/beneficial owners personally liable for unpaid taxes and civil restitution; travel bans and blacklisting by SAFE may be imposed; criminal liability for money laundering carries up to 10 years imprisonment, and if the scheme entails fraud, up to life imprisonment under aggravated circumstances.
