Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to an ordinary Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in medical device assembly. The shareholder may state the registered capital in the Articles of Association, but under Article 47 of the revised Company Law the full subscribed capital must be paid in within five years from the issuance of the business license. No blocked escrow account is required by the central registry, but contributed capital must be deposited in a dedicated company bank account in China and documented for future capital verification or enforcement by creditors.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the local competent tax bureau at the WFOE's registered address; national online platform: e-Tax Electronic Taxpayer Portal administered by the STA.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign parent establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in Mainland China: (1) register the company with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) through the national enterprise registration system; the AMR issues a Business License incorporating the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which by legal function is the entity's unified TIN/VAT ID. (2) After license issuance, tax registration is automatically synchronized with the STA under the multi-certificate integration reform; the WFOE must nevertheless log into the e-Tax Electronic Taxpayer Portal, complete the supplementary tax registration details (e.g., tax payer type, finance and accounting system, bank account information) within the statutory 30-day period, and file the initial return as scheduled. (3) The WFOE applies for general VAT taxpayer status either mandatorily upon expected annual turnover over RMB 5,000,000 or voluntarily to issue special VAT invoices and claim input VAT credits. (4) For a foreign parent with no Chinese permanent establishment, China does not issue a separate local resident TIN; a non-resident tax registration number is obtained through the withholding agent on the China-sourced income side. For the WFOE, the USCC serves as the only TIN/VAT ID for income tax, VAT, customs registration (export registration number linked to the USCC), and foreign exchange filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of assessable turnover in any continuous 12-month period, triggering mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold a small-scale taxpayer may use simplified levy rates, although exporters and input-credit claimants generally elect general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of tangible goods by the WFOE from Mainland China to customers outside China qualifies for 0% VAT under the VAT export rebate and exemption regime when the goods physically leave China under a valid customs export declaration, the sales is a genuine commercial transaction at arm's length, the proceeds are repatriated to a bank account of the WFOE in China in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules, and the export receipts are reported in the periodic VAT/export refund filing. For the electronic patient monitor hardware, there is no domestic exemption limiting zero-rating if all legal export criteria are met; the WFOE can recover or offset related input VAT through the standard 'exemption, credit, refund' mechanism. Qualifying offshore services rendered to non-resident customers may also be zero-rated or exempted where the services are supplied and consumed entirely outside China and supported by the required contract and overseas payment evidence, but the current scope is predominantly goods export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - the electronic export goods declaration accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading (or equivalent international transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / export foreign-exchange receipt confirmation from a licensed Chinese bank
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales contract
  • Packing list
  • VAT export rebate/exemption application transmitted through the STA electronic export tax rebate system
  • Any certificate of origin or regulated medical-device export documentation required for the electronic patient monitor

China mandates digital e-invoice issuance through the STA nationwide Electronic Invoice Service Platform. General VAT taxpayers are required to issue Special VAT Invoices (formally validated digital invoices) through the platform; invoice data is transmitted to the STA Golden-Tax system in near-real time for validation, anti-fraud stamping and input credit authentication. Fully digital non-physical 'electronic invoices' are the standard default for new taxpayers, and high-volume taxpayers must connect via approved API/e-interface or web-service filing, with automatic compression and anomaly controls. No standalone tax stamp is used; clearance is electronic and records are retained for 10 years by taxpayers.

The STA and Customs jointly cross-match export declarations, logistics data, bank FX receipts and supplier invoices. Erroneous or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to retroactive clawback of all VAT exemption/refund amounts plus interest, late-fee surcharge of 0.05% per day on overdue tax, and administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Severe cases involving forged export declarations or false invoicing may constitute criminal tax fraud, leading to criminal fines, confiscation and imprisonment; customs may also impose monetary penalties, revoke the exporter registration and suspend the enterprise's export-rebate qualification. Documentation must be retained at least 5 years, but STA requires 10-year e-invoice retention for audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated convertibility framework under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations. Current account foreign exchange transactions, such as trade in goods, services, and repatriation of lawfully earned profits, are generally fully convertible, requiring only documentary verification at the bank counter in the form of invoices, customs declarations, contracts, and settlement vouchers. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment contributions, shareholder loans, acquisition consideration, and outbound investments, remain subject to strict SAFE approvals, registrations, and filings. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise receiving foreign equity injection must first complete the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) information registration through the bank, and the capital must be paid into a designated foreign exchange capital account or RMB capital account. The funds can be converted into RMB under the receipt and use principle, which requires that the actual conversion aligns with the declared purpose, such as purchase of equipment, raw materials, wages, rent, or repayment of legitimate domestic loans. However, capital account funds cannot be directly used for entrustment loans, securities investment, or investment in properties beyond the business scope. Profit remittance requires an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificates, a board resolution to distribute dividends, and evidence that previous losses have been fully covered. A 10% withholding tax applies to dividends remitted to non-resident corporate shareholders unless a tax treaty rate is available. Surplus capital repatriation upon liquidation requires separate liquidation procedures and tax clearances. Overall, the RMB is convertible on current account but comprehensively administered on the capital account, with practical operational hurdles in document collection, bank discretion, and timing delays.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts under the current PRC regime, as the forced foreign exchange surrender system was removed in 2010. All goods export proceeds must be repatriated to the exporter's domestic designated foreign exchange account or RMB account through a SAFE-monitored bank. The receipt must be reported in the SAFE trade monitoring system and matched to the customs export declaration within the underlying payment term agreed in the sales contract; for ordinary open-account trade, funds should generally arrive within 360 days from customs registration, while deferred payment terms beyond 180 days require a formal deferred trade receivables registration with the bank. The exporter may retain the foreign currency in a genuine current-account foreign exchange account or convert it to RMB at negotiated bank rates, subject to verification of the transaction真实性. Banks may flag overdue receipts as anomalies and trigger enhanced due diligence if the exporter fails to provide evidence of collection within the declared term.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Revolut Business are not licensed as banks in mainland China and cannot open or maintain the WFOE's primary FDI capital account or RMB basic settlement account. They may engage in partnerships with mainland licensed banks for payment processing, but their account and clearing infrastructure is not domestically recognised for SAFE reporting, customs matching, or capital verification. Therefore, cross-border EMI viability for this WFOE is Low to Medium: Low for statutory capital flows and profit repatriation because the account must be bank-funded and SAFE-registered; Medium for ancillary cross-border trade payments if fully routed through a domestic licensed correspondent bank with proper trade documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

To inject inbound capital, the overseas shareholder must first complete SAFE FDI registration for the WFOE through the designated domestic bank. The remitting bank sends a SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment capital contribution (typically 622012) along with the WFOE's FDI registration number or investment name. The receiving bank credits the foreign exchange capital account only after validating the remittance instruction against SAFE's registration and reviewing the underlying shareholder resolution. After the funds arrive, a certified public accountant may issue a capital verification report if required for the business license amendment, and the company must use the funds strictly in accordance with declared purposes, including purchases of electronic components, employee wages, factory leases, calibration equipment, and operational expenses, following the receipt-and-use principle.Outbound trade disbursements for imported components will be cleared via SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers, commercial documentary letters of credit opened by the domestic bank, or documentary collections, each matched with the customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase contract.
