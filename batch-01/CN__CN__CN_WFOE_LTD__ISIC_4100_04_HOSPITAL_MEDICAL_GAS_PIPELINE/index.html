Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office or commercial mailbox arrangements are not permissible as the sole registered office for a PRC WFOE engaged in hospital medical gas pipeline installation and hospital engineering EPC. The Company Law of the People's Republic of China requires the registered domicile to be the location of the principal place of business, and the market regulation authority, municipal tax authority, and licensing authorities require a physical lease, utility records, office equipment, personnel presence, and storage of corporate books and project records. A commercial mailbox may be used only as an ancillary correspondence address and never for licensed medical gas engineering activities. PRC banks implementing PBOC AML/KYC rules will reject a WFOE using only a virtual office because the address cannot be physically verified and industry licensing demands operational substance. The local tax authority may list the company as an abnormal operation or abnormal tax status if the registered address is a mailbox only, which blocks invoice issuance, fund payments, project bidding, and license renewal. Therefore, a committed physical office lease evidenced by a filed lease agreement is mandatory for this entity.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Under the PRC Company Law (2023), there is no statutory minimum registered capital for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company; the registered capital is set in the Articles of Association. However, for a limited liability company, shareholders must pay subscribed capital in full within five years after establishment unless law or the Foreign Investment Negative List requires a shorter period. For a medical gas pipeline installation contractor, the company must demonstrate registered capital and net assets satisfying the applicable Construction Enterprise Qualification Standard before obtaining the construction qualification. No state escrow or blocked capital account is required for ordinary registered capital; foreign exchange remittance must be made through a foreign exchange capital account in accordance with SAFE regulations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearances include construction site fire protection design review, completion fire acceptance or filing, and a temporary fire safety opinion from the local Fire and Rescue Department for site sheds and storage areas. All copper piping and manifold brackets penetrating fire-rated walls must be installed with approved fire-stop sealants and supporting fire-resistance certificates. Work involving hot work, brazing and oxygen systems requires a hot-work permit, fire watch, gas cylinder segregation and emergency fire-fighting equipment. Occupational safety clearance is required under the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases and the Safety Production Law, including occupational hazard evaluation for copper brazing, fume extraction, gas cylinder handling, pressure testing and medical gas purity verification. Structural load inspections must confirm that manifold room supports, seismic bracing and terminal device mounts comply with the applicable building code before patient-facing systems are energized.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident enterprise; the WFOE is taxed on worldwide income at the 25% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through regime is available to shareholders).

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box classification election for a WFOE limited-liability company and no hybrid mismatch election is available. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; all entities with taxable activities must complete tax registration upon establishment regardless of turnover. A small-scale taxpayer below the threshold may use the simplified 3% collection rate, with temporary preferential reductions where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT requires physical export from China under a valid Customs export declaration, a sale to a non-resident buyer, and documentary evidence that foreign-currency consideration will be repatriated. For cross-border taxable services, 0% applies only where the service qualifies as an exported service under the STA cross-border services catalogue and is wholly used outside China. A medical gas pipeline installation service performed in China for a local hospital is a domestic 9% taxable service and cannot be zero-rated. Export of pipeline equipment as part of an EPC supply may be zero-rated if invoiced separately and exported under Customs supervision.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) as lodged with China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document issued by the carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the Electronic Tax Bureau fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) service platform or through government-certified invoice software integrated with the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoice issuance data is transmitted in real time to the tax authority; each invoice carries a unique digital serial number and encrypted stamp. Self-built systems must connect to the STA-approved invoice service API; no offline billing is permitted. Taxpayers must authenticate invoices on the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform before crediting input VAT. There is no separate fiscal stamp; the anti-fraud architecture is the mandatory real-time digital invoicing record.

If any zero-rating condition is not independently verifiable, the STA will disallow the 0% treatment, require VAT payment with late-payment surcharge, and claw back any refund already paid. For false export declarations or fabricated repatriation documentation, the penalty is a fine of not less than 50% and up to 5 times the refunded tax, placement on the taxation negative trust list, loss of export refund facilitation status, and criminal prosecution under the PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund, which carries imprisonment from 5 years to life and additional monetary penalties in severe cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with partial convertibility. Current-account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, are substantially convertible: once a bank verifies the underlying trade agreement, commercial invoice, customs declarations, and tax settlement vouchers, cross-border RMB or foreign-currency transfers may be executed. Capital-account transactions remain heavily constrained: a WFOE can receive registered paid-in capital only through a SAFE-registered foreign direct investment (FDI) code, with each inward remittance matching a specific capital account purpose declaration. Funds so received must be credited to a dedicated RMB or foreign-currency capital settlement account and cannot be converted for unrestricted use; conversion and usage require SAFE-prescribed documentation and, in certain cases, a prior bank compliance review. Export proceeds are conceptually repatriated to China, but no mandatory surrender ratio currently applies; exporters may retain foreign-currency earnings in a current-account foreign exchange account or convert to RMB at prevailing market rates. Profit repatriation from the WFOE to its foreign shareholder requires board resolutions, audited financial statements, the annual corporate income tax filing proof, and a statement that all statutory withholding taxes have been discharged. In practice, high-value cross-border transfers above USD 500,000 per batch will attract extra documentary scrutiny, making meticulous transaction twin-tracking essential. Currency autonomy is limited; long-term RMB liquidity cannot be legally fixed by the corporate treasurer without onshore hedging prior approval. These factors create a treasury environment in which bank-relationship quality and documentary discipline supersede financial technology flexibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exported goods proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic current-account foreign-currency bank account within 180 days of customs declaration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may elect to retain the foreign currency in a current-account FX account or sell it to a bank at the PBOC central-parity-based market rate. All repatriated proceeds must be entered in the SAFE trade monitoring system and reconciled with customs export data under the total-amount trade foreign-exchange protocol, with supporting proof of logistics, inspection, and settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not permitted to independently service a PRC-incorporated WFOE as its primary bank. PBOC regulations on payment institutions require a licensed RMB payment license for any local account-based service, and offshore EMIs usually hold only a foreign money service business license. They may not establish cash-based settlement accounts on behalf of a Chinese entity, nor access the RMB clearing system on a direct participant basis. At best, a fintech EMI can act as an ancillary payment/e-collection node for international inflows when partnered with a licensed bank and only for current-account transactions under strict bank oversight. However, the capital injection and dividend repatriation must travel through a SAFE-registered bank account. Therefore, treasury architecture should treat fintech EMIs as operational convenience tools with a Low viability rating, not as a substitute for Tier-1 domestic bank rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound shareholder remittances for the WFOE must be wired using SWIFT MT103 to the SAFE-designated capital account. The remitting instruction must carry the SAFE FDI registration code under purpose code '801' for on-shore foreign direct investment, with field 70 and field 72 being fully populated with the registration number and investment contract reference. The funds are automatically captured by the bank's foreign-exchange reporting system; once credited, they are held in the capital settlement account. The WFOE must then engage a certified Chinese public accountant to perform a capital verification report ('Yantu') using actual bank slips and deposit certificates, and file that report with the Administration of Industry. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not permitted to independently service a PRC-incorporated WFOE as its primary bank. PBOC regulations on payment institutions require a licensed RMB payment license for any local account-based service, and offshore EMIs usually hold only a foreign money service business license. They may not establish cash-based settlement accounts on behalf of a Chinese entity, nor access the RMB clearing system on a direct participant basis. At best, a fintech EMI can act as an ancillary payment/e-collection node for international inflows when partnered with a licensed bank and only for current-account transactions under strict bank oversight. However, the capital injection and dividend repatriation must travel through a SAFE-registered bank account. Therefore, treasury architecture should treat fintech EMIs as operational convenience tools with a Low viability rating, not as a substitute for Tier-1 domestic bank rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is statutorily required if a Chinese enterprise has annual related-party transactions exceeding: CNY 200,000,000 for tangible asset transfers, CNY 100,000,000 for intangible asset transfers, CNY 100,000,000 for financial asset/loan transactions, or CNY 40,000,000 for service transactions. Master File preparation is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed CNY 1,500,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a Gulf-format Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the Tax Administration Law Article 120 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, a WFOE must demonstrate real business purpose and economic substance. For outbound treaty relief the non-resident beneficial owner must conduct core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as strategic management, risk assumption, and ownership decision-making in its residence jurisdiction. If the WFOE's shareholder is a shell conduit with no employees, office, or autonomy, treaty benefits are denied and a reassessment penalty of 50%-300% may be applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Reporting Income

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 201 and Tax Administration Law Article 63 impose liability for the evaded tax, a 0.05% daily surcharge, and an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid amount. Criminal fines equal 1-5 times the evaded tax; prison terms range up to 3 years for normal evasion and 3-7 years for huge amounts with evasion rates exceeding 30%.

Director personal liability: The director who personally directed the false invoicing scheme faces piercing of the corporate veil: personal accounts may be frozen, real property seized, and the individual placed on a cross-border exit ban. Civil joint liability for all evaded tax, interest, and penalties attaches, and criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Non-Documented Intercompany Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 48 and STA Announcement No. 6 of 2017, the tax authority can reassess income, add interest at the central bank benchmark rate plus 5%, impose a 50%-300% penalty on additional tax, and assess a 10% penalty for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation. The royalty payments may be wholly or partially disallowed.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally signed false intercompany service agreements and facilitated profit diversion are jointly and severally liable for restored tax and penalties. The corporate veil can be pierced where assets were transferred to offshore entities without fair consideration; directors may be placed on a travel ban, have personal claims and bank deposits frozen, and face criminal fraud under Criminal Law Article 224, with imprisonment of 3-7 years and fines.
Shell Conduit Treaty Abuse / False Beneficial Ownership Declaration

Statutory sanction: STA Announcement No. 9 of 2018 denies treaty relief; the full statutory 10% dividend WHT is collected, plus late interest and penalty of 50%-300% under Tax Administration Law Article 52. The conduit is recharacterised and the WFOE is treated as having failed to withhold, making the entire underpaid amount recoverable from the WFOE as agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the conduit and signed false beneficial ownership certifications are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, and penalties. Civil restitution applies to both WFOE and personal assets; an international travel ban and blacklisting are imposed; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 (coercive tax evasion) or Article 224 (contract fraud) attaches, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years and fines of 1-5 times the evaded tax.
