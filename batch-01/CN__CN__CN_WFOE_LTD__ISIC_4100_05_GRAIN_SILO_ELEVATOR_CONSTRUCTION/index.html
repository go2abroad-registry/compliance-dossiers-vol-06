Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No general statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE under the PRC Company Law; capital is subscribed in the Articles of Association and must be fully paid within the contribution period stated therein, not exceeding five years for a limited liability company under the 2023 revision. Foreign investors must remit paid-in capital through a domestic capital account and complete State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) foreign exchange registration. There is no blocked escrow account requirement at the national level; however, if the WFOE applies for construction enterprise qualification, MOHURD rules may require specified registered capital and paid-in capital thresholds evidenced by a capital verification report.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense and fire clearance require special fire protection design review and fire completion acceptance for the silo and aeration tower, with fire separation distances, egress, hydrant flow and smoke ventilation per GB 50016-2014 (2018 edition) and GB 50974-2014. Structural load inspection requires third-party verification of slip-form concrete wall thickness, verticality, silo hopper loads, seismic zone compliance and storage load provisions per GB 50011-2010, GB 50077-2017 and GB 50113-2005 before slip-form operations. Occupational clearance requires the work safety facility three-simultaneous design, construction and completion acceptance under the Law on Work Safety, special construction scheme review for slip-form platforms, confined-space entry in silos, aerial lift operations and emergency rescue planning, together with site fire prevention arrangements per GB 50720-2011.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; territorial administration is exercised by the provincial or municipal tax service bureau where the WFOE is legally registered; online electronic portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (eTax), accessible through the STA official website (www.chinatax.gov.cn) and provincial electronic tax service systems.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which constitutes the sole statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for Chinese tax purposes. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days after the Business License is issued, complete tax registration with the competent local STA tax service bureau through the Electronic Tax Bureau (eTax) using the USCC and the legal representative's real-name authenticated eTax account; the SAMR data is automatically transmitted to the STA, but the taxpayer must supplement its taxpayer profile. Step 3: In the tax registration, file the registered address, legal representative, business scope, banking account information, accounting system, financial records location, and expected VAT taxpayer classification. Step 4: Once the STA validates the submitted information, the taxpayer registration is activated; the USCC remains the TIN, and the STA system confirmation functions as the registration proof. Step 5: If projected annual taxable sales from construction services exceed RMB 5,000,000, apply for general VAT taxpayer registration; otherwise the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer or voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status if it satisfies accounting and internal control requirements. Step 6: Begin statutory filing obligations, including VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, and any applicable property and land taxes, through the Electronic Tax Bureau.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system has no check-the-box or disregarded-entity election for WFOEs; the entity is always a resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax at 25% on worldwide income. No hybrid mismatch election is available under Chinese domestic tax law. The only relevant election is the VAT taxpayer-status election: a small-scale taxpayer may voluntarily apply for general VAT taxpayer registration if it meets the accounting and control requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic taxpayer registration; every WFOE must obtain tax registration upon incorporation. The VAT general-taxpayer mandatory registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below that, small-scale taxpayer levy rates apply, while a general taxpayer is subject to the statutory VAT rates: 13% for goods, 9% for construction services, and 6% for many services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exported from China: (1) the exporter must be a VAT general taxpayer; (2) the goods must actually be exported and leave China Customs territory; (3) the sale must be made to a non-resident buyer under an export contract; (4) the export goods must be declared to customs using an Export Goods Customs Declaration Form; (5) export proceeds must be received and repatriated in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules; and (6) the VAT zero-rating must be supported by the export tax refund (exemption) declaration and relevant documentation. Construction services physically performed in China on domestic immovable property are not zero-rated and are subject to 9% VAT. Qualifying offshore services or digital services supplied cross-border may be treated as VAT-exempt or zero-rated only if they fall within the STA's published cross-border taxable services catalogue, are supplied to a non-resident, are consumed entirely outside China, and are supported by contracts, delivery evidence, and payment repatriation records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form / export Bill of Entry issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, including the customs release/clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or equivalent international transport document evidencing physical outbound shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Proceeds Settlement Certificate (FIRC or the exporting bank's export collection and settlement confirmation issued under SAFE regulations)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract/purchase order
  • VAT special invoices for purchased goods and services reflected in the exported goods (for input VAT rebate computation)

The PRC has a mandatory electronic invoicing system administered by the STA. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the STA Golden Tax system or the Electronic Invoice Service Platform, using the taxpayer's digital U-Key or the fully digitalized electronic invoice mechanism. Fully digitalized e-invoices are generated in real time, carry a unique electronic invoice number and STA digital signature/tax-control code, and are automatically verified by the tax authority without a physical clearance stamp. Input VAT must be confirmed by the taxpayer through the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform before crediting the VAT special invoice. High-volume taxpayers may connect via system-to-system API to upload invoice data; all invoice issuance is subject to real-time invoice quota monitoring and anti-fraud digital authentication.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs may deny or claw back VAT zero-rating or refunds if the export is unlawful, the goods do not actually leave China, the declared value is materially inaccurate, export proceeds are not repatriated within the prescribed deadline, or documentation is forged or incomplete. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, a fraudulent export tax refund is subject to recovery of the refunded amount and a penalty of one to five times the tax evaded. Tax authorities may retrospectively assess without limitation period in cases of tax evasion, fraud, or intentional non-declaration. Criminal liability may also be pursued under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for export tax refund fraud, including imprisonment and confiscation of property. The entity may additionally be downgraded in the STA taxpayer credit rating, lose its export-rebate qualification, and be listed under the joint discipline mechanism for ineligible taxpayers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime in which the RMB is convertible for current account items under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008). Capital account transactions remain strictly regulated, with prior approval or registration required for most inbound and outbound investments. For a WFOE, registered capital contributions are classified under capital account and must be injected through a corresponding foreign-exchange capital settlement account after completing SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration. Once in place, the funds may be converted into RMB only after bank verification of the underlying business purpose, using the 'truthfulness and matching' principle. Profit remittance is permitted on a current account basis, allowing a wholly foreign-owned enterprise to convert RMB profits into foreign currency and remit dividends abroad, provided that audited financial statements, a profit distribution resolution, and tax settlement certificates are presented to the designated bank. Surrender requirements are limited: exporters maintain the right to retain or sell current account receipts through designated banks, with no fixed mandatory conversion ratio. However, all trade receipts must be repatriated into domestic accounts within the agreed commercial payment term, generally not exceeding 90 days, and must be adequately documented with customs declarations. The real operational hurdle is not documentary convertibility but the transaction-level compliance burden and the risk classification imposed by the bank's KYC review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE trade-in-goods framework, there is no legal mandate for a fixed conversion ratio of export foreign currency receipts to RMB. A WFOE may retain up to 100% of its export proceeds in its current foreign-exchange account or sell them to a designated bank. However, Article 9 of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations requires that current account receipts be repatriated to China and not be arbitrarily deposited overseas. The bank will enforce a repatriation-within-due-term rule: for standard open-account export transactions, collection should occur within 90 days of the export customs declaration date; where the underlying contract provides for a longer payment term, prior SAFE approval or registration must be obtained, and the extension cannot exceed 180 days without special exemption. The export proceeds must be credited to the entity's current account with a designated foreign exchange bank immediately upon receipt, and the bank will file the transaction data with SAFE via the trade monitoring system on the same banking day.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated enforcement authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not viable as the sole or primary banking channel for a Chinese WFOE. Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the PBoC's licensing framework, only banks authorized by SAFE to conduct foreign exchange business may open corporate accounts for entities registered in China and process capital-account or current-account settlements. Offshore EMIs are typically not licensed to maintain local RMB or FX accounts for Chinese domestic entities, and any attempt to route inbound FDI or trade proceeds through such channels would contravene SAFE rules on direct reporting and documentary authentication. Accordingly, an EMI can be used only in a supporting role for ancillary transactions (e.g., paying overseas vendors below regulatory thresholds) but must never be used for capital injections, foreign exchange settlement, or dividend repatriation. The effective rating is 'not feasible' for primary use; legal recourse for treasury officers is limited to licensed commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent company must remit the registered capital in foreign currency via an MT103/SWIFT wire to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital settlement account at a SAFE-designated bank. The wire message must include the full legal name of the beneficiary, the SAFE FDI registration code, and a clear remittance narrative such as 'Capital Contribution under Foreign Direct Investment'. Upon receipt, the bank validates the inflow against the SAFE FDI Registration Certificate and the interbank verification system. Previously the funds are available for conversion into RMB, the WFOE must submit a capital verification report issued by a domestic licensed CPA (or an electronic verification through the Business Registration system), a deposit confirmation, and a detailed schedule of intended expenditure, such as construction materials, equipment, payroll, and lease costs. Conversion into RMB is performed transaction-by-transaction: the bank releases the RMB only after reviewing the relevant underlying contracts, invoices, and tax registrations, consistent with the negative-list approach applied to the enterprise's business scope.Outbound settlements for imported machinery and subcontractor payments are executed through licensed banks using SWIFT MT103 for direct fund transfers and MT700/MT707 for letter-of-credit operations. For cross-border trade in RMB, the payment may alternatively be cleared through the CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) while still subject to the same documentary checks. The WFOE must present an import customs declaration, commercial invoice, purchase order or contract, transport document (bill of lading or airway bill), and any required inspection certificate to the remitting bank for authenticity review. Commercial Letters of Credit are strongly preferred for large EPC milestones because they protect against documentary discrepancies and non-performance. Documentary collections are permissible but carry higher risk for the exporter and are generally avoided in silo elevator projects. Domestic disbursements to local subcontractors and material suppliers settle through the CNAPS RMB clearing system using standard bank transfers, supported by contract and fully verified VAT invoices.Deliverable FX forward contracts (RMB forward settlement and sale) between RMB and USD, EUR, GBP, or JPY, with a maximum tenor of one year, requiring a real underlying trade or capital exposure and SAFE bank confirmation of the transaction background.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) overlay is not permitted onshore for the WFOE itself; an offshore parent may hedge its own RMB exposure via offshore NDF markets, but any settlement proceeds must not flow into the WFOE's domestic accounts.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCS) for long-term fixed-asset project loans, subject to SAFE approval and demonstrable unhedged principal exposure; the swap must be pre-filed with the licensed bank and the underlying loan must be remitted into China under the registered capital or current account rails.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Payoneer, Airwallex) are not viable as the sole or primary banking channel for a Chinese WFOE. Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the PBoC's licensing framework, only banks authorized by SAFE to conduct foreign exchange business may open corporate accounts for entities registered in China and process capital-account or current-account settlements. Offshore EMIs are typically not licensed to maintain local RMB or FX accounts for Chinese domestic entities, and any attempt to route inbound FDI or trade proceeds through such channels would contravene SAFE rules on direct reporting and documentary authentication. Accordingly, an EMI can be used only in a supporting role for ancillary transactions (e.g., paying overseas vendors below regulatory thresholds) but must never be used for capital injections, foreign exchange settlement, or dividend repatriation. The effective rating is 'not feasible' for primary use; legal recourse for treasury officers is limited to licensed commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers or intangible asset transfers, or RMB 40 million in aggregate for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is required if the consolidated related-party transaction volume exceeds RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting threshold is RMB 5.5 billion of group revenue.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in the same manner as the UAE or EU; however, the 'substance' of an enterprise is critical in countering treaty abuse and under the GAAR (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, and Circ. No. 7, effective 2017). A WFOE conducting grain-silo construction must evidence core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China, including contract execution, project management, procurement, workforce, and risk assumption, to attribute profits to a permanent establishment. Pure 'letterbox' arrangements are disregarded, and profits may be reattributed on a deemed basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Under-Reporting / Fake Invoices

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63, the taxpayer must pay underpaid tax, a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount, and late-interest at 0.05% daily. Criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 triggers a fine and/or imprisonment of up to 7 years for a materially large evasion amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and supervisors personally liable for the short-paid tax if the veil is pierced under PRC Company Law; they face asset attachment, travel bans (exit restrictions under Tax Collection Law Article 44), disqualification from management positions, and criminal imprisonment of 3–7 years plus confiscation of property for serious evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41 and Article 44, the tax authority may make a deemed adjustment and impose a penalty of up to 10% of the unreported cross-border related-party transaction value (Announcement 6 of 2017). In addition to the adjustment, late interest accrues at 0.05% daily (currently benchmark plus 5%).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham intercompany agreements face personal civil liability for the corporate tax shortfall when the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or abuse of limited liability; they may be subject to a 5-year entry ban and criminal prosecution for 'tax evasion' if the adjustment leads to a criminal threshold, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, the People's Bank of China may impose fines of RMB 2 million to RMB 10 million on the entity for non-compliance; tax authorities may re-attribute all profits to the non-resident parent and levy 10% WHT on deemed dividends under EIT Law Article 45 (CFC rules). Financial institution transaction freezing and business license revocation may also be ordered.

Director personal liability: Directors may be blacklisted by customs and border-control (exit prohibition), their assets cannot be transferred, they face a fine of RMB 500,000 to RMB 1 million, and criminal liability for money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191 carries 5–10 years' imprisonment including confiscation of personal property. Civil tort liability follows for losses caused to creditors.
