Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a WFOE limited liability company under the current PRC Company Law. The shareholder may subscribe the full registered capital in the Articles of Association, and the capital contribution schedule must be completed within five years from the date of incorporation unless a special industry rule imposes a shorter or higher threshold. No blocked escrow account is statutorily mandated for formation; however, if the light rail EPC contract is funded through a government bond escrow or performance-guarantee mechanism, the project owner will require a separate escrow / performance-guarantee account which is contractual and not a corporate law formation condition.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Protection Design Review and Completion Fire Protection Inspection certificates from the Fire and Rescue Department are required for site offices, temporary buildings, traction substations and catenary maintenance facilities. Structural load inspection and approval must be obtained for overhead catenary gantries, temporary bridges and heavy lifting platforms. The construction site must obtain a Work Safety Facility Design Review and Completion Acceptance under the Work Safety Law, file with the local Emergency Management Bureau, and register all special operations personnel such as electricians, welders, riggers and crane operators. Civil defense coordination with the local Civil Defense Office is required where underground light rail sections, station excavation or tunnel works exceed civil air defense thresholds, together with the relevant temporary construction clearance and special railway/tram interface permits.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the electronic tax portal of the provincial/municipal tax service bureau; foreign non-resident entities file initial registrations through the State Taxation Administration's online tax service platform (电子税务局, operated by the STA) after obtaining a taxpayer identifier through the State Administration for Market Regulation for the WFOE establishment process.

E-government filing portal

A wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company formed in China must first complete incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining a unified social credit code (USCC) that serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN). Thereafter, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent in-charge tax authority within 30 days of obtaining the business license, file the taxpayer registration form, legal representative identity documentation, articles of association, business license copy, bank account information, and lease/ownership proof of registered address through the electronic tax bureau portal or in-person service hall. The STA issues a taxpayer identification number equal to the unified social credit code, activates the taxpayer status, assigns tax categories (VAT, corporate income tax, stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, etc.), and sets the filing frequency and tax administration district. For purely foreign non-resident entities without a Chinese establishment, a non-resident taxpayer identification number and withholding tax enrollment are obtained via the electronic tax bureau by filing a non-resident tax registration with the competent tax authority where the payer is located or where the real estate or income source is situated.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales threshold of RMB 5,000,000 for general VAT taxpayer status; small-scale taxpayers with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 and simplified collection rate of 3% (reduced statutory concessions may apply). Entities that do not meet the threshold may still voluntarily register as general VAT taxpayers if they meet the accounting and information requirements; mandatory general taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 unless the entity meets the simplified small-scale taxpayer conditions and does not apply for general status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to exported goods, cross-border taxable services, and certain offshore services rendered by Chinese residents to overseas recipients, under the VAT export tax rebate (exemption and credit) system. The statutory criteria require the goods to be physically shipped from a Chinese customs supervised area to a destination outside the customs territory of China according to a customs export declaration, with the sale made between a Chinese registered seller and an overseas buyer; the seller must receive payment in foreign currency or RMB settled through approved cross-border channels, and the goods must be actually exported within the prescribed time limits. For services, zero-rating applies to cross-border services that are consumed entirely outside China and satisfy the administrative announcement criteria on cross-border taxable services zero-rating and VAT exemption, including international transportation, research and development services, design services, software services, and other qualifying offshore services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) completed in the China International Trade Single Window and bearing the customs clearance release record
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical export of the goods from China customs territory
  • Official export invoice or commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas customer
  • Foreign exchange receipts record (涉外收入申报单) or bank receipt voucher evidencing inbound remittance from the overseas buyer, and where applicable the receipt of export proceeds as filed through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)
  • Letter of Credit or sales contract, if available, to corroborate the export transaction and payment terms
  • Customs clearance record of the goods, including the export goods after verification record and export rebate declaration list submitted via the export tax rebate filing system
  • Foreign exchange settlement and sale verification documents prescribed by SAFE to prove receipt of the export proceeds within the statutory time limit

China operates the nationwide e-invoice system under the State Taxation Administration's fully digital electronic invoice (全面数字化的电子发票, also called 数电票) platform. Taxpayers must use the STA's official electronic invoice service platform to issue legally valid digital invoices; the platform performs real-time fiscal clearance, assigns unique invoice codes, and may require risk-based anti-fraud controls. General VAT taxpayers must use the special VAT invoice system for output VAT invoicing and input VAT credit certification through the VAT invoice comprehensive service platform (增值税发票综合服务平台). The e-invoice platform uses real-time data transmission, automatic revenue reconciliation, and digital tax control stamping; no separate physical offline invoice stamping is permitted except under transitional paper invoices which are being phased out. All invoice data must be reported to the STA electronic database, with mandatory API integration for enterprise resource planning systems or approved third-party fiscal service providers.

The exporter must substantiate each zero-rated export transaction through the electronic export tax rebate system of the State Taxation Administration. If the foreign exchange proceeds are not received within the required time limit or the export is later found to have been falsely declared as exported, the tax authority will claw back the refunded VAT and charged interest, and impose penalty fines of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded amount under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection; moreover, failure to declare or mis-declaration of exports may lead to customs fines under the Customs Law, loss of tax rebate eligibility, blocking of future rebate filings, downgrading of the taxpayer credit rating, and criminal liability for fraudulent export rebate claims under the Criminal Law, including imprisonment and fines for the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a hybrid foreign exchange control regime in which the RMB is fully convertible for most current-account transactions, such as trade in goods and services, while capital-account transactions remain subject to registration, verification, and supervisory control by SAFE. For the proposed WFOE engaged in urban light rail transit EPC, inbound equity capital from abroad must be routed through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account opened at a licensed domestic commercial bank. Every conversion of those funds into RMB for project expenditure is scrutinized by the designated bank against the declared business scope and the underlying construction and EPC contracts. Convertibility is therefore asymmetric: export and trade proceeds can be settled freely after authenticity review, but cross-border outbound flows such as dividend remittances, shareholder loan repayments, and disinvestment proceeds require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board resolutions. Surplus capital cannot be diverted to non-business purposes or speculative financial investments. Profit remittances are permitted once tax and audit obligations are satisfied, but all transfers must occur through the formal banking channel. Offshore RMB clearing is permitted via the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or the correspondent banking network, while physical RMB cash movement remains tightly regulated. This creates a bank-centric treasury architecture where capital account funds must be ring-fenced, conversion risk must be actively managed, and each disbursement must be supported by authentic trade or investment documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE trade-in-goods administration framework, there is no mandatory surrender requirement and no compulsory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange account. However, export earnings from goods trade must be repatriated to China through a designated bank within 90 days of the customs export declaration. If collection occurs later than 90 days, the enterprise must complete trade credit and receivable registration in the SAFE Trade Credit Reporting System. Any conversion of export proceeds into RMB requires authenticity documents such as the customs declaration, commercial invoice, contract, and proof of shipment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the State Council of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low. Offshore digital electronic money institutions and cross-border fintech payment platforms do not hold a commercial banking license in Mainland China and are not authorized by PBOC or SAFE to provide domestic CNY clearing, receive statutory capital contributions, issue RMB corporate accounts, or complete the SAFE foreign exchange capital registration for a WFOE. They cannot support capital verification for foreign direct investment or act as a clearing rail for government bond escrow. Their use is limited to non-statutory ancillary cross-border invoicing or outside-China treasury operations. The statutory treasury function for the WFOE must be maintained with a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be wired from the foreign shareholder's own corporate bank account to the WFOE's SAFE-registered capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank using SWIFT MT103. The remittance purpose code must clearly identify a foreign direct investment equity injection into a wholly foreign-owned enterprise. On receipt, the bank verifies the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate and the underlying EPC business scope. Funds may be converted to RMB only for genuine business uses linked to the urban light rail project, including construction materials, labor, machinery, and subcontractor payments. If a capital verification report is required for statutory or local licensing purposes, a licensed Chinese accounting firm will issue a capital verification certificate after confirming the funds actually arrived in the capital account. Turnover: An independent certified Chinese CPA audit is legally required for the WFOE if any one of the following quantitative benchmarks is met in the fiscal year: (i) balance-sheet total assets exceed RMB 50 million at year-end; (ii) annual operating revenue exceeds RMB 80 million; (iii) average annual headcount exceeds 300 employees. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for annual related-party transfers of tangible property; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for related-party financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (including services). A Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. These thresholds trigger mandatory contemporaneous Local File and Master File preparation under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47, allowing recharacterization of arrangements that lack bona fide business purpose or economic substance. Under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, non-resident taxpayers claiming treaty benefits must satisfy the beneficial ownership test, which considers whether the entity has substantial business activities, employees, assets, and real decision-making authority. For the ISIC 4210 urban light rail transit EPC WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — engineering, procurement, construction management, contract negotiation, risk assumption, invoicing, and project financing decisions — must actually be performed and managed in China with commensurate staff, premises, and risk. A letterbox or shell WFOE with no local substance cannot rely on treaty or transfer pricing benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law (TCAL), the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax, imposes a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and levies a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the Criminal Law; fraudulent VAT invoicing can additionally invoke Criminal Law Article 205, which carries fines and imprisonment ranging from short-term detention to life imprisonment for particularly huge amounts.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law principles where a director abuses the WFOE's limited liability personality. Directors who direct, approve, or conceal false declarations are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; their personal bank accounts, cars, and real estate may be seized through asset restitution proceedings. As responsible persons of a tax-dishonest enterprise, they may be placed on the Joint Punishment for Tax Dishonesty blacklist, subject to exit bans (travel restrictions), restrictions on high consumption and high-speed rail / civil aviation travel, disqualification from serving as directors, and potential criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years, or 3-7 years where the amount evaded is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax payable.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41 and 46 and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, the tax authority may make transfer pricing adjustments, disallow excessive related-party interest under the 2:1 debt-to-equity ratio, recharacterize excessive debt as equity, and impose a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge on the underpaid tax. If the pricing scheme is determined to be tax evasion, TCAL Article 63 applies, exposing the WFOE to a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length transactions without obtaining proper transfer pricing studies, board resolutions, or economic substance documentation may be personally ordered to compensate the company for the resulting tax penalties, statutory interest, and audit costs. If the intercompany arrangement is a sham used to evade Chinese tax, the directors and controlling shareholders can be held civilly liable under the corporate veil piercing doctrine, subjected to travel bans via the tax dishonesty blacklist, and prosecuted criminally under Criminal Law Article 201 for conspiring in tax evasion.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse Lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), the State Taxation Administration may recharacterize the arrangement, deny the claimed treaty benefits, and impose an upward adjustment to taxable income. SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 requires denial of DTA benefits where the recipient lacks beneficial ownership and economic substance. The resulting underpayment attracts a 0.05% per-day late-payment surcharge and, if intentional, a penalty of 50%-500% of the tax underpaid under TCAL Article 63.

Director personal liability: Where the WFOE is a sham or mere instrumentality, courts and tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and parent shareholders jointly liable for unpaid corporate tax, fines, and creditor liabilities. Directors may be personally required to restore diverted assets, and may have personal assets frozen or forfeited through civil asset restitution. They may also be blacklisted as tax-dishonest persons, restricted from leaving China, and criminally prosecuted for tax evasion if they knowingly used the shell structure to defraud the Chinese tax authority, resulting in imprisonment of up to 7 years for the most aggravated cases.
