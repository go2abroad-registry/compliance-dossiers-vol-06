Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a general wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The registered capital is a subscribed amount stated in the Articles of Association and must be paid by each shareholder within five years from the date of incorporation unless the Articles or the applicable regime impose a shorter period. No blocked escrow account is required under Chinese law for the capital contribution account; the sole shareholder must transfer contributions from an overseas account to the company's designated capital contribution settlement account at a licensed bank, and the bank will file the receipt with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange under the foreign direct investment (FDI) system. EPC contract advances and performance security may be held in escrow only if the commercial contract so provides.

Funding & unlock path: Fire protection design review and final acceptance certificate must be obtained from the Fire and Rescue Department before commissioning; regular fire safety inspections and annual testing of fire suppression systems are mandatory. Civil defense approval is required for any underground structures, effluent basins, and confined spaces under the Civil Defense law. Structural load inspections and building completion acceptance are required for all tanks and process buildings. Occupational safety clearance includes an occupational disease hazard assessment under the Occupational Disease Prevention Law, confined-space entry permits, registration of pressure vessels, gas detectors, and hazardous process safety management for any sludge digestion or chemical storage areas.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered locally through the competent tax service bureaus; online filing and registration through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) portal

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE, incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license is automatically the PRC tax identification number (TIN/VAT ID). Complete all required post-incorporation tax registration steps with the local STA office within 30 days through the e-Tax Bureau: verify entity legal status as a resident enterprise, assign legal representative, finance officer, accounting system basis, bank deposit account information, and VAT taxpayer status. Because tax registration is integrated with SAMR under the one-license-one-code system, no separate TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is used for all VAT, EIT, payroll and withholding filings. A foreign investor that is itself a non-resident enterprise with China-source income but no permanent establishment must separately register for a non-resident enterprise tax identification number on the e-Tax Bureau before its Chinese withholding obligations can be performed.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic tax registration is mandatory as soon as the WFOE receives its business license, regardless of turnover. For general VAT taxpayer classification, the mandatory economic threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; turnover below this level results in small-scale taxpayer status unless the taxpayer elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to goods physically exported from and simultaneously delivered out of the PRC customs territory where the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer, files a Customs Export Goods Declaration, receives consideration in foreign exchange from the overseas buyer, and completes export tax refund (exemption) procedures. Qualifying cross-border services, including design and commissioning services for water treatment works where the services are wholly consumed outside mainland China and paid in foreign exchange, are also zero-rated where they fall within the cross-border tax services zero-rated catalogue. Domestic construction, installation and commissioning performed inside China are not zero-rated and are taxable at the applicable 9% or 6% rates.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (customs declaration form for exported goods)
  • Original Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing export movement
  • Foreign exchange receipt confirmation / export collection verification record from the receiving bank (inward remittance evidence)
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • VAT invoice for export sales issued under the electronic tax invoice system
  • Technical acceptance or commissioning certificate evidencing offshore delivery of services, where applicable
  • Certificate of origin (where required for customs preference or transit)

The STA mandates electronic invoice issuance through the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system on the National Electronic Tax Bureau; invoice data is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT special invoices require tax-control clearance and carry an STA-assigned anti-fraud invoice code and digital tax-control stamp before lawful issuance. High-volume taxpayers must integrate their billing software with the e-Tax Bureau by approved API to obtain invoice authorisation and clear issued invoices before monthly VAT returns are filed.

If zero-rating is claimed without full export movement, foreign-currency receipt and statutory documentation, the STA rejects the claim, claws back any VAT refunded, charges daily late-payment surcharge (0.05% per day) from the date of refund, and imposes a penalty under the PRC Tax Administration Law of between 50% and 500% of the tax involved for evasion or fraudulent refund claims. Criminal exposure under the PRC Criminal Law for export tax fraud may result in imprisonment and fines. A retrospective audit is not time-barred in cases of fraud; otherwise the general audit recovery period is three years, extendable to five years for understatement by tax planning, and refunds may be clawed back accordingly.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

On the current account, China has historically achieved de facto convertibility: business operating income from cross-border trade and services can be converted into RMB at market rates through licensed banks, subject to truthfulness and documentation review. The capital account remains formally controlled: equity injections into a WFOE require SAFE foreign investment registration and are drawn through a capital account, while cross-border loans, derivatives and portfolio flows require specific quotas or approvals. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds in the post-2012 regime, so a WFOE may retain earned foreign currency and settle the amount when commercially prudent. Profit remittance by a WFOE is permitted after payment of corporate income tax and completion of an annual statutory audit; the bank performs a tax clearance check, and dividends must be sourced from after-tax retained earnings. The principal remaining hurdle is administrative documentation: banks require contracts, invoices, customs records, and tax certificates to validate each remittance, and capital account proceeds cannot be used outside the permitted business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio. Under SAFE administration, export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency; the repatriation and verification cycle is normally 180 days from export customs declaration. For advance export receipts, the WFOE must ensure the receiving bank completes declaration in the Goods Trade Monitoring System; failure to repatriate within the trade-cycle window triggers SAFE compliance review. No mandatory sale to the bank is imposed, and the conversion decision belongs to the enterprise.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable and not permitted for a mainland WFOE. In China, only licensed banks are empowered by PBOC and CBIRC to hold current and capital account deposits, conduct foreign exchange settlement, and access the RMB and FX clearing systems. Non-bank fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks cannot issue onshore account numbers or process local RMB clearing for a foreign-invested entity; overseas EMIs can only offer movement on correspondent banking and would not satisfy SAFE capital verification or profit remittance documentation. A WFOE must maintain its primary treasury channel through a domestic tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions must be routed via licensed banks in mainland China after obtaining SAFE foreign investment information registration for the WFOE. The overseas shareholder gives SWIFT MT103 instructions with a purpose code for direct investment as paid-in capital and must be named as the remitter. The bank will credit the capital account and, before converting to RMB, will review the resolution, articles of association, and the SAFE registration. After funds are received, a PRC CPA must issue a capital verification report; if a World Bank escrow is used, the project escrow proceeds should be kept distinct from the FDI registered capital, and withdrawals to the WFOE will follow the escrow agreement and SAFE cross-border escrow rules.Outbound disbursements for EPC procurement may be executed through direct SWIFT MT103 transfers for smaller service or equipment payments, import documentary letters of credit with a confirming bank for large machinery purchases, and documentary collections on either documents-against-payment or documents-against-acceptance terms where commercial risk is managed by both banks. Each payment requires a SAFE trade declaration using contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, insurance certificate, and customs declaration. For service-related outflows, tax clearance and service trade declaration are required. The WFOE should procure a dedicated foreign currency disbursement account at the same tier-1 bank to create a full audit trail.Onshore deliverable CNY FX forward contracts against USD or EUR for contracted import, export, and capital-reflow exposures, requiring underlying trade or financial documentation and settlement through a licensed PRC bank.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps for long-term project financing in foreign currency, subject to SAFE registration and demonstration of genuine hedged exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are offshore instruments and are not generally permissible for a mainland WFOE to book directly; resident entities cannot hedge through offshore NDFs because SAFE requires onshore real delivery and proof of the underlying trade.Structured options are permissible only where the underlying exposure is genuine and the product is sold by a licensed PRC bank as part of a demonstrable risk-management purpose; speculative derivative transactions are prohibited.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law, every company, including a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), must prepare annual financial accounting reports and have them audited by a lawfully established Chinese CPA firm. No balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount quantitative threshold exempts a WFOE from mandatory audit; the obligation attaches purely to corporate registration. For applicable accounting-standard calibration, the 2017 National Economic Statistical Classification for construction and water infrastructure (ISIC 4220 / NAICS 237110) defines a medium or large enterprise if any of the following indicators are exceeded: (a) total assets above RMB 50 million (approx. USD 7 million), (b) annual operating turnover above RMB 50 million (approx. USD 7 million), or (c) average employees exceeding 300. Entities exceeding these indicators must prepare full PRC Enterprise Accounting Standards (CAS) financial statements; smaller WFOEs may apply the simplified Small Enterprise Accounting Standards (SEAS) but remain fully subject to the mandatory annual audit.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China imposes Local File requirements when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion in a financial year; a Country-by-Country report notification applies when the group consolidated revenue equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply an OECD-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the tax authorities rigorously enforce substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and anti-treaty-abuse doctrines. For a WFOE engaged in municipal sewage treatment EPC, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in China and include engineering design, procurement, project management, construction oversight, commissioning, financial control, and risk management. A pure pass-through or shell entity with no employees, premises, assets, or real decision-making cannot support treaty withholding reductions and will face recharacterization under the GAAR.
