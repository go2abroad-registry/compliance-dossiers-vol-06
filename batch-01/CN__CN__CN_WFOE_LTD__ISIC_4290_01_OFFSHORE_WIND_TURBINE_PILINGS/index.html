Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the PRC Company Law for a WFOE; however, the Articles of Association must state a registered capital and shareholders must actually pay their paid-in capital within five years of incorporation (or a shorter period mandated by the actual investment project) unless the sector imposes special requirements. For offshore wind engineering, the registered capital must be sufficient to meet the Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development qualification standards and contract performance requirements. There is no statutory blocked escrow account, but all paid-in capital must be remitted to the company's designated capital contribution bank account and must be supported by a verified foreign exchange capital account.

Funding & unlock path: The onshore project office and steel pile assembly yard must obtain a civil defense construction inspection record and a fire safety acceptance certificate from the local fire and rescue authority, including proof of structural load inspection, installation of automatic fire alarm and suppression systems, and approved emergency evacuation routes. Offshore, all vessels and lifting equipment must hold current marine classification certificates issued by the China Classification Society, and dynamic positioning and navigation safety clearances from the Maritime Safety Administration. The company must obtain a workplace occupational health hazard evaluation certificate from the local health authority, covering noise, vibration, dust, and confined-space entry, and every crew member must hold an offshore survival, first aid, and marine firefighting certificate approved by the national maritime training authority. The fire permit is mandatory for any temporary living quarters and fuel storage on the installation vessels or artificial islands.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online administration is conducted through the National Tax Service Platform (e-Tax), the China International Trade Single Window for customs-trade data integration, and the VAT Invoice Management System under the Golden Tax Phase IV architecture.

E-government filing portal

For a China-incorporated WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon approval of the Articles of Association and incorporation is the sole statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer ID. Tax registration is completed through the one-stop integrated registration mechanism at formation: SAMR automatically pushes the USCC and legal-person registration data to the competent STA office; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. If the automatic push fails, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration at the competent tax authority within 30 days of the SAMR business license issuance using the standardized registration application available in the e-Tax system, submitting the business license, approved articles of association, shareholder identity documents, legal representative identity materials, and proof of registered office. A foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China but deriving Chinese-source income must register as a Non-Resident Enterprise with the competent tax authority at the location of the payer or real estate/property location in order to obtain a TIN; the application requires the foreign incorporation certificate, tax residence certificate, contract evidencing Chinese-source income, and the withholding agent engagement details. For the WFOE itself, the USCC is used for all VAT, Corporate Income Tax (CIT), individual withholding, stamp duty, and export VAT refund filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for a WFOE limited-liability company. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese resident enterprise subject to CIT on worldwide income, and as a general VAT taxpayer once annual taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold; below that threshold it may elect simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment for VAT, but this is an invoicing and VAT-rate election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover within a rolling 12-month period for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; the WFOE is nevertheless required to complete tax registration immediately upon corporate formation regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (quarterly RMB 300,000) are exempt from VAT on domestic sales, but exporters must generally register as general VAT taxpayers to claim zero-rating and input-VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered general VAT taxpayer may apply 0% VAT and claim a refund of attributable input VAT on export sales of offshore wind turbine pilings only when the goods physically leave China, the export is declared to and released by China Customs, the title and risk have passed to the overseas buyer, the sales proceeds are received from a non-resident payer, and the export is not listed on any prohibited or suspended export goods catalogue. For offshore installation and marine construction services performed outside China for non-resident clients, the zero-rating or exemption eligibility additionally requires that the service is supplied from China but consumed entirely outside China, with a written contract stating offshore consumption and evidence of actual delivery outside China. The statutory documentation package must evidence the commercial reality of the cross-border transaction, not merely corporate form.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window and stamped/released by China Customs, including the assigned export declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing shipment of the pilings to the overseas buyer and the port of discharge
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract specifying INCOTERMS and transfer of risk to the non-resident buyer
  • Packing list and export goods inspection certificate (CIQ) where the pilings fall within mandatory inspection catalogue
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation evidencing that the foreign exchange from the overseas buyer was collected and repatriated into the WFOE's domestic foreign-exchange settlement account
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Verification record digitally submitted to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the settlement bank

The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the National VAT Invoice Management System under the mandatory Full Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime. FDEI are digitally signed by the STA, carry the tax authority's unified electronic seal, and are transmitted in real time through approved APIs to the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Paper invoices, if still permitted in transitional zones, must be synchronised with the VAT Invoice Management System before the end of the fiscal month, and no private offline invoicing is lawful. The system performs real-time cross-matching of Invoice, Tax control, Customs export declarations, and foreign exchange collection records; discrepancies automatically trigger tax-risk alerts and may suspend the VAT invoice allocation quota.

STA uses Golden Tax Phase IV data matching with China Customs and SAFE to verify export declaration, physical outbound shipment, invoice consistency, and foreign-currency repatriation. Any false export declaration or fraudulent VAT refund claim triggers immediate clawback of the full refunded input VAT, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid or clawed-back tax under the Tax Administration and Collection Law, and an administrative penalty of 50% up to five times the falsely refunded amount. Where the fraudulent amount is significant, the case is referred to the criminal authorities; Criminal Law Article 204 provides for fixed-term imprisonment or life imprisonment in the most aggravated cases, together with a fine of one to five times the fraudulent refund and confiscation of the taxpayer's VAT invoice issuance privileges, downgraded tax credit rating, and mandatory post-refund inspection status for a period of at least three years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bank-led current account convertibility regime with registration-based capital account management. A WFOE may freely settle current account receipts and pay current account obligations through designated banks after documentary authenticity review, but capital account receipts, including equity and debt injections, must be matched to a SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration and a specific purpose code before entry. Currency conversion itself is not prohibited, but the funds are ring-fenced: FDI capital settled into RMB must be used within the WFOE's approved operating scope and is not freely available for portfolio investments, entrusted loans, real estate beyond negative-list limits, or unregulated domestic lending. Profit and dividend repatriation requires audited financial statements, tax compliance certification, and SAFE/bank current-account outflow declaration. For a cross-border wind-monopile installation WFOE, the operative constraint is not convertibility at the spot tier but the capacity to evidence commercial authenticity for every USD or CNY payment, especially marine charter hire and offshore service invoices. Full capital-account convertibility is absent; the constituent regime is current account free convertibility plus registered capital account managed convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory conversion ratio: none. The pre-2010 mandatory surrender policy was replaced by an autonomous foreign-currency retention regime. The WFOE must repatriate export and service proceeds through designated foreign exchange banks and may either retain the receipts in foreign currency or convert them into RMB. For goods export proceeds, collection should occur within the contractually agreed payment terms and is monitored by the bank under SAFE trade inventory rules; where open-account export proceeds remain unreceived beyond 180 days after customs declaration, the bank and exporter must report to SAFE and provide a commercial explanation. For offshore wind installation service revenue, proceeds should be received within the invoice maturity and supported by signed contracts, completion certificates, and tax filings.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as primary treasury rails for a mainland WFOE because they lack a People's Bank of China payment license and cannot participate in SAFE's current and capital account purpose-code declaration architecture. They may support the offshore parent treasury, but the WFOE itself must maintain clearing, FX settlement, and compliance accountability at a licensed tier-1 domestic commercial bank. A local licensed third-party payment institution may provide ancillary cross-border RMB collection, but it cannot replace the corporate bank account for statutory FDI capital verification and project trade settlement.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque beneficial ownership chain with an offshore holding parent and multiple intermediate shell layers above the mainland WFOE.

Root cause: Opaque beneficial ownership chain with an offshore holding parent and multiple intermediate shell layers above the mainland WFOE.

Defense evidence: Notarized certificate of incorporation, current constitutional documents, register of directors and shareholders for every entity in the ownership chain, audited financial statements, a full organizational chart with percentage holdings, UBO declarations with copies of national identity documents and passports, source-of-wealth statements for every person with 10 percent or more beneficial ownership, and any trust or nominee declarations.
Cross-border disbursements to shipowners, vessel charterers, and port-service payees located in flag-of-convenience or high-risk maritime jurisdictions.

Root cause: Cross-border disbursements to shipowners, vessel charterers, and port-service payees located in flag-of-convenience or high-risk maritime jurisdictions.

Defense evidence: Vessel IMO number, flag state registry certificate, ISM and ISPS certificates, Protection and Indemnity insurance certificate, charter party and sub-charter party contracts, detailed vessel invoices, AIS track records for installation activity, sanctions and enforcement screening reports confirming no match against OFAC, EU, UN and other restricted lists, and project-specific end-use statements for marine assets.
Mismatch between the licensed construction scope and actual payment nature, including large mobilization payments, rental of hydraulic hammers, or lease of installation vessels without clear project linkage.

Root cause: Mismatch between the licensed construction scope and actual payment nature, including large mobilization payments, rental of hydraulic hammers, or lease of installation vessels without clear project linkage.

Defense evidence: Current business license showing construction scope aligned with ISIC 4290 and NAICS 237990, statutory offshore construction permits, executed turnkey installation contract, detailed project schedule with mobilization and demobilization milestones, equipment and vessel lease agreements, invoices, progress valuations from the employer, and a treasury confirmation letter showing how each payment maps to a completed work stage.
Third-party remitter funding or round-tripping capital that does not match the declared shareholder identity and registered capital structure.

Root cause: Third-party remitter funding or round-tripping capital that does not match the declared shareholder identity and registered capital structure.

Defense evidence: Board resolution approving capital injection, original SWIFT remittance instructions identifying the remitter as the registered shareholder, bank statements from the remitting entity showing the source of funds, intercompany loan or support agreement for any supplemental funding, shareholder register, SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Business Registration form, and a People's Republic of China certified public accountant capital verification report.

Turnover: China does not apply size-based balance-sheet, turnover or headcount exemptions for corporate statutory audits. Under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law, every limited liability company, including a WFOE, must submit its annual financial report to an accounting firm for audit at the end of each fiscal year. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File transfer pricing documentation is required if, in the preceding fiscal year, the WFOE exceeds any of the following thresholds with related parties: tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million; financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is required if the total annual related-party transaction value exceeds RMB 1 billion, or if the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion (triggering Country-by-Country Reporting obligations) and the enterprise is designated to prepare a Master File.

Economic substance scope: China enforces the substance-over-form doctrine and a General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. For an offshore wind piling WFOE, economic substance is demonstrated through actual employees, operational equipment, project management, and risk-taking in China. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for marine installation and piling services are located where the personnel, vessels, and equipment operate; therefore, the income from such services must be reported in China. Merely booking revenue through a Hong Kong or tax-haven related entity without real activity will result in a re-allocation of profit under the arm's-length standard, denial of treaty benefits, and application of anti-abuse penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law (TCAL), the tax authority will recover the evaded tax and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the amount underpaid. If the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, Criminal Law Article 201 applies: imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine; for substantial amounts (typically over RMB 100,000 and exceeding 30% of payable tax), imprisonment from 3 to 7 years and heavier fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered, authorized, or knowingly benefitted from the false reporting may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties under Company Law Article 20 through piercing the corporate veil. They may also face exit restriction (travel ban) and blacklisting under TCAL Article 17, civil asset restitution, and criminal imprisonment up to 7 years, plus confiscation of personal assets obtained through the evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of TCAL and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (SAT Announcement No. 6 of 2016), the tax authority may re-determine taxable income using a deemed pricing method (typically TNMM) and assess additional tax. The underpaid tax is subject to a fine of 50% to 500% under TCAL Article 64, plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05%; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation may trigger an additional 5% monthly interest surcharge on the adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the non-arm's-length transactions and failed to ensure documentation compliance can be held personally liable for the resultant tax shortfall if the company's separate legal personality is abused (i.e., personal asset tracing), while exit restrictions and travel bans may be issued for the legal representative. In aggravated cases, criminal tax evasion liability may attach with imprisonment up to 3 years or longer if the underpayment is large.
DTA Abuse / Phantom Beneficial Ownership (Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement No. 35 of 2019, the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, re-characterize the payment as Chinese-source income subject to the statutory 10% withholding tax, and impose late interest and penalties. If the false declaration is deemed tax evasion, TCAL Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and Criminal Law Article 201 may prosecute the conduct as tax fraud, leading to imprisonment of 3 to 7 years for significant amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed or certified false beneficial ownership declarations face personal civil liability for the additional withholding tax and penalties via the corporate veil doctrine, a travel ban and blacklisting under TCAL Article 17, and criminal liability under the tax fraud provisions. They may also be personally liable to creditors if the corporate façade is pierced, with asset restitution orders and imprisonment up to 7 years in egregious cases.
