Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE under current PRC law. The registered capital must be fully subscribed within the time limit specified in the Articles of Incorporation (typically 20-30 years for construction projects, but subject to project duration). Capital contributions must be remitted through a foreign exchange capital account (FDI) and verified by a Chinese CPA firm. No escrow account is required, but the capital must be injected in accordance with the subscribed schedule and used only for lawful business purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification (消防验收意见书) from the local Fire and Rescue Department, covering temporary structures, fuel storage, and electrical installations. Structural load inspections for any on-site scaffolding or temporary seawall forms must be certified by a licensed structural engineer. Occupational safety approval from the local Emergency Management Bureau, including a Safety Production Permit and a site-specific safety plan. Additionally, the company must obtain a Maritime Safety Permit from the local Maritime Safety Administration for the operation of dredging vessels, including crew certifications and vessel inspection certificates.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic compliance is conducted through the nationwide National Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-tax platform), linked from the official STA website www.chinatax.gov.cn and administered by local STA offices.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the foreign investor first completes market entity incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR-issued business license carries the mandatory Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), an 18-digit identifier that functions as the legal taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax, VAT, and all other PRC taxes; no separate STA-issued TIN is generated for a resident WFOE. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must activate and complete its tax registration information electronic confirmation through the e-tax platform, identify its competent local tax office, determine VAT status (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), set tax filing periods, and apply for invoice issuance eligibility. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment that must withhold PRC tax or pay China-source tax, a separate non-resident taxpayer file is opened through the withholding agent or directly via the e-tax portal; this does not replace the resident WFOE's USCC tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide a pass-through, check-the-box, or hybrid mismatch election for a WFOE. A WFOE is a resident enterprise subject to entity-level taxation. Voluntary election of general VAT taxpayer status for a small-scale VAT taxpayer is an administrative VAT status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the mandatory economic turnover threshold for registration as a VAT general taxpayer; all WFOEs must complete basic tax registration upon establishment irrespective of turnover, and entities below the threshold may remain small-scale VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods export, a VAT general taxpayer may claim zero-rating, meaning output VAT exemption with refund of relevant input VAT, where the goods are actually exported from the PRC customs territory under an export declaration, the exporter is the legal customs declarant, the transaction is genuine and consistent with invoice and contract records, and the export proceeds are repatriated through official banking channels. Qualifying offshore services may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt only where the service is listed by the Ministry of Finance and STA as a cross-border taxable service consumed wholly outside China; for marine dredging and breakwater EPC work, the physical export of materials and equipment can be zero-rated on the goods portion, while the construction and dredging service element must be separately assessed under the cross-border service zero-rating and exemption regime. Supporting evidence must demonstrate performance outside China, delivery to the overseas counterparty, and collection in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration) matched with electronic customs clearance records through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document showing the goods physically left a PRC customs port and arrived at the foreign destination
  • Bank export proceeds receipt / inward foreign exchange settlement slip (or cross-border RMB collection confirmation) evidencing repatriation of the trade receivable from the overseas buyer

China mandates the STA's digitalized electronic invoicing framework, including the Full Digital Electronic Invoice system, for the issuance, delivery, and real-time fiscal clearance of VAT invoices. VAT invoices must be generated through the national e-invoice service platform, with taxpayer access via authenticated portals or open APIs; each invoice receives a national tax-control number, digital signature, encrypted anti-fraud measures, and is reported to the STA in near real time. Transitional paper invoices remain subject to the legacy tax-control invoice administration rules. The e-invoice platform forms part of the Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud architecture.

The STA and local tax offices perform matching checks among export declarations, VAT invoices, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts. Export zero-rating claims that are unsupported by these records or that rely on fabricated shipments are rejected. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, false export tax refund claims trigger recovery of the full VAT refunded, late payment surcharge, and administrative fines of up to five times the tax fraudulently obtained; criminal liability under the Criminal Law may also apply where fraud is established. Retroactive recovery of fraudulent export refunds is not subject to the ordinary three-year collection limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Current account transactions are broadly convertible and do not require prior approval, subject to bank-led authenticity and completeness review. Capital account transactions remain subject to registration, filing, or approval: the WFOE's founding foreign investment must be registered with SAFE under the FDI foreign exchange regime before the offshore shareholder remits equity capital. Once registered, the capital enters a dedicated FDI foreign exchange capital account. The enterprise may settle that foreign exchange into RMB, but the settlement must be supported by genuine business documentation under the 'foreign exchange settlement of capital' rules. There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds, but export foreign exchange receipts must be reported and deposited into a designated onshore account. Profit remittances are permitted after the annual audit, the board resolution, and the tax clearance certificate are presented, and the bank reviews the validity of the underlying dividend distribution. Practical hurdles include the negative list for foreign investment, UBO disclosure, capital verification by a certified public accountant, and a prohibition on using RMB settled from capital to make entrusted loans or engage in out-of-scope equity investments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender or fixed conversion ratio currently exists. Export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to the onshore designated settlement account and enter the export verification/receipt system; banks monitor the timing relative to the export customs declaration and the commercial terms, with the general verification window of 90 days extendable to 180 days under approved deferred settlement structures. Conversion of export proceeds into RMB is at the enterprise's option and must satisfy the bank's underlying trade authenticity review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / Not viable as a primary treasury rail. Fintech EMIs are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC to provide onshore RMB settlement, foreign exchange conversion, or capital account services to a mainland China WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs cannot register the required FDI capital account, cannot comply with SAFE reporting, and cannot support statutory capital verification, tax clearance, or profit repatriation. Any reliance on an offshore EMI for the WFOE's structured cash flows would create a compliance failure and expose the entity to loss of foreign exchange settlement rights. The legally viable structure requires a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank as the licensed onshore clearing and custody bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore parent must complete SAFE FDI registration and open an FDI foreign exchange capital account at the selected Tier-1 bank. The injection wire must be a SWIFT MT103 using transaction purpose code 701010 (inbound FDI capital contribution) in the payment message, with the reference 'CN_WFOE_LTD capital injection'. After the bank performs authenticity and sanctions screening, the funds are credited to the capital account and can be settled into RMB only on a genuine, self-use basis. A certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report using the bank's proof of receipt and the registered capital schedule; the settlement RMB is held in the capital settlement account and must be debited to operational expenditures within the corporate scope. No inbound capital may be used for entrusted lending or equity investment outside the business scope.For dredging subcontractors, equipment suppliers, fuel, and insurance payments, use SWIFT MT103 for ordinary corporate remittances; for large equipment purchases and structured port authority milestones, use irrevocable commercial letters of credit at sight or usance, or documentary collections against shipping and port documents. All outbound transfers require underlying contracts, invoices, customs or service trade declarations, tax withholding proof where applicable, and corporate board authorization. For cross-border RMB transactions, the WFOE may use CIPS through the domestic Tier-1 bank with the same authenticity and tax compliance checks.Deliverable FX forwards with licensed onshore banks on a real exposure basis, subject to SAFE real-need verification and prohibited for speculative positions.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD/EUR for long-term offshore capex, debt principal, and interest hedging, requiring underlying registered foreign currency exposure.Plain-vanilla FX options and FX swaps for hedging genuine current and capital account exposures; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally not permissible onshore for SAFE-registered entities and offshore NDF contracts should be avoided.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: No quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold applies to a PRC WFOE. Under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law, every limited liability company is required to engage a lawfully established accounting firm to audit its annual financial report at the end of each fiscal year, regardless of asset size, revenue, or employee count. Foreign-invested enterprises are likewise required to submit audited financial statements as part of the annual information report; therefore the audit mandate is universal, not threshold-based.
