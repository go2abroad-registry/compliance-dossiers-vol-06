Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed on a WFOE limited liability company under the PRC Companies Law or Foreign Investment Law. The registered capital amount is fixed by the Articles of Association and subscribed by the foreign shareholder. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Companies Law, a limited liability company shareholder must pay subscribed capital in full within five years from establishment unless a shorter period is prescribed by law or the Articles. Capital must be remitted into a Chinese capital account through the bank foreign-exchange settlement system after the company is registered. No offshore blocked escrow account is required; capital may not be paid out offshore before registration and must be used for lawful business purposes, subject to the Foreign Investment Negative List.

Funding & unlock path: The leased premises must pass fire-protection design review or acceptance and obtain a Fire Safety Filing or Inspection Certificate where applicable. Heavy storage areas must be supported by a structural load inspection certificate issued by a qualified design unit. Special equipment, including elevators, cranes, pressure vessels and electrical testing rigs, must be registered and inspected under the PRC Special Equipment Safety Law. The site must maintain a written workplace-safety management system, an emergency-response plan, fire-extinguisher logs, electrical safety audits and personal protective equipment. Construction and installation projects require a Construction Enterprise Safety Production License and the designation of an on-site safety officer with valid qualifications.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; operational filings are made through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online portal operated by the STA and the provincial tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For a Chinese WFOE, the foreign investor first registers the limited liability company with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) and receives a business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Under the one-license-one-code reform, the AMR synchronizes the legal-entity registration to the STA, so the USCC automatically becomes the taxpayer identification number for corporate income tax and VAT; no separate TIN application is required for the resident entity. The company must then complete post-incorporation tax formalities in the e-Tax Bureau, including reporting the legal representative, financial accounting system, bank deposit account, business scope, and invoice-issuing settings. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no establishment or place of business in China but derives China-source income, or that supplies cross-border taxable services and requires direct VAT refund treatment, the non-resident must apply through the e-Tax Bureau or in person at the competent tax office for a non-resident enterprise tax file number by submitting the tax registration form, constitutional documents or foreign business registration certificate, appointing an authorized tax representative, and providing a bank account. If the income is collected by a withholding agent, the withholding agent performs the withholding registration and the non-resident should obtain a certificate of tax residence from its home jurisdiction for treaty relief. The non-resident must retain the assigned tax file number for all VAT invoice, refund, and treaty-benefit filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the entity level; there is no pass-through treatment. For VAT, the default status is small-scale VAT taxpayer until VAT general taxpayer registration becomes compulsory or is voluntarily elected, and general taxpayer status is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000.

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election exists in Chinese domestic tax law. The only relevant statutory election is the VAT general taxpayer election: an enterprise below the compulsory threshold may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer to obtain input VAT credit, subject to STA approval, and once registered as a general taxpayer it may not switch back to small-scale taxpayer status unless the STA provides otherwise. Small-scale taxpayers may also elect the simplified levy method where applicable. No other entity classification elections are available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales is the compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are exempt from VAT under the current 2023-2027 policy window, but if annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 the entity must register as a general VAT taxpayer regardless of the small-scale exemption. For SCADA/construction-type installation supplies, the same turnover threshold applies for general taxpayer status, with the applicable VAT headline rate being 13% for equipment sales, 9% for construction and installation services when treated as construction, and 6% for standalone modern technical or engineering services if separately contracted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating for exported goods applies only where the goods are physically exported from the customs territory of China, the exporter files an export customs declaration, the sale is made under an export sales contract, and the exporter receives the export sales proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory foreign-exchange repatriation period. For SCADA and substation-automation services supplied to a foreign customer, zero-rating or exemption is available for cross-border taxable services consumed entirely outside China and not within the Cross-Border Taxable Services Negative List; the exporter must evidence the offshore nature of the service, the service agreement, actual delivery and consumption outside China, and receipt of foreign currency. The zero-rated export sale must be reported in the VAT return at rate zero, and the attributable input VAT is refunded through the export tax refund and exemption mechanism.

  • Export Customs Declaration (electronic export goods declaration with customs release and clearance record issued by China Customs)
  • Commercial invoice and signed export sales or engineering service contract
  • Packing list and weight memo
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical movement of goods from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt and settlement voucher issued by a SAFE-designated bank showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT invoice, special or ordinary, issued at zero rate under the VAT Invoice Management System
  • Export tax refund and exemption declaration with reconciled input VAT allocation worksheet filed through the e-Tax Bureau export tax refund module
  • For cross-border services: service contract, proof of offshore consumption, foreign-exchange receipt voucher, and customs documentation for any temporarily exported installation equipment

China operates real-time VAT invoice administration through the STA VAT Invoice Management System. General VAT taxpayers must issue VAT special invoices or ordinary invoices under the tax control system, and the fully digital electronic invoice system generates tax-control invoice numbers centrally and transmits issuance details to the e-Tax Bureau in near real time with anti-fraud QR codes and tax-control digital signatures. There is no physical stamping of fully digital invoices, but all invoices must be declared in the VAT return and reconciled with the electronic invoice data. Enterprises must use an STA-certified electronic invoice service platform, process red-letter credit notes through the e-Tax Bureau, and follow the mandatory monthly VAT filing, invoice quota, and exception-reporting procedures.

Export tax refund claims are subject to paperless classified management, risk-rule screening, and on-site verification by the STA and China Customs. If the exported goods are not supported by the required customs declaration, FIRC, or invoice evidence, or if foreign exchange is not repatriated by the statutory deadline, the tax authority may disallow the zero-rating, claw back the refund received, and impose daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05 percent plus statutory interest. Fraudulent or fictitious export claims constitute export-tax-refund fraud; penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law include an administrative penalty of 50 percent to 500 percent of the unpaid or fraudulently refunded tax, loss of export tax refund status and taxpayer credit rating, and unlimited retrospective recovery in fraud cases. Criminal referral may follow under the Criminal Law, rendering the responsible persons liable to imprisonment and fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with a bifurcated convertibility structure. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, and royalties, are generally fully convertible, provided that genuine underlying documentation and SAFE filing requirements are satisfied. Capital account items, however, remain heavily regulated and require case-by-case approval, filing, or registration through SAFE or the PBOC. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) established under Chinese law must register its foreign exchange capital base with SAFE and may only convert registered capital into Renminbi within the scope of approved operating purposes, subject to a genuine transaction verification mechanism. Profit remittance abroad is permitted after annual audit, but the enterprise must demonstrate tax clearance, filing of the Foreign Exchange Payment and Receipt Enterprise Declaration, and compliance with the circular on dividend remittance. Surrender requirements for export proceeds currently mandate that enterprises retain export foreign exchange receipts in their accounts, subject to a proportional repatriation quota and periodic reconciliation with SAFE. Practical hurdles include documentary rigidity, time lag in verification, the risk of false transaction rejections during anti-fraud screening, and the need to coordinate with tax bureaus for foreign exchange payment tax certificates. For a SCADA and substation automation integration contractor, services may be classified under current account services trade if properly contracted, but cross-border software licensing, maintenance retainers, and equipment imports could trigger technology import registration and capital account scrutiny. The overall assessment is that the Renminbi is convertible on current account with high compliance friction, while capital account transactions remain controlled and require structured, licensed banking intermediation to avoid penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and surrendered into a domestic foreign exchange account within the statutory timeline prescribed by SAFE; currently the repatriation deadline is 90 days from the date of export customs declaration unless extended under approved open-account arrangements. Mandatory conversion ratios are determined on a macro-prudential basis: exporters are required to convert at least 50% of export foreign exchange receipts into Renminbi through the domestic banking system, with the remaining balance permitted to be held in foreign currency, subject to quarterly position limits and the Risk-Oriented Foreign Exchange Receipts Verification System. For a WFOE engaged in cross-border SCADA equipment procurement and integration services, the enterprise must ensure every inward remittance is matched to a customs declaration or services trade contract, and the conversion must occur via a domestic designated bank with a valid SAFE registration code.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and neobank-style digital banking platforms are legally barred from serving as primary foreign exchange conversion and capital account settlement agents for foreign-invested enterprises in mainland China. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a China-resident WFOE, contemporaneous Local File is mandatory where annual related-party transactions reach any of these threshold amounts: (a) tangible-property transactions - total related-party purchases/sales above RMB 350 million; (b) intangible-property transactions - total above RMB 100 million; (c) financial-asset transactions (loans, guarantees, equity transfers) - total above RMB 100 million; (d) other related-party transactions that are not tangible/intangible/financial - above RMB 40 million; Master File is mandatory where total related-party transactions of the whole consolidated group exceed RMB 1 billion in the preceding year.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the PRC tax authorities apply OECD-equivalent substance-over-form doctrines through EIT Law Art. 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), Art. 18 (arm's length standard), Art. 46 (thin capitalization) and Art. 11/12 (beneficial owner/treaty shopping). A WFOE claiming DTA benefits of its non-resident shareholder must demonstrate genuine business purpose and that the recipient of dividends, interest, or royalties exercises core income-generating activities (CIGA) - for SCADA/substation automation this means owning employees, equipment, engineering tools, project-management decision-making, technical risk allocation, and bank accounts in the jurisdiction of residence; otherwise tax authorities may recharacterize payments as hidden dividends or deny treaty protection and impose transfer-pricing penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer-pricing evasion and sham service fee profit strip through a low-tax/zero-tax jurisdiction

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Arts. 41-44 and Art. 48 and SAT Announcement on transfer pricing documentation: tax authority recharacterizes the fee, adds back the entire deductible amount, applies an upward adjustment, levies 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge (Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 32), imposes a civil penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax under Art. 63, and may impose a supplementary interest of 5% above the base loan rate for significant adjustments under Art. 48.

Director personal liability: If the scheme is treated as intentional tax evasion, the criminally responsible director may be prosecuted under Criminal Law Art. 201: imprisonment up to three years and a fine for relatively large evaded tax, or three to seven years and a fine where the amount is huge; civilly, the directors can be ordered jointly and severally liable under Company Law's veil-piercing rules where they abused corporate personality to evade tax, and may be subject to an exit ban/blacklisting of the legal representative under tax collections and enforcement procedures.
False invoicing and VAT fraud embedded in SCADA project supply chain

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Art. 205 (false issuance of VAT special invoices) and Arts. 208/210 (falsely purchasing invoices, tax fraud) apply, with fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for particularly large amounts; PRC VAT and Tax Collection Administration Law Arts. 63/64 impose reinstatement of credit, 50%-500% civil penalties, and criminal referral for the representative/actual controller.

Director personal liability: Directors or actual controllers who order, approve, or knowingly sign such invoices are subject to criminal liability under Art. 205 as co-perpetrators; the court will pierce the corporate veil if the invoice fraud was used to drain assets and distribute illicit profits to the parent, making the directors personally liable to restore the defrauded tax revenue and compensate creditors; the responsible legal representative may be placed on the tax authority's serious-tax-crime blacklist and banned from exiting China.
Conduit dividend recycling and tax-treaty abuse - sham holding structure

Statutory sanction: Tax authority invokes EIT Law Art. 47 and Art. 48 (GAAR), as well as the PRC's limitation-on-benefits/beneficial-ownership clauses in DTAs; the treaty benefit is denied, the dividend is re-sourced as a domestic distribution taxed at 10%, the payer/recipient is assessed for the difference, plus late-payment surcharge under Art. 32 and a 50%-500% civil penalty under Art. 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and effect the conduit-chain resolution while knowing the arrangement lacks commercial rationale may be subject to personal civil liability for the corporate tax shortfall under such circumstances where they knowingly caused the corporation to violate tax law; in extreme cases, the criminal court treats the arrangement as concealment of taxable income under Criminal Law Art. 201 and Art. 28 (accomplices), exposing them to imprisonment for up to seven years plus fines; the legal representative can also be prevented from leaving the country until the tax obligation is settled.
