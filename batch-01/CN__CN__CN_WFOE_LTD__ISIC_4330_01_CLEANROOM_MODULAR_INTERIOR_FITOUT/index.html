Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For an ordinary WFOE limited liability company in China, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital. Under the Company Law, each shareholder must subscribe to the registered capital as set out in the Articles of Association and must actually make full payment within five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter period is imposed by special sector regulations. Contributions may be made in cash or in non-cash property lawfully available for contribution. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account; contributions are paid directly into the company's basic deposit or foreign currency capital account. However, because the WFOE will perform cleanroom modular interior fitout contracting, the registered capital and audited net assets must meet the thresholds of the provincial housing and urban-rural development authority when applying for Construction Enterprise Qualification (for example, Building Decoration and Finishing Engineering Professional Contractor).

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense approval from the Municipal Civil Air Defense Office is required only where the fitout is inside an existing civil air defense works area; otherwise no civil defense permit is applicable. Fire clearance: carry out fire protection design filing or review and completion acceptance filing or inspection with the Fire and Rescue Brigade, and obtain fire safety certification before lawful occupancy. Occupational clearance: file the construction site workplace safety plan with the local emergency management authority; ensure all special operation personnel hold PRC work safety certificates; and obtain stamped structural verification from a qualified design institute before installing cleanroom HVAC plant, partition walls, and raised floors where load-bearing or structural alterations are involved.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operated through the unified National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) web portal and the VAT invoice services platform.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE's Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the market-registration business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation; under the multi-license one-code integration system, the USCC is the tax registration number and the VAT registration number. Immediately after formation, the WFOE must complete tax-registration information confirmation and real-name authentication for the legal representative, financial officer, and tax officer in the e-Tax Bureau; verify the competent tax-authority district; confirm the taxpayer profile and tax type; decide/apply for small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status; apply for invoice type and invoice quota; and execute a three-party electronic tax-payment agreement with a local commercial bank. For a foreign non-resident entity that itself derives China-source income but has no Chinese legal entity, a non-resident enterprise tax registration/code can be established through the e-Tax non-resident tax service module, generally triggered by withholding obligations; no non-resident TIN is needed merely to hold WFOE equity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law for a WFOE: no check-the-box election, no treaty/domestic pass-through election, and no fiscal-union or group-consolidation election is available. The WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide taxable income. Under VAT, an entity below the CNY 5,000,000 general taxpayer threshold may elect general VAT taxpayer status to claim input credits, but this is an invoice-administration election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual VAT taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; tax registration is mandatory immediately upon business formation regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, a 0% VAT rate/refund applies only when the seller is a VAT general taxpayer; the goods are physically exported under customs supervision with a Customs Export Goods Declaration; foreign-exchange proceeds are repatriated to the WFOE in mainland China; and the customs declaration, commercial contract, VAT invoices, and SAFE trade-monitoring records match. Cross-border taxable services (including qualifying digital, software, R&D, design, and other catalogue services) are zero-rated only if listed in the statutory cross-border service zero-rating/exemption catalogue and consumed entirely outside mainland China; cleanroom construction/installation performed inside mainland China is not zero-rated and is normally 9% VAT, while a standalone supply of modular panels/equipment without installation may attract the 13% goods rate.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export release electronic manifest from China Customs)
  • Ocean bill of lading, air waybill, or international express shipment receipt evidencing export dispatch
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed overseas sales contract
  • Export sales VAT invoice(s) or export invoice documents
  • SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System record or bank receipt advice evidencing export proceeds repatriation
  • Letter of credit documents and bank settlement records, if used, as supplementary evidence
  • Export VAT refund (exemption) declaration data submitted through the e-Tax Bureau export refund module

China requires VAT invoices to be issued through the national Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice service on the e-Tax Bureau platform. Special VAT invoices are subject to central book management, invoice-number allocation, tax-rate/credit mapping, and near-real-time transmission of issuance data to the STA; input credits are claimed only after invoices are authenticated and matched through the VAT invoice service platform. There is no physical fiscal stamp, but anti-fraud controls operate through unique invoice identifiers, abnormal-invoice detection, real-time digital reporting, and certified API/batch interfaces for high-volume taxpayers.

Unverified or fraudulent zero-rating is subject to disallowance, clawback of the refunded VAT, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and further monetary penalties. Under the PRC Tax Administration Law, obtaining an export tax refund by false export declaration or false documentation exposes the taxpayer to recovery of the refund and a penalty of one to five times the refunded amount. Criminal liability for export-tax-refund fraud under the PRC Criminal Law can extend to life imprisonment and confiscation of property for large-scale offences. The limitation periods for assessment do not apply to fraud or concealment, and STA/Customs/SAFE cross-matching can result in 'abnormal taxpayer' status, restriction on invoice issuance, loss of export refund facilitation, and heightened audit scrutiny on all future export declarations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a hybrid convertibility regime: current account items are generally convertible upon successful verification by designated banks, but capital account items remain heavily regulated. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in cleanroom modular interior fit-out must track all cross-border receipts and payments under the relevant trade and service mes this is a test. ni.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of customs declaration, extendable to 180 days for high-tech or project-based exports with prior SAFE approval. Since 2012, there is no mandatory conversion quota; enterprises may either retain proceeds in a Foreign Exchange Settlement Account (within the bank-approved quota) or sell them to a designated bank for RMB on a voluntary basis. The bank calculates the net export amount and applies a 25% mandatory surrender ratio for all export proceeds exceeding RMB 500,000 per transaction; for cleanroom fit-out service contracts, the ratio applies to the full invoiced amount after deductible offshore expenses. The proceeds must enter the state-run Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Payment and Receipt Verification System before any onward disbursement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs and offshore digital payment platforms have effectively zero viability for holding WFOE transaction deposits or executing local RMB clearing in China. These entities are not licensed as Chinese commercial banks, cannot open interbank settlement accounts at PBOC, and are legally barred from servicing domestic-currency liabilities for mainland-incorporated companies. They may serve a supplemental role only as third-party payment processors (e.g., Paytm-style aggregators) for collection of progress payments from foreign clients, provided they partner with a licensed Chinese bank for FX conversion and remittance. However, they cannot act as the primary banking rail or liquidity warehouse. Any attempt to route operational capital through an EMI would trigger automatic AML and FX control red flags with SAFE and the local branch of the PBOC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for the WFOE must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the Capital Escrow Account maintained at the selected Tier-1 bank, using the settlement code 1101 (direct investment - registered capital injection) in the field 61 reference. The remitter must be the actual foreign shareholder, and the bank will verify the inward remittance against the business registration certificate and the SAFE Filing Certificate issued by the local SAFE branch. The source of funds must be evidenced by the shareholder's audited corporate accounts and the most recent six-month bank statements. Within five banking days of receipt, the bank will issue a Foreign Exchange Capital Verification Report (FDI Report), and the local certified public accountant (CPA) will use this report to complete the capital verification and issue an audit confirmation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border fintech EMIs and offshore digital payment platforms have effectively zero viability for holding WFOE transaction deposits or executing local RMB clearing in China. These entities are not licensed as Chinese commercial banks, cannot open interbank settlement accounts at PBOC, and are legally barred from servicing domestic-currency liabilities for mainland-incorporated companies. They may serve a supplemental role only as third-party payment processors (e.g., Paytm-style aggregators) for collection of progress payments from foreign clients, provided they partner with a licensed Chinese bank for FX conversion and remittance. However, they cannot act as the primary banking rail or liquidity warehouse. Any attempt to route operational capital through an EMI would trigger automatic AML and FX control red flags with SAFE and the local branch of the PBOC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File thresholds under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42: annual related-party tangible property transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; and related-party service transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. The Master File is required when the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and China-related transaction or entity conditions are met.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regimes, but economic substance is fundamental to Chinese CIT treatment. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of a cleanroom modular fitout contractor must be performed in China: contract negotiation, design engineering, procurement, cleanroom installation management, quality control, payroll, and project invoicing. If the WFOE merely contracts, bills, and remits profits to a foreign related company while all real CIGA occur abroad, the Chinese tax authority may deem a Permanent Establishment, recharacterize the WFOE under General Anti-Avoidance Rule (PRC EIT Law Article 47), apply CFC rules to undistributed offshore profits, or deny treaty benefits. The entity must maintain local substance records including office lease, employee social insurance, project permits, board minutes, engineering drawings, supplier agreements, and bank flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, and 48; Tax Administration Law Articles 36 and 56; SAT transfer pricing regulations permit the tax authority to make a primary adjustment, secondary adjustment, and impose interest on the resulting underpayment at the lending benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points. If the manipulation is found fraudulent, a 50% to 500% tax evasion penalty under Tax Administration Law Article 63 may apply, and criminal referral follows under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: If directors and the legal representative knowingly sign artificial service agreements, route invoices through shell entities, or conceal the true project margin, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law (2023 Revision) Article 23. Directors may be personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and civil asset restitution to the tax authority. They may face exit restriction and blacklisting as 'dishonest taxpayers', and criminal tax evasion convictions carry imprisonment of up to 7 years plus fines for especially large amounts.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3, 37 and 40; Tax Administration Law Article 69 imposes a fine of 50% to 100% of the tax that should have been withheld on the withholding agent. Under Article 63, disguised distributions may be recharacterized as dividends, with additional tax, interest, and a 50% to 500% penalty. The principal purpose test and GAAR under Article 47 allow the tax authority to deny treaty benefits retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors and the financial officer who sign false WHT declarations or arrange circular fund flows to avoid withholding may be jointly and severally liable with the WFOE because the limited liability shield is abused to evades statutory debts. Civil restitution may require the directors to repay the unwithheld tax from personal assets. They also face travel bans, 'Dishonest Taxpayer' blacklisting, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years and monetary fines.
Falsified Corporate Income Tax Return and Construction Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 prescribes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax for false declaration and refusal to pay. Article 32 imposes daily 0.05% late-payment interest. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for tax evasion where the underpayment is large and exceeds 10% of the total tax payable; the responsible legal representative and CFO may be criminally detained, and the company faces forfeiture of property and debarment from public cleanroom construction tenders.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the WFOE's corporate veil under PRC Company Law (2023 Revision) Article 23 when the legal representative uses the company as a shell to conceal revenue and evade tax, holding the director personally liable for the full tax debt and civil damages. The director's personal assets may be seized, domestic and overseas travel blocked, the individual entered on the joint punishment list for untrustworthy taxpayers, and criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years imposed for large-scale fraudulent tax evasion.
