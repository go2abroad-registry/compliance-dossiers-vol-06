Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

China's Company Law (2023 Revision) imposes no universal minimum paid-up capital for a limited liability company. Registered capital is subscribed under the Articles of Association and must be fully paid in within five years after establishment, unless an applicable industry regulation requires a shorter contribution period. For a construction contractor holding a foundation specialty qualification under ISIC 4390, the competent construction authority may require paid-in capital sufficient to meet the qualification class and contractual liability thresholds. No blocked escrow account is imposed by the corporate registry for ordinary WFOE incorporation; however, foreign exchange controls require the foreign investor to remit contributions to a dedicated foreign-invested enterprise capital settlement account opened with a licensed Chinese bank and supervised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defence Fire Safety Inspection Certificate for temporary site offices, sheds, welding areas and storage compounds; structural verification of diaphragm wall guide walls, deep excavation support systems and access roads; occupational health and safety pre-start review under the Law on Work Safety; special equipment registration certificates for cranes, slurry pumps and pressure vessels; safe work permits for confined space entry, deep excavation and underground operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the electronic tax portal 'E-Tax Bureau' (e-Tax electronic tax office) available at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn for entity registration and ongoing compliance filings

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first obtain the Foreign Investment Business License and other statutory licenses from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), then complete tax registration within 30 days of the business license issuance. The WFOE must submit the original business license, articles of association, incorporation documents, foreign investment information report, legal representative identity documents, and lease or ownership proof for the registered business premises to the local competent tax authority. The tax authority issues a unique Tax Identification Number (TIN) in the format of the unified social credit code (18-digit USCC) which serves as the taxpayer identification number, plus a separate taxpayer registration certificate if required. Tax registration may be completed online through the E-Tax Bureau portal by logging in with the enterprise electronic certificate (e-tax certificate USB key or digital CA certificate), filing the 'Tax Registration Form for Enterprises', and uploading the required scanned documents. After approval, the entity is assigned one or more tax identifiers for enterprise income tax, value-added tax, municipal construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, and other applicable taxes. The WFOE should also register for foreign exchange administration and open a RMB and foreign currency bank account with a designated domestic bank, and the banking and foreign exchange registration need not be completed before tax registration but must be coordinated for operational continuity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not provide a check-the-box classification regime comparable to the United States. The WFOE is automatically classified as a resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. No hybrid mismatch election is available to a WFOE under domestic law; however, the entity may apply treaty eligibility for reduced withholding rates under an applicable double tax agreement and must document beneficial ownership. The only statutory elections relevant include the choice of foreign currency accounting if approved by the tax authority, the simplified VAT computation method for certain eligible construction services where the taxpayer elects the deemed deduction method (simplified method) for VAT on construction services, and the annual election for allocation of common expenses under transfer pricing documentation rules, but these are not entity classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT registration applies to all entities and individuals whose taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 per 12-month rolling period for general taxpayer status. Small-scale taxpayers with taxable annual sales below RMB 5,000,000 may register voluntarily but are not obligated to register as general taxpayers unless their turnover exceeds the threshold. Foreign enterprises engaged in construction services within China must register for VAT regardless of the threshold if they perform taxable services in China, and they may appoint a domestic withholding agent if they do not have a permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT law and implementing regulations, exported goods are zero-rated, provided that the taxpayer is a registered general VAT taxpayer and the goods actually leave Chinese customs territory under an export customs declaration (export declaration), and the foreign exchange received from the export is repatriated and settled in China under foreign exchange regulations. During the construction of deep foundation and diaphragm walls, any exported construction materials, machinery, or equipment permanently supplied to a foreign project may qualify for zero-rating only if they are separately declared as exports and title passes to the overseas buyer. Services provided to overseas recipients for use entirely outside China, including certain international construction services, are zero-rated or VAT-exempt under the 'zero-rated cross-border services' category if the recipient is a foreign entity and the service is delivered and consumed wholly outside mainland China. A WFOE must obtain a general taxpayer status to claim output VAT at 0% and must file the export declaration with the customs authority and the VAT return with the STA, and the refund is processed through the export tax rebate (export VAT refund) system. For deep foundation slurry trenching and diaphragm wall contracting, the service inherently relates to real estate located in China and does not qualify as zero-rated; rather, VAT at 9% applies to the domestic construction service output, while imported equipment and consumables used in China attract import VAT with potential credit. Only goods physically exported from China, such as surplus machinery, tools, or prefabricated materials not installed domestically, may be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Form for Exports or Bill of Exit)
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill / International Throughway Bill for the exported construction equipment or materials
  • Foreign Exchange Collection and Settlement Confirmation (Bank Receipt/Inward Remittance Certificate showing the export proceeds credited to a Chinese bank account)
  • Export Invoice issued under China's VAT invoice system with 0% tax rate and export invoice notation
  • Export Contract and Proforma Invoice evidencing the sale of goods to an overseas purchaser
  • Proof of export cargo inspection and customs release (Customs Release Certificate) when required for specific controlled goods

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice system integrated with the STA's VAT invoice system. General VAT taxpayers must use the official UKey or tax-control equipment to issue fully digital electronic invoices ('fully digitalized electronic invoices' with a 20-digit invoice number) through the E-Tax Bureau platform. Invoices are transmitted in real time to the national VAT invoice service platform and subject to automated anti-fraud verification via tax control codes, digital signatures, and the national electronic invoice monitoring system. Since April 2025, the STA is expanding full digital invoice pilots nationwide; all invoices issued by registered enterprises must include the statutory QR code and unique invoice number, and data is digitally cleared in near real time. There is no separate fiscal clearance stamp, but invoice issuance authority is granted only for already-registered tax codes, and the system automatically reconciles invoice data against VAT returns and customs import/export declarations.

The STA and customs authorities jointly audit export VAT refund claims and zero-rating documentation. If an export claim is found to be fraudulent, non-qualifying, or improperly documented, the taxpayer must reverse the zero-rating and repay the output VAT, may be denied future refunds, and may face late payment surcharges of 0.05% per day on the underpaid tax. Deliberate fraudulent export tax refunds are subject to criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties of confiscation of illegal gains and imprisonment of up to life imprisonment for amounts above RMB 5,000,000 or with aggravating circumstances. Administrative fines of up to five times the fraudulent refund amount may be imposed, and credit ratings may be downgraded, leading to enhanced scrutiny and mandatory inspection for subsequent refunds. If foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated and settled within the required 180-day time limit without a valid regulatory extension, the tax authority may deem the export sale as a domestic taxable supply and reassess output VAT at the standard rate plus surcharges and penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible on the current account following the IMF Article VIII obligations since 1996, meaning trade-related receipts and payments, dividends, interest, and royalties may generally be settled directly through designated foreign exchange banks with genuine transaction documentation. The capital account, however, remains heavily controlled: foreign direct investment (FDI) into a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) requires prior approval or filing with the Ministry of Commerce and at least a simplified foreign exchange registration with SAFE (via banks under the SAFE FITC system). Capital injected as registered paid-in capital is credited to a foreign exchange capital account (外汇资本金账户) and may be converted into RMB through a bank under the 'Payment-Use' model, strictly for approved business purposes such as procurement of domestic materials, payroll, or land lease, but not for working capital loans, equity investments, or speculative hedging. Settlement of FDI capital requires a domestic investment confirmation and, for amounts exceeding the current account threshold, a non-bank capital account payment instruction. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder is permitted only after an annual statutory audit, tax clearance certificates, and a board resolution approving the dividend distribution; the bank must verify that the WFOE has completed its corporate income tax withholding on dividends and that the underlying profits were genuinely generated from operational activities and not from currency appreciation or non-operating gains. There is no surrender or conversion quota on current-account transactions, but all outbound remittances exceeding USD 50,000 per transaction require documentary evidence of the underlying transaction and may be subject to further SAFE oversight. The overall regime necessitates a disciplined treasury structure that segregates capital-account inflows, operational RMB accounts, and foreign-currency settlement accounts to ensure full traceability and auditability under SAFE's 'Revision of Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Direct Investment' and the Personal Information Protection Law cross-border data transfer rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of 2012, SAFE removed the mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; enterprises are allowed to retain all foreign currency receipts in their export settlement foreign exchange accounts. However, repatriation is mandatory: all export foreign currency receipts must be received into the enterprise's domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration (except where SAFE grants an extended period for bona fide operational reasons). No fixed conversion ratio is imposed; the enterprise may choose to hold the foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB at the prevailing market rate at any time. The bank will verify export revenue authenticity through the electronic customs declaration data, logistics bills, and the Enterprise-Individual Foreign Exchange settlement system. Any receipt that cannot be linked to a verified export declaration within the 180-day window may be classified as a capital-account inflow and subjected to additional declaration and approval requirements.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), which operates under the PBoC as the exclusive administrative enforcing agency for foreign exchange regulations in the People's Republic of China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and virtual account providers have essentially zero legal viability as primary banking rails for a China-domiciled WFOE. The PBoC and SAFE require all foreign exchange and RMB capital transactions to be conducted through chartered domestic banks that are connected to the China Foreign Exchange Trade System (CFETS) and subject to SAFE's direct regulatory reporting. International EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer cannot provide a domestic RMB account number or process SAFE-regulated capital inflows; their services are limited to ancillary cross-border payment collection in non-RMB currencies for e-commerce or small-amount trade, and they are legally barred from offering local settlement to Chinese legal entities without a banking license from the CBIRC. The only acceptable use of a foreign EMI is as a pre-funding convenience for the offshore shareholder to aggregate funds before instructing a SWIFT transfer to the WFOE's BOC or ICBC capital account. Any attempt to use an EMI as the WFOE's principal operating account would constitute a breach of the Administrative Measures for the Foreign Exchange Settlement of Capital and violate the renminbi settlement discipline.

Where tax evasion exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the amount of tax payable, criminal liability may arise under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment of up to three years for relatively large amounts and up to seven years for huge amounts, together with fines. Major tax violations are published by the State Taxation Administration, resulting in joint disciplinary blacklisting, travel restrictions, and credit impairment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Chinese enterprise must prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) for related-party transactions exceeding: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions. A Master File is due where the multinational group's combined revenue is RMB 2 billion or above and a Country-by-Country Report is required where group consolidated revenue is RMB 5.5 billion or above. For the WFOE, all intercompany construction, machinery rental, know-how, and management service transactions must be benchmarked under the arm's-length standard.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Special Tax Adjustment rules, rather than a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime. For a WFOE performing ISIC 4390 deep slurry trenching and underground continuous cut-off wall works, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be genuinely performed by the WFOE: project bidding, geotechnical site assessment, slurry mixing and circulation, diaphragm wall excavation, reinforcement installation, concrete pouring, quality inspection, safety supervision, equipment ownership or leasing, and invoicing. If the WFOE lacks employees, management, physical premises, equipment, or risk-bearing capacity, tax authorities may recharacterize income, deny treaty benefits, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and assess penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Invoicing and Inflated Project Costs

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Administration and Tax Collection (TAL), the taxpayer is ordered to pay the underpaid tax within a prescribed period, plus a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount, and daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of unpaid tax. Under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, tax evasion above RMB 50,000 constituting more than 10% of payable tax is criminal tax fraud; Article 205 imposes punishment for issuing false special VAT invoices, including sentences up to life imprisonment for extraordinarily large amounts.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced under PRC Company Law where abuse of corporate limited liability fraudulently harms creditors or state tax revenue. Directors who organize false invoicing face personal civil restitution for the tax loss, asset freezing, travel bans and exit-control restrictions, inclusion on the serious tax violation blacklist with consequences for visas and business licenses, criminal imprisonment up to seven years for aggravated evasion, and disqualification from serving as directors.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse on Outbound Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under TAL Article 69, a withholding agent that fails to collect or remit withholding tax is subject to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the uncollected tax, plus the taxpayer remains liable for the tax. Under Article 47 of the EIT Law, the GAAR may disregard the treaty arrangement, recharacterize the shareholder as non-eligible, and assess the full 10% WHT with late surcharges. The unilateral declaration of treaty benefits without supporting TRC and beneficial ownership documentation is an administrative violation and may be treated as tax fraud in aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve unlawful distributions or sign false treaty applications expose themselves to personal joint liability for the unremitted tax where company assets are stripped or the company becomes insolvent. Civil asset restitution may be ordered, travel bans and blacklisting under the joint disciplinary mechanism apply for unpaid taxes, and criminal liability can arise for intentional withholding tax evasion, including imprisonment up to seven years and fines.
Shell Company Structure and Fake Economic Substance with Fraudulent Preferential Claim

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Special Tax Adjustment rules, the tax authority may recharacterize the entire arrangement, attribute income to the entity performing real functions and risks, deny the Small Low-Profit preferential rate, and impose a tax shortfall with late surcharge. TAL Article 63 imposes a 50% to 500% fine for false returns. False preferential tax claims also trigger administrative blacklisting and multi-department joint punishment, including suspension of qualification for government construction tenders.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law when shareholders use the limited liability entity to evade tax obligations, causing directors who are also shareholders to bear unlimited joint liability. Directors signing false substance certificates or tax preferences are personally liable for restitution of tax benefits, subject to asset seizure, blacklisting, exit bans under enforcement measures, and criminal liability for fraud if the false claims exceed criminal thresholds. In geotechnical contracting, the absence of licensed professional engineers on site can also lead to revocation of safety and construction licenses and personal criminal liability for workplace safety incidents.
