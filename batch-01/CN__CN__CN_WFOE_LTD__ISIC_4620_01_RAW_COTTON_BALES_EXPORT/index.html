Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists for an ordinary trading or brokerage WFOE. The shareholders determine registered capital in the Articles of Association, but under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law all subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from the issuance of the business license unless a licensed-sector rule imposes a shorter period. For this low-risk international cotton brokerage WFOE there are no blocked escrow or mandatory initial deposit rules; foreign currency capital, once remitted, must pass through the company's designated foreign exchange bank account and be reported to SAFE through the FDI information registration system before use.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); taxpayer filings and registrations are made through the official STA Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform and the competent local STA office; customs matters are simultaneously governed by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) through the International Trade Single Window

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) formed in China first registers with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all Chinese taxes. The WFOE must then complete post-incorporation tax registration at the competent local STA through the e-Tax platform, including real-name authentication, tax type and scope identification, legal representative and financial officer enrollment, activation of the electronic tax filing account, and linkage to the VAT invoice management system. For foreign non-resident enterprises without an establishment or place of business in China, the non-resident enterprise must obtain a non-resident tax identification number either by direct registration with the STA or through a withholding agent, in accordance with the Measures for the Tax Registration of Non-Resident Enterprises issued by the STA, in order to satisfy withholding VAT/income tax and invoice-related obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Law, and is concurrently a VAT taxpayer classified as either a general VAT taxpayer or a small-scale VAT taxpayer depending on turnover)

Available election forms

None equivalent to US check-the-box; China does not offer pass-through taxation elections for a WFOE. Available limited elections under VAT: a small-scale taxpayer may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer where accounting and input VAT credit requirements are satisfied; a general VAT taxpayer may not elect downward. No hybrid mismatch elections exist under Chinese domestic tax law; general anti-avoidance rules and interest limitation provisions apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of cumulative taxable sales in any 12 consecutive months triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold the enterprise is registered as a small-scale VAT taxpayer. A foreign/non-resident seller with no place of business in China that makes VAT-taxable supplies in China is required to register for VAT or have the VAT collected through a withholding agent irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under China's VAT export exemption and refund regime, a registered general VAT taxpayer is eligible for the 0% VAT rate on goods that are physically exported from China, declared to and cleared by GACC, and supported by written export contracts and irrevocable documentary evidence that the goods left the customs territory and were sold to an overseas buyer. The enterprise must keep separate records for export and domestic sales, retain the full export documentation package, collect the export proceeds from abroad in foreign currency through a designated bank, and have valid input VAT invoices for the exported raw cotton bales. The exported goods must not be under export prohibition or require a statutory export license; the payload confirms that no statutory license is required for the raw cotton bale export scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the official electronic export declaration record generated by GACC through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (original ocean or multimodal transport document) or equivalent air/rail/road waybill evidencing movement of the raw cotton bales from China to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the Chinese statutory equivalent export foreign-exchange receipt voucher issued by a designated Chinese foreign-exchange bank confirming inbound proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract/purchase order evidencing the transaction terms
  • Original VAT special invoices issued to the WFOE by upstream suppliers for the raw cotton bales, substantiating the input VAT claimed for refund
  • Customs inspection or valuation records where applicable to demonstrate export authenticity

All VAT invoices are issued through the STA's VAT Invoice Management System and the fully digitalized electronic invoice framework. The enterprise's invoicing system or ERP must integrate with the e-Tax platform via STA-approved API interfaces for real-time invoice issuance, upload, and authenticity verification. Each VAT invoice carries a unique national invoice number and is transmitted to the STA central system at issuance. Transaction data is monitored in real time through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform, which validates invoice authenticity and detects abnormal issuance. There is no physical anti-fraud stamp in the digital invoice regime; compliance is enforced through digital risk-control flags, blacklist monitoring, and mandatory red-letter invoice procedures for corrections or cancellations.

The STA and GACC jointly subject export VAT refund claims to strict audit and risk-monitoring. If a zero-rating/export-refund claim is found to be based on false customs declarations, forged VAT invoices, fictitious export transactions, or failure to substantiate foreign-exchange repatriation, the tax authority will claw back the full refunded VAT, impose late payment interest, deny or suspend the enterprise's export tax refund qualifications, and impose administrative fines of up to five times the refunded amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. In serious cases involving fraudulent export-tax-refund conduct, criminal liability arises under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, punishable by monetary fines, asset confiscation, and imprisonment of varying terms depending on the amount and circumstances. Retrospective review can extend into prior tax periods within statutory limitation periods, and fraud claims are subject to extended recovery and adjudication timelines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade-related cross-border payments and profit repatriation, provided that supporting documentation such as contracts, invoices, and customs declarations is submitted to the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, notably foreign direct investment (FDI) capital injection, outward investment, cross-border loans, and securities investment, remain subject to a qualified, registration-based approval regime administered by SAFE. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) engaged in raw cotton export trading, the central operational FX rule is the SAFE 'registration of FDI' requirement, under which the foreign investor must complete a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate and open a 'FDI capital account' before injecting equity capital. Once capital is injected in foreign currency, the WFOE may either retain it in a foreign currency capital account or convert it into RMB through a domestic bank at the market rate; however, the use of such RMB funds is strictly audited and cannot be diverted beyond the registered business scope. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory; instead, exporters may retain up to the approved quota in foreign currency settlement accounts, but all export receipts must be repatriated to China via designated banking channels within the period set by the customs declaration and SAFE's trade verification system, typically 90 days for open account or as specified in the export contract. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is permitted annually after audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement, and completion of the foreign exchange registration for dividends. The principal hurdles are documentary precision, alignment between Customs declaration amounts and banking settlement, and the risk of administrative penalties for late surrender or mismatched invoice numbering. Overall, the RMB is current-account convertible in practice for genuine trade transactions, with capital account clearing requiring layered SAFE registrations and 'true-to-trade' verification. The WFOE must maintain meticulous trade documentation and a qualified finance officer familiar with SAFE's digital trade monitoring platform to avoid frozen settlement channels.

In addition, the following quantitative benchmarks confirm and intensify the audit presentation requirement: (i) if the WFOE has total assets exceeding RMB 50 million on the balance-sheet date, annual operating revenue exceeding RMB 30 million, or more than 200 employees, it is classified as a non-small enterprise and a full certified CPA audit report must be retained for tax, customs, bank-lending and parent-consolidation purposes; (ii) any cross-border foreign exchange payment or reinvestment requiring a CPA net-asset or source-of-funds certificate will mandate a certified audit report; and (iii) if SAMR selects the WFOE in its random annual-report inspection, an independent CPA audit must be produced regardless of size.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% gross withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise that has no permanent establishment in China, under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and its Implementation Regulations, unless reduced by an applicable double tax treaty.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return and final settlement must be filed and settled within 5 months after fiscal year-end, which for a China calendar tax year means no later than May 31 of the following year; quarterly provisional CIT returns must be filed within 15 days after the close of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 50 million for intangible property, RMB 50 million for intercompany services, or RMB 50 million for interest/financing. Master File is mandatory where total related-party transactions in the prior year exceed RMB 1 billion. Documentation must be contemporaneous and prepared in accordance with SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a zero-tax ESR regime, but the Enterprise Income Tax Law, GAAR, and treaty beneficial ownership rules impose an economic substance requirement. For a raw cotton export WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities include contract negotiation and execution, pricing and risk assumption, staff functions in China, bank and letter-of-credit management, and actual control of the trade cycle. If the WFOE is only a paper entity while the true economic functions are performed offshore, authorities may deny treaty benefits, reallocate profit, pierce the shell structure and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Export tax rebate and invoice fraud

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Art. 204 punishes defrauding the state of export-tax refunds with up to 5 years' imprisonment for relatively large amounts, 5-10 years for large amounts, and 10 years to life for particularly large amounts, with monetary fines up to one to five times the defrauded amount and confiscation of property. Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 63 additionally requires repayment of evaded tax plus a penalty of 50%-500% of the amount underpaid and a daily late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who participate in false invoicing face criminal imprisonment and personal fine liability. The corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law where the WFOE is used as an instrument of fraud, exposing personal assets to civil restitution and judicial asset preservation. The tax authority may issue an exit-exclusion notice preventing directors from leaving China, and directors can be placed on the dishonesty blacklist.
Transfer pricing evasion, thin capitalization and absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Arts. 41, 43, 46, 47 and 48, the tax authority may adjust the related-party transaction, disallow excessive interest, recharacterize the arrangement, impose special-interest at 5% annually on underpaid tax and levy a 50%-500% penalty on the tax underpaid. Failure to file required contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation also triggers separate penalties and activation of discretionary adverse assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham pricing contracts may be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts where the court finds an abuse of the corporate form. Civil asset restitution and travel bans apply, and deliberate tax evasion from TP manipulation reaches criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines and confiscation.
Withholding tax default, dividend stripping and DTA conduit abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold tax is liable for a fine of 50%-300% of the amount that should have been withheld; collection proceedings may be enforced against corporate assets, plus 0.05% daily default interest. If the non-resident beneficiary is unavailable or foreign recovery fails, Chinese enforcement will target the WFOE and its controlling persons.

Director personal liability: A director who orchestrates dividend stripping or treaty-shopping can be held personally and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The legal representative may be prohibited from leaving China under the exit-exclusion regime, named on the dishonesty blacklist, barred from serving as a director, and subject to criminal prosecution for tax evasion or fraudulent avoidance up to 7 years' imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced for personal asset execution where the WFOE was no more than a fraudulent conduit.
