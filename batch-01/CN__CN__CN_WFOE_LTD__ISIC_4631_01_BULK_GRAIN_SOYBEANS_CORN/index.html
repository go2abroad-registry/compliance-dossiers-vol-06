Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a standard WFOE under PRC Company Law. The registered capital must be subscribed in full by shareholders within five years from establishment, unless a special industry licensing rule imposes a shorter period or a higher threshold. There is no requirement for an upfront blocked escrow account; however, the actual amount of paid-in capital must be traceable through bank records and documented in the capital contribution schedule annexed to the Articles of Association. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; registration, filing, and invoicing are performed through the nationwide Electronic Tax Administration (E-Tax) online portal operated by STA and local tax service bureaus, integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV system.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE limited liability company, tax registration is integrated with incorporation before the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). Upon issuance of the business license, the WFOE is automatically assigned a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the TIN and VAT registration number; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must then activate its taxpayer account at the competent tax service bureau through the E-Tax portal using real-name authentication of the legal representative and financial officer, after which STA prescribes the applicable taxes, including VAT, enterprise income tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and stamp duty. Before claiming export zero-rating, the WFOE must also complete the export VAT refund registration in the E-Tax export-tax module and register with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through its designated bank for goods-trade foreign exchange receipts. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without any Chinese establishment, statutory registration is completed under the non-resident enterprise tax registration procedure at the competent Chinese tax authority of the payer or withholding agent; the non-resident entity submits its home-country certificate of incorporation, tax treaty residence certificate, board authorization or power of attorney, and the relevant cross-border contract, and is assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number for withholding, contract filing, and electronic declaration. No minimum contributed capital is required for WFOE tax registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A WFOE limited liability company is a PRC tax resident and is taxed as a corporation at the entity level; it is not a pass-through entity.

Available election forms

None for entity classification. PRC Corporate Income Tax law does not provide a check-the-box election, pass-through election, or hybrid-mismatch election; the WFOE is always an opaque resident enterprise. The only material election is VAT status: a taxpayer with annual taxable turnover at or below RMB 5,000,000 may apply to be treated as a small-scale taxpayer rather than a general VAT taxpayer, or may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer on specified conditions; this election affects input VAT deduction and invoicing but does not alter income tax entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every Chinese enterprise must complete tax registration upon incorporation; there is no economic turnover threshold below which entity-level tax registration is excused. For compulsory general VAT taxpayer classification, annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 trigger mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; at or below RMB 5,000,000, the taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer, which applies the statutory simplified levy rate applicable from time to time rather than the normal VAT input-credit mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rating is available when: (1) the WFOE is a registered VAT general taxpayer with export business in its approved scope; (2) the physical soybeans/corn leave mainland China customs territory and are covered by a Customs export declaration; (3) the export sales contract, commercial invoice, payment, and transport document name the same exporter and a non-resident buyer; (4) the goods are actually shipped out of a PRC port; and (5) the export proceeds are collected in foreign exchange through a designated Chinese bank within the statutory repatriation period. Qualifying cross-border services, such as vessel brokerage or chartering services supplied to an overseas party and consumed wholly outside mainland China, may be zero-rated or exempt if within the STA cross-border service catalogue; a brokerage service rendered for a domestic Chinese party or consumed in China remains taxable at 6%. China mandates digitalized fapiao VAT invoices under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. General taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the STA E-Tax platform or through STA-certified tax-control software using STA-published system-to-system interface standards; ERP and accounting system integrations may connect only via the official digital invoice interface. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime for the renminbi (RMB). Under the current account, RMB is fully convertible for bona fide trade and service transactions; however, every cross-border payment must be supported by genuine underlying contracts and undergoes rigorous verification by the settlement bank. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, portfolio investment, cross-border loans, and guarantees, remain subject to approval, registration, or quota limits administered by SAFE. For a WFOE, the registered capital inflows must be credited to a designated capital escrow account, and subsequent RMB conversions must comply with the Negative List and the Foreign Investment Law. Profit remittances by WFOEs are permitted after annual audits, corporate income tax finalisation, and submission of audited financials and tax clearances to the remitting bank; the bank must verify the absence of any outstanding foreign exchange liabilities or unresolved fines. Additionally, surplus working capital from current operations can be converted into foreign exchange only within SAFE's operational current account framework, and any deviation triggers supervisory inquiry.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE reform, the mandatory surrender of export proceeds has been eliminated. A-class enterprises may retain all export receipts in their foreign exchange accounts without any required conversion. However, the export proceeds must be received and liquidated into the designated export collection account within 180 days after the customs export declaration date, leaving an export verification and reporting form filed through the bank in the electronic port system. The conversion ratio is discretionary; nevertheless, if the enterprise chooses to hold foreign currency, any excess position exceeding the approved account cap must be converted to RMB within 30 days. B- and C-class enterprises face a mandatory conversion obligation of 100% until their compliance status is restored.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are legally barred from providing local domestic settlement services to mainland China entities. The PBOC requires all non-bank payment institutions to hold a Payment Business License (non-financial institution payment license) and meet severe onshore clearing requirements. While a few cooperate with Chinese banks via a white-label model, the WFOE would still need a traditional domestic bank account for statutory registrations and foreign exchange conversions. Moreover, SAFE mandates that all cross-border receipts and payments relating to goods trade be tracked through the bank's verification system. Consequently, the viability of using an offshore EMI is low; they may only be considered as an auxiliary tool for collection of small non-trade service fees, and never as the primary clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign direct investment into a Chinese WFOE flows through the following mandatory route: (1) the shareholder registers the investment with the local market supervision administration via the Foreign Investment Information System; (2) SAFE (or its delegated bank) issues a foreign exchange registration code for the FDI; (3) for capital infusion, the foreign shareholder instructs a remittance from a bank outside China to the WFOE's capital escrow account (FDI capital account) opened with an approved domestic bank; (4) on each credit, the bank verifies the purpose code '124 – FDI supplement' and ensures the amount does not exceed the registered total investment amount; (5) RMB conversion happens at the spot rate, and funds must be placed in RMB under the enterprise's domestic settlement account; (6) capital verification is performed by a Chinese CPA, issuing a capital verification report that is then filed with the tax authority.For trades payable by the WFOE to overseas grain suppliers, the permitted clearing rails are: (i) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for direct settlement of open-account terms; (ii) irrevocable documentary letters of credit (LCs) at sight or 30-90 day tenors opened by the Chinese bank, operationally executed via SWIFT MT700, with the LC issued as transferable if brokerage involves a chain of swap counterparties; (iii) documentary collections (D/P, D/A) under the Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522). The bank will require the presentation of underlying sales contracts, invoices, bills of lading, insurance documents, and the compliance officer must verify the trade has been entered into the bank's cross-border receipts and payments direct reporting system. Additionally, each disbursement must be assigned an appropriate payment purpose code (e.g., 130101 for imports of agricultural commodities).Deliverable RMB forward contracts (CNY/FCY) with tenors up to 12 months negotiated with the onshore bank under the enterprise's real demand principle.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term investment and debt servicing, provided the notional aligns with verified exposure.FX swaps (currency exchange and re-exchange) for cash flow smoothing.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore; only specially eligible free trade account holders may use offshore NDFs via approved offshore banks, subject to SAFE supervision.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Payoneer are legally barred from providing local domestic settlement services to mainland China entities. The PBOC requires all non-bank payment institutions to hold a Payment Business License (non-financial institution payment license) and meet severe onshore clearing requirements. While a few cooperate with Chinese banks via a white-label model, the WFOE would still need a traditional domestic bank account for statutory registrations and foreign exchange conversions. Moreover, SAFE mandates that all cross-border receipts and payments relating to goods trade be tracked through the bank's verification system. Consequently, the viability of using an offshore EMI is low; they may only be considered as an auxiliary tool for collection of small non-trade service fees, and never as the primary clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42, contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions reach RMB 100 million for tangible-asset transfers, intangible-asset transfers or financial-asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions and services; Master File documentation is mandatory when prior-year related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion or cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million; annual related-party transaction declaration schedules remain obligatory at lower thresholds and must be reconciled with all non-resident payments and withholding filings.

Economic substance scope: China has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations statute of the Gulf-Cooperation-Council type, but the tax authorities apply equivalent substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance rules under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice 2017 No.6, together with the OECD principal-purpose test in DTA cases. The WFOE must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities inside China: grain sourcing and supplier identification, price negotiation, vessel chartering decisions, marine insurance placement, letter-of-credit opening and discounting decisions, risk management and board control. If those functions are nominally moved to a Hong Kong, Singapore or BVI affiliate with no staff and no decision-making power, the SAT may reattribute the profit to China, assert that the offshore affiliate has a Chinese permanent establishment, deny treaty benefits, and impose withholding tax and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: The conduct violates the PRC Invoice Management Measures, PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 and Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205; the tax authority will assess all underpaid tax, impose a 0.05% daily surcharge, levy an administrative penalty of 50%-300% of the underpaid amount, and, where false special VAT invoices are involved, confiscate proceeds and impose fines of up to five times the unlawful amount; extremely serious false-invoicing cases carry imprisonment up to life.

Director personal liability: If the corporate form is abused as a mere façade, courts and tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil and make the participating controlling shareholder and directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and surcharges; directors and de facto managers who knowingly cause the false invoicing are criminally liable personally, face travel bans and blacklisting on the tax-integrity and social-credit dishonesty lists, may be disqualified as company directors, and in serious evasion cases can receive up to seven years imprisonment for tax evasion or life imprisonment for false special VAT invoice offences.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 and 46 and SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42 empower the SAT to reattribute the intermediary and interest profit to the WFOE on an arm's-length basis; the excessive interest is non-deductible under the 2:1 thin-capitalization limit and the disallowed amount is recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to 10% dividend withholding; the transfer pricing understatement penalty is 50%-300% of the additional tax if contemporaneous documentation is absent, false or materially unreliable, plus daily surcharge of 0.05%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements, approve circular offshore invoices or issue misleading board minutes are exposed to personal civil liability for the restitution of the underpaid corporate tax, interest and penalties; where the trial court finds that the corporate veil is being used to evade tax obligations, the shareholders and directors may be held personally liable for the entire shortfall; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 can follow for intentional misstatement to the tax authority, with travel restrictions, blacklisting, personal asset seizure and imprisonment for directly responsible directors.
Treaty Shopping / Dividend Stripping Through DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: SAT Public Notice 2017 No.6 beneficia-ownership rules and EIT Law Article 47 general anti-avoidance rules permit the tax authority to disregard the conduit company, deny treaty relief, reassess the full statutory 10% dividend withholding tax, impose a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and an administrative penalty of 50%-300% of the under-withheld amount; the scheme also triggers the principal-purpose test under the applicable DTA and may terminate mutual-agreement-procedure access and subject the non-resident recipient to a compliance blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE are in charge of withholding and remittance and, if they knowingly execute the dividend remittance through a sham treaty company, they may be held civilly liable for the unwithheld tax and penalties as responsible persons; the legal person veil can be pierced where the arrangement is a sham, allowing tax authorities to recover from personal assets; issuing false tax-residency certificates or false beneficial-ownership declarations gives rise to criminal liability for tax evasion and fraudulent documentation, and directors face imprisonment, exit bans, blacklisting and disqualification under the applicable company-law and criminal-law sanctions.
