Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. For an ordinary WFOE limited liability company engaged in wholesale of raw cane sugar, the Company Law imposes no statutory minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account. The sole shareholder must declare a subscribed share capital reasonably corresponding to the proposed scale of operations and must pay in the subscribed capital within five years from establishment unless an amended capital contribution timetable is approved. A dedicated capital contribution bank account is required for receipt of funds, but funds are not held under escrow release conditions. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the E-Tax Bureau (Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform) operated by the STA and its provincial tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the entity is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license; the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number for all Chinese tax filings. Under the multi-license integration system, tax registration is automatically transmitted to the competent local office of the STA and no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must then complete its tax profile in the E-Tax Bureau using the legal representative's real-name authentication, including taxpayer classification, tax filing period, accounting system, bank account information, and linking of the export customs registration. A foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment that earns China-source income must instead apply for a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number at the competent STA office or through the E-Tax Bureau, using the foreign registration certificate, notarized Chinese translation, and evidence of the responsible person or withholding agent.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is mandatory for all enterprises upon formation; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, while an enterprise with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply the 0% export VAT rate to the physical vessel shipment of VHP raw cane centrifugal sugar only when the goods are actually exported from China, the export is declared to China Customs in the WFOE's own name, the WFOE is the exporter of record, title and risk pass to the overseas buyer outside China, ocean transport documents verify the actual shipment, and the export foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated through a SAFE-approved bank. As a foreign trade enterprise, the WFOE claims the 'exemption and refund' export VAT mechanism: the export sale is zero-rated and the input VAT on the domestic purchase of the sugar is refunded through the official export tax rebate process, subject to the product-specific export rebate rate published by the Ministry of Finance and the STA for the applicable HS heading of raw cane sugar. Offshore digital services are outside the scope of this commodity export.

  • Bill of Lading or ocean sea waybill evidencing vessel shipment of the raw cane sugar
  • Special VAT invoice for the domestic purchase of the raw cane sugar
  • Signed export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Bank-issued Export Proceeds Receipt or export collection verification record produced through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) banking system, which is the Chinese functional equivalent of an inward remittance certificate
  • Certificate of origin and any destination-country inspection or import certificate required for unrefined centrifugal cane sugar

China operates a centralized electronic VAT invoice control system rather than a Latin-American-style fiscal stamp. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved tax control device (Tax UKey) or through the Full Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform; invoice data are transmitted to the STA in near real time and validated against the taxpayer's registration, tax control device, and invoice quota. Special VAT invoices used for export must be matched electronically with the China Customs export declaration and, for an export tax refund claim, the value of the invoice must reconcile to the customs declaration, bill of lading, and foreign exchange receipt; mismatches automatically block the refund workflow and trigger a tax desk audit.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by cross-matching China Customs export declaration data, special VAT invoice data, bills of lading, and SAFE export foreign-exchange receipt records. If the 0% rate is not substantiated, the STA disallows the zero-rating, claws back any VAT refund already paid, and collects statutory interest. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, underpaid VAT is recovered with a monetary penalty ranging from 50% to five times the tax underpaid; fraudulent export tax refund claims are punishable by recovery of the refunded amount, a penalty of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained, downgrade or suspension of export tax rebate status, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. Retrospective recovery is generally limited to three years in non-fraud cases, five years in specified cases, and is unlimited where false declaration or fraudulent conduct is involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an administratively managed current-account-convertible exchange system. The RMB is fully convertible for genuine trade and current-account items, but capital-account transactions remain subject to registration, approval, and post-event supervision. For a WFOE importing raw cane sugar, current-account settlement through a licensed domestic bank is routine: the importer presents the purchase contract, customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and shipping evidence; the bank then debits CNY and purchases FX or debits the entity's foreign-currency current account. Export receipts can be retained in foreign-currency accounts without mandatory surrender. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is classified as current account and is permitted after completion of the annual external audit and income tax filing; the bank will require audited financial statements, corporate income tax payment certificates, a board resolution declaring dividends, and the WFOE's foreign-exchange registration records. The main convertibility hurdles are documentary and timing related: mismatched remitter and beneficiary names, missing tax payment certificates, delayed trade verification, or attempted use of capital-account funds beyond the registered business scope can suspend FX purchases. Therefore, the treasury discipline for this commodity trader must include a complete SAFE registration at the designated bank, strict reconciliation of every remittance with the underlying sugar transaction, and a clean audit trail from contract to delivery.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender percentage exists in China. SAFE abolished the old forced conversion regime, and a WFOE may retain up to 100% of export foreign-currency proceeds in its retained foreign-currency current account or convert them freely to RMB through the onshore spot market. For export trade receipts, the exporter must collect proceeds through a domestic designated bank and match them with the corresponding customs export declarations in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. If an advance export receipt is received, the physical export must be declared within the statutory 30-day matching window; if extended, a SAFE registration must be obtained before the bank will release the trade-verification hold. Failure to match exports and receipts can lead to the WFOE being downgraded to a B/C trade credit classification, which triggers full documentary inspection and slows future settlements.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility: Nil for standalone onshore settlement. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, or similarly structured non-bank financial technology companies are not licensed by the PBOC as banking institutions or clearing members and cannot open domestic RMB accounts for a mainland WFOE. China requires trade and capital-account flows to pass through SAFE-designated domestic banks, and foreign EMIs cannot provide the required trade authenticity verification hooks into the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. A WFOE that attempts to route commodity trade receipts or supplier payments through an offshore EMI will face severe documentary rejection, account freezes, and AML escalations. Fintech can only participate as a front-end interface or reporting/API layer sitting on top of a licensed domestic bank; the legal settlement venue must remain onshore with a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration at its designated Tier-1 domestic bank, after which the bank issues an FDI registration code. The foreign parent must remit the equity capital via SWIFT MT103 using the exact legal name and bank account of the WFOE as the beneficiary. The remittance should be annotated with the cross-border purpose category for direct investment equity injection (FDI capital contribution), not a trade settlement purpose code. On receipt, the bank posts the capital inflow into the WFOE's FDI capital account and files it with the SAFE capital information system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like certain offshore centres, but it applies the 'economic substance' and 'beneficial ownership' doctrines through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the Special Tax Adjustment Regulation, and the Anti-Tax-Avoidance rules. A WFOE must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — purchase negotiation, vessel chartering, sugar cargo risk management, logistics oversight, customer invoicing, and treasury decisions — are actually performed in China by local staff with real authority and information. If the WFOE is merely a booking vehicle directing profits to a foreign parent without employees, premises, decision-making power, or business risk, the tax authority may disregard the legal form, recharacterise income, impose an imputed taxable profit, and deny treaty or incentive benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Levies and Collection, the tax authority shall recover the evaded taxes and impose a monetary fine of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. Under Criminal Law Article 201, tax evasion involving a large amount (generally RMB 50,000 or more) constitutes criminal tax fraud, punishable by imprisonment of up to seven years and confiscation of illegal gains, plus fines. The tax authority may also suspend the company's VAT qualification, revoke export refund status, and blacklist the enterprise through the serious-tax-violation blacklist mechanism.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and responsible financial officers who intended to conceal or falsify tax filings may be named in criminal prosecutions as directly responsible persons. Personal civil liability arises for the unpaid tax, penalties, and surcharges if the corporate form is used as a sham to evade tax; the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the tax debt. Travel restrictions and exit bans (blacklisting) are routinely imposed under the border control rules, preventing directors from leaving China until the tax liability and penalties are settled.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Intercompany Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under the Special Tax Adjustment rules in Articles 41–48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and State Taxation Administration Announcement 2017/6, the tax authorities may make a transfer pricing adjustment, re-determine the arm's-length price, and impose an adjustment tax plus interest. The interest rate on assessed transfer-pricing adjustments is the base lending rate plus 5 percentage points. Failure to prepare or submit contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation within the statutory period exposes the enterprise to penalties up to RMB 50,000 and may lead to a rebutted arm's-length presumption in the tax assessment.

Director personal liability: Where transfer pricing mispricing is part of a scheme to strip profits from China and avoid Chinese tax, the tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil if the WFOE is found to be a sham or conduit. The responsible directors may be personally assessed for the compensatory tax, surcharges, and penalties if they caused or participated in the fraudulent transfer-pricing design; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion may attach to individuals who also used offshore entities to conceal the real beneficial ownership. Directors' bank accounts and personal assets may be frozen, and they may be placed on the exit-blacklist preventing departure from China until full compliance.
Shell Enterprise / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorises the tax authority to recharacterise the entire arrangement and adjust the enterprise's taxable income in accordance with the economic substance. Under the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, a company that engages in an arrangement with no reasonable commercial purpose and obtains a tax benefit shall be reassessed and subject to additional tax, interest, and penalties. Fraudulent use of a DTA to obtain a reduced withholding rate on profit repatriation can result in denial of treaty benefits, reclassification of dividends as taxable income, and an additional 10% withholding tax on the hidden distribution, plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorise or maintain a shell WFOE for tax avoidance may face personal liability for the restructured tax assessment under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, especially when the enterprise is used to commit fraud or evade existing liabilities. The court may order a director to return assets stripped out of China, may impose a travel ban pending tax clearance, and may recommend criminal prosecution for tax evasion if the scale of undistributed profit or treaty abuse exceeds the criminal threshold. Imprisonment terms for directly responsible individuals can range from three to seven years under aggravated-conviction standards, along with civil restitution of the lost revenue and all statutory surcharges.
