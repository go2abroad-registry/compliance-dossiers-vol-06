Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code. Step 2: complete the mandatory multi-department joint registration, under which the local State Taxation Administration office synchronises the Unified Social Credit Code and automatically assigns the Taxpayer Identification Number; for a PRC resident company the Taxpayer Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code itself. Step 3: log into the State Taxation Administration E-Tax Bureau portal, complete taxpayer classification, elect or apply for general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable sales are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000 or if export refunds will be claimed, and obtain the electronic tax registration identity. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual VAT taxable sales for mandatory general taxpayer registration; all WFOEs must complete basic tax registration upon incorporation irrespective of turnover. Below the threshold the entity may be classified as a small-scale taxpayer, but an export-oriented wholesale trader should elect general taxpayer status to access input VAT credit and the export tax refund mechanism.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of dried goji berries from PRC customs territory qualify for the VAT export zero-rating and export tax refund regime if the seller is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are actually shipped out of China under an export customs declaration, the sale is made to an overseas non-resident buyer, title and risk pass outside China, and the exporter collects the export proceeds in foreign currency through a licensed bank. The goods must be classified as primary processed agricultural products; if processing exceeds primary processing, the general goods VAT rate and the corresponding export refund treatment apply. The exporter must issue the zero-rated export invoice, report the zero-rated export in the VAT declaration, and submit the export tax refund declaration electronically through the E-Tax Bureau using the customs release data.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration with customs declaration and release numbers
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or confirmed export purchase order
  • Original Bill of Lading, airway bill, railway consignment note, or equivalent international transport document
  • Bank receipt or credit advice evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign currency through the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice issued by the exporting WFOE
  • China Customs inspection and quarantine release documentation, including a phytosanitary certificate where required by the destination country
  • Export tax refund declaration data transmitted by the State Taxation Administration E-Tax Bureau and the China International Trade Single Window

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice clearance regime. Taxpayers must issue Special VAT Invoices or General VAT Invoices through the State Taxation Administration digital e-invoice system, including the Full Digitalised Electronic Invoice platform, and upload invoice data to the Golden Tax IV comprehensive tax information system in real time. Invoices carry a cryptographic QR code, a unique electronic invoice number, the unified goods and services tax classification code for dried goji berries, and are transmitted API-to-API to the tax authority; physical stamping is replaced by digital confirmation. Customs declaration data are matched with VAT export declarations under the China International Trade Single Window to support automated anti-fraud cross-checks for export zero-rating.

Tax authorities and Customs conduct automated and desk audits of export refund claims. Unsupported, untrue, or fraudulent zero-rating claims trigger retrospective clawback of the refunded VAT, late payment surcharge, and penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection; fraudulent export declarations to obtain refunds are additionally punishable under the Criminal Law for defrauding export tax refunds, with fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for aggravated amounts. Customs administrative penalties for false declarations and foreign exchange penalties for non-repatriation or false verification may also apply. Recovery periods are extended without limitation where fraud or wilful misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility and a highly regulated capital account supervised by SAFE. For a WFOE engaged in the bulk export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, export receipts under current account trade transactions are generally convertible to RMB, subject to genuine transaction verification, supporting documentation such as customs declarations, contracts, and invoices. However, export proceeds must be repatriated into China and are subject to compulsory surrender rules, meaning that unless retained in a permitted foreign currency account within SAFE-designated quotas, the funds must be converted into RMB. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are capital account transactions requiring an audited financial report, tax clearance certificate, board resolution, and a foreign exchange payment application processed through a domestic bank under SAFE supervision. Additional hurdles include the need for prior Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration, the real-need principle requiring underlying business justification, and strict bank scrutiny of every conversion and remittance. While the current account is largely convertible, the capital account remains heavily monitored, and any attempt to circumvent surrender or conversion rules through offshore fintech platforms would violate Chinese foreign exchange law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless an extension is granted by SAFE under exceptional circumstances. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% under the uniform settlement framework, meaning that all export proceeds must be either converted into RMB or retained in a designated foreign currency account only within the permissible quota approved by SAFE based on actual operational needs.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs without a licensed branch in mainland China are generally not viable as the primary banking channel for a WFOE due to strict SAFE regulations requiring foreign exchange conversions and export surrender to be executed through licensed domestic banks. Offshore EMIs cannot legally access China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) on behalf of a resident WFOE, nor can they provide compliant RMB conversion or capital account services. Their use is limited to incidental ancillary functions such as initiating small-value payments for non-regulated international expenses, but they must not serve as the main operational account provider for export receipts or capital inflows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed through the bank designated in the SAFE Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, using payment purpose code 622 'Foreign direct investment - capital injection' under China's International Payments Statistics framework. The receiving bank will verify the notarized joint venture contract, WFOE business license, SAFE registration code, and the board resolution approving the injection. Funds are first credited to a Capital Settlement Account before conversion to RMB, and the bank must complete a Chinese CPA-issued capital verification report (验资报告) and file it with the local Administration for Market Regulation. Any subsequent foreign currency loan requires a separate Foreign Debt Registration with SAFE, including the loan agreement and audited financial statements.For outbound trade settlements, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers are the primary rail for export receipts and direct disbursements, with CIPS available for RMB-denominated cross-border clearing. Commercial letters of credit (LCs) are recommended for larger botanical wholesale transactions, payable through the issuing bank and negotiated via the WFOE's domestic account bank. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) may be used for lower-risk repeat buyers. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China requires contemporaneous Local File documentation when annual related-party tangible-asset transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File documentation is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The goji berry export WFOE must also file the annual related-party transaction disclosure schedule regardless of threshold if any intercompany transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not codify offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations or a separate Core Income-Generating Activities test, but tax authorities apply Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the general anti-abuse rule with a substance-over-form analysis. Arrangements lacking a reasonable commercial purpose, especially intercompany profit stripping to low-tax jurisdictions, are disregarded and income is reallocated to the entity executing the CIGA. DTA beneficial ownership, permanent establishment attribution, and indirect-transfer taxation under SAT Announcement No. 7 (2015) increasingly rely on CIGA and actual economic substance in the WFOE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 63 and 64 empower recovery of underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest, and an administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to three years for tax evasion of a relatively large amount, and three to seven years with fines for a huge amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize false filings may be personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax by piercing the corporate veil. The legal representative is placed on the tax blacklist, triggering a travel ban, asset freezing, and restrictions on leaving China until liabilities are settled. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment, personal fines, and confiscation.
Shell Company and Transfer Pricing Abuse

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 45 and 47, together with SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, allow tax authorities to re-determine income under the arm's length principle, impose transfer pricing adjustments, assess late-payment interest, and impose penalties for failing to produce contemporaneous documentation. If the arrangement is wilful, the underpaid tax is subject to a fine of 50% to 500% under the Tax Collection Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute the artificial intercompany contracts may be required to restore diverted corporate assets under corporate veil-piercing doctrine, and the company and its controllers are blacklisted for tax arrears, producing travel bans and denial of executive asset transfers. If the abuse constitutes deliberate tax evasion, the directors face criminal liability and imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.
AML and Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering Law of the People's Republic of China imposes confiscation of illegal gains and fines at institution level of up to RMB 5 million, with fines on directly responsible personnel of up to RMB 1 million. Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with imprisonment and criminal fines, with a maximum prison term of ten years for serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure such transactions lose limited liability protection; personal assets are subject to freezing, confiscation, and civil restitution. Travel bans and blacklisting apply during investigation and enforcement, and criminal conviction results in lengthy imprisonment, personal fines, and disqualification from corporate management.
