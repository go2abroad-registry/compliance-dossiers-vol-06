Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-owned WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) contained in the SAMR-issued Business License is the statutory TIN/VAT ID; no separate TIN certificate is issued. Procedure: (1) The foreign investor must notarize, legalize or apostille and Chinese-translate its home-country registry documents; (2) file WFOE establishment with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); (3) after the Business License is issued, complete the mandatory tax registration through the e-Tax Bureau within 30 days; (4) declare legal representative, financial officer, tax accountant, accounting standards, bank accounts and invoicing systems; (5) elect general VAT taxpayer status or small-scale taxpayer status based on expected annual sales; (6) apply for export tax rebate qualification and register export collection bank accounts with SAFE if cross-border trade will be performed; (7) use the 18-digit USCC in all VAT filings, VAT invoices, customs declarations and EIT returns. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT upon incorporation. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating/export VAT rebate is available when the WFOE is a registered general VAT taxpayer, the matcha powder is actually shipped out of Chinese customs territory under an approved export goods declaration, title and risk have passed to the foreign buyer, export proceeds are repatriated through designated foreign exchange bank channels within the statutory period, valid domestic VAT special input invoices corresponding to the exported batch are held, and the export is reported in the VAT return before the refund claim. The applicable rebate rate is the STA-authorized export rebate rate for the product's tariff line; if the rebate rate is 0%, the export sale is VAT-exempt/zero-rated but the related input VAT is not refundable.

China operates the Full Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under Golden Tax Phase IV. Invoices must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform and transmitted in real time to STA servers; the system applies anti-fraud digital stamping through unique invoice codes, digital tax seals, and automated clearance validation. Taxpayer ERP systems are required to integrate using STA-published e-invoice APIs. Paper special VAT invoices continue to use tax-control devices with 20-digit invoice codes and tax control data uploads. Failed clearance or invoice code mismatch blocks lawful invoice use and initiates tax risk review.

The STA electronically cross-checks every zero-rating/export rebate claim against customs declarations, VAT invoice system data and SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data. If zero-rating or rebate is not substantiated, the tax authority recovers the VAT exempted or refunded, assesses late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, imposes a statutory penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax, suspends or cancels the exporter's rebate qualification, and may place the entity on the tax-related credit blacklist. Fraudulent export refunds through forged customs documents, fake export invoices, or fictitious export transactions trigger criminal liability for tax evasion/fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China as well as administrative clawback measures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed float regime for the Renminbi (CNY) with strict bifurcation between current account and capital account transactions. Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008) and SAFE Circulars, current account items, including cross-border payments for trade in goods and services, are generally freely convertible following documentary verification and a legitimate underlying transaction. Export proceeds, however, must be repatriated to China and are subject to a centralized monitoring and verification system; while the mandatory 100% surrender requirement was abolished in 2012, exporters must still route foreign currency receipts through designated foreign exchange accounts and either settle into CNY or retain within approved caps, with all such receipts requiring declaration through the cross-border payment system. Capital account convertibility remains tightly controlled: foreign direct investment into a WFOE requires FDI registration, capital injection via a dedicated capital account, and post-arrival capital verification by a licensed accountant. Profit remittances by a WFOE to its foreign shareholder are treated as current account transfers under dividend distributions, requiring audited financials, tax clearance certificates, and a valid foreign exchange settlement notice issued by the bank after verification of the underlying audited profit and reserve requirements. Practical hurdles include SAFE's 'real demand' principle, which prohibits speculative pre-positioning of funds, and the need for a board resolution and tax withholding certificates before any outward remittance of dividends or liquidation proceeds. Non-trade current transfers such as royalties and management service fees face enhanced scrutiny and require deemed income contracts plus tax payment proof. Overall, the CNY is Highly Convertible on the current account (with controls), but only Partially Convertible on the capital account, mandating a disciplined pre-approval and documentation workflow for every material cross-border fund movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds of the WFOE, being receipts on current account, must be repatriated into China within 30 days of the date of customs declaration for exportation, unless a SAFE approved extension or special deferred arrangement applies. The current regulatory framework no longer imposes a mandatory fixed conversion ratio; instead, exporters may retain up to the approved foreign exchange settlement account ceiling, with any excess balance required to be converted into CNY. All export receipts must be verified against the customs declarations through the 'Service and Trade Foreign Exchange Administrative Regulations' and reported via the bank under the 'International Receipts and Payments Statistics'. In practice, the bank requires a full set of trade documents (contract, invoice, bill of lading, customs form) and will assign a payment type code (e.g., 'EX01') to each credit. Failure to repatriate within the statutory 30-day window or to produce matching documentation may lead to inclusion on SAFE's 'Focus List' and mandatory 100% surrender of all future receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign exchange administrative agency, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment institutions (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer) have a Low viability for serving a Mainland China-based WFOE as a primary banking partner. Under the PRC Law on Commercial Banks and the Non-bank Payment Institution Regulations, only locally licensed commercial banks or authorized non-bank payment institutions operating under a PBOC Payment Business License (Type III for cross-border) may legally hold CNY deposit accounts or facilitate FX settlement for domestic entities. An offshore EMI does not possess a China banking license, cannot maintain a RMB clearing account in the CNAPS system, and cannot directly interface with the SAFE cross-border monitory platform. Accordingly, an offshore EMI may be used solely as a secondary collection channel for foreign customers, provided it obtains a money services business license in its host jurisdiction and the WFOE transfers the collected funds through a SWIFT credit into its own domestic USD/CNY bank account. However, such EMI intermediation introduces a 'payee-payer mismatch' red flag, which significantly raises AML review burden and may be scrutinized by SAFE as potential 'payment splitting' or 'structured settlement'. Therefore, the treasury blueprint mandates that the WFOE maintains its primary liquidity and all trade settlement rails exclusively through Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, with offshore EMIs relegated to test merchant collection only for low-value, high-frequency niche e-commerce sales (not for bulk matcha powder shipments).

However, such EMI intermediation introduces a 'payee-payer mismatch' red flag, which significantly raises AML review burden and may be scrutinized by SAFE as potential 'payment splitting' or 'structured settlement'. Therefore, the treasury blueprint mandates that the WFOE maintains its primary liquidity and all trade settlement rails exclusively through Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, with offshore EMIs relegated to test merchant collection only for low-value, high-frequency niche e-commerce sales (not for bulk matcha powder shipments).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million in annual aggregate related-party transactions, or category-specific thresholds such as RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers and RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, financial asset transfers, services, or related-party financing, trigger mandatory Local File preparation; the Master File is required when group consolidated revenue and transaction scale meet the thresholds announced in SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer-pricing shell and artificial profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Special tax adjustment under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 48 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures: the tax authority may reallocate profit to China, add statutory interest from the due date of the underpaid tax, and impose penalties under Tax Administration Law Articles 63 and 64. The lack of substance also invalidates any treaty benefit under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false transfer-pricing documentation and board resolutions is treated as having knowingly caused the underpayment; civil joint and several liability arises when the director uses the limited-liability entity to defraud Chinese tax or creditors. Criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 applies if mispricing deliberately understates taxable income, with imprisonment of up to seven years, fines, asset seizure and an exit ban from China.
False invoicing, cash skimming and concealed overseas sales

Statutory sanction: Penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus late surcharge under Tax Administration Law Article 63; false VAT invoicing is punishable under Criminal Law Article 205, and for large amounts, imprisonment can reach life plus property confiscation. Customs undervaluation of matcha imports and exports triggers customs penalties and administrative confiscation.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil where the director commingles corporate funds with personal funds and uses the WFOE as an alter ego. Under PRC Company Law Article 20, the director becomes jointly liable for unpaid tax, interest and penalties. Travel bans, asset seizure and the social-credit blacklist follow; a major fake-invoice scheme can lead to life imprisonment.
Unwithheld outbound dividend, interest or royalty remittance

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent must pay the unwithheld tax out of its own funds under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 38; Tax Administration Law Article 69 imposes a fine of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax. If the treaty claim is denied, the WFOE must remit the 10% WHT plus interest and penalties; where a distribution is disguised as a loan, thin-capitalization rules may add deemed-dividend WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who order remittance despite knowing of the withholding duty are personally liable for restitution of the State's tax loss. They are blocked from obtaining a tax-clearance certificate and placed on the exit-ban list, preventing departure from China. In willful evasion above the statutory threshold, the director faces criminal tax-evasion prosecution, imprisonment, confiscation of personal assets, and execution over personal bank accounts and equity in the foreign parent.
