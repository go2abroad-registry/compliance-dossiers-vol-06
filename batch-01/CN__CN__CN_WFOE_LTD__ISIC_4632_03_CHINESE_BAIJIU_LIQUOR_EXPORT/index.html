Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; local administration by the competent district or municipal tax bureau with jurisdiction over the WFOE registered address; online portal: the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) unified online service platform

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE limited liability company, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Tax Identification Number; no separate TIN certificate is issued. The procedure is: (1) after the State Administration for Market Regulation registers the company and issues the business license, its data are transmitted to the STA, completing the initial tax registration automatically; (2) within 30 days, the legal representative or authorized fiscal officer must complete real-name authentication on the e-Tax Bureau using the Chinese national identity card or foreign passport and file the supplementary tax registration details, including the accounting system, bank account record, registered address, and authorized tax representatives; (3) the WFOE must register as a VAT taxpayer and either be classified or voluntarily apply as a general VAT taxpayer if projected annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000 and accounting records are sound; (4) the WFOE must activate its electronic tax digital certificate and invoice control environment through the STA portal; and (5) a foreign non-resident enterprise with no Chinese establishment is not issued a USCC and does not go through this WFOE procedure; its Chinese counterparty performs VAT and income tax withholding registration under the counterparty's own tax file, while the non-resident entity may provide a home-country tax residence certificate to claim treaty benefits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; a WFOE cannot elect pass-through treatment and is automatically an entity-level taxpayer. The only available elections are administrative VAT status choices: voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer before exceeding the CNY 5,000,000 threshold, and selection of the export tax exemption and rebate method once the export documentation and general taxpayer status are satisfied; neither election alters the entity's income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; a WFOE is registered for VAT immediately upon incorporation regardless of turnover, while a non-resident seller without an establishment may be subject to VAT collection through the domestic purchaser's withholding mechanism rather than separate local registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of Chinese baijiu abroad qualify for the 0% VAT rate and, where applicable, exemption of the Excise (Consumption) Tax, with refund of Excise Tax already paid if the product is within the export rebate catalogue, provided the goods are actually exported from Chinese customs territory, the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer, the export transaction is backed by a genuine foreign sale and physical movement evidence, the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated within the statutory period, and the export tax refund declaration is filed through the e-Tax Bureau with the supporting documentation bundle. The zero-rating is not a mere exemption; the WFOE may claim refund of residual input VAT attributable to the exported baijiu. Qualifying offshore digital services are outside this goods-export scope, and goods exiting through special customs supervision zones qualify only when final departure from China is evidenced.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export Bill of Entry) issued by the General Administration of Customs through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical export, destination port, and shipment date
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list signed by the WFOE and accepted by the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract or purchase order from the foreign buyer
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation, traditionally the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds under the SAFE trade foreign exchange monitoring system
  • Cargo inspection or health certificate and any export license required for spirits under Chinese food safety and customs commodity inspection rules
  • Freight forwarder loading records and the customs electronic exit-clearance log for the specific shipment

China mandates e-invoicing through the STA's Golden Tax System and All-Electronic Invoice platform. The WFOE must issue VAT invoices on the e-Tax Bureau portal or through an STA-connected electronic invoice service provider using the official API. At issuance, the system allocates a unique national invoice number and code, generates an OFD/XML invoice file, affixes a STA digital signature, and transmits the full invoice metadata to the STA central invoice database in real time, functioning as the anti-fraud digital stamp. If a paper special VAT invoice is required, the WFOE must issue it through the authorized tax-control device and report its invoice data during the statutory filing period. Red-letter (credit-note) invoices require a system-issued reversal number and must be transmitted to the STA before the credit note becomes effective.

The STA and customs authorities cross-audit export zero-rating claims by matching the customs export declaration, electronic VAT invoice data, shipping documents, and the bank foreign exchange receipt record. If any mandatory document is missing, false, or inconsistent, the zero-rated status is denied and the refund is clawed back, with VAT and applicable Excise Tax reassessed at the standard rates plus daily late-payment surcharge. For fraudulent export rebate claims using forged customs declarations or FX receipts, the Tax Collection Administration Law and Criminal Law impose recovery of the refund, a fine of 50% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained amount, downgrade of the taxpayer credit classification with suspension of expedited export rebate processing, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund. The general assessment limitation period is three years, extended to five years for special circumstances, with no limitation period where fraud or intentional misrepresentation is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility and a qualified, quota-controlled capital account. Current account trade and service flows are generally freely convertible once the underlying goods trade declaration, customs bona, and payment reconciliation are processed through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions remain subject to pre-registration and supervision: inbound FDI requires SAFE foreign investment registration, and the foreign shareholder's capital contribution must be paid into a dedicated foreign currency capital deposit account and subsequently settled into RMB through a capital settlement account. Profit and dividend remittances are permitted on a current-account basis once audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and a resolution of the board of directors or shareholders have been submitted to the remitting bank; in practice the bank will also verify the absence of outstanding tax liabilities or foreign exchange penalties. Since June 2012, export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory conversion or a forced surrender ration; exporters may retain 100% of eligible receipts in foreign currency settlement accounts and decide freely on conversion or retention. Nonetheless, the exporter's charter bank must verify the export proceeds in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System within 90 days of the shipment date, failing which the receipts cannot be released from the verification hold and the exporter may face regulatory penalties. Inbound FDI settlement remains monitored through the Payment-in-Advance and Negative List system, so the WFOE must ensure that all capital uses fall within the permitted scope and that any leased asset, purchase of real property, or subsequent reinvestment complies with the relevant industrial policy and anti-avoidance rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 2012 No.13 and subsequent goods trade verification rules, export proceeds are not subject to any mandatory conversion ratio or surrender quota; 100% may be held in a domestic bank foreign-currency settlement account. The statutory timeline applicable to the export settlement procedure is 90 days from the customs declaration date, within which the export receipts must be repatriated and matched against the electronic export record in the Trade Monitoring System. For receivables arising from goods shipped on open account with a maturity greater than 90 days, the exporter is required to file a pre-registration with SAFE through the account bank, and the deferred collection period may extend up to 180 days for certain verified lines of business. The bank will suspend or freeze the proceeds if the verification documents are incomplete, if the invoice or bill of lading is discrepant, or if the buyer's remittance references no matching sales contract.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) exercising the delegated authority of the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMIs with offshore licenses, such as Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut, or Wise Business, are not viable and are generally legally barred from servicing Mainland-China-incorporated WFOEs as their primary treasury provider. These providers do not hold a commercial banking license issued by the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and therefore cannot open foreign currency settlement accounts in China, cannot obtain a SWIFT direct membership permitting MT103 clearing against local RMB liquidity, and are not permitted to access the SAFE Trade Monitoring System or foreign exchange settlement services. At best, such an EMI could be used at an offshore-node level for receipts from certain non-China sales channels after the export documents have been cleared through a licensed onshore bank, but no compliant WFOE export treasury should be structured with an unlicensed EMI as the primary clearing rail. The practical and regulatory reality is that the entire FX lifecycle, including export receipt collection, capital settlement, export verification, and hedging, must be executed through the chartered domestic banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital injection must be pre-registered with the local SAFE branch before the remittance is initiated. The foreign shareholder shall transfer the registered US-dollar or other permitted convertible-currency amount from its overseas bank into the WFOE's capital escrow account at the designated domestic bank, using the SAFE-filed FDI capital contribution identifier. The bank will complete a 'capital settlement' and convert to RMB at the spot exchange rate, crediting the RMB capital settlement account. The purposes for which the settled RMB may be used are strictly limited to the negative-list compliant operating expenditure of the WFOE, including raw-material purchase, equipment purchase, salary, legacy rents, intellectual-property royalties, and bona-fide trade payables. All disbursements must be supported by contractual invoices and a formal capital verification report issued by a certified public accountant within six months of injection, or the bank may refuse further settlement transactions. The regulatory purpose code for the transfer should be classified by the remitting bank as 'Equity Investment to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in Mainland China' and must be cross-referenced to the SAFE FDI approval number.The WFOE will execute its outbound trade payments for the purchase of baijiu inventory and logistics through three principal clearing rails. First, standard open-account import or export payments will be routed over the SWIFT network using MT103 with the relevant Trade Monitor reference code and a harmonized description of the cross-border goods service. Second, documentary trade settlements will employ irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by the buyer's offshore bank and advised through the WFOE's maintaining bank, with payment against presentation of a clean-on-board bill of lading, commercial invoice, and certificate of quality. Third, for high-ticket spirits cargoes or repeat transactions without open-account terms, documentary collections will be applied under the Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522) by handing the drafts and documents to the WFOE's bank for presentation to the buyer's bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs with offshore licenses, such as Airwallex, Payoneer, Revolut, or Wise Business, are not viable and are generally legally barred from servicing Mainland-China-incorporated WFOEs as their primary treasury provider. These providers do not hold a commercial banking license issued by the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and therefore cannot open foreign currency settlement accounts in China, cannot obtain a SWIFT direct membership permitting MT103 clearing against local RMB liquidity, and are not permitted to access the SAFE Trade Monitoring System or foreign exchange settlement services. At best, such an EMI could be used at an offshore-node level for receipts from certain non-China sales channels after the export documents have been cleared through a licensed onshore bank, but no compliant WFOE export treasury should be structured with an unlicensed EMI as the primary clearing rail. The practical and regulatory reality is that the entire FX lifecycle, including export receipt collection, capital settlement, export verification, and hedging, must be executed through the chartered domestic banking system.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions reach: tangible property (inventory) transfers RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers RMB 10 million; financial asset transfers RMB 100 million; or any other related-party transactions RMB 40 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required when the multinational group's prior-year consolidated revenue reaches RMB 5.5 billion. If the WFOE's baijiu export sales to a foreign distributor exceed RMB 200 million, the Local File must be prepared before the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in Caribbean or Gulf jurisdictions; however, economic substance is indispensable under Chinese law: a WFOE claiming treaty benefits must have substantive business premises, full-time operational management, sufficient personnel, bank accounts and decision-making authority in China; and the core income-generating activities (CIGA) analysis is used to determine permanent establishment and beneficial ownership. A WFOE that is an empty booking vehicle for baijiu exports, with no real business control, will be treated as a sham under CIT Law Article 47 GAAR, treaty benefits will be denied, and the profits may be re-allocated or re-characterized as taxable to the controlling foreign shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsifying Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law (TAL) Article 63: the tax authority may recover the underpaid tax and impose a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid amount; overdue tax accrues a daily 0.05% surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201: a taxpayer who evades tax by fraud or concealment is punishable by up to 3 years in prison if the amount is large, and 3-7 years when the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of payable tax.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 where directors and de facto controllers have abused the WFOE's limited-liability form to shelter personal assets. Directors face civil restitution for the state's tax loss, personal travel restrictions and exit blacklisting (tax clearance is required before leaving China), and, if personally implicated in the fraudulent invoicing scheme, criminal imprisonment as joint offenders.
Improper Dividend Withholding and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 69: the withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable for 50%-300% of the unwithheld tax; the non-resident remains liable for the full 10% tax, and daily late-payment interest is charged. A rejected treaty claim triggers assessment of WHT together with Article 47 GAAR reclassifying the intermediary's substance as lacking.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly declare dividends without WHT, or who cause the WFOE to be stripped of funds so that tax cannot be collected, may be personally assessed to the extent of the wrongful distribution under veil-piercing principles. Criminal exposure includes fraud under Criminal Law Article 201, blacklisting under the tax non-resident exchange-control list, and civil liability for the full tax shortage if the company is dissolved after the dividend drain.
Shell Operation / No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and TAL Article 35 allow the tax authority to compute taxable income by deemed-profit or reasonable methods; Article 47 (GAAR) permits recharacterization of arrangements lacking commercial purpose. The entity may be treated as a non-resident PE of its foreign parent, making the parent taxable on the sales and subject to 25% corporate tax on the China-source profit plus penalties.

Director personal liability: If the WFOE is a pure conduit, courts may disregard it and hold the foreign shareholders and local directors personally liable for the unpaid tax under Company Law Article 20. Directors face personal asset tracing, exit restrictions/blacklisting, professional disqualification, and criminal penalties for tax evasion when the sham structure was knowingly used to deprive the Chinese exchequer of revenue.
