Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in craft beer and beverage wholesale and distribution under the 2024 Negative List. The sole shareholder determines the registered capital in the Articles of Association; the Company Law generally requires subscribed capital to be paid in full within five years of registration unless a shorter period is mandated by a special industry rule. No blocked escrow account is required for ordinary distribution activities, but the registered capital amount must be commercially consistent with the declared total investment, and the paid-in capital must be remitted through a bank acting as a State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) agent bank for direct investment foreign exchange registration.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; online registration, filing and clearance are handled through the national Electronic Tax Bureau portal ('Electronic Tax Bureau') and, for export transactions, the China Customs Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the foreign investor first completes company formation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau or local tax service hall; the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number for all PRC tax purposes. The WFOE must then complete customs registration with China Customs for import/export filing, register for VAT invoice issuance, and apply for general VAT taxpayer status if projected annual VAT-taxable sales will exceed the statutory threshold. For a foreign non-resident parent without a Chinese establishment, no separate Chinese TIN is ordinarily required unless it is directly liable for Chinese-source income tax; the WFOE remains the resident taxpayer for trading operations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. A China WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese tax resident enterprise subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income. The only available status election is VAT taxpayer classification: below the annual sales threshold the WFOE may apply for small-scale taxpayer simplified VAT treatment, while above the threshold it is required to register as a general VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual VAT-taxable sales is the threshold above which a taxpayer that is currently small-scale must register as a general VAT taxpayer. There is no minimum turnover exemption for basic tax registration; every WFOE must obtain tax registration within 30 days of obtaining its business license, regardless of revenue.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export VAT zero-rating is available to a registered WFOE exporter for craft beer products that are physically exported from the PRC customs territory and sold to a non-resident buyer. The WFOE must hold foreign trade operator registration, customs export registration, and file an electronic export customs declaration. The exported goods must be covered by a valid export sales contract, shipped under transport documentation, and the proceeds must be collected from outside China. Where all conditions are met, VAT is charged at 0% on export and the attributable input VAT is refundable through the STA export tax rebate procedure. Cross-border digital services are not relevant to this craft beer distribution scope.

  • Bill of Lading (sea or air waybill, as applicable)
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer
  • Export packing list

China requires electronic invoicing through the STA national invoice system. Full digitalised electronic VAT invoices are generated via the nationwide electronic invoice platform using the WFOE's Unified Social Credit Code, and are transmitted to the STA in near real-time with digital authentication controls. Before an input VAT credit can be claimed, the purchaser must authenticate the invoice through the statutory VAT invoice comprehensive platform. High-volume taxpayers integrate through API-based fiscal machine/e-invoice interfaces; all invoices carry mandatory digital signatures and anti-fraud tax control codes. No private invoice formats are recognised for VAT deduction unless issued through the STA-controlled system.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to strict STA and Customs audit. If the required documentation is incomplete, the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the 180-day period, or the export declaration is found to be false, the taxpayer may be required to repay the VAT refund with late-payment surcharge, lose eligibility for export tax rebates, and be assessed an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Customs may separately impose penalties for false export declarations; serious cases may attract criminal liability for tax evasion or smuggling against the responsible individuals and legal representatives.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. The RMB is convertible for current-account transactions, including trade in goods, freight, insurance, royalties, interest, and profit remittances, subject to genuine-transaction verification. Capital-account transactions remain regulated and are opened through SAFE registrations, quotas, and approvals on a case-by-case basis. A WFOE receiving equity capital from abroad is treated as foreign direct investment and must complete SAFE FDI registration before the onshore bank can open and credit the capital-account escrow account. Current-account export proceeds for cold-chain keg sales must be repatriated to China and credited to an export settlement account; they can be retained in foreign currency within the SAFE-approved quota or converted into RMB at the onshore market rate. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is permitted after annual audited financial statements, the corporate income tax settlement, the legal reserve fund allocation, and the required dividend withholding tax certificate have been completed. SAFE monitors trade flows through cross-referencing customs declarations, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and payment records. Capital-account outflows such as external debt repayments, intercompany loans, and foreign equity investments require specific registration and cannot be executed freely. Exchange-rate risks are managed through the PBOC daily fixing and the ±2 percent onshore RMB/USD trading band, while hedging products must meet the real-demand principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless the bank has registered an approved deferred collection extension under exceptional open-account trade terms. The proceeds may be retained in foreign currency on the exporter's SAFE-verified settlement account up to the enterprise's approved retention quota; any amount above that quota must be converted into RMB at the market exchange rate. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio for qualifying exporters, but the entire receipt must be either retained within the quota or settled through a licensed Chinese bank before the SAFE trade monitoring system closes the associated export collection file.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Conditional / Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in Mainland China and cannot open the statutory RMB basic deposit account or SAFE-designated capital-account for a WFOE. They cannot provide clearing in the CIPS system or issue commercial documentary letters of credit. A WFOE engaged in cold-chain craft beer distribution must maintain its primary account relationship with a licensed PRC commercial bank. Domestic fintech payment institutions with a SAFE cross-border RMB/FX pilot license may be used as auxiliary gateways for collection and reconciliation of small-value cross-border e-commerce payments, but they cannot replace the bank for export proceeds repatriation, FX settlement, or capital injection compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must be routed through SAFE's FDI registration system. The WFOE must first complete FDI registration with the onshore bank or SAFE, then open a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account. The overseas parent should send an SWIFT MT103 wire to that account using the FDI equity contribution purpose code specified in the SAFE cross-border receipt/payment code list and must reference the WFOE's Articles of Association and FDI registration number. The receiving bank will perform a capital verification matching the remitter, the amount, the currency, and the registration. After settlement into RMB, the funds may be used only for permitted operating purposes under the negative list, including purchase of brewing equipment, cold-chain infrastructure, raw materials, rent, salaries, and the entity's normal trade disbursements. The WFOE must not use capital inject funds for unauthorized domestic equity investment, onshore loans, or repayment of RMB loans unless permitted by SAFE.For settlements with overseas suppliers including hops, packaging, refrigeration parts, and freight services, the WFOE will use mainstream clearing rails. Direct open-account payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 with proper underlying trade contracts, invoices, customs declarations if applicable, and logistics evidence. Commercial letters of credit are issued through MT700/MT707 and settled against compliant drafts and transport documents. Documentary collections are cleared through bank-to-bank presentation of title documents under URC 522. For RMB-denominated cross-border trade, the WFOE may settle through CIPS and SWIFT CNY channels while keeping separate current-account files for export and import transactions. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Conditional / Low for primary treasury use. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in Mainland China and cannot open the statutory RMB basic deposit account or SAFE-designated capital-account for a WFOE. They cannot provide clearing in the CIPS system or issue commercial documentary letters of credit. A WFOE engaged in cold-chain craft beer distribution must maintain its primary account relationship with a licensed PRC commercial bank. Domestic fintech payment institutions with a SAFE cross-border RMB/FX pilot license may be used as auxiliary gateways for collection and reconciliation of small-value cross-border e-commerce payments, but they cannot replace the bank for export proceeds repatriation, FX settlement, or capital injection compliance.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate under CIT Law Article 4

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross outbound dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise shareholder, under CIT Law Article 37 and Implementation Regulation Article 91; the standard 20% statutory rate is reduced to 10% by State Council rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when controlled tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000; controlled intangible-asset transactions exceed RMB 100,000,000; controlled financial-asset transactions exceed RMB 100,000,000; or other controlled transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000; Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions in a single year exceed RMB 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations to onshore WFOEs, but CIT Law Articles 45 and 47 and the Beneficial Ownership standard require the WFOE to conduct its core income-generating activities in China. For a craft beer distribution WFOE, CIGA includes demand forecasting, inventory ownership and risk, cold-chain logistics control, customer contracting, pricing, credit, and insurance. A WFOE hollowed out into an offshore shell, or a shareholder claiming treaty benefits without substance, will lose relief and face recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulation Article 120 authorize recharacterization where an arrangement lacks reasonable commercial purpose; treaty benefits are denied; the underpaid CIT and withholding tax become payable with 0.05% daily late-interest and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 23 (2023 revision), courts may pierce the corporate veil where shareholders or directors abuse limited liability; directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of diverted funds or unpaid tax; the legal representative may be exit-banned or blacklisted for unresolved tax liabilities, and intentional evasion can trigger imprisonment for up to seven years under Criminal Law Article 201.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 46, Implementation Regulations Articles 119-123, and SAT Public Notice No. 42/2016 authorize adjustment; the tax authority applies the best arm's-length method, disallows excess interest, adjusts taxable income, and charges special tax adjustment interest at a punitive margin over the base rate in addition to the 0.05% daily late payment surcharge; false transfer pricing documentation is penalized under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 at 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute sham transfer pricing agreements may be named in the tax adjustment and held personally liable if corporate assets were diverted through fake invoices or round-tripped loans; criminal exposure for fraudulent tax evasion can lead to imprisonment; personal bank accounts and travel rights may be restricted until restitution is made.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 3, 37, and 38 require the withholding agent to withhold and remit 10% on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes a fine of 50% to 3 times the unwithheld tax on the WFOE, plus daily late payment interest under Article 32; fraudulent withholding returns are subject to Article 63 fraud penalties and potential criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial director are personally exposed: civil restitution of the unwithheld tax, personal fines, exit bans and blacklisting until taxes are settled, and criminal imprisonment for up to seven years where the intentional false payment scheme constitutes tax evasion; courts may disregard the corporate entity where the WFOE was operated as the shareholder's alter ego.
