Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law does not impose a minimum registered capital for a general wholesale foreign-invested enterprise. The Company must state a subscribed registered capital in its Articles of Association, and under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law all subscribed capital must be actually paid in full within five years after establishment. No blocked escrow account or government-designated capital deposit account is required, and no paid-in capital threshold applies to ISIC 4633 wholesale trading.

Funding & unlock path: The registered office and any ancillary warehouse shall obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Approval or Filing Record from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade before occupancy. The premises must meet the requirements of the Fire Prevention and Control Law, including the installation of fire extinguishers, emergency lighting and exit signage; a structural load certificate must be obtained if pallet racking or shelving is installed; and an occupational safety and health declaration must be filed with the local human-resources and workplace-safety supervision agency. For a pure office or non-storage use, the civil-defense clearance is limited to the simplified fire-safety commitment statement endorsed by the property management and local fire authority; for any warehouse, a full fire-protection acceptance inspection is required. This is separate from the food license and is mandatory before employees may lawfully occupy the premises.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The PRC Company Law does not impose a minimum registered capital for a general wholesale foreign-invested enterprise. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online service platform.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is established as a Chinese resident legal person. On incorporation, the Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues a Business License carrying an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the primary Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. The SAMR automatically shares incorporation data with the STA, and the entity must complete the statutory tax registration/reporting process in the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of the Business License date. After filing, the WFOE must register its accounting system, bank deposit accounts, payroll and social security profile, and, for export operations, apply for General VAT taxpayer status and export tax refund/rebate eligibility. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese legal entity that earns China-source income may obtain a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identification number through a one-off tax registration filed with the competent tax authority of the withholding agent or asset/property location, before performing withholding registration.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election mechanism. A qualifying small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect to register as a General VAT taxpayer where the statutory accounting and input-credit conditions are met; otherwise no elective entity-level tax classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic tax registration; all newly established entities must register with the STA. Compulsory General VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) in any consecutive 12-month period; below this amount, small-scale taxpayer status applies until a valid election or registration occurs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated for VAT purposes to the extent that they are physically exported from China, the exporter is a General VAT taxpayer, the goods have been cleared by the China Customs Administration through an export customs declaration, and the export proceeds are repatriated in accordance with foreign exchange administration rules. Whole and skimmed milk powder is eligible for export zero-rating/tax refund unless it appears on the prohibited or restricted export list. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating applies where the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise, consumed wholly outside China, and supported by contract and foreign-exchange receipt evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration form, export tax refund copy)
  • Transport document (Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Railway Cargo Waybill)
  • Foreign Exchange Receipts Declaration and bank collection/settlement receipt evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Special Value-Added Tax invoice(s) for the purchase of the exported goods
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list

China operates a Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under the STA. VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the Electronic Tax Bureau or through STA-certified software/API integration; each electronic invoice receives a unique digital invoice number and is transmitted to the STA's tax control and validation system in near-real time. The FDEI regime does not require physical anti-counterfeit stamping or a paper fiscal seal for fully digitalized invoices; transactions are risk-monitored using big data, invoice code verification, and red-letter invoice confirmation. Legacy tax-control equipment remains available only for residual paper-invoice scenarios, and all invoices must be reported and reconciled to the tax authority.

The STA and China Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims through online document matching, customs clearance verification, and foreign-exchange reconciliation. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, if the export declaration is false, or if the supporting documents are forged or do not correspond to the physical export, the tax authority may disallow the zero-rating, claw back the refunded VAT, impose daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid or refunded tax per day, and impose administrative penalties of 0.5 to 5 times the evaded or refunded tax amount under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious cases may be referred for criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax rebate under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and may also trigger customs penalties, suspension or cancellation of export tax rebate status, and downgrading of the enterprise customs credit rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current account convertibility as an IMF Article VIII member, permitting unrestricted settlement of trade and service transactions, but imposes comprehensive capital account controls. All cross-border foreign exchange transactions must be conducted through designated foreign exchange banks and reported to SAFE. For a WFOE engaged in dairy commodity trade, the initial equity injection and subsequent capital increases require Foreign Exchange Registration (FDI registration) with SAFE, followed by account opening at a domestic bank under a Capital Account. Funds settled from a capital account can only be used for approved business purposes and cannot be diverted to equity investment or loan activities without prior approval. The RMB exchange rate is managed with reference to a basket of currencies and daily midpoint fixing; trade proceeds may be converted at spot or negotiated rates, while profit repatriation is allowed only after annual pending audit, tax reconciliation, and payment of 10% withholding tax on dividends, evidenced by tax clearance and audited financial statements. The convertibility assessment is therefore bifurcated: efficient for current account payments such as import settlement, but restrictive for capital flows such as intra-group loans, equity transfers, and cross-border financing. Additional hurdles include a five-year foreign exchange registration validity review, historical settlement verification, and strict anti-mock-trade documentation checks under the 2020 Banking Payment Settlement Rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory export surrender in 2012. Export proceeds from whole and skimmed milk powder must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the standard 120-day reporting period from the date of shipment, unless deferred collection is approved by SAFE. No mandatory conversion ratio applies; enterprises may retain the foreign currency or sell at the market rate to a designated bank. Proceeds must be reconciled against the Customs-issued export declaration in the SAFE monitoring system, and any discrepancy above the de minimis threshold triggers an investigation. Failure to collect export proceeds within the statutory timeline without valid justification attracts regulatory penalties and a downgrade of the entity's trade credit classification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex and Currencycloud are not licensed to maintain onshore RMB or foreign exchange accounts for Chinese legal entities. Foreign exchange legislation requires all cross-border payments to be routed through designated foreign exchange banks licensed by SAFE. EMIs can provide payment aggregation for offshore treasury visibility but cannot replace the WFOE's onshore banking relationship for capital account movements or statutory trade reconciliation. The Administrative Measures for Payment Services restrict non-bank institutions from settling cross-border goods trade without a PBoC license; therefore, any EMI engagement must be structured as an auxiliary tool operating behind the tier-1 domestic bank primary clearing mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing begins with FDI registration through a designated foreign exchange bank under SAFE, followed by the opening of a Capital Account for the WFOE. Shareholders remit equity funds from offshore accounts using SWIFT MT103 with payment purpose code 622901 (remittance for registered capital). The receiving bank verifies the payment against the SAFE FDI system, credits the capital account, and handles foreign exchange settlement into RMB under the statutory payment order. A Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report within 30 days of receipt. Settled RMB can be used for operational expenses, procurement of raw milk powder, and general working capital, but cannot be deployed into equity investments, loan issuance, or real estate purchases without prior SAFE approval.For settlements with overseas dairy suppliers, the WFOE may use SWIFT MT103 for open account payments, supported by the import contract, customs declaration, and tax invoice submitted to the bank. Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight or usance) are preferred for large milk powder shipments and require a cash deposit or bank credit line. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for low-risk counterparties within the agricultural commodity sector. All outbound payments must be classified under current account purpose codes matching the ISIC 4633 commodity scope. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the PRC Accounting Law, the Company Law, the Enterprise Financial Accounting Report Regulations (State Council Decree No. 287) and the Foreign Investment Law regime, a mandatory independent CPA audit is required for: (1) every wholly foreign-owned enterprise regardless of size, because a WFOE is a foreign-invested entity required to file truthful annual financial information under the FDI reporting system and to maintain audited books for cross-border settlement; (2) any company whose year-end total assets exceed RMB 50,000,000, whose annual operating revenue exceeds RMB 50,000,000, or whose average full-time headcount exceeds 300 (the large/medium-size enterprise test); (3) any dairy or food trading entity holding a food business licence or import/export registration, because the food-safety public interest triggers audit as a supervisory condition even if the quantitative thresholds are not met; and (4) any enterprise engaged in related-party cross-border transactions above the State Taxation Administration's transfer-pricing documentation threshold (annual related-party transactions in goods or other property exceeding RMB 200,000,000 aggregate, or financial transactions exceeding RMB 100,000,000). A dormant WFOE cannot avoid the obligation without an express shareholders' resolution and legal advice; in practice and for credit/permitting purposes the annual audit is always completed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million of related-party tangible-property turnover triggers Local File preparation; related-party intra-group services above RMB 40 million also trigger Local File documentation; related-party intangible or financing transactions above RMB 100 million trigger Local File documentation; Master File is required in China when the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and the conditions of SAT Announcement 2016 No.42 are satisfied.

Economic substance scope: PRC applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the DEMPE framework under BEPS; a WFOE must show substantive decision-making, personnel, premises, and risk management in China for purchasing and selling milk powder; an offshore shell holding company cannot claim DTA withholding relief; the core income-generating activities concept is applied together with the beneficial-owner rule under SAT Notice 2015 No.16, and lack of substance may trigger recharacterisation of profit flows and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, evaded tax is recovered with daily late surcharge and an administrative fine from 50% to 5 times the evaded amount; under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, if evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax legally payable, the controller may be imprisoned for up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years for huge amounts, and fined.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under the Company Law if the director used the WFOE as an instrument of fraud; the director's personal bank accounts, residence, and vehicles may be restrained; the director may be blacklisted by tax authorities, subjected to an exit ban, disqualified from serving as legal representative, and may face criminal detention and imprisonment.
Shell Company and DTA Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority denies treaty relief under SAT Announcement 2015 No.16; the WFOE as withholding agent must pay the unpaid 10% WHT, daily late surcharge, and a withholding-agent penalty from 50% to 3 times the unwithheld amount under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; the offshore entity may also be recharacterised as having a permanent establishment in China, causing additional CIT on attributable profit under the Enterprise Income Tax Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false residency certificates or nominee arrangements are jointly liable for restitution of the unpaid withholding tax; travel bans and tax blacklisting apply; if the scheme is deliberate and material, directors may face criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law.
Disguised Profit Repatriation, Thin-Capitalisation and Fraudulent Remittances

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 and 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement 2016 No.42, the tax authority recharacterises excessive interest or royalties as dividends, disallows the deduction, makes transfer-pricing adjustments, collects 25% CIT plus 10% WHT, and imposes late surcharges and fines from 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; fraudulent cross-border remittances also trigger SAFE foreign-exchange penalties and AML sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are personally liable for approved remittances; personal assets may be traced and frozen; they are blacklisted and prohibited from leaving China until tax liabilities are settled; where false invoices or forged agreements are used, directors face criminal liability and imprisonment for tax-related fraud.
