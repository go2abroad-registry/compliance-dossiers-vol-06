Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A purely virtual office or commercial mailbox is highly likely to be rejected for a WFOE engaged in bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper export. Although China does not universally prohibit virtual offices, the State Taxation Administration requires an actual business address for VAT registration and invoice issuance; the bank under PBOC KYC and AML rules will require a physical inspection, original lease, rental invoices, utility bills, and proof of operations such as storage and staff. For the dehydrated spice trading industry, L/C and TT payments increase scrutiny. A virtual office should therefore be used only as a secondary correspondence address and never as the registered office if bank accounts and export licensing are to be obtained.

Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; official online portal is the National Electronic Tax Service Platform (commonly known as e-Tax).

        
E-government filing portal
The WFOE is established via the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) and obtains a Business License. The AMR record is electronically synchronized to the STA; within 30 days of incorporation the WFOE must complete tax registration through e-Tax. The WFOE (i) confirms the information generated by the business license, (ii) completes real-name authentication of the legal representative and financial officer, (iii) declares its applicable taxes (VAT, Corporate Income Tax, stamp duty, local surcharges), (iv) selects its VAT status (General or Small-scale taxpayer), and (v) obtains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) which serves as both the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. For export operations the WFOE subsequently registers with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) under the same USCC to obtain the customs declaration registration code, and it completes the export tax rebate qualification filing via e-Tax. A foreign non-resident investor does not require a separate Chinese TIN unless it derives China-source income or has a permanent establishment; if required, application is made through a withholding agent and the foreign investor's home-country tax status certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None in the sense of check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. China does not provide pass-through check-the-box elections for WFOEs. The only statutory election directly relevant is the VAT taxpayer status election: an entity with annual taxable sales below CNY 5,000,000 may elect to be registered as a General VAT taxpayer to claim input VAT credits, which is necessary both for export refunds and for issuing export VAT invoices.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
VAT registration is mandatory upon establishment for all Chinese entities and branches with any taxable sales. Compulsory General VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below that threshold an entity may remain a Small-scale taxpayer or elect General taxpayer status. For a WFOE exporting dehydrated vegetables/spices, the entity should be registered as a General VAT taxpayer from inception to obtain input VAT refunds/export rebates.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods physically exported from mainland China (including WFOE-owned bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper) qualify for the VAT zero rate (0%) and corresponding refund/exemption of input VAT where the WFOE is a General VAT taxpayer, has completed export tax rebate qualification, and the exports are made under regular customs supervision. The goods must be genuinely shipped overseas; the sale must be a real cross-border sale to a foreign buyer; and the export proceeds must be repatriated through formal banking/foreign-exchange channels. The tax-exemption/zero-rating is evidenced by the electronic customs release date and supported by an export declaration showing the applicable HS code (ISIC 4638 / HS 0712, 0904 or related). If the underlying standard export tax rebate schedule provides a lower export refund rate, the difference is non-refundable but the VAT rate on the export sale remains 0%.

China operates digital VAT invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV environment. General taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the STA's Digital Electronic Invoice Platform, and invoice data are transmitted in real time to tax authorities. Each invoice carries a unique digital identification code, an electronic signature (electronic stamp), and is subject to automatic risk-control matching against contracts, customs declarations, payments and cost entries. Traditional paper invoices are being phased out and replaced by full-digital electronic invoices (FDEIs) which are cleared electronically; physical firm countersignature is not required. Taxpayers must keep OCR-accessible electronic invoice files and submit VAT returns with automatic invoice reconciliation data.

      
If the export zero-rating is substantiated with false declarations, forgery or non-collection of export proceeds, the tax authority will claw back any VAT refund previously granted, treat the export as domestic taxable sale at the full VAT rate (13%), impose late payment interest at 0.05% per day, and under the Tax Collection Administration Law may impose a penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refund improperly obtained. Customs and tax authorities may jointly apply export rebate blacklisting and administrative penalties; serious fraud also triggers money-laundering customs enforcement and criminal anti-fraud liability for individuals involved. The export documentation and forex receipt records should be retained for at least 5 years.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with disparate convertibility treatment for current-account and capital-account transactions. Current-account FX is substantially convertible: export proceeds, service fees, and ordinary trade disbursements may be settled through designated banks under truth-in-identification verification. Exporters may keep receipts in foreign currency or convert at market rates; no statutory surrender ratio exists. Capital-account activity remains controlled. For a WFOE, FDI registered capital must be injected through a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account after SAFE/FDI registration, converted into RMB only after bank verification, and used within the approved business scope. Dividend/profit remittance is permitted after annual audit, tax clearance, and SAFE reporting; banks review board resolutions and tax payment vouchers. Loans, guarantees, and offshore investments remain subject to registration or approval. For dehydrated spices and vegetables export, routine settlement does not require advance regulatory permit, but every remittance must carry genuine trade documentation. Repeated discrepancies between invoice prices, customs declarations, and logistics documents may trigger SAFE onsite inspections and freezing of future payments. Overall, current-account convertibility is strong; capital-account transactions require disciplined regulatory routing.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
There is no mandatory conversion ratio or fixed statutory time limit for surrendering export receipts under current SAFE rules. Exporters must repatriate overseas proceeds into the PRC through authorised banks without unjustified delay and may retain 100% in a foreign-currency export settlement account or convert to RMB at prevailing market rates. The remitting bank must be provided with customs declaration, commercial invoice, packing list, and bill of lading for verification. Retention of export proceeds in offshore accounts is not permitted unless SAFE specifically approves an offshore account under a current-account framework.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated foreign-exchange supervision to State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Overseas digital EMI platforms are not viable as primary treasury rails for a mainland-incorporated WFOE. PRC banking law requires corporate deposits to be held with licensed domestic depository institutions, and current-account and capital-account FX flows must clear through regulated banks connected to SWIFT, CIPS, and CNAPS. Overseas EMIs such as cross-border neobanks generally lack the PRC branch licenses, SAFE counterparty status, and physical verification capabilities required to open statutory capital escrow or export settlement accounts for a WFOE. Any use of an unlicensed EMI for corporate treasury would create a KYC gap, invite PBOC/SAFE sanctions, and impede capital verification. Feasibility rating: Low. The compliant route is to maintain primary operating accounts at tier-1 domestic commercial banks and use EMIs, if at all, only for ancillary sales-side receivables outside China with explicit board approval and full reconciliation.

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: PRC Company Law and PRC Accounting Law impose a mandatory annual independent CPA audit on every limited liability company, including a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, with no exemption based on thin capitalization, small balance sheet, low turnover, or small headcount. The audit obligation is triggered by the mere fact of being a registered PRC company; this WFOE therefore cannot rely on any small-entity audit waiver. Complementary quantitative classification rules for a wholesale/trading company (ISIC 4638 / NAICS 424490) are: full PRC Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises are required when the entity has 20 or more employees and annual operating turnover of RMB 5 million or more; below those thresholds the simplified Small Enterprise Accounting Standards may be applied, but a CPA audit remains mandatory because the entity is a foreign-invested enterprise. Additional audit mandates would also arise if the company were a public issuer, financial institution, or state-controlled entity, none of which apply to the target WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if a Chinese taxpayer has annual related-party tangible property transfers above RMB 200 million; annual intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; annual financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; or annual other related-party transactions in aggregate above RMB 40 million. Master File is required when the multinational group's ultimate parent has consolidated group revenue above RMB 5.5 billion in the preceding fiscal year. The annual Controlled Transactions Schedule (Form G100000) must accompany the annual tax return whenever related-party transactions exceed the statutory reporting threshold.

      
Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply the Cayman/BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations, but equivalent substance principles operate through CIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Abuse Rule), SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 7 on indirect transfer of Chinese assets, and the beneficial ownership tests in SAT Bulletins 2015 No. 60 and 2018 No. 9. The WFOE must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA), including procurement, inventory holding, pricing, risk management, logistics coordination, and credit control, are genuinely performed in China by its own employees and assets. If these functions are shifted to a zero-tax shell or a conduit holding company solely to obtain treaty relief, the tax authority may recharacterise the structure, deny treaty benefits, impose 25% CIT on the diverted income, and collect 10% withholding tax at distribution, with late payment interest and fraud penalties.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and False VAT Invoicing in Export Sales
        
Statutory sanction: CIT Law and Tax Administration and Collection Law Article 63 require payment of the underpaid tax, late surcharge at 0.05% per day, and an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax. Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion with up to three years' imprisonment for relatively large amounts and up to seven years for huge amounts; Article 205 punishes false special VAT invoicing with imprisonment up to life.

        
Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors and the legal representative jointly and severally liable for the tax debt if separate legal personality is abused to defraud creditors or the State. The tax authority can freeze personal bank accounts, seize personal assets, impose an exit ban through blacklisting, and refer the directors who signed false books to public security for criminal investigation, resulting in personal imprisonment and restitution of the evaded tax.

      

      
      

        Transfer-Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin-Capitalisation Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Articles 41-44 and SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42, the tax authority may adjust the profits, compute taxable income by deemed methods, disallow interest above the statutory 2:1 debt-to-equity ratio under Article 46, and impose a penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax as well as late surcharge. Failure to produce required TP documentation may also result in the burden of proof shifting to the taxpayer and an adjusted assessment based on the best available comparables.

        
Director personal liability: If the director knowingly causes false TP documentation or arranged artificial circular loans to strip profit, the limited liability shield can be pierced under company law; the director is personally liable for restitution of unpaid tax and interest, may have personal bank and real-estate assets seized, and is subject to travel blacklisting. Where the false documentation is used to evade tax above the criminal threshold, the responsible director faces personal criminal prosecution and imprisonment of up to seven years.

      

      
      

        Treaty Shopping and Sham Beneficial Ownership of Dividends, Interest, or Royalties
        
Statutory sanction: The competent Chinese tax authority will deny the DTA claim under the General Anti-Abuse Rule in CIT Law Article 47, SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 60, SAT Bulletin 2018 No. 9, and the BEPS Principal Purpose Test contained in the Multilateral Instrument. The WFOE must withhold the full statutory 10% dividend/interest/royalty WHT plus 6% VAT and surcharges on royalties/interest; the deficiency is assessed with 0.05% daily surcharge and a 50%-500% fraud penalty. An offshore indirect transfer of the WFOE may be re-sourced as Chinese taxable under SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 7, creating additional 25% CIT exposure and 10% withholding at exit.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations, prepare sham substance documents, or instruct the company to remit funds without withholding face civil liability for the un-withheld tax and interest, restitution orders, asset freezing, and exit bans. If tax evasion is intentional, criminal liability follows under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment for the responsible natural persons; the corporate veil is pierced where the conduit arrangement is an abuse of corporate form to evade tax.
