Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Company Law and the Implementing Regulations on Market Entity Registration, a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must maintain an actual, verifiable business address within Mainland China that matches the address stated in the registration certificate. A virtual office or commercial mailbox is ordinarily rejected by SAMR for registration, and it is also rejected by municipal tax authorities and commercial banks for AML/KYC and invoice purposes in the off-price finished garments wholesale industry. The entity must submit a lease agreement, property ownership certificate, landlord authorization, site inspection photograph, and evidence of operational management at the registered address. For VAT invoice issuance and corporate bank account opening, a physical premises with commercial signage, inventory storage capacity, and staff attendance is expected; a post-office mailbox or serviced address alone will not establish sufficient commercial substance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE under the Foreign Investment Law (2019). The registered capital is determined by the shareholders and must be fully subscribed within the period specified in the Articles of Association (typically 20-30 years). The capital must be paid into a dedicated capital contribution account in China, and funds must be used for the company's business operations. There is no escrow requirement, but the capital must be deposited in accordance with the subscribed schedule.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate (消防安全检查合格证) obtained from the local Fire Department after inspection of the warehouse. Occupational safety per the Work Safety Law, including emergency exits, fire extinguishers, and employee training. The warehouse must also pass a structural load inspection if it exceeds standard capacity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax platform).

E-government filing portal

For a PRC WFOE, the sole statutory taxpayer identifier is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The WFOE completes incorporation with SAMR and, upon completion of industrial and commercial registration, the tax authority automatically registers the entity for Value-Added Tax and Enterprise Income Tax. The WFOE then logs on to the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days to confirm its tax registration information, bank account, accounting standards, invoice issuer status, and to apply for VAT general taxpayer qualification. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China obtains a tax identification number by filing a non-resident tax registration with the competent tax authority, directly or through its withholding agent, using the e-Tax platform, before reporting China-sourced income or receiving a VAT invoice from a Chinese withholding payer.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide check-the-box, pass-through, hybrid mismatch, or consolidated election mechanisms; a WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. General anti-avoidance and transfer pricing rules apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic VAT registration because all SAMR-incorporated enterprises are registered for VAT automatically. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the entity is usually a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Qualified export of physical goods: the WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer with export tax refund qualification. Goods must actually leave China under a customs export declaration; the buyer must be a non-resident overseas entity; title and risk must pass offshore; the sale must be a genuine foreign trade transaction at arm's length; and the goods must not be prohibited or restricted for export. For qualifying exports, output VAT is 0% and the recoverable input VAT is refunded under the Export Tax Refund (Exemption and Refund) Scheme.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form) with official release records and export database match
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document/air waybill showing the goods left China and destination abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt and settlement note evidencing collection and repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT Special Invoice or export sales invoice issued to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list, commercial invoice, sales contract, and electronic export tax rebate declaration data submitted through the STA export tax rebate system

Golden Tax Phase IV mandates real-time, state-side fiscal clearance. All Special VAT Invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices must be issued through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Platform; each invoice must carry an official tax-control digital stamp, unique invoice code, QR code and electronic signature. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time. Third-party systems must connect through the official tax-control interface or authorized API; there is no optional paper approval step.

All export zero-rating claims are cross-checked among customs declaration data, VAT invoice data, bank FX receipts, and STA export rebate files. A false export declaration, forged invoice, missing payment, or unrepatriated export proceeds invalidates zero-rating and triggers repayment of the VAT refund plus daily late-payment surcharge. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, fraudulent export refunds are subject to recovery of the entire amount defrauded and a penalty of one to five times the amount; the STA can also suspend export rebate treatment. In serious cases, Criminal Law Article 204 (Export Tax Refund Fraud) imposes criminal liability including imprisonment up to life and confiscation of property. The recovery period for fraudulent refunds is unlimited, so retrospective clawback is not barred by the ordinary three-year or five-year limitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict current account convertibility and capital account control. The RMB is convertible for trade-related current account transactions under the Uniform Rules on Foreign Exchange of the People's Bank of China, but capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections, shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, require SAFE registration, verification, and in some cases prior approval. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in off-price wholesale liquidation of branded overstock apparel must open a capital account Foreign Exchange Capital Account (FCA) to receive foreign equity contributions, and must execute specific SAFE protocols such as the Foreign Investment Information Report and capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Surrender of export proceeds is largely relaxed: exporters may retain up to 100% of their foreign exchange earnings in current accounts, subject to the SAFE rolling 30-day verification window and annual quota for retained balances. However, profit remittance is not automatic; the WFOE must demonstrate audited financials, tax clearance certificates from the State Taxation Administration, and compliance with the Capital Account Foreign Exchange Settlement for Renminbi Conversion requirements. Foreign exchange settlement of capital funds is restricted to genuine business operations within the registered business scope, and disbursements for inventory liquidation purchases must be backed by contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and logistics bills. While the SAFE reform agenda has liberalized many current account items, the bank reality check demands that all cross-border receipts and payments be supported by an underlying transaction ledger and filed through the SAFE Foreign Exchange Monitoring Platform. Non-trade current account payments above USD 50,000 require additional documentation and account-smart monitoring; therefore the WFOE must maintain a dual-currency bank account architecture with separate CNY basic deposit account and USD current account, plus a dedicated FCA for equity injections. In summary, RMB is fully convertible on the trade current account but the capital account remains regulated, with compulsory SAFE registration, verification, rendering and post-verification of onshore expenses; these are manageable for a properly structured WFOE treasury, but fintech EMIs have no license to act as capital receiving banks for Chinese resident WFOEs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 2012 and subsequent reforms, export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion or surrender; the exporter may retain foreign currency in a current account indefinitely subject to the SAFE rolling verification of the underlying export business within 30 calendar days after the funds are credited. If the WFOE chooses to settle to CNY, the settlement must be completed within the SAFE direct settlement quota and must be supported by the export customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and the electronic expedited verification record in the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; the retention ratio is effectively 100% for current account receipts, but the bank will apply an automatic allocation: a percentage equal to the outstanding export verification quota may be blocked from conversion if the customs data record mismatch is unresolved.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI solutions are legally not viable for the WFOE as its primary transaction banking or capital-receiving layer. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, all cross-border trade-related FX receipts and payments for a mainland-incorporated WFOE must be settled through a domestic bank that has a SAFE terminal license for the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and the Capital Account Information System. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut do not hold a Chinese banking license nor a SAFE direct settlement qualification; they cannot issue a Foreign Investment Information Registration stamp nor perform capital account verification. They may be used only as an auxiliary collection tool for overseas e-commerce marketplace payouts outside China, provided the WFOE maintains a separate domestic account for SAFE-recordable proceeds and ensures that no SAFE-mandated transaction remains unrecorded. The compliance burden is simply too high to rely on an EMI for inbound equity, distributor advances, or Chinese tax clearance linked profit repatriation; hence the recommendation is to use tier-1 domestic banks for all regulatory-recorded flows and to limit EMI usage to non-regulated receivables from foreign wholesale clients that are already settled by a domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the WFOE must be remitted from the overseas parent company bank account directly to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account (FCA) at a SAFE-designated domestic bank. The remittance instruction must include an accurate payment purpose code, typically 'Introducing foreign direct investment into China', and the receiving bank must complete the Foreign Investment Information Report within the SAFE online system. Within 15 days of receipt, the bank obtains the FIE registration number and the WFOE applies to the local SAFE branch for the capital account FX settlement quota; the funds can then be settled into CNY for payment of supplier, warehouse, and payroll expenses in the domestic basic deposit account, but each CNY settlement over RMB 500,000 requires a supporting purchase invoice or contract. A certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report within 30 days after the contributed funds are actually received. Any future capital increase or shareholder loan must be registered, and even a successful reporting does not eliminate the requirement to file the quarterly report on foreign investment operations.Outbound trade disbursements for the off-price garments business are routed via SWIFT MT103 for high-volume, low-individual-value supplier payments under USD 50,000, with full remittance information including the trade invoice and contract number. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI solutions are legally not viable for the WFOE as its primary transaction banking or capital-receiving layer. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, all cross-border trade-related FX receipts and payments for a mainland-incorporated WFOE must be settled through a domestic bank that has a SAFE terminal license for the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and the Capital Account Information System. Foreign EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut do not hold a Chinese banking license nor a SAFE direct settlement qualification; they cannot issue a Foreign Investment Information Registration stamp nor perform capital account verification. They may be used only as an auxiliary collection tool for overseas e-commerce marketplace payouts outside China, provided the WFOE maintains a separate domestic account for SAFE-recordable proceeds and ensures that no SAFE-mandated transaction remains unrecorded. The compliance burden is simply too high to rely on an EMI for inbound equity, distributor advances, or Chinese tax clearance linked profit repatriation; hence the recommendation is to use tier-1 domestic banks for all regulatory-recorded flows and to limit EMI usage to non-regulated receivables from foreign wholesale clients that are already settled by a domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File documentation is required when total annual related-party transaction volume reaches RMB 1 billion or more, or when the WFOE participates in a cost-sharing arrangement and is selected by the tax administration. The Annual Related Party Transaction Report must be filed with the annual CIT return regardless of threshold where covered transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the Gulf Cooperation Council sense, but anti-avoidance law under EIT Law Article 47 applies substance-over-form where a structure has no reasonable commercial purpose. For treaty purposes, tax authorities apply Beneficial Ownership and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) principles, examining where sourcing, pricing, inventory management, logistics, credit risk, and strategic decisions for the off-price garment liquidation business are actually performed. A WFOE acting as a bare title-holder with management exercised outside China may be recharacterized as lacking substance, and a foreign recipient that merely receives dividends without executing any CIGA will be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority collects the underpaid tax plus a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years for less serious evasion and 3-7 years for large amounts, plus fines and confiscation of property. E-Tax cross-matching with customs, bank settlement, and VAT invoice databases creates a near-automatic detection mechanism.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or signed false financial statements face piercing of the corporate veil: they are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties, personal bank accounts and property can be attached, and they are placed on the joint punishment list for dishonesty, resulting in travel bans, exit restrictions, denial of high-speed rail and flight privileges, and imprisonment up to 7 years if criminal liability is established.
Beneficial Ownership / Treaty Shopping Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: The tax bureau denies treaty benefits because the intermediary is not the beneficial owner, recharacterizes the payment, and assesses the full 10% statutory WHT on gross dividends. The WFOE as withholding agent is liable for the under-withheld amount under Tax Collection Law Article 69. EIT Law Article 47 GAAR permits the tax authority to disregard the conduit structure and adjust the taxable income, with late-payment surcharge and, where fraud is proved, a 50%-500% evasion penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham conduit structure are personally liable for the WHT shortfall and penalties in civil asset restitution proceedings; responsible officers may be blacklisted and banned from leaving China until the tax liability is settled. If corporate assets are insufficient because dividends were stripped out, courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and hold directors personally liable for company debts, with criminal tax-evasion charges available where deliberate misrepresentation is proven.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41 and 46, the tax authority makes a transfer pricing adjustment using an approved arm's length methodology, denies the interest deduction for excessive debt, and assesses the resulting tax shortfall plus daily late-payment interest. False TP documentation supporting manipulated margins triggers a 50%-500% penalty under Tax Collection Law Article 63. Noncompliance with documentation requirements shifts the evidentiary burden to the taxpayer and may result in special tax adjustment interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the TP policy and knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing or thin-capitalization structures are subject to personal civil liability where the company cannot pay the adjusted tax; the corporate veil can be pierced for abuse of limited liability. The legal representative and finance director may be blacklisted, denied exit from China, and have personal assets frozen for restitution. Where the TP manipulation amounts to intentional tax evasion, criminal penalties including imprisonment are applicable to the responsible directors.
