Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China and its Electronic Tax Administration portal (e-Tax); local STA branch at the WFOE's registered address is the competent authority for routine filings.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE, tax registration is not a separate application. The State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license with a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), and this USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the national multi-certificate integration system; the USCC becomes the permanent taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days of SAMR issuance, the legal representative or authorized tax agent must log into the Electronic Tax Administration, complete real-name authentication, and perfect the tax profile: financial institution bank account information, accounting system details, statutory representative identity documents, registered office lease deed, articles of association, expected business scope aligned to ISIC 4646, whether the entity is a small-scale or general VAT taxpayer, and designated contact for tax correspondence. A foreign investor cannot obtain a separate TIN for the WFOE before SAMR registration; after formation, the foreign shareholder may additionally obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration number only if it derives direct Chinese-source income separately from the WFOE.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan) of annual taxable sales; exceeding this amount makes VAT general taxpayer registration mandatory. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of ophthalmic eyewear frames from China customs territory qualifies for zero-rating (export exemption with credit and refund for general VAT taxpayers) if the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the goods cross the customs frontier, the export is evidenced by a customs release record, a genuine export sales contract exists, payment is received from overseas in convertible currency through designated foreign exchange channels, and no value-added tax is collected at the domestic standard rate. Export of goods without an export customs declaration is not zero-rated.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (officially released Customs Declaration Form under the H2018 customs clearance system) with export declaration number and customs release date
  • Commercial invoice / export sales invoice reflecting the actual overseas buyer and foreign-currency consideration
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) issued by the carrier showing the consignor and consignee and port of final discharge
  • Foreign exchange proceeds collection evidence: Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export collection voucher or export proceeds entry record in the bank cross-border foreign exchange monitoring system, recording the overseas remitting party and amount
  • Customs agent authorization records and packing list, if requested in audit

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-controlled digital channels. The nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice ('all-electronic invoice') platform is progressively adopted; every invoice must be generated using STA-assigned invoice codes, contain a real-time anti-counterfeiting QR code and cryptographic stamp, and invoice issuance data is transmitted to the STA's integrated tax application system in real time. VAT special invoices intended for input VAT credit carry invoice authentication via the national VAT invoice check-and-clearance platform; no physical pre-printing of blank official invoices is required once the electronic platform is fully onboarded. Purchasers must verify invoices online before crediting input VAT, and the WFOE must connect its ERP or billing system to the e-invoice interface or use the e-Tax directly.

Export zero-rating is audited by the STA through cross-referencing customs release data, banking foreign exchange receipt data, and VAT invoice data. If foreign exchange is not received within the 180-day statutory period, or documentation is incomplete, the export may be reversed to domestic taxable sales and VAT charged at 13%; the taxpayer is liable to repay any refunded VAT plus late payment surcharge. Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating or export rebate claims trigger the PRC Tax Administration Law penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax falsely obtained, a clawback of the full refund with daily late surcharge, and, in serious cases, referral for criminal prosecution for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a strict but partially liberalized convertibility system. Current account transactions such as export sales, import payments, service fees, and profit remittances are generally convertible and may be settled through designated banks after verification of genuine underlying documents. Capital account transactions remain fundamentally controlled: inbound equity subscriptions must be routed through a SAFE-registered FDI capital account; loan conversion, outward investment, and cross-border guarantees require separate registration or approval. The WFOE must obtain a Foreign Investment Information Report (FIRS) record with a SAFE filing identifier before any shareholder remittance; once received, capital proceeds can be converted into RMB only for permitted operating purposes under the payment-for-settlement post-monitoring system. Profit remittance for a wholesale distributor requires audited financial statements, tax filing certificates, and a board resolution; funds cannot be remitted before completing corporate income tax and withholding or income settlement. There is no mandatory foreign exchange surrender for export revenue, but all foreign currency receipts must be brought onshore and reported to SAFE. In practice, the largest hurdle is documentary: banks must see contracts, invoices, customs data, tax records, and FIRS registration before each significant FX conversion or remittance, making treasury management compliance-intensive but operationally acceptable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China does not impose a statutory export surrender ratio; the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%. Export proceeds must be repatriated to a SAFE-registered foreign exchange settlement account and either maintained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at the onshore market rate through a designated bank. The bank ties each receipt to the corresponding export customs declaration and trade contract; unexplained receipts or unmatched proceeds are subject to SAFE post-event monitoring. Although no fixed day-count is codified as a hard surrender deadline, an export collection that remains unreported or unexplained beyond 90 days after customs exit will trigger a SAFE scrutiny request and may suspend the WFOE's trade foreign exchange designation. Prompt repatriation is therefore mandatory in practice.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for direct account services, but Medium as an ancillary integration layer. The PRC regulatory regime does not recognise an offshore electronic money institution as a lawful deposit-taking or settlement entity. PBOC and SAFE do not authorise foreign digital EMIs to open or operate the WFOE's primary settlement account, hold RMB customer funds, or perform onshore FX clearing. An offshore EMI may only act as a treasury-visibility, invoicing, or payment-orchestration tool if the actual funds are held and settled through a Chinese licensed Tier-1 bank or a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution. Because the WFOE must maintain a bank account in China subject to SAFE trade monitoring, the operating treasury account must remain at a domestic commercial bank; the EMI cannot replace the bank account and is legally barred from direct servicing as a standalone account provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All incoming equity capital must be sent by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital escrow account, never to an operating settlement account. The remitter must use a capital-account transaction purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment, and the receiving bank will verify the FIRS registration number, business licence, and articles of association before accepting the credit. After receipt, the WFOE may apply to convert the foreign currency into RMB only for permitted operating purposes, with settlement executed under the SAFE capital-account-income payment-confirmation mechanism and supported by contracts or invoices for rent, salaries, inventory, software, logistics, and similar operating expenditures. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed any of the following RMB thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File is mandatory when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply an OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations regime as seen in certain offshore financial centres, but Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 imposes a General Anti-Abuse Rule requiring reasonable business purpose and economic substance. For a WFOE, core income-generating activities include distribution functions, procurement decisions, inventory risk, credit risk, management control, personnel, assets, and bank accounts maintained in China; failure to evidence such substance can trigger recharacterization, denial of intercompany deductions, upward income adjustments, and denial of treaty benefits at the shareholder level.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Invoicing Fraud

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63 requires recovery of unpaid tax plus a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes a fine and imprisonment of up to 3 years where the evaded tax is relatively large and exceeds 10% of tax due, and imprisonment of 3 to 7 years plus fines where the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of tax due.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly fabricates accounts may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law and be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and civil damages; the tax authority may issue an exit restriction or blacklisting order under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law, and the director may face personal criminal imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 allows the tax authority to adjust taxable income to an arm's-length result, and Article 44 permits an assessment based on available information if TP documentation is refused or inadequate; adjusted tax may attract daily late-payment surcharges and potential penalties for concealment if the pricing is fraudulent, and the tax authority may add an interest uplift on underpaid CIT arising from transfer pricing adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution if an artificial transfer pricing arrangement is used to evade tax or injure creditors; unpaid adjusted tax can trigger travel bans and blacklisting; if the director knowingly signed false TP documentation or used sham contracts to evade tax, criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may apply with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
Shell Company, Lack of Substance and Abusive Treaty-Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and related anti-abuse rules allow the State Taxation Administration to recharacterize an arrangement lacking reasonable business purpose, deny treaty benefits, disallow intercompany deductions, and reclassify non-deductible profit distributions; AML and corporate registration regulations also permit fines and administrative penalties for misuse of shell entities.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers may have the corporate veil pierced where the shell form is abused to defraud the tax authority or creditors; they can be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and restitution, face travel bans and blacklisting for non-payment, and may be prosecuted for criminal tax fraud or money-laundering offenses involving imprisonment and fines if the structure was deliberately used to conceal ownership or evade tax.
