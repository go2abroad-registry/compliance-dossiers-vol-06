Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an ordinary WFOE limited liability company. The registered capital is determined by the shareholders in the Articles of Association pursuant to Article 26 of the PRC Company Law and must be fully subscribed, with payment periods clearly stated. No blocked escrow account is required by national company law. For wholesale distribution of active pharmaceutical ingredients, the provincial Medical Products Administration will nevertheless assess paid-up substance and operational storage capacity before issuing the Drug Distribution License. Foreign capital contributions must be remitted into a designated foreign currency capital account opened with a licensed bank and may be settled for legitimate operational use after completing foreign direct investment reporting with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing and administration portal is the National Electronic Tax Office (Electronic Tax Bureau / e-Tax system), which handles VAT, Corporate Income Tax, and export tax rebate filings.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE formation: Step 1: Register the wholly foreign-owned limited liability enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the Foreign Investment Law. Step 2: Obtain the Business License bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); under the multi-license integration and multi-certificate integration reform, the USCC is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Step 3: Complete post-establishment tax activation in the Electronic Tax Office, including legal representative identity verification, tax administrator confirmation, accounting system filing, and bank deposit account reporting. Step 4: Determine VAT taxpayer classification; if annual taxable sales of goods meet or exceed RMB 5 million, general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory; when below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies by default but the WFOE may elect general VAT taxpayer treatment if accounting is sound. Step 5: If a foreign non-resident enterprise such as the overseas parent itself derives PRC-source income or must carry out withholding obligations, it must separately complete non-resident enterprise tax registration at the competent tax authority using its home-country registration and tax-residence evidence, after which the STA assigns a non-resident taxpayer reference code; this does not replace the WFOE's own USCC/TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not offer 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch entity classification; the WFOE is by law a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Law. Available elections relevant to indirect taxation are: (a) voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer when turnover is below RMB 5 million; (b) the optional simplified VAT collection method at 3% for specifically enumerated categories only, and generally unavailable or restricted for large API wholesale exporters; and (c) the ability to forgo small-scale taxpayer preferential treatment in order to preserve input VAT credit credibility. No statutory hybrid mismatch election exists in PRC domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5 million annual taxable sales; the WFOE is a VAT taxpayer from establishment, and general VAT taxpayer registration becomes mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed this threshold; voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold for wholesale API businesses with adequate accounting and invoicing capacity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply the zero VAT rate and claim input VAT refund via the PRC export tax rebate scheme if: (a) the entity is the exporter of record and holds the required pharmaceutical API wholesale license and any applicable NMPA-controlled-export authorization; (b) the goods physically depart from a PRC customs port and are recorded in the GACC export declaration database; (c) the sale is made with a genuine overseas buyer, in foreign currency, and the transaction is not a sham or intra-group circular trade; (d) the export invoice, sales contract, customs declaration, transport documents, and bank receipt of payment are mutually consistent; and (e) the export tax rebate declaration is filed in the Electronic Tax Office within the statutory rebate period. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating would require that the services are supplied to a non-resident, wholly consumed outside China, and have no nexus with PRC real estate or goods; a service export contract, non-resident evidence, overseas invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt proof would be required in place of customs documents.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export clearance form with Customs bill number issued by the General Administration of Customs)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual export
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order with HS code, price, Incoterms, and destination port
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Voucher / Bank Collection Notification / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) proving receipt of export proceeds
  • Pharmaceutical API export health or sales certificate and any controlled-export permit issued by the National Medical Products Administration for the relevant API category

China enforces real-time VAT invoice clearance through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax Phase IV system. The WFOE must issue invoices through the National VAT Invoice Management System or, after the digitized e-invoice reform, through the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform, where each invoice receives a unique tax-verified invoice number generated in real time and is cleared and archived in the national tax database. The system is accessed through the Electronic Tax Office or authorized e-invoice platform APIs; anti-counterfeiting is supported by the national invoice verification portal and digital invoice signature controls. For exported pharmaceutical API goods, the export declaration data from the General Administration of Customs (GACC) must be electronically matched in the STA export tax rebate system before the zero-rated refund can be claimed.

Export zero-rating claims are cross-validated by the STA against GACC customs transaction data and SAFE foreign-exchange collection data. If the input VAT refund has been paid and the claim is subsequently found false, overstated, unsupported, or if the export proceeds are not repatriated and declared within the applicable 180-day SAFE/export-verification deadline without a lawfully registered extension, the STA will require repayment of the rebated VAT plus statutory late-payment interest, and may impose an administrative penalty of up to 50 percent of the tax involved. Deliberate fraudulent export declarations with intent to obtain VAT refunds can trigger criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebate under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and SAFE may impose separate foreign-exchange administrative penalties for non-repatriation or delayed reporting. The WFOE's high-risk pharmaceutical classification does not alter general VAT rules but increases document-integrity audit sensitivity; a missing FIRC, unmatched customs record, or inconsistent transport chain will result in rejection or retrospective clawback of the zero-rating benefit in the reviewed period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed current-account convertibility regime while retaining capital-account controls. For a WFOE engaged in the wholesale distribution of active pharmaceutical ingredients, routine trade receipts and payments are current-account items and may be processed freely through designated banks provided the underlying transactions are genuine and supported by invoices, customs declarations and logistics evidence. Capital-account operations are not freely convertible: the WFOE must complete foreign direct investment (FDI) foreign exchange registration before injecting capital, open a dedicated capital account, and use the funds only for approved business purposes under the truthfulness and self-use principle before settling into RMB. There is no mandatory surrender rule for export proceeds in China; exporters may retain foreign exchange in settlement accounts or convert it at onshore bank rates. Profit remittance, however, carries procedural hurdles: an audited balance sheet and income statement, a tax payment clearance certificate, a board resolution declaring the dividend, a certified profit report, and proof that the distributions are made only from after-tax profits. Capital account inflows such as FDI equity also require registration with SAFE and verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Overall, operational liquidity is convertible within the current-account framework, but the WFOE must maintain full SAFE reporting and documentary discipline for every cross-border flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE goods-trade administration regime, export proceeds must be received in a domestic bank through a foreign exchange settlement account or trade settlement account; there is no mandatory surrender ratio or fixed conversion ratio. For A-class trade enterprises, receipts may be retained in foreign currency indefinitely or settled into RMB at the bank spot rate by the enterprise's own decision. The statutory timeline is the contractual payment term reported in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and banks normally require export receipts to be matched to the corresponding customs export declaration before verification is closed; if the enterprise is downgraded to B/C-class, SAFE may impose an additional waiting period, often 90 days, before conversion, or require prior registration.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

The viability of offshore fintech EMIs is low for the mainland WFOE. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut and Airwallex are not licensed by the People's Bank of China or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to maintain onshore RMB accounts, open SAFE-registered capital accounts, or provide FX settlement for capital-account transactions. They can neither act as the designated bank under SAFE's FDI registration nor submit mandatory inward/outbound reporting to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Their role is limited to ancillary payment services in foreign jurisdictions; they cannot clear RMB through CIPS on behalf of the WFOE. Using an offshore EMI as the primary treasury channel would create an immediate KYC failure and a regulatory breach. The WFOE should rely exclusively on licensed domestic tier-1 commercial banks for all onshore account structures, while an overseas treasury satellite could use a fintech payout rail only after formal whitelisting by the group treasury and never for capital account flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration at the selected bank and open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account in its own name. The foreign investor then remits registered capital through SWIFT MT103 from its own bank account; the remittance must indicate the SAFE FDI registration number and use the SAFE-designated purpose code for foreign direct investment equity contributions. The bank records the incoming capital in the SAFE Capital Account Monitoring System; after receipt, the WFOE may settle the capital into RMB for permitted business use or keep it in foreign currency. Every injection must be followed by a certified public accountant capital verification report within the statutory timeframe, and the bank will conduct a post-verification check that the funded amount matches the approved registered capital schedule. No third-party pre-funding, cash deposits or account-to-account pass-throughs are acceptable for capital injection.For direct supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 with the correct goods-trade transaction code and submit the customs declaration, commercial invoice and transport documents to the bank. For bulk pharma API purchases from overseas or high-value sales settlements, issue an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit via SWIFT MT700 through Bank of China, ICBC or China Construction Bank, or use Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) where the bank acts as principal. RMB cross-border trade payments can be cleared using CIPS through the domestic bank; USD, EUR and other currencies clear over SWIFT correspondent banking.
