Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital is prescribed for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise organized as a limited liability company. The sole shareholder must determine registered capital in the Articles of Association and must subscribe it in full within five years from the date of incorporation in accordance with Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law. There is no blocked escrow account or mandatory initial deposit; however, foreign currency capital contributions must be routed through a bank-authorized foreign exchange account and reported under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange capital account regime.

Funding & unlock path: The company's warehouse and office must obtain fire protection acceptance filing (construction fire protection record) from the local housing and urban-rural development authority where the building is newly constructed or renovated, and a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate is required for premises open to the public. The premises must comply with Code for Fire Protection Design of Buildings GB 50016-2014, including fire zones, fire extinguishers, emergency exits, emergency lighting, smoke alarms, and safe storage of flammable liquids. Civil defense authorities may require an emergency response plan and quarterly fire drills. Occupational clearance requires personal protective equipment for personnel handling biological reagents, eyewash stations, first aid kits, safety data sheets for each reagent, and annual workplace safety training. No Safety Production Permit is required for non-manufacturing wholesale distribution.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up share capital is prescribed for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise organized as a limited liability company. The sole shareholder must determine registered capital in the Articles of Association and must subscribe it in full within five years from the date of incorporation in accordance with Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); centralized online filing and compliance administration are conducted through the STA Unified Electronic Taxpayer Portal (national e-tax bureau platform), supplemented by the local municipal tax service bureau where the WFOE is resident.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for Chinese tax purposes. Step 2: Within 30 days of issuance of the Business License, complete tax registration confirmation on the STA Unified Electronic Taxpayer Portal; appoint a legal representative, financial officer and tax filing agent; pass real-name authentication; and confirm the taxpayer division item (general VAT taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer). Step 3: Activate the electronic invoice service on the STA digital invoice platform and apply for the issuance quota of fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices. Step 4: Register with the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) for import/export declarations and obtain the customs registration code required for cross-border goods shipment. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident enterprise that derives China-source income without a Chinese permanent establishment, the competent STA tax office issues a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Identification Number upon withholding registration or place-of-business registration; for the WFOE itself, the USCC serves as the single, lifetime Chinese tax identity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch entity election. Available statutory elections include: (a) voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer even if annual taxable turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 compulsory threshold, subject to adequate accounting and controls; (b) small-scale taxpayer treatment at the simplified percentage levy while the taxpayer remains below the mandatory general-registration threshold; and (c) for exporters, the statutory election among the applicable VAT export-tax-rebate computation methods (exemption, credit and refund method versus exemption and refund method). Hybrid-mismatch directional rules: None for domestic tax classification purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in aggregate taxable sales in any rolling 12 consecutive months; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer, and voluntary general-VAT registration remains available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A WFOE exporter may apply the 0% VAT export rate to eligible IVD diagnostic reagents only when: (1) the goods are physically declared to GACC and leave Chinese customs territory, supported by an electronic customs export declaration; (2) the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer with sound books and a valid export-tax-rebate file; (3) the transaction is genuine and shows consistent goods flow, invoice flow, customs flow and payment flow; (4) export proceeds are received, declared and repatriated through a designated bank foreign-exchange account; and (5) the exporter submits the prescribed export-tax-rebate digital dossier with full, accurate product classification, valuation and destination data. Offshore digital services may qualify only where supplied to non-resident recipients and where separate statutory zero-rating/refund conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Bill of Entry generated by GACC with customs release status)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Chinese territory
  • Original export sales contract, commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (bank collection confirmation recorded in the SAFE trade monitoring system) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT Special Invoice or standard VAT invoice evidencing the domestic input VAT borne on goods or services used in the exported product
  • Export Tax Rebate declaration digital dossier covering the HS code, declared value, product identity and consistency matching data

China operates the comprehensive digital electronic invoice regime under the Golden Tax IV system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA electronic invoice platform via encrypted API connectivity and carry a unique 20-digit ticket number, an anti-fraud element and dynamic QR verification. Every invoice issuance, receipt confirmation, credit-note and red-letter cancellation is transmitted to the tax authority in near real time; physical sealing or manual stamping no longer authenticates fully digitalized invoices. Large-volume issuers must integrate their accounting systems with the e-tax platform and comply with real-time data clearing, anti-fraud digital stamping and automated invoice-state surveillance.

STA and GACC jointly examine export zero-rating claims under the Golden Tax IV risk-control system, cross-matching export declarations, digital invoices, transport documents, bank collections and counterparty information. If a 0% VAT export claim is unsubstantiated, falsely documented or found to be part of an export-refund fraud scheme, the authorities will (1) reclassify the sale as domestic taxable supply at the statutory 13% VAT rate and claw back any refunded input tax; (2) impose late-payment surcharges and an administrative penalty of one to five times the tax amount evaded; (3) suspend or revoke the exporter's export-tax-rebate qualification for one to several years; (4) downgrade the taxpayer credit rating to Class D and trigger enhanced perpetual audit scrutiny; and (5) transfer the file for criminal prosecution under the crime of fraudulent export-tax-rebate claims where the evaded refund reaches the statutory criminal threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system under PBOC and SAFE, with distinct current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade-related payments, service fees, and after-tax dividend remittances, are generally freely convertible at onshore market rates once a WFOE presents authentic underlying documents such as contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax payment certificates. Banks conduct authenticity verification and settlement directly without requiring separate SAFE approval for most current account items. Capital account flows, however, remain strictly controlled. FDI capital injection, equity transfer proceeds, cross-border loans, liquidation proceeds, and foreign debt require SAFE registration, a dedicated capital account, and post-event supervision. Export proceeds are no longer subject to a mandatory surrender ratio; an enterprise may retain up to 100 percent of export receipts in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB at any designated bank. Profit remittance by a WFOE is treated as a current account dividend, but the paying bank requires audited financial statements, resolution of the board or shareholders, and proof of corporate income tax settlement and statutory reserve allocation. Practical hurdles for a WFOE include non-resident signature and identity verification, negative-list industry checks, bank-level sanctions screening, and the requirement that capital account RMB be used only for the registered business scope. Any change in use requires supplementary registration or SAFE filing, and round-tripping indicators such as same-day back-to-back transfers or use of conduit jurisdictions will trigger enhanced due diligence. Banks maintain strict record-keeping, and suspicious transactions are reported to the China Anti-Money Laundering Monitoring and Analysis Center. Overall, the current account is highly convertible for legitimate trade and services, while capital account transactions are tightly registered but workable through standard banking channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated into the exporter's domestic foreign exchange settlement account and reconciled through the SAFE goods trade verification system within 180 calendar days of the relevant customs declaration date. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100 percent of export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or convert all or part into RMB through a designated domestic bank. The bank processes the conversion and simultaneously performs authenticity checks against the export declaration, invoice, and transport documents. Failure to complete receipt verification on time exposes the company to SAFE on-site inspection, downgraded classification, and possible suspension of current account FX purchase and payment facilities.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI solutions are not legally viable as a primary onshore treasury or clearing rail for a mainland WFOE. RMB clearing, trade verification, VAT issuance, customs reconciliation, FDI capital injection, and dividend remittance must pass through a licensed domestic bank in China. Offshore EMIs such as foreign digital wallets or non-bank payment platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to maintain onshore RMB settlement accounts or to act as the registered bank for foreign exchange control purposes. They may only be used as an ancillary collection account outside China for foreign trade proceeds before being remitted into the WFOE domestic account, but using an EMI to bypass SAFE reporting or to route trade payments in a way that obscures the underlying transaction is a serious compliance violation. Therefore, the treasury structure should rely exclusively on tier-1 domestic commercial banks for account operation and cross-border clearing, with any offshore EMI limited to non-mainland receivable collection and fully mirrored in the accounting records.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration either through the bank or directly with SAFE and obtain the FDI foreign exchange business registration code or certificate. The WFOE then opens a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a designated domestic bank. The parent remits registered capital via SWIFT MT103 from an account held in the true parent entity name; the payment purpose field should reference the FDI registration number and the nature of funds as foreign direct investment capital injection. The WFOE should not receive registered capital into an ordinary current account. After receipt, the bank records the inflow in the SAFE capital project information system. The WFOE may then settle the foreign currency into RMB and transfer it from the capital account to the RMB basic operational account only under approved destination categories restricted to the registered business scope. A licensed accountant should issue a capital verification report after the funds are physically received. All RMB proceeds used for rent, payroll, inventory, and third-party payments must be supported with the corresponding contracts, invoices, and expense evidence.For outbound disbursements related to purchases of diagnostic reagents, use a dedicated foreign currency current account and route payment instructions through SWIFT MT103 for standard telegraphic transfers. For large or multi-shipment reagent supply contracts, a commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600 and transmitted via SWIFT MT700 is appropriate; for trusted counterparties with established trade history, documentary collections such as documents against payment and documents against acceptance may be used, but only through the WFOE bank and with attached negotiable transport documents and commercial invoices. For RMB-denominated cross-border settlements, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System directly or SWIFT MT103 routed through a CIPS participant bank. All outbound payments must be supported by contracts, commercial invoices, customs declarations, tax or foreign exchange purpose codes, and logistics documentation. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Universal statutory audit under PRC Company Law: every limited liability company, including a WFOE, must produce annual financial statements and have them audited by a lawfully established PRC accounting firm before submission to the shareholders for approval. There is no balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount de-minimis exemption: even a zero-revenue WFOE with one shareholder and no employees is required to undergo an annual independent CPA audit. A quantitative audit trigger also exists for cross-border related-party transactions under the annual CIT transfer-pricing reporting forms, and if the entity applies for an import/export license or bank credit line, lenders and regulators commonly require audited financial statements with a clean opinion.
