Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic compliance and filing through the official Electronic Tax Bureau portal (e-Tax Bureau) accessible at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with regional administration performed by the local state tax bureau of the State Taxation Administration in the WFOE's registered jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Administration for Market Regulation issues the WFOE an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which the State Taxation Administration recognizes as the sole taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate taxpayers, including for VAT purposes; no separate VAT identification number is issued. The market regulator transmits the incorporation data to the tax authority through the government data-sharing platform, after which the legal representative, financial officer and authorized tax agent must complete the initial taxpayer information collection and confirm tax category registration for corporate income tax, VAT and surtaxes through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that needs a PRC TIN must file a non-resident enterprise tax registration or record-filing with the competent tax authority of the place where the withholding agent or the source income is located, submitting its certificate of incorporation, authorization of a tax agent or contact person, and proof of foreign tax residence; if the foreign enterprise is only a shareholder/parent of the WFOE and has no PRC VAT registration, PRC-source taxes are generally settled through withholding by the domestic payer rather than through a full VAT registration. The USCC must be quoted on all VAT invoices, customs export declarations and tax filings.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover (annual taxable sales) is the unified threshold for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; any taxpayer with annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 must register as a VAT general taxpayer, while taxpayers below that threshold are generally treated as small-scale VAT taxpayers unless voluntary general taxpayer registration is elected with the approval of the tax authority and adequate internal accounting controls are demonstrated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods from the PRC is eligible for the export zero-rating/exemption-and-refund regime. The WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer; the SSD and CPU chip goods must actually leave the PRC customs territory, must be consigned to the overseas buyer, must be sold and invoiced as an export transaction, and the export proceeds must be collected and repatriated through the bank within the applicable foreign exchange administration period. The export zero-rating claim requires a valid customs export declaration, transport documentation, commercial documentation and input VAT special invoices for the purchased goods, and the corresponding export VAT refund/zero-rating declaration must be submitted through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal. Qualifying cross-border digital services may also be zero-rated where the recipient is located outside the PRC and the service is consumed outside the PRC, but this payload is principally a physical goods wholesale export operation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) validated by PRC Customs
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill / Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the WFOE for the export sale
  • Sales contract or purchase order with the overseas customer
  • Input VAT special invoice and purchase documentation supporting refundable input VAT on the exported goods
  • Bank credit advice or foreign exchange collection voucher for export proceeds matched to the SAFE goods trade foreign exchange monitoring system
  • Export VAT refund/zero-rating declaration and export tax refund application data submitted through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices exclusively through the State Taxation Administration's VAT Invoice Management System and the national digitalized electronic invoice platform. Invoice data must be uploaded to the tax authority's central database in near real time at issuance; the invoice number, taxpayer identifiers and tax-control signatures are verified through the Golden Tax System (Phase IV) anti-fraud data environment. Fully digitalized electronic invoices carry a mandatory anti-forgery digital stamp and cryptographic control, and high-volume taxpayers may use the official API-docking invoicing interface in the Electronic Tax Bureau portal. Paper VAT special invoices, if used, must be acquired through the tax authority and recorded using approved tax-control devices, with the invoice issue records reconciled to the monthly VAT return.

The tax administration and PRC Customs jointly cross-validate every export zero-rating/rebate claim against the customs export declaration, transport documents, input VAT invoices, bank foreign exchange collection records and the SAFE goods trade monitoring data transmitted by banks. If the export is unverified, misdeclared, abandoned, re-imported in the same condition without authorization, or the export proceeds are not collected and repatriated within the applicable deadline, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rate treatment, recompute output VAT, claw back refunded input VAT and impose statutory late fees. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, fraudulent export tax refund claims through false export declarations or other deceptive methods are subject to recovery of the defrauded refund and an administrative fine of 100% to 500% of the refunded amount, plus suspension of export tax refund handling rights for a specified period; serious cases constitute the crime of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law and may be punished by imprisonment up to life, fines and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB regime with current account convertibility for bona fide trade and service transactions, but retains strict capital account controls. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise may receive foreign currency registered capital and, after completing FDI registration with SAFE and the designated bank's authenticity review, convert the proceeds into RMB for approved business purposes. The conversion is not freewheeling; each capital account settlement must be supported by a genuine expenditure purpose and the funds must be used within the approved scope. Profit remittance to the offshore parent is permissible after the annual statutory audit and tax clearance, but requires demonstration of cumulative paid-in capital, verified retained earnings, and compliance with dividend withholding tax (currently up to 10% unless an applicable double tax treaty provides a lower rate). Repatriation of liquidation proceeds also requires SAFE closure formalities and bank verification. Consequently, the regime is best described as current account open, capital account conditionally open, with administrative approvals, document-heavy bank reviews, and stringent purpose-code matching. Operators must keep comprehensive records including contracts, customs declarations, tax payment certificates and board resolutions, and must allow sufficient lead time for multi-tier compliance reviews before any significant cross-border transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE goods trade framework, there is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds; a compliant exporter may retain foreign currency in a designated foreign exchange settlement account or convert it into RMB at market rates. The statutory surrender-like obligation is replaced by repatriation: export receipts arising from goods trade must be remitted back to China through designated banks generally within 180 days of the export customs declaration, unless an approved offshore account arrangement or centralized FX pooling facility has been authorised. The exporter must reconcile the receipt against the customs declaration and provide underlying transaction documentation on the bank's request; any overdue receipt is subject to the trade foreign exchange monitoring system's automatic alert and can trigger compliance investigation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neo-banks and non-bank payment institutions are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open onshore RMB accounts or to intermediate SAFE-registered foreign direct investment flows on behalf of a mainland WFOE. Their cards or multi-currency wallets cannot satisfy statutory capital verification, tax clearance, or customs-linked trade settlement requirements in China. They may be considered only for ancillary cross-border expenses outside China, but never for the WFOE's statutory capital injection, dividend repatriation, or Chinese customs-filed merchandise trade settlements. Engagement with any unlicensed EMI for these flows is a compliance threshold and will be rejected by the domestic custodian banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore parent must remit by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign exchange capital account with the chosen tier-one domestic bank, clearly stating the SAFE inward direct investment purpose code registered at the time of the FDI declaration, the remitter's name, and the remark of registered capital contribution. After conversion, a Chinese certified public accountant must issue an independent capital verification report for subsequent use in business licensing, tax filings and SAFE/statistics bureau updates. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as global neo-banks and non-bank payment institutions are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to open onshore RMB accounts or to intermediate SAFE-registered foreign direct investment flows on behalf of a mainland WFOE. Their cards or multi-currency wallets cannot satisfy statutory capital verification, tax clearance, or customs-linked trade settlement requirements in China. They may be considered only for ancillary cross-border expenses outside China, but never for the WFOE's statutory capital injection, dividend repatriation, or Chinese customs-filed merchandise trade settlements. Engagement with any unlicensed EMI for these flows is a compliance threshold and will be rejected by the domestic custodian banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB. Local File threshold: related-party tangible property purchase or sale transactions above RMB 200 million annually; intangible property transfers above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. Master File threshold: MNE group with annual consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion engaged in related-party activities involving the Chinese entity.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations in the manner of OFCs, but the tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine through the EIT Law General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 47) and the special anti-abuse rules for treaty benefits. Foreign investors cannot strip wholesale profits from the Chinese WFOE merely by inserting an offshore intermediary with no employees, no premises, no decision-making authority, and no risk-bearing capacity. Core Income-Generating Activities for semiconductor wholesale trading include inventory management, customer credit approval, logistics and inspection oversight, price negotiation, storage, product quality conformity, and risk assumption; if these are actually performed in mainland China, the related-party compensation and profit allocation must reflect those functions, assets, and risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Reporting and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law, tax evasion leads to recovery of underpaid tax, a daily late payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, a taxpayer who evades a large amount of tax may be imprisoned and criminally fined; extremely large evasion may attract a sentence of 3 to 7 years with confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and actual controllers may have the corporate veil pierced under Article 23 of the PRC Company Law if they confuse the WFOE's assets with their personal assets, defraud creditors, or abuse limited liability. Individual civil restitution may cover the full underpaid tax, interest, and penalties. Tax authorities may also impose exit restrictions, commonly called blacklisting, on the legal representative to prevent travel outside China until tax liabilities are settled.
Shell Company Structure and Abuse of Treaty/GAAR Substance Rules

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 and 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may recharacterize the transaction, impute taxable income to the Chinese WFOE, apply the 25% standard rate on adjusted profit, impose underpayment interest, and deny treaty benefits for a non-beneficial owner. The Special Tax Adjustment rules allow fines and interest for contemporaneous documentation failures. Criminal fraudulent arrangements may be prosecuted as tax evasion or money laundering.

Director personal liability: The personal assets of directors may be reached in civil restitution for losses caused by intentional anti-avoidance arrangements, particularly where the director signed false loan agreements, false distributor agreements, or false board resolutions. Exit bans and blacklisting apply to the legal representative if the company attempts to relocate profits and strip assets before paying tax. Criminal prosecution may be initiated for participation in a fraud ring designed to evade CIT and withholding tax.
AML and Foreign Exchange Violations in Cross-Border Chip Payments

Statutory sanction: Foreign exchange evasion may be punished under the PRC Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration with fines up to 100% of the illegal foreign exchange amount, confiscation of unlawful proceeds, and suspension or revocation of the enterprise's foreign exchange business qualification. The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines on institutions and responsible persons for failing to perform beneficial ownership identification, large-transaction reporting, or suspicious transaction reporting duties; criminal money laundering under Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law carries imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers face personal fines, disqualification from serving as directors, and civil asset restitution if they knowingly executed or directed sham trade documentation to move funds out of China. Travel bans and blacklisting by the tax and foreign exchange authorities are available against the legal representative and beneficial owners. Criminal conviction for laundering or illegal foreign exchange business can lead to substantial terms of imprisonment and confiscation of personal property.
