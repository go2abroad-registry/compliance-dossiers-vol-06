Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), including the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau online filing portal accessible through the official STA e-Tax Bureau system at www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

The wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) is a Chinese resident limited liability company; after incorporation, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues a Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the legal Taxpayer Identification Number for all PRC tax purposes. The foreign shareholders then complete tax registration with the local competent STA office within 30 calendar days of the Business License date through the Electronic Tax Bureau. The tax registration activates the WFOE's VAT and Corporate Income Tax files, requires selection of VAT taxpayer status (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), registration of bank settlement accounts, and, where the WFOE will export, separate export tax rebate qualification filing. The USCC is used for VAT, Corporate Income Tax, customs, and export refund filings; no separate foreign non-resident TIN is used for the Chinese entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim zero-rated VAT for goods exported from PRC customs territory where the buyer is located outside China, the goods physically leave China under the WFOE's export customs declaration, the sale is documented as an export sale, and the foreign currency export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC bank account within the prescribed foreign exchange collection period. For this passive electronic components distribution scope, zero-rating applies to the exported MLCCs and resistors; the WFOE is then entitled to VAT input credit and refund for the related purchases.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Customs Export Goods Declaration) issued through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical export from Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Export Sales Contract with the overseas buyer
  • Export sales invoice (export commercial invoice) matching the customs declaration
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate/Advice and SAFE International Balance of Payments Receipt Declaration
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Record from the SAFE Trade in Goods Monitoring System

China mandates VAT invoice issuance through the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoicing systems, including tax-control equipment and the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform. Electronic invoices are transmitted to and cleared by the STA in near real time; special VAT invoices are subject to the national invoice authentication and clearing process before input VAT credit may be claimed. Anti-fraud controls include the STA Golden Tax system, unique nationwide invoice numbers, tax-control codes, two-dimensional anti-counterfeit stamping, and data cross-matching between invoices, customs exit records, and foreign exchange receipts.

The STA and General Administration of Customs cross-match export declarations, electronic invoices, and SAFE foreign exchange receipt records. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export or without verified foreign exchange repatriation, the STA will disallow the zero-rating, reverse the input VAT refund, assess tax with daily late-payment surcharge (0.05% of the overdue tax per day), and impose penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid or refunded tax. Fraudulent VAT refund claims also expose the WFOE and responsible officers to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including imprisonment and criminal fines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains de facto current-account convertibility for trade and services, but its capital account remains regulated. The WFOE may freely settle lawful receipts from export sales of passive electronic components into RMB through a designated-bank system, provided the underlying trade is supported by customs declarations, invoices, and logistics records. However, capital-account transactions, particularly inbound FDI equity injection, foreign debt, and profit repatriation, require SAFE registration and must route through a licensed bank's capital account waterfall. Registered capital is admitted on a payment or voluntary settlement basis; the RMB generated by foreign-currency capital can only be used for bona fide operating expenditures, not for equity investment or illegal securities purchases. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is permitted once the WFOE produces audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, board profit-distribution resolution, and a SAFE-intended FX purchase record. Dividends are generally unrestricted on a retrospective basis, but the bank will still screen the shareholder for PEP status and sanctions exposure. Ceaseless verification, rather than quota rationing, is the true operative constraint. The overall assessment is a semi-liberalized trade-friendly current-account regime with administratively heavy, but navigable, capital-account controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to a mainland designated-bank foreign-currency settlement account; the receipt must be booked and reported to SAFE through the bank within 30 days of the funds being credited to the exporter. No compulsory conversion ratio applies - China has abolished forced surrender. A WFOE may retain export proceeds in foreign currency up to its verified current-account scale or settle them into RMB at its discretion. If export proceeds remain outstanding for more than 90 days after customs declaration, the forward collection arrangement must be separately reported to the local SAFE branch as a trade credit; pre-import payments require similar reporting. The bank monitors the enterprise's total goods-trade receipts and payments against customs data; any unexplained timing mismatch can trigger a downgrade from Category A to Category B or C verification status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low for primary treasury clearing. Mainland China does not recognise offshore fintech EMIs as authorised foreign-exchange banks; only licensed domestic designated banks and SAFE-approved non-bank payment institutions may conduct cross-border settlement and RMB conversion. An overseas EMI can be used administratively as an upstream collection account for a foreign marketplace or receivables clearing vehicle, but all funds must be swept into the WFOE's mainland designated-bank account for SAFE reporting. If the EMI does not hold a PBOC cross-border RMB or foreign-exchange payment licence, it cannot lawfully issue a Chinese bank account number or settle export proceeds in the WFOE's name. Therefore, the WFOE should treat EMIs as a receivable aggregation tool, not as a treasury account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be initiated by the exact offshore shareholder named in the WFOE approval; the SWIFT cover or remittance should use SAFE's capital-project FDI purpose code for equity capital and should quote the SAFE capital-project information system confirmation code in the wire instructions. The funds may be received only in the WFOE's designated foreign-currency capital account at a Chinese licensed bank. After receipt, the bank determines availability, a certified public accountant verifies the paid-in capital, a capital verification report is filed with SAMR, and the RMB proceeds must be used strictly for operating purposes through payment-based settlement or a legally approved voluntary settlement account.For paying overseas suppliers in passive electronic component trades, the WFOE may use a SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments after matching invoices with customs declarations; an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by its Chinese bank where banks examine transport documents and commercial invoices; or documentary collections on D/P or D/A basis through the remitting bank. Every outward payment must be supported in the SAFE trade verification system by the import or export customs data, contract, invoice, packing list and, where applicable, a bill of lading or air waybill. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory for a China resident enterprise with annual related-party tangible-asset (purchase or sales) transactions exceeding RMB 200 million; the threshold is RMB 100 million for transfers or use of intangible property and financial asset transfers and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File documentation is required when the MNE group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion, and CbC reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China's general anti-avoidance rule, EIT Law Article 47 and STA Announcement 2/2016, empowers the tax authority to disregard any arrangement lacking reasonable commercial purpose and economic substance. For a WFOE distributing excess electronic inventory, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include procurement negotiation, inventory holding and warehouse management, pricing, order fulfillment, logistics, credit and risk control. If all CIGA are performed by the Hong Kong or offshore parent and the WFOE is only a passive holding of electronic records, the tax authority can attribute the income to the company where the functions are actually performed, deny DTA benefits, reclassify the WFOE as a low-risk distributor under TNMM, and impose a routine margin plus 25% EIT. The economic substance doctrine also applies to treaty relief: a dividends/royalties recipient must have offices, employees and real decision-making in its residence state, otherwise the DTA claim is treated as treaty shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation on Passive Electronic Inventory

Statutory sanction: The tax authority applies EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 114 to adjust the WFOE's taxable income to an arm's-length distribution margin, recovers EIT at 25% on the adjusted profit, adds a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and imposes a 50% to 500% penalty under Tax Collection Law Article 63 if a false related-party transaction is proved. Repeated serious adjustments may trigger a general anti-avoidance reassessment under Article 47 and retroactive reclassification of the WFOE as a commissionaire.

Director personal liability: Founders and directors who knowingly approve inflated related-party invoices and sign false annual EIT returns risk personal civil liability for restitution to the company and tax authority under Company Law and piercing-of-the-corporate-veil doctrine. The legal representative is subject to exit control and blacklisting for overdue taxes; criminal conviction under Criminal Law Article 201 can produce a fine, asset confiscation and imprisonment up to 7 years, plus disqualification from acting as a director in China.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion Through Disguised Distributions / Cross-Border Service Fees

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Law Article 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold is fined 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax, the tax authority recovers the full WHT from the foreign shareholder, and the fee itself is disallowed as a deductible expense under EIT Law Article 8. Foreign-exchange and falsified service fee transactions also violate China's foreign exchange controls and can be recharacterized as a dividend distribution with retroactive WHT, daily surcharge and 50%-500% penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the WFOE to issue false service invoices and remittance instructions become personally accountable for the unpaid withholding if the WFOE's assets are depleted by the disguised distribution. They can be added to the tax blacklist, banned from leaving China, and held jointly and severally liable for damages. If the concealment is prosecuted as tax evasion or money laundering, the responsible director faces up to 7 years imprisonment for tax evasion and up to 10 years for money laundering, with forfeiture of personal assets.
Shell Company / Zero-Economic-Substance Structure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 25, 47 and 120 allow the tax authority to re-assess income under substance-over-form, deny treaty benefits, and treat the shell as a permanent establishment or deemed resident of the owner. It can recover 25% EIT on the imputed income, impose statutory 10% WHT on deemed dividends, and add a 50%-500% penalty for willful avoidance. A false annual report filed on behalf of the shell is itself a tax fraud under Tax Collection Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: De facto directors and the nominee legal representative face civil recovery of diverted corporate funds, personal joint and several liability for taxes where the veil is pierced, asset freezing and travel bans. In egregious cases involving use of offshore accounts to hide income, Criminal Law Article 191 (money laundering) and Article 201 (tax evasion) apply, with maximum custodial sentences of 7-10 years, confiscation of property, and a permanent entry/exit ban from mainland China.
