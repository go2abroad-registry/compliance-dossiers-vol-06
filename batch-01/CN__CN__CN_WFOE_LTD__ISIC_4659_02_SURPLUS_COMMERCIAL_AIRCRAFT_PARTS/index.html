Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed for a WFOE engaged in wholesale distribution of certified civil aircraft parts. Under the 2023 Company Law, shareholders must pay the full subscribed capital within five years from incorporation unless the Articles prescribe a shorter period. No blocked escrow account rule or pre-incorporation paid-up deposit is required at the national level; the foreign investor must remit contributed capital only through a licensed Chinese bank account in compliance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange foreign direct investment registration procedure.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Acceptance Certificate and Occupancy Fire Permit from the local Fire and Rescue Section; civil defense and structural load inspection certificate for the warehouse; occupational health and safety notification and emergency response plan approval from the local Emergency Management Bureau. All physical premises must pass fire safety inspection before any surplus commercial aircraft parts are received, stored or distributed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the local competent tax authority at the WFOE's registered address and the nationwide 'Electronic Tax Bureau' (China Tax e-Service) electronic tax portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor obtains approval/registration from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: Under the integrated one-form one-window registration reform, tax registration is completed automatically with the STA when the Business License is issued, and the USCC becomes the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for both Corporate Income Tax and VAT. Step 3: The enterprise logs into the Electronic Tax Bureau, verifies its basic registration data, electronic invoice quota, and tax type (VAT general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer). Step 4: For non-resident investors without a permanent establishment, the WFOE acts as the withholding agent for Chinese-source income tax and ensures the non-resident's identifier is recorded in the tax registration. Step 5: The enterprise completes VAT general taxpayer registration if it intends to claim export VAT refunds; compulsory general taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds the statutory threshold.

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system does not provide check-the-box or similar flow-through elections for a WFOE LLC. The WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, and no hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; a taxpayer with annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 must register as a VAT general taxpayer, while smaller taxpayers may voluntarily register if they meet accounting requirements. A newly formed WFOE is tax-registered at incorporation regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of surplus commercial aircraft parts is eligible for zero-rating and input VAT refund under the PRC export tax rebate regime if the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically declared for export and cleared by PRC Customs, the goods are not restricted or prohibited for export, the export proceeds are received from an overseas buyer and repatriated to China within the statutory verification period, and the purchase-side VAT special invoices are valid and verified. Zero-rating is substantiated by the customs export declaration data linked to the VAT return.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form for Goods (customs-verified exit record and export customs clearance endorsement)
  • Original Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List matching the export contract, goods description, quantity, and value
  • Bill of Lading (or original Air Waybill) evidencing the physical export movement of the aircraft parts out of PRC territory
  • Export Proceeds Collection record / bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Receipt issued by a SAFE-designated bank proving repatriation and settlement of the export foreign exchange income
  • Original VAT Special Invoice(s) for the purchase of the aircraft parts to support input VAT credit offset
  • Export Sales Contract and related commercial or technical documents (including airworthiness or surplus condition certificates) required to verify the transaction for customs and tax audit purposes

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and mandates the issuance of Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (XML digital invoices) through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA. The platform validates the buyer's Unified Social Credit Code, applies an anti-fraud digital stamp, and records every invoice in the National VAT Invoice Management System before delivery. Taxpayers must archive the XML source files and are prohibited from tampering with e-invoice data.

Export tax refund claims are subject to STA risk-control review and post-claim audits; files must be retained for at least 10 years. If the exported goods are not actually exported, the export declaration is fraudulent, or the proceeds are not repatriated within the deadline, the tax authority will retroactively claw back the refunded VAT, impose a late-payment surcharge, and may impose an administrative fine of 50% to 5 times the amount fraudulently refunded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal liability for fraudulent export VAT refunds may arise under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties including imprisonment, fine, and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account; current account transactions enjoy free convertibility under a verified documentary framework. Foreign-funded enterprises (WFOEs) may convert foreign currency registered capital into RMB for legitimate operating purposes after completing FDI Foreign Exchange Registration with SAFE. Capital injections must be booked through a domestic bank's Capital Account (Capital Verification) mechanism, and the use of RMB converted capital is subject to the Foreign Investment Access Negative List and thin-capitalization scrutiny. Profit remittance is permitted upon submission of audited financial statements, tax completion certificates, and an FX payment application; dividends remitted abroad are treated as current account outflows and are generally remittable without prior SAFE approval, but the remitting bank must verify underlying authenticity. Surplus commercial aircraft parts trading proceeds need to be supported by trade documents, invoices, customs declarations, and, where applicable, export control licenses, to avoid being classified as suspicious capital flows. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds; however, all export proceeds must be repatriated to the domestic banking system, and the exporter has the option to settle into RMB or retain the foreign currency in a legally approved settlement account. The system remains a managed float with a daily central parity rate; standard onshore hedging instruments are permissible for genuine trade-related exposure, but speculative and offshore NDF positions are tightly restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade administration rules, export foreign currency receipts for the WFOE must be repatriated to a domestic banking channel within the statutory trade recovery period, generally 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, or as extended under an approved trade credit facilities code. No standing mandatory conversion ratio applies; the exporter may freely elect to settle all or a portion into RMB or retain the foreign funds in an approved Foreign Exchange Settlement Account, subject to the declared trade receipts and supporting customs/invoice/shipping evidence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low legal and operational viability for onshore WFOE treasury services. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to open Chinese domestic collection and settlement accounts for a locally registered WFOE. Their cross-border rail support may supplement international receivable collection, but they cannot replace the mandatory domestic clearing through China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or cross-border RMB via CIPS; any attempt to route restricted FDI capital flows or trade settlements through an unlicensed EMI could trigger SAFE violations and bank de-risking. The recommended architecture is to use a licensed Chinese Tier-1 commercial bank as the primary settlement bank, with EMIs only as ancillary receivable visibility tools, and only where the EMI has a Chinese-track partnership with a PBOC-licensed clearing bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity: non-resident shareholders layered through offshore Cayman/Hong Kong holding vehicles with unidentified ultimate individuals.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity: non-resident shareholders layered through offshore Cayman/Hong Kong holding vehicles with unidentified ultimate individuals.

Defense evidence: Provide notarized corporate register extracts for every entity in the ownership chain; a signed beneficial ownership declaration; a group corporate structure chart; certified passport copies of all direct and ultimate shareholders; an external legal opinion confirming ultimate beneficial owner identification; and a sworn statement confirming no nominee shareholdings.
Sanctions and export-control exposure on surplus commercial aircraft parts, including the possibility that items may fall under dual-use export control classifications or US ITAR/ECCN regimes.

Root cause: Sanctions and export-control exposure on surplus commercial aircraft parts, including the possibility that items may fall under dual-use export control classifications or US ITAR/ECCN regimes.

Defense evidence: Submit an export control classification memorandum distinguishing civil aircraft parts from dual-use and defense articles; an end-user certificate and end-use undertaking from overseas buyers; the ECCN or EAR99 classification from a licensed customs broker; any applicable China customs or Ministry of Commerce license responses; and a compliance attestation signed by the WFOE responsible officer.
Trade-based money laundering red flags: mismatched invoice unit pricing, abnormal surplus aviation value discounting, and discrepancies among bill of lading weight, container count, and declared customs value.

Root cause: Trade-based money laundering red flags: mismatched invoice unit pricing, abnormal surplus aviation value discounting, and discrepancies among bill of lading weight, container count, and declared customs value.

Lack of physical substance: the WFOE shows a shell-like profile with no local employee payroll, office lease, or operational activity consistent with the declared aircraft-parts trading business.

Root cause: Lack of physical substance: the WFOE shows a shell-like profile with no local employee payroll, office lease, or operational activity consistent with the declared aircraft-parts trading business.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI capital injections must be routed through SWIFT MT103 wires using the SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment, quoting the registered WFOE name and capital verification project reference. Funds must be received into a designated Capital Account before any conversion to RMB; the bank will execute the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration with SAFE, and a certified public accountant performs capital verification after receipt and conversion. Commingling capital injection proceeds with current account trade receipts is prohibited; the WFOE must maintain separate ledger in accordance with SAFE capital account controls and the Foreign Investment Negative List.Outbound disbursements for surplus aircraft parts should use SWIFT MT103 for high-volume commercial settlements; commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) via the issuing and confirming banks for large durable aircraft part transactions; and documentary collections (documents against payment or documents against acceptance) for medium-value open-account trades. All disbursements must reference the underlying export/import customs declaration and commercial documents to satisfy SAFE authenticity checks; payment instructions must cite the appropriate trade purpose code, such as goods import (101) or goods export adjustment, and must be supported by the original invoice, bill of lading or air waybill, and packing list.Onshore deliverable FX forwards with maturities up to 12 months for genuine trade and investment exposureOnshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps permitted only for hedges with a real trade or FDI backingStructured RMB options on current-account settlements for hedging spot/forward volatilityNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for direct corporate hedging without a SAFE-qualified pilot approval; access is effectively restricted to offshore CNH markets, and the onshore WFOE should instead rely on deliverable forwards through a licensed Chinese bank to avoid regulatory breach

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Taxpayer identification, legal name, registration number, registered address, registered capital, industry classification, permit data for distribution of certified civil aircraft rotables and avionics surplus, and resident enterprise status declaration. The WFOE must declare itself as a PRC resident enterprise, even though its shareholder is non-resident. The resident label triggers worldwide income taxation at 25% and requires attachment of the business license and any aviation parts distribution licenses. If the non-resident parent has a place of management, branch or dependent agent in China, that existence must be separately disclosed to avoid an undeclared permanent establishment.
Box 2 Accounting standards, fiscal year period, functional currency and foreign currency conversion method used in preparing the tax return. Books must be maintained under PRC Accounting Standards with RMB as the functional currency. Foreign currency export receipts from surplus aircraft parts sales, intercompany loans and royalties are translated at the spot exchange rate adopted by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Retain airway bills, customs declarations, import/export licenses and airworthiness certificates for every foreign-currency transaction.
Box 3 Total revenue from sales of surplus commercial aircraft rotables and avionics, net of sales discounts and returns, plus other operating income, investment income and exchange gains or losses. The WFOE as a Chinese resident must report all global revenue, including export sales to non-resident customers. Export sales of aircraft parts may benefit from VAT zero-rating, but the income remains Chinese-source CIT income because the seller is a Chinese resident. Do not divert sales revenue to an offshore related company without economic substance; the full sales margin should reflect the WFOE's procurement, inventory, pricing, credit and logistics functions.
Box 4 Cost of sales, including purchase price of surplus aircraft parts, import customs duty, freight, insurance, inspection, repair, certification, warehousing and logistics costs. For purchases from non-resident suppliers, verify that customs valuation and invoice price are consistent and that import VAT is not duplicated as a deductible expense. If the supplier is a related party, the intercompany purchase price of surplus aircraft parts must be benchmarked under the arm's length principle, preferably using the Comparable Uncontrolled Price method where third-party catalogue prices for the same certified used aviation parts exist. An inadequate markup for a distribution WFOE is a primary audit target.
Box 5 Operating expenses: staff salaries, rent, professional fees, insurance, marketing, warranty, business entertainment and general administrative expenses. Management, technical, marketing, or maintenance service fees paid to a non-resident related party are deductible only if the service was actually rendered, benefits the WFOE, is priced at arm's length, and the applicable withholding tax is paid. Business entertainment is subject to a 60% deduction limitation capped at 0.5% of annual revenue. Unsupported or non-substantive fees paid to a foreign parent will be added back and may trigger a cross-border service fee TP audit.
Box 6 Related-party transaction disclosure schedule: purchases, sales, services, interest, royalties, guarantees, intercompany asset transfers and financing transactions with Chinese and non-resident related parties. The annual return must include the Statutory Related Party Transaction Schedule when related-party transaction thresholds are exceeded. For a WFOE distributing aviation spares, disclose all foreign suppliers in the same group, all export customers that are associated enterprises, intercompany pricing methods, benchmarking study summaries and whether a Local File or Master File is required. Incomplete or false disclosure can result in an upward adjustment and penalty even if the transfer price itself is defensible.
Box 7 Reconciliation from accounting profit to taxable income: add-back of non-deductible expenses, adjustment of tax-exempt revenue, limitation of related-party interest and carryforward of tax losses. The general loss carryforward period is five years. A qualifying high-technology or small and medium technology enterprise may carry forward losses for ten years. Repeated annual losses in the WFOE while related offshore companies earn substantial margin on the same aircraft parts will be treated as profit shifting and may be recharacterized under China's General Anti-Avoidance Rule and economic substance doctrine.
Box 8 Preferential rate claims, R&D super deduction, foreign tax credit, and any reduced tax treaty benefit applicable to the taxpayer. The standard 25% rate applies unless the WFOE qualifies for a statutory preference. No foreign patent box may be used by the Chinese distributor. If the WFOE has a foreign branch or pays tax abroad on foreign-source income, attach the foreign tax credit computation; the credit cannot exceed the China CIT attributable to the same item of income. Treaty rate reductions for dividend, interest and royalties are not claimed in Form A; they are applied separately by the payer in the non-resident withholding regime.
Box 9 Computation of final CIT payable, deduction of quarterly provisional prepayments, and resulting balance due or refund. Form A finalizes the WFOE's own corporate income tax. This box does not include dividend withholding tax on profit repatriation. Before the annual return is closed, verify that all non-resident withholding returns for interest, royalties and technical service fees paid during the year have been separately filed and paid, and that any planned dividend distribution has a funding source of after-tax retained earnings.
