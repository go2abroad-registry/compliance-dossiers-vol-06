Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from mainland China by a registered VAT general taxpayer are zero-rated under the VAT export relief/refund mechanism, provided: (a) the sale is genuinely made to an overseas buyer and title/risk passes offshore; (b) the copper cathodes are declared to China Customs for export and leave the customs territory; (c) the WFOE is registered under the export tax refund/export exemption regime; (d) the export sale proceeds are received through SAFE-regulated banking channels and repatriated within 180 days; and (e) the STA export tax refund audit can match the customs, logistics, invoicing, and foreign-exchange records. Qualifying services exported to non-residents may be zero-rated only if consumed entirely offshore, but for this copper cathode trading WFOE the zero-rating analysis is goods-based.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) cleared by China Customs, identifying the WFOE, the relevant HS code for Grade A refined copper cathodes, and the export date
  • Original Bill of Lading or multimodal transport document evidencing physical shipment from a mainland China port to the overseas buyer
  • Export commercial invoice and the VAT invoice issued for the export sale
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher and state-accepted trade-in-goods income declaration/report from SAFE (the China-recognized equivalent of an inward remittance certificate) proving receipt and settlement of the export proceeds
  • Input VAT special invoices and customs duty/tax payment vouchers for the purchased copper cathodes, required for input VAT apportionment and export refund calculation

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's national VAT invoice management system and are cleared in real time by the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. The STA is implementing the fully digitalized electronic invoice (all-digital e-invoice) regime: invoices carry a unique invoice code/number, are transmitted to the tax authority as structured data, are rendered as OFD files, and include the tax authority's cryptographic digital seal. Once cleared, any alteration invalidates the invoice. Approved taxpayers and software vendors may use the e-Tax platform's service interfaces/APIs for automated issuance and reporting; no non-ST-approved offline invoicing is permitted for general taxpayers.

STA and China Customs perform document-matching audits across the customs declaration, bill of lading, export invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt before granting VAT zero-rate export refunds. Any false, incomplete, or unsubstantiated claim is subject to retrospective clawback of the refunded VAT, daily late-payment surcharge, administrative fines of 0.5 to 5 times the tax amount under the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, SAFE monetary penalties for non-repatriation or illegal foreign-exchange settlement, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital controls, while endorsing substantive convertibility for legitimate current-account trade flows. For a WFOE engaged in physical copper cathode wholesale, payments for exports of goods, import settlement, transportation premiums, insurance, and trade-related commissions are treated as current-account transactions; after documentary verification and bank review, these can be settled in CNY or retained in FX without seeking individual project-level approval. Capital account flows remain heavily regulated: equity capital injections from the offshore parent, shareholder loans registered as foreign debt, dividends and profit remittances, and any cross-border principal repayment require separate SAFE registration, FDI information verification, or debt-registration paperwork. Profit distribution from a WFOE is permissible in CNY-denominated retained earnings converted to FX, but the paying bank must confirm audited financials, prior tax settlement, and compliance with the foreign exchange registration number from the FDI approval. Conversion of USD/CAD or EUR into CNY is straightforward once the beneficial purpose is trade-verified; however, speculative stock, real-estate, and non-business use of capital-account CNY is prohibited. The practical effect for a copper trading company is a fully usable current-account convertibility layer complemented by a tightly supervised, document-intensive capital-account clearance process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China and returned to the bank within 90 days after the customs declaration date of the related export, unless SAFE grants an advance approval for overseas retention. There is no fixed mandatory conversion or surrender ratio; the exporter may keep USD in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert to CNY at a licensed bank. Every conversion or retention must be recorded with a current-account trade purpose code and matched against the export customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and collecting-bank advice to satisfy the export-verification and collection-compliance framework.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Unviable for independent operation. China does not recognize offshore fintech EMIs as banks, licensed deposit-taking institutions, or settlement members within the domestic CNY clearing system. A foreign EMI cannot legally hold the WFOE's settlement accounts, issue a CNY bank account number, or clear cross-border RMB without a domestic banking partner. Foreign digital payment firms may act only as technology orchestration layers or front-end aggregators that route transactions into a licensed domestic bank's custody and clearing rails; all regulatory liability, FX conversion, SAFE reporting, and KYC obligations remain with the licensed bank. Attempting to place the primary clearing relationship with an offshore EMI would violate PBOC payment-regulation requirements and expose the WFOE to frozen funds or administrative sanctions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset and intangible asset transfers; RMB 40 million for all other related-party transactions. Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations arise when the MNE group meets the SAT consolidated revenue threshold of approximately RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not label its framework as ESR or CIGA, but the tax authorities apply substance-over-form, economic substance and beneficial ownership principles under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9. A WFOE physically trading copper cathodes must show core income-generating activities in China: actual control of LME warrants, inventory custody, logistics and delivery risk, commercial negotiation, credit and financial risk management, and strategic sales decisions. A passive invoice-issuing entity without staff, office, bank account control or decision-making will be treated as a shell and may be denied treaty benefits, have withholding restored and have taxable income reassessed on a deemed basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 44 and Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority reassesses taxable income and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax, plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05%. Criminal liability attaches under Criminal Law Article 201 when the evasion exceeds the statutory amount and percentage thresholds.

Director personal liability: Directors and responsible officers who sign false invoices or direct transfer pricing manipulation may have the corporate veil pierced under Company Law Article 23 (2023 Revision) and be held personally and jointly liable. Tax blacklisting and travel bans may be imposed on the legal representative. Criminal penalties include up to 3 years imprisonment for relatively small evasion and 3 to 7 years for huge evasion, with fines and asset confiscation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Related-Party Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may invoke EIT Law Article 44, disregard the taxpayer's pricing and re-determine taxable income using independent comparables. Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 62 permits fines up to RMB 10,000 for missing declarations, while transfer pricing adjustments carry late-payment surcharges; intentional concealment can attract a 50% to 200% penalty.

Director personal liability: If the documentation failure is part of a deliberate scheme to hide related-party payments to foreign shareholders, the responsible chief executive and chief financial officer face personal civil liability, blacklisting, credit restrictions and exit bans. In fraudulent cross-border transactions, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal fines and imprisonment for the responsible persons.
Shell Entity and Treaty Shopping for Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9, the tax authority may disregard the shell, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize payments as taxable dividends or China-source income, and impose 10% WHT plus late-payment interest and penalties of 50% to 500% if the arrangement constitutes tax evasion. Regulatory and anti-money-laundering authorities may also deregister the entity and flag the LME warrant flows.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 23 (2023 Revision) and order directors to satisfy the tax loss from personal assets. Travel bans, property preservation and blacklisting may be imposed on the legal representative. Where treaty abuse crosses into money laundering or fraudulent cross-border financial schemes, criminal liability can extend to 7 to 10 years imprisonment together with fines and asset confiscation.
