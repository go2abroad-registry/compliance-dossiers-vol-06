Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its provincial/municipal tax service branches; online portal: State Taxation Administration Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; electronic invoice issuance and verification through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise as a limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License; the USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the WFOE under the one license, one code system. Step 2: upon issuance of the Business License, SAMR automatically transmits enterprise registration data to the STA, and the STA deems tax registration completed; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods physically shipped out of the Chinese customs territory by a VAT general taxpayer is zero-rated for VAT. For a WFOE engaged in cross-border physical consignment of recycled scrap copper wire, the applicable statutory method is the export tax refund and exemption method for trading enterprises: output VAT on the export sale is exempted and input VAT incurred on the purchase of the scrap copper wire is refunded by the STA, provided that: (1) the WFOE holds export tax refund registration in the e-Tax system; (2) the goods are declared in the e-Tax/customs export declaration system and cleared by China Customs; (3) the goods physically departed from China and were sold to an overseas customer; (4) the export sale is duly invoiced and payment is collected in foreign exchange through banking channels and verified with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); (5) all VAT invoices and input VAT deductions are authentic and traceable; and (6) the recycled scrap copper wire is lawfully exportable under its customs commodity classification and all applicable export licensing, environmental, and recycled-metal control requirements are satisfied. If the WFOE is not a general VAT taxpayer, export zero-rating is unavailable and the export sale would be treated under small-scale taxpayer levy rules.

Since the national rollout of the Full-Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system, all newly established enterprises, including the WFOE, must issue VAT invoices through the official STA Electronic Tax Bureau / National Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each FDEI is generated as an XML data file with a unique invoice number and a tax control number, transmitted in real time to the STA central invoice database, validated and cleared by the tax authority before use. Anti-fraud verification is achieved through automatic data matching of invoice records against VAT returns, customs export declarations, and bank foreign-exchange receipts; there is no physical stamp on FDEI. If traditional paper VAT invoices are used, they must be printed from the VAT invoice system through tax-control equipment and include the anti-counterfeit tax-control code; offline or manual invoice issuance outside STA systems is prohibited and invalid for input VAT crediting and export tax refunds.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs share electronic export, invoicing, and payment data; each export refund return is risk-ranked and may be subject to administrative inspection of the exporter, supplier, transport documents, and customs data. If the zero-rating claim is rejected, the export sale is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and the WFOE must pay 13% output VAT with late-payment surcharge. If a refund was already paid, the STA recovers the refunded amount with statutory late-payment interest; imposes a penalty of 0.5 times to 5 times the amount of tax erroneously refunded; suspends or revokes eligibility for export tax refunds; downgrades the exporter tax credit and risk rating; and in serious fraud cases such as forged invoices, false export declarations, or concealed input VAT refers the matter for criminal liability for defrauding export tax refunds under the Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment, detention, fines, and forfeiture of property for especially large cases. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline is also policed: failure to collect or verify export proceeds by the deadline causes the zero-rating to be reversed and VAT to be collected unless a genuine default is waived by the tax and SAFE authorities.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China runs a managed floating exchange rate regime. The RMB is convertible on current account (goods, services, income) for trade and ordinary business, but capital account transactions remain subject to SAFE approval and monitoring. A WFOE's registered capital receipt is regarded as a capital account inflow and must be booked in a foreign currency capital account at a designated bank; after settlement into RMB, the funds can be used for current-account operational expenses. Profit remittance, however, is treated as a current account item and is permissible provided that the enterprise has audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a board resolution declaring dividends. There is no mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; rather, the exporter must repatriate the foreign exchange income to a domestic account and complete SAFE's collection verification before using the funds. Despite liberalisation, payments to affiliated offshore entities may require commercial background documentation to avoid disguised capital flight. Overall, RMB moves within a daily band against the USD/CFETS basket, and the central bank retains open-market tools to manage excessive speculation, so convertibility is full on trade-related transactions but not absolute capital-account openness.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE monitoring system, export foreign currency receipts from the trade of recycled scrap copper wire must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account in China within 90 days of the customs export declaration date, unless an extension is approved by the local SAFE branch. Once repatriated, the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in its current-account bank account or sell it to a designated bank at the prevailing market rate; there is no mandatory conversion ratio for general exporters. The enterprise must also complete the SAFE export proceeds collection verification and reconciliation process by submitting the relevant customs declarations, bill of lading, and bank credit slips, ensuring that every receipt is matched against an underlying shipment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual related-party tangible property transfer threshold triggers contemporaneous Local File preparation; RMB 1 billion aggregate related-party transaction threshold triggers Master File; RMB 100 million threshold applies to related-party intangible property transfers and related-party financial transactions. Special audit selection may require documentation even below these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime of the UAE type, but the substance-over-form principle, beneficial ownership doctrine, General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, and the special anti-abuse rules under SAT Circulars require that a WFOE engaged in recycled scrap copper trading demonstrate real commercial substance in China. Core Income-Generating Activities include purchase order negotiation, inventory custody, quality inspection, logistics control, invoicing, treasury management, and export decision-making. A WFOE that only acts as a nominal seller while the foreign parent or affiliate performs all substantive functions and assumes all risks will be disregarded for tax purposes; profits will be reallocated to the functional capitalist, and the China-source income may be taxed on the foreign enterprise or exposed to secondary withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law: recapture of unpaid taxes plus surcharge and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Article 201 of the Criminal Law: imprisonment up to 7 years and fines for heavier tax evasion; Article 205 of the Criminal Law imposes penalties for false VAT invoices, up to life imprisonment in severe cases.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow officers who knowingly direct false invoicing or under-declaration can be personally liable for restitution of the fraudulent tax refunds and underpaid taxes. Chinese tax authorities can blacklist the entity, freeze bank accounts, impose exit bans (travel restrictions) on legal representatives, and refer criminal cases resulting in imprisonment. Company Law veil-piercing doctrines allow creditors and the state to recover damages from directors who abuse limited liability to perpetrate fraud.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership standard and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule; the Chinese tax authority will recharacterize the transaction, impose withheld tax at the statutory 10% or higher rate, add daily late surcharges, and apply penalties of 50% to 500%. Under SAT and Supreme Court anti-avoidance guidance, shell companies lacking substance will not be recognized as the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and the shell company who sign false tax-residency declarations or operate without economic substance may be held liable for unpaid tax and penalties. Travel bans (exit restrictions), asset freezing, and blacklisting under the Taxpayer Credit Management System can be imposed. Where the scheme involves fraudulent cross-border transfer of proceeds, Criminal Law liabilities for tax fraud and money laundering may apply, leading to imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets.
AML / Cross-Border Trade-Based Money Laundering and Foreign Exchange Fraud

Statutory sanction: Articles 191 and 312 of the Criminal Law criminalize money laundering; the Anti-Money Laundering Law of China imposes monetary penalties, suspension of operations, and revocation of licenses for covered institutions and beneficial owners. The Foreign Exchange Regulations impose fines of 5% to 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount, with confiscation of transfer proceeds; customs false declarations trigger fines, forfeiture of goods, and revocation of export licenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure trades to avoid AML reporting or foreign exchange controls face personal fines, travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is a mere façade for personal enrichment or unlawful fund transfers; directors' personal bank accounts, immovable assets, and equity interests may be confiscated. Conviction for trade-based money laundering in serious cases can attract imprisonment up to 10 years, plus unlimited fines and forfeiture.
