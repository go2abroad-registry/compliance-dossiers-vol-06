Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to an ordinary WFOE engaged in wholesale trade; the sole shareholder determines the amount in the Articles of Association. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, all subscribed capital must be paid in full within five years after incorporation unless the Articles specify a shorter term. There is no blocked escrow account requirement under general PRC company law, but the capital contribution must be made to a bank capital contribution account in China and must be genuine; no withdrawal is permitted before lawful reduction or liquidation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic tax administration and filing is performed through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform, accessible through the STA official portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor incorporates a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a limited liability company under China's Foreign Investment Law and obtains a Business License from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the Business License contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), an 18-character code that functions as the enterprise Tax Identification Number for CIT, VAT, customs and foreign exchange purposes. Step 2: Within 30 days after issuance of the SAMR Business License, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent STA office through the e-Tax platform, during which the USCC is activated as the national taxpayer identification number and the enterprise tax file is created. Step 3: The WFOE registers for Value-Added Tax, selects its VAT taxpayer status (small-scale taxpayer or general taxpayer), and enrols in the STA VAT invoice issuance system. Step 4: The WFOE enters into the bank-tax electronic payment agreement and opens the designated tax payment account to remit CIT, VAT, local surcharges, stamp duty, and withholding taxes electronically. Step 5: If the foreign corporate shareholder has Chinese-source income without any establishment or place of business in China, the foreign shareholder must separately complete non-resident enterprise tax registration, either directly with the STA or through the WFOE as withholding agent, in order to obtain a non-resident tax registration record for corporate income tax withholding; this non-resident registration is separate from the WFOE's resident enterprise tax identity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration. Any entity that holds a SAMR Business License and performs taxable supplies must complete VAT tax registration through the e-Tax platform even if its turnover is below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered VAT general taxpayer may zero-rate exported goods, including dry bulk granular elemental sulfur under ISIC 4669, when the goods are physically exported from China's customs territory; the WFOE acts as the exporter of record on the customs export declaration; the sale is made to a non-resident customer under an export sales contract or letter of credit; the goods are released by China Customs and carried abroad; and the export proceeds in foreign currency are repatriated to China through the banking system within the prescribed SAFE deadline. The entire transaction is then subject to 0% VAT, with an input VAT credit or refund available for VAT borne on procurement, freight, and port handling charges. Offshore digital services are outside the scope of this trading entity and would require separate qualification under the published zero-rating list for cross-border services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Customs Declaration Form and electronic release record accepted by China Customs)
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading evidencing shipment from a Chinese port to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export sales contract or letter of credit and corresponding commercial invoice
  • Packing list and weight/quality certificate for the dry bulk sulfur cargo, where required by the Letter of Credit or customs inspection
  • Export transaction bank statement and customs verification data filed with the SAFE

All VAT invoices must be issued and transmitted in real time through the STA's tax-control electronic invoice system. The current nationwide regime requires use of the fully digitalized electronic invoice employing a State Taxation Administration-issued digital signature, unique invoice code, and QR anti-counterfeiting stamp, with real-time fiscal clearance by the STA. Large-volume issuers may integrate through STA-certified system-to-system application programming interfaces, but no invoice data may be altered, cached, or re-coded outside the STA-controlled clearing architecture. The Golden Tax Phase IV system cross-matches invoice data with customs export declarations, logistics manifests, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) receipt records to prevent fraudulent VAT credits and export refunds.

Export zero-rating and VAT refund claims are scrutinized by the STA using automated Golden Tax Phase IV matching against customs release records, shipping company manifests, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts. If the STA determines that the export was fictitious, falsely classified, not physically shipped, or that foreign exchange was not repatriated within the statutory deadline, it will deny the zero-rating, retroactively claw back any input VAT credit or refund already paid, reinstate 13% output VAT on the transaction, and impose a daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% on the underpaid tax. The doctrine of tax evasion under the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China permits administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and retrospective reassessment without the ordinary limitation period where fraud or evasion exists. Cases involving forged export declarations, false invoices, or fraudulent export tax refund may be referred to criminal authorities under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with consequences including imprisonment, criminal fines, and confiscation of the proceeds of crime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains full current account convertibility for trade in goods and services, while the capital account remains strictly controlled through SAFE registration, quota limits, and designated bank channels. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in granular sulfur ore trade may lawfully receive US dollars or other foreign currency export proceeds and voluntarily convert such receipts into RMB at licensed banks at the prevailing market spot rate; no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed under current rules. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to a registered domestic foreign currency account within the statutory trade-monitoring window, generally 90 days after the export customs declaration, unless a deferred extension is filed with SAFE. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is permitted after annual statutory audit and corporate income tax settlement, requiring audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring dividends, a tax clearance certificate from the State Taxation Administration, and a valid SAFE foreign exchange registration file. Capital account inflows such as paid-in capital injection, external borrowing, and equity acquisition require dedicated FDI or external-debt accounts, and every disbursement from these accounts must be presented against genuine supporting documents covering the approved business scope. Convertibility is therefore commercially free for trade and dividend flows, prudently monitored, but substantively restricted on speculative capital and non-trade related transfers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, mandatory surrender of export proceeds has been abolished; export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days of the export customs declaration, extendable to 180 days through a deferred-receipt registration, and conversion into RMB is voluntary at the bank's market rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal feasibility is effectively nil. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange and the People's Bank of China authorise only state-licensed commercial banks to conduct foreign exchange settlement, capital account custody, and cross-border trade payment processing for a mainland WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognised as licensed banks in China and are legally barred from servicing the resident corporate treasury of this entity; using one would constitute illegal cross-border payment business, triggering fines, account suspension, and criminal exposure under the Administrative Measures for the Settlement, Sale and Payment of Foreign Exchange and the Payment and Clearing Rules. Mainland-licensed non-bank payment institutions may only support domestic RMB payments or act as a technical conduit paired with a settlement bank for RMB-denominated cross-border trade, but they cannot serve as the primary USD clearing rail. Accordingly, the WFOE must route all FX, capital injection, and trade settlements through tier-1 licensed banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete the Foreign-Invested Enterprise Foreign Exchange Registration at the local SAFE branch or through the designated bank's e-port system, and then open a dedicated FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account. The inbound wire must be originated from the offshore shareholder's own corporate bank account using the official Chinese cross-border payment purpose code for equity capital injection, sent via SWIFT MT103 directly to the WFOE's capital account. The receiving bank will verify the SAFE registration, the signed capital contribution schedule, shareholder incorporation documents, and the economic substance of the FDI. After the remittance lands, the WFOE must obtain a capital verification report from a licensed Chinese CPA and secure a capital account payment confirmation before converting foreign currency to RMB. The converted RMB must be held in a designated RMB capital account and deployed only for approved operating expenses, procurement, rent, wages, and domestic taxes; repayment of shareholder loans or securities investment is prohibited without separate SAFE approval.For international purchases of granular elemental sulfur and associated dry-bulk logistics, the WFOE should maintain a USD-denominated operating account at its designated tier-1 bank. The preferred clearing rail is an irrevocable at-sight commercial letter of credit issued under UCP600, with the Chinese bank sending SWIFT MT700 to the beneficiary's bank and discharging payment via MT103 or MT202 after presentation of compliant shipping documents. For smaller and repeatable brokerage transactions, direct SWIFT MT103 payments with a matching trade purpose code and a signed contract are acceptable, but the bank will systematically cross-check each payment against the import customs declaration in the SAFE trade-monitoring system. Documentary collections (D/P at sight or D/A with tenor) are legally available but carry higher counterparty risk and should be restricted to high-confidence long-standing counterparties. All outbound disbursements must be supported by invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, marine insurance certificates, and quality analysis reports to satisfy anti-trade based money laundering vigilance.Onshore deliverable FX forward contracts with maturity aligned to the underlying export or import trade exposure; the WFOE must remain a SAFE-registered end-user and cannot execute speculative forward books beyond a rolling 90-day underlying look-forward.Cross-currency swaps (CNY/USD or CNY/EUR) strictly used to hedge interest rate and principal cash flows on a SAFE-registered external debt liability or FDI capital structure, subject to genuine underlying exposure and prior SAFE approval.FX options are permitted onshore only under structural zero-cost collar or risk-reversal strategies, with no naked option premium trading and with bank-verified documentary support for the trade flow.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in RMB are not legally accessible to an onshore WFOE; only offshore parent entities may transact NDFs, and the WFOE cannot use them to hedge its domestic CNY exposure because SAFE prohibits onshore corporate access to the offshore RMB NDF market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility is effectively nil. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange and the People's Bank of China authorise only state-licensed commercial banks to conduct foreign exchange settlement, capital account custody, and cross-border trade payment processing for a mainland WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognised as licensed banks in China and are legally barred from servicing the resident corporate treasury of this entity; using one would constitute illegal cross-border payment business, triggering fines, account suspension, and criminal exposure under the Administrative Measures for the Settlement, Sale and Payment of Foreign Exchange and the Payment and Clearing Rules. Mainland-licensed non-bank payment institutions may only support domestic RMB payments or act as a technical conduit paired with a settlement bank for RMB-denominated cross-border trade, but they cannot serve as the primary USD clearing rail. Accordingly, the WFOE must route all FX, capital injection, and trade settlements through tier-1 licensed banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed the following RMB thresholds: tangible property transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party services or agent/brokerage transactions exceeding RMB 20 million. Master File is required where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion and the taxpayer has cross-border related-party transactions; CbC Report is required where group consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: PRC GAAR under CIT Law Article 47 allows recharacterization of arrangements lacking reasonable business purpose. The State Taxation Administration applies BEPS substance principles, requiring the WFOE to perform and control Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, retain local decision-makers for procurement, shipping and brokerage, bear real commercial risk, and maintain payroll, office and systems consistent with its taxable profits. Absence of CIGA and a defensible functions, assets, risks (FAR) analysis will cause profit reattribution under CIT Law Article 41 and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / shell-company profit shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily late interest under Article 32; CIT Law Article 47 GAAR permits income recharacterization and adjustment. A shell with no CIGA cannot claim treaty protection for dividends and may be treated as a controlled foreign company of its shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly design or sign false accounting records become personally jointly liable for the tax debt under the PRC Company Law corporate-veil-piercing doctrine; they may face exit bans and inclusion on tax enforcement blacklists; under PRC Criminal Law Articles 201 and 211, directly responsible persons can be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment of up to seven years plus fines and confiscation of assets for fraudulent tax evasion.
Fraudulent dividend treatment and WHT treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may recharacterize disguised distributions as dividends and recover WHT plus late interest. Under TCAL Article 63, false declarations incur a penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid withholding tax; if the WFOE as withholding agent fails to withhold, Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50%-300% of the un-withheld amount; treaty benefits are denied retroactively and the case may be disclosed to the relevant treaty partner.

Director personal liability: Directors certifying false TRCs or beneficial-owner forms face personal civil liability for restitution of the WHT shortfall; they may be blacklisted as untrustworthy tax debtors and subjected to exit bans; knowingly using forged tax-residency or ownership documents is a serious fraud offense and, where it causes tax loss, directors may be prosecuted under PRC Criminal Law Articles 201 and 211, carrying imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and CIGA misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 44 authorizes the tax administration to assess taxable income by deemed methods; failure to keep and submit documentation is punishable under TCAL Article 62 with fines of RMB 2,000-10,000; serious false documentation causing underpayment is penalized under Article 63 as 50%-500% of the unpaid tax, plus late interest and transfer-pricing adjustment assessments.

Director personal liability: The director who signs contemporaneous documentation and tax returns is the responsible supervisory person; if document fraud negligently or intentionally causes tax loss, the director is civilly jointly liable for the tax and can be placed on the tax blacklist with travel restrictions; if deliberate false documentation amounts to fraudulent tax declaration, criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of up to seven years, and Article 211 also imposes penal liability on directly responsible persons for unit tax crimes.
