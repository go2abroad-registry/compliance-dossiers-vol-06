Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No official statutory minimum registered capital for a general trading WFOE under PRC Company Law; however, the registered capital must reasonably match the proposed business scale and operating expenses, and the WFOE must actually contribute the subscribed capital within five years of incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China. Online statutory portal: the unified E-Tax platform, also known as the Electronic Tax Bureau, operated by the STA for corporate income tax, value-added tax, invoice issuance and export-tax-refund filings.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated WFOE first receives a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation. The USCC constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number. Tax registration is completed automatically through data sharing between SAMR and the competent STA tax service office; if automatic activation does not occur, the WFOE's legal representative must complete supplemental tax registration through the E-Tax portal within 30 calendar days after business license issuance, and then register for the export-tax-refund/exemption record in the E-Tax platform if export zero-rating is intended. For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no permanent establishment in China but with Chinese-source income, the payer must withhold and the non-resident must obtain a special non-resident tax record/registration number through the E-Tax portal or the competent local STA office, using its foreign home-country TIN and a China-specific non-resident taxpayer code; no separate physical tax registration certificate is required for pure cross-border income tax withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not offer check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through elections. The only available elective classification is the VAT general taxpayer election: a taxpayer with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may voluntarily register as a VAT general taxpayer if it can satisfy the accounting and invoice-control requirements; a taxpayer above that threshold is compulsorily a VAT general taxpayer. Corporate income tax remains entity-level at 25% for WFOEs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is mandatory from business establishment with no turnover-based threshold. The separate VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan); taxpayers at or below that amount are small-scale taxpayers and generally use simplified VAT collection rates of 3% (or temporary reduced rate of 1% where applicable).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer-exporter may claim China's export zero-rate with input-VAT credit/refund only when the goods are physically exported from Mainland China under a Customs Export Goods Declaration naming the WFOE or the principal as exporter, legal title passes to a non-resident buyer, and the foreign purchaser pays in an approved foreign-currency or RMB settlement. The WFOE must first complete export-tax-refund/exemption record filing with the STA. For a pure re-invoicing hub in which goods do not enter or leave Mainland China customs, the transaction is outside the PRC VAT levy and is not zero-rated export VAT; it is documented as an offshore foreign-to-foreign trade with contracts, transport documents and bank flows that evidence no PRC customs involvement. Qualifying cross-border offshore digital services zero-rating, under Finance and Taxation 2016 No. 36, is not relevant to this goods re-invoicing scope but would require proof that the service is consumed wholly outside China and paid by a non-resident.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Declaration for Export Goods) with the electronic release/clearance data matched by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill / multimodal transport document issued to a non-resident buyer and showing shipment from a PRC port or bonded area to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sale/purchase contracts for the re-invoiced goods
  • Supplier VAT special invoice plus the STA invoice authentication/verification record, if input-VAT refund is claimed
  • Bank-issued export foreign-exchange receipt voucher or settlement/collection confirmation, recorded in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, proving actual repatriation of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Completed E-Tax export-tax-refund declaration package (the electronic refund application generated from the official STA export-eligibility data)
  • Foreign exchange receipt status report showing the export proceeds were received or reported as deferred within the 180-day monitoring period
  • If the WFOE acts as a re-invoicing intermediary rather than exporter of record, a customs-route proof package consisting of third-party bills of lading, first-sale invoice, second-sale invoice, and non-PRC bank payment routing to prove the goods never entered PRC customs territory

The STA mandates electronic invoicing through the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. VAT invoices must be issued from the STA-approved E-Tax/e-invoice module, which transmits invoice data to the STA in real time at issuance. The system applies an STA-generated unique invoice number and electronic anti-fraud signature/digital stamp; invoices that have not been digitally cleared by or reported to the STA are not valid for input-VAT deduction, customs export rebate, or third-party commercial payment. The buyer must authenticate the invoice in the VAT invoice service portal before using it to claim input VAT. Enterprises with annual sales exceeding the local threshold must use the digital e-invoice format; paper or self-developed invoicing is prohibited without a certified VAT tax-control device connected to the STA.

Export zero-rating and VAT refunds are automatically cross-checked by the STA against China Customs release data, enterprise bank account receipts, and the SAFE foreign-exchange monitoring system. If the STA finds that the goods did not physically leave China, the payment was not repatriated within 180 days of export (with no SAFE deferred payment registration), the invoice was declared 0% without a valid customs declaration, or the export documents are false or fabricated, the VAT refund will be clawed back retroactively with delay interest at 0.05% per day, plus an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. If fraudulent export tax refund activity is established, the enterprise also loses export-tax-refund qualification for up to three years and may be subject to criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. The same clawback period catches unverified invoices and re-invoicing transactions where the underlying goods movement cannot be documented.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated foreign-exchange framework. Current-account transactions, covering merchandise trade, transportation, insurance and profit remittances for foreign-invested enterprises, are administratively convertible provided that the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by invoice, contract, customs records and other documentary evidence. Capital-account transactions, however, remain strictly controlled: inbound foreign direct investment, external loans, guarantees, securities investments and outbound capital disposals require SAFE registration, designated-bank licensing, and post-arrival supervision of use of funds. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation and current-account verification, but the post-2012 reform abolished mandatory surrender, so conversion of export receipts into RMB is optional within an approved current-account foreign-exchange position. Profit remittance by a WFOE is permitted only after the statutory audit is completed, corporate income tax and withholding taxes are paid or cleared, the board resolution on profit distribution is passed, and the bank has reviewed the capital account FDI registration, balance sheet and tax certificate; in practice, mismatched purpose codes, delayed tax payments, or inconsistencies between re-invoice flows and customs declarations can block or delay the remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated onshore through a PRC designated bank within 180 days from the relevant export customs declaration date; the amount received must be matched with the verified customs value before dispersal. The mandatory conversion ratio is nil after the 2012 SAFE reform, so the WFOE may retain the receipts in a current-account foreign-exchange account or settle them into RMB at its option. Nevertheless, every export receipt must be reconciled with the underlying trade verification data, and any foreign-exchange balance retained without a matching trade file beyond the verification window becomes a regulatory exception requiring SAFE explanation.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the designated foreign-exchange regulatory authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from opening domestic RMB current accounts or FDI capital escrow accounts for a PRC-incorporated WFOE because PBOC banking and SAFE foreign-exchange licensing require the treasury and clearing accounts to sit with a locally licensed bank. An EMI cannot host SAFE-registered capital inflow, cannot issue a Chinese settlement account, and cannot provide auditable trade-verification rails. The only permissible use is as an auxiliary payment collection or settlement channel via an underlying PBOC-licensed payment institution or partner bank for small-ticket or e-commerce payouts; even then, the WFOE should maintain its primary bank accounts at a licensed tier-1 PRC bank to satisfy KYC and regulatory inspections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must complete the initial foreign-investment information report with the market-regulation authority and obtain a Foreign-Exchange Business Registration with SAFE or its designated bank before any cross-border capital remittance. Foreign capital injection should be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the bank-designated FDI capital account, and the payment message must clearly carry the SAFE/balance-of-payments transaction code 6021 or 6022 for direct-investment establishment or capital increase, the creditor and shareholder name, the WFOE enterprise code, and the remittance purpose text equity capital contribution to a PRC wholly foreign-owned enterprise under FDI registration. Upon receipt, the designated bank conducts capital-account confirmation with SAFE's digital system, obtains the company's willing-to-convert statement where applicable, and releases funds into the local RMB account only for permitted purposes. A PRC-licensed CPA subsequently performs capital verification by reconciling the MT103 credit, bank statement, SAFE registration notices, resolution and general ledger; the resulting capital verification report forms the basis for the bank's audit trail and future profit repatriation.For ordinary open-account re-invoicing settlements, the WFOE will initiate SWIFT MT103 telegraphic-transfer payments from its RMB or USD operating account through the designated bank, attaching contract, invoice and transport documents for current-account trade verification. For larger or credit-backed deals, the bank will issue irrevocable commercial letters of credit, usually confirmed by a foreign advising bank, to secure the underlying goods payment; alternatively, documentary collections such as documents against payment and documents against acceptance offer a lower-cost route where the bank separately handles negotiable documents. The local clearing side may use CNAPS or RMB cross-border trade settlement rails when invoices are denominated in RMB, or the bank's correspondent network for USD and EUR via New York or London clearing; all disbursements must bear a SAFE current-account purpose code such as goods-trade code 121010 and must be supported by the same trade file as the corresponding import or export declaration. XT_Transfer and similar digital cross-border rails may be used only as supplementary collection or payout interfaces behind a PBOC-licensed payment institution, never as the primary bank clearing certificate for SAFE inspections.Deliverable onshore forward foreign-exchange contracts concluded with a licensed designated bank under SAFE real-trade-background rules; contract amount and maturity must be consistent with the WFOE's current-account receivables or payables and cannot be rolled purely for arbitrage.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for the PRC entity; they may be transacted only by the offshore parent or group treasury centre with an offshore branch, and the WFOE cannot book or settle NDF cashflows inside China.Cross-currency swaps permissible only with a PRC-licensed bank for genuine mismatched-currency treasury exposure, limited to the committed trade or loan notional and tenor, with SAFE registration and forward-position reporting where required.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from opening domestic RMB current accounts or FDI capital escrow accounts for a PRC-incorporated WFOE because PBOC banking and SAFE foreign-exchange licensing require the treasury and clearing accounts to sit with a locally licensed bank. An EMI cannot host SAFE-registered capital inflow, cannot issue a Chinese settlement account, and cannot provide auditable trade-verification rails. The only permissible use is as an auxiliary payment collection or settlement channel via an underlying PBOC-licensed payment institution or partner bank for small-ticket or e-commerce payouts; even then, the WFOE should maintain its primary bank accounts at a licensed tier-1 PRC bank to satisfy KYC and regulatory inspections.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related party transactions reach RMB 200,000,000 for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100,000,000 for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100,000,000 for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40,000,000 for other related-party service transactions. Master File obligations arise for qualifying large taxpayers under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016); Country-by-Country reporting is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue reaches RMB 5,500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: China enforces economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the beneficial ownership test under SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018), the principal purpose test (PPT) under the OECD Multilateral Instrument, and the special tax investigation rules. A Chinese re-invoicing hub must perform Core Income-Generating Activities within China, including procurement negotiation, logistics coordination, inventory management, credit and market risk assumption, invoicing, treasury, and management decision-making. If the WFOE is a letterbox with no staff, premises, or operational control, the tax authority may disregard the legal form, recharacterize the transaction, attribute profit to the actual functions performed, deny treaty benefits, impose 25% CIT, and assess WHT shortfalls.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and economic substance violation

Statutory sanction: GAAR recharacterization under the Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Articles 120-123; tax authority imposes deemed taxable income at 25%; late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day; civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax under TCAL Article 63; blacklisting as a seriously dishonest taxpayer with joint disciplinary restrictions.

Director personal liability: The new PRC Company Law (2023) Article 23 pierces the corporate veil for single-member and group structures; directors who intentionally or grossly negligently cause third-party harm are jointly and severally liable under Article 191. The legal representative and directors face exit bans under TCAL Article 44, asset freezes, and, where the scheme constitutes tax evasion, criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
Transfer pricing manipulation and false documentation

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing adjustment under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41-48 and SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016); correction of taxable income at the full 25% rate; interest surcharge with up to 5 additional percentage points for lack of contemporaneous documentation; TCAL Article 63 penalty of 50% to 500%; issuance of false special VAT invoices triggers Criminal Law Article 205, punishable by up to life imprisonment and fines or confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or authorize false invoices and fraudulent intercompany contracts may be prosecuted as directly responsible persons for a unit crime, facing personal fines and imprisonment; civil joint liability arises under the Company Law Articles 23 and 191; asset restitution and compensation orders attach to personal estates; exit bans and blacklisting apply to directors and senior officers.
Withholding tax evasion and sham treaty relief

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the unrecovered withholding tax plus late surcharge under TCAL Articles 32 and 69, with a penalty of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld; false treaty claims are retrospectively denied for up to 5 years under SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018); the 10% statutory WHT and interest are reassessed; in aggravated cases the fraud penalty reaches 50% to 500% under TCAL Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors face piercing liability for corporate tax debts, WHT shortfalls, interest, and penalties; travel bans and property preservation orders are issued; criminal liability arises under Criminal Law Articles 201, 203, and 204 for tax evasion and fraudulent export refund or withholding schemes, with imprisonment ranging beyond 7 years in severe cases; the court may additionally impose fines, disqualify the director from holding office, and order civil restitution to the tax authority.
