Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in ordinary internet retail and cross-border e-commerce (ISIC 4791 / NAICS 454110). The Articles of Association must state the registered capital and contribution schedule, and under Article 47 of the Company Law (2023) all subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from the date of business license registration. There is no government-blocked escrow account requirement; contributed capital must be deposited into the company's capital contribution account at a licensed bank in China, and the receiving bank will complete Foreign Investment (FDI) registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange before the shareholder remits funds. Minimum paid-up capital rules do not apply unless the company later enters regulated sectors such as banking, insurance, securities, or internet financial services.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for an industrial civil defense fire permit. However, the registered office must comply with the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China (2021 amendment) for ordinary commercial premises, including maintaining fire-fighting equipment, evacuation access, and basic fire safety management. If the company later operates its own warehouse or fulfillment center in China, it must obtain the fire safety inspection certificate (建设工程消防验收备案) from the local Fire and Rescue Department, complete structural load inspections for the facility, and obtain occupational health and safety approval under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) and the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform via official provincial e-tax portals under etax.chinatax.gov.cn, supported by the 12366 tax service hotline

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Unified Social Credit Code appearing on the business license serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for the enterprise. The WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA office through the e-Tax Bureau immediately after obtaining the business license, filing financial accounting system details, payroll records, bank account information, and legal representative/responsible person data. For foreign non-resident enterprises that do not have a permanent establishment in China but derive Chinese-source income, a non-resident enterprise tax registration must be completed with the competent STA office (via the e-Tax Bureau or in-person window) to obtain a non-resident tax code/TIN; the application requires the overseas incorporation certificate (and notarized/legalized Chinese-language translation if applicable), board resolution appointing a tax representative or agent, evidence of Chinese-source income, contracts, and Chinese bank account details. After registration, monthly/quarterly CIT, VAT, withholding, and ancillary filings are submitted through the e-Tax Bureau using digital certificates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under China's enterprise income tax regime; no US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election exists. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise taxed at 25% CIT on worldwide income. For VAT, if annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory; below that threshold, the enterprise may elect small-scale taxpayer status (currently 1% or 3% levy rate, with monthly RMB 100,000 exemption), but such election is not a hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Small-scale VAT taxpayers (annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000) may register voluntarily; monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (or quarterly not exceeding RMB 300,000) are exempt from VAT, subject to statutory conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods sold by the registered WFOE to overseas consumers or through the Amazon FBA cross-border model are eligible for 0% VAT and/or VAT export refund if: (i) the goods are physically exported from China customs territory and leave China, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration; (ii) the WFOE is the exporter of record and maintains export registration/qualifications; (iii) the goods are export-rebate-eligible (not prohibited or restricted from rebate); (iv) sales are made to non-resident buyers with title transfer outside China; (v) export proceeds are collected through banking channels and verified with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); and (vi) cross-border e-commerce transaction data (order, payment, logistics) is declared under the applicable customs supervision code, including Code 9610 (cross-border e-commerce retail direct export) or Code 9810 (export to overseas warehouse/FBA), as appropriate.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Customs Clearance Bill / Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the overseas Amazon fulfillment center or final consumer
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE Foreign Exchange Collection Verification / bank electronic receipt
  • Export Sales Contract or order documentation
  • Cross-border e-commerce transaction data (order ledger, payment record, logistics tracking) under Customs supervision code 9610/9810
  • Amazon FBA shipment and storage/handover records
  • Customs export manifest and electronic export data transmitted to Customs and STA

China operates a state-controlled VAT invoice system under Golden Tax Phase IV. All VAT general taxpayers must use STA-approved tax control equipment and the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform to issue VAT special and general invoices in real time; invoice data, including amounts, tax rates, and tax control codes, is transmitted digitally to the STA. The Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (Fully Digital E-Invoice) system replaces paper invoice formats and uses a unified nationwide anti-forgery QR code and electronic signature for authentication; software vendors and enterprises integrating e-invoicing must use STA-certified APIs and data protocols. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; fiscal clearance is achieved through real-time electronic reconciliation of issued invoices against VAT returns. Cross-border e-commerce export transactions require clearly marked zero-rated invoices and full supporting customs declaration data.

If zero-rating or export VAT refund is unsupported by authentic customs declarations, logistics records, or foreign exchange collection evidence, the STA will claw back the VAT refund plus charge a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, refusal or evasion may trigger an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; serious fraud is referred to criminal authorities under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China (fraudulent export tax rebate), with penalties up to life imprisonment and confiscation of assets in major cases. Export enterprises are also subject to customs penalties for false export declarations, temporary suspension or revocation of export tax rebate qualifications, inclusion on the STA serious-tax-violation blacklist, and extended tax assessment periods (up to 10 years) for cases involving concealment or fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict tiered convertibility rules. Current account transactions are generally freely convertible once the underlying trade is verified through SAFE's trade monitoring system, with banks performing documentary checks against contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents. Capital account transactions remain tightly controlled. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must complete foreign investment registration, open a designated foreign exchange capital account at a licensed domestic bank, and remit equity capital in the registered amount. Subsequent use of RMB converted from foreign exchange capital must comply with the negative list and cannot be used for unapproved onshore investments, securities speculation, or lending to third parties. Profit remittance to the overseas parent is permitted only after annual tax settlement is confirmed by a Chinese CPA and the required tax certification is presented to the bank. A withholding tax clearance certificate, audit report, and board resolution must accompany the remittance. Payment to non-resident suppliers for cross-border trade is similarly reviewable. Current account export proceeds must be repatriated into China and are subject to verification against customs records, while capital account flows such as share capital increases, external debt borrowing, and dividend distributions require SAFE registration or filing in advance. In practice, conversion inhibition appears not in an absolute prohibition of RMB conversion but in stringent documentary integrity requirements, the application of the 2019 Foreign Investment Law negative list, and the bank's duty to exercise a genuine-trade and business-reality test. Therefore, a WFOE should treat currency convertibility as administratively available but operationally contingent on impeccable reconciliation of trade, customs, tax, and treasury records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds arising from the cross-border e-commerce sales must be repatriated to China unless SAFE has specifically approved overseas retention for an onshore entity. Under the current SAFE framework, there is no statutory mandatory percentage conversion into RMB; after repatriation to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account at a licensed bank, the WFOE may settle the proceeds into RMB at its election up to 100% of the remitted amount. The bank must verify the export collection/verification data against the customs declaration, sales contracts, logistics bills, and Amazon settlement reports, and the enterprise must ensure collection within the generally applied 90-day export collection period, although an A-class trade company can, in practice, use the SAFE electronic system to record beyond-due receipts. B-class and C-class enterprises face stricter by-transaction review, extended documentary submission, and heightened monitoring. Funds excluded from the seller's Amazon account by refunds or chargebacks must be mapped directly to original transactions. Use of the foreign exchange settlement account must comply with the permitted business scope and stated capital contribution purposes.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its appointed administration, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-medium, conditional viability. Offshore EMI platforms such as Stripe and Airwallex cannot legally replace a licensed domestic clearing bank for the WFOE's onshore export proceeds repatriation, capital contribution settlement, or trade payment disbursement. PBOC and SAFE require cross-border trade funds to be collected and settled through a licensed domestic bank or a PBOC-registered cross-border payment institution. Airwallex may operate with a Chinese payment institution license through an onshore partner for cross-border payment collection and payout, and Stripe can be used as an international collection front-end, but the actual RMB clearing and FX settlement must occur through the domestic bank relationship. The WFOE should treat any offshore EMI account as a receivable reflection point, not as the jurisdiction's lawful clearing rail. The bank underwriting view will be skeptical of undisclosed third-party payment flows; therefore, every EMI arrangement must be supported by executed platform agreements, provider licenses, and a transparent reconciliation system feeding directly into the bank's trade verification files.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-medium, conditional viability. Offshore EMI platforms such as Stripe and Airwallex cannot legally replace a licensed domestic clearing bank for the WFOE's onshore export proceeds repatriation, capital contribution settlement, or trade payment disbursement. PBOC and SAFE require cross-border trade funds to be collected and settled through a licensed domestic bank or a PBOC-registered cross-border payment institution. Airwallex may operate with a Chinese payment institution license through an onshore partner for cross-border payment collection and payout, and Stripe can be used as an international collection front-end, but the actual RMB clearing and FX settlement must occur through the domestic bank relationship. The WFOE should treat any offshore EMI account as a receivable reflection point, not as the jurisdiction's lawful clearing rail. The bank underwriting view will be skeptical of undisclosed third-party payment flows; therefore, every EMI arrangement must be supported by executed platform agreements, provider licenses, and a transparent reconciliation system feeding directly into the bank's trade verification files.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% unified corporate income tax rate under Article 4 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax under Article 37 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise, such as a WFOE, to a non-resident enterprise shareholder, unless a bilateral Double Tax Treaty (DTA) reduces that rate. For example, under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA, a qualified beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the paid-up share capital for at least 12 months may receive a 5% rate; most other Chinese DTAs provide treaty dividend rates between 0% and 15%, commonly 5% for direct holdings and 10% for portfolio holdings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under State Taxation Administration Announcement [2016] No. 42, as amended, contemporaneous Local File transfer pricing documentation is mandatory if, in a fiscal year, any related-party transaction category exceeds the statutory threshold: tangible property transfers exceeding RMB 200 million, intangible property transfers exceeding RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million, provision of services exceeding RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. An enterprise with aggregate related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion must also prepare a Master File. Even below these thresholds, the tax authority may require documentation where transactions occur with related parties located in low-tax jurisdictions or where the enterprise is loss-making, has low profitability, or has a history of adjusting transfer prices.

Economic substance scope: China applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, allowing recharacterization of transactions that lack a reasonable commercial purpose and reduce tax liability. For Chinese tax purposes, and in determining beneficial ownership for DTA relief, the tax authority examines substance over corporate form: personnel, premises, business functions, decision-making, assumption of economic risk, and actual performance of core income-generating activities (CIGA). A WFOE engaged in Amazon FBA must maintain adequate e-commerce operation functions in China: procurement, inventory risk, pricing, order handling, logistics control, after-sale service, Amazon account management, and treasury decisions. If the WFOE is a shell that contracts all functions to an offshore parent while only booking title, China may impose tax on the parent's related profit under GAAR, deny treaty relief to the non-resident shareholder, or attribute income to an actual place of effective management in China. The OECD BEPS-aligned beneficial ownership doctrine is applied through Announcement [2018] No. 9, requiring that the recipient have the right to use and enjoy the dividend, interest, or royalty independently, with no formal obligation to pass the income to another person and with substantial business activity in its claimed residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-Border Revenue Concealment and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 63 and 64 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, the taxpayer must pay the underpaid tax plus a daily 0.05% surcharge, and is liable for an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. If the evasion is criminal, Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law imposes a fine and imprisonment of up to three years where the amount evaded is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, and imprisonment of three to seven years where the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax payable. Articles 211 and 212 extend criminal responsibility to directly responsible directors, supervisors, and senior managers.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Article 23 of the PRC Company Law where directors abuse the limited-liability entity form to conceal income or assets; the director/actual controller can be held jointly liable for unpaid taxes, civil restitution of diverted assets, and compensation to the tax authority as a creditor. The director's name will be placed on the Joint Punishment List for Serious Tax Dishonesty, causing travel restrictions/exit control (blacklisting) by the tax and exit-administration authorities, restrictions on high consumption, credit downgrading, disqualification as legal representative, and personal asset attachment. If the evasion scheme is proved, the director and financial officer face criminal imprisonment for tax evasion and possible additional sanctions under money-laundering or foreign-exchange laws.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41(1) and Article 41(2) of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may use reasonable methods to re-determine taxable income at arm's length and recharacterize the transaction under Article 47 (GAAR). Under the Special Tax Adjustment rules, the tax authority may add interest at the central-bank benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points on the tax underpaid arising from a transfer pricing adjustment and pursue a ten-year lookback. The underpaid tax itself is subject to the 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 where there is fraudulent concealment; failure to provide contemporaneous TP documentation or the filing of negligent/incorrect related-party forms triggers fines under Articles 44 and 70 of the Tax Administration Law and the tax authority is empowered to deem taxable income based on available evidence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the related-party payment structure may be treated as having participated in tax evasion if they knew that the intercompany charges were artificial and unsubstantiated. Under the Company Law veil-piercing doctrine, the personal assets of directors can be reached where the WFOE is undercapitalized, commingles funds with the parent, or uses the offshore arrangement to avoid debts including tax debts. The responsible director may be blacklisted, restricted from leaving China, ordered to pay restitution to the company/tax authority, and criminally prosecuted under Article 201 of the Criminal Law as an aider or organizer. Where the WFOE becomes insolvent due to the stripping, the parent and directors may also face creditor and bankruptcy clawback actions.
Beneficial Ownership Abuse / Shell Conduit / Treaty Shopping on Outbound Dividends

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits and recharacterize the shell as a conduit, requiring supplemental 5% withholding (from 5% to 10%) on the dividends, plus interest on late under-withholding and penalties under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law of 50% to 300% of the amount not withheld. Under the GAAR in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 120 of the Tax Administration Law Implementation Rules, the arrangement may be collapsed and additional tax imposed on the income as if earned directly by the ultimate resident. If the shell is used to conceal a Chinese tax resident's actual place of management, the shell itself may be treated as a Chinese tax resident, exposing its world-wide income to 25% CIT and imposing additional penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative of the WFOE, as withholding agents, are personally liable where they knowingly participate in false withholding declarations; if the shell is abused to evade the dividend WHT, the directors may be jointly and severally liable for the short-paid withholding, face blacklisting and exit-control restrictions, and suffer civil asset seizure. If the arrangement is part of a broader scheme to defraud the Chinese revenue authority, the responsible persons face criminal liability for tax evasion under Article 201 and for false invoicing/false accounting under Articles 205 and 201 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment and substantial fines.
