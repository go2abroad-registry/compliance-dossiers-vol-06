Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected for WFOE registration and bank onboarding in China. PRC Company Law and market entity registration rules require a registered address that is a lawful place of business with a valid lease or property certificate; a commercial mailbox is not a registered address. For an ISIC 4791 internet retail DTC brand, the absence of a physical store does not excuse the requirement. Local tax authorities will flag an address as abnormal during address verification, and banks conducting AML/KYC will decline account opening if no premises, staff, or operating systems can be physically inspected. A government-designated enterprise incubation address may be accepted only where the incubator supplies a legal lease, public filing documents, and demonstrable substance, but a private virtual office or mailbox remains impermissible.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a general WFOE engaged in ISIC 4791 internet retail. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, the sole shareholder must pay the full amount of subscribed capital within five years from the date of issuance of the business license. No blocked escrow account is required for this activity. Inbound foreign exchange capital must be remitted into a SAFE-registered capital contribution account and documented in the company financial records, but the funds are not frozen and may be applied to lawful operating expenses after the bank verifies the inward remittance.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing through the STA e-Tax Bureau platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the competent provincial or municipal tax service bureau where the WFOE is registered.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is formed with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Tax registration is automatically completed through China's multi-certificate integration system when the Business License is issued. The foreign shareholder then completes the 'new taxpayer onboarding package' via the e-Tax Bureau, including legal representative identity verification, accounting system and bank account filings, tax type identification, and VAT taxpayer status selection. For cross-border DTC exports, the WFOE must also complete Foreign Trade Operator Filing with MOFCOM, Customs Registration with the General Administration of Customs to obtain a customs declaration code, and filing for export tax rebate (exemption) qualification with the competent tax authority. No separate VAT ID number is issued beyond the USCC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Chinese VAT law, exported goods are zero-rated, provided the WFOE is a registered export enterprise, files a valid export customs declaration through the China International Trade Single Window, transfers title to the overseas buyer, and repatriates the export proceeds in foreign exchange. For cross-border e-commerce retail exports, the retail export commodity declaration list may be used under customs supervision code 9610, and the VAT exemption or input VAT rebate may be claimed if the enterprise is a general VAT taxpayer and holds lawful purchase VAT special invoices. Qualifying cross-border services are zero-rated only where specifically enumerated in STA cross-border taxable services rules, otherwise the standard 6% VAT rate applies.

China requires electronic invoicing through the STA-authorized VAT Invoice Management System using tax control UKey, and is rolling out Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoices (FDEI) on the STA Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. FDEI invoices are transmitted to the STA in real time, with automated anti-fraud validation, digital signatures, and tax control stamping; no private invoice templates are permissible.

Fraudulently obtaining an export tax refund triggers recovery of the refund plus a fine of one to five times under Article 66, and criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC for fraudulent export tax refunds. The taxpayer credit rating will be downgraded and future export tax rebate eligibility may be suspended.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a mixed convertibility regime. Under the IMF Article VIII current-account obligations, RMB is fully convertible for genuine current-account transactions, including cross-border trade in goods and services, e-commerce settlement, and profit remittances, subject to documentary verification by a licensed designated bank. Capital-account transactions, however, remain tightly controlled by SAFE and the PBOC. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may legitimately receive equity capital injections in USD, HKD, or cross-border RMB under the Foreign Investment Negative List; the inbound funds must be deposited into a specialized FDI capital account and converted into RMB only on a payment-in basis supported by contracts, invoices, or tax declarations. Capital verification by a statutory Chinese certified public accountant is required, and the enterprise must complete tax registration, annual audits, and corporate income tax settlement before declaring dividends. Profit remittances are treated as current-account outbound payments, permitting the parent to repatriate after-tax profits once the bank has verified the authenticity of the remittance using audit reports, tax payment certificates, and board resolutions. There is no blanket profit remittance quota, but the lack of proper supporting documentation will trigger bank rejection. The system is therefore open and workable for current account flows, while capital-account mobility such as principal redemption, intercompany loan repayment, and equity transfer remains heavily gated through SAFE registration, filing, or approval requirements. Treasury teams must pre-clear all capital-account movements and maintain meticulous underlying documentation to avoid classification as unauthorized cross-border financing or tax leakage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The People's Republic of China no longer operates a mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio for export receipts. Under SAFE's Total Amount Monitoring regime for goods trade, export foreign-exchange proceeds must be repatriated into the exporter's onshore current-account foreign-currency deposit account or settled into RMB through a licensed designated bank, generally within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless the bank has approved a legitimate offshore retention registration. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is zero; a WFOE may retain 100% of its export proceeds in a foreign-currency current account or settle any portion at the prevailing market rate. Receipts must be electronically matched against customs declarations, and significant unexplained timing mismatches, excess retention beyond monitoring thresholds, or undocumented foreign exchange receipts will generate automated compliance exceptions requiring written evidence from the exporter.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign-exchange control and administrative authority operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut Business and Wise cannot lawfully maintain onshore RMB deposit, settlement, or basic operating accounts for a China-registered WFOE because they do not hold the PBOC banking license and are not members of CIPS or the domestic RMB clearing system. They may serve only as ancillary payment facilitators for collecting international DTC webstore settlement from Stripe, PayPal, or Shopify Payments, and those settlement proceeds must ultimately be routed into a licensed onshore commercial bank account. As a primary treasury rail for payroll, VAT, customs duty, statutory capital conversion, or dividend remittance, cross-border fintech EMIs have Low viability and pose significant compliance risk under SAFE and PBOC regulations. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must first complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration through the receiving domestic bank or a licensed intermediary. The equity injection is then executed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's account to the WFOE's onshore FDI foreign-currency or cross-border RMB capital account, using the SAFE/BOP transaction code for direct investment equity contribution and a remittance narration explicitly stating 'Capital contribution to CN_WFOE_LTD under approved FDI registration'. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the purpose code, registers the capital account using RCPMIS reporting, converts the foreign currency to RMB on a genuine payment-in basis, and the statutory auditor performs capital verification. The WFOE may only draw down from the capital account after the funds are placed into the basic operational settlement account and the use of funds is substantiated by contracts, tax invoices, payroll schedules, or vendor payment instructions. Inbound injections with vague purpose codes, back-to-back same-day withdrawals, or conversion without underlying payment documentation will be rejected by the bank and may trigger SAFE penalties.For the DTC e-commerce business, outbound payments comprise inventory procurement, logistics fees, advertising spend, and technology subscription costs. The WFOE should execute direct supplier payments using SWIFT MT103 with a proper BOPS trade or service code, invoice, contract, and tax proof. For higher-value or letter-of-credit-dependent procurement, commercial letters of credit are the preferred clearing rail as they provide documentary security while remaining fully reconcilable for tax and customs purposes. Documentary collections are acceptable for trusted suppliers under open-account-like trade terms. Cross-border payments in RMB should be settled through CIPS using the participating domestic tier-1 bank, with SWIFT as a fallback.
