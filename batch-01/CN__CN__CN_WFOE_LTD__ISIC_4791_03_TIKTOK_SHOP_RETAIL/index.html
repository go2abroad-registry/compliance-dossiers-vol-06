Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China are eligible for zero-rated VAT provided the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer and the export is properly declared to customs. The exporter must obtain the export customs declaration (报关单) and evidence of foreign exchange receipt. China mandates the Golden Electronic System (Phase III) and the Digital Electronic Invoicing (DEI) platform. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current account convertibility under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, meaning that trade and service related cross-border payments can generally be settled through designated banks without per-transaction SAFE approval. However, the RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), inbound equity capital contributions must first be registered with SAFE under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) regime. Only after obtaining the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate can the foreign parent remit capital in USD, EUR, or other convertible currencies to the WFOE's foreign currency capital account at a domestic bank. Once credited, the WFOE may convert these funds into RMB at the spot exchange rate for lawful business purposes, but not for domestic equity investment, on-lending, or the purchase of real estate outside the approved business scope. Profit remittance abroad is treated as a current account item and is permissible provided the WFOE has completed annual audit, filed corporate income tax returns, paid all applicable taxes, and submitted an application to the remitting bank with supporting board resolutions and audited financial statements. Banks may require tax payment certificates and a written confirmation that the profit represents genuine after-tax retained earnings. Surrender requirements apply to export proceeds; all proceeds from current account trade must be repatriated into China and are subject to bank verification against customs declarations. There is strong regulatory scrutiny over the use of capital account funds and the risk of round-tripping. For an e-commerce WFOE, each monthly settlement from TikTok Shop, received through licensed cross-border payment institutions or directly from overseas payment platforms, must be reconciled with underlying sales transactions, logistics records, and customs export data to satisfy the banks and SAFE on the real trade nature of the flows. Overall, the system imposes rigorous documentary controls but provides a lawful and predictable mechanism for a WFOE to convert and remit funds if compliance files are meticulously maintained.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from goods or services must be repatriated to China and either retained in an approved foreign currency settlement account or converted into RMB through a designated bank. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio applicable to every export receipt; the current principle is full repatriation with free settlement choice. However, the bank must verify the receipt against the electronic customs declaration in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Under SAFE practice, export proceeds should be received and verified promptly; for advance receipts, export clearance must generally occur within 30 days, and for normal export settlement, the verification should be completed within 90 days from the date of customs declaration. Any overdue discrepancy requires explanation, supporting contract evidence, and may trigger reporting to SAFE.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise or similar non-bank financial technology companies have low viability as the primary banking rail for a mainland China WFOE. Under PRC Banking and FX regulations, a domestically domiciled legal entity is required to maintain its basic deposit account and foreign currency capital account with a licensed commercial banking institution operating in China. Digital EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot provide RMB settlement accounts, capital verification receipts, or SAFE filing services. They may be utilized only peripherally as cross-border payment collection tools outside mainland China for receiving consumer payments from certain marketplaces, and even then only when the EMI holds an appropriate license in the relevant foreign jurisdiction. The legally compliant and operationally sound approach is for the WFOE to use domestic Tier-1 commercial banks as the formal clearing backbone while employing licensed cross-border payment processors solely for trade gross-up settlement, always followed by bank reconciliation and customs data alignment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent company must remit the subscribed capital in compliance with SAFE FDI registration. The remittance should be executed through SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code that clearly identifies the transfer as foreign direct investment into the Chinese WFOE. The payment must be received into the WFOE's designated foreign currency capital account opened at a domestic Tier-1 bank after the bank obtains the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. Upon receipt, the WFOE must complete capital verification through a licensed PRC certified public accountant, who will issue a formal capital verification report confirming the actual settlement of foreign currency into RMB and the lawful application of funds. All conversions must follow the spot rate quoted by the bank on the transaction date, and the bank will report each capital receipt into the SAFE monitoring system. The WFOE must ensure that the entering capital is never used for business purposes outside the approved scope, such as repayment of RMB loans, equity investments, or entrusted loans.For ongoing procurement of goods from domestic suppliers, the WFOE will use standard RMB enterprise transfers through the People's Bank of China's High-Value Payment System or CIPS for RMB cross-border payments. For cross-border trade settlements with overseas suppliers, the WFOE may instruct its bank to send SWIFT MT103 messages using the correct international transactions category code supported by invoices, contracts, and customs declarations. Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are appropriate for larger transactional purchases where assurance of payment or shipping documents is required. For daily TikTok Shop operational disbursements such as influencer payouts, third-party logistics charges, and platform advertising costs, the WFOE may route those through licensed cross-border payment service providers, but the equivalent RMB or foreign currency must be backed by documented commercial transactions and should be reconciled against the core bank account records to avoid any perception of unverified E-commerce cash sweep activity.Onshore delivery-based CNY foreign exchange forward contracts with a domestic bank under a realized trade exposure, requiring the WFOE to present underlying sales contracts, export customs declarations, and receivables aging reports.Cross-currency swaps strictly limited to SAFE-registered foreign currency loans or capital market transactions with a genuine underlying debt and documented hedging need; speculative use is prohibited.FX options sold by banks to enterprises as hedging tools only when the option is part of a certified real trade exposure; structured derivatives with embedded leverage are not approvable for a standard WFOE without special SAFE approval and are strongly discouraged.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise or similar non-bank financial technology companies have low viability as the primary banking rail for a mainland China WFOE. Under PRC Banking and FX regulations, a domestically domiciled legal entity is required to maintain its basic deposit account and foreign currency capital account with a licensed commercial banking institution operating in China. Digital EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot provide RMB settlement accounts, capital verification receipts, or SAFE filing services. They may be utilized only peripherally as cross-border payment collection tools outside mainland China for receiving consumer payments from certain marketplaces, and even then only when the EMI holds an appropriate license in the relevant foreign jurisdiction. The legally compliant and operationally sound approach is for the WFOE to use domestic Tier-1 commercial banks as the formal clearing backbone while employing licensed cross-border payment processors solely for trade gross-up settlement, always followed by bank reconciliation and customs data alignment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a China-resident WFOE must prepare a Local File when its annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million for tangible-property transfers, RMB 10 million for intangible-property/services transactions or RMB 50 million for financial related-party transactions; a Master File is required when the ultimate parent's audited consolidated group revenue reaches or exceeds RMB 1 billion in the relevant fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China is not an Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) jurisdiction in the offshore-zone sense, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement No. 6 of 2015 applies an equivalent substance-over-form doctrine. A WFOE engaged in TikTok Shop retail must demonstrate that board authority, procurement, local merchant operations, inventory management, influencer contracting, logistics decisions, customer service and marketing management are actually performed by its own staff in mainland China. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of a social-commerce distribution enterprise must sit within the Chinese WFOE; if the non-resident TikTok group controls sales algorithms, contractual pricing and profit retention, or if the WFOE is merely a letter-box aggregator of receivables, the tax authority may disregard the WFOE, reattribute the TikTok Shop profit to the offshore principal, reject treaty benefits and impose China EIT on the economic substance owner.
