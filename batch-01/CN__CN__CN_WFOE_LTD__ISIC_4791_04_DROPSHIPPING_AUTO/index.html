Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permitted for a WFOE engaged in automated dropshipping, unless the virtual office provider offers a lawful, non-residential business license in the jurisdiction of registration and an actual physical premises subject to inspection. Under the Regulations on the Control of Enterprise Registration (State Council Decree No. 746, 2021), all enterprises must have a registered address that can accept legal process, and the address must be consistent with the place of business operations as declared to tax authorities and customs. For cross-border e-commerce and dropshipping activities, the State Administration of Taxation (SAT) and the local tax bureaus require a physical office that can produce, upon request, complete records of purchase orders, sales orders, logistics receipts, and banking transactions. Further, the Payment Management Rules (PBOC Order No. 1, 2010) and the AML Rules require banks and payment service providers to perform physical verification of the registered business location before opening a merchant account; therefore, a mailbox-only address is rejected by all major commercial banks and payment gateway providers (such as Stripe and PayPal) when underwriting cross-border merchants. In practice, a WFOE must maintain a residential-quality leased office space (面积) of at least 10 square meters, with a desk, a dedicated telephone line, a physical sign, and fire-safety compliance certificates. The virtual office may be used only as an ancillary administrative support, never as the sole registered address. The risk of rejection by tax authorities is high if the address appears on a list of high-incidence virtual office registrations, and the entity may be designated as 'address abnormal' (地址异常), leading to a freeze of its tax invoice quota and inclusion in the list of enterprises with abnormal conditions, which blocks further name changes, share transfers, and termination of social insurance account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. For VAT purposes, the WFOE is an independent VAT taxpayer and is responsible for its own output VAT and input VAT recovery.

Available election forms

None for U.S. or multilateral check-the-box elections and no general pass-through election under Chinese corporate income tax law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no de minimis VAT registration threshold for an enterprise with a Chinese legal entity; VAT registration accompanies business registration. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer turnover threshold is RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales; below that threshold a taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer, and small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are eligible for the VAT exemption policy unless they issue special VAT invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China by the WFOE qualify for the 0% VAT rate and the associated export VAT refund if: the WFOE holds customs registration and export tax refund qualification; the goods are cleared by China Customs under an export declaration showing the WFOE as the sender/consignor; the goods leave China Customs territory; title passes to a non-resident buyer or to the overseas dropshipping platform; and the foreign currency sales proceeds are repatriated to the WFOE's Chinese bank account. For cross-border e-commerce retail exports, export shipment records and customs supervision logistics data substantiate the zero-rating; digital services provided to overseas recipients and consumed wholly outside China may also qualify for zero-rating if the same service export documentation and FX receipt rules are satisfied.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration, being the statutory export declaration equivalent of a Bill of Entry, showing the customs declaration number, HS classification, export FOB value, quantity, and export date
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing that the goods were delivered to the overseas buyer or overseas warehouse outside China Customs territory
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE and matching packing list
  • Customs clearance logistics data from the cross-border e-commerce retail export or dropshipping logistics platform
  • Foreign exchange collection certificate issued by the WFOE's domestic bank, namely the bank exchange memo or inward remittance certificate, evidencing the receipt of the export sales proceeds in foreign currency from the overseas buyer or platform
  • Export VAT refund declaration submitted through the STA export tax refund electronic system and the reconcilable record in the China Customs Single Window
  • Transportation contracts, customs broker authorizations, and warehouse receipts for sales fulfilled from bonded warehouses or overseas warehouses

China requires issuance of VAT invoices through the national electronic invoice system under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. The fully digitalized electronic invoice platform assigns a unique invoice number and tax authority control code, applies the taxpayer digital certificate for anti-counterfeit digital stamping, and transmits invoice data to the STA in near real time. Registered VAT taxpayers must upload all special and general VAT invoice issuance data, and cross-border e-commerce and dropshipping sellers are required to issue electronic VAT invoices through the e-Tax platform; no paper-only fiscal till regime applies. The STA reconciles invoice data, customs export declaration data, treasury receipts, and foreign exchange collection records electronically.

The STA and China Customs automatically reconcile the export VAT refund claim against the customs export declaration, logistics movement records, payment gateway data, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If any documentation is inconsistent, false, or incomplete, the zero-rating is denied and the claimed export VAT refund is clawed back, together with late payment surcharge and interest. Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, fraudulent tax refund or tax evasion gives rise to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or wrongly refunded. Fraudulently obtaining an export tax rebate is also a criminal offense under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment up to life imprisonment for particularly grave amounts, plus fines and confiscation of property. The competent tax authority may additionally revoke the WFOE's export tax refund qualification and downgrade its tax credit rating, customs AEO status, and foreign exchange collection designation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible on current account for trade and service transactions, subject to strict genuineness review under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations; capital account convertibility remains tightly controlled. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must obtain SAFE registration (directly or via a delegated bank) before remitting inbound equity capital, shareholder loans, or any cross-border financing, and the funds must enter through a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account. Capital account receipts are governed by the negative-list approach: they cannot be used for offshore equity investment outside the approved scope, prohibited onshore equity participation, or repayment of local RMB loans without explicit SAFE approval. Profit remittance to the overseas parent requires audited financial statements, a tax settlement certificate, and completed capital verification; the paying bank will review the profit allocation resolution before executing the outbound dividend wire. Export proceeds from the dropshipping operation must comply with the Total Amount Verification System, and although no mandatory conversion to RMB is imposed, retention limits and reporting obligations are enforced. The principal convertibility hurdles are documentary proof of underlying trade, strict account purpose restrictions, and the need for prior registration/investigation for every capital movement, making free repatriation essentially conditional on professional compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File thresholds under Announcement 42 (2016) of the State Taxation Administration: Related-party transactions for a single fiscal year exceeding RMB 40 million (approximately) for the Local File, and aggregate related-party transactions exceeding RMB 400 million (approximately) for the Master File plus enterprise-specific turnover thresholds; the Related Party Transaction Reporting Form (年度关联业务往来报告表) must be submitted with the annual CIT return if the taxpayer engages in related-party transactions, regardless of amount, beginning with the tax year 2016 in the new disclosure form; enterprises with an annual related-party revenue amount up to RMB 10 million and other controlled transactions up to RMB 4 million may be exempt from the full reporting schedule, but must still file the basic disclosure. The WFOE dropshipping business must prepare a contemporaneous Local File when the aggregate value of related-party purchases, sales, royalties, interest, and service fees exceeds RMB 40 million in a year. A Master File is required when the total related-party transactions exceed RMB 400 million or when the enterprise is part of a multinational group with aggregate turnover exceeding RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: For China resident enterprises, there is no dedicated Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to the UAE or BVI, but the tax authority applies the 'substance over form' concept and general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and Articles 120-123 of the Implementation Regulations of the EIT Law. The doctrine of substance is used to disregard artificial structures, recharacterize related-party transactions, and attribute income to the entity that performs the core income-generating activities (CIGA) and assumes the economically significant risks. For a WFOE engaged in automated dropshipping, the CIGA analysis requires that the WFOE has a physical presence (office), employees making strategic decisions on procurement, pricing, and logistics, and the ability to control risks associated with inventory, customer service, and supply chain; if the WFOE merely executes routine support and the parent controls all risks and interfaces with customers, the Chinese tax authority may allocate more income to the WFOE under the profit split or residual profit split approach. The CFC rules (Article 45 of EIT Law) may attribute undistributed profits of foreign subsidiaries to the WFOE if the foreign entity is in a low-tax jurisdiction, fails the active business test, and does not distribute income. The so-called 'beneficial owner' and 'substance' requirements under the DTA context further deny treaty benefits to shell companies without real business activities. Additionally, the State Taxation Administration has published Anti-Avoidance Work Procedure (Announcement 13/2016) enabling substance and economic purpose assessments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fraudulent Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, tax evasion results in an order to pay the evaded taxes within the statutory time limit plus a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded amount; under Article 44 and Announcement 6 (2016) the tax authority may impose a special TP investigation adjustment with interest at 5% above the benchmark lending rate; failure to provide contemporaneous TP documentation triggers a RMB 10,000 documentation penalty and an additional 5% daily late payment surcharge; Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes criminal liability if the evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and is more than 10% of taxable income, with imprisonment up to seven years and fines; the tax authority may impose tax on deemed dividends for unrepatriated profits under CFC rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved, prepared, or signed false tax returns or intercompany agreements may be jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax underpayment due to fraudulent conduct; under the Criminal Law, directors and the actual controller may be prosecuted as natural persons for tax evasion (imprisonment up to three years for lesser amounts, seven years for aggravated amounts), for making false tax declarations, or for forging evidence; directors may face a travel ban (出境限制) and blacklisting on the State Taxation Administration's major dishonesty list (税收违法'黑名单'), preventing departure from China, restricting high-consumption behavior, and affecting creditworthiness; civil restitution to the company is available in derivative actions, and if assets are transferred to offshore accounts, personal asset freezing orders may be imposed under the Asset Preservation Law.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure and Non-Resident Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Abuse Rule in Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and Article 120 of the EITL Implementation Regulations, the tax authority may disregard the entity or recharacterize income, making the offshore parent assessable in China as having a permanent establishment; Article 115 of the Tax Collection Administration Law allows the tax authority to take tax preservation measures, including freezing bank accounts and seizing assets; for failure to register or maintain substance, the tax authority may cancel the tax registration and impose the maximum late-filing penalties; under Circular 601 and the MLI, treaty benefits are denied, and the WFOE may be subject to a 10% WHT on all undocumented outbound remittances, with penalties under Article 69 (50%-3 times of under-withheld tax).

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the WFOE to operate without substance and use treaty-shopping structures may be held civilly liable for the resulting tax shortfall, interest, and penalties if they signed the returns and agreements; under the Company Law Article 20, courts may pierce the veil and hold the controlling shareholder and directors jointly liable for corporate debts if the entity is used to evade law or harm the interests of creditors or the State. Directors may be subjected to travel restrictions, property seizure, blacklisting on the major dishonest taxpayer list, and in egregious cases criminal prosecution for tax evasion. A senior officer who knowingly signs a false beneficial ownership declaration may face imprisonment up to seven years and monetary fines under Article 201 or 205 of the Criminal Law.
AML / Foreign Exchange Control Violations and Fraudulent Outbound Remittances

Statutory sanction: Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (Article 12, 39-42, 48), unauthorized cross-border transfer of foreign exchange or evasion of mandatory repatriation is punishable by fines up to 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount for transfer of foreign exchange, and up to 5% of the unrepatriated amount for failure to repatriate; serious cases carry criminal liability under the Foreign Exchange Criminal Interpretation for illegal foreign exchange trading (illegal business operation) with imprisonment up to five years; under the Anti-Money Laundering Law of China, failing to implement AML compliance for virtual assets or cross-border e-commerce payment arrangements can result in fines of up to RMB 5 million and responsible director fines; the public security authorities may impose criminal detention for fraud or smuggling; the State Administration for Market Regulation may revoke the business license for illegal business operations without a license (dropshipping does not require a specific license but requires Customs registration for import/export).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the entity to make false customs declarations, fraudulent remittance applications, or who use personal accounts to receive cross-border dropshipping revenue can be personally criminally prosecuted for illegal business operation, money laundering, or fraud. Under the Money Laundering Law, a director who knowingly facilitates laundering of proceeds may face imprisonment up to seven years for money laundering under Article 191 of the Criminal Law, or five years for illegal foreign exchange transactions; the court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20 of the Company Law if the corporate form is used to evade law and damage creditors, holding the sole shareholder and directors personally liable for all debts; directors may be placed on the 'dishonest judgment debtor list' (laolai) limiting their travel, high-speed rail, airline travel, and credit; personal assets in China may be frozen and forfeited if derived from criminal proceeds; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) can impose lifetime bans on individuals who commit serious foreign exchange violations.
