Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its provincial electronic tax bureaus via the STA e-Tax online portal (Electronic Tax Bureau / e-Taxation platform).

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Register the WFOE as a foreign-invested limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Business License. The 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically the PRC taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes, including VAT. Step 2: Within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, complete tax registration at the competent STA tax service bureau by filing the USCC, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identity, financial accounting system, bank account information, and expected tax types (corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and local surcharges). Step 3: Through the e-Tax portal, confirm the taxpayer profile and register for the VAT taxpayer identity: small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000, or general VAT taxpayer if the threshold is met or voluntarily selected. Step 4: Apply online for an STA-issued digital certificate / tax-control UKey for invoice issuance and electronic filing credentials. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident entity without a PRC legal entity, the STA does not issue a stand-alone VAT registration number; instead, the non-resident's Chinese payer must perform withholding registration and withholding tax reporting unless the non-resident establishes a permanent establishment in China. A cross-border e-commerce seller using only offshore services with no PRC nexus is not required to register for VAT, but any physical inventory, WFOE, or onshore cash-on-delivery collection agent creates VAT registration exposure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None for corporate income tax check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. For VAT, eligible WFOEs with annual taxable turnover below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to be treated as small-scale VAT taxpayers; a small-scale taxpayer with sound accounting may elect general VAT taxpayer status. Once the company exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in aggregated taxable sales within any 12-month period, classification as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory and irreversible for the calendar year.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No exemption from basic tax registration for commercial legal entities; all WFOEs must register. The compulsory general VAT taxpayer threshold is annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan), after which the entity must use the standard VAT taxpayer regime, issue VAT special invoices, and claim input VAT credits.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported from China are zero-rated (exempt with credit/refund of input VAT) only where: (i) the goods are sold to an overseas buyer and leave the PRC customs territory; (ii) the export sale is recorded in the customs export declaration and, where applicable, in the cross-border e-commerce retail export supervision framework; (iii) the seller is a VAT general taxpayer or an approved export rebate taxpayer; (iv) the exported goods are included in the VAT export rebate catalogue; and (v) export proceeds are repatriated and verified in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. In the cash-on-delivery mail-order model, eligible cross-border e-commerce retail exports may be declared using the dedicated retail export customs declaration and tax rebate procedure, subject to item-level order data matching. Qualifying cross-border taxable services rendered wholly outside China are similarly exempt from VAT under the STA's cross-border taxable services policy when the recipient is overseas and the service is consumed outside China.

The STA mandates electronic invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure and has rolled out fully digitalized electronic invoices for the majority of taxpayers. Invoice numbers are allocated by the STA, not by the taxpayer; invoice issuance data is reported to the national VAT invoice management system in near real-time. Each accepted invoice carries a tax-authority cryptographic digital signature / anti-fraud stamp and a QR code. Paper invoices, where still permissible, must be printed only through STA-controlled tax-control equipment (UKey), and aggregate issuance totals must be declared in the VAT filing. Cash-on-delivery retail receipts do not replace the VAT invoice; a compliant VAT invoice must be issued for each taxable sale, and for exports the export invoice is declared in the customs and export rebate systems.

      
The STA and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, logistics waybills, item-level e-commerce transaction data, VAT invoices, and foreign exchange collection records. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day window without a justified extension, or if the documentation is false, incomplete, or inconsistent, the zero-rating is revoked; the export sale is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply and assessed to 13% VAT; any refunded input VAT is clawed back; late payment surcharges are applied at 0.05% per day; and under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law the taxpayer may be subject to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Intentional misrepresentation or forged export documentation constitutes fraudulent export tax refund under PRC criminal law, carrying criminal confiscation, fines, and imprisonment for responsible individuals, in addition to the loss of the export rebate certification and retrospective tax assessment without the ordinary time limitation.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime in which the renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods, cross-border e-commerce settlement, and approved dividend remittances, while the capital account remains subject to strict SAFE control, registration, and purpose-based verification. Since the 2012 reform, SAFE abolished the old mandatory surrender and export proceeds collection system, so a WFOE may lawfully retain export and e-commerce receipts in a foreign currency settlement account or convert them into RMB with a designated bank, provided the funds are repatriated into China through an approved banking channel and reported to SAFE under the trade and income verification system. Inward foreign direct investment equity contributions must be registered with SAFE after the incorporated WFOE obtains its business licence and Foreign Investor Establishment Joint Registration certificate, and the resulting funds are held in a dedicated foreign exchange capital account. Profit remittance by a WFOE is permitted under the current account but requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance certificates, bank payment forms, board resolutions approving the profit distribution, and tax withholding vouchers; the remitting bank conducts a genuine-trade and look-through authenticity review. Capital account items such as cross-border intercompany loans, equity pledge, debt-to-equity swaps, or early payback of shareholders' capital are not freely convertible and require separate SAFE approval. Consequently, the treasury must map every capital flow to an official transaction purpose code and maintain a complete evidentiary chain for both the banking relationship and the SAFE electronic reporting file.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export and cross-border e-commerce receipts from genuine trade must be repatriated into mainland China through a designated bank within the approved settlement cycle, generally no later than 90 days from the date of the relevant customs export declaration or delivery confirmation, subject to SAFE verification and bank reconciliation. There is currently no mandatory fixed percentage surrender requirement; a WFOE may retain 100% of eligible foreign exchange receipts in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB at the prevailing spot rate, provided the underlying customs declarations, logistics documents, order data, and consumer transaction records are made available to the bank for authenticity review.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and equivalent international electronic money institutions are not licensed as deposit-taking banks in mainland China and cannot legally offer local account services as a substitute for a Chinese licensed bank. They may be used only as auxiliary collection or payout rails for an overseas customer-facing e-commerce channel, but the WFOE must still maintain a domestic Tier-1 bank account for SAFE filing, foreign exchange settlement, tax processing, and capital verification. Mainland banks generally reject or heavily restrict transfers arriving from unlicensed offshore EMIs when the beneficiary is a WFOE, because the source of funds lacks a robust trade/purpose code trail. The low-to-medium viability of fintech EMIs is therefore limited to outbound collection support for COD sales generated by the e-commerce platform, while the legal corporate treasury remains onshore with a domestic licensed bank and a SAFE-registered foreign exchange account.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain traced to offshore trusts, nominee arrangements, or opaque shell holding entities that prevent the bank from identifying the natural persons controlling the WFOE.
        
Root cause: Ultimate beneficial owner (UBO) chain traced to offshore trusts, nominee arrangements, or opaque shell holding entities that prevent the bank from identifying the natural persons controlling the WFOE.

        
Defense evidence: Provide a complete beneficial ownership register on the bank's KYC form, notarised certificates of incorporation and certificates of incumbency for each shareholder, trust deeds or nominee declarations, passport copies of every natural-person UBO holding 10% or more, and an organisational chart showing direct and indirect shareholding percentages. If any UBO appears on a sanctions or politically exposed persons (PEP) database, provide citizenship and tax residency evidence, a detailed source-of-wealth statement, and negative media checks.

      

      
      

        Heavy volumes of small-value cash-on-delivery (COD) receipts collected through logistics companies in fragmented amounts, which can be misread as structuring, money movement from unlicensed money transmitters, or smurfing transactions.
        
Root cause: Heavy volumes of small-value cash-on-delivery (COD) receipts collected through logistics companies in fragmented amounts, which can be misread as structuring, money movement from unlicensed money transmitters, or smurfing transactions.

        
Defense evidence: Submit certified COD settlement reports from the logistics carrier, the signed agency collection agreement between the WFOE and the logistics provider, monthly bank and remittance reconciliations, an order-level e-commerce ledger containing order ID, tracking number, delivery PIN/confirmation, and customer signature or digital acknowledgement, plus the customs declarations and product descriptions for the underlying low-value goods. Also provide the company's internal AML policy and a commitment to quarterly transaction trend reporting and adverse-media screening.

      

      
      

        Trade-based money laundering indicators from inconsistent counterparty remitters, product description mismatches, inflated or underpriced goods, circular flows between related e-commerce sellers, or rapid pass-through settlement with no operational substance.
        
Root cause: Trade-based money laundering indicators from inconsistent counterparty remitters, product description mismatches, inflated or underpriced goods, circular flows between related e-commerce sellers, or rapid pass-through settlement with no operational substance.

        
Defense evidence: Assemble original commercial contracts and purchase orders, commercial invoices, air waybills or bills of lading, customs electronic clearance records, third-party inspection certificates, independent price benchmarking evidence for the specific product category, and identity documentation for all major suppliers and logistics partners. Submit a written narrative explaining any circular or routed transfer, and provide supplier bank-confirmation evidence to prove the counterparty actually holds the designated account.

      

      
      

        Capital injection from the foreign parent or shareholder funded by intra-group loans, undistributed earnings in a non-operating entity, crypto-asset liquidation proceeds, or unexplained large deposits inconsistent with the investor's operating revenue.
        
Root cause: Capital injection from the foreign parent or shareholder funded by intra-group loans, undistributed earnings in a non-operating entity, crypto-asset liquidation proceeds, or unexplained large deposits inconsistent with the investor's operating revenue.

        
Defense evidence: Deliver a source-of-funds package for the investing entity comprising 12 months of bank statements, audited financial statements, the board profit-distribution resolution or loan agreement, corporate registry extracts, and a lawful-funds declaration. The remittance route must flow through recognised international banks and exclude unlicensed digital-asset platforms. A Chinese licensed certified public accountant must prepare and issue the capital verification report after funds are received, confirming the paid-in capital and compliance with the subscribed registered capital schedule.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound foreign direct investment must be remitted from the parent company via SWIFT MT103 or an equivalent high-value wire into the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must carry the official SAFE/BOP transaction purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise equity contribution, commonly referenced as FDI capital injection or 'investment equity capital', and the bank will automatically update the SAFE system via the FDI receipt confirmation module. After the bank confirms the credit, the WFOE may convert the foreign currency into RMB at the market spot rate or schedule an FX trading execution, subject to the bank's entity transaction review. The converted RMB must be placed in the domestic capital account and used strictly within the WFOE's approved business scope; use for entrusted loans, repayment of cross-border borrowings, or domestic securities investment is prohibited without a separate SAFE approval. A local CPA then issues a capital verification report, and the shareholder funds must be fully settled before the company can easily remit phantom profits or dividends.For outbound trade settlements to overseas suppliers, logistics providers, and marketing partners, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 wires for small-value transactions, documentary collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) for medium-risk bilateral trade, and commercial letters of credit (sight or usance L/C) for high-value or repeat procurements requiring protection. Chinese banks process same/next-day RMB disbursements through CIPS or the CNAPS clearing system for RMB payment instructions, and foreign currency disbursements clear through SWIFT or global correspondent networks. Every outward disbursement requires the presenting bank to receive the underlying trade contract, invoice, transport document, customs declaration where relevant, and e-commerce/COD reconciliation report; the bank will map the payment to the proper business/trade purpose code before processing. Domestic settlement to logistics carriers and COD collection agents is executed through RMB interbank clearing and must be accompanied by a merchant payment service agreement and periodic reconciliation.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, and equivalent international electronic money institutions are not licensed as deposit-taking banks in mainland China and cannot legally offer local account services as a substitute for a Chinese licensed bank. They may be used only as auxiliary collection or payout rails for an overseas customer-facing e-commerce channel, but the WFOE must still maintain a domestic Tier-1 bank account for SAFE filing, foreign exchange settlement, tax processing, and capital verification. Mainland banks generally reject or heavily restrict transfers arriving from unlicensed offshore EMIs when the beneficiary is a WFOE, because the source of funds lacks a robust trade/purpose code trail. The low-to-medium viability of fintech EMIs is therefore limited to outbound collection support for COD sales generated by the e-commerce platform, while the legal corporate treasury remains onshore with a domestic licensed bank and a SAFE-registered foreign exchange account.

Master / Local File threshold: Local File transfer pricing documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed any of the following thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property, service transactions or financial asset transactions; and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transaction declaration form must be filed with the annual CIT return whenever controlled transactions exist.

      
Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations filing, but the tax authorities apply the economic substance doctrine through the beneficial ownership test, the place of effective management concept, and the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. A WFOE engaged in COD e-commerce must demonstrate local warehouse, logistics control, inventory procurement, cash collection oversight, customer support and decision-making. Core Income-Generating Activities are assessed by reference to where contracts are negotiated, where operational decisions are made, where personnel are located and where risks are assumed. If the WFOE is merely a booking vehicle with a foreign parent conducting all activities through remote payment rails, treaty benefits will be denied and the profit will be reattributed to the actual business functions in China.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Understatement of COD Revenue
        
Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes back collection of the evaded tax, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and a fine of 50% to five times the evaded tax. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law applies where the concealed tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the payable tax, with imprisonment up to seven years, criminal fines and confiscation of assets.

        
Director personal liability: A director or controlling shareholder who directed, assisted or benefited from the concealment may have the corporate veil pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law when the entity is operated as a sham or used for fraud, resulting in personal civil liability for the unpaid tax and penalties. The personal bank assets may be frozen, exit restrictions and a blacklisting travel ban may be imposed under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and criminal charges may lead to personal imprisonment and the seizure of personal property.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Shopping
        
Statutory sanction: The tax authority may invoke the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the implementing regulations to recharacterise the structure, disallow incurred deductions, adjust taxable income to the substance-owning party, and impose interest and administrative penalties. Treaty benefits are denied under the beneficial ownership doctrine, and the enterprise is subject to full statutory withholding taxes and transfer pricing penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who signed false intercompany contracts, created artificial board minutes or knowingly engaged in treaty shopping can be personally liable for the resulting tax adjustments. If the WFOE is insolvent or has been liquidated, the tax authority may pierce the corporate veil and seek restitution from directors and shareholders who received misappropriated profits. The relevant persons are also subject to travel restrictions, bank account freezes and blacklisting. If tax evasion is found to be deliberate, criminal imprisonment applies to the responsible director personally.

      

      
      

        AML / Cash-on-Delivery Cross-Border Remittance Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, serious violations expose the institution to administrative fines of up to RMB 20 million, suspension of business or revocation of licences. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose penalties of 30% to 100% of the illegal cross-border settlement amount. The tax authority may additionally apply Article 63 penalties on the undisclosed income underlying the offshore remittances.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, supervisors and the actual AML compliance officer face personal fines and prohibition from holding corporate management positions. Where the transfer of funds knowing that they derive from illegal commercial conduct constitutes laundering, Article 191 of the Criminal Law imposes imprisonment up to ten years and fines. Personal travel bans, asset seizure and blacklisting may be imposed in administrative enforcement, and directors who direct or knowingly facilitate the scheme bear civil joint and several liability for the shortfall.
