Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A shell virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address is not permissible for a WFOE in mainland China. SAMR requires a definite, verifiable registered address within the administrative district, and the tax authority requires an actual place and effective business contact for invoice issuance, payroll, social insurance, and inspection. A shared-service or clustered registered address may be accepted in some free-trade zones and approved incubation zones, but the company must still maintain physical staff, business records, and communications at that address or a declared operational subsidiary site. For this B2B cross-border e-commerce platform, payment banks and Alipay/Pay-related onboarding will reject a mailbox-only address; at minimum a rent-backed office or licensed co-working space with lease agreements, utility invoices, and local management access is required.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise under current Chinese law. The registered capital amount, currency and contribution schedule are set by the shareholders in the Articles of Association and must be reasonably consistent with the business scope. There is no blocked escrow account requirement. The declared capital must be actually paid under the agreed schedule and the amount may not be removed or withdrawn except under lawful capital reduction or dissolution procedures.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau (电子税务局) portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must first complete commercial incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN). Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration at the competent local tax authority through the Electronic Tax Bureau, binding the USCC to tax filings. The applicant must submit the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, and proof of registered address. Upon activation, the taxpayer obtains an electronic tax account, VAT taxpayer qualification (general or small-scale taxpayer), and the right to issue electronic invoices through the STA-approved digital invoice system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods applies when the WFOE exports physical goods from China customs territory under an export sales contract, completes the electronic customs export declaration (报关单), and obtains customs release records. For services, zero-rated cross-border supply applies to services sold to non-resident recipients where the services are rendered entirely outside China or where the recipient uses them outside China; B2B marketplace facilitation services satisfying these conditions qualify. The taxpayer must claim the zero rate on the VAT return, file an export tax refund declaration, and retain complete supporting documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (报关单) with electronic release message
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) issued by a licensed carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (出口收汇核销单) or bank statement evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the offshore buyer
  • Special VAT invoice for export (if issued under the export taxation rules)

China mandates full digitalized electronic invoicing for registered enterprises under the Shanghai and nationwide Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (全电发票) reform. Taxpayers must integrate business systems with the STA e-invoice platform via API or web-based services for real-time issuance, transmission, and archiving. The Golden Tax System IV (金税四期) performs central real-time verification of invoice data, transaction matching, and anti-fraud risk analytics. Issuance is not subject to pre-printing fiscal clearance, but all invoice data must synchronize with the STA within the statutory reporting cycle; non-compliance triggers automated blocking of issuance rights.

The STA and General Administration of Customs may jointly audit export zero-rating claims for up to five or more years retrospectively. Any unsubstantiated, fraudulent, or non-repatriated export transaction triggers recovery of the refunded VAT amount, late payment surcharge, and monetary penalties ranging from 50% to five times the refunded sum under the Tax Administration Law. Failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days may invalidate the zero-rating and lead to clawback, customs fines, downgrade of customs credit rating, suspension of export tax refund qualification, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent refunds under Chinese Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility for trade and services, allowing WFOE treasury operations to buy and sell foreign currency through designated banks at market rates once the underlying transaction is verified. The capital account, however, remains strictly controlled and requires ex ante SAFE or bank-delegated registration for inbound FDI, offshore lending, and foreign-currency loans. Export proceeds are repatriated and can be retained in a foreign-exchange account or settled into RMB without a forced conversion ratio, but banks must electronically match the funds against customs declarations and commercial invoices. Profit remittance by a WFOE is treated as current-account income only after tax: the bank must see an audited statutory financial statement, corporate profit distribution resolution, tax clearance certificate, and a statutory audit opinion confirming realized profits. Once converted capital is in RMB, the WFOE cannot invest it in entrusted loans, securities, or non-operating real estate unless specifically permitted; every cross-border flow must be tagged with a valid purpose code and reconciled with the enterprise's actual business scope. For a B2B marketplace, receipts from overseas merchants require careful segregation between the platform's own settlement income and agency collection flows to avoid AML layering concerns.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to Mainland China and registered through the bank's trade monitoring system; the standard verification and surrender window is 180 days from collection or customs declaration. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero, so a WFOE may retain 100 percent of verified export proceeds in a domestic foreign-exchange account or sell them to a designated bank for RMB at market rates, subject to proof of actual trade background and customs data matching.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange control regulator and licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Not viable for domestic Mainland treasury. A WFOE cannot rely on offshore fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Wise Business, or Airwallex as its primary clearing bank because they lack PBOC/SAFE licenses to open RMB or foreign-currency settlement accounts inside China and are legally barred from being the direct banking interface for a PRC entity. Such EMIs may only support the offshore parent side of the structure, routing verified capital into China through a licensed bank; they cannot clear Alipay-settled marketplace proceeds or execute SAFE-regulated FX transactions on behalf of the WFOE. The sole compliant structure is a wholly domestic tier-1 commercial bank account, complemented by the platform's payment institution license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore parent must wire the FDI capital contribution by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign-exchange capital account at a licensed PRC bank. The remittance message must carry the SAFE-recognized purpose code for inbound direct investment capital contribution and reference the FDI registration or Foreign Investment Basic Information Report filed for the entity. The receiving bank verifies the capital against the business license capital scope, board resolution, and FDI file, then records the flow in SAFE's cross-border foreign-currency reporting system. Capital held in the escrow capital account is quarantined until a licensed PRC certified public accountant issues a capital verification opinion confirming the injection has been received and booked; only then may the foreign currency be settled into RMB or used for approved operating needs. Settlement to the RMB basic account must preserve the audit trail of the original purpose code and is not available for entrusted loans, equity investment, securities transactions, or loans to non-affiliated third parties.For supplier payments and paid-out merchant balances under the B2B marketplace, banks process SWIFT MT103 messages for low-value current-account payments, commercial letters of credit for high-value physical goods, and documentary collections when documents are time-staged. Every outbound remittance must be preceded by the underlying trade contract, customs declaration where goods move cross-border, tax invoices, and a SAFE-compliant purpose code. RMB cross-border flows may clear through CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) or the applicable RTGS rail, while foreign-currency flows clear through SWIFT correspondent banking. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: China imposes a universal audit mandate under the PRC Company Law: every limited liability company, including a WFOE, must have its annual financial statements audited by an independent licensed CPA firm. There are no balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption thresholds for ordinary WFOEs; the audit obligation is triggered by simple status as a company. Financial institutions, listed issuers, and platform companies designated by regulators may be subject to additional asset, transaction-volume, or risk-based audit and assurance requirements, but the baseline is non-quantitative and mandatory for all companies.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividend distributions made by the Chinese WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder; treaty relief can reduce this to 5% or 10% depending on the treaty and whether the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the capital, with many bilateral DTA protocols.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly provisional CIT returns and payments are due within 15 days after each calendar quarter-end; the final annual CIT return, supporting schedules, and any balance payment are due no later than 31 May of the following year; annual tax settlement must reconcile the four provisional payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under PRC SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42, a Local File is mandatory if any one of the following related-party transaction thresholds is exceeded in a tax year: tangible asset transfers over RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers over RMB 100 million; financial-asset transfers over RMB 100 million; related-party financing interest over RMB 10 million; or related-party service transactions over RMB 40 million. A Master File is generally required where the multinational group had consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5 billion in the prior tax year and the Chinese entity is engaged in related-party transactions; Country-by-Country reports may separately be triggered by group revenue thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulation filing system in the Gulf-Council style, but economic substance is enforced through the Enterprise Income Tax Law general anti-avoidance rule under Article 47, the reasonable commercial purpose doctrine, and special tax adjustment rules. For the B2B marketplace WFOE, core income-generating activities must be performed in China, including seller onboarding and due diligence, contract negotiation, platform technology and algorithm control, price and promotion decisions, risk assumption, customer and seller support, payment settlement, and logistics quality control. If these functions are merely booked offshore or performed by shell entities, the Chinese tax authority may reattribute profit to the WFOE, deny intercompany deductions, and reject the non-resident shareholder's treaty benefits under the principal-purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Fraud and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 44 authorize the tax bureau to make an arm's-length recharacterization and transfer-pricing adjustment, with interest calculated at the prevailing benchmark rate plus up to five additional percentage points; failure to file or maintain TP documentation is separately punishable under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 62 (RMB 2,000-10,000) and Article 64 (50%-5x of the underpaid tax where false accounting causes underassessment).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 where a director or controlling shareholder abuses the limited liability structure to evade tax; the responsible director and actual controller become jointly and severally liable for the tax loss. If the conduct is intentional and concealing, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes fines and imprisonment of up to seven years, and the legal representative is subjected to a departure ban, asset freezing, and inclusion in the State Taxation Administration's major tax violation blacklist.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Profit Remittances

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 requires the withholding agent to remit the tax that should have been withheld and imposes a fine of 50% to three times the amount not withheld; if the underpayment also results from a false return, Article 63 applies with a fine of 50% to five times the underpaid tax. Repeated refusal to pay may trigger enforcement against the company's bank accounts and a blacklisting order.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve dividend declarations without arranging WHT become personally implicated; the legal representative may be blocked from leaving China under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 until the full tax, surcharge, and penalties are paid. Willful concealment is treated as criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, exposing the responsible officer to personal fines, imprisonment of up to seven years, asset restitution, and police/immigration control.
Shell Entity and Lack of Economic Substance with False Preferential Claim

Statutory sanction: The Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 (general anti-avoidance rule) and Article 48 permit the tax authority to recharacterize the arrangement, recompute taxable income, deny the preferential rate, and impose benchmark-based interest plus an additional adjustment of five percentage points; a false claim of a tax preference is a fraudulent reduction of tax liability under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, attracting a fine of 50% to five times the underpaid tax plus daily late surcharge.

Director personal liability: The courts and tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil and treat the WFOE as the alter ego of the foreign parent if it is used purely for tax evasion; shareholders, directors, and actual controllers are jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 applies where false registration, false books, and fraudulent treaty abuse are proven, with imprisonment of up to seven years, fines, confiscation of tax evasion gains, blacklisting, and exit restrictions.
