Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise under the Company Law or the Foreign Investment Law. Under Article 47 of the Company Law (2023), the registered capital must be fully subscribed by shareholders within five years after establishment unless a separate law, administrative regulation or State Council decision prescribes otherwise. Foreign shareholders may contribute in freely convertible foreign currency or RMB, and funds must be remitted into a designated capital foreign exchange settlement account opened with a SAFE-authorized bank. There is no general blocked escrow account requirement; a capital escrow arrangement is triggered only for licensed financial or quasi-financial sectors. A mandatory capital verification report is not required for ordinary WFOE establishment registration.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety and Civil Defense: For the leased warehouse and office, the operator must obtain a fire safety inspection record or certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade covering fire separation, exit capacity, automatic fire alarm and sprinkler requirements if floor area thresholds are triggered, portable extinguisher placement, emergency lighting and smoking prohibition. A civil defense air-raid shelter obligation applies only to newly constructed buildings in designated urban areas and is not triggered by a tenancy in an existing building. Occupational Clearance: The local Emergency Management Bureau must be notified of the workplace, a production safety responsibility system and emergency plan must be filed, employees must receive work-safety training for material-handling equipment, forklift licenses must be obtained for powered industrial trucks, and the warehouse structure must be certified by the landlord or a qualified inspection unit to bear storage rack loads. If any worker may be exposed to hazardous substances, an occupational disease hazard registration with the local National Health Commission bureau is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic filing and registration via the STA Electronic Tax Hall (e-Tax Hall) — the sole authorized enterprise tax registration portal in Mainland China.

E-government filing portal

A WFOE qualified as a limited liability enterprise must first be incorporated with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), which issues a business license containing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) serving as the national Tax Identification Number. Immediately after incorporation and in all circumstances within 30 calendar days, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent local STA office through the e-Tax Hall, providing the USCC, articles of association, legal representative and financial officer identity materials, and registered address proof. After verification, the STA activates the taxpayer account, assigns a taxpayer type (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), and links the VAT registration to the same USCC. Foreign ownership does not alter the procedure; because China is a unitary jurisdiction, no separate state or municipal tax registration is required.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No financial turnover threshold triggers tax registration itself; every incorporated enterprise must obtain tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance. For VAT general taxpayer classification, a taxpayer mainly selling goods must have annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000. Below that threshold, the default is small-scale taxpayer treatment unless the entity elects general taxpayer status to charge VAT at 13% and claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For subscription box goods physically exported from China, the VAT rate is 0% if: (i) the goods are declared for export under a valid customs export declaration; (ii) the sale is made to an overseas buyer and the goods actually leave Mainland China through a designated customs port; (iii) export proceeds are repatriated through bank channels in accordance with foreign exchange regulations; and (iv) the exporter completes the export tax refund/exemption filing within the prescribed statutory period. The WFOE must maintain full evidence of the underlying supply contract, payment, customs release, and physical movement of goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing physical export and destination
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (FIRC) evidencing receipt of export consideration in foreign currency

China uses a nationwide VAT invoice system (fapiao) integrated with the STA Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Electronic special and standard VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-authorized e-invoice service platform; invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in real time at issuance. General taxpayers must use tax-control digital encryption equipment or the all-digital fully electronic e-invoice system to generate invoices. There is no per-transaction fiscal stamping mandate, but issued invoice data must be reported and reconciled generally before the 15th of the following month. The all-digital fully electronic invoice system is being rolled out nationwide, requiring API-based integration or taxpayer portal issuance.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized under STA Golden Tax audits and joint customs-STA verification. If a zero-rating or export refund claim is found to be false, incomplete, or fraudulently substantiated, the STA will claw back all previously refunded input VAT plus statutory late-payment surcharge, impose a fine of up to five times the fraudulent refund or unverified credit under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection, suspend or revoke the taxpayer's export tax refund eligibility, and refer criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refunds under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, carrying imprisonment and fines depending on the amount involved. Retrospective assessment can reach back the full limitation period where fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system and maintains strict but increasingly streamlined foreign-exchange administration. Current-account transactions, including trade in goods, are broadly convertible upon truthful verification, while capital-account transactions remain approval- or registration-controlled. A WFOE is able to receive and pay foreign currency on the current account for its subscription-box import/export needs, but each payment must be supported by contract, invoice, customs declaration and a licensed domestic bank acting as the settlement bank. Capital inflows, such as registered capital from the overseas shareholder, require an FDI registration with SAFE, and the funds must enter a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account. Profit and dividend remittance to the foreign parent is permissible after annual audit, tax settlement, and presentation of financial statements; however, banks will independently review legitimacy and may request tax payment certificates. The RMB exchange rate is determined via the onshore interbank market with a daily fixing and a +/-2% daily margin for spot transactions against the US dollar. There is no full free-floating parity, and offshore RMB positions do not automatically convert onshore; any arbitrage between onshore and offshore CNH is regulated. The practical effect is that WFOE cash flows are observable to SAFE and the bank, and treasury policy must preserve source documents, maintain trade-based justification, and file statutory foreign-exchange declarations before cross-border transfers can clear. Capital convertibility is not automatic: even a legitimately earned profit must go through the bank's verification of audited accounts, reserve draws, and tax clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from subscription-box sales to overseas customers must be repatriated into Mainland China and received in a domestic bank account, generally within 180 days after the customs export declaration or the contractual payment due date unless a prior SAFE-approved deferral is obtained. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the statutory regime does not require the WFOE to sell export foreign currency for RMB. The WFOE may either retain the foreign currency in its current-account foreign-exchange account or convert it at the interbank market rate through its designated bank. All export receipts must be matched to customs declarations through SAFE's trade in goods verification system, with discrepancies, advance receipts and late collections reported within the bank's prescribed time line and, where necessary, through the digital foreign-exchange administration platform.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms, including global card acquirers such as Stripe and Authorize.Net, are not viable as the primary treasury settlement channel for a Mainland China WFOE. They may be used only as front-end merchant gateways for collecting card payments from non-Chinese consumers, but the resulting funds cannot be held in an onshore WFOE bank account until cleared through a regulated cross-border acquirer/remittance arrangement. Without a local branch license or a PBOC/SAFE-approved cross-border payment licence, an offshore EMI cannot perform RMB-denominated FX conversion, open domestic settlement accounts, or undertake customer KYC and capital-verification obligations imposed on licensed Chinese banks. The viable structure is to keep Stripe/Authorize.Net as the e-commerce collection layer outside China and route net settlements through an international bank to the WFOE's designated domestic Bank of China or ICBC account using a compliant cross-border foreign-exchange settlement service such as bank's E-commerce Cross-Border Collection, with each remittance labelled as export proceeds and matched to underlying orders and customs records. Overall feasibility rating: Low for EMI-led banking; acceptable only when the EMI is a card acquirer and the actual settlement bank remains a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas shareholder must first complete the SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration through the local branch and obtain an FDI code/foreign-exchange control notice before remitting capital. The injection should be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated foreign-exchange capital account at the Tier-1 bank, using a clear payment narrative such as 'registered capital contribution to [WFOE name] under FDI registration [reference]'. The bank will rely on the SAFE digital foreign-exchange platform to verify the capital amount, the shareholder identity, and the business scope; no surplus above the registered capital amount may be received unless an amendment of registration is filed. Upon receipt, the bank performs capital verification and records the inflow; the WFOE must then use the funds under the approved current-account purpose, preserve underlying vouchers, and where conversion to RMB is required, settle through the onshore interbank rate with the same bank. Funds should not be commingled with unrelated operational receipts, and all transfers must be traceable from the remitter's bank to the final utilization account.For routine supplier payments and service fees, use SWIFT MT103 wires through the onshore bank after submission of contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration (where import/export movement is involved) and tax payment certificate. For larger manufacturing or cross-border procurement of subscription-box inventory, open a Commercial Letter of Credit through the Tier-1 bank, as it provides documentary control and protects advance-payment risk. For established suppliers with low risk, Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) are available and cheaper, but they offer no payment guarantee and should be reserved for non-critical purchases. RMB-denominated settlements with Chinese suppliers should clear through the domestic bulk electronic payment system (HVPS) or CIPS for cross-border RMB supply-chain payments. All outbound disbursements must be consistent with the approved foreign-exchange current-account purpose codes, and the bank will require a clear statement that each payment is bona fide, trade-related and not a disguised capital outflow or tax evasion.Onshore deliverable RMB/foreign-currency forward contracts executed with a licensed Chinese commercial bank against a real current-account trade exposure, including subscription-box export receivables and import payables, supported by commercial contracts and customs records.Spot FX swaps and simple FX forwards used only to manage short-term cash-flow mismatches in the WFOE's cross-border current-account receipts and payments, subject to bank credit limits and SAFE periodic reporting.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) for long-term hedge of shareholder loan or FDI capital-account exposures, but only where a genuine debt/investment contract exists and SAFE registration/approval has been obtained; unapproved CCS on non-trade positions is not permitted.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not statutorily permissible onshore for this WFOE as a speculative hedging instrument; any offshore NDF booked by the foreign parent for itself is outside the WFOE's onshore banking perimeter and must not be linked to its onshore account.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms, including global card acquirers such as Stripe and Authorize.Net, are not viable as the primary treasury settlement channel for a Mainland China WFOE. They may be used only as front-end merchant gateways for collecting card payments from non-Chinese consumers, but the resulting funds cannot be held in an onshore WFOE bank account until cleared through a regulated cross-border acquirer/remittance arrangement. Without a local branch license or a PBOC/SAFE-approved cross-border payment licence, an offshore EMI cannot perform RMB-denominated FX conversion, open domestic settlement accounts, or undertake customer KYC and capital-verification obligations imposed on licensed Chinese banks. The viable structure is to keep Stripe/Authorize.Net as the e-commerce collection layer outside China and route net settlements through an international bank to the WFOE's designated domestic Bank of China or ICBC account using a compliant cross-border foreign-exchange settlement service such as bank's E-commerce Cross-Border Collection, with each remittance labelled as export proceeds and matched to underlying orders and customs records. Overall feasibility rating: Low for EMI-led banking; acceptable only when the EMI is a card acquirer and the actual settlement bank remains a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016-42, a Chinese entity must prepare a Local File for related-party transactions when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, related-party services exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is mandatory when the consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion in the prior fiscal year. The annual related-party transaction disclosure form (Table G100000) is attached to the CIT return and must list all non-resident related parties.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute like the UAE; however, the substance-over-form principle and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations require that the WFOE realize the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the subscription box business in China—including inventory ownership, curation, marketing decision-making, and customer contract execution—rather than acting as a shell that merely holds invoices and routes profits to an offshore affiliate. Substantial economic presence is required to claim treaty benefits and to resist recharacterization of intercompany arrangements. The State Administration of Taxation may apply the CIGA standard to deny treaty relief to any entity lacking actual employees, assets, and business risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will make an arm's-length transfer pricing adjustment under Article 41 of the EIT Law, resulting in additional CIT of 25% on the reallocated profit plus a 5% per quarter interest surcharge on the underpaid tax under Article 121 of the Implementation Regulations. If the adjustment is deemed negligence due to improper documentation, a penalty of up to RMB 5,000; if intentional evasion detected, a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Under Article 20 of the Company Law, the corporate veil is pierced where a director uses the WFOE to commit fraud at the expense of the state tax authorities; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the underpaid CIT, penalties, and interest, with personal asset attachment and freezing. The tax authority can obtain a No-Leave Notice (exit denial) against the legal representative until full restitution. If the understatement exceeds RMB 50,000 per taxpayer or 10% of taxable income, the director may face criminal proceedings under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for Tax Evasion, punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years for moderate evasion and 3 to 7 years plus fines where the amount is huge and accounts for more than 30% of reported income.
Disguised Dividend Repatriation Through Royalty and Interest Payments

Statutory sanction: The tax authority recharacterizes the payments as dividends under substance-over-form and the GAAR (Article 47 EIT Law). Dividend withholding at 10% is imposed on the disguised distribution, and the deductible status is denied, causing an additional 25% CIT. A 50% to 5 times penalty is applied on the under-declared withholding tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and late surcharges at 0.05% per day accrue.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the sham royalty or finance agreements are jointly liable for the full unpaid withholding tax and penalties, with their personal assets subject to attachment through a court order obtained by the tax authority. The representative director may be placed on the National Tax Offender Blacklist, receiving a travel ban and prohibition from re-entering China until the debt is cleared. If the fraudulent invoicing for royalties exceeds RMB 1 million and causes tax losses exceeding RMB 100,000, directors face criminal penalty under the False VAT Invoice provisions plus tax evasion imprisonment of 3 to 7 years.
Failure to File Annual CIT Return and Related-Party Disclosure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority imposes a RMB 2,000 to RMB 20,000 late-filing penalty plus a daily interest surcharge of 0.05% on unpaid tax from the due date. Failure to submit transfer pricing documents triggers a penalty of RMB 10,000 to RMB 20,000 and an assessment under the unreasonable-income power in Article 115 of the Implementation Regulations, allowing deemed taxation based on a reasonable industry margin (typically 10% to 15% of turnover). The unfiled WFOE is blacklisted within the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 bad-debt mechanism; its bank assets are seized for unpaid liabilities.

Director personal liability: The use of the WFOE as a pure shell without filing and with diverted profits permits the tax authorities to pierce the corporate veil under the principle of abuse of legal personality, imposing unlimited personal liability on the sole director and shareholder for all tax liabilities, surcharge, and penalties. The director acquires a statutory non-exit status, is barred from leaving China, and may be summoned for criminal liability under Article 203 of the Criminal Law for Refusal to Pay Tax, with imprisonment of 3 to 7 years where the amount is large. Personal civil assets, including offshore bank accounts traced from the subscription proceeds, are subject to confiscation in the enforcement proceeding.
