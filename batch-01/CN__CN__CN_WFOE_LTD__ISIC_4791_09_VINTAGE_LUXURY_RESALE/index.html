Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. PRC company law imposes no minimum registered capital or mandatory blocking escrow for WFOEs in the online resale sector. The shareholder may fix authorized and subscribed capital in the Articles of Association. Paid-in capital must be remitted into the entity's bank account in RMB; contribution schedules, if any, should be completed within the timeframe stated in the Articles and disclosed through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System. Real investments must be reported to SAFE and SAMR; capital cannot be released for non-operating use or shareholder loans outside foreign-exchange regulations. For vintage luxury resale, no statutory escrow-account requirement applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal is the nationwide unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) system.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_WFOE_LTD, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the SAMR business license. The WFOE must complete tax registration in the STA e-Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days after formation: login using the electronic business license, complete real-name authentication of the legal representative, file registered capital, actual business scope, accounting and bank information, and activate invoice issuance and export tax rebate rights. A true foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment is not granted the resident USCC; it must either appoint the domestic purchaser/WFOE as withholding agent for China VAT and income tax or, where the foreign entity itself has taxable activities in China, apply to the local STA office for a non-resident tax identification code using the foreign registration certificate, a Chinese tax process agent authorization, and bank confirmation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT tax registration is mandatory from the commencement of taxable business; there is no turnover-based VAT registration exemption. Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. Current small-scale taxpayer relief exempts monthly taxable sales up to RMB 100,000 (quarterly RMB 300,000) from VAT, but that is an exemption threshold, not a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of consigned and secondhand luxury goods qualifies for the PRC 0% export VAT rate only when the WFOE is a VAT-registered exporter in good standing, the goods are physically cleared outward through GACC customs supervision, the export sale is supported by genuine order and title-transfer documentation, the goods actually leave the PRC customs territory within the declared period, and the corresponding foreign exchange export proceeds are repatriated through a licensed bank. Export declarations made under cross-border e-commerce retail export models are eligible if the platform order data and customs supervision serial are reported through the China International Trade Single Window and the STA e-Tax Bureau. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only if they are expressly listed in the STA cross-border zero-rated services catalogue; an online resale or consignment platform service itself is not automatically zero-rated merely because the buyer is overseas.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) released by GACC through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or multimodal transport document proving physical exit of goods from PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or licensed bank foreign exchange receipt records proving collection of export consideration in foreign currency
  • Statutory commercial invoice and signed packing list
  • Export sales contract, consignment order or platform order evidence
  • VAT special invoice or Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice issued through the FDEI-Golden Tax IV system for the export sale
  • Customs declaration packing list describing the secondhand luxury goods, serial numbers, values and quantities
  • Cross-border e-commerce transaction data log or customs supervision serial if cleared under the e-commerce retail export model

The STA requires all VAT invoices, including export sales invoices, to be issued through the national Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV real-time fiscal clearing infrastructure. Each valid invoice carries a tax-control matrix code, encryption digital signature and is transmitted in near real time to the STA e-Tax Bureau; invoice numbers cannot be generated outside the fiscal platform. Enterprises integrate ERP systems through STA-approved digital invoice service providers and API gateways, and export zero-rating declarations are cross-validated before refund release.

Export zero-rating claims are automatically matched by Golden Tax Phase IV against GACC customs data and bank FX receipt data. Failure to repatriate export proceeds before the prescribed deadline, or submission of false invoices, false customs declarations, forged FIRC receipts or circular foreign-exchange evidence, triggers immediate refund suspension, retrospective clawback of the VAT refunded, late-payment surcharge and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the refunded tax. Cases meeting the criminal threshold are transferred for prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund under the PRC Criminal Law, with imprisonment and monetary penalties for individuals responsible. The clawback can be imposed retroactively within the statutory assessment period, which is extended in cases of bad faith or fraud.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains RMB convertibility for current account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but capital account transactions remain subject to control. A WFOE may use its current account for genuine trade, fees, employee compensation and repatriation of legitimate profits, but each foreign currency payment is examined by the bank on a transaction-by-transaction basis against supporting documents. Foreign exchange for registered capital must be registered with SAFE and settled through a designated foreign currency capital account. Dividend remittances, foreign loan repayments, equity transfers and asset liquidation proceeds require completion of SAFE ODI/FDI registration, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and bank-level verification of prior capital contribution. The WFOE must maintain accurate trade background documentation and customs data; any mismatches may cause delay or rejection. The overall assessment is: operating cash flows are largely convertible, but capital flows are monitored tightly; profit repatriation is permissible only after tax and audit formalities are complete.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency received by the WFOE must be repatriated to a domestic bank account in China through approved banking channels within the SAFE monitoring window, generally within 90 days after customs declaration of the goods, unless SAFE approves offshore retention. There is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio under the current receipts-and-payments monitoring regime; the WFOE can convert into RMB at market rate or keep balances in a foreign currency current account. Every receipt must be matched with customs declaration data and supported by sales contracts/invoices for trade background verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign exchange administration authority under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. China requires non-bank payment institutions to obtain a Payment Business License from the PBOC; offshore fintech EMIs are legally barred from opening domestic CNY accounts or acting as direct clearing banks for a mainland WFOE. Such EMIs may only function as front-end payment gateways when paired with a licensed Chinese bank or a licensed local payment institution. The WFOE must keep its primary treasury and clearing relationship with a domestic tier-1 commercial bank.

Defense evidence: Twelve months of source-of-funds bank statements, source-of-wealth questionnaires for all beneficial owners and material consignors, SWIFT payment trail reports, customs declarations and import/export logistics records, and a detailed cash-flow forecast showing the expected volume of consignment sales.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital from the offshore parent must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank subject to SAFE FDI registration. The remittance instruction must clearly state the purpose code for injection of registered capital and reference the FDI confirmation certificate issued by SAFE. Upon receipt, the bank will settle the foreign currency into RMB within the permitted capital account usage categories; the Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming the contribution. All inbound wires must be traceable from the actual overseas shareholder bank account and must not be commingled with trade proceeds or personal funds.Trade settlements to overseas suppliers, consignors and service providers should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers, commercial letters of credit for larger inventory purchases, and documentary collections such as documents against payment or documents against acceptance for consignment trades. Turnover: Under the PRC Company Law (Article 164/165 of the 2018/2023 revisions) every limited liability company, including every WFOE, must prepare annual financial reports and have them audited by an accounting firm licensed in China; there is no general exemption based on size, industry, or ownership. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's State Administration of Taxation Public Notice [2016] No. 42 requires the contemporaneous Local File where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfer, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfer, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfer, or RMB 40 million for all other related-party transactions; the Master File is required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue reaches RMB 5.5 billion or the enterprise is the designated master-file filer; a Country-by-Country Report filing is triggered at the same RMB 5.5 billion group revenue threshold, and all documentation must be filed with the annual CIT return within the filing year.

Economic substance scope: Although China itself is not an ESR-designated zero-tax jurisdiction, the Chinese tax authorities apply the Economic Substance Doctrine through substance-over-form, the beneficial ownership rule, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, and the Principal Purpose Test under the Multilateral Instrument; the WFOE must demonstrate that the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the vintage luxury resale business — sourcing and authentication, pricing decisions, inventory ownership and risk-bearing, logistics and platform contracting, and final customer acceptance — are performed by locally staffed employees in mainland China with local board decision-making and adequate capital. An arrangement in which the WFOE is a mere invoice-issuing shell while the commercial substance is executed in Hong Kong, Singapore or an offshore affiliate will result in profit reattribution to China, denial of treaty benefits, and potential deemed dividend treatment of manipulated cash flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Loss Creation

Statutory sanction: Administrative penalty of 50% up to five times the underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge under Articles 32 and 63 of the Tax Administration Law; where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents 10% or more of the taxable amount, criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC applies, with fines and confiscation of laundered proceeds.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the WFOE is undercapitalized, commingled with personal assets, or used as a facade for fraud; the legal representative, directors and actual controllers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, fines and civil restitution out of personal assets; the court may order travel and exit bans under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, freeze personal bank accounts, and impose criminal imprisonment of up to seven years for tax evasion, together with personal fines and disqualification from serving as directors of any Chinese company.
Shell Company and Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Special Tax Investigation and Adjustment under Articles 41-48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, permitting recharacterization of intercompany transactions, upward adjustment of Chinese taxable income, and imposition of interest at the one-year benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points from the transaction year; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule may re-determine the whole structure and apply 25% CIT on deemed taxable profits, with penalties for concealment of income.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who signed false intercompany contracts or fraudulent transfer pricing documentation are civilly liable for restitution of the underpaid tax; under the amended Company Law, courts may pierce the veil when corporate assets are insufficient and director conduct caused the abuse, exposing personal assets; criminal liability as accomplices to tax evasion may lead to imprisonment of up to seven years and personal fines; the entity and its directors are blacklisted under the serious tax violation publicity regime, triggering banking restrictions, denial of export rebates, procurement debarment, and exit bans.
Money Laundering and AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, institutions face fines of up to RMB 5 million and responsible individuals face fines of up to RMB 500,000 for AML violations; engaging in money laundering constitutes a criminal offense under Article 191 of the Criminal Law where the proceeds of crime are laundered through commercial transactions, carrying imprisonment of five to ten years and fines for serious circumstances, with all proceeds and instrumentalities confiscated.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives and compliance officers who knowingly facilitated the laundering transactions are subject to criminal prosecution with imprisonment up to ten years, personal fines, and asset confiscation; the corporate veil is pierced so that personal assets answer for forfeiture and civil damages; convicted individuals face travel and exit bans, inclusion on the national integrity blacklist, a permanent bar from holding positions in financial or e-commerce businesses, and disqualification from serving as directors of any Chinese enterprise.
