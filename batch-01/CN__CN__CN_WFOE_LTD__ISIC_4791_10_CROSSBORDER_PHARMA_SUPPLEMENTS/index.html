Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in ordinary retail/e-commerce. The sole shareholder determines registered capital by the Articles of Association; capital contribution must be made in full within five years of license issuance. No blocked escrow account requirement is stated by the Company Law or the Foreign Investment Law for this ISIC 4791 scope. Banking AML/CFT screening and foreign-exchange verification are separate and not capital escrow.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online tax registration and compliance are performed through the unified Electronic Tax Office (e-Tax) platform operated by the provincial tax service bureaus under STA supervision.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identifier is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically assigned at company registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation. Within 30 calendar days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration in the Electronic Tax Office, including verification of legal representative, accounting/finance contact, registered address, capital contributions, business scope, and bank account information. The USCC becomes the TIN/VAT ID and no separate tax number is issued. The WFOE must also file for VAT taxpayer status (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), apply for electronic invoice privileges, and complete customs registration for import/export, plus foreign exchange trade registration with SAFE through its bank. A foreign non-resident entity without an establishment in China is not required to obtain a Chinese TIN for simple passive shareholding; where China-source income arises, the Chinese payor/withholding agent completes non-resident withholding tax registration and pays under the withholding procedure through the Electronic Tax Office.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the customs territory of China, including dietary supplements and nutraceuticals sold online to overseas consumers, are eligible for VAT zero-rating or export exemption/refund, provided the WFOE has valid customs export registration, the goods leave China under a recognized export customs supervision mode, sale proceeds are collected from abroad, and the export is evidenced by customs declaration and logistics records. Qualifying cross-border digital services may also enjoy zero-rating under the VAT zero-rated services rules, but the present ISIC 4791 scope is goods-based e-commerce.

  • Export goods customs declaration form (China Customs export declaration/clearance record generated through the China Single Window)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract (or cross-border e-commerce order record)
  • Bill of lading for sea freight, air waybill for air freight, or courier/postal receipt for express export consignments
  • Foreign exchange collection record: bank export proceeds receipt and SAFE trade-monitoring verification record (export collection verification certificate) evidencing repatriation within the statutory deadline
  • Cross-border e-commerce consumer order and payment data for online retail export channels, linked to the customs declaration
  • Dietary supplement export qualification dossier: product registration/filing certificate and customs export filing for food/health food supplements, as applicable

China mandates electronic invoicing under the fully digital electronic invoice (full e-invoice) reform. VAT invoices must be issued through the Electronic Tax Office using the tax-control UKey or the e-invoice system; invoice data are transmitted in near real-time to the Golden Tax System (Phase IV) for anti-fraud validation. Special VAT invoices carry statutory cryptographic codes, digital stamps, and QR codes, and are reconciled at issuance. Export transactions must align the electronic invoice with the customs export declaration data in the China Single Window.

Tax authorities and customs jointly audit export transactions by cross-checking invoice data, customs declarations, logistics/track records, payment flows, and SAFE foreign exchange collection records. Failure to substantiate actual export or repatriation of foreign exchange within 180 days results in automatic revocation of zero-rating, clawback of any VAT refund or export rebate, overdue interest and late-payment surcharges, and administrative penalties of 0.5 to 5 times the tax involved under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China. Intentional false export declarations or export refund fraud are referred for criminal prosecution under Chinese criminal law, with credit downgrade and loss of customs and export tax facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility largely unrestricted, while the capital account remains subject to strict approval and quota regimes. For a WFOE engaged in cross-border e-commerce, RMB convertibility on current account items such as trade-related payments and service fee remittances is permitted provided genuine trade documentation and customs declarations are submitted. Capital account transactions, including equity injections, foreign debt, and profit repatriation, require SAFE registration, special FX accounts, and annual audit verification. Dividend remittances are permitted after tax clearance, but the bank will require proof of accumulated profits, audit reports, tax payment certificates, and prior SAFE filings. Funds converted from capital contributions cannot be used for purposes beyond the registered business scope, and there is a matching principle for trade settlements. Surrender requirements have been relaxed to a voluntary system, yet all export proceeds must be repatriated to China and handled through the authorised banking system within a prescribed declaration window, subject to authenticity screening and anti-transfer pricing review. In practice, liquidity buffers may be trapped by settlement mismatches and documentation delays, necessitating robust treasury planning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the customs declaration date unless prior SAFE exemption is obtained. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may retain FX in current account accounts or convert to RMB at market rates, but all receipt/filing must be reported via the bank's digital system within 5 working days of receipt. The 'origin verification' procedure requires presenting customs declaration, commercial invoices, transport documents, and goods receipt confirmation to support the inward remittance.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low / Not Viable. Under PRC banking and payment regulations, non-bank financial institutions and offshore digital EMIs cannot open domestic accounts or provide RMB clearing services without a formal Chinese banking licence. Foreign EMIs such as Wise or Revolut have no RMB clearing licence and are legally barred from servicing an onshore WFOE as a primary settlement bank. A WFOE must utilise the domestic banking system for all CNY-denominated operations; for international collections, it can use licensed payment institutions (e.g., LianLian, PingPong) only for e-commerce platform settlements, but the underlying treasury and compliance obligations still require an onshore bank account.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: PRC Company Law (2018), Article 164 imposes an unconditional annual independent CPA audit on every limited-liability company, including a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise; the audit obligation is triggered by the legal form of registration, not by any balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold. No WFOE is exempt on size grounds. For supplementary classification, a WFOE is treated as a medium or large enterprise for financial-reporting purposes when retail operating revenue reaches approximately RMB 20 million, wholesale revenue reaches approximately RMB 40 million, total assets reach approximately RMB 30 million, or headcount reaches 300; such classification expands the required audit scope and the depth of ASBE disclosures, but the audit mandate already applies in all cases. Additional audit-report requirements arise from bank financing, cross-border merchant acquiring, food-license regulatory review, MOFCOM foreign-investment reporting, and SAFE capital-account supervision.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File preparation is required when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for intercompany service fees; a Master File and Local File are both required when the enterprise's aggregated related-party transaction turnover exceeds RMB 2 billion.

Economic substance scope: China's tax authority applies a thorough substance-over-form audit under CIT Law Article 47 and the OECD BEPS general anti-avoidance framework; a WFOE must demonstrate real operational capacity in mainland China covering Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as procurement decisions, inventory control, order approval, payment handling, accounting and board meetings. A pure-shell arrangement with a single mailbox address, no managerial employees and no local decision-making triggers reclassification of profits, disallowance of treaty benefits and an assessment of unexplained offshore income as a deemed dividend.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Article 48 permit the tax authority to make an arm's length adjustment and recalculate taxable income; under TAL Article 63 the resulting underpayment attracts a civil penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax, plus a 0.05% per day late surcharge; the solution agreement between enterprises is nullified to the extent it constitutes an illegal tax avoidance scheme.

Director personal liability: Upon establishment of intentional tax evasion by the board, the corporate veil is pierced under a fraud exception; directors may be jointly and severally liable for the outstanding tax and penalties, personal bank accounts are subject to freezing, the director is blacklisted and a travel ban (出境限制) is imposed; a criminal conviction under Criminal Law Article 201 carries imprisonment for up to seven years with fines.
Corporate Income Tax Fraud and Shell Company Under-Filing

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 63 provides that tax evasion by false or incomplete tax declarations triggers a penalty of 50%-500% of unpaid tax and unpaid tax collection plus interest; TAL Article 23 further obliges the enterprise to preserve all payment records. The company is placed on the national tax credit blacklist and its public credit rating is downgraded, blocking bank financing, bond issuance and new licences.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the false filings are personally liable under corporate disregard doctrines; the court may pierce the veil to recover assets transferred out of the WFOE, order civil restitution, impose a ban on leaving China, confiscate personal real estate and vehicles, and under Criminal Law Article 203 the responsible officer may be imprisoned for up to seven years if the tax due is concealed or transferred.
AML Violation, Unlicensed Cross-Border Drug-Product Sales and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Violates the Anti-Money Laundering Law Article 16 and Article 29 (customer due diligence and suspicious transaction reporting), the Food Safety Law Article 124 (illegal sale without licence) and the VAT Invoice Management Regulation; penalties include fines up to RMB 10 million, licence revocation, seizure and confiscation of illegal proceeds, and under Criminal Law Article 205 a conviction for false VAT invoicing is punishable by up to life imprisonment and heavy fines.

Director personal liability: The director and actual controller are jointly and severally liable for the full tax and customs losses, are barred from future board service, face civil asset forfeiture and travel blacklisting; if personal commingling of funds is proven, the corporate veil is completely pierced and the director's personal residence, accounts and shareholdings are subject to restitution; a criminal conviction may result in a determinate imprisonment of between three and ten years plus a monetary fine.
