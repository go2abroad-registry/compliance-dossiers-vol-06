Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected as the registered office for a WFOE in mainland China. The Company Law and SAMR registration rules require a physical, fixed, individually identifiable business address within the registered administrative division. Provincial tax authorities and bank compliance teams frequently reject virtual office addresses for e-commerce business models because they cannot verify business substance, staff presence, or storage capacity. For a pet supplies e-commerce WFOE, municipal SAMR offices may accept a co-working or incubator address only if the following conditions are met: formal lease of desk or standalone room, notarized lease with landlord identity, visible company name plaque, physical records of operations, and a dedicated Chinese address for service of legal process. Even then, bank KYC teams will almost always require physical inspection before opening a corporate bank account; the local tax bureau may deny the renewal of registration if inspection reveals no substantive activities.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the consolidated nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) platform for entity onboarding, filing, and VAT administration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident shareholder forms the WFOE as a limited liability company before the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the business licence (foreign-invested enterprise licence) carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the statutory TIN/Corporate Code of the enterprise and is automatically pushed to the State Taxation Administration via the 'multi-licence integration' registration mechanism, completing tax registration without a separate TIN application. The WFOE then activates an account on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, completes identity verification for the legal representative and financial officer, completes tax attribute identification (classification of turnover taxes, CIT, stamp duty, and payroll withholding), secures an electronic taxpayer-payment agreement, and, if export activity is anticipated, files export tax-refund registration and obtains the Customs import-export registration code in the customs clearance system. Since 2023 the electronic fiscal identity is fully activated online; no in-person appearance is generally required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box or hybrid classification election comparable to the US or EU regimes; the WFOE is irrebuttably a resident enterprise taxed entity-level on worldwide income. The only indirect-tax elections are the opt-in to general VAT taxpayer status if annual turnover is below RMB 5 million, the limited simplified VAT method available to certain general VAT taxpayers, and the classification of the enterprise as a general VAT taxpayer (13%/9%/6% output with input crediting) once the statutory turnover threshold is exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for registration: every incorporated enterprise must complete tax registration within 30 days of the issuance of its business licence, irrespective of revenue. The RMB 5,000,000 (approx. USD 690,000) cumulative 12-month turnover threshold is the mandatory point across which a taxpayer must adopt general VAT taxpayer status with full input-output crediting; below that level it may remain or elect as a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to 3% levy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT under the export tax-refund system when the goods physically leave the customs territory of mainland China under customs supervision, the sale is made to an overseas purchaser, the export customs declaration matches the commercial invoice and transport document, and the export foreign-exchange proceeds are received or collection is verified under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange monitoring system. Chinese exporter tax law applies the 'exemption, offset and refund' method, which zero-rates the export turnover while refunding creditable input VAT attributable to the export. The export of pet supplies and pet-care devices falls within the standard taxable goods category subject to the general export-refund schedule; export through cross-border e-commerce supervision can utilize the dedicated CBEC customs models where applicable, provided the e-commerce transaction records, platform settlement files and logistics tracking are retained.

  • Customs export declaration (Customs Export Declaration filing record) electronically released by the General Administration of Customs with final discharge status code.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a mainland China port/airport to the overseas consignee.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and underlying export sales contract matching the declared HS codes, quantities, unit prices and total cross-border value.
  • Customs inspection (where selected) or green-channel release records confirming actual goods movement.
  • Export foreign-exchange receipt verification record generated through the bank under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System - the Chinese functional equivalent of the FIRC / inward remittance certificate.

China has rolled out the national fully digital e-invoice system (the full-digital electronic invoice environment) operated by the State Taxation Administration's national unified invoice service platform. Invoicing is real-time online: every invoice must be issued through the platform before delivery to the counterparty; the platform assigns a nationwide invoice number and encryption/QR verification. Paper invoices are no longer valid for most B2B transactions. Anti-fraud controls require automatic cross-matching of each VAT special invoice to the buyer's verified input-invoice ledger; mismatches blocking input credit and trigger risk alerts. Export zero-rate sales must be documented in the same e-invoice platform using the export ordinary invoice category. There is no offline or batch-issued fiscal invoice within the scope of the new system; all invoice issuance is subject to allocated monthly quotas and real-time revenue-agency visibility.

Audited export refund positions are subject to retrospective review for at least five years during which the exporter must retain the full supporting single-document file as a continuing compliance obligation; fraud carries an unlimited recovery period. Findings of mismatch, fictitious export, false invoicing, or unrepatriated forex result in revocation of the zero-rating, clawback of input VAT already refunded, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, administrative fines of one to five times the tax refunded, deregistration from the export-refund system and referral to judicial authorities for the criminal offence of fraudulent export-tax refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC (maximum penalty life imprisonment plus confiscation).

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account items, including cross-border trade settlements, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by contracts, invoices, and customs declarations. Capital account items are strictly controlled: foreign direct investment must be registered with SAFE, paid into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and settled into RMB only for approved business purposes. There is no automatic capital account convertibility. Export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory surrender; however, all foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic bank and either retained in a foreign exchange account or converted into RMB through a designated bank under SAFE trade verification. Profit remittance hurdles require proof of after-tax profits, a statutory audit, tax payment certificates, a board resolution approving the dividend, and SAFE reporting through the bank; the bank applies a real need and source of funds review. A WFOE cannot freely convert working capital into USD without underlying current account justification; any cross-border financing or intercompany loan requires special SAFE registration. The practical effect is a high compliance overhead, but legitimate e-commerce receivables and trade payables flow well through Tier-1 banks and licensed cross-border payment platforms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign exchange account or settle them into RMB with a designated bank. The statutory timeline requires goods-trade export proceeds to be repatriated and credited to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account through a designated bank, with the receipt reported within 30 days of the value date under SAFE's trade in goods verification framework. If proceeds are received through third-party e-commerce platforms such as Stripe or PayPal, the amounts must be traceable to the WFOE's export customs declarations and consolidated into the SAFE electronic reporting system. Any failure to repatriate or prolonged unexplained retention triggers alerts under SAFE's foreign exchange verification and monitoring mechanism.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for core treasury. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by PBOC to open domestic settlement accounts, access CNAPS, or perform RMB and FX clearing for a Chinese resident entity. Under SAFE rules, the WFOE must settle export proceeds through a domestic bank or a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution. EMIs may only be used as ancillary collection channels if their payout flows into a designated domestic bank account and the transaction data is fully reconcilable. For this ISIC 4791 e-commerce model, the best practice is to connect Stripe and PayPal directly to a licensed cross-border Internet payment platform, for example PingPong, Payoneer, or LianLian Global, and settle into a Tier-1 Chinese bank account, rather than using an EMI as the primary treasury vehicle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory if annual related-party transactions reach: CNY 200 million for tangible property transfers; CNY 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transfers; CNY 40 million for related-party service transactions. Master File documentation is required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed CNY 2 billion. Country-by-Country reporting applies when the group's consolidated revenue is at least CNY 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not yet apply an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute such as in some offshore hubs, but the tax authorities apply the economic substance doctrine through Article 47 of the Tax Administration Law, anti-avoidance rules, and the BEPS Principal Purpose Test. A WFOE must demonstrate substance through local employees, office premises, decision-making personnel, bank accounts, customer contracts, and the substantive performance of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China. A letterbox/shell WFOE is vulnerable to treaty-shopping denials, derecognition of the corporate entity, and recharacterization of cross-border royalty arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent E-commerce Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Law on Tax Administration and Collection: recovery of unpaid tax, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and a monetary penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid tax. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law: criminal tax evasion punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years, substantial fines, and confiscation of property for extremely large amounts involving a high proportion of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, a court may pierce the corporate veil where a director abuses the limited liability structure, commingles personal and corporate assets, or uses the WFOE to evade tax. Directors then face unlimited personal civil liability for unpaid tax and restitution; departure restriction and tax blacklisting may be imposed; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 7 years and additional criminal fines.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Thin Capitalization and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 42 of the PRC Law on Tax Administration and Collection authorize the tax authority to adjust taxable income under the arm's length principle and recharacterize abusive arrangements. SAT Public Notice [2017] No. 6 permits upward adjustments, secondary adjustments, and a 5-year look-back period. A transfer pricing adjustment accompanied by evidence of false documentation attracts the same 50%-500% penalty as tax evasion; intentional fraud can lead to criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally execute sham transfer pricing agreements for the purpose of stripping Chinese profits may be held personally liable for corporate tax deficiencies if the company is left insolvent. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and require reimbursement from personal assets. Directors may also be subject to travel bans, asset freezes, and criminal tax fraud charges where fabricated transfer pricing documentation was submitted to the tax authority.
Shell Company / Money Laundering / AML and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Articles 8 and 34 of the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law impose corrective orders, warnings, fines of up to CNY 1 million or higher for institutions, and fines on responsible officers. Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law punishes money laundering with imprisonment of up to 10 years and fines. Tax Authorities may apply substance-over-form recharacterization under Article 47 of the Tax Administration Law, deny treaty benefits, and deregister the empty WFOE.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow beneficial owners face joint and several restitution liability for laundered or undeclared funds. The court may pierce the corporate veil where the shell structure was used to conceal true ownership and evade Chinese tax. Sanctions include civil asset restitution, blacklisting and travel bans, freezing of personal bank accounts, unlimited liability for corporate debts, and criminal imprisonment up to 10 years for money-laundering offenses.
