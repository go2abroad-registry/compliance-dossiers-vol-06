Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Chinese law does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned limited liability company. The registered capital is determined by the shareholders in the Articles of Association and may be subscribed in installments according to the contribution schedule. No blocked escrow account is mandated for furniture retail e-commerce; however, the foreign shareholder must remit the subscribed capital into a designated foreign direct investment capital account through a SAFE-authorized bank if foreign currency is introduced.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no Civil Defense fire permit is required for the pure online retail business office. The selected registered office must nonetheless comply with ordinary fire prevention requirements under the Fire Protection Law and the landlord must provide a lawful certificate of building fire safety inspection if the premises are occupied for an office use. If warehousing or furniture delivery handling is introduced in the future, the operator must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Authority and comply with workplace safety obligations under the Work Safety Law.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online service platform; official website: www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a PRC-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) shown on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 days of obtaining the Business License, the WFOE completes integrated tax registration with the local STA office via the e-Tax platform; the tax authority then records the USCC as the taxpayer identification number and issues a formal taxpayer registration certificate. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China, a non-resident TIN may be obtained by filing a registration with the STA office at the place where the Chinese withholding agent is located, or by self-registration through the e-Tax platform before applying for preferential treaty treatment; the non-resident TIN is used for withholding and taxation on China-source income.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise of China and is subject to Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at the statutory rate of 25% on worldwide taxable income; no pass-through classification is available. — For a PRC-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) shown on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise of China and is subject to Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at the statutory rate of 25% on worldwide taxable income; no pass-through classification is available.

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax legislation does not offer check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification elections. The only relevant statutory election is the indirect tax status election between small-scale VAT taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer, which may be exercised when annual taxable sales are below RMB 5,000,000 and the taxpayer can maintain accurate accounting and a fixed place of business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover up to RMB 5,000,000 qualifies for small-scale VAT taxpayer status; once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, the taxpayer is required to register as a general VAT taxpayer. A taxpayer can voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer if it meets the accounting and fixed-place-of-business conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods, including oversized furniture, are eligible for zero-rating under the PRC export VAT zero-rating and refund policy where: (i) the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer; (ii) the goods have been declared to and processed by Chinese Customs under an export customs supervision mode; (iii) the goods have actually left China within the specified time limit; (iv) export sales are evidenced by an export invoice and foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory collection period; and (v) the export tax refund declaration is filed within the prescribed declaration period. Qualifying cross-border e-commerce export consignments using customs trade modes 9610, 9710 or 9810 are also zero-rated if the same export and foreign-exchange criteria are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) processed by the General Administration of Customs of China and bearing the 18-digit customs declaration number
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement from China for the oversized furniture shipment
  • Export commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank receipt/statement issued by a licensed Chinese bank evidencing receipt and settlement of the export proceeds in foreign exchange
  • Special VAT invoice or ordinary VAT invoice issued for the export sale and clearly identified as a zero-rated export
  • Export contract or purchase order; for cross-border e-commerce, the customs e-commerce export manifest (mode 9610, 9710 or 9810) and supporting logistics tracking records

The State Taxation Administration enforces the Golden Tax Project (Phase IV) and the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through tax-authorised channels: general VAT taxpayers use the Special VAT Invoice System or the Full-digital Electronic Invoice platform, and invoice data is transmitted to the STA in near real time. The national Invoice Verification Platform and anti-fraud digital stamping/encryption controls are mandatory for all full-digital electronic invoices; paper invoices require tax-control equipment with a digital fiscal seal. Cross-border sales must additionally link tax invoice data with customs export declaration data through the integrated e-Tax and customs clearance systems.

Chinese tax and customs authorities conduct joint post-clearance audits of export VAT zero-rating and refund claims. If the claim lacks genuine physical export, contains forged or falsified customs or foreign-exchange documents, does not meet the mandatory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, or otherwise violates the Export Tax Rebate Administration Measures, the STA denies the claim, clawbacks the refunded input VAT and assesses a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day plus an administrative penalty ranging from 0.5 to 5 times the tax involved. Fraudulent export tax refund is a criminal offence under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law if the refunded amount exceeds RMB 50,000; conviction can lead to imprisonment up to life imprisonment, fines and confiscation of property. The exporter may additionally lose its export tax refund qualification for up to three years and be publicly listed in the STA's penalty notices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates an administratively supervised managed float with asymmetric convertibility: the current account is broadly convertible after banks perform genuine transaction review, while the capital account remains heavily controlled and requires SAFE registration, filing, or approval. For a furniture e-commerce WFOE, ordinary trade receipts for exported goods and freight services are payable into a foreign exchange current account and may be retained or exchanged into RMB at market rates without a mandatory surrender ratio. Dividend remittances to the overseas shareholder are classified as current account outbound transfers and are permissible once the annual audit is completed, all Chinese taxes, including corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax, are cleared, and a board resolution is filed; the bank will check the tax payment receipt and audit report. The registration of paid-in capital through the foreign exchange information system must be completed before any capital account disbursement. Severe frictions appear on capital account outflows, such as cross-border loans, guarantees, offshore investments, or intangible-asset royalty payments, which face SAFE registration thresholds and can require a pre-approval certificate. Any attempt to use trade contracts as a conduit for capital flight will be blocked under SAFE's cross-border trade matching and anti-money-laundering checks. Profit remittance is easier for a genuine trading company with auditable sales but still requires complete customs declarations and e-commerce settlement records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No fixed mandatory surrender ratio applies since the 2012 SAFE reform. Export-generated foreign exchange can be credited to a current-account FX account and converted to RMB at any time at the prevailing onshore rate through a designated bank. However, the proceeds must be physically repatriated to China, and payment should be received within the trade credit period stated on customs records; if collection slides past 90 days from the export customs date, the entity must report the unusual delay through the bank to SAFE's monitoring system. Every receipt must be matched to the export customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and payment purpose code. If the bank challenges mismatched settlement, the WFOE must provide original commercial documents and a reconciliation explanation. Voluntary RMB conversion is permitted without a percentage cap.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail because overseas non-bank electronic money institutions do not hold Chinese payment institution licenses and cannot open onshore RMB or foreign exchange corporate accounts for a resident WFOE. PBOC and SAFE require domestic settlements, tax payments, customs bonds, and statutory capital transactions to pass through licensed domestic banks. Cross-border e-commerce payment facilitators such as licensed third-party payment institutions and merchant-of-record solutions can support payout collection from Stripe and Affirm-style rails only if they obtain approval under the PBOC payment business framework and partner with a Chinese bank for settlement. Such EMIs may be used as an ancillary collection and disintermediation tool, but not as the legal clearing house for the WFOE balance sheet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital must be routed through a SAFE-designated capital account at a tier-1 Chinese bank. Before wiring, the WFOE must complete the foreign exchange registration for FDI with SAFE through the bank, and the bank will issue a foreign exchange business registration number. The remitter should send a SWIFT MT103 with purpose code relating to foreign direct investment registered capital, ensuring the remittance amount exactly matches the approved capital injection in the articles of association and the foreign investment information filing. After settlement, the bank checks authenticity and the WFOE engages a Chinese CPA firm to issue a capital verification report confirming the capital is paid in. The RMB funds converted from the capital account may be used for bona fide operations such as inventory purchases, warehouse leases, logistics, and platform marketing, but cannot be used for securities investment, inter-company loans, real estate beyond permitted scope, or entrusted lending.For cross-border trade disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 wires for urgent supplier prepayments and under-threshold commercial settlements, while larger import transactions should be handled through irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by a Chinese bank, with the beneficiary supplier presenting compliant documents. Documentary collections are also possible for low-volume oversized furniture components but carry settlement risk when shipping and payment are not simultaneous. Each outbound transfer must carry the correct SAFE trade purpose code and be supported by purchase contracts, invoices, and where applicable customs declarations. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: PRC law imposes no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption. The mandatory audit trigger is legal personality under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law: every company, including a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with limited liability, must prepare annual financial statements and have them audited by a Chinese CPA firm. A WFOE in the online retail of oversized furniture is therefore required to obtain an annual independent audit regardless of whether it has zero revenue, a minimal balance sheet, or a single employee. Separate audit-related mandates may apply if the enterprise claims a net operating loss in its CIT final settlement, carries out cross-border related-party transactions in excess of the relevant transfer-pricing documentation threshold, or applies for a tax refund or special regulatory treatment.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to its non-resident corporate shareholder are subject to Chinese withholding income tax at 10% (the 20% statutory rate applicable to non-resident enterprises is reduced to 10%), unless a Double Tax Treaty reduces the rate. Treaty withholding on dividends may be as low as 5% where the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the capital of the distributing company, or 10% in other cases.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The tax year in China is the calendar year (January 1 to December 31). The annual corporate income tax return and final settlement must be filed within 5 months after the end of the fiscal year, i.e., by May 31 of the following year. Quarterly prepayments are due within 15 days after each quarter-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42 and related transfer pricing rules, contemporaneous Local File documentation must be prepared when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 100 million for service transactions. Master File preparation becomes compulsory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million in the relevant tax year.

Economic substance scope: China does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but tax authorities apply the substance-over-form principle and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47. For a WFOE selling oversized furniture online, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in mainland China: order processing, inventory control for the Chinese marketplace, pricing decisions, marketing to Chinese consumers, customer service, returns handling, logistics coordination, and strategic supply-chain decision-making. A shell WFOE with no employees, no operating decision-making, and no bank account or premises in mainland China will be treated as lacking economic substance; treaty benefits on outbound payments may be denied and profit will be reallocated to China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41, 44, 48 and SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42, the State Taxation Administration may re-determine taxable income by applying the arm's length standard, assess additional corporate income tax, and levy late-payment interest. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, intentional false reporting or concealment may yield penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and loss of loss carryforwards; tax authorities may also look back 10 years for abusive transfer-pricing structures.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inflated cross-border invoices or who direct the falsification of transfer-pricing documentation may face civil liability for improperly diverting corporate assets. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where a director or controlling shareholder uses the WFOE for fraudulent purposes or to evade tax, exposing the director's personal assets. Tax authorities may impose exit-control travel bans on legal representatives who fail to pay assessed tax, and criminal tax evasion charges under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years where the evasion is severe and repeated.
Tax Evasion Through False Annual Return or Concealed Revenue

Statutory sanction: Pursuant to Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, taxpayers who conceal income, utilize false accounting, or deceitfully fail to pay tax are subject to recovery of unpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge, and a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Failure to file within the statutory period may also incur fines under Article 62. Where tax evaded reaches the criminal threshold and constitutes tax fraud or evasion under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, the company may be fined and the responsible officers may be criminally prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the WFOE to file fraudulent tax returns may be personally liable for the tax loss where the corporate form is abused to retain unlawful profits. Courts may order restitution of diverted funds and attach personal bank accounts and real estate. Tax authorities may apply exit-control restrictions preventing directors from leaving China until the tax debt is settled. Criminal liability includes imprisonment of up to 3 years for ordinary evasion and up to 7 years for very large evasion amounts, together with confiscation of proceeds and fines.
DTA Abuse, Beneficial Ownership Shell, and Improper Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in EIT Law Article 47, reclassify the payment as a taxable dividend, and impose the domestic-law 10% withholding tax plus interest and late-payment penalties. If the conduit arrangement was falsely documented, penalties of up to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed, and the 10-year special tax adjustment lookback period may be applied.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham contracts, approve excessive royalty payments to shell entities, or transfer corporate value without commercial justification may be sued by creditors or the tax authority for breach of fiduciary duty and fraudulent asset stripping. Corporate veil-piercing provisions allow courts to hold directors personally liable for the unpaid tax and restitution where the shell arrangement was used to evade state claims. Personal travel bans and asset freezes may be imposed, and criminal sanctions for tax evasion or smuggling may include imprisonment up to 7 years for directors who orchestrated the abusive repatriation structure.
