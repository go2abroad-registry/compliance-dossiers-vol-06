No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE LLC under current PRC law; the registered capital amount must be reasonable and aligned with the company's business scope. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau web-based platform administered by the provincial-level tax service bureaus of STA.

E-government filing portal

For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the procedure begins after the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the WFOE Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID) for all tax matters; no separate TIN application is made because SAMR shares incorporation data with STA under the multi-license integration reform. Within 30 days of obtaining the Business License, the WFOE must log onto the Electronic Tax Bureau and complete tax-registration supplementary filings: confirm the legal representative and tax handler details, select the competent local tax authority, file accounting and bank account information, confirm value-added tax (VAT) taxpayer classification, activate electronic tax-payment channels, and obtain the digital certificate/e-tax stamp required for e-invoice issuance. For export operations, the WFOE must additionally complete China Customs registration, the China Electronic Port user-vault opening, the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System registration, and, if using cross-border e-commerce retail export, registration as a cross-border e-commerce enterprise in an approved comprehensive pilot zone. The tax authority will not issue a separate paper tax certificate; the USCC appearing on the Business License is the operative tax registration evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None, in the sense of U.S. check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; China has no separate entity classification election. A WFOE is automatically a Chinese resident enterprise for enterprise income tax on its worldwide income and an entity-level VAT taxpayer. The meaningful elective mechanisms are VAT elections: a small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer if it has sound accounting and a fixed place of business; an eligible VAT taxpayer may elect the simplified collection method for specified transaction categories; and, in cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot zones, the WFOE may elect the invoice-free VAT/consumption-tax exemption for qualifying retail exports where no purchase VAT special invoice is available, instead of claiming the zero-rating/export-rebate mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No zero-turnover registration threshold: every entity engaged in taxable activities in China must complete tax registration. Once annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory; below that threshold the entity is a small-scale VAT taxpayer, and small-scale taxpayers currently may qualify for an exemption for monthly sales not exceeding CNY 100,000 or the equivalent quarterly amount under applicable relief policies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim VAT zero-rating/export rebate for outdoor camping equipment exported overseas if the goods are physically moved out of the PRC customs territory, the WFOE is the exporter of record on the Customs export declaration, the export is made under an appropriate customs supervision code (for example, 0110 general trade, 9610 cross-border e-commerce retail export, or 1210 bonded export) through the China International Trade Single Window, the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer with valid VAT special invoices or other deductible input documents for the exported goods, and the export proceeds are received through a licensed bank or cross-border payment institution and repatriated to a bank account in China. For goods sold to foreign consumers through e-commerce platforms in approved cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot zones, if valid purchase VAT invoices are unavailable, the invoice-free exemption applies instead of zero-rating; under that exemption, output VAT and consumption tax are exempt but the related input VAT cannot be refunded. If the HS-code-specific export rebate rate is lower than the 13% standard rate, the unrefunded input VAT difference remains a cost.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export-rebate copy) filed in the China International Trade Single Window and containing the Customs Release/Audit Exit date for the goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and sales contract or e-commerce order record identifying the overseas buyer, HS code, quantity, value and payment terms
  • Cross-border e-commerce order, payment and logistics electronic manifest data (order number, transaction ID, courier/warehouse dispatch records) when using the 9610/1210 retail-export supervision codes
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or postal/courier dispatch certificate proving the goods physically departed from the PRC customs territory
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher (bank settlement note or SAFE International Receipts and Payments Statement) or the corresponding report in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System proving that the export proceeds were repatriated
  • VAT special invoice(s) or purchase receipt(s) for the exported goods (required for the export-rebate/zero-rating claim; not required for the invoice-free exemption)

All invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled invoice system. Fully digital e-invoices are issued via the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform with near-real-time transmission to the Golden Tax System IV; each invoice carries a unique national invoice number, anti-forgery QR code, and a digital tax-control signature/stamp. Special VAT invoices used for input-VAT deduction and export rebate are cross-checked automatically with China Customs export data, SAFE foreign-exchange data, and VAT declarations. Non-compliance with e-invoice clearance or data integrity rules can block the issuance of new invoices and nullify input-VAT credits.

The STA continuously reconciles export-rebate claims with China Customs exit records, SAFE trade-in-goods foreign-exchange data, bank settlement flows, e-invoice purchase data and enterprise-income-tax returns through the Golden Tax System IV and the joint customs-tax-banking supervision mechanism. If the claimed zero-rating is found to lack a genuine export movement, to use forged or false VAT special invoices, or to fail the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement without an approved statutory exception, the tax authority will retroactively claw back all VAT refunds and exemptions, assess late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day (18.25% per annum), impose administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, downgrade or cancel the enterprise's export-rebate qualification, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund; the Customs and SAFE may separately impose penalties for false export declarations and foreign-exchange evasion, including fines and enterprise classification downgrades that prevent future cross-border e-commerce export rebates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB satisfies current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; a WFOE engaged in internet retailing of outdoor camping equipment may freely settle trade-related FX receipts and payments with tier-1 licensed banks in China without per-transaction SAFE pre-approval, provided the required underlying documentation such as commercial invoices, bills of lading, customs declarations, and platform settlement reports is retained. Capital-account items, by contrast, remain regulated: FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and any transfer under the capital account must be registered with SAFE, channeled through a dedicated capital escrow account, and supported by a capital verification report. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is permitted after annual tax clearance and statutory audit, but the bank will request the audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and the SAFE registration number. Export proceeds from e-commerce sales may be retained in a foreign-currency settlement account or converted into RMB at the onshore market rate at the bank's discretion; there is no forced conversion ratio. The practical hurdles are not currency inconvertibility but strict documentary verification, the need for real foreign-investment registration, and the SAFE post-event monitoring system that requires every capital movement to be traceable to an economic contract or customs record.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account administration, export proceeds for goods sold over the internet must be repatriated into China within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless an offshore retention arrangement is individually approved by SAFE. The WFOE must monitor receipts through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; if payment is overdue beyond the 90-day window, a delayed-payment explanation and import/export warrant must be submitted. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under current rules; the WFOE may retain up to 100% of its export foreign-currency income in its foreign-currency settlement account at a SAFE-designated bank or may sell any portion to the bank at the prevailing onshore RMB exchange rate. However, the bank will verify each receipt against the Electronic Port customs data and will reject any credit that exceeds the invoiced value or lacks a valid underlying trade identity.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the apex monetary regulator; SAFE executes and supervises all foreign-exchange controls in China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a treasury substitute. China's payment-services law, enforced by PBOC and SAFE, requires any non-bank payment institution to hold a Payment Business License; foreign EMIs such as Stripe are not licensed to open deposit or settlement accounts for a mainland-incorporated WFOE. PayPal has a limited licensed presence in China but acts only as a payment gateway and cannot replace a licensed bank account or perform FX clearing on behalf of the entity. The WFOE may use Stripe/PayPal for online checkout or collection aggregation, but all receivables must be repatriated into a SAFE-registered onshore bank account supported by export documents. Direct utilization of offshore EMI accounts with balances resident abroad would breach SAFE repatriation rules and expose the WFOE to AML penalties. Therefore, run the operational treasury through tier-1 domestic banks and use fintechs solely for point-of-sale gateways.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must first complete SAFE FDI registration and obtain a unique Foreign Exchange Registration number for the WFOE. The parent then initiates a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the WFOE's capital escrow account at the chosen tier-1 Chinese bank, inserting the SAFE FDI registration number in field 70 and using the appropriate payment purpose code for inbound foreign direct investment equity capital. The beneficiary name must match the Chinese business license exactly. Upon receipt, the bank credits the funds to the capital escrow account and requires a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report before the funds are released for operating purposes. The WFOE may then settle the foreign currency into RMB through the bank at the onshore spot rate or retain the foreign currency in a separate settlement account. All subsequent movements out of the capital account, including payment for inventory, rent, payroll, or trade services, are electronically monitored by SAFE and must not exceed the approved business scope.The WFOE may disburse funds through multiple clearing rails depending on the trade counterparty. For small recurring payments to overseas suppliers, freight forwarders, or e-commerce platform fees, use SWIFT MT103 with the relevant current-account purpose code and underlying invoice/contract evidence. For high-value purchase of outdoor camping inventory from a foreign supplier, the preferred rail is a Commercial Letter of Credit, either at sight or on a usance basis (e.g., 60 or 180 days after B/L date), issued by the tier-1 bank, with the beneficiary presenting original B/L, inspection certificate, and commercial invoice. For lower documentary risk and consignment trade, Documentary Collections under DP/DA terms are viable, but the WFOE must retain full control of the shipping documents via the collecting bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a treasury substitute. China's payment-services law, enforced by PBOC and SAFE, requires any non-bank payment institution to hold a Payment Business License; foreign EMIs such as Stripe are not licensed to open deposit or settlement accounts for a mainland-incorporated WFOE. PayPal has a limited licensed presence in China but acts only as a payment gateway and cannot replace a licensed bank account or perform FX clearing on behalf of the entity. The WFOE may use Stripe/PayPal for online checkout or collection aggregation, but all receivables must be repatriated into a SAFE-registered onshore bank account supported by export documents. Direct utilization of offshore EMI accounts with balances resident abroad would breach SAFE repatriation rules and expose the WFOE to AML penalties. Therefore, run the operational treasury through tier-1 domestic banks and use fintechs solely for point-of-sale gateways.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a Local File must be prepared when related-party transactions in a tax year exceed: RMB 100 million for tangible-asset transfers, RMB 10 million for intangible-asset transfers, RMB 10 million for financial-asset transfers, and RMB 10 million for related-party services. A Master File is required for a constituent entity of a multinational group whose annual global consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and that has related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not maintain a standalone Economic Substance Regulation analogous to Gulf jurisdictions, Chinese tax authorities apply substance-over-form principles, the general anti-avoidance rule under EITL Article 47, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis when determining profit attribution, beneficial ownership, and treaty entitlement. The WFOE must demonstrate local decision-making, inventory management, marketing, customer service, order fulfillment, and significant people functions in China; a shell entity or conduit without CIGA will be recharacterized and may lose treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false revenue and fabricated expenses

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration and Collection Law Article 63 requires recovery of the unpaid tax, daily late-payment interest, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years where the tax evaded is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, and 3 to 7 years where the tax evaded is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: A director who signs the false return or personally directs the concealment can be held personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax and penalties through civil asset seizure, placed on the State Taxation Administration Dishonest Taxpayer list, blacklisted with exit restrictions preventing departure from China, and imprisoned for up to 7 years with personal fines and confiscation of assets.
Transfer pricing mispricing and contemporaneous documentation failure

Statutory sanction: EITL Article 41 empowers the tax authority to adjust taxable income to the arm's-length standard; TACL Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% on the underpaid tax; special tax investigation rules may impose additional interest and a retroactive reallocation of income. Failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation can be treated as a separate non-compliance and result in denial of deductions and adverse extrapolation.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who approve fabricated transfer prices without business substance can be personally liable for the tax deficiency under civil fraud and unjust enrichment principles, prohibited from leaving China through the dishonest taxpayer blacklist, and criminally prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years where the mispricing causes tax evasion.
Shell company and treaty shopping with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: EITL Article 47 and SAT Public Notice No. 9 of 2018 allow the tax authority to disregard the intermediary, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the payment as direct to a non-resident not entitled to relief, impose the full 10% domestic withholding tax, plus late payment interest and a 50% to 500% penalty on the unreported tax through TACL Article 63.

Director personal liability: A director who controls the shell, directs the dividend or royalty flow, and causes the WFOE to under-withhold can be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid WHT and civil damages, placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist with exit restrictions, and imprisoned for up to 7 years if the arrangement is proved to be fraudulent tax evasion rather than merely aggressive tax planning.
