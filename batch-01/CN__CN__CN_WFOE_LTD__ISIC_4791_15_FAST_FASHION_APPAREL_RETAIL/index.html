Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic return and registration filing is performed through the national Electronic Taxpayer Service System (e-Tax) and the Full Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform operated at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; competent provincial and municipal tax service bureaus administer day-to-day compliance for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China.

E-government filing portal

For a China-incorporated WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identifier; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Procedure: (1) Incorporate the WFOE with the local Market Regulation Administration and obtain the business license, which is automatically linked to the tax authority via integrated government data exchange; (2) complete post-incorporation tax confirmation, banking account reporting, legal representative and controller real-name authentication, and accounting software records in the local e-Tax portal within 30 calendar days after issuance of the business license; (3) confirm VAT taxpayer status by being classified as a small-scale taxpayer paying at the simplified levy rate or applying for VAT general taxpayer status where annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 or where voluntary registration is appropriate; (4) file an export tax refund (exemption) registration application through e-Tax for the future zero-rating of cross-border sales; (5) register trade receipts with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and open the relevant RMB and foreign currency bank accounts; and (6) apply for invoice issuance limits and activate the full-digital e-invoice service on the STA platform. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese legal presence would instead obtain a tax source withholding ID from the payer's local tax bureau, but the target entity is a Chinese-resident WFOE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Chinese tax law. The WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident limited liability enterprise. The only relevant statutory election is the VAT taxpayer status choice: an entity with annual sales below the CNY 5,000,000 general taxpayer threshold may elect to remain a small-scale taxpayer or voluntarily register as a VAT general taxpayer; production-type and trading-type exporters may also select the applicable export tax refund computation method (exemption and refund, or exemption, credit and refund) based on their qualification and supply chain model.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese resident enterprises must complete tax registration upon incorporation regardless of turnover. For VAT general taxpayer classification, the mandatory annual taxable sales threshold is CNY 5,000,000; below that threshold an enterprise is registered as a small-scale taxpayer. Under the current STA/MLF policy, small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding CNY 100,000 (quarterly CNY 300,000) are exempt from VAT within the applicable policy period, while the headline standard VAT rate on apparel goods sold domestically is 13% for general taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating for exported fast-fashion apparel is available if the WFOE is a registered VAT taxpayer (general taxpayer recommended), the goods physically exit China and are released by customs under the export declaration, the export transaction is made to an overseas buyer now or through a qualifying cross-border e-commerce retail export channel recognized by the General Administration of Customs, the sale is recorded in the accounting books, the export proceeds are repatriated within the statutory period, and the customs/foreign-exchange data are matched with the tax declaration. Qualifying offshore digital services, if any ancillary services are supplied by the WFOE, can also be zero-rated only if listed in the existing cross-border taxable service zero-rating catalogue and supported by contracts, offshore use, and foreign exchange retention evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed through the China International Trade Single Window and confirmed by the General Administration of Customs
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, air waybill, or international express waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank/settlement proof of export proceeds received from the overseas buyer
  • Export commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract/purchase order, and e-commerce payment reconciliation record
  • SAFE export proceeds collection/verification record or bank's foreign exchange trade monitoring report
  • Cross-border e-commerce order, logistics, and payment trail where the sale is made through a B2C platform using customs supervision model codes for retail e-commerce exports

China requires use of the Full Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Every FDEI must be issued and transmitted to the tax authority in real time with a unique national invoice number, tax-control digital signature, and QR code; physical tax-control device constraints are being phased out. The tax authority sets a dynamic invoice quota and pre-authorized total invoice amount per taxpayer, blocking any issuance above the quota and requiring an online quota adjustment application. E-commerce sellers must connect their sales systems or payment platforms to the e-invoice system by API or use the e-Tax supplementary issuance module, ensuring real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, and seamless reconciliation of each electrical retail transaction.

The STA, customs, and SAFE jointly verify export declarations, logistics movement, and foreign exchange receipts through the International Trade Single Window. Where the WFOE claims export zero-rating without a complete documentation bundle or with false or unverified export customs data, the tax authority disallows the zero-rated treatment, recovers all previously refunded VAT, and imposes a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% of the underpaid amount. Fraudulent or serious misdeclaration subjects the exporter to administrative fines up to five times the refunded or evaded VAT, suspension/cancellation of export tax refund qualification, and retrospective clawback for the statutory assessment period, which is extended in fraud cases; criminal referral is mandatory where the amount reaches the threshold under the Criminal Law, carrying severe penalties including imprisonment for fraudulent export tax refund.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under a fundamental current-account convertibility framework. Trade and service receipts may be converted into and out of Renminbi at designated banks without central-bank approval, provided the underlying transaction is real and verifiable. Capital-account transactions, including FDI equity injection, shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital, are subject to registration, filing, and in some cases separate approval by PBOC/SAFE. A WFOE in fast-fashion e-commerce benefits from a low-risk trade profile, but the bank will still apply authenticity controls to all receipts, especially high-volume/low-value parcel flows. Surrender-style mandatory conversion no longer exists; export proceeds may be held in an onshore foreign exchange account or settled to CNY at the bank's quoted rate. Dividend remittance is permissible through the current-account profit remittance channel after annual audit, tax clearance, and board approval. However, outbound remittances of liquidation proceeds or capital redemption remain capital-account events and require SAFE registration and matching tax filings before the bank will debit the account. Practical hurdles include documentary verification, matching invoices with customs declarations, source-of-wealth proof for the foreign shareholder, and negative-list industry screening. These are compliance frictions, not statutory exchange prohibitions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Guidelines, export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign exchange account within the statutory trade-credit period, generally 180 days from the customs export declaration date, and may then be either retained in full in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted into Renminbi at the enterprise's discretion. There is no mandatory conversion percentage for Category A enterprises; the permissible conversion ratio is effectively 0% to 100% based on genuine trade needs. If the exporter plans to retain funds offshore for commercial cash-flow purposes, a prior SAFE filing or approval is required through the enterprise's settlement bank. Category B and C enterprises face a stricter regime, including transaction-by-transaction bank review, documentary inspection, mandatory settlement in certain higher-risk cases, and suspension of simplified offshore retention.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign exchange administration authority to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury solution. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC as deposit-taking institutions and cannot open statutory RMB settlement accounts or FDI capital accounts for a mainland WFOE. They are legally barred from serving as the WFOE's main liquidity account where the entity must maintain regulatory capital, receive equity injections, and execute statutory FX filings. The only acceptable role is as an auxiliary cross-border collection aggregator through a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution, which would settle into an onshore bank account after performing trade-data reporting. For all capital inflows, outbound disbursements, FX hedging, and dividend remittance, the WFOE must use licensed onshore banks or SAFE-registered payment institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent should remit the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 into the WFOE's dedicated FDI foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. The MT103 beneficiary must exactly match the WFOE's registered legal name and bank account; the remittance message must clearly state that it is an FDI equity injection and include the SAFE-designated direct investment purpose code for capital contribution, as confirmed by the receiving bank. The receiving bank will verify the corporate establishment documents, the foreign-shareholder ownership structure, the SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration record, and the applicable negative-list industry status. If the capital is nominated in foreign currency and converted into Renminbi, the bank executes the conversion at the onshore market rate and records the transaction in the SAFE capital-account monitoring system. The WFOE must then obtain the bank-issued capital-inflow confirmation and use capital funds only for permitted business purposes, including operating expenses, procurement, salaries, and eligible investments, while retaining invoices and contracts for each payment to support subsequent post-transaction audits.For outbound trade and operating disbursements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency payments to overseas suppliers, logistics providers, marketing platforms, and professional-service vendors. For larger bulk procurement from overseas suppliers, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Documentary Collection can be used when shipment documents, insurance certificates, and commercial invoices are required to mitigate trade risk. Domestic supplier settlements in Renminbi should be executed through the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or the banks clearing network, while cross-border settlements in Renminbi with counterparties abroad may use CIPS. For B2C e-commerce refunds and platform fee disbursements, the treasury team should integrate Shopify Payments, Klarna, and card-acquirer settlement reports with the bank's expected reconciliation format, and all outbound foreign currency payments must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, customs or logistics proof, tax declarations, and a SAFE-compliant purpose code.Deliverable USD/CNY forward settlement-and-sale contracts (远期结售汇) supported by genuine trade receipts, export contracts, and customs declaration records; speculative positions are prohibited.FX swaps (掉期) between USD and CNY for managing timing mismatches between trade-receipt inflows and supplier-payment outflows, subject to real-demand documentation and bank credit limits.Cross-currency swaps (货币掉期) for long-term foreign-currency capital or liability cash flows, permitted only after SAFE registration or filing and a demonstrated underlying exposure.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as a standalone onshore hedge for the WFOE; the onshore entity may only access deliverable forwards, while any offshore CNH NDF must be executed by the offshore parent for its own exposure and cannot be booked into the WFOE's balance sheet.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary treasury solution. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC as deposit-taking institutions and cannot open statutory RMB settlement accounts or FDI capital accounts for a mainland WFOE. They are legally barred from serving as the WFOE's main liquidity account where the entity must maintain regulatory capital, receive equity injections, and execute statutory FX filings. The only acceptable role is as an auxiliary cross-border collection aggregator through a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution, which would settle into an onshore bank account after performing trade-data reporting. For all capital inflows, outbound disbursements, FX hedging, and dividend remittance, the WFOE must use licensed onshore banks or SAFE-registered payment institutions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 1 billion aggregate related-party transactions in the prior year for Master File preparation; Local File preparation is mandatory when prior-year related-party tangible asset transfers reach RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers or royalties reach RMB 100 million, financial asset/loan transactions reach RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions reach RMB 40 million; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared before the CIT filing deadline and submitted within the statutory period upon tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations label but enforces substance over form through Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 (general anti-avoidance rule), Article 48 (special tax adjustment periods), and judicial economic-substance principles; a WFOE engaged in fast-fashion e-commerce distribution must perform its distribution, inventory-holding, risk-bearing, procurement, marketing, and customer-service functions with its own employees, assets, and decision records; a non-resident beneficial owner claiming treaty relief must actively conduct its Core Income-Generating Activities in its home jurisdiction; shell subsidiaries, circular financing, and Chinese-sourced e-commerce revenue routed through overseas payment accounts will be recharacterised and adjusted.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 47 and 48, the tax authority may make special tax adjustments, disregard the shell entity, recharacterise diverted income as taxable Chinese-source income, deny deducted management fees, impose 10% WHT on deemed dividends or royalties, and assess interest and daily surcharges; under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 36 and 62, fines up to RMB 10,000 for filing violations and 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax for fraudulent under-declaration may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controlling shareholders who abuse corporate personality to evade tax are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, interest, and penalties under PRC Company Law Article 23; civil asset restitution may be ordered out of personal estates; the legal representative and responsible directors may be placed on the tax serious dishonesty blacklist, resulting in travel bans, denial of exit visas, public dishonesty records, disqualification from serving as director, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to three years for relatively large evasion or three to seven years for huge evasion.
Tax Evasion through Unreported Offshore E-Commerce Receipts

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority must recover the underpaid tax and impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded amount; customs misdeclaration and e-commerce smuggling may trigger confiscation of goods and fines under Customs Law; false VAT invoices or fake logistics documentation may trigger Criminal Law Articles 205 and 208, and unlawful settlement through offshore payment rails may cross into anti-money-laundering enforcement.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFOs, and actual beneficial owners are subject to personal civil liability for restitution of diverted corporate assets under Company Law; they may be blacklisted for tax-related dishonesty with travel restrictions and cancellation of exit-entry approval; criminal conviction for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 or for money laundering under Article 191 can result in imprisonment up to three years, and for tax evasion involving huge amounts exceeding 30% of tax payable, imprisonment of three to seven years plus fines and forfeiture.
Fraudulent Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion Profit Shifting / Related-Party Financing

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 46 (thin capitalisation) and Article 47 (general anti-abuse), the tax authority may recharacterise excessive interest as dividends, disallow royalty deductions, and make transfer-pricing adjustments within a ten-year special tax adjustment period; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof and may result in penalties and interest surcharges under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 56 and the State Taxation Administration related-party transaction reporting rules.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior financial officers who knowingly execute false pricing, issue backdated invoices, or conceal related-party transactions are personally liable for the resulting tax assessments and civil damages; the responsible persons face tax blacklisting and travel bans; deliberate presentation of false returns or forged transfer-pricing documents to the tax authority is punishable under Criminal Law Article 201 and, if VAT invoices are fabricated, under Article 205 with imprisonment of up to seven years, together with fines and confiscation of assets.
