Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise engaged in online retail of maternity and infant care commodities under ISIC 4791, a virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address is generally not permissive for tax registration, bank account opening, or the issuance of a business license. The Company Law requires a registered address capable of receiving legal documents and performing actual management functions. Municipal tax authorities, the local market supervision administration, and commercial banks require a physical premises lease, a property ownership certificate, or a written lease filing with the local housing and urban-rural development authority. Remote-only companies may satisfy the physical-address requirement with a serviced office or a landlord-owned registered address only if the landlord permits tax inspection, regulatory visits, and bank due-diligence site visits, and the local SAMR accepts the address as a lawful business domicile. For e-commerce operators, the State Administration for Market Regulation allows a residential address to be registered for businesses under exempted residential-commercial conversion rules only where the local municipal government has promulgated a 'one address multiple licenses' or 'separate licensing' policy; the compliance team must obtain a written confirmation from the district administrative approval bureau before relying on any residential or virtual address.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to an ordinary online-retail WFOE in the People's Republic of China; the sole shareholder may determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, all share subscriptions must be fully paid in within five years after the date of incorporation (or in accordance with a more restrictive sector-specific rule, if any). No blocked escrow account or pre-deposit verification is required; however, foreign-currency capital must be injected through a designated capital account with a licensed bank and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange under the Foreign Exchange Registration Letter.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to the online retail business as no factory or chemical premises are involved. For the commercial office/warehouse used by the WFOE, the landlord/lessee must ensure the premises has obtained a fire safety inspection certificate or fire safety acceptance record under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China; the WFOE should implement an emergency evacuation plan, install fire-extinguishing equipment, and conduct a first-year workplace safety inspection. If the company operates any occupied workspace, occupational safety and health registration with the local emergency-management authority is expected under the Production Safety Law. If the company later opens a dedicated warehouse over 1,000 square meters or a high-cube distribution center, the local Fire and Rescue Brigade must issue a separate Civil Defense/Fire Protection Certificate before operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with electronic filing and registration conducted through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the entity's statutory taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes. Step 2: Complete the initial tax registration through the STA e-Tax portal within 30 days of business license issuance, submitting legal representative identity documents, articles of association, bank account information, registered address proof, and the board resolution appointing the financial officer and tax handler. Step 3: Undergo the taxpayer identification verification and calibration of tax regime; the WFOE is auto-registered as a Value Added Tax taxpayer, and if estimated annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, the entity must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold, it may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless voluntary election to general taxpayer status is filed. Step 4: Apply for the VAT invoice quota and activate the fully digitalized electronic invoice system in the e-Tax portal, including the digital anti-counterfeiting encryption activation. Step 5: Register the entity's customs and foreign trade operator credentials with Customs for export VAT zero-rating eligibility, and complete the opening of a foreign exchange settlement account with a designated domestic bank for export proceeds repatriation. For a foreign non-resident parent entity not itself registered in China, it does not receive a separate TIN; rather, it is subject to withholding through the WFOE or by direct filing under the STA's non-resident tax registration module when earning Chinese-source income.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China's Corporate Income Tax law does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election; the WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income. No hybrid mismatch elective classification exists for domestic or cross-border structures, and Chinese tax authorities classify entities based on their statutory legal form and place of incorporation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory once annual taxable sales of goods exceed RMB 5,000,000; all enterprises formed in China are automatically registered for basic VAT with the State Taxation Administration, while sales below the threshold may apply the small-scale taxpayer simplified method (currently at a 3% levy rate unless a temporary reduction applies).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods applies when: (i) the goods are physically exported from China's customs territory and declared under the customs export declaration; (ii) the sale is made by a Chinese resident VAT-taxable enterprise holding foreign trade operator registration and export license where required under customs regulations; (iii) export proceeds are received from an overseas buyer in a freely convertible currency and are repatriated to China's designated foreign exchange accounts within the statutory deadline; (iv) the goods are not on the prohibited or restricted export list or are accompanied by valid export permits if restricted; and (v) the transaction is supported by an authentic sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, and customs clearance evidence constituting a complete paper trail. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas recipients are also zero-rated when the services are consumed wholly outside China and are not classified as domestic value-added services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international carriage
  • Commercial Invoice in foreign currency
  • Packing List
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Collection Verification (FIRC or bank receipt)
  • Export contract with overseas buyer

China mandates real-time electronic invoicing through the fully digitalized electronic invoice (all-digital e-invoice) system under the Golden Tax Project Phase IV. VAT invoices must be transmitted automatically and continuously to the STA's centralized fiscal platform through approved APIs or the e-Tax portal, carrying a unique digital anti-counterfeiting number and cryptographic stamp. Any invoice that fails to clear the STA fiscal control system is invalid for tax credit and export refund purposes. Enterprises must retain invoice issuance logs, blockchain-style digital audit trails, and reconcile all sales records against the e-Tax data warehouse before, during, and after each VAT filing period.

The State Taxation Administration and General Administration of Customs conduct post-export verification and desk audits using cross-matching between customs export data, VAT invoice data, foreign exchange settlement records, and enterprise financial accounts. Any zero-rated export subsequently found to lack a genuine customs export declaration, to have no actual foreign exchange repatriation, or to contain materially false or forged documentation will trigger immediate disallowance of the zero-rating. The exporter must repay all input VAT credits claimed plus interest, and will be subject to a penalty of 50% up to five times the underpaid VAT under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. Customs may additionally impose administrative fines up to 50% of the declared value of exported goods for false declarations. Where fraudulent export invoices or deliberately fabricated export declarations are used to obtain VAT refunds, the case may be referred for criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, which carries criminal detention or imprisonment for up to ten years, or life imprisonment in aggravated circumstances, together with confiscation of property. The entity's export tax refund qualification will be suspended or permanently revoked, and its tax credit rating downgraded to the lowest tier.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods and services, profit remittances, dividends, and interest payments) under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2008, as amended). Capital account items remain heavily regulated, with all cross-border capital injections, debt financing, and equity investments requiring prior SAFE registration or approval through designated banks. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in online maternity and baby supplies ecommerce, the underlying operational cashflows are classified as current account receipts from domestic sales and cross-border export ecommerce. Export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency accounts as a matter of enterprise volition, but must be repatriated to China within a statutory period (generally 180 days from the date of customs declaration unless extended by SAFE). Surrender requirements are no longer mandatory as a fixed ratio; rather, enterprises may choose to convert export earnings to RMB or hold foreign currency, subject to verification of genuine trade background. Profit remittance to overseas shareholders is permitted provided the WFOE has completed prior year statutory audits, settled all applicable corporate income tax (including withholding tax on distributed profits at 10% under domestic law, potentially reduced under a tax treaty), and presented board resolutions together with audited financial statements to the remitting bank. Practical hurdles include thorough review by the remitting bank of the declared business scope, historical tax filings, and the absence of prior foreign exchange violations. Any suspicion of round-tripping or mismatch between invoice values and actual trade flows will trigger a genuine verification process, causing delays. Capital account injections, such as an increase in paid-in capital or cross-border shareholder loans, must be registered with SAFE through the Foreign Exchange Registration System and are subject to use restrictions; the RMB funds converted from capital contributions may only be used within the enterprise's approved business scope and may not be deployed for entrustment loans, securities investment, or real estate development outside the stated purpose.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Annual related-party transactions under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42 trigger mandatory Local File preparation when other intercompany transactions exceed RMB 40 million, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million or tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million; Master File obligations apply where the multinational enterprise group meets the consolidation or cross-border transaction thresholds under Chinese transfer pricing rules.

Economic substance scope: China Mainland does not impose UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, the beneficial-ownership test and the substance-over-form doctrine require the WFOE to perform Core Income-Generating Activities in mainland China. The WFOE must show local employees, operational office or infrastructure, board decisions, procurement, inventory management, order fulfillment, marketing and risk control in China; a passive shell siphoning e-commerce profits to an offshore entity without substance will have profits recharacterized, treaty benefits denied and withholding tax recalculated.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of underpaid tax plus daily surcharge of 0.05%, administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount under Articles 63 and 64 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion exceeding the statutory threshold, carrying imprisonment up to seven years in severe cases, fines and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Where directors caused or participated in the fraudulent filing, the corporate veil may be pierced under Company Law principles of separate personality abuse, making directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties and surcharges from personal assets; directors face travel bans, exit restrictions, blacklisting as dishonest judgment debtors, asset freezes, disqualification and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company and Double Tax Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the general anti-avoidance rule in Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and implementation regulations; reassessment at the statutory 10% withholding rate on the entire distribution; late-payment surcharge and 50% to 500% penalty for underwithheld tax; public disclosure and inclusion in the serious tax violation blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial ownership declarations or directed treaty-shopping structures may be personally liable for unpaid withholding tax and penalties through veil-piercing; civil asset restitution, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans preventing departure from China, director disqualification and criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion if the treaty relief was intentionally deceptive.
AML / Cross-Border E-Commerce Payment and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Control Regulations impose penalties of 5% to 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount, suspension or revocation of foreign exchange operation qualifications and blacklisting; customs false declaration may lead to forfeiture of goods, fines up to five times the dutiable value and criminal smuggling charges; VAT and Corporate Income Tax recomputation with late payment surcharges also applies.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally fined under foreign exchange and customs laws, subjected to asset attachment, travel bans, exit restriction orders and blacklisting as serious violators; if the conduct constitutes smuggling or foreign exchange fraud involving significant amounts, directors face criminal imprisonment up to ten years or more, monetary penalties, confiscation of personal assets and disqualification from serving as directors of Chinese enterprises.
