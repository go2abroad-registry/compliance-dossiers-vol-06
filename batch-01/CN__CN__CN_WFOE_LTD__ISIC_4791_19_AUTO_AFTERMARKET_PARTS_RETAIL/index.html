Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account are required for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaging in online retail within an unrestricted sector. The WFOE must nonetheless state a registered capital amount in the Articles of Association and must implement an enforceable capital contribution schedule. The sole shareholder must wire or transfer the subscribed capital within the agreed schedule, and the paid-up capital may not be diverted to related parties, returned to investors, or withdrawn through fictitious loans or artificial transactions. Capital contributions in installments must be completed within the contribution period specified in the Articles of Association, and the final paid-in capital amount must be reported to the local AMR through the annual information disclosure system.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through provincial, municipal and county tax service bureaus; online registrations, filings and compliance actions are performed on the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) taxpayer portal and supported by the National 12366 Taxpayer Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the Business License, which carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: Under the multi-license integration regime, the SAMR automatically transmits the formation data to the STA so the USCC becomes the statutory Tax Identification Number without a separate initial tax-registration application. Step 3: Within 30 days of license issuance, complete the supplementary tax registration in the e-Tax portal by confirming the legal representative, finance controller, registered address, fiscal year, accounting system, bank settlement account, business scope, and intended VAT taxpayer status. Step 4: The STA activates VAT and CIT profiles and issues the electronic taxpayer profile; the WFOE may then be classified as a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer or a General VAT Taxpayer in accordance with statutory rules. Step 5: Register the employer withholding obligations for individual income tax, social insurance, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and stamp duty, and complete real-name authentication with tax-control digital certificate for e-Tax access. Step 6: For foreign shareholders, file their non-resident tax identification data and treaty residency information in the e-Tax corporate information layer, and establish a domestic finance/tax filing authority or authorize a licensed tax agent for continuous compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No PRC corporate income tax check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid mismatch election is available; a WFOE is always taxed at entity level as a PRC resident enterprise. The relevant statutory elections are VAT-related: a taxpayer whose annual taxable sales are below CNY 5,000,000 may elect registration as a General VAT Taxpayer; if annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, General VAT Taxpayer registration is mandatory. A Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer may elect the simplified levy method, with the statutory levy rate applicable to small-scale taxpayers. No other entity classification elections are available for this structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales on a rolling 12-month basis is the statutory threshold for mandatory registration as a General VAT Taxpayer; below this threshold an entity may remain a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer. A WFOE is not exempt from holding a VAT identity because the USCC obtained at incorporation is simultaneously the VAT/TIN under the integrated tax registration system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim the 0% VAT export rate on automotive replacement parts, the WFOE must (1) be a General VAT Taxpayer; (2) sell the goods to a non-resident buyer and cause the goods to be physically exported from mainland China, either under the WFOE's own customs declaration or through a licensed export agent under a deemed-export arrangement; (3) receive export proceeds in foreign currency or legitimate cross-border RMB from the overseas buyer; (4) retain complete customs, logistics, invoicing, payment, and foreign-exchange records that match the export declaration; and (5) ensure the goods are not prohibited from export. Output VAT on exported goods is 0%; input VAT on purchases attributable to the exported goods is refundable only up to the statutory VAT export rebate rate applicable to the HS-8708 auto parts category. A Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer is not eligible for the zero-rate-with-refund mechanism and may only use exemption-without-refund unless it registers as a General VAT Taxpayer.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Export Declaration Form) with release endorsement and matching electronic customs data; the Bill of Entry is an import instrument and is not used for Chinese exports
  • Original Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document, such as an air waybill, rail/road consignment note, or express waybill, evidencing the physical departure of the goods from mainland China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank/SAFE-filed Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation, together with foreign-exchange settlement and receipt records linked to the export transaction
  • Prepared commercial invoice, packing list, signed export sales contract or purchase order, and, for e-commerce sales, platform transaction records and logistics tracking data reconciled to the customs declaration and VAT return

China mandates near-real-time fiscal e-invoicing under the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice reform. VAT invoices must be issued from the taxpayer's digital e-invoice account on the e-Tax platform; invoice data is cleared and uploaded to the STA before the invoice can be used as an input-VAT credit voucher. Special VAT invoices are subject to buyer-side confirmation in the National VAT Invoice Verification and Comprehensive Service Platform, and mismatches are blocked. Anti-fraud controls include the national invoice code and number database, seller-buyer data matching, aggregate sales/risk monitoring, cross-matching with customs and banking data, and the statutory electronic-format requirement; fully digitalized invoices need no physical tax-control stamp but carry a unique electronic invoice number and digital authentication.

The STA and China Customs perform retrospective export zero-rating and refund audits using risk-based cross-matching of customs declarations, e-invoices, logistics tracking, bank receipts, and SAFE foreign-exchange collection data. If the documentation bundle is incomplete, the export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or the zero-rating is not substantiated, the VAT refund is reversed or suspended and the sale may be re-assessed at the 13% standard VAT rate with a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Fraudulent export-rebate claims are sanctioned under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection by recovery of the refund plus a monetary fine of one to five times the fraudulently obtained amount; criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund can result in imprisonment or life imprisonment with fines and property confiscation. The STA may also impose enhanced monitoring, invoice restrictions, and retrospective review for the full legal assessment period, including indefinite recovery where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime that maintains RMB convertibility for current account transactions and controlled convertibility for capital account transactions. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise engaged in online retail of automotive replacement parts, current account receipts and payments, including export sales proceeds, supplier import costs, logistics fees, and e-commerce platform service charges, are broadly convertible subject to genuine trade verification under the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment management system. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment capital injection, require bank-led SAFE foreign exchange registration, an approved equity capital account, and post-injection reporting through the SAFE annual FDI declaration. Profit remittance is conditional upon tax clearance, audited financial statements, payment of any applicable dividend withholding tax, and completion of the annual SAFE registration renewal. Export proceeds must be repatriated through designated banks; conversion may be performed at market rates, but the retained foreign currency balances remain subject to periodic SAFE reporting. Overall, RMB convertibility is genuine on the current account and permissible on the capital account only with rigorous documentary evidence and regulatory supervision.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, all export foreign exchange proceeds of the WFOE must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign currency settlement account without delay after receipt from foreign buyers; the bank determines the exact time-bound credit deadline, generally understood as 90 calendar days from the export customs declaration date. There is no mandatory 100% conversion ratio because SAFE has abolished the forced surrender requirements; the enterprise may convert its export receipts into RMB at the prevailing market rate or retain the foreign currency in a settlement account, with every transaction reported through the cross-border payment verification system for trade in goods.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech E-money institutions have low viability as primary legal clearing rails for a mainland China WFOE. The PBOC and SAFE frameworks require formal foreign exchange settlement to occur through licensed domestic banking channels; a pure offshore EMI without a Chinese payment business license cannot hold RMB deposits or execute FX conversion for a Chinese entity. PayPal is permitted to act as an overseas payment aggregation tool where buyer funds ultimately need to be repatriated through a domestic bank, while Stripe does not maintain an official licensed Chinese onshore receiving entity for general e-commerce settlement. Such EMIs should therefore be used only as downstream sales transaction facilitators, not as corporate treasury settlement rails, and any platform payout must be remitted to the WFOE's domestic tier-1 bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas investor shall transmit registered capital via SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection, naming the WFOE as beneficiary with the bank's designated foreign exchange capital account. The receiving bank must verify the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, the updated business license, the corporate seal registration, and the electronically filed applicant identity details. Upon credit, a licensed Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report to support the subsequent conversion of foreign currency into RMB and onward transfer to the operational RMB settlement account. All funds must be utilised strictly for the approved WFOE business scope and reported in the SAFE annual direct investment statistics.Outbound supplier payments for automotive parts are settled through three clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for open-account and letter-of-credit reimbursements, irrevocable documentary letters of credit for large or high-value purchase orders requiring bank payment undertaking, and documentary collections when the WFOE and foreign supplier have an established trade history. For RMB trade settlements with belt-and-road and swap-covered counterparties, the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) is the recommended rail, while foreign-currency settlements flow through corresponding banking networks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech E-money institutions have low viability as primary legal clearing rails for a mainland China WFOE. The PBOC and SAFE frameworks require formal foreign exchange settlement to occur through licensed domestic banking channels; a pure offshore EMI without a Chinese payment business license cannot hold RMB deposits or execute FX conversion for a Chinese entity. PayPal is permitted to act as an overseas payment aggregation tool where buyer funds ultimately need to be repatriated through a domestic bank, while Stripe does not maintain an official licensed Chinese onshore receiving entity for general e-commerce settlement. Such EMIs should therefore be used only as downstream sales transaction facilitators, not as corporate treasury settlement rails, and any platform payout must be remitted to the WFOE's domestic tier-1 bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under State Taxation Administration Announcement [2016] No. 42, the Local File is mandatory for any of the following in a tax year: intangible-property transfers greater than RMB 100 million, financial-asset transactions greater than RMB 100 million, tangible-property transfers greater than RMB 200 million, or other related-party transactions greater than RMB 40 million. The Master File is prepared when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion or when otherwise required by written SAT notice.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations to ordinary WFOEs, but the Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 GAAR, the substance-over-form doctrine, and the beneficial-ownership rules require the WFOE to prove actual existence, premises, payroll, inventory risk, and decision-making. For an auto-parts e-commerce operation, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) comprise sourcing, quality inspection, inventory management, online platform operations, payment processing, and customer service. If these functions are performed abroad by the parent or a sister company, the WFOE may be treated as a shell, denied treaty relief, or recharacterized for the entire profit stream.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fabricated procurement and false special VAT invoice crime

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection: recovery of underpaid tax, 0.05% daily late surcharge, and monetary penalty of 50% up to 500% of the underpaid amount. Article 205 of the Criminal Law: for false special VAT invoice issuance or purchase, imprisonment of 3 to 10 years plus fine, or life imprisonment and confiscation of property for exceptionally large amounts.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes or benefits from false invoicing is jointly and severally liable to restore the corporate treasury; Chinese courts may pierce the WFOE's limited liability veil; the director is subject to a restrictive exit or travel ban, freezing of personal assets, disqualification as legal representative, and criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Articles 205 and 201, typically 3 to 7 years and up to life where the false-invoice amount is especially huge.
Transfer pricing manipulation and undocumented related-party transactions

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 permit tax authority adjustment to the arm's-length result; the taxpayer pays the additional CIT, daily late surcharge, administrative penalty of 50% to 500% if the adjustment is characterized as evasion, and overdue adjustment interest is charged at the PBOC benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points for non-compliant arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer-pricing documentation or misstate intercompany contracts face direct civil liability, a travel ban, disqualification orders, and Chinese criminal tax-evasion liability under Criminal Law Article 201; financial institutions may also blacklist the company and the directors for serious tax-credit defaults.
Shell WFOE and abusive treaty claim without economic substance

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law GAAR permits the tax authority to recharacterize the arrangement, deny the DTA benefit, impose the statutory 10% withholding tax, assess additional CIT plus daily late surcharge, and levy an administrative penalty under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 63 if the abuse involved falsified beneficial-owner declarations.

Director personal liability: If a director signs false beneficial-ownership declarations or causes dividend distributions while the company is insolvent, the court can disregard limited liability under civil law for malicious reduction of assets; directors face exit bans, personal execution of tax arrears, and criminal liability for fraudulent capital flight or false declarations if funds were routed abroad under Criminal Law Article 190 or Article 201.
