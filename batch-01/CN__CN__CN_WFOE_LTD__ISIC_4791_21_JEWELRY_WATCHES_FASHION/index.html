Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing administered through the national e-Tax portal (China Electronic Taxpayer Administration Portal).

E-government filing portal

For the incorporated WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation is the statutorily recognised taxpayer identification number (TIN). The WFOE must complete online tax registration within 30 calendar days of business licence issuance through the local STA e-tax platform, uploading the business licence, articles of association, beneficial ownership and equity structure, board resolution on tax affairs, office lease or proof of address, and operational bank account details, after which the STA activates the taxpayer status and VAT invoice entitlements. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese fixed place of business, the statutory TIN procurement procedure requires filing a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the local STA office where the Chinese revenue-producing activity arises or where the withholding agent is located; the local STA then issues a non-resident taxpayer identification number, and the appointed Chinese withholding agent administers all VAT and CIT withholding filings. Cross-border e-commerce vendors selling into China through approved cross-border e-commerce platforms may instead obtain a VAT registration through the platform or a fiscal representative as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no U.S.-style check-the-box election and no elective hybrid entity regime. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese tax resident enterprise subject to entity-level taxation on its worldwide income. The only relevant indirect tax election is that a small-scale VAT taxpayer with annual taxable turnover below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to be registered as a general VAT taxpayer if it maintains proper accounting records, which is an invoice regime election rather than a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold above which mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies; every WFOE is required to complete VAT registration immediately upon obtaining its business licence regardless of turnover, and non-resident suppliers of cross-border services must register with the STA or appoint a domestic withholding agent to collect VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT zero-rating for exported jewellery, watches and fashion goods applies where the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from China under a valid customs export declaration, the sale is made to a foreign non-resident buyer, the export consideration is received through a licensed Chinese bank and repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export (unless an express extension is granted), and the customs declaration, logistics documents and banking receipts are consistent. Qualifying offshore digital services (including qualifying software, data and design services) supplied by Chinese tax residents to non-resident customers may also be zero-rated only if the service is expressly listed in the STA zero-rated cross-border services catalogue, is consumed entirely outside China, and the exporter retains contracts, invoices and FX settlement evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form processed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Chinese bank export FX settlement certificate evidencing repatriation
  • Export VAT Refund Application and reconciliation schedule mapping exported goods to sales invoices and FX receipts

Invoice issuance is mandated exclusively through the STA digital electronic invoice platform and its provincial e-tax interfaces. Taxpayers must connect invoicing systems to the STA via the licensed tax-control API, using an STA-issued tax key to apply a cryptographic tax-authority seal, unique invoice code and anti-fraud matrix/QR stamp. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in near-real time at the point of issuance; manual offline invoice runs are prohibited for general taxpayers, and the platform performs automatic cross-matching with customs export declarations and bank FX settlement data for export VAT purposes.

The STA and customs/foreign exchange authorities execute cross-checking of export declaration, freight and FX settlement data. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or the goods are deemed never to have been physically exported, the zero-rating is retroactively reversed and the exporter must pay output VAT at 13% from the export date plus daily overdue surcharge at 0.05% of the unpaid amount. Any VAT refund already obtained is clawed back with interest. Fraudulent zero-rating claims, counterfeit export declaration documents or false FIRC/FX settlement certificates trigger penalties of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Tax Administration and Collection Law, export tax rebate privilege suspension, credit blacklisting, and criminal liability for export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law, including imprisonment up to life sentence for large-amount cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system in which the CNY is broadly convertible for current-account purposes but is not freely tradable or offshore-deliverable. On the current account, genuine trade in goods and services can be settled through SAFE-designated banks with invoice, contract and customs documentation; banks apply the real-client, real-transaction rule. On the capital account, the WFOE's inbound equity capital is permitted only after FDI information registration and negative-list review; the contribution is lodged in a Foreign-Currency Capital Account and may be converted to CNY at a licensed bank, but the bank must verify that the settled funds will not be used in prohibited sectors. Profit distribution to the foreign parent qualifies as current-account direct-investment income, yet remittance requires audited financial statements, previous-year enterprise income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax clearance, and a notarised board resolution. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days of the customs export declaration, but no fixed mandatory conversion ratio exists; the enterprise may retain 100% in foreign currency or settle 100% into CNY at the onshore market rate, subject to SAFE monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign-currency current account within 180 days of the Customs export declaration, unless a longer trade-receivables period has been approved and recorded in the SAFE trade-monitoring system. There is no statutory mandatory conversion percentage; the exporter may keep the entire receipt in foreign currency or sell the entire amount to the onshore bank at the prevailing market price, provided every export receipt is matched to the original customs declaration and bank report within the reporting window. Proceeds cannot be deposited offshore or diverted to personal accounts; any use must be consistent with the registered business scope.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment platforms are not viable as the primary treasury account or clearing provider for a mainland WFOE. Under PBOC payment-licence rules and SAFE cross-border payment administration, a payment institution must hold a PBOC payment business licence and a SAFE cross-border payment permit before it can open onshore accounts or handle settlement of Chinese-domiciled merchants. Offshore EMIs generally do not obtain these licences and are therefore legally barred from servicing the WFOE as a domestic bank. They may operate only as an auxiliary front-end collection gateway for overseas consumers (Stripe, PayPal or similar), in which case funds must be settled into the WFOE's onshore licensed bank account through a registered cross-border acquiring or settlement partner. Feasibility rating: Not viable as primary account; auxiliary gateway viability is Medium only if the EMI uses a PBOC/SAFE-licensed settlement channel and all transactions are independently reported.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent must remit equity capital by SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 with cover details) to the WFOE's foreign-currency capital account at a SAFE-designated Tier-1 Chinese bank. The wire narrative should clearly state 'equity capital contribution' and reference the FDI registration number, and the bank must record the transaction with the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code for direct-investment equity capital (typically 622011 or the code set out in the bank's cross-border reporting manual). On receipt, the bank will verify the FDI registration, negative-list eligibility, remitter identity and purpose code; once satisfied, the funds can be settled into CNY in a designated capital-settlement account and moved to the operational RMB account after the board resolution and intended-use statement are provided. Capital verification is completed through a Chinese certified public accountant's capital-verification report or a bank-confirmed paid-in-capital audit trail, and the subsequent use of settled funds must not enter prohibited investments such as securities or entrusted loans.Supplier payments and third-party logistics costs should be executed by SWIFT MT103 wire transfer for direct FX disbursements, supported by the import contract, commercial invoice and customs declaration. For larger jewellery, watch or raw-material purchases, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit at sight is preferred, with the document package including bill of lading/airway bill, insurance certificate, commercial invoice, packing list and certificates of origin. Documentary collections (D/P at sight or D/A) can be used for recurring, trusted counterparties when a full L/C is not required. For RMB-denominated trade contracts, the WFOE should use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) through its Tier-1 bank to reduce FX conversion costs, with each outbound payment reported in the SAFE cross-border reporting system and matched to the underlying trade documents.Deliverable CNY/USD and CNY/EUR forward contracts through an onshore SAFE-designated bank, tenor up to 12 months, backed by real current-account exposure documentation (export orders, import invoices, royalty or logistics contracts).Onshore cross-currency swaps between CNY and USD/EUR for capital-account hedging of the registered FDI capital or trade-related receivables, subject to SAFE review and demonstration of a genuine underlying exposure.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the mainland WFOE itself because the CNY is not deliverable offshore; only the foreign parent entity may execute offshore NDFs against the WFOE's currency exposure, and the WFOE must never enter speculative or uncovered currency positions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment platforms are not viable as the primary treasury account or clearing provider for a mainland WFOE. Under PBOC payment-licence rules and SAFE cross-border payment administration, a payment institution must hold a PBOC payment business licence and a SAFE cross-border payment permit before it can open onshore accounts or handle settlement of Chinese-domiciled merchants. Offshore EMIs generally do not obtain these licences and are therefore legally barred from servicing the WFOE as a domestic bank. They may operate only as an auxiliary front-end collection gateway for overseas consumers (Stripe, PayPal or similar), in which case funds must be settled into the WFOE's onshore licensed bank account through a registered cross-border acquiring or settlement partner. Feasibility rating: Not viable as primary account; auxiliary gateway viability is Medium only if the EMI uses a PBOC/SAFE-licensed settlement channel and all transactions are independently reported.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million of annual related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million of related-party intangible asset or financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million of other related-party service transactions; the Master File is required when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion; contemporaneous documentation must be filed within 15 days of a tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: China applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form doctrine to disregard structures without a reasonable commercial purpose. For treaty claims, the Bespoke Economic Substance and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) tests are applied by Chinese tax authorities when reviewing offshore intermediate holding companies; a Hong Kong, Singapore, or other treaty intermediary will not obtain treaty benefits if it lacks premises, employees, bank accounts, board decisions, risk assumption, and substantive control over the Chinese WFOE. Although mainland China does not have an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing regime comparable to offshore financial centers, the WFOE must maintain real business substance in China and the foreign parent must demonstrate its own substantive activity in its residence jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Reporting of E-Commerce Revenue on Jewelry and Watches

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law authorizes recovery of the underpaid tax with daily interest at 0.05% and a fine of 50% to 5 times the tax understated; Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years and monetary fines for fraudulent tax evasion; Article 205 exposes directors to criminal liability for false issuance of VAT invoices, including aggravated custodial sentences in serious cases.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors personally directed or participated in the concealment of revenue; directors and legal representatives are jointly and severally liable for restitution of tax, interest, and penalties, and may face freezing of personal bank accounts, seizure of personal assets, blacklisting and travel bans (limited departure), and criminal imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Default and Treaty/Substance Abuse on Dividends and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37 and 40 impose withholding at 10% on China-source payments to non-residents; Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 3 times the tax not withheld; Articles 47 and 120 of the EIT Law allow treaty benefits to be denied where the structure lacks commercial substance and may recharacterize the income with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve unpaid withholdings or false treaty claims are personally exposed to civil restitution, bank account freezes, seizure of personal assets, blacklisting and exit bans, and criminal liability for tax evasion if the non-withholding was intentional; they may also be subject to information exchange under the Common Reporting Standard and Mutual Legal Assistance agreements.
Falsified Transfer Pricing Documentation / Thin Capitalization and Shell Service Charges

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 45 and 46 together with SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 empower the tax authority to make special tax adjustments based on the arm's length standard; if documentation is not provided, the interest surcharge is increased by 3 percentage points above the benchmark loan rate; Article 110 of the Tax Administration Law imposes fines up to RMB 50,000 for non-cooperation; additional CIT, interest, and a 50% to 5 times penalty may be assessed for understatement.

Director personal liability: A director who signs false transfer pricing documentation or directs the creation of shell service contracts can be held civilly liable for the resulting tax underpayment, subject to personal asset seizure and travel restrictions; criminal exposure arises when the false documentation is part of a scheme to evade tax, leading to tax evasion imprisonment of up to 7 years and additional criminal liability for fraudulent financing, forgery, or corruption-related offenses.
