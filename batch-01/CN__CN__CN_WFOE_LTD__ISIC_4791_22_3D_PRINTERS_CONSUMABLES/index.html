Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing portal: Electronic Tax Bureau of the State Taxation Administration (etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

A WFOE is incorporated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Upon issuance of the business license, the SAMR assigns a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes; no separate VAT registration number is issued. The SAMR shares the registration record electronically with the STA, completing automatic tax registration. The WFOE must then submit taxpayer supplementary information, financial accounting system records, bank deposit account information, VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale), and electronic invoice service activation through the Electronic Tax Bureau. Foreign non-resident shareholders must provide notarized and legalized identity and incorporation documents for the SAMR record.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A WFOE is incorporated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Upon issuance of the business license, the SAMR assigns a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes; no separate VAT registration number is issued. The SAMR shares the registration record electronically with the STA, completing automatic tax registration. The WFOE must then submit taxpayer supplementary information, financial accounting system records, bank deposit account information, VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale), and electronic invoice service activation through the Electronic Tax Bureau. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods qualifies for 0% VAT when the WFOE, as a VAT general taxpayer, exports the 3D printers and consumables from mainland China to a destination outside mainland China under a valid customs export declaration and can evidence physical movement, delivery, and receipt. Input VAT on purchased goods is refundable through the export-rebate mechanism. Cross-border digital or remote services qualify for zero-rating if supplied to a non-resident recipient, consumed entirely outside mainland China, and paid for in foreign exchange or RMB settled from foreign exchange. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports under the dedicated e-commerce customs supervision model are also eligible for zero-rating or exemption in accordance with STA and General Administration of Customs rules.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration with customs release record (electronic data via the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or express/post consignment proof evidencing export delivery
  • Commercial invoice and packing list as filed with the customs declaration
  • Export sales contract or order confirmation
  • Bank receipt for export proceeds in foreign exchange or SAFE export proceeds collection verification record (inward remittance certificate equivalent)

China mandates the national fully digitalized electronic invoice system. VAT invoices are issued through the Electronic Tax Bureau or approved straight-through enterprise APIs and reported in real time to the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Each invoice carries a statutory invoice code, serial number, QR code, and cryptographic verification; no physical fiscal stamp is affixed. Batch invoicing requires API integration with the unified national VAT invoice service platform for real-time anti-fraud clearance.

If the export goods do not leave China, documentation is inconsistent with customs or SAFE records, proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or the zero-rating claim is otherwise unverifiable, the STA will deny or claw back the zero-rated treatment and recover the export VAT refund. Tax authorities may impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05 percent and a penalty of 50 percent up to 500 percent of the unpaid or improperly refunded tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent obtaining of export tax refunds is also criminal fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with exposure to imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of assets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under a bifurcated current and capital account system. The current account is broadly convertible for genuine trade and service transactions, subject to documentary review and post-verification by designated banks. The capital account remains heavily controlled: foreign direct investment requires Foreign Investment Record Filing with corresponding capital injection, use, and repatriation constraints. Export proceeds generally must be repatriated and may be retained in foreign currency or settled into renminbi at the enterprise's discretion, subject to return-of-capital verification and authenticity checks. Profit remittance by a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is permitted only after annual audit, tax clearance, and the issuance of a Foreign-Invested Enterprise Profit Distribution resolution; the distribution must be funded from verified after-tax profits and must comply with the registration and authenticity review procedures of the designated receiving bank. Dividends may be remitted offshore in either RMB or foreign currency, but the supporting documentation package must include tax payment proofs, audited financial statements, and the board shareholder resolution. Any offshore borrowing, intercompany loan repayment, or outward guarantee must be filed with SAFE under the macro-prudential foreign debt management framework. Sovereign debt, equity conversion, and large cross-border transactions require a higher level of regulatory scrutiny and can be delayed by anti-money laundering and capital-flow screening.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts from 3D printer and consumables cross-border retail sales must be repatriated to China. There is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio; enterprises are allowed to retain export proceeds as foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB at a licensed bank. Repatriation must be completed within the statutory time frame of no more than six months from the date of customs declaration unless a genuine extension, such as settlement delay due to e-commerce return claims, is approved by the bank based on underlying documentation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), administratively under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) and offshore virtual banking platforms are largely non-viable for legal entity banking in mainland China due to strict banking licensing requirements and the prohibition on unlicensed deposit-taking and payment services. Only licensed Chinese payment institutions, such as Alipay and WeChat Pay, operate within the sanctioned payment-clearing framework and are not substitutes for a corporate bank account. Offshore digital banks and neobanks cannot provide compliant MTL/EMI account services to a mainland-incorporated WFOE unless they operate a licensed branch or wholly owned sub-bank within China's banking regulatory framework. The recommended route is to use a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for local accounts and to rely on international RMB clearing through CIPS or SWIFT for inbound and outbound corporate funds. Use of unlicensed offshore EMI rails for this entity would create capital control violations, expose the treasury operation to frozen funds, and fail SAFE and PBOC compliance checks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment is routed through the WFOE's capital escrow account opened with the designated Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The foreign shareholder must remit funds from an overseas corporate account with the SWIFT MT103 message containing a clear purpose description matching SAFE's Foreign Exchange Capital Project Registration System entry. The bank verifies the Foreign Investment Record Filing, business license, and SAFE registration, then credits the capital account. Subsequent conversion to RMB is done against a 'Capital Use True-Platform' payment instruction listing the recipient, purpose, and corresponding transaction evidence. For e-commerce operating capital, the injected funds are only allowed for self-use and must not be used for domestic equity investment, entrusted loans, or non-business real estate. The bank will monitor the capital account usage through the SAFE digital capital monitoring platform and may require the exporter to produce import orders and service procurement contracts.Trade disbursements for the purchase of 3D printers, resin filaments, spare parts, and logistics services are settled via the domestic bank's SWIFT MT103 for supplier invoices below the documentary credit threshold, using the correct trade purpose codes such as '121010' for general goods import and '122030' for freight logistics. Larger or relationship-based purchases can be settled via irrevocable commercial letters of credit (MT700) with delayed payment terms, while smaller repeat purchases can use documentary collections under URC522. The bank will perform first-then-Xiaoshen verification (pre-settlement audit) and will require the underlying customs declaration, commercial invoice, and proof of goods receipt to release the outward remittance. A separate RMB clearing rail such as CIPS can be used for cross-border RMB payments to Chinese suppliers that have banking relationships with overseas branches, but SAFE's payment purpose record must remain consistent.Onshore forward foreign exchange contracts (spot plus forward or plain CNH/USD CNY forward) for transaction exposure backed by real trade documents with licensed domestic banks, subject to the bank's credit line and SAFE trade genuineness checks.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not allowed for onshore Chinese resident entities except for specific hedging scenarios approved by SAFE for eligible foreign corporates through designated onshore financial institutions; therefore the WFOE should strongly prefer deliverable forwards and currency swaps over NDFs.Currency swaps onshore for foreign currency debt or RMB hedging with a tenor matching the underlying trade or financial liability, requiring bank liquidity approval and SAFE registration where applicable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 and the PRC EIT Law if any related-party transaction category in a tax year exceeds: tangible asset transfers RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions RMB 40 million. Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations are triggered at group level where the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the prior year equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion; all controlled transaction forms must be attached to the annual CIT filing.

Economic substance scope: China does not administer a separate Economic Substance Regulations code of the offshore-centre type, but the PRC EIT Law Articles 41, 47, and 48 apply an equivalent anti-abuse framework: arrangements without a reasonable commercial purpose may be adjusted or disregarded by the State Taxation Administration, and treaty benefits or deductions will be denied where the non-resident counterparty lacks substance. Core income-generating activities (CIGA) for an online retail WFOE of 3D printers and consumables include inventory ownership and risk, procurement, pricing, logistics, payment acceptance, customer service, warranty decisions, and the assumption of marketplace chargebacks; those functions cannot be artificially assigned to an offshore shell entity if the people and day-to-day control remain in China. A foreign principal that merely licenses a brand and holds IP while the WFOE performs all CIGA may create a Chinese permanent establishment for that foreign principal and expose the WFOE to withholding and transfer-pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Documentation Default and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, and 48 authorize the tax authority to re-determine taxable income on an arm's length basis and to impose benchmark interest plus five percentage points from the adjustment period; PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 62 allows fines up to RMB 10,000 for documentation failures, and LTAC Article 63 allows fines of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax where false evidence is used.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the false related-party declarations are jointly exposed to civil liability for the short-paid tax under the PRC Civil Code joint tort rules; they may be placed on the STA serious tax violation blacklist, subjected to an exit-control and travel ban, and personally prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 where the amount of concealed taxable income is large, carrying imprisonment of up to seven years plus fines.
Shell Company Fictional E-commerce Turnover and Export-Rebate Fraud

Statutory sanction: PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 63 penalizes false filings with 50%-500% of the evaded tax; Article 66 specifically penalizes falsely obtaining export tax refunds with a fine of one to five times the refund claimed and suspension of export tax rebates; Criminal Law Article 204 punishes fraudulent export-rebate arrangements from imprisonment up to ten years for serious cases and up to life imprisonment where the amount is especially huge.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and signing directors who orchestrated the fictitious sales and export documents are jointly and severally liable to return the fraudulently obtained rebates with money damages; they are subject to asset freezing, entry-exit restrictions and blacklisting under the social credit enforcement system; and they face personal fraud convictions, with imprisonment terms scaled according to the amounts involved and with the penalty treasury pursuing their personal assets after corporate assets are exhausted.
Disguised Profit Repatriation, Treaty Shopping and AML Concealment

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 37 obligates the WFOE as a withholding agent to withhold 10%; failure to withhold triggers LTAC Article 69, which imposes a fine of 50%-300% of the uncollected tax, together with the unpaid tax and daily surcharges; EIT Law Article 47 permits the tax authority to recharacterize the treaty-shopped payments as dividends and apply income-true-up together with special adjustment interest; serious cross-border concealment engages Criminal Law Article 191 (money laundering) and Article 201 (tax evasion).

Director personal liability: The directors who controlled the offshore conduit and concealed the true character of the profit distribution can be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid withholding tax and penalties, blacklisted for major tax violations, banned from leaving China while obligations are unresolved, and subject to criminal imprisonment for tax evasion and money laundering, with the court piercing the corporate veil where the companies were used as a sham to evade tax.
