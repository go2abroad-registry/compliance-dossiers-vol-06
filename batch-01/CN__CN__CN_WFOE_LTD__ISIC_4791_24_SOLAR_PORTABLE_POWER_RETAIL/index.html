Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal is the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), operated nationally by the STA and provincially by local offices, accessible through the STA's official website and local tax service portals.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, tax registration is integrated with market registry formalities. After issuance of the business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), the enterprise tax registry is automatically populated by the State Taxation Administration via interoperability with the State Administration for Market Regulation. The USCC serves as the enterprise's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT payer identification number. Foreign non-resident enterprises, where they have no permanent establishment but derive Chinese-source income, must apply for a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identity through the competent STA office where the withholding agent is located, or where the income arises, by submitting a tax residence certificate issued by the country of residence, an application for tax registration, and the underlying contract or agreement evidencing Chinese-source income. The STA will then issue a non-resident taxpayer identifier in the form of a tax identification number embedded in the system for withholding reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime comparable to the United States, and no hybrid mismatch elections are available. Enterprise income tax classification follows the Enterprise Income Tax Law's resident enterprise and non-resident enterprise dichotomy, supplemented by the special tax adjustment and controlled foreign corporation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises carrying on business in China must complete VAT registration immediately upon issuance of the business license; there is no monthly minimum threshold to register. A turnover threshold of RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales triggers mandatory classification as a general VAT taxpayer, while presumptive or small-scale taxpayer status applies below that threshold with simplified collection rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of VAT applies to exported goods, including portable battery generators and solar panels, where the taxpayer with general VAT status sells and physically ships the goods from mainland China to a foreign buyer outside the customs territory. Eligibility is established when the customs authority releases the goods under an export declaration reflecting the export enterprise, commodity HS code, quantity, and destination. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports may use the special cross-border e-commerce export regime, which likewise grants a 0% VAT rate and, in appropriate designated markets, export VAT rebates if the goods are shipped from a registered bonded warehouse or declared through the e-commerce retail export channel. The taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous records of the transaction, platform sales opulence trading, logistics trail, and foreign exchange receipts to demonstrate authentic cross-border trade.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration submitted via the China International Trade Single Window with electronic release data
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international courier shipment manifest covering the entire carriage
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt record and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) export proceeds verification demonstrating repatriation
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sale contract or platform purchase order
  • VAT export rebate electronic application e-file submitted through the e-Tax Bureau export tax rebate module

China operates a mandatory national electronic invoice control regime under the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Unified E-Invoice Service Platform. VAT invoices, including fully digitised electronic invoices (FDEI), must be issued in real time through STA-authorised systems; invoice metadata, recipient details, and amounts are transmitted to the STA electronically at the moment of issuance. All newly registered enterprises, including WFOEs, must complete FDEI configuration in the e-Tax Bureau before issuing VAT invoices. No offline invoice creation or manual fiscal stamping is permitted for general VAT taxpayers engaged in retail e-commerce.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinised by the STA against customs release data, bank foreign exchange settlement records, and logistics information. In the event of a false declaration, fraudulent VAT invoice use, failure to collect or repatriate export proceeds, or inability to substantiate the zero-rated export, the taxpayer is liable to repay any VAT refund or zero-rated input credit claimed, pay a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% on the outstanding tax, and be subject to a monetary penalty of between 50% and 500% of the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Criminal accountability for export VAT refund fraud or fraudulent issuance of VAT invoices may also attach under the Criminal Law, and the responsible persons may additionally be placed on the State Taxation Administration's list of seriously dishonest taxpayers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is fully convertible on the current account for bona fide trade and service transactions once the underlying economic activity is verified under SAFE's trade authenticity framework. Capital account convertibility, however, remains administratively controlled. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) receiving FDI equity injections must first complete foreign exchange registration with SAFE through a designated domestic bank, open a capital account foreign currency settlement account, and register each inward remittance. Proceeds converted into RMB under the capital account settlement mechanism must be used within the entity's business scope on a positive-list basis; prohibited outflows include domestic equity investment, lending, trust financial products, and non-operating real estate purchases unless separately licensed. Profit repatriation is treated as a current-account remittance after compliance with tax law: the WFOE must present certification of annual audits, tax clarity certificates, profit reserve accrual evidence, and audited financial statements to the remitting bank, which will verify the amount and SAFE registration. Dividends are legally distributable only after corporate income tax settlement and required reserve provisions. The surrendering requirement for export proceeds has been progressively liberalized, but trade-related foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China, and their authenticity must be supported by customs declarations, logistics documents, and invoices before conversion. A WFOE engaged in cross-border e-commerce retail must therefore build accounting infrastructure that reconciles platform receivables, export declarations, and foreign exchange receipts in real time to withstand bank and SAFE scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a mandatory foreign exchange surrender ratio or a compulsory conversion rate on export receipts. Under SAFE's trade in goods administration rules, all export proceeds from a WFOE must be repatriated into a domestic current-account foreign exchange settlement account unless SAFE has approved an offshore foreign exchange account for specific trade structures. The enterprise may choose to retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or convert them into RMB through the designated bank. Repatriation timing is generally required within 90 days after the export customs declaration date, with a possible extension to 180 days for enterprises on SAFE's approved whitelist or for established recurring trade patterns. Each export settlement transaction must be matched against the export collection and verification system, supported by the electronic customs declaration, commercial invoice, airway bill or bill of lading, and platform transaction records where applicable, before the bank processes the conversion.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMI platforms have low feasibility as primary treasury rails for a Chinese WFOE. Chinese banking law requires an entity's statutory corporate account and capital verification process to be held with a licensed domestic commercial bank that reports directly to PBoC and SAFE. Offshore EMIs are not licensed to hold the WFOE's RMB capital account, receive FDI equity injections, or issue the foreign exchange registration certificate required by SAFE. EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Payoneer may be used only as ancillary collection tools for overseas e-commerce platform proceeds and cannot legally replace a domestic bank account for statutory capital, taxation, customs, audit, or dividend remittance purposes. Their ability to service a Chinese-domiciled entity is legally constrained because they do not hold a China banking or payment institution license necessary for local safekeeping of RMB, and any cross-border settlement they facilitate must ultimately be routed through a domestic designated bank that performs SAFE filing and AML screening. Therefore, the WFOE must prioritize a Tier-1 domestic bank as the primary banking counterparty and treat EMIs merely as secondary payment aggregation rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Chinese Local File is required for a tax year if the enterprise has controlled transactions exceeding: (i) RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; (ii) RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; (iii) RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; (iv) RMB 100 million for related-party services; or (v) RMB 40 million for other controlled transactions. A Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transaction amounts reach RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transactions schedule must be filed regardless of these thresholds, and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be submitted within 20 days upon tax authority demand.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the tax authorities apply substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, place-of-effective-management, and Controlled Foreign Company rules. For a WFOE engaged in direct online retail, the core income-generating activities must be verifiable in China: local management and employees, procurement decisions, inventory warehousing, order fulfilment, payment collection through Stripe/Affirm rails, after-sales service, and substantive e-commerce platform contracts. A letterbox company with no local staff or functions will be denied treaty benefits, may be treated as having no genuine Chinese operating substance, and is exposed to general anti-avoidance rule adjustments, deemed dividend reclassification, and 10% withholding plus interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorize re-assessment at arm's length; Administrative Measures for Special Tax Adjustments allow corresponding income adjustments and interest charges. Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge. If the scheme is found fraudulent, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally design sham related-party pricing can be personally liable through piercing of the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 where the corporate form is abused to evade tax or harm creditors. Personal civil liability extends to restitution of the wrongfully shifted profits and unpaid tax. Criminal liability for tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to seven years, monetary fines, and exit restrictions on the legal representative through the tax authority's border-control notification mechanism.
Shell Company Without Economic Substance and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Beneficial Ownership rules under State Taxation Administration circulars allow the tax authority to deny treaty benefits, re-characterize the entity, or impose the full 10% statutory dividend withholding tax. Denial of treaty relief, together with underpaid tax surcharges and administrative penalties, may be applied. Tax Collection Law Article 44 permits the legal representative to be prohibited from leaving China until tax liabilities are discharged.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who sign false treaty-benefit declarations or establish the WFOE to abuse treaty relief face personal civil liability for restitution and tax penalties. Under Company Law Article 20, the corporate veil may be pierced if the entity is a mere instrument for tax avoidance, exposing directors' personal assets. Repeated treaty shopping may trigger criminal fraud charges; senior management may also face public tax-honesty blacklisting and travel bans.
Criminal Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax and daily late surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 creates criminal liability for tax evasion where the amount evaded is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax due; large-scale evasion carries up to three years' imprisonment; huge amounts carry three to seven years' imprisonment with fines. Exit-exit controls and asset seizure are authorized for unpaid major tax liabilities.

Director personal liability: Because tax evasion is committed by the WFOE as a legal entity, the directors, legal representative, actual controller, and CFO who initiated or knowingly approved the false return can be held personally responsible. The court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and order personal civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties. Criminal convictions result in direct imprisonment, fines, disqualification from serving as directors, and post-release restrictions on senior management roles.
