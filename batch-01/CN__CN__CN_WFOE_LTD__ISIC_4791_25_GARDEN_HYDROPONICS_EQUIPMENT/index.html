Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE engaged in online retail under current PRC company law and the Foreign Investment Negative List. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform, accessible via the nationwide electronic tax payment and digital invoice service portals.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in China, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN). The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate the WFOE under PRC Company Law through SAMR and obtain the business license, in which the USCC is automatically designated; (2) data sharing between SAMR and the State Taxation Administration automatically creates the basic tax registration; (3) the legal representative or appointed tax agent must log on to the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of business license issuance to complete the tax registration profile, including bank account filing, legal representative identity proof, accounting system declaration, and VAT taxpayer status confirmation; (4) determine whether the entity qualifies as a VAT general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer; if annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, VAT general taxpayer registration is mandatory, while voluntary registration is permitted below the threshold; (5) register the digital fiscal invoice capability through the STA digital invoice platform, obtain a tax control U-key, and configure invoicing software; (6) for foreign non-resident enterprises that do not have a Chinese establishment, no USCC is obtained; such non-resident enterprises must use the contract filing procedure for withholding tax, under which the Chinese payer or buyer/withholding agent files the tax information and remits taxes on behalf of the non-resident. Because the payload entity is a Chinese WFOE, the USCC-based resident TIN procedure applies.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - the People's Republic of China does not provide check-the-box entity classification elections or hybrid mismatch elections. A WFOE is mandatorily classified as a resident enterprise and taxed at the entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Where cross-border hybrid instruments or disregarded arrangements are present, the STA may apply substance-over-form principles and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules to deny mismatch benefits, but these are administrative adjustments rather than elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based registration exemption for incorporated entities: any enterprise selling goods or taxable services in China must register for VAT upon receipt of its business license. The small-scale taxpayer classification is available where annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000; above that threshold, VAT general taxpayer registration is mandatory. Foreign non-resident sellers without a Chinese establishment may face VAT compliance via the platform, logistics provider, or buyer as withholding/collection agent under the relevant cross-border e-commerce administration rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT law, exported goods are zero-rated (0% VAT) rather than exempt, provided that: (i) the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer; (ii) the goods are physically shipped out of the PRC customs territory under a valid export customs declaration; (iii) the sale is an arm's length transaction with a genuine overseas buyer evidenced by contract and commercial invoice; (iv) export proceeds are collected in foreign exchange and fully repatriated to China within the statutory deadline; and (v) the exporter files the export tax refund (zero-rating) declaration through the STA export rebate module with all supporting documents. Cross-border e-commerce retail export of the hydroponics and gardening equipment may qualify for the same zero-rating treatment where the customs declaration order, payment data, and logistics data can be matched digitally.

  • Export Customs Declaration (customs clearance bill of export) issued by the General Administration of Customs of China
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or cross-border e-commerce logistics manifest) evidencing physical departure from China
  • Commercial Invoice issued in the exporter's name to the overseas buyer
  • Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract (purchase order or order confirmation)
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt / International Income Declaration (repatriation proof, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC))
  • Export Tax Refund Declaration (filed electronically through the export tax rebate module of the Electronic Tax Bureau)

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's digital invoice platform (fully digitalized electronic invoice system) or through approved tax-control software integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA, which centrally allocates invoice codes and numbers, and each digital invoice carries a statutory anti-fraud stamp or verification code generated by the tax-control U-key. Large taxpayers may use the Electronic Tax Bureau's application programming interface (API) to issue invoices automatically from enterprise resource planning systems. Monthly reconciliation of invoice issuance against VAT return data is mandatory; any mismatch may trigger real-time blocking of further invoice allocation or issuance.

Export zero-rating is scrutinized through documentary review and post-refund audit. If the supporting customs, logistics, or foreign exchange repatriation documentation is missing, inconsistent, or unverified, the refund is disallowed, and any VAT already refunded is clawed back with interest at 0.05% per day on the overdue amount. Filing a fraudulent export tax refund under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection triggers a penalty of 50% to five times the refund or tax amount obtained, plus restoration of the original refund and interest. Serious cases constitute the crime of fraudulent export tax refund under PRC Criminal Law, which may result in criminal fines and imprisonment. The exporter may also be placed on a restricted tax-rebate status, subjected to customs inspection enhancement, and receive cross-department joint discipline through blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a dual-track exchange control system. The RMB is currently convertible for current account transactions once genuine underlying trade is verified, while capital account flows are subject to prior SAFE registration and bank-level supervision. Foreign direct investment, external debt, share transfers, and outward remittances of proceeds require filings or case-by-case approval with SAFE. Dividends and profits of a WFOE may be remitted outside China after annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax compliance, and a board resolution; however, the remitting bank will verify that there are no unreconciled tax liabilities, unresolved SAFE compliance findings, or outstanding capital verification defects. Currency settlement onshore is conducted at the market-based RMB central parity rate, and the bank will treat all non-trade flows with enhanced documentary scrutiny. Profit remittance hurdles therefore include mandatory statutory audit, tax clearance proof, matching of remittances to after-tax distributable profits, full performance of registered capital contribution obligations, and presentation of the FDI registration record along with the board-approved distribution resolution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE has abolished compulsory surrender of trade proceeds. Export foreign exchange receipts must still be repatriated into China through a designated trade collection account within 90 days of customs declaration unless an SAFE-approved deferral applies. The WFOE retains full discretion to hold the foreign currency or convert to RMB at the onshore market rate; there is no mandatory conversion ratio. For cross-border e-commerce exports, these receipts must also be reconciled against customs supervision codes and platform settlement statements to satisfy SAFE reporting obligations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low direct viability. A China WFOE cannot rely on an offshore digital EMI as its principal clearing bank because foreign EMIs are not banks under PRC banking law and cannot hold the WFOE's RMB or foreign-exchange accounts. PayPal, Stripe, and similar facilitators may be used only as collection front-ends for overseas e-commerce proceeds; the actual treasury settlement must flow through a licensed domestic bank or a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution. Offshore EMI accounts should therefore be limited to pre-funding and reconciliation and not be used for statutory capital, tax, or dividend clearing.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low direct viability. A China WFOE cannot rely on an offshore digital EMI as its principal clearing bank because foreign EMIs are not banks under PRC banking law and cannot hold the WFOE's RMB or foreign-exchange accounts. PayPal, Stripe, and similar facilitators may be used only as collection front-ends for overseas e-commerce proceeds; the actual treasury settlement must flow through a licensed domestic bank or a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution. Offshore EMI accounts should therefore be limited to pre-funding and reconciliation and not be used for statutory capital, tax, or dividend clearing.
