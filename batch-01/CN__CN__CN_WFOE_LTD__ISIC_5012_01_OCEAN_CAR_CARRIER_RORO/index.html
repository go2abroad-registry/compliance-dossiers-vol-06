Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the current Company Law. A WFOE is not required to maintain a blocked escrow account or to pay in registered capital at incorporation. The sole shareholder must, however, pay the subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of incorporation unless the Articles of Association prescribe an earlier date. For international ship transportation, the Ministry of Transport licensing criteria require proof of a merchant vessel under the company's ownership or bareboat charter and financial capacity, but this is a licensing condition, not a capital escrow requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance is required for the shore office as a simple civil defense filing with the Fire and Rescue Department. For the vessel, a SOLAS Cargo Ship Safety Construction Certificate and Cargo Ship Safety Equipment Certificate must be issued by the classification society or flag authority. Occupational clearance requires each seafarer to hold a valid STCW certificate of competency, a medical fitness certificate, and a seafarer's employment agreement approved under the Maritime Labour Convention; company safety management plans must be certified under the ISM Code, and the port authority may perform port state control inspections.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a Chinese tax-resident limited liability company taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no pass-through or direct-receipt preference; qualifying Free Trade Port or encouraged-industry enterprises may access preferential CIT rates (e.g., 15%) while remaining entity-level taxpayers.

Available election forms

None for entity-classification pass-through; mainland China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. Administrative elections available: small-scale vs. general VAT taxpayer registration, simplified VAT collection for certain service categories, and export tax refund/zero-rating filing status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities with taxable business activities must complete VAT taxpayer registration upon incorporation. Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 generally mandate general VAT taxpayer registration; entities at or below that threshold remain small-scale taxpayers unless voluntary general registration is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

International ocean RoRo carriage is zero-rated as an international transportation service under the PRC VAT regime. Eligibility requires: (1) the voyage involves carriage of cargo between a PRC port and a foreign port, or solely between foreign ports; (2) the supplier holds the statutory international water transport license; (3) the service is actually exported, i.e., the cargo is shipped under a customs export declaration and consigned to a non-PRC party; (4) the consideration is received in foreign currency from non-resident customers; (5) the entity has completed VAT zero-rate/export refund filing with STA; and (6) all supporting customs, transport, and bank documentation is retained for audit. Qualifying offshore digital services, if any separate scope, must demonstrate foreign consumption and payment from non-resident counterparties.

  • Ocean Bill of Lading (RoRo bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange collection receipt
  • Export Collection Verification record
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice
  • International Water Transport Permit / business registration certificate
  • Voyage and cargo manifest

China operates Golden Tax Project Phase IV through the National Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice Service Platform; taxpayers must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices with STA-controlled invoice numbers, transmit invoice data in near real-time to STA systems, and use the official tax digital account for reconciliation. System-to-system (API) integration is available only through STA-approved electronic invoice platform channels. Anti-fraud controls include real-time digital stamping, unified invoice code and QR, automatic comparison of invoices against customs declaration data, and post-issuance verification by the VAT Invoice Check System.

STA and GAC may jointly audit zero-rated export transactions. Failure to produce customs clearance evidence, valid bill of lading, FIRC, or proof of foreign-currency repatriation within 180 days triggers reclassification as a domestic taxable supply, retroactive VAT at 9% or applicable rate, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and a penalty range of 50% to five times the tax underpaid pursuant to the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent zero-rating, false export documents, or fake invoices further expose the WFOE to denial of refund, clawback of any refunded input VAT, customs fines, public blacklisting, and criminal liability for tax evasion or export-tax-refund fraud under PRC criminal law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under which the RMB is convertible for current account transactions but subject to approval and registration on the capital account. Foreign exchange for genuine trade, charter hire, bunker payments, port disbursements, and service fees may settle freely through designated commercial banks, but capital injections, cross-border loans, equity transfers, and outbound investments require SAFE registration or bank verification. Current account convertibility supports regular payments for the operation of deep-sea Ro-Ro vessel transport. Profit remittance is permitted once audited financial statements, a valid tax clearance certificate, board resolutions, and the underlying dividend distribution documents are presented; banks perform end-beneficiary review and may request a declaration of the true purpose. Retained earnings cannot be remitted before corporate income tax liabilities are settled. Capital account items such as foreign shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, and equity disposal remain closely controlled. There is no general forced foreign exchange surrender for exporters, but export proceeds must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign currency account or converted at a market-quoted rate. Non-compliance with SAFE reporting requirements can trigger penalties, downgrade of banking facilities, and loss of operational approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts for ocean Ro-Ro transportation services and related vehicle export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign exchange bank account within 180 days from the date of export settlement, unless an extension is approved by SAFE. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio; the statutory minimum conversion requirement is effectively 0 percent, meaning export proceeds may be held in foreign currency or voluntarily sold to the designated bank at the prevailing market rate, subject to SAFE monitoring and reporting.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), exercising foreign exchange control through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital electronic money institutions have a Low feasibility rating for serving a mainland China WFOE in a direct clearing or custody capacity. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and PBOC licensing rules, only SAFE-designated commercial banks may operate onshore foreign exchange settlement, RMB clearing, and cross-border trade payment processing for resident entities. Offshore EMIs are not authorized to open renminbi or foreign currency accounts for WFOEs, nor may they legally act as the receiving bank for export proceeds. Their role is limited to providing software-driven payment instruction, reconciliation, and distribution functionalities while all clearing and foreign exchange execution must be routed through a licensed domestic tier-one bank. Using a fintech EMI as the primary transaction account would constitute unlicensed cross-border financial activity in China and would breach SAFE reporting obligations. Therefore, the WFOE treasury structure must place domestic bank accounts at the centre and may optionally engage an EMI only as a front-end payment interface.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound shareholder capital for the WFOE must be paid through an FDI foreign exchange capital account opened with a designated SAFE-approved domestic bank. Before receipt, the WFOE must complete foreign direct investment registration through the SAFE or the local authorized bank, and then obtain a capital account foreign exchange business registration. The overseas shareholder transmits the funds via SWIFT wire transfer with a declared investment purpose code, usually structured as a foreign direct investment equity injection. Upon receipt, the bank performs name screening, sanctions checks, and comparison against the SAFE-registered investment amount. The funds are then converted into renminbi in a capital settlement account only after a valid capital verification report is issued by a licensed Chinese public accounting firm. No merger, acquisition, or asset acquisition from third parties is allowed without a special SAFE/FIUTS registration.Outbound payments for Ro-Ro vessel operations, charter hire, bunker fuel, port charges, crew wages, and overseas vehicle terminal fees are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with proper purpose-of-payment codes and supporting shipping documents. For large supplier or shipyard payments, a commercial irrevocable letter of credit is recommended, especially where the beneficiary is a foreign shipowner, shipbuilder, or terminal operator, because it reduces counterparty risk and provides documentary discipline through presentation of original transport and commercial documents. Documentary collections may be used for lower-value and recurring trade settlements when both parties have established payment history. All outbound transactions must be reported through the SAFE banking system, and any foreign exchange payment above the statutory threshold requires a trade-in fair copy declaration and the original invoice, contract, or relevant shipping document.Onshore deliverable RMB forward contracts allowed through SAFE-designated commercial banks for hedging genuine current account and approved capital account exposures, with mandatory real transaction background documents and no speculative open positions.Cross-currency swaps permitted for matching WFOE foreign-currency debt, shareholder loans, or long-term trade receivables where the underlying exposure is supported by signed contracts and board-approved treasury policy.Offshore non-deliverable forwards may be executed by the overseas group treasury only for portfolio-level hedge consolidation; they cannot be booked directly by the mainland WFOE as onshore derivatives without a valid SAFE/PBOC derivative qualification and underlying trade documentation.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions have a Low feasibility rating for serving a mainland China WFOE in a direct clearing or custody capacity. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and PBOC licensing rules, only SAFE-designated commercial banks may operate onshore foreign exchange settlement, RMB clearing, and cross-border trade payment processing for resident entities. Offshore EMIs are not authorized to open renminbi or foreign currency accounts for WFOEs, nor may they legally act as the receiving bank for export proceeds. Their role is limited to providing software-driven payment instruction, reconciliation, and distribution functionalities while all clearing and foreign exchange execution must be routed through a licensed domestic tier-one bank. Using a fintech EMI as the primary transaction account would constitute unlicensed cross-border financial activity in China and would breach SAFE reporting obligations. Therefore, the WFOE treasury structure must place domestic bank accounts at the centre and may optionally engage an EMI only as a front-end payment interface.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Enterprise Income Tax Law, Article 4; no separate national/local corporate income tax for ordinary enterprises)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10%, imposed on the gross amount of dividends paid by the PRC WFOE to its non-resident enterprise shareholder, under Articles 3(3), 27 and 37 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. An effective rate lower than 10% may apply only where an applicable Double Taxation Agreement reduces the rate (commonly 5% for a beneficial owner directly holding at least 25% of the voting capital; certain treaties provide other rates; no treaty reduction is automatic).

Mandatory Return Breakdown
