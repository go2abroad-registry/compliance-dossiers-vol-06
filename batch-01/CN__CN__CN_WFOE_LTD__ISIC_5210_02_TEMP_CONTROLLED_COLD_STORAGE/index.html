Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a general WFOE engaged in cold-chain logistics warehousing. The subscribed registered capital must be paid in full within five years from the date of issuance of the business license pursuant to Article 47 of the Companies Law, unless a special industrial regulation imposes a shorter period. The contribution amount, method and schedule must be set out in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow account or mandatory capital verification report is required for this ordinary warehousing activity; however, any capital injection from a non-resident shareholder must enter Jiangsu through a foreign exchange capital account and be reported under the SAFE foreign-invested enterprise information registration procedure. The registered capital must be sufficient for the intended scale of the cold-storage facility to satisfy the commercial board and the industrial licensing authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; primary online portal is the National Unified Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with the STA gateway at https://www.chinatax.gov.cn and export rebate processing through the same e-Tax system integrated with the General Administration of Customs (GACC).

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically granted by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon incorporation and printed on the Business License. There is no separate TIN application. The WFOE must complete tax registration in the e-Tax system within 30 days of the business license date, file base entity information, complete real-name authentication for the legal representative, financial officer and tax agent, and report the opening of any bank deposit account within 15 days of account opening. The same USCC is used as the VAT registration number for Chinese VAT purposes. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China, the competent STA branch at the place where the withholding agent/payer of China-source income is located will issue a dedicated non-resident TIN upon filing of the Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration Declaration; the home-country tax reference number is also recorded, and the Chinese payer typically acts as statutory withholding agent for both CIT and VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods by a VAT general taxpayer from the PRC customs territory to a destination outside China is eligible for the 0% VAT export rate with refund of relevant input VAT (export exemption and rebate), provided the goods are not subject to export prohibition, a valid PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration is filed by the exporter, ownership of the goods passes to an overseas buyer, payment is received from the overseas buyer in foreign currency and repatriated to China in accordance with SAFE rules, and the value, quantity and product description in the customs declaration, commercial invoice and foreign-exchange receipt are consistent. For cold-chain perishable pharmaceuticals, any statutory export licensing and cold-chain logistics records must accompany the shipment. Warehousing/storage services physically performed within China (cold-room storage) are generally subject to 6% VAT because the services are consumed in China; zero-rating is limited to the export of goods and to cross-border services expressly listed in the STA cross-border VAT zero-rate announcement.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (PRC Cargo Export Declaration, the exact statutory customs export form equivalent to a Bill of Entry) with the 18-digit customs declaration number and exit customs endorsement
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a PRC port and arrival/destination outside China
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list exactly matching the customs declaration value, quantity and cold-chain product specification
  • Export proceeds collection verification / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a PRC-designated bank, or SAFE export-collection digital record, showing actual receipt of foreign currency from the overseas buyer
  • Bank foreign exchange settlement record and SAFE repatriation verification confirming the funds were surrendered/converted or lawfully retained under SAFE rules

All VAT invoices must be created through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System Phase IV infrastructure using Tax Control digital devices or certified electronic-invoicing API interfaces. Special VAT invoices are transmitted to the STA at issuance in real time; the system assigns unique digital signatures, QR anti-fraud monograms and an electronic invoice number. Universal Electronic Invoices are cleared through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform and can be authenticated on the National Invoice Inspection Platform. Enterprises must submit invoice data through e-Tax or an STA-certified API gateway, and invoice data is cross-checked against customs export declarations, export rebate filings and foreign-exchange receipt records. No paper invoice is required for export zero-rating substantiation, but the invoice and its metadata must match the customs and foreign-exchange documentation exactly.

The STA, customs and SAFE may jointly perform post-clearance audits comparing the customs declaration, e-invoice records, transport and temperature-monitoring logs, insurance documents, bank statements and foreign-exchange receipts. If 0% zero-rating is unsubstantiated, the refunded VAT together with any unpaid VAT is clawed back with daily late-payment interest at 0.05% of the overdue amount; under Article 66 of the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent export refunds are punishable by recovery of the refund plus an administrative penalty of between 100% and 500% of the amount falsely obtained. Criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC for fraudulent export tax refund can lead to criminal fines plus imprisonment, with life imprisonment available for especially large amounts. Negative audit findings also trigger retrospective adjustment to the prior VAT returns, loss of subsequent zero-rating eligibility, and suspension or revocation of the Export Tax Refund Qualification.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which RMB is convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, but capital-account transactions remain under a registration and quota-based control system led by SAFE. A WFOE must complete Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration and open a Capital Account-Foreign Exchange Settlement Account at a licensed domestic bank; proceeds from registered paid-in capital are settled into RMB only after the bank verifies the SAFE filing and the underlying contractual purpose. Current-account operating flows, including rent, power, service fees, imported refrigeration equipment, and third-party logistics costs, are settled on presentation of invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and tax records. Profit and dividend remittance outside China is permissible but requires a certified annual audit, income tax filing, retained-earnings computation, and a bank administrative review for current-account repatriation; there is no unrestricted free convertibility for balance-sheet restructuring, intercompany netting, or cross-border guarantees unless separately registered. Export proceeds must be repatriated into China; while no fixed mandatory surrender percentage now exists, banks may require voluntary settlement or retention in an approved account. Any funds used outside the permitted positive list, such as RMB entrusted loans to unaffiliated parties, domestic equity investment, or non-self-use real estate, are prohibited and will trigger AML and SAFE non-compliance consequences. The policy environment therefore supports transactional convertibility, not full asset-based convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade monitoring and verification rules, export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated through a designated domestic bank and declared in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. For ordinary open-account exports, the receipt must be collected within 90 days from the export customs declaration date; any overdue unmatched item must be reported by the bank. For letter-of-credit and documentary-collection exports, the inward proceeds must be received by the payment value date plus a maximum permissible grace of 90 days from the maturity date; extensions beyond this threshold require advance SAFE registration for delayed export proceeds. Since the 2012 current-account administration reform, there is no fixed statutory conversion percentage for export receipts; the WFOE may retain up to 100% of the foreign currency in a current-account foreign exchange account or voluntarily settle up to 100% into RMB at the prevailing interbank rate. In SAFE trade-investment facilitation pilot zones, eligible low-risk enterprises may adopt automatic settlement regimes, but this is a policy-driven facilitation measure rather than a mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign exchange control agency operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - legally impracticable as the primary banking rail. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions by the National Financial Regulatory Administration or the People's Bank of China and cannot hold SAFE capital-account registration, issue tax-compliant domestic transaction records, or participate in CIPS clearing for the WFOE. Cross-border capital injection must enter through a licensed domestic tier-1 bank. An offshore EMI may be used by the overseas parent only for pre-funding in hard currency before the MT103 wire to China, but it cannot service the WFOE's balance-sheet liquidity or execute RMB clearing onshore without a Chinese banking licence.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if in the prior fiscal year the related-party transactions of the WFOE meet any of the following thresholds: RMB 20 million? Careful: official threshold for goods is RMB 200 million; RMB 100 million for other tangible assets, intangible assets, and financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for intercompany services; Master File must be prepared when the aggregate related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report threshold applies when the group’s consolidated revenue is at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations like the UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in China’s CIT Law Article 47 empowers the tax authority to recharacterize transactions lacking commercial rationale. The WFOE must demonstrate real economic substance through owned or leased temperature-controlled warehouse facilities, employed human resources, decision-making personnel, and independent risk control for cold-chain operations. The core income-generating activities (CIGA) include logistics planning, refrigeration monitoring, inventory custody, goods-handling services, and final customer invoicing; if those CIGA are carried out abroad or by the parent, the Chinese tax authority may reattribute profits to the parent and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false revenue and expense reporting

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Administration Law of China Articles 63 and 64, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax and imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the amount evaded; Article 203 of the Criminal Law of China punishes tax evasion with criminal fines and imprisonment of up to seven years for large amounts.

Director personal liability: If the director or beneficial owner personally directed the evasion, signed false financial statements, or utilized the WFOE legal form to conceal assets, a recourse court may pierce the corporate veil under China’s Company Law, imposing joint and several civil liability over the director’s personal assets. The offender is placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist, subject to a travel ban, restriction on exiting or entering China, prohibition from serving as a director of other enterprises, and criminal imprisonment where the fraudulent amount exceeds the criminal threshold.
Abuse of the corporate form with no economic substance

Statutory sanction: Under China’s GAAR (CIT Law Article 47) and Implementation Rules, the tax authority may disregard the entity, adjust its taxable income, deny the claimed fee deductions, levy a penalty of up to 50% of the adjusted tax deficiency, and impose interest; if the arrangement is deemed to violate the DTA anti-abuse limitations, the reduced withholding rate is revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who knowingly exploited the WFOE’s separate legal personality to evade tax or external creditors may have the corporate veil pierced, making them personally liable to the tax authority for the full amount of tax, penalties, and interest. Courts may issue a travel restriction order, freeze personal bank accounts, and blacklist the director; criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion can result in up to seven years’ imprisonment.
False treaty relief and disguised profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Administration Law and China’s Implementation Measures for Tax Treaty Benefits, the tax authority disallows the treaty benefit, recovers the underpaid withholding tax, imposes a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and a monetary penalty of up to five times the tax evaded; false documentation may also be referred for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law for forging official documents.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the false TRC/beneficial-ownership form or ordered the filing of a false withholding return is personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax. A criminal conviction for tax fraud or document forgery can lead to imprisonment for up to three years for forgery and up to seven years for tax evasion. The director’s personal assets may be frozen, he or she is blacklisted as a dishonest person subject to high-consumption restrictions, denied exit visas and border privileges, and barred from corporate management offices.
