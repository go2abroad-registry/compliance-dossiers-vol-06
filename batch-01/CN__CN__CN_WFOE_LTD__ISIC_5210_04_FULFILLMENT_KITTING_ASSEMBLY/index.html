Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the local Municipal Administration for Market Regulation with jurisdiction over the registered address; foreign investment information reporting is concurrently filed with the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) via the Foreign Investment Comprehensive Management Information System.

Governing statute

Company Law of the People's Republic of China (Revised 2023, effective 1 July 2024), particularly Articles 1-29 (formation and registration), Articles 32-40 (registered capital and contribution rules), Articles 46-52 (organizational structure of the limited liability company), Articles 74-85 (duties of directors and officers), and the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Foreign-Invested Enterprises as interpreted in conjunction with the Foreign Investment Law (2019) and its Implementing Regulations, along with the Regulations on the Registration Administration of Market Entities (State Council Order No. 746, as amended).

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of Establishment of a Foreign-Invested Limited Liability Company (企业设立登记申请书) accompanied by the Articles of Association, the Foreign Investor's Certificate of Incorporation and Good Standing, the Registered Office Lease or Certificate of Title, the Capital Contribution Confirmation, the Power of Attorney for the filing agent, and the appointment documents for the legal representative, executive director, supervisors and general manager; filing of the Foreign Investment Information Report (外商投资信息报告) through the integrated SAMR one-stop portal.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Company Law for an ordinary light industrial/fulfillment services WFOE; the registered capital is an subscribed amount recorded in the Articles of Association and must be contributed by the shareholder(s) within the period specified in the Articles, not exceeding five years from establishment for general enterprises; no blocked escrow account is required by statute. The amount should nevertheless be commercially reasonable to support the business scale, foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), banking limits, licensing and customs/bonded logistics arrangements; for a fulfillment/kitting operation, an indicative subscribed capital of RMB 500,000 or more is recommended, though no minimum legal floor applies.

Funding & unlock path: The WFOE must obtain a Fire Safety Filing/Record (ocp: 建设工程消防验收备案/备案抽查) with the local Fire and Rescue Brigade for the leased warehouse premises, accompanied by a Civil Defense completion inspection where the warehouse building is a newly constructed or structurally altered facility. The lessee shall ensure a structural load inspection is performed by a qualified design institute if the kitting floor loads exceed 500 kg/m² or if multi-tier racking is installed; the structural design must show the live load capacity in accordance with the current load code GB 50009-2012. A workplace safety approval is enforced by the local Emergency Management Bureau under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China, with a written responsibility system, 'double prevention' risk classification and control, a full safety training file for all operatives, and a special equipment pre-use inspection for forklifts and hydraulic lift tables under the Special Equipment Safety Law of the People's Republic of China. Worker protection includes a temperature-controlled environment, ergonomic risk controls for kitting tasks, chemical agent safety data sheets if any adhesive or cleaning agent is introduced, and mandatory safety messaging in Chinese. The civil defense certificate is confirmed through the fire acceptance check or the filing receipt, which must be kept on the premises and produced on demand during municipal safety inspections.

Paid-in capital rule: None under the Company Law for an ordinary light industrial/fulfillment services WFOE; the registered capital is an subscribed amount recorded in the Articles of Association and must be contributed by the shareholder(s) within the period specified in the Articles, not exceeding five years from establishment for general enterprises; no blocked escrow account is required by statute. The amount should nevertheless be commercially reasonable to support the business scale, foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), banking limits, licensing and customs/bonded logistics arrangements; for a fulfillment/kitting operation, an indicative subscribed capital of RMB 500,000 or more is recommended, though no minimum legal floor applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic filing portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax@China) accessible via https://tpass.chinatax.gov.cn (or provincial e-Tax portals)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) simultaneously constitutes the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate the WFOE with SAMR and obtain the Business License containing the 18-digit USCC; (2) complete the post-incorporation tax registration within the statutory timeline through the local STA office or the electronic tax bureau, where the USCC is automatically activated as the tax registration ID; (3) register for VAT status (small-scale or general VAT payer) as required; (4) receive confirmation of the tax-supervision relationship with the competent tax authority. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in China but with taxable activity, a simple TIN is obtained through withholding registration at the source or under applicable double-tax-treaty rules; however, the standard WFOE holding the USCC is the normal resident taxpayer structure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) simultaneously constitutes the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate the WFOE with SAMR and obtain the Business License containing the 18-digit USCC; (2) complete the post-incorporation tax registration within the statutory timeline through the local STA office or the electronic tax bureau, where the USCC is automatically activated as the tax registration ID; (3) register for VAT status (small-scale or general VAT payer) as required; (4) receive confirmation of the tax-supervision relationship with the competent tax authority. For a foreign non-resident entity not incorporated in China but with taxable activity, a simple TIN is obtained through withholding registration at the source or under applicable double-tax-treaty rules; however, the standard WFOE holding the USCC is the normal resident taxpayer structure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None (China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime). The only relevant election is the VAT payer status option: an entity below the RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover threshold may voluntarily register as a general VAT payer, while entities above the threshold are mandatorily general VAT payers. Additionally, a qualifying WFOE may elect to be treated as a tax-exempt enterprise for small-scale VAT purposes if conditions are met. No corporate tax classification election is available under Chinese law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales (excluding VAT) exceeding RMB 5,000,000 mandates registration as a general VAT payer; voluntary registration is available below this threshold for entities making taxable supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are eligible for zero-rating (i.e., exemption from output VAT with full refund of input VAT) provided the goods are physically exported from mainland China, the exporter is a general VAT payer, the export transaction is genuinely concluded with a non-resident buyer, and the entity files the export declaration with the appropriate customs office. Qualifying goods must meet the statutory conditions set forth in the Interim Regulations on VAT and the relevant Ministry of Finance/STA export rebate policies. The export must be supported by legally effective customs declarations, transportation documents, and foreign exchange receipts. For cross-border services, zero-rating applies only to explicitly enumerated categories; warehousing and kitting services performed domestically for goods subsequently exported are generally treated as taxable domestic services and do not qualify for zero-rating, while the physical goods themselves, when exported, do qualify.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Export Declaration Form issued by China Customs, with the assigned export customs transaction number)
  • Bill of Lading (for sea shipments) or Airway Bill (for air shipments) issued by the carrier evidencing international carriage
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank collection confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign buyer)

All VAT taxpayers must use the new Fully Digitalised E-invoice (Shudan Gi) system under the Gold Tax Phase III/IV (Jin San/Jin Si) infrastructure. General VAT payers must issue tax control invoices through the official electronic invoice filing platform, with mandatory real-time upload of invoice data to the STA, automatic cryptographic stamping, and risk-based clearing. The system enforces unified invoice templates, digital signatures, and real-time verification. Taxpayers must maintain a valid Tax Control Device (electronic tax U-Key) or use the free e-invoice platform to issue invoices in accordance with the Announcement on Deepening the Comprehensive Digital Invoice Reform. Non-compliant issuance triggers automatic system rejection and administrative penalties.

Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims are subject to immediate clawback of the refunded input VAT, accrued late payment interest at the statutory rate, and administrative fines under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection (PRC Tax Administration Law) and the Customs Law. If the exporter fails to provide the mandatory customs and foreign-exchange documents within the prescribed period, or if the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the VAT refund is invalidated and the entity must repay the refund with interest, plus a penalty of 0.05% per day on overdue amounts. In cases of deliberate false declaration or fraudulent export refund schemes, criminal liability may be pursued under the Criminal Law for tax evasion, with fines ranging from 50% to five times the amount evaded and custodial penalties for responsible personnel. Additionally, the STA may revoke the general VAT payer status, impose an elevated scrutiny access class for future refunds, and list the entity in the tax dishonesty blacklist with adverse consequences for cross-border trade and financing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China currently maintains RMB convertibility for current account transactions under the SAFE framework, while capital account transactions remain subject to a registration and approval matrix. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise may freely convert export proceeds and service income in the current account, and may purchase foreign exchange for genuine current account payments without SAFE pre-approval. Capital account transactions, including inbound FDI equity contributions, outbound investment, offshore loans, and profit repatriation, require SAFE registration or filing with the onshore custodian bank. Dividends or profits may be remitted abroad after annual audit and corporate income tax settlement, provided the WFOE has satisfied tax withholding on dividends (10% treaty rate where applicable). No explicit compulsory FX surrender ratio is currently imposed; however, trade-related receipts must be repatriated within the 180-day goods trade verification cycle. Cross-border flow rails are monitored through the bank's RCPMIS and ASOne reporting systems.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory verification and repatriation period: 180 days from customs export declaration. Mandatory conversion: none, as China abolished compulsory FX surrender in 2012; enterprises may retain export proceeds in designated foreign exchange settlement accounts or convert to RMB at any time; banks apply real-demand checks and trade verification through the goods trade monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMIs are not legally recognized as deposit-taking institutions in mainland China; they cannot open corporate Basic Deposit Accounts or handle foreign capital verification. They may only act as technology-enabled payment processing agents if they hold a PBOC cross-border payment license, but the WFOE must maintain its primary settlement relationship with a licensed domestic commercial bank for all regulated flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injection for the WFOE must be remitted through SWIFT MT103 from the non-resident shareholder's offshore bank account to the WFOE's dedicated FDI capital account held at the chosen domestic bank. The remittance instruction must cite SAFE's FDI registration code and the exact purpose code for equity injection. On receipt, the bank must settle the conversion against a current account or perform direct RMB settlement with the CPA verification report; the entity cannot set off any offshore expenses or pending receivables against the capital contribution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, contemporaneous local file documentation is mandatory for the WFOE when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A master file is required when the enterprise's annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting is required for Ultimate Parent Entities in China whose consolidated group revenue for the prior fiscal year exceeded RMB 5.5 billion. The foreign-exchange and tax authorities may also demand an arm's length statement for any significant cross-border related-party receivable or payable.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate the offshore 'Economic Substance Regulations' regime found in certain zero-tax jurisdictions, but applies a robust economic substance and anti-avoidance doctrine embedded in EIT Law Article 41 (related-party transactions), Article 45 (controlled foreign corporations), Article 46 (thin capitalization), Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), and Article 48. The State Taxation Administration will disregard an arrangement that lacks commercial purpose and will attribute taxable profits to the Chinese entity where the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for fulfillment/kitting/assembly are in China. A qualifying WFOE must demonstrate local personnel, operational control, inventory and pricing risk, purchase orders, warehouse and assembly execution, invoicing, and profit-relevant decision-making. A letterbox WFOE with no CIGA, no staff, and no operational substance can be recharacterized and have its income re-attributed to a non-resident principal, exposing the WFOE to additional tax, interest, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63 requires recovery of the evaded CIT and imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the evaded amount plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge under Article 32. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion; if the evaded amount reaches the criminal threshold and the ratio to taxable income is serious, the responsible individual may be sentenced to up to 3 years' imprisonment, and for huge evaded amounts with a ratio above 30%, 3 to 7 years' imprisonment plus fines. False VAT invoice behavior is also punishable under Criminal Law Article 205, with potential life imprisonment in extreme cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly order or facilitate fraudulent accounting may have the corporate veil pierced under Company Law Article 20 and relevant judicial precedent; they are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution out of personal assets. Tax authorities may freeze personal bank accounts, attach director property, place the director on the serious tax violation blacklist, and impose travel bans through the exit restriction mechanism. Criminal prosecution may lead to a personal prison sentence for the director.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 37 imposes WHT, and Tax Collection Law Article 69 penalizes the WFOE as withholding agent for a sum equal to 50% to 3 times the unwithheld tax; the tax authority will also demand payment of the unpaid WHT and daily interest. If the conduit arrangement is designed to obtain unintended treaty benefits, EIT Law Article 47 allows the competent authority to deny treaty relief, restore the 10% WHT, and assess late-payment interest. Where the conduct is fraudulent, Criminal Law Article 201 applies to the individuals responsible.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve dividend declarations without reserving WHT may be held personally liable where the corporate entity is rendered insolvent or where the distribution is in substance an asset-stripping device. The civil court may pierce the veil and order directors to restore the WHT plus penalties from personal assets. The directors are also exposed to travel bans, asset freezes, and Chinese 'blacklisting' as dishonest taxpayers; deliberate concealed distributions can result in personal criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing and Shell Company Abuse Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44, 46, and 47 empower the State Taxation Administration to make an arm's length adjustment, recharacterize financing arrangements, and disregard transactions lacking commercial substance. SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 and Announcement 2017 No. 6 impose contemporaneous documentation requirements; failure to supply documentation triggers interest on the adjusted tax plus an additional 5 percentage point annual surcharge. The special tax investigation look-back period may extend to 10 years. Non-deductible interest is recharacterized as a dividend subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: If the WFOE is found to be a sham instrument created for profit stripping, the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold the directors jointly and severally liable for the Chinese tax liability. Directors can be ordered to make civil restitution from personal assets, are subject to exit bans and blacklisting, and may face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 if they knowingly signed false statements or concealed taxable income to sustain the fraudulent pricing arrangement. In egregious cases, the responsible director may be imprisoned alongside corporate fines.
