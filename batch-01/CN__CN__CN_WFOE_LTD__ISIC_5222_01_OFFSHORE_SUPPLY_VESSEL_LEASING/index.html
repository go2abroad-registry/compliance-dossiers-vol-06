Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies under the PRC Company Law for a WFOE limited liability company; the subscribed registered capital may be paid in full within five years from the date of incorporation under Article 47. However, if the WFOE applies to the Ministry of Transport for a domestic water transport operating permit for coastal offshore supply vessel operations, the regulator requires minimum paid-in operating capital of RMB 2,000,000 for coastal cargo water transport and RMB 500,000 for inland waterway cargo transport, evidenced by a capital verification certificate issued by a licensed accounting firm or a bank escrow confirmation; such capital must remain committed during the licensed period and may not be withdrawn absent regulatory approval. For purely international offshore maritime auxiliary or bareboat chartering activities, no statutory capital floor exists, but the commercial bank and SAFE may impose an escrow or net-asset covenant for AML and maritime charter financing purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Acceptance Recordation and, where the shorebase premises are open to personnel or the public, the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate under the Fire Protection Law; structural load inspection certificate for any shoreline warehouse or equipment storage facility; workplace safety registration and the simultaneous design, construction, and operational safety review under the Work Safety Law for any new or converted facility; vessel-side compliance includes the SOLAS safety construction certificate, fire-fighting equipment and life-saving appliance surveys by China Classification Society, and the Safety Management Certificate under the ISM and NSM Codes subject to periodic Port State Control inspections by China MSA; crew members must hold statutory competency certificates, medical fitness certificates, and manning endorsements issued or recognized by China MSA before deployment on offshore supply vessels.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online platform and the provincial/municipal STA office where the WFOE is registered.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the foreign investor(s) first incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a legal-person business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for VAT and Enterprise Income Tax purposes. The WFOE must then complete national tax registration with the competent STA office within 30 days of obtaining the business license, filing via the Electronic Tax Bureau with the business license, articles of association, shareholder and legal representative identity documents, registered office proof, and bank deposit account information. The STA activates the VAT taxpayer profile (small-scale or general taxpayer) and the Corporate Income Tax registration in the same procedure. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that earns China-source income from the vessel charter or leasing arrangement, the resident WFOE or paying agent must apply for withholding tax registration and obtain the non-resident supplier's China TIN or withholding serial number at the competent STA office of the payer; if the non-resident entity must self-file in China, it applies for a non-resident tax registration using its foreign incorporation certificate, board resolution, representative appointment, and the underlying charter agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid entity classification elections. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident Chinese legal entity taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no transparent treatment is available for a single-owner WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic tax registration: every WFOE must register for VAT with the STA within 30 days of business license issuance. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Below that amount the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer, but a cross-border zero-rating or VAT refund claim generally requires general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT rules, the 0% rate applies to international transportation services, including cross-border voyage and time charter operations, when the service is supplied to a foreign counterparty and the vessel is engaged in international transport of goods or passengers. For a bareboat or operating lease of the offshore platform supply vessel with the vessel physically used outside China, the lease is VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated under the cross-border consumption exemption for tangible movable property. To claim 0% on qualifying charter services, the WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer and demonstrate that the service is wholly consumed outside China, the foreign charterer has no PRC consumption nexus, and consideration is paid from outside China.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (customs export clearance record in the China International Trade Single Window, with export-rebate seal if cargo is involved)
  • Original Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing the vessel move or cargo carriage from China to a foreign port
  • Signed voyage charter, time charter, or bareboat lease agreement identifying the foreign charterer and the offshore/outside-China operational site
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the SAFE trade-monitoring exchange settlement record showing receipt of charter hire in convertible foreign currency
  • Fully digitalized VAT invoice (e-fapiao) issued for the zero-rated or exempt cross-border transaction and the export-related VAT refund/zero-rating application ledger

The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-fapiao) platform under the Golden Tax System IV. The e-invoice is digitally signed and stamped with an anti-counterfeit QR code and transmitted to STA in near-real time at the time of issuance. The entity's accounting/billing system must either use the STA-hosted web issuance module or connect through an approved API interface. No offline or retroactive invoice clearance is permitted; the buyer's USCC/China TIN must be validated, and invoice data are automatically cross-matched with customs, foreign exchange, and VAT filing data.

The STA automatically cross-matches e-fapiao invoice data, customs export declarations, Bill of Lading information, SAFE foreign exchange collection records, and bank remittance receipts. If the WFOE cannot substantiate the zero-rated export or the wholly-foreign-consumption test is not met, the STA will deny or claw back the refunded input VAT, assess the unpaid VAT plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose administrative penalties ranging from 50% to five times the tax involved. Knowingly fraudulent zero-rating or export refund claims are referred for export tax fraud prosecution, with criminal liability imposed on both the responsible individuals and the entity.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a two-tier convertibility structure. Current-account foreign-exchange transactions, once underlying trade documents are verified by a licensed commercial bank, are generally fully convertible; however, every transaction must satisfy the bank's real-need review and adhere to the SAFE trade-proceeds reporting requirements. Capital-account movements, including cross-border debt, portfolio investments, and outbound guarantees, remain restricted and are only admitted on a case-by-case basis under SAFE approvals or quota schemes such as Qualified Foreign Investor programs. The CNY is not freely convertible outside the mainland, and offshore currency formation is limited. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, equity capital can be injected via SAFE-registered capital accounts, but all expenditures must be supported by authentic documentation. Profit remittance to the offshore parent is permitted only after the annual statutory audit, the payment of PRC corporate income tax, and a 10% dividend withholding tax (possibly reduced under a tax treaty), with SAFE approving the outward remittance. There is no compulsory surrender of export receipts, but full repatriation is obligatory; exporters may retain foreign exchange in designated accounts and settle at their discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current-account foreign exchange management, export proceeds from offshore vessel chartering must be repatriated into China within the statutory collection term, commonly 90 days from the contractual invoice or bill-of-lading date, extendable to 180 days for large-ticket ship leasing with supporting trade-finance documentation. There is no mandatory percentage-based surrender to the central bank; however, the full amount of export foreign-exchange receipts must be credited to a designated foreign-exchange current account or settled into CNY with a domestic designated bank. Any deviation or delayed repatriation must be separately reported to SAFE with a justifiable trade-risk explanation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low legal viability. Non-bank fintech EMIs are not authorized to independently open payment accounts or provide cross-border banking services for onshore Chinese entities. Under the Administrative Measures for Non-Bank Payment Institutions and PBOC licensing frameworks, only licensed payment institutions may engage in domestic settlement, and foreign-owned EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, and Revolut do not hold the requisite license to act as the exclusive or primary treasury rails for a Chinese WFOE. They can only cooperate as third-party payment facilitators through a domestic designated commercial bank and cannot hold escrow capital, issue bank-confirmed payment instruments, or provide FX hedging. Therefore, fintech EMIs are recommended only as auxiliary disbursement rails for small operational expenses after full KYB review.

Capital injection must follow SAFE's FDI registration. The offshore parent wires the equity consideration via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-supervised Capital Account in a licensed domestic bank. The wire must cite the foreign-exchange purpose code for FDI registered capital. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low legal viability. Non-bank fintech EMIs are not authorized to independently open payment accounts or provide cross-border banking services for onshore Chinese entities. Under the Administrative Measures for Non-Bank Payment Institutions and PBOC licensing frameworks, only licensed payment institutions may engage in domestic settlement, and foreign-owned EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer, and Revolut do not hold the requisite license to act as the exclusive or primary treasury rails for a Chinese WFOE. They can only cooperate as third-party payment facilitators through a domestic designated commercial bank and cannot hold escrow capital, issue bank-confirmed payment instruments, or provide FX hedging. Therefore, fintech EMIs are recommended only as auxiliary disbursement rails for small operational expenses after full KYB review.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party transactions exceeding CNY 200 million for tangible assets, CNY 100 million for intangible/financial assets, or CNY 40 million for services. Master File: aggregate related-party turnover at CNY 1 billion or special circumstances under STA Announcement 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime comparable to Caribbean offshore centres, but anti-abuse rules, substance-over-form doctrine, and GAAR (CIT Law Article 47 and Tax Collection Law Article 35) achieve materially the same effect. A WFOE chartering offshore platform supply vessels must perform in China the core income-generating activities: charter negotiations, operational dispatch and voyage management, vessel maintenance/repair supervision, market and credit risk control, and strategic decision-making. Insufficient substance in China, combined with booking charter profits through Hong Kong or another no/low-tax jurisdiction, will invite treaty denial, re-characterization of income, transfer pricing adjustment, and blacklisting of the WFOE and its legal representative.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through false cost and revenue reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63: recovery of underpaid tax and penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; daily late-payment surcharge 0.05%; tax credit rating blacklisting and publication of serious dishonesty in tax matters.

Director personal liability: Company law veil-piercing (Company Law Article 20/23, 2023) makes shareholders jointly liable when abuse of corporate personality causes failure to pay taxes. The legal representative is subject to exit ban/restricted departure under Art. 44 exit-control rules until tax is settled. Directors who deliberately instruct false accounts commit Criminal Law Article 201 tax evasion: imprisonment up to 3 years plus fine; if the amount and ratio are huge or exceed 30%, imprisonment 3-7 years; personal bank accounts and property may be seized for restitution of evaded tax.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Related-Party Non-Disclosure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Article 44 permit the tax authority to adjust the transaction by an arm's-length method and to assess a deemed taxable income where documentation is absent. Tax Collection Law Articles 44 and 64 impose fines up to CNY 10,000 for missing documentation and 50%-500% penalty on adjusted underpaid tax; interest runs from the date underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers who knowingly certify false transfer pricing documentation are treated as directly responsible personnel under Criminal Law; if the TP adjustment creates an overdue tax debt, the legal representative is subject to a travel ban and asset preservation measures. If the foreign parent/shareholder abuses separate personality to treat the WFOE and parent treasury as one cash pool, the court may pierce the veil and impose joint personal or parent liability for the entire adjusted tax, interest, and penalties.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Profit Repatriation and Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 69: withholding agent failing to withhold is subject to a fine of 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount; tax authority may recover the unwithheld tax from the non-resident and, if unrecoverable, from the withholding agent. Unremitted withheld tax is compounded with 0.05% daily surcharge and may become a priority claim over other creditors.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and persons in charge may be enjoined from leaving China until the tax and penalties are paid. Criminal exposure arises if withheld funds are misappropriated: embezzlement/misappropriation sanctions under Criminal Law, or Personal Liability under the Company Law veil-piercing if the parent shareholder caused the WFOE to operate as a mere instrumentality, transferring distributable profits offshore without satisfying the statutory withholding debt.
