A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible as the sole registered address for a WFOE in the maritime crew recruitment industry. The State Taxation Administration, local SAMR, and commercial banks require a physical operating site evidenced by a lease contract or property ownership certificate, because crew recruitment and payroll handling demand actual business substance. Banks will reject virtual offices for opening basic deposit accounts and multi-currency payroll accounts, and may decline SWIFT wire services without proof of physical staff and records. A serviced office with a short-term lease is acceptable if the lessor provides a valid lease, a property certificate, and the company maintains a nameplate and statutory records at the address.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed by China's Company Law for a WFOE in the ship crew recruitment agency service category. However, under Article 47 of the Company Law 2023, the subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Association must be paid in full within five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter term is prescribed by law or the articles. No blocked escrow account is required by SAMR for this activity. Separately, Maritime Safety Administration (MSA) licensing practice may require the company to demonstrate sufficient paid-in funds or bank availability to satisfy seafarer wages, repatriation and contingency liabilities in the seafarer service agency licence application; the exact amount is examined by the local MSA branch.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification: The registered office and any crew waiting area must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate or fire acceptance sign-off from the local Fire and Rescue Section of the Emergency Management Bureau before commencing business. Structural load inspection: Not required for standard office occupancy; required only if a heavy crew equipment storage room or training hall is later added. Occupational safety: Workplace safety registration with the Emergency Management Bureau and appointment of a designated safety officer are required if the office accommodates more than fifty employees; at all times emergency exits, fire extinguishers, and evacuation signage must comply with the Fire Protection Law and the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No general VAT registration exemption: every entity making taxable supplies must obtain VAT status. Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 mandates registration as a general VAT taxpayer; turnover equal to or below this threshold qualifies for simplified 'small-scale taxpayer' treatment (with possible reduced levy rates under prevailing relief measures).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate may be claimed for (a) exported physical goods after customs clearance and export declaration, provided the goods physically leave Chinese customs territory and export proceeds are repatriated; and (b) cross-border services explicitly listed as zero-rated under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and later circulars, including international transportation services, offshore outsourcing, and certain R&D/technology services rendered wholly to non-resident clients and consumed entirely outside China. Crew recruitment and manning services are not categorically listed as zero-rated; eligibility depends on factual classification as an international transportation auxiliary service consumed outside China. The taxpayer should obtain a written tax ruling from the STA before applying 0% and must maintain contemporaneous evidence of the nature and place of supply. If the service is deemed consumed within China, the standard VAT rate (typically 6% for business-support services) applies. Unjustified zero-rating is treated as an incorrect tax refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) with China Customs export endorsement – required for physical goods exports; not applicable to pure crew recruitment services.
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical departure of goods – for goods exports; replaced by crew embarkation/disembarkation records and shipowner confirmation for crew services.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher (e.g., SWIFT credit advice, Trade Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within the prescribed deadline.
  • Cross-border service contract (crew manning/recruitment agreement) clearly stating the parties, foreign-currency consideration, scope, and place of performance.
  • Service completion certificate (e.g., signed attendance records, vessel assignment orders, or payroll documentation) proving the service was rendered to a non-resident recipient and consumed outside China.

Golden Tax Project IV mandates real-time fiscal invoicing and clearance. VAT invoices must be issued exclusively through the STA's All-Digital Electronic Invoice (ADEI) system or the authorized VAT invoice management system. Each invoice is transmitted electronically to the STA at the moment of issuance, carries a unique digital encryption code, and is authenticated via the national VAT invoice verification portal. General taxpayers are required to maintain an API or e-tax interface that submits invoice data to the STA in near real-time. Paper invoices are being phased out; unauthorized issuance or non-integration can lead to invoice rejection, blocked issuance, and penalties.

Under Articles 63 and 64 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Article 204 of the Criminal Law, false zero-rating or fraudulent export refund claims trigger full clawback of the refunded or exempted VAT, late-payment interest, administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax, revocation of export tax refund qualifications, and criminal prosecution for tax fraud – including imprisonment up to life and confiscation of property in severe cases. Chinese tax authorities cross-check customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, and VAT filings through the Golden Tax data matching system to detect anomalies.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility and controlled but gradually liberalized capital-account convertibility. The PBOC and SAFE jointly administer foreign exchange via the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, the Regulations on Direct Investment Foreign Exchange Control, and SAFE business guidelines. For a WFOE engaged in ship crew recruitment and maritime staffing, collection of service fees from overseas shipowners is a current-account receipt and is generally convertible on the basis of authentic supporting contracts, invoices, and SAFE-verified transaction documentation. Capital-account transactions, including FDI equity injections, cross-border shareholder loans, and profit repatriation, require SAFE pre-registration or filing, designated bank account usage, and transaction-level bank verification. There is no mandatory surrender ratio in China; export and service-trade proceeds may be retained in domestic foreign-currency settlement accounts or voluntarily converted into RMB at onshore market rates. However, repatriation is required under SAFE current-account rules, and any unrepatriated offshore retention must be individually approved. Profit remittance hurdles are material: the WFOE must complete annual statutory audit, file corporate income tax returns, obtain tax clearance, determine distributable profit under PRC GAAP, register the distribution with SAFE, and submit resolution, audit report, tax vouchers, and bank forms. Remittance cannot exceed distributable after-tax profit, and banks may reject transactions without an underlying business contract or with mismatched beneficiary/purpose codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE practice, export and service-trade foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of invoice issuance, customs declaration, or completion of service delivery, unless an offshore retention extension is approved by SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; exporters are permitted to retain all foreign currency in designated foreign-currency settlement accounts or sell at negotiated onshore CNY rates. The statutory compliance requirement is settlement with a licensed domestic bank under a genuine trade or service transaction, with full supporting documentation maintained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign exchange administration institution directly under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), the central bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and effectively non-viable for a mainland China WFOE. Offshore fintech EMIs, digital-only banks, and electronic money institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, and other unlicensed virtual account providers are not authorized by the PBOC, CBIRC, or SAFE to open or maintain RMB-denominated or foreign-currency corporate accounts for entities in mainland China. They cannot provide compliant SWIFT correspondent account relationships with Chinese banks for capital injection, trade settlements, payroll clearing, or profit repatriation. Legal barriers include the PRC Banking Law, the Administrative Measures on Renminbi Bank Settlement Accounts, and PBOC payment licensing rules. The correct recommendation is to use a tier-1 licensed domestic commercial bank as the primary account bank and, if needed, a domestic bank subsidiary with cross-border licence for local settlement; offshore EMIs must not be used for any regulated treasury function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first complete MOFCOM/AMR establishment and SAFE FDI registration for the WFOE, then open a domestic foreign-currency capital escrow account at a tier-1 Chinese bank. The inward remittance wire should be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code for foreign direct investment capital injection, using the exact FDI company name as beneficiary and the SAFE capital account number, and with all fields matching the registered entity. The receiving bank will verify the transaction, record it in SAFE's foreign exchange monitoring system, and permit conversion into RMB only for approved operating purposes. Within 30 days after receipt, a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report, and the WFOE must file the capital contribution record with SAFE. No direct RMB credit to operational accounts is permitted before the foreign currency capital is settled under the bank's SAFE-controlled remittance and settlement workflow.For crew payroll and related maritime staffing disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 transfers through the domestic account bank under the SAFE service-trade payment category for maritime transportation or labour remuneration, attaching crew payroll manifests and tax certificates where required. For vessel provisioning and commercial purchases, use Commercial Letters of Credit opened or advised by a licensed Chinese bank, with UCP600-compliant documents including invoice, bill of lading, and insurance. For low-value or repeat supplier payments, Documentary Collections are acceptable upon commercial invoice and transport documents. All outbound disbursements must have a real service or trade contract, supporting invoice, and bank-recorded purpose code; salary remittances to seafarers' overseas bank accounts must be made directly from the WFOE's defined payroll account and cannot be aggregated through third-party payment processors unless the processor is a licensed domestic bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and effectively non-viable for a mainland China WFOE. Offshore fintech EMIs, digital-only banks, and electronic money institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, and other unlicensed virtual account providers are not authorized by the PBOC, CBIRC, or SAFE to open or maintain RMB-denominated or foreign-currency corporate accounts for entities in mainland China. They cannot provide compliant SWIFT correspondent account relationships with Chinese banks for capital injection, trade settlements, payroll clearing, or profit repatriation. Legal barriers include the PRC Banking Law, the Administrative Measures on Renminbi Bank Settlement Accounts, and PBOC payment licensing rules. The correct recommendation is to use a tier-1 licensed domestic commercial bank as the primary account bank and, if needed, a domestic bank subsidiary with cross-border licence for local settlement; offshore EMIs must not be used for any regulated treasury function.
