Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account is required under the PRC Company Law for WFOEs. The shareholders set the subscribed capital in the articles of association and must complete contribution within the five-year statutory period from incorporation for a limited liability company unless a special law or regulation imposes a shorter period or a sectoral minimum. Non-monetary contributions require valuation and legal transfer. The NFRA insurance surveying licence may impose separate capital or professional indemnity requirements; those are assessed by NFRA and are not part of company registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); the electronic filing and registration portal is the national E-Tax Bureau (Electronic Tax Bureau) platform operated by the STA through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

The foreign investor must first complete name pre-registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). After SAMR approves the WFOE establishment, the business license is issued bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for both Corporate Income Tax and VAT; SAMR data is automatically synchronized to the competent tax authority under the one-window service system. The WFOE must then complete tax initialization in the E-Tax Portal, including real-name verification of the legal representative, filing of the financial/accounting system and bank account, application for invoice types and quotas (including fully digitalized electronic invoices), and VAT general taxpayer qualification if required. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment that earns China-source income registers through the withholding agent at the competent district tax authority under the non-resident enterprise withholding registration procedure.

13%

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under China's VAT export and cross-border service rules, exports of goods are zero-rated and eligible for export VAT refund, and eligible cross-border services supplied by a Chinese resident taxpayer to an overseas recipient for consumption wholly outside China may qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption under the VAT reform measures and subsequent STA announcements. Marine insurance claims surveying will qualify for zero-rating only if the service recipient is an overseas insurer/underwriter without a domestic establishment, the service is consumed and documented offshore, the subject risk, vessel, or cargo is not territorially taxable in China, and foreign-exchange receipts are received from outside China. A marine survey physically performed on Chinese territory for vessels or cargo located in China will generally be treated as a domestic taxable service subject to 6% VAT rather than zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) / electronic customs export release notice issued by the General Administration of Customs of China
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, air waybill, or equivalent transport document proving physical export of goods (not applicable to service-only marine surveying)
  • Fully digitalized electronic invoice / commercial invoice describing the zero-rated export supply and overseas customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign-exchange receipt confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the foreign counter-party
  • Signed cross-border service contract or underwriter mandate letter, claim file, survey report, vessel/cargo location evidence, and foreign customer status documentation demonstrating offshore consumption

The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice system under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. Invoice data are transmitted in real time to the STA's national VAT invoice service platform; each fully digitalized electronic invoice is assigned a unique electronic invoice number, and no physical fiscal stamp is required. Issuance, red-letter reversals, corrections, and voiding must be reported to the tax authority through the official e-invoice platform or an approved third-party API interface, and real-time fiscal clearance/anti-fraud digital matching is mandatory.

The STA cross-matches export declarations, electronic invoice metadata, transport logistics documents, and foreign-exchange receipts with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Failure to substantiate zero-rating within the required period results in reversal of the zero-rated claim and reassessment at the applicable domestic VAT rate (6% for services or 13% for goods), plus late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and an administrative tax penalty between 50% and 500% of the underpaid tax. Fraudulently obtained export VAT refunds are clawed back in full and may attract a penalty of up to five times the refunded amount, with criminal liability potentially arising under PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund. Ordinary assessment periods generally run for three years, extended to ten years for major understatements, with no time limit in cases of fraud or non-payment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a bifurcated convertibility structure. RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade in services, marine survey fees, insurance premiums, and routine operating disbursements, provided the underlying transaction is genuine, documented, and filed through the SAFE-monitored bank reporting system. Capital account items remain heavily regulated: foreign direct investment, cross-border financing, offshore guarantees, and any form of capital injection require either SAFE registration, bank-level Foreign Exchange (FX) registration, or PBOC approval. For a WFOE formed as a marine insurance surveyor, the initial paid-in capital must enter through a designated Foreign Investment Capital Settlement Account, be verified by a qualified capital verification report, and be used strictly within the approved business scope. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is a current account transaction, but it is practically gated by annual statutory audit completion, prior-year tax clearance, minutes of the shareholder distribution declaration, and the foreign exchange payment notice filed through the remitting bank; the bank will also screen for any outstanding SAFE sanctions or undisclosed related-party cross-border exposures. Surrender requirements are now largely repatriation-oriented rather than mandatory conversion: registered exporters and service providers must repatriate offshore foreign currency receipts through domestic banks, but may hold eligible current account receipts in foreign currency settlement accounts or convert to RMB at quoted spot rates. The practical hurdles for repatriating dividends are not convertibility itself but the evidential burden: banks demand audited financials, tax payment certificates, and contractual evidence of the underlying service income, and any mismatched invoice, contract, or tax return will stall the remittance for compliance review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory closed-period surrender of export proceeds. The legal rule is repatriation: all current account foreign exchange receipts arising from the marine surveying service contract must be brought into the entity's domestic foreign currency settlement account and cannot be retained offshore without SAFE approval. The converting entity may retain the foreign currency or sell it to a bank at the prevailing market rate; there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio. However, the bank must match each receipt to the underlying export/service transaction in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System, and any receipt unresolved against a contract or customs declaration beyond the standard monitoring window is flagged as a compliance exception.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low and legally restricted. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as a typical foreign fintech account provider or international wallet cannot lawfully domicile or service a Mainland-domiciled WFOE's primary treasury operations because they lack a PRC banking license and are not recognized as qualified settlement institutions under SAFE regulations. Such platforms are also barred from opening domestic CNY accounts or holding the entity's capital escrow balance. They may be used only as sub-ledger aids or for non-banking ancillary payment initiation, never as the primary regulated clearing rail. The bank account must sit with a licensed domestic commercial bank; Wise and similar services are not a substitute for corporate banking compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection must arrive through an SWIFT or cross-border remittance into the FDI Capital Settlement Account at the chosen Tier-1 domestic bank after the company has obtained its business license, opened the bank account, and completed FX registration with SAFE through the bank. The payment must carry a clear purpose code describing foreign direct investment capital injection, and the remitter must match the registered shareholder exactly. The bank will verify the FDI declaration, monitor the actual payment against the registered capital amount, require a capital verification report in Chinese issued by a licensed PRC accounting firm, and then transfer eligible amounts into the RMB settlement account only after full review. Parent company loans must be separately registered as foreign debts if they exceed regulatory thresholds or maturities, and equity injections cannot be disguised as operating revenue.For operating outflows such as surveyor subcontractor fees, reinsurance payments, and vessel equipment procurement, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with full remittance purpose codes and contract evidence held at the remitting bank. For large or documentary trade settlements involving vessel repairs, spare parts, or marine equipment, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Documentary Collection may be used, supported by commercial invoices, transport documents, and inspection certificates. All outbound disbursements must be matched to an underlying current account transaction; service payments require the signed survey contract, tax payment certificate for withholding if applicable, and a receipt from the foreign party. Capital account outflows, such as dividend remittances, require an additional distribution resolution, audited financials, tax clearance, and the SAFE-related remittance filing through the bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the PRC special tax adjustment documentation rules (SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42), a Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions reach: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers or license/use arrangements; RMB 100 million for financial asset transactions; or RMB 40 million for provision of services. A Master File may be required when prior-year related-party revenue reaches the applicable high threshold, and Country-by-Country Reporting is mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime; however, the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, together with the beneficial ownership test in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, impose substance-over-form and economic substance requirements. A WFOE marine insurance surveyor must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China: employing qualified marine surveyors and inspectors, maintaining local office and inspection equipment, making key commercial decisions in China, assuming genuine professional liability and operating risk, and deriving its performance-based service revenue from actual economic functions. If the WFOE claims treaty benefits, distributes profits, or charges intercompany fees while having little or no economic substance, the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the transaction, deem taxable income, and pierce the corporate veil where shareholders abuse limited liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 32, 63, and 64, the tax authority will recover the underpaid tax, impose daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and levy an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. If the amount of tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents more than 10% of the tax due, PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability: up to three years imprisonment or detention and a fine; for huge amounts and more than 30% of the tax due, three to seven years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, actual controllers, and chief financial officers who knowingly approve or participate in false accounting cannot hide behind corporate limited liability. Under the PRC Company Law veil-piercing doctrine, a shareholder that abuses the separate legal personality of the WFOE or co-mingles corporate and personal assets is jointly and severally liable for creditors' losses. Responsible individuals may be subject to exit-control measures, travel bans, property freezing, and asset restitution. Criminal conviction for tax evasion carries direct imprisonment, fines, and prohibition from serving as company director or manager.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority is empowered under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, and 48 to make special tax adjustments, adjust taxable income according to arm's-length comparables, and assess additional tax and interest on the underpaid tax. Failure to provide related-party transaction documentation allows deemed income assessment and may also trigger penalties under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 where the substance of the arrangement amounts to fraudulent underpayment. Similarly structured transactions may be denied deduction, and the interest charged may include a benchmark loan interest rate plus a punitive uplift of five percentage points under the special tax adjustment regime.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief officers who sign false transfer pricing documentation or approve artificial service fee structures face personal civil liability if the WFOE becomes insolvent as a result of excessive profit drains. The courts may pierce the corporate veil where the arrangement is used to divert corporate assets for private benefit or to evade taxes. Officers may be blacklisted by the tax authority and prevented from leaving China while tax adjustments are outstanding; in egregious cases, criminal fraud and evasion charges may extend to the individuals who approved the TP scheme.
Profit Repatriation Withholding Non-Compliance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37 and 40 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69, the withholding agent must withhold and remit the tax on the gross dividend; failure to do so exposes the WFOE to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unremitted withheld tax, recovery of the unpaid tax, and daily late-payment surcharge. A treaty claim will be denied if the recipient is found to be a conduit lacking beneficial ownership, meaning the full statutory 10% WHT and penalties apply. Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the transaction may be recharacterized and the tax benefit disallowed.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who authorize dividend payments without ensuring withholding remittance are personally exposed to penalties and possible criminal liability if the non-remittance constitutes fraud or misappropriation. The tax authority may impose travel bans on representatives of the WFOE until outstanding withholding tax is settled. If the shareholder or director has used the WFOE as a mere instrument to extract profits and evade creditor or tax claims, the corporate veil may be pierced and the shareholder may be held personally liable for the unpaid withholding taxes, penalties, and interest.
