Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The PRC Company Law imposes no minimum registered capital and no blocked escrow account for ordinary wholly foreign-owned limited liability companies. The shareholder must subscribe the full registered capital in the Articles of Association and satisfy the paid-in contribution within five years of incorporation, on the payment schedule stated in the AOA. The capital contribution may be cash or lawful non-monetary assets. For the maritime bunkering business, the transport and port regulator may require proof of net assets or a bank guarantee sufficient for vessel operation, fuel inventory and third-party marine liability; concurrently, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) requires the WFOE to complete foreign investment registration before the inward capital transfer is credited, and the capital injection must be placed in the enterprise capital account in accordance with the foreign exchange administration rules.

Funding & unlock path: The WFOE must obtain the fire safety completion acceptance certificate and the operating-occupancy fire inspection approval from the Municipal Fire and Rescue Department for all fixed buildings, fuel storage areas, pump houses and shore-side installations, with structural load inspections evidenced by the engineering design review and completion acceptance documents. Civil defense construction approval, or a verified exemption, is required for any new building meeting the statutory people's-air-defense threshold; for leased premises, the lessor's civil defense and fire certificates must be preserved in the corporate operations file. The company must complete the workplace safety pre-evaluation, sign the consolidated workplace safety condition declaration, carry safety production liability insurance, appoint full-time or part-time safety officers as required, and submit bunkering and dangerous-goods handling safety procedures to the Maritime Safety Administration. All operational staff must hold valid seafarer certificates, tanker training endorsements, dangerous goods handling certificates and oil-transfer supervision qualifications required by the port and maritime authorities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Taxpayer Portal (e-Tax Bureau, 国家税务总局电子税务局).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), then complete tax registration at the competent local tax authority within 30 days of the business license issuance. The statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is assigned by the State Taxation Administration and is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 统一社会信用代码), which serves as the sole tax identification number. The WFOE must file the Tax Registration Form (税务登记表), submit the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity documents, and proof of registered address. Foreign shareholders must provide notarized and authenticated incorporation documents and passport copies. After verification, the STA issues a tax registration certificate and activates the e-Tax account. The WFOE must also complete payroll registration, social insurance registration, and, if applicable, value-added tax general taxpayer status registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods is zero-rated under Article 15 of the Provisional Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Value-Added Tax, provided the goods are physically exported from China and customs export procedures are completed. For offshore marine fuel bunkering supplied to international vessels, the supply may qualify as export zero-rating if it is loaded onto the vessel as stores or fuel for the international voyage and documented as exported goods under customs supervision. The enterprise must be a general VAT taxpayer, obtain the customs export declaration, receive foreign currency settlement, and provide export receipts and transport documents. Digital services and offshore supplies not physically crossing customs may only qualify under specific cross-border service zero-rating rules where the recipient is offshore and the service is wholly consumed outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) / Bill of Entry
  • Bill of Lading (B/L)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange income receipt
  • Bunkering delivery receipt and ship's bunker requisition/delivery note
  • Export VAT invoice (增值税普通发票 or export invoice)
  • Supply contract and commercial invoice
  • Customs supervision warehouse or bonded oil supply documentation where applicable

China mandates the use of the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform (电子发票服务平台) and the Unified Electronic Invoices System. VAT invoices must be issued through the e-invoice system with real-time transmission to the tax authority. The system applies strict invoice quota controls, cryptographic tax-control devices (税控设备), and enterprise digital stamping. Businesses must use the 全电发票 (fully digitalized electronic invoice) system in pilot and now nationwide rollout, with automatic invoice data clearance to the STA. No private offline invoicing is permitted.

The State Taxation Administration may deny zero-rating and impose the standard 13% VAT plus late payment surcharge if the export is not fully substantiated within the required period. Fraudulent export tax rebates are punishable under the Criminal Law of the PRC as tax evasion crimes, including fines ranging from 50% to five times the fraudulent amount and imprisonment of up to life in serious cases. The STA also applies forfaitory clawback of VAT exempted or refunded, plus interest penalties of 0.05% per day, and may disqualify the entity from export tax rebate facilitation procedures.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed float regime with strict controls on the capital account. The renminbi is conditionally convertible on the current account, allowing trade and services transactions, while capital account transactions such as foreign direct investment, debt, and equity flows require prior approval or registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For a WFOE engaged in ship bunkering, the capital contribution in foreign currency must be converted into RMB through a designated foreign exchange bank after filing a Foreign Investment Registration certificate. Profit repatriation is treated as a current account item, but requires submission of audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance certificates, and board resolutions confirming dividend distribution. All outward remittances must be supported by genuine underlying documentation; otherwise, banks will reject them. Additionally, the WFOE must retain export proceeds in its designated bank account or voluntarily convert them; forced surrender requirements have been abolished. However, capital inflows for investment may be subject to a prudential ratio and the real-business principle. In practice, convertibility is enabled only with full compliance, and restrictions remain on the use of RMB for offshore speculative transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under China's current foreign exchange rules, export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender ratio. The exporter must repatriate the proceeds through a designated bank within the prescribed period (normally 90 days after customs declaration), file the trade in goods and services with the bank under the Trade Foreign Exchange Settlement system, and provide corresponding customs declaration forms and transaction documents. No minimum conversion into RMB is required; the company may retain the foreign currency in its current account or sell it to the bank at the prevailing market rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally permitted to service corporate entities in mainland China for cross-border banking or FX facilitation. Only banks licensed by the PBOC and SAFE may handle foreign exchange for onshore entities. Offshore fintech EMIs are therefore not a viable channel; attempting to use them would create legal and regulatory risks. Net score: extremely low viability.

The PRC Company Law requires all domestic companies, including wholly foreign-owned enterprises, to prepare annual financial accounting reports and obtain an independent audit from a PRC-qualified accounting firm. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

A calendar-year WFOE must file quarterly provisional returns within 15 days after the end of each quarter and must file the annual return and pay the balance of tax within 5 months after the end of the calendar year, i.e. by 31 May of the following year.
