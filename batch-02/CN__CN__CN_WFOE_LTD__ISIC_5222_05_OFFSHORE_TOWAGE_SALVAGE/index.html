A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not an acceptable registered address for a WFOE in the maritime towing and salvage sector. PRC company registration, tax registration, bank account opening, and maritime transport licensing require a physical business premise in the registered jurisdiction. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary WFOE and no blocked escrow or capital impound account is required. The registered capital is subscribed by the shareholders and paid in cash according to the capital contribution schedule in the Articles of Association. For the maritime towage and salvage license, the competent authority may require evidence of financial capacity, fleet ownership and insurance, but this is not a corporate registration escrow condition.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety acceptance inspection is required for the registered office, shore command base and warehouse; civil defense structural inspection is required for cranes, mooring bollards, quay fixtures and lifting equipment; occupational safety and health registration is required under the local Emergency Management Bureau; China Classification Society certificate of compliance is required for onboard firefighting systems and life-saving appliances; maritime labour certificate and STCW crew documentation must also be maintained.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online fiscal portal is the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax / Electronic Tax Administration platform) administered by STA and the local competent tax offices

E-government filing portal

China operates a unified social credit code system: the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the sole TIN/VAT registration number; no separate taxpayer identification certificate is issued. The foreign parent and the WFOE must (1) complete SAMR enterprise name approval and, where relevant, obtain the maritime towing/salvage business permit from the competent maritime authority; (2) file the articles of association and registration particulars with the State Administration for Market Regulation through the national enterprise registration portal and receive the business license with USCC; (3) rely on automatic inter-departmental data transfer to STA to complete basic tax registration; (4) activate the WFOE's e-Tax taxpayer account using the legal representative's identity verification; (5) complete supplementary tax registration and VAT taxpayer status determination (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) through the e-Tax platform; (6) apply for electronic invoice types, invoice quota, and export zero-rating/refund registration; and (7) for a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China, file with the local STA or use the domestic purchaser-withholding mechanism to obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification code for withholding, exemption or refund filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is a PRC resident enterprise for corporate income tax and a VAT taxpayer on all taxable Chinese supplies, with no pass-through eligibility

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not provide check-the-box entity classification or hybrid mismatch elections. The only applicable status election is the small-scale VAT taxpayer versus general VAT taxpayer election under the PRC VAT regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies to PRC-incorporated WFOEs for basic tax registration; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises when annual taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period exceeds RMB 5,000,000, with voluntary general taxpayer registration available below that threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported outside China's customs territory and qualifying cross-border taxable services supplied to overseas clients and consumed outside the PRC are eligible for the 0% export VAT rate. Offshore deep-sea tugboat towage and maritime salvage contracts performed outside the PRC customs territory, for a non-resident principal, settled in foreign currency, with service performance documented outside mainland China, may be classified as cross-border taxable services and zero-rated. The WFOE must file the zero-rating/export refund declaration in the e-Tax platform and assemble the evidence package. Services rendered wholly or partly in PRC waters for domestic counterparties, or coastal towage within China, are not zero-rated and are taxable at the applicable positive VAT rate (generally 6% for maritime auxiliary services or the applicable rate where supply/goods elements are present).

  • GACC export customs declaration (electronic EDI customs export form; required only for physical goods/equipment export accompanying the service)
  • Full set of signed ocean Bills of Lading or equivalent towage voyage order/master's receipt evidencing vessel departure from a PRC port to an overseas or high-seas performance area
  • Cross-border towage/salvage service contract (ISIC 5222 scope) naming the overseas client, vessel identification, salvage/towage price and payment terms in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice and packing/settlement statement prepared in the invoiced foreign currency
  • Master's salvage report or vessel position log certified by the master/operator confirming performance occurred outside mainland China
  • Bank foreign-exchange inward receipt and SAFE service-trade foreign-exchange collection/declaration record evidencing timely repatriation of the export/service income
  • Zero-rated export VAT declaration and fully digitalized e-invoice record filed through the e-Tax platform

All VAT invoices must be issued through the national unified electronic invoice service platform. Fully digitalized electronic special VAT invoices are generated with a unique invoice code, encrypted tax-control number, anti-counterfeiting QR code and digital stamp, and are transmitted to STA in real time at issuance. Red-letter (negative) invoices require prior online clearance. Enterprises must transmit electronic invoice data through the official e-Tax/API interface; handwritten, paper-only or non-cleared invoices do not support input VAT credit or export zero-rating.

STA and GACC may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively. Where documentation is missing, inconsistent or false, the zero-rated treatment is clawed back and VAT is assessed at the applicable positive rate, with late payment interest from the date the tax was originally due. Administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law may reach 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid tax. Fraudulent export refund arrangements involving forged customs or foreign-exchange documents, circular trade or false service performance can trigger suspension or revocation of export tax refund qualifications, referral to customs/antismuggling and SAFE enforcement, and criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi remains partially convertible outside the mainland. Current account receipts and payments, including cross-border towage, salvage, bunkering, and ancillary maritime support services, are generally convertible through supervised banks upon submission of genuine trade or service contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and SAFE transaction reporting. Capital account transactions are strictly controlled: the WFOE's registered capital injection must enter through a dedicated SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account, must be traced to the overseas investor's own bank account, and cannot be repurposed or converted into offshore CNY without a fully documented internal payment order. Profit remittance is permitted only after the WFOE has completed its statutory PRC audit, filed corporate income tax returns, settled any related stamp and dividend withholding taxes, and demonstrated via bank-side authenticity review that distributable retained earnings genuinely exist. In practice, conversion and outward remittance face documentary intensity rather than an outright ban: every conversion requires a fund-use declaration, every outward payment requires an underlying contract and invoice, and SAFE's Banking Interbank Foreign Exchange System must capture the correct transaction and revenue codes. Thin capitalisation, round-tripping, and payment-chain opacity are the principal enforcement triggers during the bank's enhanced due diligence review of a maritime salvage-towage treasury profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export or service-trade foreign currency receipts, and China no longer imposes a full-surrender requirement. However, all export and maritime service proceeds must be repatriated into the designated onshore bank and either retained in an eligible foreign currency settlement account or converted to Renminbi. For goods exports, the statutory repatriation deadline is generally 180 days after customs release unless SAFE approves a deferred collection. For cross-border towage and salvage service receivables, the customary bank-enforced inward remittance deadline is 180 days from the invoice or completion-of-service date. Upon receipt, the WFOE must complete revenue verification and SAFE transaction declaration within five working days; any overdue collection must be justified to the reviewing bank and may require SAFE prior consent.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated foreign-exchange regulatory authority granted to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

The overseas parent must remit registered capital via SWIFT MT103 using its own segregated bank account as the sole remitter. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low — legally untenable as the primary treasury and clearing rail inside Mainland China. PBOC and the National Financial Regulatory Administration do not authorize non-bank payment institutions or offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Payoneer to open corporate bank accounts for WFOEs, perform SAFE-regulated foreign exchange settlement, or receive FDI registered capital. Their permissible role is limited to ancillary cross-border receivable collection in jurisdictions outside China, and they can never serve as the onshore settlement account for capital conversion, profit remittance, or trade payables from the WFOE. All material liquidity must therefore be held with a licensed onshore commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory in RMB terms where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 40 million for related-party services, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is mandatory where the enterprise's aggregate annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion. The WFOE must also file the annual related-party transaction report with its CIT return regardless of whether a Local File threshold is exceeded.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance-over-form doctrine is applied through Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, STA Announcement 2018 No. 9 on beneficial ownership, and the BEPS Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test. A WFOE performing deep-sea tugboat towage and salvage must prove its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China: ownership or long-term charter of the towage fleet, employment of master and crew in China, dispatch and salvage command decisions made from China, revenue contracts signed by Chinese management, and risk management held in China. If the WFOE is an artificial shell structured to access treaty benefits or to shift salvage profit to a foreign affiliate, the STA may recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty relief, impose statutory WHT and apply GAAR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion under the Tax Collection Administration Law and Criminal Law

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law imposes collection of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% of the overdue amount, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law criminalizes the act where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, with imprisonment up to 3 years for relatively large evasion and 3 to 7 years for huge evasion coupled with serious means and false documentation.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced if the limited liability company is used as a vehicle to evade tax and abuse creditor interests; directors who order, sign or conceal false accounts are jointly and severally liable for the civil tax debt and can be personally blacklisted under the travel restriction system. Enforcement courts may freeze personal bank accounts, prohibit the transfer of personal assets, prohibit purchase of high-value goods, and ban overseas travel. Directors and the legal representative may additionally face imprisonment as principals or accomplices in the tax evasion.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and Conduit Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and STA Announcement 2018 No. 9, the tax authority may deny beneficial owner status and recharacterize the dividend, interest or royalty payment; the statutory 10% WHT becomes immediately due together with the 0.05% daily late surcharge. A withholding agent that fails to withhold or remits the tax improperly is subject to a fine of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; the denial may also treat the recharacterized amounts as hidden dividends liable to further CIT adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve distributions without verifying the ultimate beneficial owner and without resolving the treaty claim are exposed to personal liability for the shortfall where company assets are insufficient. A director who signs false beneficial-ownership declarations or forged TRCs can be prosecuted for document falsification and tax fraud; personal property can be preserved, the director may be named on the social credit blacklist, and overseas travel may be prohibited while the administrative penalty and tax recovery are pending.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 to 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and STA Announcement 2016 No. 42, the STA may conduct a special tax investigation and adjust the taxable income according to the arm's-length principle. Where documentation is absent, incomplete or unreliable, the tax authority may estimate the adjustment and apply interest loading of 5 percentage points above the PBOC benchmark rate in addition to the daily late-payment surcharge. False or incomplete related-party transaction reporting may lead to administrative fines up to RMB 100,000, denial of deduction for undocumented management fees, and recharacterization of unpaid advances as constructive dividends subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally structure intercompany prices to strip profit from China can be held civilly liable for the corporate tax adjustment and interest if their actions amount to abuse of corporate personality; the court may pierce the veil when the WFOE is insolvent and cannot satisfy the adjusted liability. Criminal liability arises where the transfer-pricing manipulation is accompanied by fraudulent invoicing and deliberate false tax returns under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, with possible imprisonment, personal fines, director disqualification, and blacklisting under the enforcement and exit-embargo rules.
