Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Company Law of the People's Republic of China does not impose a statutory minimum registered capital for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company. The sole shareholder must state the subscribed capital in the Articles of Association and contribute that capital in full within five years from the date of establishment for companies registered after 1 July 2024. No blocked escrow account or proof of paid-up capital is required at the time of company incorporation. However, for the customs bonded truck fleet sector, the road transport regulator and the customs authority may, during the subsequent sectoral licensing phase, assess whether the registered capital and available funds are sufficient to cover the planned fleet size, customs bond obligations, insurance and safety liabilities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, served locally through the competent district/city tax bureaus and the E-Tax online platform (the official electronic tax filing and invoice portal of the STA).

E-government filing portal

After registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) on the Business License is the statutory taxpayer identification number for the WFOE. The WFOE must complete the tax registration supplement through the E-Tax platform with the competent local STA office within 30 days of business establishment, covering enterprise income tax, VAT, and withholding obligations. It must file its bank account details, legal representative/controller information, and tax accountant contact; register for VAT; and apply for general VAT taxpayer status once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 (or elect it earlier to preserve input VAT credits). Foreign investors must provide their home-country corporate registration/passport evidence and complete the SAFE foreign-investment registration through the receiving bank. Because the entity will operate a customs-bonded truck fleet, it must also complete China Customs registration for supervised/bonded transportation and obtain the electronic GPS seal and customs transit approvals before moving export cargo.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every WFOE must carry a VAT identity; mandatory upgrade to general VAT taxpayer occurs when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. Below that threshold small-scale VAT treatment (simplified rate) may apply. For the bonded trucking scope, applicable VAT rates are 9% for road transportation services, 6% for auxiliary/logistics support, and 0% for qualifying cross-border/international transport services; goods remain at the 13% standard rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT rate applies to exported goods that are legally declared to China Customs and actually leave China, and to qualifying cross-border taxable services supplied to overseas purchasers and consumed outside China. For the bonded road feeder service, zero-rating is substantiated only when the service forms part of an international transport chain, is performed under customs transit supervision, and is evidenced by a through air waybill/international consignment note or equivalent multimodal export document; revenue must be received from an overseas counterparty in foreign exchange. If the feeder movement is merely a domestic leg to an airport for a separate export contract, that domestic transport service remains taxable at 9% (or 6% for ancillary logistics support), and zero-rating cannot be claimed on the feeder service itself.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration) carrying the customs clearance electronic data
  • Master Air Waybill / Bill of Lading or International Road Consignment Note (CMR) covering the onward international carriage
  • Bonded customs transit seal record and GPS geo-location log for the sealed truck movement to the port/airport
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales/service contract identifying the overseas counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign exchange collection receipt matching the declared export transaction

VAT invoices are governed by the STA national VAT invoice system. China uses digitalized e-invoices: the taxpayer issues through STA-approved invoicing software connected to the E-Tax platform, the invoice XML is transmitted in near real time to the STA e-invoice service platform, and the system assigns a national unique invoice number and digital fingerprint. Buyers verify invoices through the national invoice verification platform and the digital tax account. The bonded trucking WFOE must therefore maintain STA-compliant e-invoicing capability; VAT special invoices are required where the customer claims input credits, and export-related documentation must be supported by e-invoice records.

STA, China Customs, and SAFE operate cross-matching audits of export declarations, e-invoice data, customs transit records, and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rated export or cross-border service support is found fraudulent or unsubstantiated, the refund/output VAT exemption is clawed back with statutory late payment surcharge, a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and possible criminal liability for fraudulent tax-credit/export-rebate filing under Chinese law. The tax authority may retrospectively reassess beyond the ordinary limitation period where fraudulent intent is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China. The Renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, subject to genuine transaction verification and the matching of customs declarations, invoices, and contracts. However, the capital account remains heavily regulated: inbound foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise requires FDI registration with SAFE through the bank and the opening of a capital account (foreign exchange settlement account) before any injection. Foreign currency received from the parent must be designated with a specific payment purpose code and cannot be used for purposes outside the approved business scope. While China no longer imposes mandatory forced surrender of export proceeds, all export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through designated banks and reconciled with the Trade Monitoring System; the enterprise may voluntarily convert to RMB or retain the foreign currency subject to its current account foreign exchange account ceiling. Dividend distributions to foreign shareholders are permitted after the annual statutory audit and corporate income tax final settlement; the remittance must be supported by an audit report, tax payment certificates, board resolution approving the distribution, and a SAFE-registered remittance application. Practically, profit extraction is separable from capital reduction or equity transfer, both of which require additional legal and tax procedures. Customs bonded trucking operators must also ensure that any duty-free or bonded-related cash flows are segregated and fully verifiable through the customs POD system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of export clearance, unless an extension is approved by SAFE. Current law imposes no mandatory conversion ratio; 100% of proceeds must be repatriated through a designated forex bank and registered in the Trade Monitoring System, but the enterprise may voluntarily settle 0-100% into RMB or retain the foreign currency within the ceiling of its current account foreign exchange account. The retained foreign currency balance cannot exceed the verified annual operating revenue and trade-related receipts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Airwallex, MultiPass) are not licensed banking institutions in China and cannot directly provide a WFOE with domestic RMB clearing through CNAPS or a local bank sort code. They are legally barred from acting as a primary account provider for a Chinese-domiciled entity with a valid business license; only banks holding a banking license from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and designated as a Forex Bank by SAFE may handle FX verification, capital settlement, and cross-border trade payment reconciliation. Certain fintechs operate through licensed China bank partners as payment gateways, but this does not remove the statutory KYC duty, SAFE registration obligation, or the requirement for a physical corporate bank relationship. The viability of using an offshore EMI as the main treasury rail is therefore very low and should be rejected for capital and operating accounts; at most, an EMI may support ancillary inbound remittance as a payment instruction service, but the final clearing must be executed via the domestic commercial banking system under the real-name account of the WFOE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must complete FDI registration with SAFE through the chosen domestic bank under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system, open an FDI capital account (foreign exchange settlement account), and receive the inbound remittance from the overseas parent company using SWIFT MT103 with an SAFE-designated FDI payment purpose code. After receipt, a licensed Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the injection, after which funds can be converted into RMB only for payments within the approved business scope, truck procurement, rental deposits, insurance, and operational expenses. For single-investment amounts exceeding the macroprudential threshold, additional SAFE filing or approval may be triggered under the cross-border financing macroprudential adjustment rules, and the WFOE must ensure the negative list restrictions do not apply to its road feeder and customs bonded logistics activities.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 for documentary remittances related to spare parts, technology licensing fees, and overseas service charges; Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for imported vehicles, telematics equipment, and major fleet-related capital goods requiring supplier financing; and Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) for trusted open-account transactions with established overseas logistics partners. Domestic RMB disbursements to fuel suppliers, toll authorities, maintenance workshops, and independent drivers should be executed via CNAPS ACH or the bank's enterprise internet banking platform, with every payment supported by a tax invoice, transport contract, or trip record to maintain a clear audit trail and comply with SAFE and tax bureau requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut Business, Airwallex, MultiPass) are not licensed banking institutions in China and cannot directly provide a WFOE with domestic RMB clearing through CNAPS or a local bank sort code. They are legally barred from acting as a primary account provider for a Chinese-domiciled entity with a valid business license; only banks holding a banking license from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and designated as a Forex Bank by SAFE may handle FX verification, capital settlement, and cross-border trade payment reconciliation. Certain fintechs operate through licensed China bank partners as payment gateways, but this does not remove the statutory KYC duty, SAFE registration obligation, or the requirement for a physical corporate bank relationship. The viability of using an offshore EMI as the main treasury rail is therefore very low and should be rejected for capital and operating accounts; at most, an EMI may support ancillary inbound remittance as a payment instruction service, but the final clearing must be executed via the domestic commercial banking system under the real-name account of the WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required when annual related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions in aggregate exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is required when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report threshold is RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council ESR/CIGA framework, but the Economic Substance doctrine under the State Taxation Administration rules and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EITL Article 47) prevents treaty and tax-rate shopping. A trucking WFOE should demonstrate CIGA-style substance: locally employed drivers, dispatch and maintenance teams, GPS-monitored customs seals under WFOE control, vehicle ownership or multi-year leases, invoices and contracts, board meeting records, and risk-management decision-making. Lack of substance may cause the tax authority to deny treaty benefits, recharacterize royalty/dividend payments, and impose penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False VAT Invoicing in Bonded Trucking Operations

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the taxpayer must pay the underpaid tax and interest plus a penalty from 50% to 5 times the amount underpaid. Under PRC Criminal Law Article 205, issuing false VAT invoices is punishable by imprisonment up to life and confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 and Civil Code Article 83 where the director abused the separate personality of the WFOE to evade tax and defraud customs. The director is jointly and severally liable for restoration of corporate assets; may be placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist with exit-restriction and disqualification; and if personally involved, may face criminal imprisonment of up to 15 years or life for VAT fraud.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 empowers the STA to make special tax adjustment under GAAR and recharacterize the arrangement; SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9 denies treaty benefits if the claimant is not the beneficial owner. Sanctions include repayment of under-withheld tax, daily 0.05% interest, and penalties of 50% to 5x underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law.

Director personal liability: If the director signs treaty-benefit declarations knowing the WFOE has no economic substance, he or she is exposed to personal restitution for the diverted withholding tax, being named in the major tax violation blacklist, a travel ban under Exit-Entry Administration Law Article 12, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to 7 years for substantial underpaid tax.
Customs Bonded Vehicle Violation / Smuggling Through Feeder Falsification

Statutory sanction: Customs Law Article 82 requires confiscation of smuggled goods, transport equipment and proceeds and imposes fines of up to 3 times the evaded duties; Criminal Law Article 153 imposes fixed-term imprisonment (up to life for large or aggravated smuggling).

Director personal liability: A director who organizes or allows GPS tampering can be prosecuted as a smuggler, with corporate veil piercing to recover unpaid customs duties from personal assets; customs authorities may revoke the bonded transport license, blacklist and ban the director from undertaking customs affairs, and impose imprisonment of 3 to 10 years (or life where the evaded duty and product category are aggravating).
