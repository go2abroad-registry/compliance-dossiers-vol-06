Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The 2023 Company Law imposes no uniform minimum paid-up capital for ordinary limited liability companies. For this wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the statutory minimum is zero for the aircraft engine dry leasing scope. Registered capital must be subscribed in an amount reasonably commensurate with proposed business operations and paid in full within five years from the date of issuance of the business license unless the shareholders agree on an earlier schedule. No escrow or blocked account is required by law; a bank may, however, require an earmarked capital account where lease finance or loan covenants call for custodial arrangements.

Funding & unlock path: Office-only facility: the building owner's fire safety inspection certificate and a fire safety self-inspection record suffice; no Civil Defense fire permit is required because there is no manufacturing storage or processing area. Workplace safety is limited to general obligations under the Work Safety Law, including emergency exit maintenance and employee safety training. If the WFOE later establishes a hangar or maintenance workshop, it must apply for planning approval, fire protection design review, completion fire acceptance from the local Fire and Rescue Department, and register the workplace with the relevant occupational safety regulatory body.

Paid-in capital rule: The 2023 Company Law imposes no uniform minimum paid-up capital for ordinary limited liability companies. For this wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the statutory minimum is zero for the aircraft engine dry leasing scope. Registered capital must be subscribed in an amount reasonably commensurate with proposed business operations and paid in full within five years from the date of issuance of the business license unless the shareholders agree on an earlier schedule. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; administrative filings and registrations are processed through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal operated by STA, with taxpayer service supported by the 12366 tax service hotline.

E-government filing portal

For the newly established Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the foreign shareholder first incorporates the entity through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) national enterprise registration system; the issued business license contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which automatically constitutes the WFOE's taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax and VAT. Within 30 days of issuance, the legal representative or authorised tax agent must complete tax registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), confirming the primary tax authority, tax filing period, and subject classification. For VAT purposes, the WFOE must classify itself as a small-scale taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer; if cumulative taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, general taxpayer registration is mandatory, while below the threshold general taxpayer registration may be elected where the statutory conditions are met. The WFOE then registers for electronic invoice issuance through the STA e-invoice service platform or an STA-certified e-invoicing API, obtains its e-invoice quota, and begins real-time digital invoice transmission. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment or place of business that leases aircraft engines into China and derives Chinese-source lease income subject to withholding, the non-resident enterprise must complete a separate tax registration with the local STA sub-bureau where the payer or withholding agent is located; STA assigns a dedicated non-resident enterprise TIN for corporate income tax withholding and VAT obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax at 25% on worldwide income; no pass-through or flow-through treatment is available, and it is not treated as a territorially exempt or tax-holiday entity.

Available election forms

None available for entity classification. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box, partnership-style transparency election, hybrid mismatch election, or tax consolidation election for a WFOE limited liability company. The only administrative elections are the VAT small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer election, and the simplified taxation election for certain prescribed services where specifically authorised by STA circulars; there is no election to alter the entity-level tax character of the WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when cumulative taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the statutory simplified levy rate.

China has mandated the Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice (FDEI) reform. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA e-Tax/e-invoice service platform or through an STA-certified enterprise e-invoicing API; each invoice must carry an invoice code, encryption signature, and anti-fraud QR code in the standard OFD/XML data format. Issued invoice data is transmitted in real time to STA's Golden Tax Project Phase IV central data platform, where it is automatically reconciled with VAT returns, customs export declarations, bank payment data, and foreign-exchange receipt data. Newly established WFOEs are expected to adopt fully digitalised e-invoicing from inception; legacy tax-control devices remain permitted only during the express transitional period allowed by local STA.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a current-account convertible regime for trade and services while retaining administrative controls over capital-account flows. The WFOE may freely accept and settle foreign currency lease rentals, pay cross-border service fees, and remit distributable profits after tax, provided that SAFE reporting, tax withholdings, customs declarations, and anti-money-laundering controls are satisfied. The RMB does not float freely; the onshore daily band is managed by the PBOC against a currency basket, and SAFE supervises all foreign exchange transactions through the bank reporting system. Capital inflows such as equity injections, shareholder loans, and external debt require FDI registration, foreign debt registration or quota approval, and an onshore capital account before conversion. Profit remittance, although current-account in principle, is deferred until the statutory annual audit, and the bank requires tax clearance certificates, board resolutions, audited financial statements, and supporting lease contracts. Surrender of export and service proceeds is no longer mandatory, but lease revenues must be repatriated to China and credited to a domestic foreign-exchange current account within the statutory reporting periods; funds can be retained in foreign currency or converted at the operator's election. The practical result is that the WFOE treasury must maintain segregated capital and current accounts, dual bookkeeping, and robust documentation for every FX movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, foreign currency export proceeds must be repatriated to China and received into an onshore foreign-exchange account within 180 days of the customs export declaration. For service trade such as aircraft engine dry leasing, the proceeds must be repatriated and reported within the applicable SAFE reporting window, generally 180 days from the invoice or contractual due date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100 percent in foreign currency or convert into RMB at market rates through a licensed bank. However, the entire amount must be captured on the bank's SAFE reporting system and supported by the lease agreement, invoice, and proof of actual service delivery; any late repatriation, netting without approval, or unreported foreign receipt triggers enhanced scrutiny and penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-viable as a primary treasury rail. China does not grant offshore fintech EMI or digital bank licenses for local onboarding, and a cross-border digital EMI cannot open a CNAPS account, settle RMB directly, or accept the WFOE as a client in a banking capacity. Such platforms are legally barred from servicing mainland entities as deposit-taking institutions. They may act only as ancillary payment facilitators if the underlying funds are held by a licensed onshore commercial bank and the EMI has a registered commercial presence in China; even then, the official SWIFT/CIPS banking rails and SAFE reporting remain mandatory for capital injections and lease-related cross-border flows.

Compliant routing requires completion of SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration with a unique FDI code; injection by each foreign shareholder from its own bank account via SWIFT MT103 using the stated purpose of equity investment or registered capital contribution for the WFOE; crediting of funds to the onshore Foreign Exchange Capital Account in the WFOE's name; authenticity review by the receiving bank before conversion of funds into RMB; negative-list compliance screening where applicable; engagement of a licensed Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report within the statutory capital contribution period; and retention of the SWIFT advice, bank credit slip, CPA report, SAFE receipts, and corporate resolutions for audit.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% of the gross dividend paid to a non-resident enterprise shareholder when the dividend is China-source and the recipient has no permanent establishment in China, under PRC EIT Law Articles 3(3), 27(4) and 37. The rate can be reduced, most commonly to 5% for a corporate shareholder resident in a treaty jurisdiction that beneficially owns at least 25% of the equity, under the relevant DTA, including the China-Hong Kong double tax arrangement.
