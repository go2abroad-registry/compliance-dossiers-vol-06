Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under national law. No minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise limited liability company under the Company Law of the PRC. Shareholders must subscribe to the registered capital in the Articles of Association and pay the subscribed capital within five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter or longer period is otherwise prescribed by law. For a port stevedoring terminal, the port licensing authority may assess financial capability and projected capital expenditure as part of the Port Operation License application, but this is not an escrow or blocked-account requirement. Paid-in capital is credited to the company's capital account maintained at a licensed bank and may be applied to lawful business purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Air Defense (Civil Defense) construction approval is required for terminal fixed buildings and any personnel shelter area, as determined by the local civil air defense authority. Fire safety certification includes a fire protection design review and a completion fire safety acceptance certificate issued by the Fire and Rescue Department for all buildings, warehouses and vessel-berthing installations. Occupational and workplace safety clearance under the Work Safety Law requires a terminal safety assessment, a safety production responsibility system, emergency response plans, dust-control measures, and training records for stevedores and signalmen. Special equipment such as quay cranes, belt conveyors, mobile hoppers and forklifts must be inspected and registered with the municipal Market Regulation Administration before first use. The terminal must also carry an approved Port Facility Security Plan and appoint a Port Facility Security Officer in accordance with the ISPS Code and Chinese maritime safety regulations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-tax) national unified platform at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, operating through provincial e-tax portals.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the local Market Regulation Administration (SAMR) as a foreign-invested limited liability company and obtain the Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Complete post-issuance tax registration via the Electronic Tax Bureau, including confirmation of taxpayer details, tax types (CIT, VAT, stamp duty, etc.), filing frequency, bank account filing, and statutory bookkeeping requirements. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment or fixed base in China, file a separate non-resident enterprise tax registration through the withholding agent or directly with the STA using e-tax, and obtain a non-resident taxpayer code for EIT/VAT withholding. Step 4: Register in the VAT invoice management system and apply for e-invoice issuance quotas and digital invoice titles. Step 5: File foreign exchange receipts with SAFE through the designated settlement bank; no separate TIN is required for foreign exchange administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections for WFOEs. A WFOE is an entity-level CIT taxpayer on worldwide income; the only available VAT status election is small-scale taxpayer treatment for annual taxable turnover below RMB 5,000,000, which does not alter the entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for VAT registration of a WFOE; registration is triggered by entity formation and taxable business activity. General VAT taxpayer status is mandatory when annual taxable services turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer status may apply at a 3% simplified levy rate (with temporary relief rates when lawfully announced).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China may be zero-rated/refunded under the export-tax refund system only when the exporter files a valid Export Goods Customs Declaration, the goods physically depart from mainland China, title passes to the overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are received and reported in accordance with SAFE rules. For the WFOE's stevedoring services at marine docks, 0% VAT is not available because loading/unloading of bulk cargo is physically performed in mainland China and is subject to VAT at 6% as a logistics auxiliary service. Qualifying cross-border services wholly consumed outside China may be zero-rated only if the invoice recipient is overseas, the service is in the statutory zero-rated catalogue, and documentary evidence proves performance and consumption outside China.

  • Original Export Goods Customs Declaration (export tax rebate special copy)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or equivalent sea waybill evidencing outbound shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign exchange proceeds receipt confirmation issued by the settlement bank under SAFE monitoring
  • Export sales contract and any amendments
  • Bulk cargo terminal operational evidence, including draft survey report, weight certificate, stevedoring tally sheet, and terminal handling charge records
  • Special VAT invoice or VAT special payment book substantiating the domestic purchase of export goods where refund is claimed

The STA mandates issuance of all-digital electronic invoices (Su Dian Piao) through the national electronic invoice service platform, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the Golden Tax information system. Each invoice carries a unique invoice number and anti-counterfeit QR code; Special VAT Invoices require pre-approved quotas in the e-tax system and recipient verification through the national VAT invoice verification platform. The e-invoice platform exposes official interfaces/APIs for enterprise ERP reconciliation, and taxpayers must ensure that billing, clearing, and fiscal reporting are synchronized with the STA framework.

Zero-rating and export refund claims are subject to joint data cross-checks by the STA, General Administration of Customs, and SAFE. Failure to collect export proceeds within 180 days from customs declaration, or any mismatch among customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, invoices, and bills of lading, triggers clawback of the refund/zero-rating benefit with daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05%, and administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the evaded/refunded tax. Knowingly fraudulent export refund claims are punishable as criminal export-fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with imprisonment and fines; affected taxpayers are also downgraded in tax credit rating and suspended from facilitated export-refund processing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by SAFE under the People's Bank of China. For current account items, including port stevedoring service revenue, foreign exchange conversion is generally permissible once legal identity, contract authenticity, commercial invoices, and bank verification procedures are completed. Capital account items, however, remain tightly controlled: all foreign direct investment injection requires FDI registration and deposit into a designated capital account, and any conversion must strictly match the declared business purpose. Profit remittance by a wholly foreign-owned enterprise is treated as a current-account-like return on investment but is burdened by prior-year audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, bank compliance review, and SAFE post-verification checks. There is no statutory mandatory surrender of export proceeds; the WFOE may retain foreign currency earnings in an onshore foreign exchange account or settle them into RMB at prevailing bank rates, but the underlying transaction must be settled within the legally prescribed period. In practice, repatriation of service income must be supported by the port terminal operating license, stevedoring contracts, billing statements, and proof of actual service delivery. Any deviation triggers quarantine in the bank's post-verification system and potential SAFE administrative penalties. Convertibility is therefore real for genuine trade-related flows but not free for speculative capital movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current account administration, export-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days for goods export transactions, while service trade export income must be repatriated within 180 days following invoice issuance under the prevailing service trade filing framework. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency, or voluntarily settle into RMB at the bank's prevailing exchange rate. Longer settlement periods require documented contractual justification and SAFE registration.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability Rating: None for onshore regulatory purposes. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital banking platforms are not licensed by SAFE or the People's Bank of China to hold or clear RMB capital account transactions, FDI registration, tax payments, or statutory capital verification for a Chinese WFOE. Such EMIs may provide ancillary offshore receivables collection or CNH liquidity support, but they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for FX compliance, SAFE filing, capital account management, or trade payment authentication. Any proposal to route statutory capital or settlement flows through an EMI is legally non-viable and will not satisfy Chinese corporate banking onboarding requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared if related-party transactions in a single tax year exceed RMB 10 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 10 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 40 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (services) per China SAT Bulletin 16/2016; Master File is required if the ultimate parent group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the enterprise's related-party transactions exceed the local thresholds, or if the group's revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and is in an excluded industry such as financial services. Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) applies where the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue in the prior year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion (approximately EUR 750 million) and the group files CbC in a jurisdiction with a qualifying competent authority agreement.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as formulated in Chinese tax law are applied through the substance-over-form principle (Art. 47 EIT Law and the GAAR rules in SAT Announcements 2017/6 and 2018/13). For a stevedoring WFOE, core income-generating activities (CIGA) include: terminal operation management, cargo-handling coordination, stevedoring labor deployment, equipment allocation and maintenance decision-making, port safety compliance, and client negotiation. The WFOE must demonstrate its own operating premises (paid lease or ownership), qualified full-time employees with day-to-day management authority, actual decision-making functions in China, bearing of economic risks, and bank accounts. A WFOE that merely signs contracts on behalf of a foreign head office, passes stevedoring revenue to an offshore master company, or outsources all core activities to a related party without an arm's-length reward will be recharacterized under the GAAR, triggering an anti-abuse assessment, denial of treaty benefits, and imposition of penalties equal to 50% of the underpaid tax plus daily surcharges for up to 10 years under the special tax investigation regime.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

False or fraudulent corporate income tax filing / tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 25 of the Administrative Measures for Tax Collection: civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the statutory due date; revocation of operating license recommended by tax authority; public announcement of tax violations. Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law of PRC, tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 triggers criminal liability, with imprisonment of up to 7 years when the amount is merely large, or imprisonment of 3-7 years plus fine when the amount is enormous; unit (company) crimes lead to fines against the WFOE and direct criminal liability for the legal representative, financial officer, and any authorized director who participated in or approved the fraud.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law permits creditors and tax authorities to hold shareholders and directors personally liable if they abuse the corporate form to evade tax liability. The legal representative and director(s) who signed the false return face personal asset restitution for the unpaid tax, interest and penalties, a travel restriction/Departure Control notification (出境限制) issued by the court or tax authority, blacklisting in the tax dishonesty scheme (税收违法黑名单) which blocks public procurement, bank credit, and visa/immigration benefits, and criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion, and potential confiscation of assets. A director who knowingly instructs the preparation of false transfer pricing documents may be charged as a joint offender under Art. 25-31 of the Criminal Law, facing personal fines and custodial sentences.
Transfer pricing manipulation / thin capitalization / erosion through related-party financing

Statutory sanction: Articles 41-48 and 111-121 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement No. 6/2016 on Special Tax Adjustments: tax authority may make an 'economic substance' recharacterization and impose tax based on a deemed arm's-length margin (often using TNMM for terminal operating services); underpayment penalty of 50%-500% under Art. 63; interest on underpaid special tax adjustments is 0.05% per day plus a 5 percentage point annual add-on for serious cases; CbC non-compliance fines up to RMB 100,000 per report and master/local file fines up to RMB 10,000-50,000; subsequent related-party transaction adjustments may also be denied, leading to full CIT, VAT, and customs VAT liabilities.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and those directors with senior management authority who personally signed the false related-party disclosure or caused the underpayment are jointly and severally liable with the company under the piercing provisions of Company Law Art. 20 and Art. 21. They may face personal recovery of the tax underpayment and penalties, blacklisting on the tax authority's serious breach list, exit bans (blacklist), and criminal penalties under Art. 201 if the manipulation intentionally caused tax underpayment of more than RMB 50,000; if the loan is structured as a disguised dividend, the director who directed the sham financing may be liable for criminal fraud, with imprisonment of up to 7 years. The court can also freeze personal accounts and property when the WFOE is found to be the director's alter ego.
Shell company or sham economic substance for treaty abuse and tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 47 EIT Law (General Anti-Abuse Rule) and SAT Announcement No. 7/2017 (Determination of Place of Effective Management) and Announcement 9/2018 (new GAAR implementation): the tax authority may disregard the intermediary and reattribute income to the economic substance jurisdiction, resulting in full Chinese CIT at 25% on deemed China-source income, additional 10% withholding on recharacterized dividends, late payment surcharge of 0.05% daily, a penalty of 50%-500% under Art. 63, and denial of treaty benefits with retroactive revocation. China's anti-shell measures align with the OECD Multilateral Instrument (MLI) where the China-HK arrangement is subject to the PPT/LOB; a shell holding company can be stripped of DTA relief. The administrative fine for failing the substance declaration under the prevailing tax return schedules is RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000, plus possible deregistration and revocation of the port stevedoring license.

Director personal liability: Directors who set up or continue to operate the sham entity risk piercing of the corporate veil based on abuse of the corporate form under Company Law Art. 20. Personal civil liability includes reimbursement of all avoided taxes, fines, penalties and interest; the legal representative may be subject to a court-ordered travel ban and inclusion in the cooperative joint punishment list (联合惩戒黑名单), which denies high-speed rail, airline, and banking privileges; criminal liability under Art. 201 (tax evasion), and if the shell is used for money laundering of port chief-related proceeds, Art. 191 (money laundering) imposes imprisonment of up to 10 years plus monetary fines. In egregious cross-border cases, the director may be flagged internationally through INTERPOL red notices for fraud and extradition, given that the underpayment exceeds USD 1 million.
