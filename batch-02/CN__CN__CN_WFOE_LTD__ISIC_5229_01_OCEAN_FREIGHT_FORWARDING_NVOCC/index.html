Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) / China Tax Service electronic portal (Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn; coordinated with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for export/fx linkage.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, after incorporation the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) issues a business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the legal Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the entity. Within 30 calendar days from the business license date, the WFOE must complete tax registration on the STA Electronic Tax Bureau using the USCC and AMR record. The STA will activate the entity's period filing calendar, VAT taxpayer profile, invoice quota, e-invoice credentials, export-tax refund/customs status, and non-resident withholding obligations. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese WFOE or permanent establishment must register for income-tax-withholding purposes with the STA office of the place where the China-source income arises or where the withholding agent is situated; the non-resident is then assigned a separate non-resident Chinese Tax Identification Number after filing the constitutive documents, home-country tax certificate, board resolution, contract with the withholding agent, bank account confirmation, and a description of the China-source activity. This non-resident TIN is used for quarterly/incident withholding declarations and for any self-declared China-source taxes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax at 25% on its worldwide income; it is not a pass-through vehicle, and no free-zone exemption applies.

Available election forms

None: PRC law does not provide check-the-box pass-through elections, entity classification elections, or hybrid-mismatch elections for a WFOE limited-liability company. The taxpayer may only select applicable accounting/filing periods, VAT general versus small-scale taxpayer treatment, and APA/treaty tax protections, none of which change entity-level CIT status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration where annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5,000,000). There is no turnover threshold for normal tax registration: every WFOE must register with the STA within 30 days of issuance of the business license, and must register/self-confirm VAT taxpayer status before issuing VAT invoices or claiming input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available (1) for goods physically exported from China and cleared by GACC as exports, and (2) for qualifying cross-border services in the STA catalogue, including international sea carriage and directly ancillary international freight-forwarding/NVOCC services, provided by a VAT general taxpayer to a non-resident enterprise and consumed wholly outside China. For the NVOCC WFOE, the 0% VAT rate is substantiated by showing the recipient of the freight-forwarding service is an overseas shipper/consignee or overseas carrier, the service fee is payable in foreign currency from outside China and is ultimately repatriated, and the movement involves an export customs declaration and an ocean bill of lading from a Chinese port to an overseas port. Domestic pre-carriage, warehousing, customs clearance, and other services inside China remain taxable at the applicable domestic VAT rate unless separately listed in the zero-rated/exempt catalogue.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Bill of Entry (GACC-approved electronic export customs clearance record) showing HS codes, quantity, FOB value, exporter, and container/trailer identifiers.
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (B/L) or sea waybill issued by the ocean carrier or NVOCC showing the Chinese origin port, overseas destination port, cargo description, container numbers, and freight payable terms.
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice in foreign currency with Incoterms, overseas buyer details, and invoice value traceable to the bank receipt.
  • Packing list, certificate of origin (if requested), export inspection records, and any export-license/permit document required by Chinese export control rules.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Receipts Confirmation issued by a Chinese bank under SAFE rules, or the electronic bank transaction record showing the inbound SWIFT/ACH credit from the overseas customer matching the invoice and customs value.
  • SAFE trade-in-goods collection and monitoring record evidencing timely repatriation/collection of export proceeds and the matching business transaction number.
  • NVOCC service agreement, master ocean bill, container-transfer and cargo-tracking evidence, and the foreign-currency settlement voucher proving that the cross-border freight-forwarding fee was received from abroad and consumed outside China.
  • The VAT electronic export-tax-refund declaration and the STA's returned confirmation file where input VAT refund is claimed for the zero-rated supply.

China mandates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDI) regime integrated with the Golden Tax System Phase IV. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's official electronic invoice platform or an STA-certified third-party API and must carry a centrally allocated invoice number, a tax-control digital certificate/signature, and an encrypted anti-fraud stamp. Invoice data are transmitted to the STA in real time or on a near-real-time accepted-clearance basis; paper invoices, over-the-counter manual invoices, or unregistered printing devices cannot substantiate input VAT credits or zero-rated export treatment. The tax authority exchanges invoice data with GACC customs declarations and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts for triangulation audits.

The STA, GACC, and SAFE jointly perform export data triangulation: customs declarations, digital VAT invoices, bills of lading, bank foreign-exchange credits, and service contracts are matched automatically. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, or submission of false B/L/customs/fx evidence, causes the 0% rate to be reversed, input VAT refunds clawed back, and a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05% (or the statutory equivalent) running from the date the refund was paid or the tax should have been paid. Under the PRC Tax Administration Law, fraudulent export-tax-refund filings expose the entity to an administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the fraudulently obtained refund; criminal liability may arise under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund, including imprisonment and confiscation of assets. The entity is additionally placed on the STA serious-tax-dishonesty joint punishment list, downgraded in customs AEO/credit grading, restricted from export-tax-refund processing, and barred from favorable foreign-exchange management classification until the issue is rectified.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a current-account-convertible but capital-account-administered foreign exchange regime. All cross-border receipts and payments on current account, including freight forwarding service revenues, must be supported by authentic documentation and are generally freely convertible at market rates through authorized banks. Capital account transactions, such as direct investment capital contributions and inbound loans, require prior registration with SAFE and are subject to real-deal verification and payment-through supervision. The RMB is not freely convertible for capital account purposes; thus, a WFOE must open a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account for inbound equity funding and may only settle that capital into RMB with a specific declared payment purpose. Repatriation of profits is lawful after issuance of audited statutory financial statements, full corporate income tax (CIT) settlement, and submission of a board resolution for dividend distribution, but the bank will require a tax certificate showing that all withholding tax obligations are discharged. Current account convertibility also means that an NVOCC receiving USD, EUR, or other currencies from overseas customers can sell those funds to a commercial bank or retain them in a foreign currency current account; however, no automatic convertibility exists for speculative or investment-driven flows. Enterprises must integrate their operations with the full set of SAFE reporting systems and honor transactional documentary rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory conversion ratio is 0% (abolished by the 2012 SAFE foreign exchange administration reform); enterprises are free to retain export earnings in foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB. Statutory repatriation requires that all export proceeds be received into China through a designated bank within the contractual due date, and for goods exports, generally within 90 days from customs export declaration; service-trade proceeds (including NVOCC freight earnings) follow the contract terms and must be remitted within the contract-validity period, normally within one year. The receiving bank conducts a documentary audit via the National Foreign Exchange Verification System (for goods) and the Service Trade Foreign Exchange Information System (for services), and any delayed or abnormal recovery triggers a report to SAFE and may require an extension application.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Under PBOC/SAFE licensing rules, a fintech electronic money institution (EMI) or digital payment provider may not independently open a settlement account or process cross-border payments for a mainland WFOE unless it has been granted a dedicated cross-border RMB/foreign settlement license. Overseas EMIs (e.g., non-Chinese licensed electronic money issuers) generally do not meet the regulatory definition of a bank in China and are barred from servicing a WFOE's main operational currency flows. A fintech solution may only be used as a front-end interface to a licensed onshore bank or an approved cross-border payment institution (like a licensed third-party payment company with SAFE approval), and even then it cannot replace the mandated bank accounts for capital injection, tax payments, or profit remittance. Therefore, the WFOE should rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks for its primary liquidity rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The global parent should remit the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account with a stated purpose code for 'FDI paid-in capital' (the bank will map the correct trade code, e.g., 929 or the bank-specific equivalent). The remittance must reference the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate and the unique SAFE registration number; after credit, the receiving bank issues a capital settlement confirmation and a Chinese CPA performs a capital verification (expert audit) and issues a capital verification report. The capital funds may be converted into RMB for cash operating expenses only after a payment-purpose verification, i.e., the 'payment-through' rule, cannot be used for equity investments or non-commercial real estate purchases, and each RMB conversion must match a specific pending invoice or contract.Outbound settlement for NVOCC operations (line haul, terminal handling, trucking, pilotage, agency charges, bunkering, insurance) should be routed through SWIFT MT103 for immediate high-value payments, with the bank retained as an instructing customer. Under PBOC/SAFE licensing rules, a fintech electronic money institution (EMI) or digital payment provider may not independently open a settlement account or process cross-border payments for a mainland WFOE unless it has been granted a dedicated cross-border RMB/foreign settlement license. Overseas EMIs (e.g., non-Chinese licensed electronic money issuers) generally do not meet the regulatory definition of a bank in China and are barred from servicing a WFOE's main operational currency flows. A fintech solution may only be used as a front-end interface to a licensed onshore bank or an approved cross-border payment institution (like a licensed third-party payment company with SAFE approval), and even then it cannot replace the mandated bank accounts for capital injection, tax payments, or profit remittance. Therefore, the WFOE should rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks for its primary liquidity rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's Local File threshold triggers at annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceeding CNY 200 million; intangible-property, financial-asset, or service transactions exceeding CNY 100 million; and other related-party transactions exceeding CNY 40 million. Master File is required for a multinational group whose global annual related-party transaction volume exceeds CNY 1 billion or when the tax authority requires it due to high risk.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form through CIT Law Articles 41, 45 and 47, anti-avoidance rules, and the Place of Effective Management doctrine. Although China does not impose an ESR regime as named in offshore territories, a foreign-owned NVOCC must maintain real employees, offices, bank accounts, operating licences, and decision-making functions in China. The Core Income-Generating Activities of ocean freight forwarding—booking, load planning, carrier contracting, house bill of lading issuance, cargo tracking, claims handling, and revenue collection—must be performed by the Chinese WFOE and not by an offshore shell affiliate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes restitution plus a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid CIT; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes fines and imprisonment up to seven years for fraudulent concealment.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance director, and actual controller face personal civil recovery, asset seizure, travel bans, blacklisting by the tax court, and imprisonment as responsible persons under PRC Criminal Law.
False Cost Invoicing / False VAT Special Invoices

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 punishes false special VAT invoice issuance or use with fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment where the false amount is huge; Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 adds underpaid tax penalties.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil when the invoice transaction is a sham; the director is jointly and severally liable for tax debt, faces personal confiscation of assets, permanent exit blacklisting, loss of bank access, and criminal imprisonment as principal or accomplice.
Shell Company / AML and Foreign Exchange Breach

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and SAFE regulations permit fines from 5% to 30% of the illegal transaction amount plus confiscation; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money-laundering with fines and imprisonment up to ten years for serious cross-border proceeds.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly benefits from the shell structure loses corporate veil protection: personal assets are subject to restitution, the director is blacklisted from SAFE cross-border transactions and banking services, travel is restricted by court order, and criminal imprisonment up to ten years may be imposed.
