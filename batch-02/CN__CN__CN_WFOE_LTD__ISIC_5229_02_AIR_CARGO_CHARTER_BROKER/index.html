Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a WFOE under the PRC Company Law; the registered capital is subscribed in the Articles of Association and may be paid according to the schedule agreed by the shareholders. No blocked escrow account is mandated by Chinese law or SAFE. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the China International Trade Single Window for cross-border trade filing.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE formed in China (Mainland), tax registration begins with registration under the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR business license bears the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number/TIN for all Chinese tax purposes, including VAT. The STA receives the registration data automatically through the inter-agency sharing mechanism, and the taxpayer completes the 'new taxpayer onboarding package' in the Electronic Tax Bureau by conducting real-name authentication for the legal representative and finance officer, confirming the competent tax office, accounting basis, taxpayer category, banking account filing, and initial VAT status election. The foreign parent/ultimate beneficial owner must submit constitutional documents, board resolutions, appointment documents for the chairman/manager and legal representative, and evidence of the registered address in China. Where the applicant is a non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment but with China-source income or VAT-withholding obligations, no full WFOE-style tax registration is required; the domestic payer or withholding agent applies for a temporary non-resident tax registration / withholding tax identification code through the Electronic Tax Bureau and performs withholding filings. A non-resident enterprise that establishes a branch or permanent establishment in China must separately register that establishment with the local STA for a TIN within 30 days of the establishment's business registration.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None with respect to CIT entity classification: the WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and has no US-style 'check-the-box' or hybrid mismatch election. The only material election is VAT status: a taxpayer with annual taxable sales at or below CNY 5,000,000 may elect to be a small-scale taxpayer or, if it meets accounting and record-keeping conditions, may elect general VAT taxpayer status; a taxpayer exceeding CNY 5,000,000 is required to be a general VAT taxpayer. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For an international air cargo charter broker whose revenue is commissions and agency fees, the service is generally classified as cargo transportation agency/brokerage under China's VAT service taxonomy, not as transportation services. Such brokerage fees are not zero-rated; they may qualify for the cross-border VAT exemption only where the service is supplied to an overseas entity and is wholly consumed outside China, as defined under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement No. 29 of 2016. Zero-rating at 0% VAT applies to this WFOE only if it acts as the actual carrier or contracting carrier for international air transport of cargo, i.e., it operates or charters the aircraft with crew and invoices the customer for transportation, not for arrangement. In that case, the service must be physically rendered between a Chinese customs territory point and an overseas destination or entirely between overseas points, the recipient must be an overseas unit, the service must be wholly consumed overseas, and the WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer. The WFOE must retain contracts, invoices, airway bills, cargo manifests, proof of departure/arrival overseas, and bank evidence of receipt of foreign exchange; export goods services handled merely as freight forwarder are not zero-rated because title to the goods and customs declarations remain in the name of the actual exporter/consignor. Qualifying offshore digital services are not the material revenue stream for this charter broker, but any separate digital booking/platform service supplied to overseas customers and wholly consumed outside China must be evaluated separately for the statutory cross-border service zero-rating/exemption list.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (出口货物报关单) generated through the China International Trade Single Window, with an export customs clearance record confirming the goods left the People's Republic of China customs territory
  • Original Master Air Waybill (MAWB) or Bill of Lading issued by the actual carrier, named with the exporter/consignor and overseas consignee, plus House Air Waybill (HAWB) where consolidation is used
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export shipping order, and the charter/booking contract evidencing the cargo movement and the contractual role of the WFOE
  • Bank foreign-exchange collection voucher supported by the International Receipts and Payments Declaration (涉外收入申报单) from the settlement bank, which is the prevailing China statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), evidencing the overseas remittance of export proceeds
  • Electronic matching package submitted through the STA export tax rebate/zero-rating declaration system linking the VAT invoice, customs export declaration, airway bill, FX collection record, and service contract
  • For zero-rated international transport services only: proof of the international route, including airport/port identifiers on the MAWB/Bill of Lading and, where applicable, the foreign customs arrival endorsement or overseas delivery confirmation

VAT invoices in China must be issued through the STA's unified electronic invoice service platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV supervision framework. General VAT taxpayers are required to issue special VAT invoices or fully digitalized electronic invoices, and the complete invoice data is transmitted to the STA in near-real time at issuance. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are the standard for newly registered taxpayers and use STA-assigned dynamic verification and digital signatures instead of physical anti-counterfeit stamps. Any negative or corrective invoice ('red-letter invoice') requires prior approval or matching authorization in the platform. The electronic invoice data is cross-linked with VAT returns, customs export declarations, and banking/FX collection records, enabling automated risk analysis; any inconsistency in the invoice code, amount, tax rate, counterparty, or customs data may suppress the clearance status and trigger an audit.

The STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly monitor export zero-rating through Golden Tax Phase IV and the China International Trade Single Window. A zero-rated export claim that is unsupported by a matched customs declaration, valid airway bill, or verifiable FX receipt will be rejected, and any VAT refund or zero output tax already claimed is retroactively clawed back together with late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day calculated from the date the VAT became payable. Under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic on the Administration of Tax Levying and Tax Collection, underpayment of VAT through false documents or misclassification is treated as tax evasion, attracting fines of 50% to five times the evaded tax; under Article 66, fraudulent export VAT refunds may attract a fine of one to five times the refunded amount and confiscation of the refund. Where the amount is material, criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law may apply, including imprisonment for organizer/principal offenders. The raised assessment period is generally three years, extended to five years for special circumstances; there is no statute of limitation for cases involving fraudulent or deliberately concealed export refund claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate system with asymmetric convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in services and goods, are generally freely convertible once truthfulness and conformity checks are satisfied; funds must flow through designated banks and be supported by authentic commercial documents such as contracts, invoices, and customs declarations. Capital account transactions remain heavily restricted and require specific approval, registration, or quota assignment from SAFE. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in air cargo charter brokerage, initial registered capital injection is treated as Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) and relies on a SAFE registration and a dedicated FDI capital account. Repatriation of profits is permitted only after audit, tax settlement, and verification of after-tax earnings, with foreign exchange purchase for dividend remittance allowed upon submission of board resolutions, audited statements, and withholding tax certificates. Surrender requirements are no longer mandatory for export proceeds; enterprises may retain foreign currency earnings in designated accounts or settle into RMB. However, all receipts abroad must be repatriated onshore within a reasonable operational cycle and matched to customs or service-trade declarations. In practice, banks are vigilant against round-tripping, trade falsification, and channeling of current account flows to circumvent capital controls. Dividend remittance, royalty payments, and cross-border loans require extra filing or registration with SAFE. The practical consequence is that treasury teams must design liquidity plans with lead times for registration, tax clearance, and bank compliance approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under contemporary SAFE rules, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds. All foreign currency receipts from exports of goods and services must be repatriated to a domestic designated bank and subjected to electronic comparison with customs declarations or service-trade verification systems. The bank-confirmed 'normal collection period' is typically 90 days from the date of export declaration; any overdue receipt requires an additional reporting form to SAFE. Enterprises may freely choose to retain the funds in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert into RMB at the prevailing market rate. For an air cargo charter broker, service-invoice settlement is verified through the Service Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and payment timing should align with contractual milestones to avoid fictitious trade red flags.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as primary transaction accounts for a China-based WFOE. Under PBOC and SAFE regulations, only licensed commercial banks in China may open local-currency basic deposit accounts and foreign-exchange settlement accounts for resident legal entities. EMIs such as Airwallex, TransferWise/WorldFirst, or similar digital platforms may have partnered with Chinese banks or acquired payment licenses for inbound/outbound e-commerce collection, but they are strictly barred from servicing a Chinese entity as a main treasury account or FX clearing channel. Any use of an EMI to bypass SAFE-regulated banking rails would trigger non-compliance, potential fines, and forfeiture of customs/tax benefits. Therefore, the blueprint mandates all liquidity flows to reside within Tier-1 domestic commercial banks; EMIs can only be considered for ancillary offshore collections in non-CNY currencies, and only through legitimate cross-border cooperation agreements with licensed Chinese banks, subject to strict transaction monitoring and reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI for the WFOE must be routed through a designated Tier-1 commercial bank capital account. The parent company or shareholder will execute a MT103 or SWIFT wire transfer with beneficiary being the WFOE's FDI capital account. Prior to the transfer, the WFOE must obtain the Foreign Investment Information Report, obtain a SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Notice (FDI Registration), and open the capital account with the selected bank. After receipt, the bank verifies the payment with the SAFE system and issues a remittance notification. The injection amount must not exceed the registered capital and must be paid in the required currency. Following receipt, the WFOE must engage a Chinese registered accounting firm to issue a capital verification report verifying that funds have been credited and that the amount aligns with the approved business purpose. For amounts exceeding USD 50,000 or equivalent, the bank will require supporting documents from the board resolution and the parent company's financial statements. Foreign exchange purchased through the capital account for RMB conversion is permitted but subject to the positive-list approach of non-trade transactions; utilization for fixed-asset purchases, salary payments, and operational expenses requires submission of verified invoices and contracts.Outbound payments for air cargo charter broker operations should be cleared through the WFOE's basic settlement account and foreign exchange business account. For straightforward operating payments, use MT103 SWIFT wire transfers with proper trade purpose codes, such as 'Transportation and Travel' for air freight or 'Management and Consulting Service' for advisory fees. For large charter lease and maintenance payments, documentary letters of credit (L/C) can be issued by the bank in favor of the overseas aircraft operator or service provider; payment under L/C occurs against presentation of clean transport documents, commercial invoices, and insurance policies. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are suitable for low-risk, well-documented periodic service fees, with the exporter's bank forwarding the documents to the WFOE's bank for acceptance or payment. All outbound trade disbursements require the submission of an International Commercial Invoice, a service agreement, proof of service delivery (cargo manifests, aircraft usage logs), and, where withholding tax applies, a tax withholding certificate or clearance letter from the Chinese tax authority. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs are not viable as primary transaction accounts for a China-based WFOE. Under PBOC and SAFE regulations, only licensed commercial banks in China may open local-currency basic deposit accounts and foreign-exchange settlement accounts for resident legal entities. EMIs such as Airwallex, TransferWise/WorldFirst, or similar digital platforms may have partnered with Chinese banks or acquired payment licenses for inbound/outbound e-commerce collection, but they are strictly barred from servicing a Chinese entity as a main treasury account or FX clearing channel. Any use of an EMI to bypass SAFE-regulated banking rails would trigger non-compliance, potential fines, and forfeiture of customs/tax benefits. Therefore, the blueprint mandates all liquidity flows to reside within Tier-1 domestic commercial banks; EMIs can only be considered for ancillary offshore collections in non-CNY currencies, and only through legitimate cross-border cooperation agreements with licensed Chinese banks, subject to strict transaction monitoring and reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PRC transfer-pricing contemporaneous documentation (Local File) is required when any single related-party transaction category of the fiscal year exceeds: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial transactions; or RMB 100 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is triggered when aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or the enterprise is part of a multinational group required to prepare a group master file.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations statutory code, but the substance doctrine operates through the beneficial ownership tests in SAT Bulletin 2009 No. 2 and SAT Bulletin 2012 No. 30 and through GAAR under Article 47 of the EIT Law. An air cargo charter broker WFOE must demonstrate actual personnel, management, premises, decision-making, contract execution and risk-bearing in China as its Core Income-Generating Activities; a shell booking company with title alone will be denied treaty benefits, recharacterized as receiving Chinese-source income and assessed at 10% WHT with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; tax authority collects underpaid tax, daily late surcharge of 0.05% and civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. If the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, Criminal Law Article 201 triggers fines and imprisonment; aggravated cases with huge amounts and flagrant fraud carry 3 to 7 years' imprisonment. Fraudulent VAT false invoice use can separately trigger Customs/exchange? In addition, Article 205 of the Criminal Law imposes penalties for false invoice issuance.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative and the financially responsible officer are subject to piercing of the corporate veil under PRC Company Law and SAT collection measures: personal civil liability for restitution of unpaid tax and company losses; immediate travel bans and exit restrictions under Article 48 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; inclusion on the State Taxation Administration Serious Dishonesty Blacklist causing visa revocation, denial of future corporate appointments, seizure of personal property, and imprisonment up to 7 years upon criminal conviction.
Shell Corporation / No Economic Substance / Treaty Benefit Shopping

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the EIT Law grants the tax authority the right to apply GAAR, recharacterize the transaction and disregard the intermediate entity; SAT Bulletin 2012 No. 30's beneficial ownership test will deny treaty relief; tax is reassessed at the 10% statutory dividend/interest WHT, VAT deficiencies are recalculated, and underpayment penalties under Article 63 plus daily surcharges apply. If the fraudulent TRC is knowingly obtained, the act may constitute fraudulent tax reduction or evasion under the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign treaty declarations, approve the shell structure and cause the tax loss are treated as active participants in tax avoidance; they may be personally liable to restore unpaid WHT and VAT, face a life-long ban on acting as a company director or senior manager if fraud is proven, be placed on the China Exit Ban and Serious Dishonesty Blacklist, and may face criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years for the false certificate route or 3 to 7 years in aggravated evasion cases.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Deficiency and Failure to Withhold on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Article 44 of the EIT Law and STA Bulletin 2016 No. 42 allow the tax authority to apply deemed income or adjust margins using CUP, TNMM, cost-plus or profit-split; the underpaying taxpayer is charged transfer-pricing interest from the adjustment year plus daily late-payment surcharges. Failure to withhold on non-resident payments violates Article 37 of the EIT Law and Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, leading to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld WHT/VAT amount and full recovery of tax from the WFOE as the liable withholding agent.

Director personal liability: The finance director and legal representative who approved and executed unreported intercompany payment transactions are personally liable for civil restitution of the uncollected withholding tax and penalties; the STA may impose an exit restriction until the tax is settled; chronic noncompliance results in blacklisting, disqualification of the directors as 'responsible persons' under the serious-dishonesty list, and criminal liability for evasion of tax obligations under Article 201 if the aggregate unwithheld tax exceeds the criminal threshold, with imprisonment of up to 7 years for persistent and large-scale conduct.
