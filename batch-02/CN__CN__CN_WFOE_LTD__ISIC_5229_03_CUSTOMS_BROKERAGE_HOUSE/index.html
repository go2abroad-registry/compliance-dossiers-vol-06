Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. The PRC Company Law (2023) abolished any minimum registered capital for ordinary limited liability companies. The WFOE may subscribe any amount of registered capital, but all subscribed capital must be fully paid-in within five years from issuance of the business license. If the customs brokerage engages in bonded transit guarantee operations, the customs authority may require a bond or guarantee amount based on the value of the customs duties, but no minimum paid-up capital of the company is prescribed. The incorporators must execute a capital contribution schedule and deposit the initial contribution to an escrow-like account only to the extent required by the customs authority for the customs broker bond.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Inspection Acceptance Certificate ('Fire Check Acceptance Form') from the local Fire and Rescue Brigade for the leased office, plus a building structural safety certificate or owner's warranty for the floor loading if any racking or bonded storage is installed; occupational workplace safety clearance from the local Emergency Management Bureau, requiring an emergency evacuation plan, fire extinguisher installations, staff first-aid and fire-fighting training records, and a written occupational health management system.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); local competent tax office in the city/district of registration; online filing portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) accessible via the STA main portal www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is first registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation and receives an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code, which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN); no separate VAT identification number is issued. The WFOE must complete tax registration confirmation on the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of the business license date, supplementing the registration with the legal representative, financial officer, tax agent, registered and operating address, bank account details, accounting standards, and chosen VAT taxpayer status. The STA then activates the tax account. A foreign non-resident shareholder is not automatically registered for Chinese tax; if the non-resident has China-source income or a permanent establishment, it must complete non-resident enterprise taxpayer registration on the same e-Tax platform and obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification before performing any tax filings or withholding certificates.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch elections. A WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Voluntary VAT general taxpayer registration is available for small-scale taxpayers but is only an indirect tax status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic VAT registration has no economic turnover threshold; all Chinese enterprises, including a WFOE, must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining the business license. Compulsory registration as a VAT general taxpayer applies when annual VAT taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below that, small-scale taxpayer status applies. Customs brokerage and logistics auxiliary services are taxed under the 6% VAT service category, while the 13% headline rate applies to goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, the 0% export VAT rate/export rebate is available where the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported, the customs declaration is authenticated by China Customs, and export proceeds are repatriated within the required foreign-exchange period. The rebate is applied under the exemption-credit-refund method for manufacturing/exporting producers or the exemption-refund method for trading exporters. For taxable services, zero-rating/exemption applies only to qualifying cross-border services supplied to a non-resident recipient and wholly consumed outside China; customs declaration and brokerage services physically performed at Chinese ports do not by themselves qualify. Bonded transit and customs-supervised zone services may be treated under special regime rules and should be confirmed through a written determination.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration generated through the China International Trade Single Window and released by China Customs
  • Export contract or sales order and packing list
  • Commercial invoice and, for trading exporters, the VAT Special Invoice for the purchased goods
  • Transport document such as Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Post Receipt evidencing goods leaving Chinese customs territory
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher recorded in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System evidencing the inward remittance of export proceeds

China requires all VAT invoices to be issued through the STA-controlled Electronic Invoice Service Platform using the fully digitalized electronic invoice mechanism or authorized tax-control equipment. Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA Golden Tax system, and each invoice carries a statutorily generated 20-digit invoice number and anti-counterfeit QR code. Taxpayers perform invoice receipt, verification, and reconciliation in the Tax Digital Account. Customs electronic export data is automatically connected to the tax system; no separate fiscal stamping is required before issuance.

The STA cross-checks export zero-rating against China Customs release data and SAFE foreign-exchange collection data. If documentation, export, or repatriation is incomplete or false, the STA may claw back refunded VAT, void the zero-rating, assess VAT at the applicable rate, impose late-payment surcharge, and impose administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Levy and Collection of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export refund claims may be prosecuted as the crime of export tax refund fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and SAFE may separately penalize failure to repatriate export proceeds or falsification of foreign-exchange receipt records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated current-account and capital-account convertibility framework. Current-account transactions, including trade-related payments and dividends that have been properly verified with supporting documents, are generally classified as convertible, subject to documentary review and reporting by SAFE and its designated foreign exchange banks. Capital-account transactions, by contrast, require prior SAFE registration or approval: inbound foreign direct investment through a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must be registered under the SAFE FDI rules, the capital contribution must be paid into a foreign-currency capital account, and any subsequent conversion into RMB must occur in a transaction-specific manner with the proceeds recorded in a use ledger. Profit and dividend remittances are treated as current-account transfers but are not automatic: the WFOE must present audited financial statements, a board resolution approving the distribution, a tax withholding certificate or tax clearance certificate, and proof of no outstanding foreign-exchange control violations. In practice, banks require the remittance to be made net of withholding taxes and will reject payment orders if the supporting audit and tax documentation is stale or inconsistent. Export proceeds enjoy broadly convertible current-account treatment, but the same flows remain subject to trade verification, invoice matching, and monitoring ratios set by SAFE to prevent disguised capital flows and trade-based money laundering. The WFOE must therefore maintain a robust treasury and compliance framework to navigate periodic macro-prudential adjustments to capital-flow management operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported via the SAFE cross-border receipt confirmation system. For standard exports, the exporter must ensure that payment confirmation occurs within the prescribed 90-day trade collection period from the date of exportation, extendable to 180 days for certain qualifying high-technology and advanced-manufacturing goods. There is no universal mandatory conversion ratio applicable to all export proceeds; the WFOE may retain foreign currency within its SAFE-approved current-account foreign exchange quota, and any balance above the authorised retention quota must be settled into RMB promptly at the prevailing onshore spot rate. One hundred percent of proceeds must be evidenced, accounted for, and matched to underlying customs declarations; unreconciled overdue receipts will trigger a compliance investigation, downgrade of trade facilitation status, and possible suspension of the customs duty payment and collection interface.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic-money institutions and digital EMI platforms have low standalone viability in Mainland China. They are not licensed to accept deposits or provide local clearing accounts as primary settlement rails for a WFOE under PRC banking supervision and foreign exchange control law. Such EMIs may operate only as technology or customer-acquisition partners to onshore Tier-1 banks, and they are legally barred from acting as the formal banking channel for capital injections, customs duty payments, or export receipt collection. Any cross-border disbursement initiated by an offshore EMI without an onshore licensed partner bank would be rejected by SAFE as an unregistered payment flow. The WFOE should therefore rely on traditional Chinese commercial banks for settlement and clearing, and may use global EMI platforms solely for utility-type expense payments outside China, subject to bank approval and a clear audit trail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas shareholder to the WFOE's foreign-currency capital account opened at the designated onshore bank. The remittance instruction must align with the SAFE FDI registration certificate and include the standard foreign-exchange payment purpose code for inward direct investment in China. Upon receipt, the onshore bank completes the cross-border banking statistics and payment confirmation, after which the WFOE may convert the capital funds into RMB in compliance with the transaction-realisation principle and transfer into a separate RMB capital funds expenditure account. A qualified Chinese certified public accountant must perform capital verification and issue a capital verification report before the funds can be used for customs brokerage operations, bonded deposits, customs duty advances or general working capital; any use outside the SAFE-approved negative-list scope is prohibited.For effective trade settlements, the WFOE should operate through the following rails: direct SWIFT MT103 payments to overseas customs authorities, bondsmen, carriers and licensees for non-documentary operational costs; commercial letters of credit (L/C) issued by the onshore bank for high-value logistics or brokerage services where the counterparty demands documentary security; documentary collections in the form of documents against payment (D/P) and documents against acceptance (D/A) for low-risk recurring transactions; and the China Customs Single Window system connected to the bank-enterprise tripartite direct debit interface for the payment of import duties, VAT and other customs charges. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: All Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprises in China are statutorily required to undergo an annual independent audit of their financial statements by a licensed Chinese Certified Public Accountant (CPA) firm, regardless of revenue, asset, or headcount size. The audit is mandated under the Accounting Law, the Company Law, and the Foreign Investment Law. Enterprises subject to compulsory annual audit include all limited liability companies with foreign investment, customs brokerage license holders, and any entity meeting any of the following: total assets exceeding RMB 50 million; annual turnover exceeding RMB 30 million; or headcount exceeding 300 employees. Additionally, customs brokerage houses with an annual volume exceeding 10,000 customs declaration entries or those applying for AEO (Authorized Economic Operator) certification are independently audited for customs compliance. Audit and reporting must be prepared in accordance with the Chinese Accounting Standards (ASBE / CAS) which are substantially converged with IFRS; enterprises with listed shares or cross-border financing must apply IFRS as endorsed by the Ministry of Finance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 400 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 200 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transactions, or RMB 100 million for other related-party transactions; Master File mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report required for a multinational group with consolidated annual revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more; all documentation must be submitted within the statutory form-filling window and contemporaneously prepared.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and the OECD-aligned Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) doctrine through EIT Law Article 47, Special Tax Adjustment Rules and treaty anti-abuse provisions. A customs brokerage WFOE must prove that it performs the functions, owns the assets and bears the risks of customs clearance and bonded transit management using its own licensed staff, physical premises, decision-making and Chinese bank accounts; shell companies with no real personnel or business activity are denied treaty benefits, profits are recharacterised as China-source service income or dividends, and the general anti-avoidance rule enables tax authority adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion and False VAT Input Claims

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 orders recovery of underpaid tax plus a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-declared tax; daily late payment surcharge is 0.05%; false customs declarations are separately penalised under Customs Law sections on smuggling/false declaration; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes fines and imprisonment up to seven years when unpaid tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents more than 10% of tax payable.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 where directors or shareholders abuse limited liability, commingle personal and corporate funds, or transfer assets fraudulently; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid CIT, VAT, customs duties and penalties; travel bans, high-consumption restrictions and blacklisting under the joint punishment system apply; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment from three to seven years and disqualification from serving as director.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 requires arm's-length pricing and Article 48 authorises recharacterisation with a 10-year look-back period; Special Tax Adjustment Rules impose late interest and documentation penalties; unpaid tax can be recovered with a 50% to 500% fine under Tax Administration Law Article 63; recharacterised excess payments may be taxed as deemed dividends with 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign sham related-party transactions and defeat tax collection are personally liable for the adjusted tax, penalties and interest if the corporate form is used as a mere instrumentality; personal asset attachment and civil restitution orders may issue; tax authority blacklisting restricts cross-border travel; willful evasion can trigger Criminal Law Article 201 liability and imprisonment.
Abusive DTA Relief, Shell Company, and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: DTA relief is denied under SAT Announcement 2015 No. 60 and Announcement 2018 No. 9 with full 10% WHT recaptured; automatic tax adjustments, late interest and a 50% to 500% false-declaration fine apply; AML Law violations subject the institution and responsible officers to monetary penalties; Criminal Law Article 191 on money laundering imposes imprisonment and confiscation of property; the customs brokerage licence may be revoked under customs administration rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or implement abusive treaty, shell-company and money-laundering structures are subject to civil restitution, joint and several liability for underwithheld tax, travel bans, court-ordered asset disgorgement, personal fines, and imprisonment from five to ten years in serious money-laundering cases; the corporate veil is pierced when the WFOE is an alter ego or sham device, exposing the director's personal assets.
