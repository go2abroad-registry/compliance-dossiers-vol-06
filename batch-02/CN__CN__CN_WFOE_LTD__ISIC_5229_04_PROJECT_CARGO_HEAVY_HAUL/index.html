Virtual office and commercial mailbox arrangements are impermissible for a WFOE engaged in OOG or heavy-haul project cargo logistics. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through provincial and municipal Tax Service Bureaus and the unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (state-level portal: etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE): first obtain the Chinese Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) as the legal entity identifier from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) business registration; then complete tax registration with the STA within 30 days of issuance of the business license through the Electronic Tax Service Platform. The WFOE must file the standard tax registration dataset, including the approved business license, articles of association, lease or ownership proof of the registered office, legal representative identity documents, capitalization and financial contribution schedule, bank account opening certificate for the basic deposit account, and, where applicable, foreign investment negative-list screening results. The USCC functions as the taxpayer identification number; the STA separately assigns a VAT registration detail code. A non-resident foreign enterprise without a Chinese business establishment may be issued a non-resident tax registration ID for withholding tax administration; however, the CN_WFOE_LTD is a Chinese resident enterprise and is registered on the national taxpayer file directly.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box entity classification elections for domestic limited liability companies. A WFOE is automatically a Chinese tax resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) on worldwide income. The only classification election relevant to this entity is the VAT general taxpayer versus small-scale taxpayer status election; no hybrid-mismatch check-the-box election is available under Chinese domestic law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT from entity creation; the mandatory general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable turnover in excess of RMB 5,000,000 within any continuous 12-month period. A taxpayer below the threshold may elect small-scale VAT status, under which the simplified VAT rate is generally 3% (with 5% applicable to certain property leasing and real estate transactions). Once the RMB 5,000,000 threshold is exceeded, small-scale status is lost and the entity must operate as a general taxpayer; logistics auxiliary and freight-forwarding services are then taxable at the 6% modern-services rate, while transport services supplied as actual carrier are taxable at 9% and sales of tangible goods at 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods exported from China, the vendor is eligible for VAT zero-rating and the export tax refund mechanism (exemption, credit, and refund) when the goods are actually shipped out of China through a customs-supervised port and the export customs declaration is completed by the export seller. For cross-border services, the STA applies zero-rating to qualifying international transportation services supplied by the carrier; logistics auxiliary services such as freight forwarding, warehousing, cargo handling, and out-of-gauge heavy-haul project logistics are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated when the service is supplied to an overseas enterprise or to an international transportation operation and the consideration is received through the foreign-exchange and customs control environment. The CN_WFOE_LTD must substantiate that the underlying project cargo was physically exported from China, that the logistics service was contractually ordered by a non-resident shipping principal or consignee, and that no taxable materialization of the service remained inside China's VAT jurisdiction.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (electronic customs export declaration with the 19-digit customs declaration reference number)
  • Bill of Lading or Sea Waybill (for ocean heavy-haul project cargo) / Master Air Waybill or international consignment note (for air or cross-border road transport)
  • Packing List and out-of-gauge heavy-lift cargo manifest with container, unit dimensions, and weight certificates
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the overseas buyer, consignee, or non-resident shipping principal

  • Bank settlement voucher or letter-of-credit advice evidencing the receipt of export proceeds

China has fully implemented the fully digitalized electronic invoice (Digitalized Special VAT Invoice) regime through the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform. All VAT invoices must be issued via the official e-invoice system and must carry the STA-assigned unique invoice number and cryptographic anti-fraud check code; no invoice is valid for input VAT deduction unless it has passed through this mandatory fiscal clearance. The Golden Tax IV digital tax control system performs real-time transmission, reconciliation, and anti-fraud verification of invoice data against customs declarations, bank foreign-exchange records, and enterprise reporting data. Business ERP or billing systems must integrate through STA-approved invoicing APIs; failure to issue through the approved e-invoice pipeline can invalidate deduction rights and attract administrative penalties.

The STA, the General Administration of Customs, and the SAFE maintain a joint data intelligence framework that cross-checks customs export declarations, VAT invoice issuance, logistics documentation, and foreign-exchange receipts. Where export zero-rating or VAT exemption is disallowed on audit, the STA claws back the VAT exemption, refund, or credit amount, imposes late-payment interest, and may assess a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying where false declaration or tax evasion is established. False export declarations also trigger penalties under the Customs Law, ranging from 50% to 300% of the customs value, suspension of import/export registration, and cancellation of export-tax-rebate status. Criminal liability is available under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, with imprisonment and fines available against the WFOE and its responsible management.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB fully convertible on the current account (trade in goods/services, dividends, and wages) under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, while the capital account remains heavily regulated and dependent on SAFE approvals or filings. The Foreign Investment Law and the 2020 Regulations on Foreign Exchange Control (State Council Decree No. 532) guarantee that profits and dividends of WFOEs can be remitted offshore after tax audits and statutory audit certificates, subject to bank verification of prior-year audited financials and tax payment proofs. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment down payments, intercompany loans, and returns on equity, require registration with SAFE through the bank-based Foreign Exchange Registration System (FDI system) and face a negative-list screening. Surrender requirements for export proceeds have been abolished; exporters may hold foreign currency in domestic or offshore accounts, but every receipt must be reported under the International Balance of Payments (BOP) statistics with genuine trade documentation. Renminbi can be used for cross-border settlement under the CIPS mechanism, reducing direct FX exposure for certain transactions, but a substantial portion of project cargo contracts remains USD-denominated, necessitating a comprehensive treasury hedging plan.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the People's Bank of China/SAFE 2020 Guidance on Foreign Exchange Operations for Current Account Items, there is no statutory mandatory surrender ratio or timeline for export foreign currency receipts. Enterprises are permitted to retain 100% of export proceeds abroad or in domestic designated foreign exchange settlement accounts. However, all inward receipts must be declared to the BOP through the bank within 30 days of collection (via the Merchant Foreign Exchange Payment and Receipt Declaration system). The bank will conduct 'genuine transaction' verification against the customs declaration, contract, invoice, and logistics documents. If the export proceeds are not repatriated within a reasonable business cycle, SAFE may require an explanation and could add the entity to its monitoring list. For imports, payment in foreign currency requires a corresponding import customs declaration or other trade documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), which exercises delegated FX administration powers

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary acquiring/payment rail for a mainland WFOE. Under the PRC Payment and Settlement Regulations, non-bank financial institutions must obtain a payment license from PBOC to provide online payment services; as of the current regulatory landscape, offshore digital EMI institutions (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Airwallex) are not licensed to open or maintain domestic foreign currency accounts for Chinese resident entities. They can only serve as ancillary remittance aggregators in some cross-border e-commerce pilots, but the WFOE's capital account controls, FX settlement, and profit repatriation must be executed through domestically licensed banks. Any attempt to route investment proceeds or trade payments through unlicensed EMIs would violate SAFE circulars on the 'capability of cross-border receiving and payment' and risk account freezing, fines, and adverse compliance findings. Therefore, the blueprint should integrate with domestic Tier-1 banks for FX and clearing, while using reputable global cash management banks for offshore salary/benefit payments if needed.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign investors make capital contributions by telegraphic transfer (SWIFT MT103) from the offshore shareholder account to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account (FDI capital inflow) established at a SAFE-authorized bank. The bank must have received the FDI registration and a unique 'FDI-related business registration code' from SAFE prior to the receipt. The MT103 supplementary details should include the purpose code (e.g., 'investment registered capital' – PAYMENT OF SHARE CAPITAL) and the WFOE's Unified Social Credit Code. After arrival, the bank performs a transaction authenticity check, issues a capital account receipt confirmation, and the company files an FDI reinvestment/transaction report with SAFE. Under the settlement process, capital can be settled into RMB ('pay-as-you-go') based on the actual use under the Negative List; each RMB settlement must be backed by invoices/contracts with the counterparty.For outbound payments related to project cargo logistics, the primary clearing rails are (i) SWIFT MT103 for milestone-based supplier and subcontractor payments in USD/EUR, requiring the service contract, commercial invoice, estimated BAF/CAF, and the bank's verification of the underlying 'real demand' to avoid 'orphan' flows; (ii) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for heavy freight equipment procurement or when dealing with new suppliers who demand a bank undertaking; the WFOE may request an L/C from its CNY bank under the current account negative list or use a USD-backed L/C; (iii) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-risk trade settlements with established vendors. To reduce FX conversion friction and comply with SAFE, all RMB-denominated cross-border outbound payments can be routed via CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System), which enables same-day settlement and is increasingly accepted by Chinese logistics suppliers. Each transaction must be recorded with the correct BOP code (e.g., 222040 for freight transportation).Long-dated FX forward contracts (up to 12 months) for export/import payment hedging, subject to real-demand verification.Cross-currency swaps for matched funding or trade-related cash flow swaps, approved by the bank's risk committee.Bought FX options (call/put) to hedge contingent USD/CNY movements on bid bonds or milestone collections.Cost-reduction structures such as collars or participating forwards are permitted only when the underlying trade flow is documented and the bank's compliance desk confirms no speculative intent.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary acquiring/payment rail for a mainland WFOE. Under the PRC Payment and Settlement Regulations, non-bank financial institutions must obtain a payment license from PBOC to provide online payment services; as of the current regulatory landscape, offshore digital EMI institutions (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Airwallex) are not licensed to open or maintain domestic foreign currency accounts for Chinese resident entities. They can only serve as ancillary remittance aggregators in some cross-border e-commerce pilots, but the WFOE's capital account controls, FX settlement, and profit repatriation must be executed through domestically licensed banks. Any attempt to route investment proceeds or trade payments through unlicensed EMIs would violate SAFE circulars on the 'capability of cross-border receiving and payment' and risk account freezing, fines, and adverse compliance findings. Therefore, the blueprint should integrate with domestic Tier-1 banks for FX and clearing, while using reputable global cash management banks for offshore salary/benefit payments if needed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible-goods transactions (such as the sale/purchase of heavy-haul equipment and project cargo materials), or RMB 40 million for service transactions (freight, haulage, subcontracting, management), royalties, and interest under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42; if neither threshold is crossed, a full Local File is optional but a Chinese CFC reporting form may still be required for related-party transactions exceeding RMB 25 million.

Economic substance scope: While Mainland China has not enacted common-law Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), Article 47 of the EIT Law and Article 46 of the TCAL import the substance-over-form doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR). The WFOE must demonstrate substantive decision-making, physical presence, employees, and revenue-generating assets in China in order to allocate logistics profit to the Chinese entity; a cross-border contract that merely routes legal title through a shell with no local management, staff, or assets will be re-characterised, causing adjustments to income and the denial of treaty benefits. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of an OOG project cargo carrier must include dispatch, route planning, procurements and contract negotiation performed in China, and the WFOE should keep a contemporaneous functional-analysis file to support the allocated return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Penalty equal to 50%-500% of the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, plus a daily 0.05% late payment surcharge and a Criminal Law Article 201 offence if the evaded amount is above RMB 50,000 and over 10% of total tax payable.

Director personal liability: The tax authority may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, making directors jointly and severally liable to deliver assets to satisfy the tax arrears; the court may impose civil restitution of the evaded amount, freeze personal bank accounts, impose a travel ban (blacklisting limiting ability to leave China), and the responsible legal representative or CFO may face imprisonment for up to seven years, depending on the amount and aggravating circumstances.
Shell Company / Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the EIT Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 64, tax authorities can re-characterise the arrangement and impose CIT on income at the full 25%, require the withholding of 10% WHT on the deemed dividend, and assess a penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; the WFOE shall not receive crediting for any offshore tax in such case.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed the false substance declaration face personal civil liability to China's tax revenue, unlimited joint liability under the veil-piercing doctrine for the enterprise's tax debts, travel restrictions, and potential criminal liability for tax evasion under Article 201; if the shell is used for money laundering alongside the avoidance, the money-laundering offence under Article 191 of the Criminal Law could render the director liable to a sentence of 5-10 years in prison and seizure of personally held assets.
False VAT Invoicing and Anti-Money Laundering (AML)

Statutory sanction: The issuance of a false VAT invoice constitutes a criminal offence under Article 205 of the Criminal Law; penalties range from fines of 0.5-5 times the false-invoice amount, confiscation of property, and imprisonment for up to life imprisonment where the amount is extraordinarily large; additionally, the tax authority will refuse the deduction and impose a VAT penalty of 50%-500% under Article 63 of the TCAL.

Director personal liability: A director that planned or endorsed the false-invoicing scheme is a co-principal offender, personally liable for the unpaid VAT and CIT plus fines; the court may strip the director's personal assets to compensate the tax administration, impose a travel ban until the penalty is paid, and imprison the director for 3-10 years (life in worst cases) for the tax fraud; any laundering of the resulting balance through cross-border transfers would also trigger Anti-Money-Laundering liability under Article 191, with the director subject to criminal confiscation and loss of director disqualification rights.
