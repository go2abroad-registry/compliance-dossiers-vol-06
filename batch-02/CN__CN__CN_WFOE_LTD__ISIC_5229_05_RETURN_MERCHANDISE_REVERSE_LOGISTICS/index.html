Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company in the reverse-logistics sector. The registered capital and contribution schedule are fixed in the Articles of Association. Under the Company Law, shareholders must fully pay their subscribed capital within five years of establishment unless a shorter period is specified. No blocked escrow account is required by law, but foreign funding must be remitted through the Company's designated foreign exchange capital account and recorded through the bank under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) information-reporting framework.

Funding & unlock path: The physical warehouse and grading center must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Section after successful fire-protection design review and completion inspection under the Fire Protection Law. Structural load inspections for racking, mezzanines, and high-density storage equipment must be certified by a licensed structural engineer and filed with the building authority. The operator must register under the Work Safety Law with the local Emergency Management Bureau, appoint a full-time or part-time work-safety officer depending on staff count, establish a workplace safety management system, provide protective equipment, and file an emergency response plan. If the facility includes underground spaces in an urban area, the local people's air defense office may require civil-defense approval or contribution in lieu of construction. No separate civil-defense permit is generally required for an above-ground general warehouse unless local urban regulations impose a collective civil-defense obligation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing via the National Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. The WFOE must complete tax registration with its in-charge local STA via the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal or in-person at the competent tax service hall within 30 days after the Business License is issued, filing the Business License, Articles of Association, legal representative identity proof, registered address document and bank account information. A non-resident foreign enterprise that derives China-source income without a permanent establishment or registered branch must file non-resident enterprise tax registration with the in-charge tax authority of the withholding agent or the enterprise's place of business to obtain a non-resident tax identification number; once obtained, the same number is used for corporate income tax and indirect tax filings.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not operate U.S.-style check-the-box or elective pass-through treatment for WFOEs; hybrid mismatch outcomes are countered by the Enterprise Income Tax Law general anti-abuse rule (Article 47), controlled foreign corporation rules (Article 45) and substance-over-form doctrine. For VAT, an entity may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status if its annual taxable turnover is below the mandatory threshold, but this election does not alter corporate income tax entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All business entities must complete VAT registration simultaneously with tax registration upon establishment. Mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. For the target reverse-logistics/reclassification services, the applicable rates are 6% for logistics auxiliary services, 9% for domestic cargo transport, 13% for sale of returned goods and 0% for qualifying exports of goods and cross-border services; small-scale VAT taxpayers may use the simplified levy rate method below RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China and services expressly designated as cross-border zero-rated services in the Ministry of Finance/STA cross-border taxable service catalog can be invoiced at 0% VAT. The exporter must be a VAT general taxpayer, the transaction must be genuine and the exported goods must have completed customs exit; for cross-border services, the recipient must be a non-resident and the service must be rendered wholly outside China. Returned merchandise that is re-exported after reverse-logistics grading or repackaging must be declared to customs as an export and supported by transport evidence to sustain 0% VAT.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration bearing customs release/exit confirmation
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export sales or service contract with the non-resident counterparty
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank/State Administration of Foreign Exchange records evidencing receipt of the offshore sales proceeds
  • VAT special invoice or export invoice, and the VAT export-refund declaration submitted through the VAT export-refund system

China mandates digital fiscal e-invoicing through the STA's national electronic invoice platforms. Legal invoices (ordinary or VAT special invoices) must be issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform or the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform, transmitted in near real time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV monitoring system, and must carry the taxpayer's Unified Social Credit Code, digital tax-control signature, anti-counterfeit QR code and mandatory invoice content. High-volume taxpayers may use application-to-application/electronic data interchange interfaces; there is no separate local physical fiscal stamping.

The STA automatically cross-checks export zero-rating claims against customs export declarations, SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data, transport records and invoice information. If the documentation is false, incomplete, or the foreign currency is not repatriated within the statutory period, the claimed zero rating will be clawed back, the input VAT credit will be disallowed or recovered, and late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day will run from the due date under Article 32 of the Tax Administration Law. Fraudulent export refund claims attract a penalty of 50% up to five times the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, up to life imprisonment; tax records and export dossiers remain open for audit for at least ten years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a hybrid exchange-control regime: current account transactions are nominally convertible for trade and service payments under the IMF Article VIII obligation, while capital account transactions remain permission-based and are strictly routed through SAFE-supervised channels. A WFOE's initial equity capital injection from a foreign parent is classified as a capital account inflow, requiring prior foreign investment information reporting, SAFE registration, and deposit in a designated bank capital account before any conversion into RMB. Dividend remittance or profit repatriation is treated as current account profit distribution and is generally permitted after statutory annual audit, board approval, and corporate income tax settlement, supported by a tax clearance certificate. However, outbound capital reduction, liquidation proceeds, shareholder loan repayments, and high-value equity transfers still require specific SAFE approval, tax certificates, or audited balance sheet support. Export proceeds are repatriated through the banking system and may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB at onshore market rates, with no mandatory minimum sale ratio. The practical friction for a WFOE is not the routine receipt of trade money but the structural documentation burden around each capital-account event, the mandatory use of a SAFE-licensed designated commercial bank, and the heightened evidentiary review for any payment that deviates from the registered business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE export monitoring rules, export proceeds must generally be repatriated into a domestic foreign currency account no later than 90 days from the export customs declaration date or the commercial payment due date recorded in the trade contract, unless an extension is filed and documented in SAFE's export proceeds verification system. There is no statutory fixed surrender ratio; exporters may keep 100% of repatriated proceeds as foreign currency balances in their licensed foreign exchange accounts or settle 100% into RMB at the prevailing onshore bank rate. Each receipt must be matched to the relevant customs declaration and an export collection transaction nature code, and the bank will automatically monitor overdue collection and settlement mismatches for anti-money-laundering and trade verification purposes.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-conditional viability. Digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex may serve only as an ancillary collection or disbursement overlay for international e-commerce settlements, but they cannot legally replace a PBOC/SAFE-regulated Tier-1 commercial bank for Chinese mainland corporate account opening, RMB clearing, capital account registration, capital verification, or statutory FX reporting. Offshore EMIs without a PBOC banking license or a formal Sino-foreign joint-venture banking license are not permitted to open primary corporate settlement accounts or handle an onshore WFOE's capital account funds. The practical architecture is to keep all statutory capital and tax-related flows under a domestic Tier-1 bank and use licensed cross-border payment facilitators only for low-value marketplace reverse-logistics refunds and platform collections, always with clear reconciliation to the bank ledger.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership chain terminating in an opaque offshore parent holding structure or discretionary trust

Root cause: Beneficial ownership chain terminating in an opaque offshore parent holding structure or discretionary trust

Defense evidence: Full corporate ownership chart from ultimate individual beneficial owners to the WFOE; certificate of incorporation and memorandum of association of each intermediate holding entity; current register of shareholders and directors; trust deed or declaration of trust when a trust is the ultimate owner; passport copies of all ultimate beneficial owners; source-of-wealth documentation for equity injection such as bank statements and auditor-certified financial statements; bank reference letter from the parent's primary relationship bank.
Reverse-logistics transaction flows mixing commerce refunds, chargebacks, and refunded shipment values with ordinary trade proceeds

Root cause: Reverse-logistics transaction flows mixing commerce refunds, chargebacks, and refunded shipment values with ordinary trade proceeds

Defense evidence: Master service agreement or marketplace seller contract with the relevant e-commerce platform; written returns, repair, and disposition policy; ERP or logistics system export showing each transaction matched to a return authorization ticket, RMA number, or shipment ID; bank account statements reconciled to platform settlement reports; sample customs declarations for returned goods and re-exported graded merchandise; detailed narrative memo explaining any refund exceeding the original invoice amount or any payment from a third-party payer not identical to the original buyer.
High-frequency, low-value inbound RMB receipts from numerous overseas marketplace payers creating a fragmented transaction profile

Root cause: High-frequency, low-value inbound RMB receipts from numerous overseas marketplace payers creating a fragmented transaction profile

Defense evidence: Current and historical list of active e-commerce platforms and major merchant counterparties; payment gateway settlement agreements and fee schedules; KYC/AML file on each dominant counterparty; internal fraud-prevention controls including geoblock and velocity rules; monthly volume forecast and seasonality analysis; customer refund and fraud monitoring procedure; board minute approving the use of third-party platforms.
Business license scope declares reverse logistics and quality grading but bank-observed payment flows involve third-party merchandise or unconnected warehouse operators

Root cause: Business license scope declares reverse logistics and quality grading but bank-observed payment flows involve third-party merchandise or unconnected warehouse operators

Defense evidence: Valid business license with business scope aligned to ISIC 5229; warehouse leases or bonded-zone storage agreements; customs registration certificate and customs broker contract; sample inspection and quality-grading SOP with photographic evidence; sample return goods inspection reports; insurance certificates for warehouse stock; explanation and contracts for every disburse-to-third-party service provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the foreign parent must be remitted exclusively by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account held at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The remittance instruction must include the SAFE transaction purpose code for direct investment equity injection and a payment narrative clearly reading 'Equity Investment Funds - WFOE Capital Injection'. The receiving bank will verify the Foreign Investment Information Reporting record, confirm the account is within the approved capital account quota, and then either convert the proceeds into RMB or retain them as foreign currency on the balance sheet. Within 30 days of receipt, a registered Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in contribution; that report is filed with the bank and retained as the evidence base for future profit repatriation and equity exits.Outbound disbursements for reverse-logistics trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for regular service fees, third-party warehouse charges, overseas courier payments, and platform service invoices, each supported by a service contract, invoice, customs declaration if goods are involved, and tax payment proof. Commercial letters of credit are appropriate when the WFOE imports returned goods in bulk from overseas warehouses or requires bank payment assurance for high-value consignments; the docs must be clean and include the commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and quality-grade certificate. Documentary collections, using D/P or D/A, are acceptable only for established counterparties authorized through the bank's trade compliance review. Domestic RMB disbursements to local warehouse and fulfilment partners should ride the domestic corporate ACH/CNAPS rail for settlement finality and audit traceability.Onshore deliverable foreign exchange forward contracts with a SAFE-licensed commercial bank, strictly backed by underlying trade invoices or capital-account exposurePlain-vanilla cross-currency swaps for external debt and FDI-denominated liability hedging purposesOnshore RMB FX options and swaptions permitted under real-demand principle when approved by the bank's derivatives and compliance committeeOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the mainland WFOE itself and may be considered only for the overseas parent entity

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-conditional viability. Digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex may serve only as an ancillary collection or disbursement overlay for international e-commerce settlements, but they cannot legally replace a PBOC/SAFE-regulated Tier-1 commercial bank for Chinese mainland corporate account opening, RMB clearing, capital account registration, capital verification, or statutory FX reporting. Offshore EMIs without a PBOC banking license or a formal Sino-foreign joint-venture banking license are not permitted to open primary corporate settlement accounts or handle an onshore WFOE's capital account funds. The practical architecture is to keep all statutory capital and tax-related flows under a domestic Tier-1 bank and use licensed cross-border payment facilitators only for low-value marketplace reverse-logistics refunds and platform collections, always with clear reconciliation to the bank ledger.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: annual related-party tangible asset transactions exceeding RMB 200 million; related-party intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party service transactions exceeding RMB 100 million. Master File threshold: annual aggregate related-party revenue of RMB 1 billion or more. Country-by-Country Report threshold: consolidated group revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine through Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules. For a WFOE in cross-border reverse logistics, economic substance requires real personnel, physical facilities, inventory management systems, inspection and grading operations, repair capabilities, logistics coordination, and actual risk absorption. The Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in China to support the profit attributed to the WFOE. A shell entity holding legal title but lacking decision-making power, operational control, or incidental costs may be disregarded as a conduit, and treaty benefits may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance recharacterization under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; denial of treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership doctrine; additional tax with interest; penalty of 50% to 3 times the tax attributable to the abusive arrangement under the Tax Collection and Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly execute sham arrangements face personal civil liability for restitution of corporate assets, inclusion in the tax dishonesty blacklist, travel bans and exit restrictions, and criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for tax evasion, with imprisonment up to seven years and fines.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Primary transfer pricing adjustment under Articles 41, 44, and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; interest is imposed on the adjusted amount, and penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax may apply. Failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation triggers special penalty rules and presumptive adjustment authority.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false filings or cause the corporate books to understate taxable profits may be held jointly liable for unpaid taxes under civil law, are subject to blacklisting and travel bans as major tax dishonest persons, and face criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law with imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, the WFOE as withholding agent must pay the unpaid tax, late payment interest, and a fine from 50% to 3 times the amount of the unwithheld tax. The tax authority may also disallow the hidden deduction and impose additional income tax.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and responsible financial director may be made personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax through piercing of the corporate veil, travel restrictions and asset freezes are applied, and deliberate evasion involving large amounts constitutes criminal tax fraud under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, result in serious cases in imprisonment for up to seven years.
