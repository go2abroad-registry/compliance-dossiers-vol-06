Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax platform, accessible via https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first obtain a Foreign Investment Business License or filing confirmation from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). After the business license is issued, the WFOE must complete information consolidation registration with the competent tax authority within 30 calendar days, obtaining a unified social credit code (USCC) which serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for both enterprise income tax and VAT purposes. The WFOE shall log into the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal, submit the Registration Information Form (including legal representative identity, registered address, capital contributions, business scope, bank account information, and expected chief financial officer or tax agent), and upload the SAMR business license, articles of association, lease or ownership proof of registered address, and identity documents. For a foreign parent that will receive dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees, the non-resident enterprise must also register for tax withholding purposes via the withholding agent or directly obtain a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration Number (typically the same USCC of the WFOE for domestic compliance, while the foreign entity may need a Chinese tax identification number for treaty benefit claims). In addition, the WFOE must apply for a general taxpayer status (VAT general taxpayer) if annual sales exceed the statutory threshold or elect it voluntarily. The competent tax authority will issue the tax registration certificate (load-bearing unified social credit code) and activate the taxpayer's electronic account, VAT invoice system, and monthly/quarterly filing obligations.

Available election forms

None for general WFOE classification; however, China applies controlled foreign corporation (CFC) rules and general anti-avoidance rules to cross-border arrangements. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed as an entity-level resident enterprise taxpayer; no check-the-box pass-through election exists. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual sales of RMB 5,000,000 (¥5,000,000) for general taxpayer mandatory registration; entities with annual taxable sales below this threshold may register as small-scale taxpayers. VAT registration is mandatory for all entities issuing taxable supplies in China regardless of turnover, but the small-scale / general taxpayer threshold determines the applicable calculation and invoicing regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of goods by a VAT general taxpayer are zero-rated, meaning output VAT is 0% and the exporter may claim input VAT credits or refunds. Section 4 of the Interim VAT Regulations and the Measures for the Administration of Export Tax Rebates (Export Tax Refund) allow zero-rating for goods exported from China, provided the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are actually shipped out of the customs territory, export customs procedures are completed, and sales proceeds are repatriated into China within the statutory period. For cross-border services, zero-rating applies to certain qualifying offshore services such as transportation, logistics auxiliary services for exports, and services consumed entirely outside China, subject to the catalogue of zero-rated cross-border services; container leasing revenue from leasing intermodal ISO containers to non-resident customers for use outside China may qualify if the lease is categorized as a cross-border service lease and the recipient is a non-resident enterprise, but strict documentation is required. The WFOE must be registered as a VAT general taxpayer, keep separate accounting for export sales, and submit export tax refund declarations electronically through the e-Tax platform.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Goods Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank receipt showing receipt of foreign currency from overseas counterparty
  • Foreign exchange receipt and payment verification form (State Administration of Foreign Exchange, SAFE) generated via the cross-border payments and receipts declaration system
  • Export VAT invoice (ordinary commercial invoice for export, as export goods do not require VAT special invoice)
  • Export tax rebate declaration form submitted via the e-Tax platform
  • Container leasing contract or agreement evidencing the cross-border nature of the supply and ownership/control of the container fleet

China imposes a mandatory digital fiscal invoicing regime through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the full digital electronic invoice (fully digitized electronic invoice, or shu piao) system. VAT general taxpayers must use the official tax UKey or taxpayer digital identity to issue VAT special invoices and VAT ordinary invoices through the electronic tax bureau's invoice system. The system performs real-time or near-real-time clearance, invoice number allocation, and digital stamping with tax authority control codes. Since the 2023-2025 rollout of the fully digitalized electronic invoice pilot, qualified taxpayers issue electronic invoices through the e-Tax platform without requiring physical pre-printed invoice blanks; invoice data are transmitted automatically to the tax authority. There is an anti-fraud digital stamping mechanism (tax control block /税控码) embedded in every invoice. Enterprises must report invoicing data, purchase invoice verification, and VAT filing returns electronically; general taxpayers must upload VAT special invoice data for authentication before crediting input VAT.

China's tax authorities, together with customs and SAFE, may audit export tax refund claims retroactively for at least five years, and in fraud cases for an indefinite period until discovered. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export, without timely foreign exchange repatriation, or through forged customs declarations, the taxpayer must repay the refunded VAT plus pay late payment surcharges (0.05% per day on overdue tax). Administrative fines range from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refund erroneously obtained under the Tax Collection Administration Law. If the conduct constitutes smuggling or fraudulent export tax refund fraud under Criminal Law Article 204, criminal liability may include imprisonment and fines. False or fraudulent export declarations are subject to customs penalties under the Customs Law. The exporter must keep the documentation bundle for ten years for export tax refund audit purposes and respond to tax authorities' 'doubtful verification' or 'suspension of refund' procedures if any document is inconsistent.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. The Chinese Renminbi (CNY) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, provided that the underlying transaction is genuine and documented, and the funds are settled through designated foreign exchange banks. Export proceeds are subject to mandatory repatriation and, in practice, may be retained in foreign currency accounts or converted into CNY, subject to the SAFE registration and verification systems. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise engaged in the leasing of intermodal ISO shipping containers, the core operating revenue from cross-border lease rentals is classified as trade in services under the current account and is therefore eligible for free conversion and remittance after submission of the relevant contracts, invoices, and customs or port documentation. However, capital account transactions, including the injection of registered capital, foreign debt, equity transfers, and liquidation distributions, require case-by-case SAFE approval, registration, or filing. Profit remittances by a WFOE are generally permitted without prior approval once audited financial statements and tax payment proofs are submitted, but banks will conduct a post-verification review to ensure that the profits are derived from genuine operations and that all withholding taxes have been settled. Surrender of export foreign currency proceeds is no longer mandatory as a fixed percentage under current SAFE rules, yet all receipts must be repatriated within the statutory timeframe, and any excess over operating needs should be converted or held compliantly. The practical hurdles for a container leasing WFOE include proving the reality and commercial substance of cross-border lease agreements, ensuring that lease rental receipts are matched with equipment movement records, and navigating the annual SAFE inspection and the foreign exchange settlement verification system. Overall, the regime is manageable for a low-risk standard industrial leasing business provided that the corporate treasury maintains rigorous documentation, uses the QR-code-based digital foreign exchange receipts system, and works only with licensed onshore commercial banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, a Chinese WFOE in the container leasing industry must repatriate all export proceeds, including cross-border container lease rental receipts, to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory 180 days from the date of shipment, invoice, or service completion, as applicable under the registration verification system. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent under the current SAFE framework, as China abolished the compulsory pre-2012 surrender requirement; however, enterprises are required to settle or retain such proceeds in accordance with their foreign exchange receipt verification declarations, and foreign currency accounts can hold retained proceeds but cannot exceed SAFE-approved current account quotas. All receipts must be matched to the SAFE 'Service Trade Foreign Exchange Payment and Receipt' filing, and any prolonged retention in offshore accounts without repatriation will trigger regulatory review and potential penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), under the People's Bank of China (PBC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs and virtual banking platforms without a licensed physical branch and an onshore establishment supervised by the NFRA or SAFE are legally barred from opening primary corporate deposit and settlement accounts for a PRC mainland WFOE. The cross-border viability of such EMIs is therefore Low to None for primary CNY clearing. They can provide ancillary payment accessories, such as cross-border multi-currency receivable collection in Hong Kong, but only as a supplementary collection conduit for rental proceeds outside the mainland, and no territory where the WFOE’s statutory books, tax filings, or capital verification are banked. The domestic tier-1 banks operate the China Foreign Exchange Trade System and interface directly with SAFE’s Internet Platform for Foreign Exchange Administration, giving them the only compliant rail for capital injections, foreign debt registration, and profit repatriation. Any booking of operating balances or lease rentals through a non-licensed EMI would violate the Administrative Measures for the Settlement of Foreign Exchange in Trade and lead to frozen funds, fines, and downgrade of the WFOE’s SAFE compliance rating.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital injections into a WFOE must be designated as 'Capital Contribution / FDI Renminbi or Foreign Currency Settlement' under SAFE Category 621 in the foreign exchange settlement code system, transmitted exclusively via SWIFT MT103 or China’s CIPS for cross-border RMB from the overseas parent account. The beneficiary account must be the bank's NRA capital settlement account or the special FDI capital account registered in the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration System, and the remittance must state the exact shareholder loan or equity purpose code '622 Investment Income / 621 FDI Injection' depending on the structure. Banks will then require a payment instruction matching the amount in the SAFE registration, followed by the submission of the FDI Verification Form and the signed capital increase or injection agreement. Within 30 days, the bank will execute a capital settlement in accordance with the negative list policy, converting the foreign currency into CNY at the onshore spot rate after the corporate account verifies the 'capital use commitment letter' and the actual counterparty payment instruction. For processing efficiency, the treasury should route via the parent's relationship bank using Bank of China New York, Hong Kong, or Singapore branches for a straight-through processing direct MT103 to the WFOE's Bank of China mainland branch, avoiding intermediary deductions and preserving the full invoice and contractual reference details in field 70.Outbound trade settlement and international lease disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfer when paying foreign lessees, vendors, port operators, or depot maintenance providers. For higher-value purchase orders of new ISO containers, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is recommended via the onshore bank, extending irrevocable sight or 90-day usance terms in favor of the container manufacturer in China or overseas, with presentation of a commercial invoice, bill of lading, container quality certificate, and insurance document. Documentary Collections (DP/DA) can be used for intra-group or stable counterparty trade payments to lower bank fees; however, for the main rental receivable collection, the banking rails should use SWIFT MT103 with the service trade contract reference, or the bank's Cross-Border RMB CIPS channel when the counterparty accepts CNY. For disbursements related to depot handling, repositioning and repair charges, the WFOE should use the domestic CNY clearing system (CNAPS / HVPS) for onshore suppliers, and SWIFT MT103 only for offshore port or ocean carrier payments. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs and virtual banking platforms without a licensed physical branch and an onshore establishment supervised by the NFRA or SAFE are legally barred from opening primary corporate deposit and settlement accounts for a PRC mainland WFOE. The cross-border viability of such EMIs is therefore Low to None for primary CNY clearing. They can provide ancillary payment accessories, such as cross-border multi-currency receivable collection in Hong Kong, but only as a supplementary collection conduit for rental proceeds outside the mainland, and no territory where the WFOE’s statutory books, tax filings, or capital verification are banked. The domestic tier-1 banks operate the China Foreign Exchange Trade System and interface directly with SAFE’s Internet Platform for Foreign Exchange Administration, giving them the only compliant rail for capital injections, foreign debt registration, and profit repatriation. Any booking of operating balances or lease rentals through a non-licensed EMI would violate the Administrative Measures for the Settlement of Foreign Exchange in Trade and lead to frozen funds, fines, and downgrade of the WFOE’s SAFE compliance rating.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a Local File is required when annual related-party tangible-asset transactions reach RMB 200 million; financial-asset transactions reach RMB 100 million; intangible-asset transactions reach RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions reach RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the enterprise had aggregate related-party transaction revenue of RMB 1 billion or more in the prior year.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime in the same form as certain offshore centres, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in Article 47 of the Corporate Income Tax Law, together with SAT Announcement No. 7 of 2015 and the OECD BEPS-aligned special tax adjustment rules, require substance-over-form and business purpose. A WFOE engaged in container box leasing must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), including asset acquisition, fleet management, commercial negotiation, invoicing, client contracting, maintenance decisions and treasury control, within China. If the offshore parent or a related affiliate performs CIGA and earns associated profits while the WFOE remains a low-substance cash conduit, SAT can reallocate profits to China, deny treaty benefits and impose additional tax, interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Intentional Under-declaration

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 orders repayment of the evaded tax and imposes a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05%, plus an administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the amount evaded. Criminal Law Article 201 applies where the evaded amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, carrying criminal detention or imprisonment up to three years; where the amount is huge and exceeds 30%, imprisonment from three to seven years with fines.

Director personal liability: Where directors or controlling shareholders abuse the limited liability structure, co-mingle personal and corporate funds, or orchestrate fraudulent invoicing, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law principles and they become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil damages. Responsible persons are also placed on the serious-taxpayer blacklist, triggering credit downgrades, exit bans, travel restrictions and disqualification from serving as directors; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment up to seven years.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Corporate Income Tax Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement No. 7 of 2015, the tax authority may recharacterise or disregard the artificial arrangement, deny treaty benefits, deem the income to be derived by the Chinese entity, and reassess corporate income tax with interest. Transfer-pricing recharacterisations can also give rise to an additional 50% penalty under the special tax adjustment rules.

Director personal liability: If a director knowingly participates in the shell scheme to defraud the Chinese tax authority or to evade dividend WHT, the director may be held personally liable for the revised tax and interest under veil-piercing and joint tort principles. The director will be blacklisted by the tax administration, banned from exiting China, and exposed to criminal prosecution if fabricated filings or fraudulent contracts are used; conviction under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in up to seven years' imprisonment.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Cross-Border Withholding Tax on Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Corporate Income Tax Law Articles 37 and 38 impose the statutory WHT obligation, and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 provides that a withholding agent that fails to collect or remit the tax is liable for the uncollected tax and subject to a fine of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount. Fraudulent or abusive treaty claims are treated as tax avoidance and may be disregarded under Article 47 with reassessment, interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or approve the unreported payment and stand behind the WFOE's separate legal personality to avoid collection can be personally joined in tax collection proceedings and held liable for the unpaid withholding amount. Blacklisting and travel bans apply to the legal representative and responsible directors; intentional use of cross-border invoicing or laundering channels can lead to prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 and money laundering under Article 191, carrying prison terms up to seven years or longer if the case involves an organised criminal group.
