For a WFOE engaged in ISIC 5229 dangerous goods forwarding, virtual office, co-working desk, and commercial mailbox arrangements are operatively rejected by SAMR, the tax authority and Chinese banks. A physical office with an independent lease or property ownership document, a clearly marked company nameplate, dedicated working stations, file storage, and telephone/data connectivity is mandatory at the time of incorporation. While certain free trade zones allow 'address sharing' for low-risk industries, the dangerous-goods forwarding license under the Ministry of Transport and the customs authorities requires a substantive office and a designated safety officer. Consequently, a virtual office is impermissible and the enterprise must secure a commercial lease that is subject to inspection.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account is mandated for a standard wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company under the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision). However, the registered capital must be subscribed under the Articles of Association and fully paid within five years from incorporation unless a shorter period is prescribed by the articles. For a WFOE engaged in dangerous goods forwarding, the foreign investor must remit the subscribed capital in freely convertible currency or RMB through a lawful foreign exchange capital account, and the capital is available for operational use after receipt. The registered capital must be reasonably consistent with the proposed scale of ISIC 5229 operations and the safety and liability commitments described in the application; an obviously disproportionate capital figure may be queried by the SAMR. No escrow is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire protection design review and fire acceptance or recordation certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Division; civil air-defense permit and structural load inspection where the operation uses basement or civil-defense spaces; workplace safety approval under the Work Safety Law, including a major hazardous-source registration if the facility exceeds threshold quantities; occupational hazard monitoring, declaration, and pre-employment examinations under the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases; registration of special equipment such as forklifts and pressure vessels; and a site-specific emergency response plan approved by the relevant emergency management authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax platform)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in the People's Republic of China, the statutory taxpayer identifier is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), automatically issued upon registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The WFOE must then complete tax registration in the STA Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of establishment, authenticate as a legal-person taxpayer, activate VAT taxpayer status, and register for invoice issuance quotas and bookkeeping requirements. Foreign non-resident enterprises that derive China-sourced income but have no registered permanent establishment must obtain a withholding tax registration via the payer or directly through the e-Tax portal, and have a Chinese tax identification number assigned by the tax authority for withholding VAT and Corporate Income Tax (CIT) purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or pass-through entity elections. A WFOE is taxed as a separate entity under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Hybrid mismatch rules under the Special Tax Adjustments framework (Notice No. 118/2018) may require income characterization alignment, but no elective entity classification regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero; all enterprises, including WFOEs, are mandatorily required to register as VAT taxpayers irrespective of turnover. Small-scale taxpayer classification applies when annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5 million, subject to simplified levy rates, but registration is still compulsory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported dangerous goods is available under Article 15 of the VAT Implementing Regulations if the goods are physically exported from China customs territory and the exporter files a valid customs export declaration. Zero-rating also applies to international transport services for the carriage of goods by air or sea. For dangerous-goods freight forwarding services rendered from China to non-resident carriers or overseas shippers, the international carriage portion may be zero-rated, while the forwarder agency component is generally VAT-exempt (not zero-rated but with output VAT at 0%) if the service is provided wholly outside China to a non-resident client and substantiated with the prescribed documentary evidence. Qualifying offshore digital services consumed entirely outside China may also benefit from zero-rating if exported services criteria are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - Customs Export Declaration Form duly stamped by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading (Sea) or Air Waybill (Air) evidencing physical export of the dangerous goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank SWIFT foreign exchange receipt confirmation
  • Export invoice and commercial contract between the WFOE and the overseas customer
  • Dangerous Goods Transport Documentation (e.g., Dangerous Goods Declaration or Safety Data Sheet) and packing certificate
  • STA VAT invoice records and export transaction ledger supporting the zero-rating claim

China mandates digital invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau's invoice system, with real-time reporting of invoice data (sales amount, tax rate, tax amount, buyer and seller tax IDs, tax control number) to the tax authority. Special VAT invoices are subject to anti-fraud encryption, automatic verification, and risk classification. Full digitalized electronic invoices are progressively mandatory, with paper invoices subject to immediate digital reconciliation and cross-archiving before being fiscally valid.

If export zero-rating is claimed without genuine customs exportation or foreign exchange repatriation within 180 days, the tax authority will demand recovery of output VAT, impose late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, and impose penalty fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and its Implementation Rules. Fraudulent export tax rebate claims may trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, revocation of export tax refund qualifications, inclusion in the tax violation blacklist, and customs anti-smuggling sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility and capital-account restrictions. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may generally settle RMB for goods, freight, insurance, and services trade without prior SAFE approval, but every payment and receipt must be supported by genuine trade or service documentation and reviewed by a designated domestic bank. The capital account remains tightly controlled: foreign direct investment injections require bank-level SAFE registration, a capital account/FDI collection account, and permitted use of converted RMB strictly within the registered business scope. Profit remittances by a WFOE are current-account transactions only after the annual statutory audit is completed and tax clearances, foreign exchange audit, and board resolution documents are lodged with the bank; however, dividend remittance above the audited distributable profit is not permitted. Surrender requirements are minimal for current-account proceeds, but repatriation timing rules still apply. In practice, banks apply identity and purpose-code verification to combat capital flows disguised as trade. This makes the WFOE treasury model workable, but requires disciplined documentary archiving for every cross-border movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-in-goods rules, where the DG forwarding WFOE receives export goods proceeds or licensed cross-border logistics revenue qualifying as trade receipts, the foreign currency must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of the relevant Customs export declaration or service completion date unless SAFE has approved an overseas retention scheme. Once repatriated, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may either retain the foreign currency in a domestic Foreign Exchange Settlement Account or voluntarily convert to RMB at a bank using the prevailing market rate. Each retention or conversion must be documented against the underlying export invoice, contract, Customs declaration, dangerous goods manifest, and logistics evidence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for an onshore WFOE. Foreign fintech EMIs and global digital banks are not licensed by SAFE or the PBOC to open RMB corporate accounts, to act as designated foreign exchange banks, or to perform FDI capital registration and settlement. They also cannot issue RMB-denominated clearing instructions to CNAPS or access China's FX clearing infrastructure. Accordingly, they must not be proposed as the primary treasury rail; a fintech EMI may only be used as a supplementary offshore expense card or payment instrument outside China, never for statutory WFOE capital injection, tax settlement, customs duty, or dangerous goods operational payments inside China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the WFOE FDI registered capital, the foreign parent wires funds by SWIFT MT103 to the receiving domestic bank's capital account under the SAFE FDI registration. The wire must carry the SAFE purpose code for direct investment inbound equity capital; the receiving bank verifies the company code, foreign exchange IC card, and amount against the FDI registration record. Upon receipt and entry into the capital account ledger, a licensed Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report. RMB conversion follows SAFE positive-list rules; converted capital may not be used for securities investment, entrusted loans, or repayment of shareholder loans outside approved uses. A separate RMB operating account should be maintained to preserve a clean audit trail.Day-to-day payments for freight forwarder disbursements, customs bonds, overseas agent commissions, transport charges, and DG handling fees should be routed through bank clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for supplier and agent transfers; Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value documented dangerous goods purchase contracts, particularly sea freight charter and block-space commitments; and Documentary Collections where the beneficiary receives title documents against payment or acceptance. Every outbound remittance must be supported by the relevant SAFE purpose code, underlying contract, invoice, and for freight-backed transactions a completed dangerous goods manifest or logistics proof. Domestic RMB movements are settled through CNAPS or T+1 bank clearing before conversion to USD or EUR.Bank foreign exchange forwards with real trade background: forward purchase or sale of USD or EUR against RMB up to 12 months, supported by current-account exposure documents and strictly no speculative positioning.Foreign exchange option combinations with a licensed domestic bank under the self-hedging real-demand principle, allowing collars and knock-in options only when embedded in an approved underlying trade or logistics cash flow.Cross-currency swaps only for approved term liabilities or capital-account-related principal and interest hedging, subject to SAFE registration where required and generally too restrictive for ordinary forwarding treasury activity.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as an onshore corporate hedging rail because they lack the required real-trade documentation and cannot be settled through the domestic RMB clearing system; NDF access is confined to offshore parent vehicles and remains outside the onshore compliance boundary.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise shareholder (unless a bilateral tax treaty provides a reduced rate; for Hong Kong, the DTA rate is 5% for shareholdings of at least 25%, otherwise 10%).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return must be filed and final tax paid within 5 months after the fiscal year-end (by May 31 of the following year for a calendar-year entity); quarterly provisional payments are due by the 15th day of the month following each quarter.
