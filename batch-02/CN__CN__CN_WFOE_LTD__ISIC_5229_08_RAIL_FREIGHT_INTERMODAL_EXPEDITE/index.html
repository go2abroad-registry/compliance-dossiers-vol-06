Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum registered or paid-up capital is required for a WFOE engaged in freight forwarding under PRC law. The sole shareholder must state the registered capital amount in the Articles of Association and, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, must pay the full subscribed capital within five years from the date of establishment. There is no statutory blocked escrow arrangement; the shareholder deposits capital into the company's basic or FDI foreign exchange account after registration, subject to State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) receipt and settlement rules.

Funding & unlock path: Office fire safety clearance is required before commencing business. The landlord's building must have a valid fire safety filing or fire acceptance certificate, and the company must maintain a fire safety self-inspection record, emergency evacuation plan, fire extinguishers, exit signage, and fire drills under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. No separate Civil Defense facility permit applies because no below-grade or physical industrial facility is being constructed. The company must also register employees for work safety training with the local Emergency Management Bureau, carry out occupational health and safety risk assessment, and maintain a safety management protocol. If the company later establishes a warehouse or intermodal yard, mandatory requirements would include Fire Protection Design Review and Fire Acceptance, Civil Defense lot inspection, and annual safety production compliance reporting.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum registered or paid-up capital is required for a WFOE engaged in freight forwarding under PRC law. The sole shareholder must state the registered capital amount in the Articles of Association and, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, must pay the full subscribed capital within five years from the date of establishment. There is no statutory blocked escrow arrangement; the shareholder deposits capital into the company's basic or FDI foreign exchange account after registration, subject to State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) receipt and settlement rules.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing and registration are conducted through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau integrated online service portal maintained by the local municipal or district tax service bureau.

E-government filing portal

For a China-incorporated wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), tax registration is integrated with business incorporation: after the business license is issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically transmitted to the STA and serves as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number. The WFOE must then log in to the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days, complete the taxpayer registration profile, file the enterprise bank deposit account information, designate its financial accounting system and tax representative, and apply for the appropriate VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer). For a foreign non-resident entity without an establishment or place of business in China but with China-sourced income or a Chinese VAT compliance obligation, the non-resident entity may obtain a tax identification number by applying directly to the competent local STA office or through a withholding agent; the application requires the foreign entity's incorporation certificate, certified Chinese-language translation, contract evidencing China-sourced income, and identification details of the counter-party and withholding agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election for income tax purposes; the WFOE is taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The VAT regime separately permits an eligible small-scale taxpayer to elect general VAT taxpayer status, but this is an indirect tax status election, not a corporate entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5,000,000) annual taxable sales; annual taxable turnover above this threshold mandates general VAT taxpayer registration and normal VAT compliance. All enterprises, including a WFOE, must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance irrespective of turnover; below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold a small-scale VAT taxpayer status is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT export rate is available for goods physically exported from China where the taxpayer is the exporter of record and holds a valid customs export declaration. For the WFOE's cross-border rail freight forwarding and intermodal expedited logistics services, 0% zero-rating is available only if the WFOE qualifies as providing international rail transportation services as the actual carrier of goods; if the WFOE acts solely as a freight forwarding agent, the cross-border freight forwarding service is normally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. To claim 0% or exemption, the taxpayer must maintain contracts naming the overseas customer, evidence that the service is performed in connection with goods exported from China, customs export declarations, rail waybills or through bills of lading, and proof of foreign exchange receipt from the overseas payer. For exported goods, the exporter must be identified on the customs declaration, the goods must physically leave China under customs supervision, and the export sales proceeds must be repatriated to China within the statutory foreign-exchange settlement period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) generated through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original or certified copy of the through Bill of Lading or international rail consignment note (CIM/SMGS rail waybill)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-designated bank foreign exchange settlement/receipt voucher
  • Export sales contract or cross-border freight forwarding service contract
  • Containerized rail cargo manifest, container booking confirmation and customs seal records where intermodal container transport is used

China requires VAT invoices to be issued through the STA-controlled integrated electronic invoice and tax-control system. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are issued through the Electronic Tax Bureau platform and are transmitted in real time to the STA under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Special VAT invoices must be verified online through the VAT invoice selection and verification platform before input VAT credit can be claimed. Anti-fraud controls include invoice code and serial-number stamping, encrypted digital security features, real-time invoice reporting, automated matching of invoice, payment, customs declaration and foreign exchange receipt data, and automatic risk flagging by the STA export-tax-rebate risk-monitoring system.

Tax and customs authorities jointly audit export zero-rating claims through the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the export tax-rebate risk-monitoring system. If the zero rate is claimed without a valid customs export declaration, credible rail waybill, matching invoice, or timely foreign-exchange repatriation, the STA will disallow the zero rating, require output VAT at the domestic rate, claw back any input VAT refund already granted, and impose late-payment surcharges of 0.05% per day. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, a fraudulent export-rebate claim may result in a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China provides criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds and for falsely issuing special VAT invoices.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility largely unrestricted upon documentary verification, while capital account transactions remain controlled through registration and approval mechanisms. For a WFOE engaged in international rail freight forwarding, operational receipts and payments related to cross-border freight services, transit fees, and reinsurance settlements are classified as current account items and may be processed via banks after submitting authentic trade documents through the SAFE digital trade foreign exchange monitoring platform. Capital inflows for registered capital injection must be reported to SAFE through the FDI registration system; the funds are then held in a designated foreign exchange capital account and can be converted to RMB for permitted operational uses following the negative list rules. Profit repatriation or dividend remittance to the foreign shareholder is generally permissible after the annual audit, tax clearance and a SAFE dividend remittance filing, but requires proof of after-tax profit, board resolution, and absence of unpaid capital obligations. Tight controls persist on speculative short-term cross-border flows and on renminbi derivatives beyond real demand backing, and any deviation between declared freight service invoices and actual rail bills of lading may trigger suspension of the entity's Class-A settlement rating.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular No.20 (2013) as amended, export proceeds must be repatriated into the WFOE's domestic foreign exchange account (or an approved offshore account) within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into RMB; the enterprise may retain the FX balances or convert voluntarily, provided every transaction is matched with customs data, invoices, and the consignment container-rail waybill via the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for domestic banking. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed to open yuan-denominated corporate accounts or interface directly with PBOC/SAFE clearing rails for a mainland WFOE. They may be used solely as auxiliary remittance calculation tools or for funding into an outside-China treasury vehicle, but the WFOE's settlement must remain with a locally licensed bank. Foreign EMIs cannot provide capital account FX conversion, SAFE registrations, or statutory capital verification.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax is imposed on dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to a non-resident corporate shareholder. The WFOE is the statutory withholding agent and must withhold before remitting the dividend, unless the shareholder qualifies for a reduced rate under an applicable bilateral Double Tax Agreement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Local File must be prepared when, in a single tax year, controlled tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, controlled intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, controlled financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or controlled services transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the enterprise group's total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transaction disclosure return must be filed regardless of amount whenever related-party transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: China has no UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations law, but substance is embedded in the Enterprise Income Tax Law GAAR under Article 47, the thin-capitalisation rule under Article 46, the CFC rule under Article 45, and the Beneficial Ownership test in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9. The WFOE must conduct substantive rail freight forwarding activities in Mainland China, including office occupation, full-time management, contractual negotiation, operational control, and risk assumption, especially when claiming treaty benefits on dividends, interest, or royalties. If the entity is treated as a shell or conduit, tax authorities may disregard it, recharacterise the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and assess tax at the full statutory 10% or 25% rates.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Invoicing / Offshore Revenue Diversion

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for tax evasion: imprisonment up to 3 years or criminal detention plus fines where the evaded tax amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of payable tax, and imprisonment of 3 to 7 years plus fines where the amount is huge and exceeds 30%. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange and SAFE may also impose fines for illegal cross-border money movement.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law where directors abuse the WFOE's separate legal personality to evade taxes, making them jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, surcharges, and penalties. Directors can be placed on the tax blacklisting and exit-control system, receive travel bans, face civil asset restitution, and be criminally imprisoned for tax evasion.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Facade / Improper Claim of DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 47 and 48 and Implementation Regulations Article 120, the tax authority may invoke GAAR, disregard the conduit entity, recharacterise the payments as dividends, deny treaty benefits, and assess the full 10% statutory withholding tax with daily late-payment interest. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 permits a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. The tax authority may also designate the arrangement as an abusive tax avoidance scheme and publish the case.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate treaty shopping face personal civil liability for the additional tax and penalties under Company Law veil-piercing principles. They may be subject to blacklisting and exit restrictions, and if false documents or forged board resolutions are used, criminal liability may arise for forgery of corporate instruments or fraudulent tax declaration.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Excessive Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41, the SAT may adjust taxable income to the arm's-length result. Article 44 permits a deemed profit assessment when documentation is absent. Article 114 of the Implementation Regulations and related Announcements permit late-payment interest. Administrative penalties can include fines under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 64 for underpayment of tax not amounting to evasion. The tax authority may also impose 10% withholding on disguised distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the failure to prepare or maintain transfer pricing documentation, or who sign false annual filing declarations, may be held personally liable for resulting corporate losses under Company Law duties of loyalty and care. In serious cases, they may be named in the tax authority's blacklist, restricted from leaving China, and prosecuted under Criminal Law where the transfer pricing underpayment is proven to be intentional tax evasion.
