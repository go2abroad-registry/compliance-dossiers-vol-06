Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filings and invoice reporting are processed through the Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online platform, the Golden Tax Project Phase IV VAT invoice management system, and the China International Trade Single Window for customs- and trade-related tax data flows with the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE, although foreign-owned, is a resident Chinese limited liability enterprise. It must first obtain a business license from the local Administration for Market Regulation, which issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), an 18-character code that serves as the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for STA filings, VAT invoice issuance, customs declarations, and export tax refund applications. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent local STA branch, confirm taxpayer type as small-scale VAT taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer, register for the national electronic invoice service platform, and open an export tax refund qualification file if export activities are planned. Foreign non-resident shareholders are not required to obtain a Chinese TIN purely for holding shares; the WFOE, as a resident taxpayer, is responsible for reporting and withholding any China-source taxes on shareholder transactions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax at the statutory 25% rate on worldwide income; no pass-through regime or check-the-box classification is available for foreign-owned limited liability entities.

Available election forms

None. China does not offer check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The only relevant administrative election is the optional voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer for businesses below the RMB 5,000,000 mandatory threshold, and the small-scale VAT taxpayer status for newly established entities; certain VAT supplies may elect the simplified method where explicitly permitted by STA circulars. For corporate income tax purposes, no entity classification election exists, and the WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary turnover threshold triggers ordinary VAT registration for an incorporated WFOE; the obligation arises from business registration. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. A small-scale VAT taxpayer with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 is eligible for the current VAT exemption policy; below the general taxpayer threshold, the enterprise may remain a small-scale taxpayer and apply the simplified levy rate of 3% unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating eligibility applies to a general VAT taxpayer that exports physical goods from China under a customs declaration, actually ships the goods out of Chinese customs territory, receives payment from the overseas buyer, and maintains fully reconciled export documentation. The zero-rate or export tax rebate is substantiated by matching customs export declarations with foreign exchange receipts and input VAT invoices. Domestic logistics, parcel consolidation, and linehaul services performed within China for cross-border e-commerce parcels are generally not zero-rated as goods; they are taxable Chinese VAT services at 6% unless the service is provided wholly to an overseas recipient and consumed entirely outside China under the cross-border service zero-rating or exemption provisions. Qualifying offshore digital services may be zero-rated or exempt only where the statutory place-of-supply and use-outside-China conditions are met.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form filed with the General Administration of Customs through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international parcel consignment receipt issued by the carrier or postal operator
  • Export Proceeds Receipt Confirmation from a designated foreign exchange bank, matched through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the exported goods
  • VAT special input invoices or Customs Special VAT Payment Certificates evidencing input VAT attributable to the exported goods
  • Export tax rebate declaration data submitted electronically through the STA export tax refund system
  • Parcel consolidation manifest or master and house air waybills evidencing linehaul injection from the Chinese consolidation hub to the overseas destination hub

VAT invoices must be issued through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which transmits invoice data in real time to the STA under Golden Tax Project Phase IV. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are recognized for all VAT purposes and carry a unified invoice number, digital anti-counterfeiting QR code, and mandatory machine-verifiable data fields. Invoice issuance, red-letter reversal, and credit/input VAT deduction are subject to real-time fiscal clearance and automated reconciliation. Export parcel customs clearance is performed electronically through the China International Trade Single Window, which integrates customs declaration data, foreign exchange monitoring data, and STA export tax refund records.

Export zero-rating is subject to joint audit scrutiny by the STA and the General Administration of Customs. If the zero-rated export claim is found to be unsupported by the customs export declaration, bill of lading, foreign exchange receipt, or valid input VAT invoices, the refunded or zero-rated VAT is clawed back with statutory late payment interest. Administrative penalties include fines of 50% to 500% of the tax involved for evasion and, in cases of confirmed export tax refund fraud, fines of one to five times the fraudulently obtained refund, revocation of export tax refund qualification, and prohibition on engaging in future export tax rebate transactions. Fraudulent use or issuance of VAT special invoices and intentional export tax refund fraud constitute criminal offenses under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. The recovery and audit periods are generally three years, extendable to five years in special circumstances, and there is no limitation period for cases involving fraud, tax evasion, or theft of export tax refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an administrated managed-float regime with current-account convertibility for bona fide trade and service transactions, but capital-account items remain subject to quota, registration, and purpose-matching controls. The RMB is convertible for current-account payments such as import settlement, service fees, wages, dividends, and trade-related charges once the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and tax paperwork are verified by SAFE through the bank. Capital-account inflows, including foreign direct investment, registered capital injections, cross-border loans, and returns of investment, require an FDI registration certificate, a SAFE bank confirmation, and a specific purpose code; proceeds must be placed in a capital escrow account and cannot be used for unverifiable domestic investments. Dividend remittances are permitted as current-account profit remittances after corporate income tax settlement, quarterly or annual financial statements, and the board resolution on profit distribution; the bank must file the remittance with the SAFE monitoring system. Under the 2012 reform of export verification, compulsory surrender of export proceeds was abolished, but repatriation into a Chinese foreign-exchange settlement account within the required time windows is still mandatory, and banks must confirm the authenticity of each receipt against the customs trade data. Therefore, treasury planning must anticipate regulatory delays, documentary rigidities, and the absence of free offshore or NRA capital mobility inside the onshore system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account administration, export proceeds from customs-cleared goods must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement or retained current-account balance generally within 90 days of the customs export declaration date. No statutory fixed conversion ratio is currently imposed; the exporter may choose to retain the foreign currency in an approved current-account account or convert it to RMB at a licensed bank, but the bank must verify the underlying customs data and export invoice. The retained balance is monitored monthly, and any conversion for domestic use must be supported by a genuine current-account need.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the delegated policy authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs such as digital-only multi-currency platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open local domestic RMB or forex accounts for a PRC-registered WFOE. They may function only as an auxiliary payment orchestrator through a Chinese-licensed bank gateway, or as an offshore cross-border collection and disbursement tool for the parent, and even then the WFOE must maintain its own onshore bank account for statutory capital, tax, and export verification. Overall viability as a primary domestic clearing rail is Low; supplementary use is Medium only when the EMI's bank partner holds a valid PBOC settlement license and the EMI does not hold or process segregated client funds inside China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder remits the WFOE registered capital via MT103 or MT202 through its home bank to the WFOE's capital escrow account opened with a SAFE-licensed bank in China. The payment purpose field must carry the FDI-investment FX purpose code and refer to the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate number. Upon receipt, the Chinese bank performs capital-account authenticity checks, records the receipt in SAFE's capital project information system, and, if required, the enterprise completes the capital verification statement through a local auditing firm within the statutory reporting period. Fund usage from the capital account is subject to the payment-registration and purpose-matching system, with each subsequent RMB conversion or currency account payment matched to a genuine current-account or permitted capex expense.For outbound trade settlements, the WFOE may use SWIFT MT103 for direct remittances against import or service contracts, commercial letters of credit opened through its corporate bank, or documentary collections presented through the bank. Each payment must be supported by invoices, packing lists, customs import declarations where applicable, transport documents, and a valid current-account transaction code; the bank screens the transaction against sanctions and outward-reporting requirements. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign fintech EMIs such as digital-only multi-currency platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open local domestic RMB or forex accounts for a PRC-registered WFOE. They may function only as an auxiliary payment orchestrator through a Chinese-licensed bank gateway, or as an offshore cross-border collection and disbursement tool for the parent, and even then the WFOE must maintain its own onshore bank account for statutory capital, tax, and export verification. Overall viability as a primary domestic clearing rail is Low; supplementary use is Medium only when the EMI's bank partner holds a valid PBOC settlement license and the EMI does not hold or process segregated client funds inside China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42, the WFOE must prepare and file the Local File and Master File for the tax year if any of its related-party transactions exceed the following annual thresholds: tangible-property transfers (purchases and sales of goods, machinery, materials) exceeding CNY 200 million; financial-asset transfers exceeding CNY 100 million; intangible-property transfers exceeding CNY 100 million; or aggregate other related-party transactions (service fees, management fees, commissions, interest, leasing) exceeding CNY 40 million. The Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) filing threshold applies where the multinational group's previous-year consolidated revenue is at least CNY 5.5 billion (approximately USD 800 million), in which case the WFOE must file its CbC notification within the annual EIT filing window and may be required to file the full CbCR as a constituent entity.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the equivalent substance-over-form regime is enforced through EIT Law Article 47 General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), the Special Tax Adjustment rules, Public Notice 2018 No. 9 (Beneficial Ownership and Look-Through) and Public Notice 2017 No. 37. For the WFOE parcel-consolidation hub, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are located in Mainland China: contract negotiation and SLA execution with foreign e-commerce platforms, physical parcel receiving and consolidation decision-making, sortation labor, direct-injection linehaul dispatch, carrier procurement, and assumption of inventory and delivery risk. If the group contracts away these CIGA to a foreign principal through a commissionaire arrangement, or shifts consolidated sortation revenue to the foreign parent while leaving only back-office functions in the Chinese hub, the STA will re-characterize the arrangement, re-attribute profit to the Chinese hub under GAAR, impose interest and penalties, and deny treaty relief on outbound remittances for lack of beneficial ownership and economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 63: recovery of the underpaid tax, daily late-surcharge of 0.05%, and an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201: tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and exceeding 10% of the tax payable carries up to 3 years' imprisonment; evasion exceeding RMB 250,000 and exceeding 10%-30% of tax payable carries 3-7 years' imprisonment plus a criminal fine up to RMB 1 million. False-issuance of special VAT invoices can attract separate criminal liability up to life imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil when directors used the WFOE as a mere instrument to evade tax: directors and the de facto controller become personally liable for restitution of the evaded tax and penalties out of personal assets, the legal representative and responsible directors are subjected to STA serious-tax-violation blacklisting (publication of identity and entry-exit restriction under the Exit Administration Law), travel bans prevent departure from China until tax liabilities are settled, and where the evasion was organized by the board, the persons directly in charge face imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201 plus fines and confiscation of personal gains.
Shell Company and Artificial Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and Public Notice 2018 No. 9: the STA may disregard the shell's separate existence, re-characterize the hub as a permanent establishment of the foreign parent, reassess taxable income in China, impose the statutory 10% (or 20% where treaty relief is denied) withholding on deemed distributions, and levy a transfer-pricing adjustment penalty of 10%-30% of the adjusted tax plus interest at the benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points. Where the arrangement is found to have no commercial rationale, treaty benefits are retroactively denied.

Director personal liability: Civil veil-piercing: directors who signed sham service agreements or enjoyed personal benefits from the diverted consolidation fees are jointly and severally liable for the WFOE's unpaid EIT, WHT and penalties to the extent of the diverted benefits. Personal liability extends to travel blacklisting and exit bans; in criminal cases where the shell structure was designed to defraud the tax authority, the architect directors face up to 7 years' imprisonment under tax-evasion and contract-fraud theories, and the STA's serious-violation publication scheme publicly identifies the legal representative, director and financial officer.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Reporting of Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 Articles 44-45 and TAL Article 62: penalties of up to RMB 10,000 for failure to file the related-party schedule and up to RMB 25,000 for fraudulent repetition; the STA will make a unilateral transfer-pricing adjustment, add an adjustment penalty of 10%-30% of the tax understated, and impose interest at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points on the adjusted tax. The failure to file contemporaneous documentation also reverses the burden of proof, permitting estimation of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil arises where the directors caused the documentation failure to conceal profit stripping: directors and the CFO are personally liable for the adjusted corporate tax and interest where the company was stripped of assets in anticipation of assessment; the legal representative is subject to border exit restriction and STA blacklisting; and if the TP understatement is intentional and crosses the Criminal Law Article 201 threshold, the responsible directors face imprisonment for tax evasion while the company's reputation and banking facilities (Airwallex and local clearing rails) are frozen under AML/integrity reviews.
