No statutory minimum registered capital applies to this WFOE under the PRC Company Law or Foreign Investment Law. E-government filing portal

For this WFOE structure, the entity is a Chinese resident limited liability company. Upon issuance of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) 18-digit identifier is automatically generated and serves as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration through the National Electronic Tax Administration Portal (e-Tax), selecting the competent tax authority, confirming general VAT taxpayer status or small-scale taxpayer status, and applying for VAT invoice quota and tax control authorisation. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic tax registration - mandatory upon business license issuance. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies with a 3% (or reduced 1%) levy rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods physically leaving China through customs declaration are eligible for 0% VAT and full refund of input VAT, provided the exporter has general taxpayer status and the goods are officially recorded on an export customs declaration. Qualifying cross-border services (including franchise licensing and overseas royalties supplied to a foreign franchisee) are eligible for zero-rating or exemption where the service recipient is an overseas enterprise, the services are rendered and consumed entirely outside China, and the taxpayer has filed the cross-border taxable service exemption/zero-rating filing with the competent tax authority before claiming the treatment.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (Customs Bill of Entry for Export)
  • Original Bill of Lading (ocean/air/road) with onward shipment evidence
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Bank receipt confirming inward foreign exchange for the export consignment (Export Proceeds Collection Slip with FX settlement record)
  • Foreign exchange payment/remittance certificate issued by a licensed bank in China for the export proceeds
  • Export VAT invoice (FDEI or special VAT invoice) endorsed with zero rate
  • Cross-border Taxable Service Exemption/Zero-Rating Filing (Filing Certificate) where service components are included

General VAT taxpayers must use the State Taxation Administration's national electronic invoice service platform to issue Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (FDEI) and special VAT invoices. Every invoice is transmitted in real-time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system, carries a unique electronic tax control number and QR authentication code, and is cleared against the central tax database before the buyer can legally use it for input VAT credit. Business systems must integrate with the e-Tax portal via STA-certified fiscal API interfaces; no paper or offline invoice is permitted for B2B transactions.

Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and the Measures for the Administration of Export Tax Rebates, any export zero-rating claim that fails audit or is proved fraudulent triggers retrospective clawback of all VAT refunded (including input credits), imposition of a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and administrative fine from 50% to 500% of the falsely obtained refund. If the false export scheme is determined by the tax authority or customs to constitute fraudulent export tax rebate, criminal liability under the Criminal Law applies, with penalties ranging from fixed-term imprisonment to life imprisonment in aggravated cases, plus confiscation of property. The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly conduct random cross-verification of export declaration data, shipping records, and FX repatriation within the 180-day window and may extend audits retroactively to all previously zero-rated transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is conditionally convertible on current account items and tightly controlled on capital account items. Since 1996, China maintains current account convertibility, so royalty payments, trade settlement, service charges, and interest can move cross-border once genuine trade receipts are verified by the bank and SAFE. All current account payments require an underlying contract/invoice and are subject to post-event verification. Capital account items, including FDI equity injections, domestic loans from offshore lenders, dividends, and liquidation proceeds repatriation, require SAFE registration, approval, or filing, and are not freely convertible. Foreign shareholders' capital must enter through a designated FDI capital escrow/foreign exchange settlement account, be verified by the receiving bank, and only be converted into RMB against actual business expenses. Profit remittance by a WFOE is permissible via current account channels after annual financial audit, final tax settlement, and issuance of tax payment certificates; dividends are also subject to withholding tax and SAFE remittance filing. Export receipts are not subject to mandatory conversion but must be repatriated onshore and recorded in SAFE's monitoring system. Overall, convertibility is genuine but only through licensed banks, with purpose codes and documentary audit trails enforced.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, unless an extension is granted by the bank or SAFE in specific circumstances. The current statutory mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent for ordinary current account trade receipts, because China abolished blanket surrender requirements; however, all receipts must be credited to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account held by the WFOE, and any conversion into RMB must be backed by the export invoice, customs declaration, logistics documents, and the SAFE trade item matching system. Retained foreign currency may be used for eligible current-account payments.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), under the policy direction of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the central regulator for all foreign exchange, cross-border settlement, and capital account flows within mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / not legally viable for primary treasury use. Mainland China does not recognise foreign fintech EMIs as licensed payment institutions or deposit-taking banks under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Online Payment. Such EMI platforms cannot open domestic RMB settlement accounts, cannot clear CNY through CNAPS/CIPS, and cannot act as the receiving bank for FDI capital injection or outbound forex remittances. They may be used only as ancillary customer-facing technology rails when partnered with a licensed Chinese bank, and must comply with data localisation and cybersecurity assessment rules. The WFOE's treasury must therefore rely on tier-1 domestic banks; any purported EMI corporate account should be rejected in AML underwriting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The FDI capital injection must be remitted from the shareholder's overseas bank account via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 message to the WFOE's dedicated capital escrow account (FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account) held with a SAFE-qualified bank. The remittance should use the purpose code 'FDI Equity Injection' or the corresponding Chinese direct investment code approved by SAFE, and must reference the WFOE's FDI registration certificate. After receipt, the WFOE files the actual receipt under SAFE's management system, obtains a bank-issued foreign investment capital verification letter, and completes required investment information reporting to the local Commerce Bureau. For a limited liability company structured in 2024, paid-in capital must be injected within the time limit set by China's Company Law and the articles, generally no more than five years. The bank will monitor the use of the capital account funds and require payment against actual business transactions before converting the escrowed foreign currency into RMB for operating expenditure.For import of franchise equipment, tea leaves, syrups, and branded packaging, the WFOE can use SWIFT MT103 for low-value service fees, royalties, and ordinary trade payments under an invoice or contract; Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight or usance) are recommended for large capital equipment imports; Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are suitable for established supplier relationships. Every outbound current account payment must be executed via a licensed bank with genuine trade documentation, including contract, invoice, customs declaration, and transport/tax records where applicable. Outbound royalties to a foreign franchisor require a registered licensing agreement, tax withholding, and SAFE remittance application; direct capital account outward payments require separate approval and are not routed through routine trade clearing.Onshore CNY forward contracts with licensed banks under the real demand principle, covering only projected trade or royalty exposures documented by underlying contracts.FX swaps combining spot and forward transactions for converting USD/HKD capital into RMB and reversing upon genuine dividend or trade transactions.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed by the overseas parent entity as a hedge of offshore RMB exposure, because the onshore WFOE cannot access speculative NDF markets.Cross-currency swaps restricted to hedging the currency mismatch of an actual offshore loan facility of the WFOE, with the notional amount not exceeding the loan principal and interest.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: WFOEs are unconditionally subject to annual statutory audit by a PRC-licensed CPA firm irrespective of revenue, total assets, headcount, or balance sheet size. The mandate arises from the Law on Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprises and its Implementing Rules. For other Chinese companies, statutory audit is compulsory for: (i) listed public companies; (ii) financial institutions (banking, insurance, securities, funds); (iii) state-owned enterprises and state-controlled mixed-ownership companies; (iv) companies designated by SAMR or the Ministry of Finance for targeted supervision; and (v) entities applying for special administrative licensing or public bidding where audited financial statements are required by law. No general national quantitative thresholds (turnover/assets/headcount) exempt a WFOE from the mandatory audit requirement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where controlled transactions in a tax year involve tangible asset transfers over RMB 200 million, or intangible asset transfers, financial asset transfers, loans, or services over RMB 100 million; Master File is required when aggregate controlled transaction value in any fiscal year exceeds RMB 1 billion. A WFOE must also file the Annual Related Party Transaction Report together with the annual EIT return regardless of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply offshore-type economic substance regulations to ordinary WFOEs, but Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form doctrine empower tax authorities to disregard artificial arrangements. The WFOE must perform its core income-generating activities, including franchise contract negotiation, quality control, syrup formulation, procurement, logistics, franchisee marketing, and management of related-party risks. If these CIGAs are conducted outside China or by the foreign parent while all profits are booked in the WFOE, the tax authority may recharacterize the entity as a conduit, deny treaty benefits, and assess tax on the real beneficiary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Artificial Round-Tripping Structure

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 is the general anti-avoidance rule authorizing special tax adjustments and denial of tax benefits; additional CIT plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge may be assessed. Administrative blacklisting under the joint punishment memorandum on tax collection restricts the entity's financing, tax clearance, and exit from China.

Director personal liability: If directors sign false annual financial statements or cause the shell company to deny obligations, courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 23 and impose personal liability for company debts and unpaid taxes. Directors can also be placed on the individual tax blacklist, receive travel bans, and be disqualified from serving as senior managers.
Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion via Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41-44, the tax authority may recharacterize intercompany payments, deem excessive royalties as non-deductible distributions, and impose interest on underpaid tax. If the conduct amounts to tax evasion, TCAL Article 63 permits recovery of the underpaid tax plus a fine of 0.5 to 5 times the tax amount.

Director personal liability: Executives who intentionally create false related-party records may not hide behind the corporate entity; PRC Company Law Article 23 allows personal asset restitution for misappropriation, and Criminal Law Article 201 can subject controlling officers to imprisonment of up to seven years with personal fines for willful tax evasion.
Tax Evasion, Money Laundering, and False Accounting of Franchise Cash Flows

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 201 and TCAL Article 63 impose liability for unpaid tax and fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; criminal penalties can range from up to three years' imprisonment for minor evasion to up to seven years for substantial evasion where the amount exceeds 30% of tax payable and is above RMB 100,000. The Anti-Money Laundering Law may additionally impose penalties up to RMB 5 million for non-compliant payment transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable for tax debts where they personally received or controlled suppressed cash flows; civil restitution can attach to personal assets; the director will be blacklisted by tax authorities and subjected to travel restrictions; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment up to seven years plus confiscation of property used in money laundering.
