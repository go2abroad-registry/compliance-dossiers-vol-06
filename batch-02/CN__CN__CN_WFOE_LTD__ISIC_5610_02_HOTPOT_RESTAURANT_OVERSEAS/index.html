Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required under PRC law for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise; registered capital must be determined by the actual business needs and subscribed by the shareholder, with a statutory contribution schedule not exceeding five years from incorporation unless sector-specific rules impose a shorter period. No blocked escrow account is generally required for a restaurant WFOE, but the local Market Supervision Administration may require proof that the registered capital is sufficient to fund the initial lease, renovation, equipment and working capital of the hotpot dining operation. Foreign exchange capital injection through a bank capital-account shall be recorded under the SAFE foreign exchange registration after the Business License is issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Inspection Certificate for premises open to the public before business commencement, issued by the Fire and Rescue Department, covering fire extinguishers, evacuation route signage, emergency exit doors, smoke detection, automatic sprinkler system and fire load approvals. A structural load survey is required for installation of heavy hotpot buffet furniture, walk-in freezers and suspended exhaust ducts. Occupational safety clearance from the local workplace health authority includes kitchen ventilation and heat checks, anti-slip flooring, non-slip shoe requirements, fume mask fit-testing in cleaning zones, and food-handler health certificates for all kitchen staff.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), including its national digital platform: the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) accessible through www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese WFOE (wholly foreign-owned enterprise), the entity is incorporated under SAMR with an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days after the business license is obtained, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the jurisdiction's Electronic Tax Bureau; in many localities the USCC is automatically transferred to STA so no separate tax registration certificate is issued. The WFOE must then complete VAT taxpayer status confirmation (small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer), register payroll and social contribution withholding, obtain Customs registration with the General Administration of Customs if it will export goods, and complete foreign exchange registration with SAFE through a designated bank for export sales collections. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment but with China-source tax exposure, the payer is required to withhold PRC tax and the non-resident may obtain a statutory tax identity by filing a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent STA office using a country-of-residence certificate, contract, and payment details; the authority then assigns a non-resident taxpayer file number or records the non-resident as a withholding taxpayer. The PRC VAT system is a unified national VAT; there is no separate VAT ID issued beyond the USCC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise for corporate income tax and is taxed on worldwide income at 25%; it is not a pass-through entity.

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide check-the-box pass-through elections for WFOEs or hybrid entity classification elections; anti-avoidance rules for hybrid financing and mismatched arrangements are applied through general tax administration remedies, not elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Any entity with a SAMR business license must complete tax registration regardless of turnover. For VAT general taxpayer status, mandatory registration is required once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold an entity may be classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Optional general taxpayer registration is available on application where business capacity and accounting systems satisfy STA requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China by a general VAT taxpayer, such as branded hotpot condiment packs, qualify for 0% VAT with input-credit retention and refund when the goods are actually loaded for export, customs clearance is completed, export foreign exchange is repatriated, and matching records are maintained. Services performed and consumed wholly outside mainland China, such as restaurant meals served by overseas branch operations, are outside PRC VAT and do not require zero-rating; cross-border service income may be zero-rated or exempt under STA cross-border services rules if supplied to an overseas client and properly documented. The WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer to claim zero-rating on goods; small-scale VAT taxpayers generally receive export exemption without input-VAT refund on exports.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export Bill of Entry equivalent) issued by the General Administration of Customs of China showing the WFOE as exporter
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export and destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration description and quantity
  • Special VAT invoices from domestic suppliers supporting input VAT paid on exported condiment products
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt confirmation or inward remittance certificate evidencing collection of export sales proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract or purchase order evidencing commercial terms and foreign buyer identity

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved systems. Fully digital electronic VAT invoices are generated on the STA Electronic Invoice Platform and transmitted in real time for tax-control matching under the Golden Tax Phase IV system; no additional physical fiscal stamp is applied to these digital invoices. Large taxpayers may integrate via secure API to the electronic platform. Physical paper special VAT invoices, where permitted, require STA-approved tax-control equipment and must be synchronized with STA. For export zero-rating claims, invoice data, customs declaration data, and export receipt data are cross-matched by STA and the General Administration of Customs.

Where export zero-rating or VAT refund is claimed, the STA together with Customs and SAFE may verify physical export, logistics, invoice, and receipt records. If evidence is fraudulent, false, mismatched, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory deadline, VAT is assessed at the domestic rate with refund clawback, late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export tax-refund claims may also expose responsible officers to criminal liability under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, and false export declarations may trigger penalties under the Customs Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime. The RMB is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, remittances, and profit repatriation, in accordance with IMF Article VIII obligations; however, capital account transactions remain subject to comprehensive supervision and licensing. A China-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is treated as a Chinese domestic legal person for foreign exchange purposes, and all cross-border fund movements must be reported to SAFE through designated banks. Inbound foreign equity contributions require a SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration and must be routed into a capital account deposit; funds may then be converted into RMB only for approved business purposes. Outbound investment to finance overseas hotpot restaurants requires the China WFOE to obtain Outward Direct Investment (ODI) approval from the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) and the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), with a separate SAFE registration for the actual remittance of offshore currency. Profit repatriation to foreign shareholders is permitted after annual tax settlement and independent audit, but requires proof of after-tax distributable profits and payment of withholding tax, which may be reduced under an applicable double tax treaty. Export proceeds from condiment sales to overseas restaurants are current account income and must be repatriated to China; there is no mandatory conversion, but banks will verify the transaction against customs declarations and contract documentation under the digital foreign exchange receipt system. The combined effect is a lawful but administratively heavy conversion and remittance framework, where timing delays and documentary compliance are the principal obstacles.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days of the customs declaration date, except where SAFE has granted an extension for genuine commercial reasons. Since the 2012 current account reform, there is no mandatory surrender ratio; exporters may hold foreign currency in designated foreign currency accounts or sell it to a bank at market rates. All export proceeds must be recorded in the bank's digital foreign exchange monitoring system and reconciled with the China Customs declaration, invoice, and contract. The WFOE's condiment export ties to its overseas affiliates will be scrutinised for a valid commercial rationale, arm's-length pricing, and the actual intra-company trade flow.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-bank financial technology entities and Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) have no statutory authority in China to carry out cross-border foreign exchange settlement, capital account supervision, or foreign exchange trade. Only SAFE-licensed banks may execute cross-border transfers, handle capital control registrations, and provide reconciliation services. Therefore, offshore digital EMIs cannot satisfy the WFOE's banking requirements; all cross-border transactions must flow through a licensed domestic bank. Feasibility rating: legally blocked for any material capital account activity; current-account collection could theoretically be conducted via certain licensed payment institutions only under strict SAFE pilot rules, but for a corporate WFOE with OFDI, bank-only is mandatory.

Turnover: Mandatory for every Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise as a matter of legal form, with zero size exemption. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to non-resident enterprises; 0% WHT if paid to a qualified Hong Kong/Macau resident enterprise holding at least 25% capital (subject to enhanced substance and beneficial-ownership review under STA Announcement 2018 No. 9 and 2020 No. 3); 20% for non-resident individuals, reduced to 10% under most DTA treaty provisions; dividends exempt from CIT if paid to another Chinese resident enterprise

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 1.0 billion annual related-party revenue triggers mandatory Master File; RMB 200 million annual related-party asset transfers, RMB 100 million financial transactions, or RMB 40 million other related-party transactions trigger mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation; CbC notification threshold: consolidated group revenue ≥ RMB 5.5 billion for the previous fiscal year

Economic substance scope: China follows OECD BEPS Action Plan and imposes economic substance requirements through GAAR (Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 47), general anti-avoidance rules, and case-specific substance-over-form doctrine; a WFOE acting as a pure royalty-paying, procurement, or holding vehicle without personnel, premises, decision-making, or risk-bearing functions will face recharacterization under STA Circular 2017 No. 6 (special tax adjustment) and BEPS Action 8-10; Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a hotpot group include: local restaurant operation, supply-chain sourcing decisions, quality control, brand management, franchising supervision, and risk assumption for perishable inventory and food-safety liability; all intercompany agreements must align with actual control and financial capacity; entities with no CIGA face deemed dividend reclassification, disallowed expense deductions, and 10% WHT on recharacterized distributions; Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as formalized in other jurisdictions (e.g., UAE, BVI) do not apply in Mainland China, but the Chinese GAAR and treaty abuse rules impose equivalent commercial substance tests for tax-resident claims and DTA benefit access

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Accounting & Related-Party Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Art. 63/64: fine of 50%–5x of underpaid tax; EIT Law Art. 47 and STA Special Tax Adjustment (Circular 2017 No. 6): mandatory recharacterization of profits, interest adjustment, and 5-year statute of limitations extended to 10 years for evasion; if amount exceeds RMB 100,000 and accounts for more than 10% of taxable income, criminal Tax Evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil via PRC Company Law Art. 20: director/shareholder causing evasion to the detriment of tax creditor may be personally liable for unpaid taxes; criminal liability: company's legal representative, CFO, and actual controllers may be prosecuted under Criminal Law Art. 201; imprisonment up to 3 years for evasion of RMB 100,000–1 million and >10% of taxable income, escalated to 3–7 years for larger amounts; personal assets seized for fines if commingled corporate funds; travel ban and blacklisting by STA and Exit-Entry Administration (no departure from China until settlement or security deposit)
Abusive DTA Claim and Beneficial Ownership Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefit under STA Announcement 2018 No. 9 and 2020 No. 3 (Beneficial Ownership Test); assessment of full 10% WHT differential plus 0.05% daily late surcharge; under General Anti-Avoidance Rules (EIT Law Art. 47 and Implementation Regulations Art. 120), tax authority may recharacterize the entire arrangement; penalty of 50%–5x of underpaid tax under Tax Administration Law Art. 63; possible administrative fine of RMB 10,000–100,000 for failure to maintain TRC

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and parent signatories who knowingly sign false treaty-benefit declarations may be deemed accomplices in tax evasion; personal civil liability for the tax shortfall and interest if corporate assets insufficient under Company Law Art. 20 (alter ego doctrine); blacklisting of legal representative and travel restriction; criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 for false tax filing to reduce tax; imprisonment up to 7 years for high-amount avoidance plus forfeiture of personal assets
AML & Cross-Border Payment Integrity (Anti-Money Laundering / Terrorist Financing / Sanctions Violation)

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law Art. 47: administrative fine up to RMB 5 million and suspension of business for financial institutions; Penalty for foreign-exchange evasion under SAFE regulations: fine up to 30% of illegal amount (circumvention) or 5%–10% of unreported transaction; Criminal Law Art. 191: conviction for money laundering with imprisonment up to 10 years; if involved in terrorist financing, Art. 120-1 imposes imprisonment up to 15 years; in serious cases, WFOE registration may be revoked and business license cancelled

Director personal liability: Personal criminal liability of directors and beneficial owners under Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering) and Art. 120 (terrorism); unlimited personal asset forfeiture and restitution orders; OFAC/UN sanctions designation if transaction touches U.S. or EU sanctions list could freeze personal assets and prohibit travel; POS compliance failures linked to tax evasion could trigger independent director personal liability for knowing participation; a travel ban and exit restriction is automatically imposed during criminal investigation by the Exit-Entry Administration for cross-border payment fraud
