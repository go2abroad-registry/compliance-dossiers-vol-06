Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), exercised through the local competent tax bureau at the WFOE's place of registration; all mandatory filings and registrations are performed online through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (the unified e-tax web portal operated by the STA).

E-government filing portal

Under the Five-Certificates-in-One reform, the WFOE obtains its statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) as follows: (1) incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) serving as the TIN; (2) automatic synchronisation of the SAMR registration record to the State Taxation Administration, generating the electronic Tax Registration Confirmation; (3) completion of the taxpayer profile, accounting method, and VAT general/small-scale taxpayer qualification via the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal; (4) registration of the VAT invoice-issuing system (fully digitalised e-invoice or tax-control equipment); and (5) where a foreign non-resident parent or offshore franchise counterpart earns China-source royalties or service fees, submission of the non-resident taxpayer registration and, where required, the Certificate of Tax Residence for reduced withholding under an applicable double tax treaty. No statutory minimum capital exists for the bakery WFOE; the foreign shareholding may be 100%.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD 690,000) require mandatory registration as a VAT General Taxpayer; sales at or below that threshold fall under the Small-Scale Taxpayer regime, and Small-Scale Taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are temporarily exempt from VAT under the Ministry of Finance and STA policy effective 2023 through 31 December 2027.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate applies where the WFOE is a VAT General Taxpayer holding a valid Foreign Trade Operator Filing, a customs registration code, and the export goods (fresh bakery products, Chinese pastries, frozen unbaked lines, and central-kitchen processed foods) are physically exported from Mainland China to a destination outside the PRC customs territory under an export customs declaration. The sale must be made to an offshore buyer, title must pass outside China, and the sales proceeds must be received in foreign currency and repatriated into the WFOE's export-proceeds settlement account. Input VAT on raw materials, packaging, energy, and logistics must be fully traceable for the export VAT rebate computation, and the export declaration, the special VAT invoice, and the foreign-exchange settlement record must satisfy the three-way match rule. Qualifying cross-border franchise royalties invoiced by the WFOE to non-resident franchisees are zero-rated as exported services where the intellectual property is granted to a non-resident, is wholly consumed outside China, and creates no domestic benefit or onward Chinese sale.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs clearance form with the electronic declaration number and release stamp issued by China Customs),
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical departure of the goods from Chinese customs territory,
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange settlement record evidencing the receipt of export proceeds in a permitted foreign currency by the WFOE,
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List with harmonised-system codes, quantities, unit prices, and transport seals matching the customs declaration,
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order showing the WFOE as exporter and the offshore entity as buyer,

Golden Tax Phase IV administration. All VAT invoices must be issued through the National Electronic Tax Bureau using the fully digitalised e-invoice (FDEI) module or through STA-certified tax-control equipment (tax U-key) with real-time upload to the national VAT invoice cross-check database. Each invoice carries a unique 20-digit invoice number, a full-information QR code, and the issuer's digital electronic seal; the digital clearance system validates the issuer, the transaction, and the recipient before the invoice is accepted. Red-letter reversal (credit notes) requires the prior digital clearance of the original invoice. Off-platform handwritten receipts and unsequenced paper invoices are prohibited; the three-way match among the export customs declaration, the VAT invoice, and the foreign-exchange settlement record is the mandatory precondition for VAT deduction and export refund claims.

The State Taxation Administration continuously matches the export customs declaration, the VAT invoice set, the transport documents, and the foreign-exchange settlement data (three-way match) under Golden Tax Phase IV. A zero-rated claim is allowed only after physical exit is verified and the offshore remittance is settled. If the export is reverse-invoiced, the goods are reimported, the sales are deemed domestic by the tax authority, the foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day period without approved justification, or any supporting document is false, the tax authority will claw back the VAT refund already paid and impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the refund date to the date of recovery, together with an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the wrongly refunded tax; the export VAT refund qualification may be suspended for one to three years. Suspected fraud is referred for prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, carrying imprisonment of up to ten years where the fraud amount exceeds RMB 500,000. The retrospective audit limitation period is three years for good-faith claims and five years or more for fraudulent evasion or false documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and a differentiated RMB convertibility framework. Current account items such as goods trade, service trade, royalties, and ordinary current transfers are effectively convertible after the submitting bank completes a real-demand authenticity review under SAFE rules. Capital account items, by contrast, remain strictly controlled. A WFOE needing to receive paid-in registered capital from its overseas shareholder must first complete the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment information registration and then open a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account; the inbound remittance must be initiated by the registered foreign shareholder itself and coded with a permitted investment purpose, otherwise the bank will reject the MT103. Dividend and profit remittance are treated as current account outflows but require prior annual audit of the WFOE financial statements under PRC accounting standards, payment of corporate income tax, completion of any dividend withholding tax, a board resolution declaring profits, and proof of cumulative distributable reserve balances. Converted RMB from the capital account must be applied in accordance with the company's registered business scope and cannot be invested in non-business securities, entrusted loans, or real estate outside permitted usage. In short, RMB is broadly convertible on current account and narrowly controlled on capital account, so all cross-border liquidity inflows and outflows should be pre-approved and fully documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China removed mandatory percentage-based surrender of export proceeds in 2012; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. However, foreign-currency receipts from the export of goods by the bakery chain must be repatriated to a licensed domestic bank and reported through SAFE's trade monitoring system. For ordinary goods trade, the export proceeds are expected to be collected within 90 days after the customs declaration date, with an extension to 180 days permitted for deferred-payment, high-value capital equipment, or custom-engineered central-kitchen installations; any longer period must be registered with SAFE through the receivables and payables monitoring system. The exporter may retain the foreign currency in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert it to RMB at market rates, but the bank will verify the underlying export contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and trade purpose code. No fixed proportion of export earnings must be sold to the central bank.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated administration by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail; offshore fintech EMIs are legally barred from servicing the WFOE's onshore requirements. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open RMB settlement accounts, cannot receive foreign direct investment capital, cannot complete SAFE FDI registration, cannot perform tax withholding verifications, and are not recognized as settlement banks for customs or trade payment purposes. Viability rating: Low, effectively prohibited for core banking. An offshore EMI may only be used as a non-core auxiliary collections tool outside China to aggregate occasional overseas franchisee receipts before those funds are repatriated through a licensed domestic bank, and even then all anti-money laundering, beneficiary, and cross-border remittance limits must be respected. The compliance and operational risk of relying on an EMI for the WFOE's capital account or current account clearing is unacceptable.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

After the WFOE obtains its business license, the treasurer must register the entity in SAFE’s Foreign Direct Investment system, either directly or through the selected domestic bank under delegated registration, then open a Foreign Exchange Capital Account and an RMB Basic Deposit Account. The overseas parent must initiate a SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE’s capital account with the remitter name exactly matching the registered foreign shareholder; no third-party remitter is permitted. The bank will apply the appropriate SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code for FDI equity investment from its approved code table and only then release the funds for conversion to RMB at the prevailing market spot rate under the payment-based settlement regime. Converted RMB must be parked in the capital settlement account and used strictly for approved business purposes, including bakery equipment purchases, lease, payroll, raw-material procurement, and franchise development. A PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report after each paid-in capital tranche, and the WFOE must update SAFE within the reporting period if paid-in capital changes. Any contribution exceeding registered capital requires an approved capital increase with the Administration for Market Regulation and amended SAFE registration before remittance.The WFOE should use three main clearing rails for outbound payments: first, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for advance payments, small-value trade settlements, and service/franchise royalty remittances; second, commercial letters of credit issued by the onshore bank for high-value central-kitchen equipment, packaging machinery, and large ingredient purchase contracts; and third, documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms for lower-risk trade flows. All goods-trade payments require the WFOE to be registered in the trade inventory and to provide the underlying contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration (for imports), and matching trade purpose code. For outbound service-trade disbursements such as master franchise royalties, brand training fees, and IT system charges, the company must submit a service-trade payment application, a tax payment certificate proving withholding of corporate income tax and VAT, the governing service agreement, invoice, and a valid SAFE purpose code; above certain thresholds, tax-authority verification may be required before remittance. Use of cross-border MT103 should include clear payment narratives and the correct SAFE code to avoid false-flagging by correspondent banks.Onshore deliverable RMB FX forward contracts with licensed domestic banks against real current-account or capital-account underlying exposure under SAFE’s genuine-demand principle, including hedging of imported equipment costs, ingredient purchases, and future dividend/profit remittances.Onshore RMB cross-currency swaps or FX swaps with licensed Chinese banks for rebalancing USD/CNY, EUR/CNY, and HKD/CNY cash-flow mismatches, limited to the amount and tenor of contracted trade or investment cash flows.Plain vanilla RMB currency options, either calls or puts, with a licensed bank counterparty for premium-limited, non-leveraged hedging of identified FX risk and prohibited for speculative or open-ended positions.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for the onshore WFOE as direct hedging instruments; only the offshore master-franchisor parent may use NDFs in its own offshore books, while the onshore entity must rely on deliverable onshore forwards or swaps under a NAFMII/ISDA master agreement with an approved bank counterparty.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable as a primary treasury or clearing rail; offshore fintech EMIs are legally barred from servicing the WFOE's onshore requirements. Offshore EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open RMB settlement accounts, cannot receive foreign direct investment capital, cannot complete SAFE FDI registration, cannot perform tax withholding verifications, and are not recognized as settlement banks for customs or trade payment purposes. Viability rating: Low, effectively prohibited for core banking. An offshore EMI may only be used as a non-core auxiliary collections tool outside China to aggregate occasional overseas franchisee receipts before those funds are repatriated through a licensed domestic bank, and even then all anti-money laundering, beneficiary, and cross-border remittance limits must be respected. The compliance and operational risk of relying on an EMI for the WFOE's capital account or current account clearing is unacceptable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds are exceeded: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, intercompany service transactions, or financial asset transfers; an aggregate Master File is required for related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion in the tax year.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not operate a GCC-style Economic Substance Regulations code, the STA applies a General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Notice 2009 No. 2. A WFOE that is nominally a bakery-franchise operator but performs none of the core income-generating activities - central kitchen production, franchisee management, POS collection, inventory risk, or customer credit risk - can be re-characterized as a conduit. Such entities may be denied DTA benefits, stripped of TP arrangements, and deemed to have taxable Chinese-source income attributable to the activities actually performed by the non-resident parent. The modern application is informed by OECD BEPS Action 6 and the substance-over-form analysis used in the determination of PE exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax amount plus daily 0.05% late-payment interest. Under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, if evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax due, the responsible persons are subject to up to three years' imprisonment and fine; for RMB 250,000+ evasion or more than 30% of tax due, imprisonment for three to seven years and fine.

Director personal liability: The tax authority may pierce the corporate veil where the WFOE is operated as a mere instrument for tax evasion: directors and de facto controllers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties under PRC Company Law Article 20; they may be placed on the State Administration for Market Regulation serious tax dishonesty blacklist, with exit-entry restrictions, higher loan rejection, loss of business licenses, and individual criminal liability as direct responsible officers (up to seven years' imprisonment).
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The STA may invoke GAAR under EIT Law Article 47 to re-characterise the structure, deny treaty benefits, disregard the separate entity, and reassess taxable income. Interest and penalties under Tax Collection Law Articles 63 and 64 apply; Article 6 of the GAAR Notice permits adjustments to effective taxation by reasonable methods, including disregarding the entity where its principal purpose is tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Courts have lifted the corporate veil where a WFOE is found to be a personal or parent-controlled sham used to evade taxes or debts. Directors may be ordered to satisfy company tax liabilities from personal assets; they face deletion from the national enterprise credit registry and travel bans as persons with untrustworthy acts; if the shell is used for invoicing fraud or money laundering, Criminal Law Articles 175, 191 and 201 apply with imprisonment of three to ten years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41, the STA may make a transfer pricing adjustment to the arm's length result. Article 44 allows deemed taxable income where no documentation is provided. The adjustment is subject to a 5-year revisiting window (10 years in fraud cases). Under Tax Collection Law Article 63, the TP adjustment creating an underpayment is penalised at 50%-500% of the underpaid tax plus interest, regardless of whether the original return was filed.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false TP disclosures or direct the WFOE to ignore documentation requirements may be assessed as actual controllers under Company Law Article 20. They can be held personally liable for the amount of tax to the extent the corporation is dissolved without paying the adjusted liability; they can be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving jurisdiction for cases involving significant tax underpayment; where the scheme involves false invoices or fraudulent cross-border remittance, criminal prosecution for evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may result in imprisonment of up to seven years.
