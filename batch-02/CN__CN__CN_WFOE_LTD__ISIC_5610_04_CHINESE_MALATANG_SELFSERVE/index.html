Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered locally through the China Electronic Tax Administration (Electronic Tax Bureau) e-portal; VAT invoice issuance and verification are processed through the STA's VAT Invoice Management System.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT taxpayer identification number. The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license; (2) complete tax registration with the competent local STA within 30 calendar days of business license issuance by submitting the tax registration information via the China Electronic Tax Administration portal or the tax service hall; (3) the SAMR data is pushed to the STA and the USCC becomes the taxpayer identification number, with no separate TIN certificate issued; (4) a legal representative or tax agent authenticates the identity and activates the electronic tax account; (5) maintain a financial accounting system, bank account record, and register for VAT invoice issuance quotas; (6) apply for VAT general taxpayer status when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or voluntarily. For a foreign non-resident entity with no establishment in China, the Chinese payer of China-sourced income obtains a withholding tax registration and files a contract with the local STA; the non-resident is assigned a tax identification/withholding record for the specific income stream, and the payer deducts VAT and corporate income tax at source.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the WFOE is a resident limited liability enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income; it is also a VAT taxpayer; no pass-through treatment is available)

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist in Chinese tax law. The WFOE may elect to be a VAT general taxpayer even before exceeding the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable-sales threshold in order to claim input VAT credits; alternatively it may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to the simplified collection method. No other entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 0 for WFOE tax registration because establishment and business license create immediate tax registration obligation; RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for VAT general taxpayer classification, with voluntary election below that threshold. The applicable VAT rate for catering and franchising/royalty services is 6% for a general taxpayer; sales of tangible goods are generally 13%; small-scale taxpayers use the simplified rate of 3% (with temporary preferential reductions as announced).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for zero-rated VAT or export exemption with the exemption-credit-refund method when the goods are declared to China Customs as exports, actually leave the customs territory, the sale is made to an overseas buyer, ownership passes abroad, and the foreign currency proceeds are repatriated into China in accordance with SAFE verification requirements. Qualifying cross-border services (including royalties and franchise licensing to overseas franchisees where the benefit is consumed wholly outside China) may also be zero-rated or VAT exempt under the cross-border taxable services framework established by Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent STA announcements, provided the recipient is an entity outside mainland China and no domestic consumption occurs. The WFOE must keep contemporaneous evidence connecting the invoiced supply, the customs declaration (for goods), the transport document, and the inbound foreign currency settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form issued by China Customs (export Goods Declaration/Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export from China and arrival abroad
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / foreign exchange receipt verification statement (FIRC or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing collection of export proceeds)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract with the overseas buyer
  • VAT invoice issued under the national VAT Invoice Management System for the export/zero-rated supply or, for export rebate, the STA export tax refund declaration/application dossier
  • Enterprise customs registration certificate and VAT taxpayer identity confirmation

China mandates the use of the national VAT Invoice Management System for all VAT invoices. Taxable supplies must be invoiced with either a paper Special VAT Invoice or a Full-Value Digital Invoice; invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time through the electronic invoice service platform. The system applies anti-fraud digital stamping, validates taxpayer IDs, compares invoice amounts with VAT returns and payment records, and imposes quota/face-value controls. No private offline invoice format is accepted. POS transaction records must be reconciled to VAT invoices; a POS receipt is not a substitute for a statutory VAT invoice.

The STA and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) cross-check customs export data, VAT return data, foreign exchange settlement data, and bank receipts. A zero-rated or export-refund claim that is not substantiated by the required customs, transport, invoicing, and foreign-exchange-repatriation evidence is rejected, and any refund already granted is clawed back with late-payment interest. Fraudulent export tax refund or fraudulent zero-rating is punishable under the Law of the People's Republic on Administration of Tax Collection with recovery of the refund and a monetary penalty of at least one and up to five times the refunded tax; the tax authority may also suspend the taxpayer's export tax refund eligibility for a statutory period. Where the amount is large, criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds applies, including fines and imprisonment; the statute of limitations is extended or suspended for concealment and fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility for trade and service transactions, while capital account items remain heavily regulated and subject to prior approval, filing, or registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). The renminbi is not fully freely convertible offshore; onshore conversion for the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must follow the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration for Direct Investment (FDI) process. Capital injections in foreign currency are registered and can be converted through a domestic designated foreign exchange bank under the capital account, but the resulting RMB funds must enter a Renminbi Capital Account (capital verification account) and be applied strictly to the declared business purpose, with fund monitoring until the capital verification and subsequent use reports are filed. Profit repatriation, including dividends and branch remittances, is permissible as a current account transaction if the WFOE has completed annual audit and tax clearance, but remittance abroad requires submission of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, board resolution for profit distribution, and a SAFE profit remittance form. Export proceeds from the malatang franchise licensing and supply chain operations are subject to the SAFE export proceeds repatriation requirement that overseas receipts must generally be returned to China through the domestic bank accounts of the exporting entity within the statutory period, but under recent reforms many qualifying current account receipts are exempt from mandatory conversion and may be retained in foreign currency accounts subject to the receipt report timeline. The assessment confirms that while ordinary operating payments such as royalties, trade payables, and service fees can be cleared cross-border, any outbound capital flow not tied to genuine current account transactions, such as offshore loans, quasi-equity structures, or offshore guarantee payments, will trigger strict SAFE scrutiny and may require SAFE registration or cross-border guarantee filings. The practical hurdles include lengthy documentary checks by the custodian bank, the requirement of a designated SAFE bank of record, and the risk of freezing of funds if expense purposes are inconsistent with the registered business scope of the WFOE, especially for brand licensing royalties where the underlying intellectual property license agreement must be filed with SAFE or MOFCOM.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts, including all wire payments for the sale of food products, subfranchise licensing fees and supply chain goods, must be repatriated to a domestic SAFE-registered bank account in China within 180 days of the date of shipment or service completion, unless the bank grants an extension under SAFE Circular 28/2020. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under the post-2012 SAFE reform; exporters may retain foreign currency in a settlement account or convert to RMB at their discretion, but the account position must be reported to SAFE on the normal monitoring platform and any retention exceeding the bank's open position limit requires justification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low: Offshore digital EMI platforms are not legally permitted to open domestic hold accounts or provide onshore settlement services for a Chinese mainland WFOE without a licensed banking partnership and SAFE cross-border payment qualification. Fintech EMIs may only serve as technical conduits to tier-1 Chinese banks for data transmission and remittance instruction formatting, but they cannot replace a domestically licensed bank for FX settlement, capital verification, or clearing with China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS). Any use of a non-bank EMI for direct USD/RMB conversion or outbound repatriation is barred by the PBOC Non-Bank Payment Institution rules; therefore the WFOE must anchor all clearing onto a licensed onshore bank with a designated SAFE code.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign direct investment capital must be wired through SWIFT MT103 or CNAPS to the WFOE's foreign currency capital account at the designated SAFE bank, with remittance instructions clearly citing the SAFE FDI verification code and purpose code 'Investment in operating enterprise (foreign direct investment)' per the PBOC/SAFE classification. The remitter must be the registered foreign shareholder and the payment reference must include the WFOE's unified social credit code and the SAFE FDI registration number. After funds arrive, the bank will assist in completing the capital registration and the RMB conversion, then transfer proceeds to the Renminbi Capital Account for capital verification. The WFOE must retain the incoming payment advice, SAFE Capital Account Confirmation, CPA capital verification certificate, and strictly use the funds only for purposes within the registered business scope and the approved capital-use schedule.Outbound disbursements for trade settlements should be routed through SWIFT MT103 cash transfers for royalty and service payments, with Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) or Documentary Collections (MT400/MT420) used for the export of packaged broth, sauces, and self-serve equipment to overseas franchisees. All outbound remittances require an approved SAFE payment purpose code: 'Intangible royalties and license fees' for franchise licensing revenues, 'Trade settlement' for goods import/export, and 'Services rendered abroad' for consulting and support services. The clearing must pass through the CIPS rails for RMB cross-border settlements or through SWIFT for USD and other foreign currencies, with the bank acting as the direct clearing member. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File/contemporaneous documentation is mandatory if the annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions, RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, or RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers. Master File preparation is triggered when the aggregate amount of related-party transactions exceeds RMB 1 billion or when the China taxpayer is part of a multinational group with a total group annual consolidated revenue exceeding RMB 5.5 billion and conducting covered transactions in China.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime of the type used by zero-tax offshore hubs, the tax authority applies substantive economic analysis under the Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 45, and 47, and under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in TCAL Article 122. A WFOE deriving income from a Malatang franchise licensing business must maintain staff, premises, books and records, contract negotiation, management decisions, accounting functions, and core franchising and menu-development activities inside China. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a franchise business are treated as occurring where strategic decisions regarding the trademark, brand format, franchisee selection, store standards, supply-chain oversight, and quality assessment are made. If a Hong Kong or other offshore entity is inserted merely to own the trademark and collect royalties while the WFOE performs all value-adding functions in China, the Chinese tax authority can reattribute the profit to China, disregard the offshore entity for tax purposes, impose a deemed taxable presence, and assess interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Articles 32 and 63 require full recovery of the underpaid tax plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose a fine of 50% to five times the tax shortage. Criminal Law Article 201 treats evasion of a relatively large tax amount above 10% of assessable tax as criminal tax fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to three years and a fine; evasion is considered huge and above 30% of assessable tax, increasing the penalty to three to seven years' imprisonment and a fine. In addition, the taxpayer is placed on the joint punishment 'dishonest taxpayer' list and faces revocation of business licenses, asset seizures, and cross-border travel restrictions.

Director personal liability: Where the director or supervisor authorises false accounts, commingles personal assets with company assets, authorises forged invoices, or causes the company to be unable to pay tax, Chinese courts will pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and hold the director jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax and related damages. Tax authorities may impose travel bans and exit controls under TCAL Article 44 against the legal representative and responsible director, and in a serious evasion case the director faces personal criminal prosecution, monetary fines, and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Profit Stripping Through a Shell Conduit

Statutory sanction: The Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 45, and 47 and the Special Tax Adjustments Regulations authorise the tax authority to recharacterise the transaction, disallow the inflated deductions, adjust profit to the arm's-length amount, apply the controlled foreign corporation rules, and impose interest on the adjusted tax. TCAL Article 122 confirms that arrangements without a reasonable commercial purpose are recharacterised according to their economic substance. Where documentary obligations are not met despite the RMB 40 million service or RMB 100/200 million intangible/tangible thresholds, the tax authority may presume taxable income, impose a fine of up to RMB 100,000 for failure to furnish contemporaneous evidence, and apply an additional special tax adjustment interest rate.

Director personal liability: The directors who sign intercompany agreements and approve payments that lack substance can be held personally liable for the shortfall if the asset stripping renders the company insolvent, under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine in Company Law Article 20. The tax bureau can blacklist the responsible legal representative, freeze personal bank accounts where funds were misappropriated, and restrict exit from China under cross-border tax enforcement. If the scheme also involves fraudulent refunds or falsified contracts, the directors face criminal fraud or tax evasion charges with imprisonment up to seven years and restitution of all diverted assets.
Unlawful Dividend Distribution and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 37 and Article 40 impose withholding obligations on the WFOE as payer. Under TCAL Article 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold or fails to pay over withheld tax is subject to administrative recovery, a daily surcharge on unpaid tax, and a fine of 50% to three times the tax that should have been withheld and paid. The non-resident shareholder remains liable for the tax, and the WFOE cannot deduct the dividend WHT as a corporate expense. Illegal distributions made while the company has no distributable profits are void and subject to Company Law penalties and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who declare and approve an illegal dividend without required reserves, loss offsets, or tax withholding can be ordered by the court to repay the distributed funds to the company and are liable for damages to creditors under Company Law Article 152 and Article 166. In the cross-border context, the legal representative may be listed as a dishonest violator, subjected to exit denial and visa suspension, and, when the company is liquidated without settling withholding liabilities, can be prosecuted under Criminal Law for refusal to pay tax or for false liquidation, leading to imprisonment and personal criminal fines.
