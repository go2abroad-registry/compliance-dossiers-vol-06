Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online services administered through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the competent local branch tax bureau of the district where the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is registered.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE automatically receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as its Tax Identification Number. Through the integrated multi-certificate ('one-form, one-certificate') registration mechanism, the SAMR shares registration data with the STA, and tax registration is completed without a separate physical TIN certificate. The WFOE must then activate its Electronic Tax Bureau account, obtain its digital tax certificate/login credentials, complete the VAT taxpayer status filing (small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer), register payroll and individual income tax (IIT) withholding obligations, and file any non-resident withholding registrations for foreign shareholders. A non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China that earns PRC-source income is assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number by the competent tax authority as part of withholding registration; a non-resident shareholder claiming treaty benefits must provide a tax residency certificate.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box regime or hybrid-mismatch entity election mechanism; a WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese tax-resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax on worldwide income. The only relevant election is indirect-tax status: a taxpayer with annual taxable turnover at or below CNY 5,000,000 may elect general VAT taxpayer status, while a taxpayer exceeding that threshold must be registered as a general VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 0 for basic VAT/tax registration: every WFOE must be tax-registered immediately upon incorporation through the integrated SAMR/STA registration process. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Below that threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer (statutory simplified levy rate 3%, with no input VAT credit), or voluntarily elect general taxpayer status for full input VAT crediting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China customs territory are zero-rated where the title passes to an overseas buyer, the goods physically leave China under a customs export declaration, and the export foreign-exchange proceeds are received and reported through the SAFE trade monitoring system. Cross-border services are zero-rated or VAT-exempt only if they fall within the statutory 'cross-border taxable services' list under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent STA announcements, are supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual, and are wholly consumed outside China. Domestic ghost-kitchen/delivery catering services, including food prepared and delivered in China, do not qualify for zero-rating because the place of supply is China. Qualifying separately identifiable offshore revenue, such as virtual-brand licensing or digital services supplied to a non-resident with no China-related use, may be zero-rated only when the legal, documentary, and foreign-exchange conditions are fully satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) with electronic customs-clearance release
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill for air cargo) showing shipment from China to the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt entry in the SAFE trade-monitoring system
  • Zero-rated commercial invoice and packing list
  • Underlying export sales contract, purchase order, or service agreement evidencing the cross-border transaction

China mandates centralized electronic invoice clearance under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled e-invoicing infrastructure; the Fully Digitized Electronic Invoice regime requires each invoice to carry a unique invoice number, anti-counterfeit QR code, and digital signature, with real-time transmission to the STA. General VAT taxpayers issuing special VAT invoices and ordinary digital invoices must use the Electronic Tax Bureau portal or SAT-certified tax-control systems; no private or offline invoice issuance is permitted. For ghost-kitchen/delivery operations, consumer-facing settlement invoices and platform-based transaction documentation must also pass through the same centralized clearance and digital-stamping process.

The STA and China Customs cross-match export declarations, logistics records, customs release data, and bank foreign-exchange receipts on a real-time basis. If zero-rating or export-tax-refund status is claimed without full substantiation, the STA will claw back the VAT refund and disallow any future zero-rating benefit, assess daily late-payment interest of 0.05%, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulent export or cross-border zero-rating claims are referred for criminal investigation under Criminal Law Article 204, exposing responsible persons to fines, confiscation of illegal gains, retroactive unlimited recovery periods, and imprisonment up to life imprisonment in severe cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is classified as conditionally convertible on the current account, fully compliant with IMF Article VIII current account obligations since 1996, but subject to a non-convertible capital account regime administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Under the Foreign Investment Law and its implementing regulations, foreign-invested enterprises, including WFOEs, are permitted full current account access for invoicing and trade settlement, while capital account transactions require a dedicated FDI foreign currency capital account registered with SAFE. The capital injection received in foreign currency must be credited to the capital account and converted into RMB within a compliance bank; conversions are governed by the 'real demand' principle and cannot be pre-hedged beyond the approved contract. Profit remittance by a WFOE is treated as a current account item after the audited annual accounts are prepared; the board must pass a distribution resolution, the entity must obtain tax payment receipts (including corporate income tax withholding certification for dividend distributions to the non-resident parent), and the local SAFE-branch licensed custodian bank will verify the authenticity of the distribution through the digital foreign exchange administration system. Hurdles include the absence of free floating convertibility, documentation-intensity of every conversion, and the requirement to use the SWIFT-cleared Chinese clearing system for repatriations rather than any unlicensed fintech lane.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE administration rules, export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of the export customs declaration; enterprises may apply to SAFE for an extension in justifiable commercial circumstances. Mandatory forced surrender of export proceeds to the state has been abolished since the 2012 reform; the statutory regime now imposes a 0% mandatory conversion ratio, meaning the ghost kitchen operating entity may elect to credit 100% of export proceeds to an RMB settlement account or retain them as foreign currency in an export foreign exchange settlement account. In practice, treasurers maintain retention balances below the Foreign Exchange Settlement Account upper-limit quotas and settle the majority of receipts to RMB to fund local payroll and supply chain costs. All proceeds must be tracked via the SAFE Plant Monitoring System and every credit must carry a valid trade purpose code from the current account trade catalogue.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Foreign and domestic electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, Currenxie, PingPong and Payoneer do not hold banking licences capable of registering FDI capital accounts or performing SAFE-verified forex settlement. PBOC regulations require corporate entities receiving foreign exchange for capital account purposes to use a bank authorized by SAFE; hence EMIs are legally barred from servicing the core inbound and repatriation flows of a WFOE. Practically they retain marginal viability only for ancillary cross-border marketing procurement in small-value current account items, yet their service agreements with Chinese legal entities are subject to PBOC licensing scrutiny; therefore the treasury blueprint mandates primary settlement via licensed commercial banks and keeps EMI usage outside statutory treasury flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection shall be executed by the overseas parent via SWIFT MT103 with the remittance fields explicitly tagged: Purpose Code 62012 (FDI capital injection/Investment) per the PBOC/SAFE current account catalogue; the beneficiary bank statement must reference the SAFE FDI registration number (or the 2024-standard FDI-FECRS). The beneficiary account is the dedicated Foreign Currency Capital Account (FCA) opened at the selected tier-1 bank; upon credit, the bank will require a written extraction application and a capital account use declaration (banking electronic verification) prior to conversion into RMB. The conversion must occur within the legal retention period, and a Chinese CPA performs capital verification producing the Capital Verification Report (investment verification) which the bank files to retain the 'paid-in capital' record. Any excess over the registered investment amount cannot be booked; application to SAFE for capital increase must precede subsequent dividend distributions.For operational disbursements — ingredient procurement, cloud kitchen equipment, royalty payments to brand owners and cloud service subscriptions — the blueprint prefers SWIFT MT103 for ordinary supplier invoices, Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight 90-day confirmed) for large frozen food or equipment imports, and Documentary Collections for lower-risk recurring consignments. All payments require the corresponding current account filing / electronic trade verification with underlying invoices and contracts on the SAFE Plant system; Chinese outward remittance proposals in RMB above USD 50,000 must pass bank compliance sampling. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign and domestic electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, Currenxie, PingPong and Payoneer do not hold banking licences capable of registering FDI capital accounts or performing SAFE-verified forex settlement. PBOC regulations require corporate entities receiving foreign exchange for capital account purposes to use a bank authorized by SAFE; hence EMIs are legally barred from servicing the core inbound and repatriation flows of a WFOE. Practically they retain marginal viability only for ancillary cross-border marketing procurement in small-value current account items, yet their service agreements with Chinese legal entities are subject to PBOC licensing scrutiny; therefore the treasury blueprint mandates primary settlement via licensed commercial banks and keeps EMI usage outside statutory treasury flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transfers, or RMB 100 million for receipt or payment of services. Master File preparation is mandatory where the consolidated group annual related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion. China also requires the annual related-party transaction disclosure form to be filed with the CIT return.

Economic substance scope: China has no formal Economic Substance Regulation but applies the general anti-abuse rule under CIT Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form principle. For a ghost-kitchen WFOE, minimum economic substance requires a real leased kitchen, kitchen employees, payroll, delivery platform contracts, revenue settlement in China, risk control and strategic management. If the WFOE is a mere shell, tax authorities may refuse treaty benefits, deny expense deductions, or impose withholding on deemed dividends. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be located in China and supported by board meetings, employment contracts and operating records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Unreported Digital Platform Revenue

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 63 (Tax Administration Law) imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05%; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability for tax evasion on top of tax penalties. For VAT false-invoicing, Criminal Law Article 205 may apply with monetary fines equal to the falsely-issued invoice amount.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for restitution of the diverted revenue, unpaid taxes and related surcharges. Directors may be placed on the tax-related blacklist, barred from leaving China, deprived of personal assets, and sentenced to up to seven years' imprisonment for aggravated evasion. Corporate bank accounts and delivery platform accounts will be frozen.
Shell/Conduit WFOE and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Article 47 (general anti-abuse rule) and Article 48, the tax authority may recharacterise the structure, deny the royalty deduction, deem the profit remittance as taxable dividends, assess underpaid tax, and impose special tax adjustment interest. Treaty benefits may be denied and the full statutory 10% dividend WHT with penalties may be applied.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false substance declarations or filed shell annual returns may be personally liable under civil fraud principles, ordered to restore diverted assets, placed on the 'serious dishonest person' list with travel and banking restrictions, and if the shell was used to obtain tax refunds or export rebates, criminally prosecuted and imprisoned.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and TP Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 requires arm's length pricing; Article 44 allows the tax authority to determine taxable income based on available comparable data if TP documentation is not produced; Article 48 imposes special tax adjustment interest on additional tax. Penalties under Article 63 may reach 50% to 500% for fraudulent underpayment, and a criminal referral under Criminal Law Article 201 is possible if the mispricing is deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved inflated management fees and signed inaccurate TP files may be personally liable for the additional tax, interest and penalties if the company is insolvent due to profit stripping. They may be blacklisted as defaulters, have personal property attached, and face criminal prosecution for abetting tax evasion, with imprisonment of up to seven years.
