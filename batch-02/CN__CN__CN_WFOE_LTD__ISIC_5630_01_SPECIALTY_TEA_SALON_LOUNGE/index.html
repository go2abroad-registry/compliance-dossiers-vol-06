Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and registration via the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the national electronic invoice integrated service platform.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally serves as the enterprise's taxpayer identification number (TIN). Step 2: Within 30 days of the business license issuance, complete taxpayer registration at the competent local tax authority through the E-Tax portal, thereby creating the tax registration profile. Step 3: Register for value-added tax (VAT) status, electing either the Small-Scale Taxpayer or General VAT Taxpayer status, and obtain a tax control UKey or digital e-invoice authorization. Step 4: For export activities, additionally complete customs declaration registration with the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China, foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and, if required, the exporter tax refund filing record. Step 5: For non-resident entities without a permanent establishment, a tax registration number can be obtained through withholding registration under the tax authority's temporary taxpayer code mechanism; however, a resident WFOE receives its TIN via the USCC automatic issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. The People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box pass-through classification regime; all limited liability enterprises are irrevocably subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax on their worldwide income. The only elective mechanism is the VAT taxpayer status election between Small-Scale Taxpayer and General VAT Taxpayer, and the availability of the special accounting/invoice election for small-scale taxpayers.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Mandatory registration generally applies within 30 days of incorporation regardless of turnover. For General VAT Taxpayer mandatory status, the threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold, enterprises operate as Small-Scale Taxpayers (invoicing rate 1%-3%), with VAT exemption available for small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (or quarterly sales not exceeding RMB 300,000).

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exported tangible goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate if they are moved out of the customs territory of China under an export customs declaration, and the seller is a registered General VAT Taxpayer. Cross-border services supplied to overseas recipients, including qualifying offshore digital services and services consumed wholly outside China, may also qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption. Specialty tea products physically exported, including packaged loose-leaf tea and ceremonial tea vessels, qualify upon satisfaction of the customs export filing, collection of foreign exchange, and provision of the required documentation bundle within the statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations of the People's Republic of China, RMB is fully convertible for current account items such as trade settlement, foreign-invested enterprise working capital, and profit remittance once the relevant tax and audit requirements are satisfied. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, debt financing, and portfolio investment, are subject to permission-based administration under the SAFE negative list scheme. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must register its FDI capital with SAFE and open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a domestic designated bank. Incoming equity contributions may be settled into RMB only against genuine operational expenditure and verified invoices, in accordance with the 2015 SAFE settlement-in-priority policy. Profit repatriation to offshore shareholders requires a certified audit report, a foreign-investment annual report, corporate income tax clearance, and a board resolution; withholding tax at 10% or a treaty-reduced rate is withheld by the remitting bank. There are no current-account convertibility surcharges or exchange controls within the float band; however, large or suspicious flows trigger enhanced scrutiny. Export receivables must be repatriated to China and are subject to trade verification, but can remain in foreign currency or be converted at spot rate without mandatory surrender. Overall, the regime requires bank-mediated, evidence-based FX transactions.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
China has phased out mandatory export FX surrender ratios. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China through designated banks within 90 days of customs declaration, unless extended with genuine trade evidence, and may be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at market rate at the exporter's discretion, subject to SAFE trade verification.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Not viable as the primary banking rail. Under PRC banking regulations, non-bank fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks are not licensed to open onshore corporate settlement accounts for a registered WFOE. A WFOE must maintain its statutory capital account, RMB current account, and FX trade settlement accounts with a licensed domestic commercial bank. Fintech EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex may only provide ancillary cross-border payment initiation or expense card services to connected affiliates outside China, and such usage must not replace the domestic bank account or be used to bypass SAFE administrative controls. Onboarding of a WFOE on a fintech EMI platform is therefore legally constrained and operationally redundant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The foreign investor opens an FDI foreign exchange capital account at a Chinese domestic designated bank after completing SAFE foreign investment registration and obtaining the FX registration certificate. Inbound equity funds are wired via SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE-approved FDI capital inflow purpose code; on arrival, the bank verifies the remitter against the registered investor, enters settlement in the SAFE foreign exchange administration system, and converts into RMB only upon presentation of genuine expenditure documents such as contracts, invoices, and board resolutions. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200,000,000 for tangible intercompany transactions; RMB 100,000,000 for intangible property and financial asset transfers; RMB 40,000,000 for intercompany service transactions. Group companies with consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion must also prepare Country-by-Country Reporting.

      
Economic substance scope: The SAT applies the substance-over-form principle under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EITL Article 47. A WFOE must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in mainland China, including physical premises, full-time qualified employees, genuine business decision-making, risk management, and operational control. Without these, treaty benefits can be denied, and the entity may be re-characterized as a shell company, exposing undistributed profits to immediate 10% dividend WHT on deemed distributions.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / False Tax Declaration
        
Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus seizure of assets for unpaid amounts. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years for minor evasion and 3 to 7 years for amounts exceeding RMB 1 million or with prior criminal records.

        
Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil to hold directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties. Directors face personal asset restitution, travel bans (出境限制), blacklisting on the SAT credit system, and criminal imprisonment for personally orchestrating the fraud.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Violations and Thin Capitalization
        
Statutory sanction: EITL Articles 41 and 46 empower the SAT to perform special tax adjustments, re-characterizing income and imposing additional tax plus interest at the benchmark rate plus 5%. False reporting of such payments triggers penalties under Tax Administration Law Article 63.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-compliant intercompany payments or sign falsified related-party transaction schedules are personally liable for the shortfall. They may face civil asset seizure and criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion if the conduct is shown to be willful.

      

      
      

        Shell Company and Treaty Abuse (Lack of Economic Substance)
        
Statutory sanction: Under General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EITL Article 47) and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, the SAT will deny treaty benefits and impose the full statutory 10% WHT on cumulative distributed profits, plus interest and penalties for improper tax planning.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced to collect the differential tax directly from the beneficial owner and directors. Directors are personally liable for fraudulent asset transfers, face blacklisting from future directorship roles, and can be subject to criminal penalties for organizing a sham arrangement to unlawfully reduce tax.
