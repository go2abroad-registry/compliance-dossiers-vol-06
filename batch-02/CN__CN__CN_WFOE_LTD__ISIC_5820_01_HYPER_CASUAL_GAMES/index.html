Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. China imposes no minimum paid-up capital or escrow block for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The registered capital must be subscribed in full in the Articles of Association and, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, each shareholder must pay the subscribed contribution within five years from the date of incorporation unless a shorter period is prescribed by law. The contribution may be cash or permitted non-cash property, and must be traceable into the Company's bank account or transferred in accordance with the Articles; no blocked escrow account is required at formation, but excessive or inconsistent capital declarations may be queried by the registry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the local State Tax Service bureau in the WFOE's registered district. Online portal: the STA Electronic Tax Bureau / National Electronic Taxpayer Service platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain the Business License and 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: Within 30 days, complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal or in person at the competent tax service bureau, filing the standard tax registration information confirmation, articles of association, shareholders' register, legal representative identity document, registered address proof, bank account opening certificate, and accounting system records. Step 3: The tax authority issues the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number, which for all registered enterprises is identical to the USCC, and activates VAT status. Step 4: Determine VAT classification: mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification applies if annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; small-scale taxpayer treatment applies below that threshold. Export-oriented software and game publishers should register as general VAT taxpayers proactively to access zero-rating and refund procedures. Step 5: Complete post-registration foreign exchange compliance by opening a settlement account at a designated bank and completing the SAFE foreign direct investment registration through the bank. For a foreign non-resident shareholder receiving dividends, no separate PRC TIN is required; treaty relief is claimed through the competent authority procedure and a foreign tax residence certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax at the statutory 25% rate on worldwide income. There is no pass-through taxation. For VAT, it is a VAT taxpayer from incorporation, classified as either small-scale or general VAT taxpayer according to turnover and registration rules.

Available election forms

None for entity-level CIT classification; China does not operate a check-the-box regime. The only relevant election is VAT: an eligible small-scale taxpayer with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer if it maintains sound accounting and can accurately calculate input VAT and output VAT. The election once made normally cannot be withdrawn for 36 months.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration at RMB 5,000,000 in annual VAT taxable sales. Tax registration itself is mandatory from the date of business license issuance regardless of turnover; the threshold only determines small-scale versus general VAT taxpayer classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For tangible goods exported from PRC customs territory, the exporter must prove physical exportation through the China International Trade Single Window, present an export declaration, conclude sales with an overseas buyer, and repatriate the export proceeds in foreign currency. For cross-border digital services, VAT zero-rating applies only to services expressly listed in the pilot circular implementing the Business Tax-to-VAT reform, such as software services and information system services supplied to overseas organisations or individuals and wholly consumed outside China. For this hyper-casual game publisher, zero-rating can be claimed only if the AdMob and AppLovin revenue stream is correctly characterised as an export of a listed zero-rated software or information service. If the revenue is characterised as advertising services supplied to overseas ad platforms, the supply may be VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, and the WFOE must not claim a 0% rate without a legal basis. Documentary proof must show the recipient is outside China, the service is consumed outside China, the contract is in place, and foreign exchange is received from abroad.

  • Export goods: Customs Export Goods Declaration exit-clearance copy issued through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Export goods: Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from PRC customs territory
  • Export goods: Export commercial invoice / VAT special export invoice and packing list
  • Export goods: Bank-issued foreign exchange settlement voucher / export proceeds collection confirmation evidencing receipt from the overseas buyer
  • Cross-border services: Signed cross-border service contract and detailed service description; overseas app-store or ad-platform statements proving consumption outside China; bank foreign exchange inflow voucher; export zero-rate service declaration filed via the Electronic Tax Bureau

General VAT taxpayers must issue Special Value-Added Tax Invoices through the STA Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are transmitted to the tax authority at issuance. The Golden Tax System Phase IV performs near-real-time risk matching against customs, corporate income tax, foreign exchange and upstream and downstream invoice data. China uses an electronic invoice data-control model with unique invoice numbers and nationwide digital anti-fraud monitoring rather than a centralized pre-approval fiscal stamping regime.

The STA, local tax bureaus, Customs and SAFE cross-match export declarations, invoices, foreign exchange receipts, transport documents and corporate income tax records. Where zero-rating or export refund is claimed without sufficient substantiation, the authorities will claw back the refunded VAT and reassess output VAT at the applicable rate, charge a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax underpaid under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where fraudulent export refund documents are used, the case may be referred to the criminal justice authorities under Criminal Law Article 204, with penalties including imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls. The current account is largely convertible for bona fide trade in services and goods, enabling routine inbound ad-monetization collections from Google AdMob and AppLovin to be settled through a licensed domestic bank without case-by-case SAFE approval, provided the service trade transaction is truthfully declared and evidenced. The capital account, however, remains heavily regulated: foreign direct investment into the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must be effected through an FDI capital settlement account, registered with SAFE through the receiving bank, and foreign exchange settlement into RMB is permitted only for approved business purposes with actual use verified. Profit remittance and dividend outbound transfers require a certified audit, tax payment proof, and board shareholder resolution, all subject to bank review for authenticity and AML screening. Export proceeds and foreign currency receipts generally must be repatriated into China and are subject to a mandatory surrender or retention mechanism; for most current-account receipts, enterprises may retain or settle the foreign exchange through the banking system within the statutory collection timeline under the trade in services declaration framework. There are no offshore direct convertibility channels available to the onshore WFOE, and synthetic RMB instruments or offshore cash pooling are not permissible for onshore treasury operations without specific SAFE pilot approval. Hyper-casual gaming revenues with high digital advertising volumes may raise bank scrutiny on valuation and counterparty concentration, necessitating platform settlement statements and auditable revenue logs. Overall, onshore liquidity can be managed efficiently through a well-licensed domestic tier-one bank, using standard SWIFT-current-account clearing and purpose coded remittances, with only the capital account requiring formal SAFE registration and periodic compliance reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and service-trade foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic account within the statutory timeframe; for goods exports, collection of proceeds is generally due within the customs declaration period and bank audit cycle, and for service exports, the bank verifies the service-trade contract, invoice, settlement statement, and tax zero-declaration before allowing conversion to RMB. Mandatory full surrender applies in the sense that onshore entities must sell or settle foreign currency through the domestic banking system if conversion to RMB is required, although retention of foreign exchange in the entity's own foreign currency account is permissible within SAFE limits; there is no fixed percentage surrender split other than the requirement that any foreign exchange received be brought back onshore and subject to verified bank settlement under current account rules.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks are not licensed as domestic deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot provide onshore CNY clearing, SAFE reporting, or statutory capital-account services for a mainland WFOE. A China-incorporated entity must maintain its primary settlement relationship with a licensed domestic commercial bank; offshore EMI rails may only be used as supplementary treasury tools for foreign currency facilitation outside China, and they cannot legally replace the domestic banking clearing system. Attempting to route core operating collections or capital injections through an unlicensed offshore EMI creates serious AML and sanctions compliance risk and will be rejected by the WFOE's local bank and auditor.

The parent company must remit the capital injection via SWIFT from its own account to the WFOE's FDI capital settlement account at the selected tier-one bank, with payment purpose clearly coded as equity investment under the capital account. The receiving bank performs SAFE registration verification, anti-money laundering screening, and capital account declaration before crediting; the WFOE then converts foreign currency to RMB under the bank's foreign exchange settlement system with each conversion linked to actual business expenditures. After the funds are credited, a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, and the WFOE must file the capital verification record with the local administration of market regulation. For operational foreign currency receipts from AdMob and AppLovin, the inbound SWIFT payments are treated as current-account service trade collections and are routed to the operating settlement account with service trade declaration codes, supporting platform statements, and Chinese tax payment certificates as required.For outbound operating payments such as software licensing, user acquisition advertising, overseas cloud hosting, and consulting fees, the WFOE should settle through its domestic bank using SWIFT MT103 for clean payments, with precise service trade purpose codes, contract invoices, tax withholding certificates, and bank compliance checks. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks are not licensed as domestic deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot provide onshore CNY clearing, SAFE reporting, or statutory capital-account services for a mainland WFOE. A China-incorporated entity must maintain its primary settlement relationship with a licensed domestic commercial bank; offshore EMI rails may only be used as supplementary treasury tools for foreign currency facilitation outside China, and they cannot legally replace the domestic banking clearing system. Attempting to route core operating collections or capital injections through an unlicensed offshore EMI creates serious AML and sanctions compliance risk and will be rejected by the WFOE's local bank and auditor.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by the Chinese WFOE to its non-resident corporate shareholder, imposed under Article 37 of the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law and Article 3 of the Withholding Tax Regulations. This 10% is a final withholding tax payable by the non-resident shareholder, with the WFOE acting as statutory withholding agent; a DTA may reduce the rate to 5% or lower if the shareholder is the beneficial owner and meets all treaty conditions.

Box 3 / Line 3 - Taxes and Surcharges (A100000.03) Report Chinese turnover taxes and surcharges such as value-added tax (VAT), city maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge, and local education surcharge. CIT itself is not deductible in this box. Foreign income tax paid by the WFOE on overseas advertising revenue may be available as a foreign tax credit only if properly substantiated, but must not be booked as an operating expense. Withholding tax paid on outbound dividends, interest, or royalties is not a deductible CIT expense for the WFOE. VAT on digital advertising services and cross-border royalty/technical service payments must be withheld at statutory rates if the service provider is non-resident, and proper VAT vouchers must be retained.
Box 4 / Line 4 - Selling Expenses (A100000.04) Report user-acquisition spend, advertising brokerage fees, platform distribution commissions, promotion costs, and marketing campaign costs. Advertising expenses paid to foreign ad networks or related marketing affiliates must be arm's length and supported by traffic evidence, campaign invoices, and payment records. Payments to an offshore marketing affiliate that has no employees, no traffic services, and no CIGA in the country will be disallowed. Where the non-resident payee supplies services wholly outside China, the WFOE may still be required to withhold VAT and income tax if a Chinese-source service element exists or if the recipient is a related party with no substance.
Box 5 / Line 5 - Administrative Expenses (A100000.05) Report general overhead, management, legal, audit, office rent, and depreciation of fixed assets. Management fees paid to foreign parents or group service companies are deductible only when the service is actually rendered, the charge is arm's length, and contemporaneous evidence shows both the performance and benefit to the WFOE. A pure allocation of global overhead without a service contract, invoice, and functional support is non-deductible and will be treated as a deemed dividend to the non-resident recipient, triggering dividend withholding tax and penalty interest. Director travel and entertainment expenses have statutory deduction limits and must be separately tracked.
Box 6 / Line 6 - Financial Expenses (A100000.06) Report interest expense, loan arrangement fees, foreign-exchange losses, and other financing charges directly linked to the WFOE's debt. Interest paid to non-resident lenders or intercompany treasury affiliates is subject to China-source interest withholding tax at 10% unless a DTA reduces the rate. The deduction of related-party interest is limited by the thin-capitalization ratio (2:1 debt-to-equity for non-financial enterprises), and any excess is recharacterized as a dividend for CIT purposes, resulting in additional withholding tax. The WFOE must file a related-party transaction schedule with the CIT return and maintain prescribed-form documentation proving arm's length interest rates, comparable overdue rates, and the use of funds.
Box 7 / Line 7 - Investment Income (A100000.07) Report dividend income from investee companies, interest, and other investment returns. For the WFOE, dividends received from a qualified resident Chinese company may be exempt from CIT, but dividends from a foreign subsidiary are generally taxable unless a participation-exemption condition applies. If the WFOE acts as an agent or conduit for advertising revenue and the non-resident parent owns the right to the cash flows, the profit may be imputed to the parent, and any retained profit distributions from the WFOE to the foreign parent will be deemed dividends subject to withholding tax. The line must not be used as a dumping ground for undiscriminated advertising settlements.
Box 8 / Lines 8-11 - Other Income, Adjustments, and Taxable Profit (A100000.08 to A100000.11) Report non-operating income, non-operating expenses, fair-value changes, and reconcile accounting profit to taxable profit after all add-backs and deductions. Non-resident-related deductions must be segregated into the special adjustment schedules; tax authorities may require the Special Tax Adjustment Schedule (A105000 and related schedules) for cross-border related-party items. The WFOE must also complete the Related Party Transaction Reporting Form (A100000 schedule) with aggregate amounts by transaction type, jurisdiction, and counterparty. Taxable profit is the base for final CIT; if the WFOE has not yet made a distribution, there is no direct dividend WHT at the time of filing, but once the statutory retained earnings are declared as a dividend to the non-resident shareholder, WHT will arise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million threshold for tangible-asset related-party transactions, RMB 100 million threshold for intangible-asset related-party transactions, RMB 100 million threshold for financial-asset related-party transactions, and RMB 40 million threshold for other related-party transactions, each requiring a Local File under SAT Bulletin 2016 No.42; a Master File is generally required when the aggregate annual related-party transaction amount reaches RMB 1 billion or as the taxpayer exceeds the statutory master-file trigger in the same circular. The WFOE must also file the Annual Related Party Transaction Report (A100000 schedule) regardless of size if it has related-party transactions, and must prepare contemporaneous documentation before filing the annual CIT return.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations in the same form as Cayman/BVI, but the PRC CIT Law, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 47), and operational circulars enforce an economic-substance doctrine. The WFOE claiming a software enterprise or HNTE tax incentive must itself perform the core income-generating activities (CIGA) with local employees, offices, contractual parties, decision-making, and actual control over game development, user-acquisition, revenue collection, and risk assumption. If the WFOE is a shell with no local substance and merely holds the legal title to income while the offshore affiliate performs all functions, the tax authority may recharacterize the income as belonging to the offshore entity, apply special tax adjustments, deny treaty benefits, and assess penalties. A functional-risk analysis must assign profit to the entities that actually perform development, advertising, user operations, and publishing functions, and the WFOE must earn an arm's length return commensurate with those functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and GAAR Recharacterization of Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes Article 41 and Article 47 of the PRC CIT Law plus SAT Bulletin 2017 No.6 to reassess the related-party transactions, disallow the excessive deductions, and recharacterize the arrangement in accordance with its economic substance. The adjusted tax must be paid with commensurate interest, and under Article 122 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law the enterprise may be subject to a penalty from 50% to 500% if the recharacterization reveals a deliberate underpayment; the ten-year assessment period for special tax adjustments may apply to related-party transactions. The treaty benefit claim is denied and the WFOE receives a corrective notice requiring full tax, interest, and penalty to be paid.

Director personal liability: Where the WFOE has no genuine economic substance and the directors treated the entity as a sham conduit, courts and tax authorities may disregard the separate legal personality and hold the directors personally liable to the extent of the restored tax and interest. Directors who signed the false related-party agreements, transfer-pricing documentation, or board resolutions that misrepresent the true transaction may be subject to travel restrictions, blacklisting, and a civil civil-asset restitution judgment. When the artificial scheme exceeds the criminal threshold, the director may be charged with tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law and sentenced to imprisonment of up to seven years where the amount is particularly huge.
False VAT Invoicing and AML/Facilitation of Undocumented Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Article 22 of the PRC Administrative Measures for Invoices prohibits false issuance or acceptance; the tax authority may confiscate illegal gains and impose fines of RMB 10,000 to RMB 500,000 depending on the invoice amount and gravity. Article 205 of the PRC Criminal Law criminalizes the issuance of false special value-added-tax invoices: where the amount is large, the responsible person is liable to fixed-term imprisonment of more than three years but not more than ten years; where the amount is especially huge and the circumstances are especially serious, the sentence can be life imprisonment, together with criminal fines and confiscation. The VAT input credit is denied, CIT deductions are disallowed, and the WFOE must pay full tax, surcharges, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who arrange, authorize, or fail to prevent the issuance/acceptance of false invoices are directly responsible for the criminal offense; they are personally liable for the repayment of the unlawful VAT credit and for restitution of the underpaid CIT. Personal assets may be seized to satisfy the tax and penalties, and the director is subject to exit-controls, business-restriction blacklists, and exclusion from corporate management positions. Depending on the invoice amount, the director can be imprisoned for up to ten years or, in extreme cases of especially large fraudulent invoices, life imprisonment, with criminal fines imposed in addition to civil asset restitution.
