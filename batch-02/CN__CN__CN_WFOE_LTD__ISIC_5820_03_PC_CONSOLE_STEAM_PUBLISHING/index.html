Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address is not permissible as the sole registered office for a WFOE in China. The local SAMR, tax authority, and public security bureau require a fixed physical business premises evidenced by a valid lease agreement and landlord property ownership certificate or authorization. Banks invariably conduct a physical due-diligence visit before opening the corporate account and will reject a postal-only address lacking company signage, office equipment, and personnel. For an ISIC 5820 digital game publisher, a serviced office with a dedicated desk and access to landlord property documentation may be acceptable, but it is functionally a physical office and must support the statutory inspection standard.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law do not impose a statutory minimum registered capital for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company in the digital software publishing sector (ISIC 5820). Registered capital is subscribed as stated in the Articles of Association, and each shareholder must pay the subscribed contribution in full within five years from incorporation unless a shorter period is specified in the articles. No blocked escrow account is required by national law; capital contributions must be remitted into the company's foreign exchange capital account through approved banking channels and reported under the SAFE direct investment framework.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (the Electronic Tax Bureau online portal).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The non-resident investor incorporates the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the consolidated business license carries the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the WFOE's statutory PRC Tax Identification Number. Step 2: Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration and taxpayer information confirmation through the Electronic Tax Bureau, declaring its legal representative, registered address, business scope, bank settlement account, accounting policies, capital structure, and anticipated taxable activities. Step 3: The competent tax authority opens the taxpayer file, confirms the VAT taxpayer classification (general or small-scale), and grants electronic invoicing privileges through the Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Step 4: If physical exports are made, the WFOE must register with the China Customs Single Window and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade monitoring system through a designated foreign exchange bank so that export receipts can be collected and repatriated under PRC trade procedures. Step 5: A foreign non-resident shareholder without a Chinese establishment does not receive a separate PRC VAT ID; the WFOE is the resident taxpayer and acts as withholding agent for China-source payments, reporting the foreign entity's name, country of tax residence, and foreign tax identifier in the required withholding returns.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

For corporate income tax, PRC law provides no check-the-box election, fiscal unity election, or hybrid mismatch election; the WFOE is necessarily a separate entity-level taxpayer. For VAT, the available statutory election is between small-scale taxpayer treatment (applicable while annual taxable sales remain below RMB 5,000,000) and general VAT taxpayer treatment; general status is mandatory once annual taxable sales reach RMB 5,000,000, and may be voluntarily selected if the entity maintains proper accounting records. No pass-through or tax-transparent election exists for this limited liability WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; this is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold. Tax registration using the Unified Social Credit Code is compulsory upon incorporation, so the turnover threshold governs the compulsory conversion from small-scale taxpayer status to general VAT taxpayer status rather than the initial existence of a VAT registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, 0% VAT (export zero-rating and export-tax refund) applies where the goods are lawfully exported from the PRC customs territory, title and risk pass to a non-resident purchaser, the goods are not consumed in China, and the exporter can produce customs clearance records and bank-verified foreign exchange receipts. For digital game publishing and offshore services, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available only where the service or software right is supplied to a non-resident recipient, the recipient is located outside China, the service is wholly consumed outside China, and the foreign consideration is received from outside China. The WFOE must demonstrate that Steam/PlayStation sales are offshore supplies and not downloads by PRC domestic users; if digital services are partly used in China, only the offshore portion qualifies. The claim requires a cross-border contract, a platform settlement/payout report, bank receipts evidencing foreign currency inflow, and a non-resident tax/residency certificate from the overseas platform operator; zero-rating is never automatic.

  • Customs export declaration made through the China International Trade Single Window evidencing physical export clearance and the exporter-of-record status
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or equivalent international transport document proving the goods left the PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export contract/purchase order
  • Bank-issued export proceeds receipt and SAFE Cross-Border Receipts and Payments Declaration data evidencing repatriation of foreign currency to China
  • Cross-border service/publishing agreement, Steam or PlayStation Store platform settlement statement, and non-resident recipient tax residency certificate for digital/offshore service exports

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV digital fiscal-control and invoice-clearance framework. General VAT taxpayers must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices through the STA's Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform; every invoice is transmitted to the tax authority in near-real time and carries a uniform tax-control code, encrypted electronic seal, and digital signature produced by STA-approved credentials. The system performs automatic invoice matching, red/blue invoice reconciliation, and risk-based blocking of suspicious issuances; high-volume taxpayers may connect system-to-system via an API gateway, while all other taxpayers use the state portal. No private paper tax stamps are permitted, and issuers must obtain an electronic invoice allocation from the tax authority before issuing any taxable invoice.

The STA and SAFE cross-match customs declarations, VAT invoices, service contracts, platform payout records, and bank FX receipts through the Golden Tax IV risk-control system. If the supporting documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, backdated, or contradicted by invoice/platform data, the zero-rating is denied and the supply is recharacterized as a domestic taxable supply; the WFOE must immediately pay the applicable output VAT, file amended returns, and pay a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the overdue tax. Under Article 63 of the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection, successful tax evasion or false zero-rating claims trigger a penalty of 50% to five times the tax involved and full clawback of any refunded amount. Serious cases are referred under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund, which may result in imprisonment, fines, or life imprisonment for extremely large fraud; exporters also face downgrade in the STA export-tax-refund classification, cancellation of electronic invoice quota, customs penalties for false declarations, and public credit blacklisting.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under a managed floating exchange-rate regime, but the capital account remains tightly controlled. The WFOE may receive Steam/PlayStation digital publishing revenue as current-account service income after bank verification of genuine contracts, invoices, and platform payout statements, and may hold or convert such foreign currency at domestic banks at the prevailing market rate. Registered capital injections and any financing between the foreign parent and WFOE are capital-account transactions and require SAFE registration/FDI reporting, a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account, and usage restrictions under the FDI no-negative-list and positive-use rules. Profit remittance is possible only after annual statutory audit, payment of 25% Chinese corporate income tax, withholding tax on dividend distributions, and bank review of audited financial statements, board resolution, tax clearance, and SAFE payment verification; the remittance amount cannot exceed after-tax distributable profits. There is no blanket foreign-exchange surrender requirement, but all cross-border receipts must be repatriated into a licensed domestic bank account before conversion. Full RMB free convertibility outside the current account is not available.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules there is no fixed compulsory surrender ratio or mandatory conversion percentage for export receipts. Goods-export proceeds must be brought back into China and settled through the trade-foreign-exchange monitoring system, generally within 90 days of the export customs declaration unless an extension is approved. For the WFOE's digital publishing revenue from Valve Steam and PlayStation Store, the receipts are classified as cross-border services/electronic-license income; the WFOE must present the platform agreements, royalty statements, and invoices to the settlement bank, and proceeds must be deposited into the domestic current-account foreign-currency collection account. The WFOE may elect to retain the FX balance or convert the receipts to CNY at the onshore market rate, subject to verification of the underlying commercial transaction.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange regulator and licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs have low viability in China mainland. They are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to operate corporate RMB accounts, conduct SAFE FX verification, hold registered capital, or remit dividends from a WFOE, and a WFOE may not lawfully rely on them for primary treasury operations. P2P-style virtual-account aggregators or foreign EMI cards can only be auxiliary collection tools for overseas consumer receipts outside China; they cannot clear offshore Steam/PlayStation payouts into the WFOE's regulated banking perimeter or substitute for a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Any entity promising EMI-based settlement into a WFOE would create unlicensed payment activity under PRC regulations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All registered-capital injections must be wired by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent to the WFOE's designated domestic-bank Foreign Exchange Capital Account. Before dispatch, the WFOE must complete FDI status/SAFE registration with the branch bank and obtain the FDI identification number; remittance instructions must include the creditor/shareholder name and clearly state the purpose as foreign-invested enterprise equity capital injection with the applicable SAFE capital-project transaction code. Upon credit, the domestic bank records the receipt in the Foreign Exchange Capital Project Information System, and a PRC-registered certified public accountant issues a capital-verification report. The WFOE may then convert the capital or use it only for business-scope operations; capital cannot be diverted into securities, entrusted loans, RMB settlement of foreign debt, or non-business real estate unless in the permitted positive list.For ordinary licensing/royalty payments to overseas developers or platform-related service providers, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 with the underlying publisher agreement, proforma/invoice, withholding tax vouchers, and SAFE service-trade payment form. For high-value consignments of physical media, console accessories, or collector's editions, a commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT103 matching) issued by the domestic bank through its corresponding-beneficiary bank is available under Incoterms, with transport bills and inspectable shipping documents. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are viable only for trusted repetitive trade counterparties and require complete shipping set plus bank acknowledgment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs have low viability in China mainland. They are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to operate corporate RMB accounts, conduct SAFE FX verification, hold registered capital, or remit dividends from a WFOE, and a WFOE may not lawfully rely on them for primary treasury operations. P2P-style virtual-account aggregators or foreign EMI cards can only be auxiliary collection tools for overseas consumer receipts outside China; they cannot clear offshore Steam/PlayStation payouts into the WFOE's regulated banking perimeter or substitute for a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank. Any entity promising EMI-based settlement into a WFOE would create unlicensed payment activity under PRC regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: An annual intercompany related-party transaction amount exceeding RMB 200 million for total related-party revenue/assets/outbound payments (or RMB 20 million for financial assets/equity transfers) triggers mandatory preparation of a Master File; a Local File must be prepared when intercompany controlled transactions in a single category exceed RMB 40 million, or when the entity's related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million. For video game publishing, any intercompany royalty/service arrangement of any size must still be documented reasonably under the arm's-length principle; SAT may require special adjustments even below thresholds if overall profit indicators look distorted.

Economic substance scope: For a Chinese WFOE, the Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as known in offshore centers do not apply domestically, but China applies the substance-over-form doctrine embedded in SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018 (anti-treaty-shopping), General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under CIT Law Art. 120/47, and the 2022 OECD BEPS implementation practices. The WFOE must demonstrate that it performs functions (game development, local publishing, store management), owns assets (IP, contracts, equipment), and bears risks (market risk, credit risk, IP infringement risk) commensurate with its reported profit. If the WFOE merely executes risk-free routine functions while all IP income flows to an offshore low-tax affiliate, SAT may recharacterize the arrangement under the 'corresponding adjustment' and 'substance requirement' principles. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept, through DEMPE functions (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, and Exploitation) is used to attribute profit on embedded intangibles; for Steam/PlayStation publishing, storefront contracts, user data, marketing, and payment settlement activities performed in China are CIGA and require an arm's-length reward. Additionally, China issued Circular 5 of 2021 on TP administration for digital platforms: platform commissions and online-game related-party remuneration must be aligned with value creation and commercial substance; pure cost-plus remuneration for a Chinese developer/reseller is insufficient if the WFOE performs exploitation functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion via Falsified Revenue or Expense Records (Tax Evasion)

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law (TAL) Art. 63: payment of tax underpaid plus a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; TAL Art. 64: restoration of records and penalty up to RMB 50,000 for failure to file; late surcharge of 0.05% per day on unpaid tax. Criminal liability: PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion) imposes up to 3 years' imprisonment for evasion between RMB 100,000 and 1,000,000, and 3–7 years for evasion above RMB 1,000,000 (or where proportion to tax due is high).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Art. 20 (now Art. 23 under the 2023 revisions) if a director's control of the WFOE causes asset commingling, unjust profit diversion, or evasion of taxes, leading to unlimited personal liability for corporate tax debts. Directors involved in arranging false invoices or fraudulent refunds can be held jointly liable under TAL Art. 57 and subject to personal asset freezing; in serious cases, a criminal conviction under Art. 201 leads to direct imprisonment; the tax authority may also blacklist the director under the 'Joint Disciplinary Action on Tax Payment Integrity' list, with restrictions on high-consumption travel, plane/train travel, property purchases, and exit from China.
Shell Company Substance Failure and Anti-Avoidance Recharacterization (GAAR / Beneficial Ownership Abusive Scheme)

Statutory sanction: Under PRC CIT Law Art. 47 and Art. 120, SAT may apply GAAR to recharacterize the arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and impose adjusted tax plus interest and penalty (TAL Art. 63/64). Announcement 2018 No. 9 denies beneficial-owner status for conduit companies, reverting treaty WHT rates (e.g., from 5% or 0% to 10%). Failure to maintain substance may expose the entity to revocation of the softer (5%) dividends rate and, if fraudulent paperwork filed, criminal liability under Art. 205 (forged VAT invoices) or Art. 201 (evasion).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute a sham substance arrangement face several personal exposures: (a) Personal civil liability for the company's under-withheld taxes under TAL Art. 43 (Responsibility of persons directly in charge), (b) blacklisting as a seriously illegal taxpayer that may lead to travel bans and denial of exit permits, (c) criminal liability under Art. 201 for evasion-organizing and under Art. 229 (Criminal Art. 229 in securities?) for false audits, and in extreme cases; (d) the People's Court may pierce the veil and hold the director personally liable for 100% of unpaid tax and penalty if the company is created as mere instrument of fraud. Imprisonment terms range from 3–7 years for major fraud, with additional fines equal to 50–100% of evaded amount.
Cross-Border Illegal Remittance and Forex / Export Tax Violation (Foreign Exchange and Trade Misreporting)

Statutory sanction: PRC FX Administration Regulations: unauthorized cross-border fund flow may be treated as illegal purchase/sale of foreign exchange under SAFE, subject to fines from 0.5% to 1.5 times the transaction amount (SAFE Art. 6/15, 2008 Regulations). For intentional false export/import declarations, Customs and SAT may apply Administrative Penalty under Customs Law for up to 3 times evaded tax; if the practice is laundering funds, AML Law of PRC Art. 63 imposes fines up to RMB 5 million; Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering) imposes imprisonment up to 10 years (severe persons) and fines. If sales are booked as 'software export' with inflated refunds, SAT will claw back VAT export rebates and impose a penalty of 1–5 times the fraudulently obtained export refund plus potential criminal liability for fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who maintain overseas bank accounts in their own name without declaration are personally liable under SAFE's reporting regime; failing to declare foreign-held bank accounts or move RMB proceeds abroad without approval may trigger an administrative fine. Civil liability arises for misappropriation of corporate funds: Company Law Art. 147/148 imposes restitution to the company for any personal gains from undisclosed offshore deposits. In criminal cases, directors who orchestrate fake cross-border service contracts for capital flight may be imprisoned up to 10 years under Art. 191 (money laundering) if the proceeds originate from identified crimes, or up to 5 years for obstructing tax collection. Additionally, the SAT/SAFE blacklisting mechanism prohibits directors from leaving China and blocks their domestic personal bank accounts until the tax and penalty are settled.
