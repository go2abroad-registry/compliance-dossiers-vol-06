Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide Corporate Income Tax; not a pass-through entity; no territorial exemption for foreign income; subject to VAT as an in-scope taxable person; no special tax-exempt zone classification applies

Available election forms

China does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch entity election; available statutory elections are limited to (a) voluntary registration as a VAT general taxpayer for enterprises below the RMB 5,000,000 small-scale threshold, (b) retention of small-scale taxpayer status where eligible, (c) the election to apply VAT zero-rating or exemption on qualifying cross-border services and exported goods through the e-Tax export-tax-refund module, and (d) the special software VAT treatment under which self-developed software is taxed at 13% with an immediate refund of the VAT paid above 3% of software sales

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Registration as a VAT taxpayer is mandatory at establishment for every mainland enterprise; mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate is available for: (1) tangible goods that are physically exported from mainland China, declared to China Customs under a paperless export customs declaration in the China International Trade Single Window, exported by a VAT general taxpayer, and accompanied by collected export proceeds; and (2) qualifying offshore software services, information system services, and other listed cross-border modern services supplied to overseas purchasers and consumed wholly outside mainland China. For app-store digital games, the WFOE must demonstrate through the Apple App Store Connect or Google Play Console platform sales reports that the end-user customer is located overseas and the game is not consumed in mainland China; the service contract, intellectual-property license, zero-rating declaration in the e-Tax export-tax-refund module, platform payout records, and bank inward remittance evidence must all be consistent with the declared overseas service recipient. Zero-rating is denied if the software or game is sold to a Chinese domestic purchaser or downloaded by a user within mainland China

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a rigorous managed-floating exchange-rate system, and the RMB is only partially convertible. Current-account trade in software and digital game publishing services is generally convertible for licensed commercial banks through the SAFE direct reporting system; this requires underlying contracts, invoices, tax records and platform payout statements. Capital-account transactions, including inbound WFOE equity injections, must be channelled through a foreign-invested enterprise capital account after completing FDI registration with SAFE and receiving a bank-issued FDI registration form. Surplus working capital may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted to RMB at the onshore market rate, but profit remittance and dividend outflows require audited financial statements, tax clearance, and SAFE reporting through the bank. A WFOE may lawfully remit its profit after CIT payments and reserve requirements to the foreign parent, but the procedure entails proof of tax contribution and a written no-objection from the booking bank. In practice, banks require all service export receipts to be supported by invoicing evidence and depersonalised platform payout reports, and there is no free fungibility between a company's domestic RMB cash and offshore CNY or USD balances without a genuine commercial underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory regime no longer imposes mandatory conversion; the effective mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent, meaning the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipt FX in a foreign currency settlement account or settle to RMB on a voluntary basis. The repatriation timeline is governed by the contractual payment terms; for software service exports the bookkeeper must evidence collection within the contract settlement date, and if a contract is silent, the prudent safe harbor used by major banks is 180 days from invoice issuance. All export proceeds received into the onshore bank account must be reported to SAFE by the bank through the direct reporting system, and the WFOE must ensure its SAFE business registration is current before the inward remittance is credited.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital-only banks and non-bank EMIs without a PBOC/SAFE banking license are legally barred from opening RMB current accounts or performing domestic clearing for a mainland WFOE. They cannot issue CIP-compliant Chinese payment instructions, hold SAFE registrations, or obtain a CIPS membership. At best, an offshore EMI can only service a holding-company bank account outside China, and all WFOE operating capital, royalties, and local payroll must flow through a licensed PRC commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions; RMB 100 million for intangible-asset or financial-asset related transactions; RMB 200 million for tangible-asset related transactions; with a Master File obligation generally arising when group related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. A related-party transaction disclosure schedule must accompany the annual return whenever any related-party transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Article 120 impose a general anti-avoidance and substance-over-form rule. Tax authorities will disregard contracts that lack business purpose and economic substance, recharacterize pass-through arrangements, and attribute profit according to Core Income-Generating Activities performed in China. A WFOE that keeps development staff, product managers, and strategic decision-makers in China while shifting the resulting profit to a zero-tax offshore affiliate will be adjusted. Controlled Foreign Company rules under Article 45 may also impute undistributed offshore profits where the offshore entity has no real business activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through concealed gross receipts and false accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63 requires payment of the evaded tax plus daily interest at 0.05%, and imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion where the amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable with up to 3 years imprisonment; for statutorily huge evasion, the responsible persons face 3 to 7 years imprisonment plus fines. The tax authority may freeze bank accounts, attach assets, and add the entity to the Serious Untrustworthy Taxpayer list.

Director personal liability: Civil liability is imposed under Company Law principles permitting veil piercing where abuse of corporate personality is used to defraud creditors or evade taxes, making directors jointly and severally liable for uncovered tax liabilities and restitution. Directors, the legal representative, and the finance director may be prohibited from leaving China, may have property preserved or seized, and may be criminally prosecuted as directly responsible persons. The personal bank accounts of directors who received the hidden revenue are also traceable and attachable.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell-Affiliate Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Corporate Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44 and 48 authorize the tax authority to adjust income under the arm's-length principle, disregard missing documentation and apply reasonable deemed assessments; late-payment interest runs on the adjusted tax. Under Tax Collection Law Articles 24, 35 and 63, failure to keep proper books and deflated related-party income may attract penalties from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. The treaty benefit itself will be denied if the offshore recipient is not the beneficial owner or lacks substance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured the circular payment chain can have the corporate veil pierced, becoming personally liable for restitution of the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties if the arrangement drained the company beyond its ability to pay. They may be blacklisted by name for serious tax violations and denied exit clearance. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax returns can follow where the fabricated agreements or invoices were used to hide taxable income.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 69 orders the withholding agent to pay the amount that should have been withheld and imposes a penalty ranging from 50% to 300% of the un-withheld amount. The tax authority will also recover the unpaid tax from the non-resident payee where collectable, and late-payment interest applies from the original due date. Unreported outbound payments are additionally subject to foreign exchange audit, administrative fines, and possible criminal tax referral.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who sign remittance orders knowing tax was not withheld are exposed to administrative fines, personal joint and several liability where their conduct caused corporate insolvency, travel bans, and inclusion in the tax blacklist. If the unreported foreign remittance was part of a scheme to evade taxes or launder proceeds through offshore accounts, the responsible natural persons face criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 or money laundering provisions, with custodial sentences and confiscation of personal assets.
