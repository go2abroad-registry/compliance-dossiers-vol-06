Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital or paid-up capital applies to an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The sole shareholder may subscribe to registered capital as determined in the Articles of Association, and the subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from the date of establishment under Article 47 of the Company Law (2023). There is no blocked escrow account rule for the standard WFOE. Special lower or higher capital thresholds may be triggered only if the business scope is later expanded into telecommunications, finance, logistics, or education, which are not part of this project.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of China, online via the Electronic Tax Administration Portal (e-Tax, 电子税务局); the official website is etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident enterprise establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must first obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). After approval, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the State Taxation Administration within 30 days of the business license issuance. The legal representative or authorized representative must submit the completed Tax Registration Form, the original and duplicate of the business license, the articles of association, valid passports and residence permits of the directors, lease or ownership proof of the registered office, and the bank account opening certificate. The tax authority will verify the information and issue the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings and electronic invoicing. VAT registration is automatically integrated with taxpayer status identification; the WFOE will be classified as a general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer based on turnover and business scope. For a newly established WFOE without prior revenue, the entity may initially register as a small-scale taxpayer and subsequently apply for general taxpayer status once annual sales exceed the RMB 5 million threshold or when the projected turnover warrants general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under Chinese VAT law, subject to the Export VAT Rebate (exemption and rebate) regime administered by the State Taxation Administration. The WFOE must be a registered VAT general taxpayer, the goods must physically leave China customs territory, the foreign exchange must be received and settled in accordance with SAFE rules, and the export sales must be genuinely transacted with overseas purchasers. Qualifying exported software and e-commerce platform services supplied to foreign businesses may also be zero-rated if the services are consumed entirely outside China and meet the cross-border taxable service criteria under STA Announcement 2016 No. 36. Esports community platform services that are not explicitly within the zero-rated digital services scope would standardly be subject to 6% VAT; the export zero-rating applies where the platform's fees are strictly from overseas users and the service is wholly performed and consumed outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods, 出口货物报关单)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Airway Bill (Air) or international carriage receipts for cross-border physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation (Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt and Verification Notice) proving overseas settlement of the export proceeds
  • Export invoice (commercial invoice)
  • Export sales contract
  • Export VAT rebate declaration form (if claiming export rebate)
  • Customs supervision and verification electronic data matching the STA export turnover database

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice (e-invoice) system under the State Taxation Administration's Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. All VAT taxpayers must issue official VAT invoices through the STA-approved tax control system (Golden Tax Phase IV). Taxpayers integrate the tax UKey or digital authority to communicate with the e-tax bureau API for invoice issuance, real-time validation, and anti-counterfeiting fiscal stamping. All invoices are digitally signed and include a unique 20-character invoice code. Full digital electronic invoices (fully digitized e-invoices) are being rolled out nationwide and are recognized for VAT deduction and credit. Non-compliance with invoice system integration or issuing invalid invoices triggers administrative penalties and blocks VAT input tax credits.

If the STA determines that an export zero-rating or VAT rebate claim was fraudulent, not substantiated by actual customs export records, or lacks verified foreign exchange repatriation within the prescribed period, the taxpayer must refund all VAT rebate amounts received plus administrative fines ranging from 0.5x to 5x the amount involved. Repeated violations or counterfeit banks and customs documentation trigger stern smuggling and tax evasion penalties under the Law of the PRC on Administrative Penalties for Tax Evasion, including additional late payment interest and potential criminal liability for tax fraud. For digital service exports, failure to document the offshore consumption and overseas customer status, with the beneficiary outside China and no Chinese tax nexus, will reverse the zero rating and subject the entire amount to 6% VAT plus penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII; trade in goods and services settlement is freely permitted through designated banks after documentary review. Capital account items remain subject to SAFE/PBOC registration, approval or filing. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) receives foreign direct investment (FDI) into a SAFE-licensed foreign currency capital account; RMB conversion is allowed only after the authenticity of the intended use has been verified and generally must follow the real underlying transaction principle. Capital account income may not be invested in equity, securities or real estate beyond the permitted scope without specific approval. For profit remittance, repatriation of dividends to a foreign parent is treated as a current account item, available after annual audit, tax clearance, board resolution and the relevant enterprise foreign exchange registration; no separate SAFE approval is required for genuine dividend outbound remittances. The RMB exchange rate remains managed rather than free-floating, with monitored daily reference rates and fluctuation bands. Non-resident shareholding, equity transfer proceeds and subsequent capital reductions require foreign exchange change registration. Overall, convertibility is liberal for trade and profit remittance, but capital-flow-driven movements are tightly policed, and a negative list restricts activities such as internet content and cultural services in certain sectors.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not impose a mandatory surrender ratio or a fixed statutory surrender deadline for export proceeds. Export-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to the exporting entity's books and reported in the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; banks verify the customs declaration, invoice and logistics data. The WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency account or sell the receipts to a bank for RMB at any time based on commercial need. If funds are retained onshore, they may be used for current account payments; if converted, the conversion must be supported by truthful transaction documentation. Overdue or unexplained export receipts trigger authenticity inquiries by the receiving bank under SAFE's trade supervision framework.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking or treasury channel. China does not grant banking licenses to foreign fintech EMI platforms, and such entities cannot maintain segregated client settlement accounts, access RMB clearing, or provide corporate deposit and FX execution capabilities for an onshore WFOE. Cross-border payment processors such as PayPal, Stripe and Airwallex may act only as third-party payment service providers under the PBOC payment-licensing framework, in partnership with licensed domestic institutions, and they cannot replace the WFOE's mandated Tier-1 commercial banking relationship. The treasury should therefore maintain all core liquidity and regulatory FX obligations with Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, while using licensed payment aggregators exclusively for merchant collection and payout rails.

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a foreign-invested enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder; residence enterprise profits distributed to foreign shareholders generally subject to 10% CIT under Article 58(2) of the EIT Law, unless reduced to 5% or 0% under tax treaty / double tax agreement (DTA) as applicable.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual Corporate Income Tax Return must be filed within five months after the fiscal year end (i.e., by 31 May of the following year). Quarterly advance CIT filings are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter (i.e., 15 April, 15 July, 15 October, and 15 January for quarter four, while annual final settlement is due by 31 May).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Chinese Local File must be prepared if the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in a single fiscal year satisfies one of: (1) purchase of tangible goods from related parties exceeds RMB 400 million; (2) sale of tangible goods to related parties exceeds RMB 400 million; (3) transfer of intangible property to related parties exceeds RMB 100 million; (4) purchase of intangible property from related parties exceeds RMB 100 million; (5) receipt of services from related parties exceeds RMB 100 million; (6) provision of services to related parties exceeds RMB 100 million; (7) other related party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is required when the total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or other conditions under the government administrative regulations. A Country-by-Country Report is required for a group with consolidated annual revenue in the previous year of at least RMB 5.5 billion. The Annual Related Party Transaction Report must include all related-party transactions irrespective of these thresholds; intercompany service fees must be supported by contracts, cost allocation records, and evidence of need and benefit.

Economic substance scope: China applies Six-Factor Economic Substance / Substance-over-form under the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) of the Corporate Income Tax Law Article 47 and the implementation rules (SAT Notice 2009 No. 2); a WFOE deriving esports platform income must demonstrate substantive business operations (core income-generating activities such as platform development, tournament operations, user management, and key decision-making performed by employees in China), real offices, payroll, risk control, strategic decision-making, and assets in the jurisdiction. For claims of treaty benefits for outbound dividends or royalties, Beneficial Ownership rules (SAT Notice 2018 No. 9) require economic substance and ban agent or conduit arrangements. Esports platform as a 'digital business' may be exposed to DPT-type inquiries if functions/risks are shifted offshore; ESR equivalent scrutiny arises under the Chinese GAAR and the permanent establishment rules for non-residents: if the WFOE acts as a dependent agent of its foreign parent, the foreign parent could be deemed to have a PE in China, increasing compliance risk. Chinese entities are also subject to the Common Reporting Standard and must maintain robust substance records demonstrating that platform IP, tournament matchmaking algorithms, and player databases are owned and actively managed with meaningful decisions in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Related-Party Invoicing and Artificial Expense Inflation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Article 47 (GAAR); SAT Notice 2009 No. 2; and Tax Administration Law Article 63, tax authorities may reallocate income, disallow deductions, impose late surcharge, and levy an additional penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Under Tax Administration Law Article 63, if the false invoicing is deemed fraud, the penalty is 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount; criminal referral for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 can impose a fine equal to 1-5 times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 3-7 years.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 (now Article 20/21 under 2023 revision) where the controlling shareholder or director abuses the independent legal personality of the company, strips assets, or confuses corporate assets with personal assets, causing serious loss to creditors. Directors and senior managers are personally liable for compensation; where evasion is willful, the director can be blacklisted by the tax authority and subject to travel restrictions (exit restrictions under the Exit-Entry Administration Law and joint disciplinary memorandum). Criminal exposure: the legal representative and directly responsible officers can be criminally prosecuted under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 211, with possible fixed-term imprisonment from 3 to 7 years (or up to 7 years where the amount evaded is enormous and over 30% of tax due) plus monetary penalties and confiscation of personal assets.
Shell Company Substance Failure and Abuse of Tax Treaty / Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Notice 2018 No. 9, treaty benefits must be denied for conduit companies without beneficial ownership; the WFOE is reassessed at statutory 10% WHT plus interest and late penalties. Under GAAR (EIT Law Article 47) and SAT Notice 2009 No. 2, tax authorities may disregard the entity and reattribute the offshore shell's income to the Chinese entity, subjecting it to 25% CIT plus 50%-500% penalty for underpayment; administrative blacklisting under the Tax Compliance Information Disclosure and Joint Punishment mechanism.

Director personal liability: If the shell structure is used to avoid Chinese CIT, directors and the actual controlling person are jointly and severally liable under Company Law for fraudulent incorporation or asset confusion; courts may pierce the veil and seize director's personal bank accounts and assets. Tax authorities may obtain court-ordered travel bans and an entry/exit blacklisting against the legal representative. Criminal liability may be imposed for Money Laundering where the diverted profits are channeled through shell bank accounts (Criminal Law Article 191), with imprisonment of 5-10 years plus fines; or for tax evasion Article 201 where the ringleader faces 3-7 years.
Failing to Withhold or Pay Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends/Royalties and Cross-Border Digital Service Payments (AML/CTF + Tax Fraud Nexus)

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 69: the withholding agent (WFOE) is liable for 50% to 300% of the unrepaid withheld tax; additionally, late payment interest at 0.05% per day from the due date, and the overseas recipient is jointly liable where the WFOE is unable to recover. The failure to pay withholding tax also triggers administrative fines under the Foreign Exchange Administrative Regulations; serious willful non-payment is a tax evasion offense under Criminal Law Article 201, and where the payment platform is used to launder profits, Money Laundering offense under Criminal Law Article 191 can apply with imprisonment for 5 up to 10 years and fine of RMB 200,000-500,000 or up to 2 million where serious.

Director personal liability: Directors as the 'directly responsible executives' are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax if they ordered or endorsed the unlawful remittances under Tax Administration Law Article 30 and the Supreme Court's interpretation on corporate creditors and taxation. The legal representative may be banned from leaving China by the tax authority and border control authorities as a means to enforce payment obligations. If the WFOE is later unable to satisfy tax debts because directors lacked good faith or dissipated assets, the court can pierce the veil, and directors can be held personally liable for unpaid tax. Criminal liability: prison sentence of 3-7 years for tax evasion plus confiscation of assets; if fraud in foreign payments is connected with false customs or banking declarations, additional charges under the Foreign Exchange Control regulations produce parallel imprisonment penalties.
