No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a general wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The registered capital amount and contribution schedule are fixed in the articles of association. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) nationwide online taxpayer platform.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is registered with the local Administration for Market Regulation, which issues a Business License containing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the sole statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Tax registration is completed automatically via the multi-certificate integration data sharing between the market regulator and the STA. The WFOE then activates its e-Tax account through real-name authentication of the legal representative, files its bank deposit account and accounting methods, and confirms VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale). A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment must separately complete non-resident taxpayer registration through its Chinese withholding agent or via e-Tax, after which the STA assigns a non-resident TIN for withholding and compliance purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for initial tax registration of an incorporated WFOE; VAT general taxpayer registration becomes mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, unless the taxpayer elects general taxpayer status earlier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero VAT rate applies to exported goods and to qualifying cross-border services/intangible-asset supplies, including film copyright licensing, where the supply is made to an overseas recipient, is wholly consumed outside mainland China, and the foreign-exchange consideration is received and repatriated to China. For independent film licensing, the WFOE must demonstrate through the licensing agreement and downstream exploitation evidence that the licensed rights are used offshore; if the rights are partly exploited inside China, zero-rating is denied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration) — required only for physical export of goods; not applicable where the export is an intangible film licensing right
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill — required only for physical goods movements
  • Commercial invoice and film licensing agreement evidencing the grant of distribution/licensing rights to an overseas film distributor
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt / export proceeds collection voucher issued by a licensed Chinese bank evidencing repatriation of the license fee within the statutory deadline
  • Cross-border service/intangible-asset supply evidence pack, including proof of overseas recipient identity, proof of offshore consumption/exploitation, and the e-Tax zero-rating/export tax exemption declaration where required

VAT invoices (including Special VAT Invoices) and fully digitalized electronic invoices are issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform / Electronic Tax Bureau. The platform performs real-time schema validation, applies an STA digital anti-fraud stamp/tax-control code, and transmits invoice data to the national tax database. General taxpayers must use the official e-invoice interface and national electronic invoice data specification for system-to-system API integration; Special VAT Invoice input tax credits must be verified/certified through the STA invoice verification platform before deduction.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to pre- and post-audit by the STA using customs declaration data, bank foreign-exchange receipt data, and invoice data matching. If required documentation is missing or false, the STA will claw back the zero-rated VAT benefit retroactively, charge late-payment interest/surcharge, and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export-tax-rebate conduct may also be prosecuted under the Criminal Law and can lead to suspension of the taxpayer's export tax exemption/refund qualification and enhanced monitoring.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. The People's Bank of China (PBC) and SAFE administer current-account convertibility for legitimate trade and service transactions, while the capital account remains subject to strict registration, approval, and quota controls. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in film copyright licensing, profit remittances and royalty outflows are treated as current-account items and can be processed once the underlying commercial contract, invoice, and tax clearance certificate are verified by the designated bank. However, registered capital injections, equity transfers, and shareholder loan repayments are capital-account events that require FDI registration with SAFE or its delegated banks, plus a foreign exchange capital account, capital verification, and use-based settlement. A foreign-invested entity must complete the foreign exchange registration for inbound direct investment and open a capital account in the designated custody bank. Surrender requirements historically forced exporters to convert FX; after policy liberalization, SAFE permits enterprises to retain export proceeds in foreign currency, but receipts must be collected onshore and reported through the Bank of China settlement system. Profit repatriation is permitted after audit, tax filing, and with an authentic profit-resolution document pack. Practical friction remains: the paying bank must inspect title/licensing agreements, service income support, and the beneficial ownership structure, while the receiving bank applies post-registration supervision and traps suspicious matching failures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts are no longer subject to mandatory surrender in China; since foreign-exchange liberalization, enterprises may retain all export proceeds in foreign currency, provided they are routed through onshore Chinese banks, reported via SAFE's monitoring system, and matched against customs declarations within 90 days for trade data reconciliation. The actual foreign exchange verification is waived for most current-account receipts, but the funds must be settled or retained through a designated Foreign Exchange Settlement Account and documented in the Enterprise Foreign Income and Payment System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and its administrative arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI cross-border viability is very low for a mainland China-domiciled WFOE. SAFE requires all cross-border payments, FX settlement, and current/capital account transactions to occur through licensed onshore commercial banks or designated clearing institutions. Foreign digital EMIs holding no China banking or FX license cannot legally clear RMB or foreign exchange for a PRC entity, cannot receive registered capital, and cannot file SAFE capital-account reports. Such EMIs may only serve as auxiliary payment-request tools if the actual clearing and foreign-exchange conversion are executed through the onshore Tier-1 bank, which makes direct EMI onboarding a regulatory and operational non-starter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound FDI capital injection, the shareholder must remit foreign currency directly into the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account opened at the designated TIER-1 Chinese bank after the entity has completed SAFE inbound direct investment registration and received the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate or the bank's auto-generated FDI code. The remittance should be a single clean wire under SWIFT MT103 bearing the remitter shareholder's name, the WFOE's name, the stated purpose of 'Foreign Direct Investment Capital Injection', and the SAFE payment code for investment in equity/capital. The receiving bank then performs capital-account verification, checks the FDI data against SAFE's Administrative Information Management System, converts the foreign currency into RMB only for permitted use items inside the Negative List, and records the transaction as 'settled capital'. Capital verification is evidenced by the bank's Foreign Exchange Capital Account Transaction Confirmation and a CPA-issued capital verification report, which must match the current registered capital amount in the business license and articles of association.Outbound film-licensing disbursements, including minimum guarantees and royalty splits, should be cleared through the onshore bank using SWIFT MT103 for outright service payments, Commercial Letters of Credit for structured title-delivery milestones, and Documentary Collections only for contractually verified payment and receipt of rights. Each payment requires an outward remittance application, the tax withholding completion certificate and deposit receipt, the original or electronic copyright license agreement, the China tax filing record for non-resident enterprise income tax, and the bank's verification of the beneficiary's sanctions and PEP status.
