Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A WFOE engaged in commercial CGI visual effects and 3D character animation under ISIC 5911 cannot be registered at a bare virtual office or commercial mailbox because PRC company registration law requires a fixed and physically verifiable registered domicile. A compliant shared workspace, incubator address, or creative-park office may be accepted only if there is a real desk or studio space, a lease contract in Chinese, server or render-farm infrastructure, management access for SAMR and tax officials, and an address-services provider authorized by the local park. Local tax authorities and banks will normally reject an isolated mailbox for VAT invoice control and for opening Swift or Wise B2B settlement accounts. The WFOE should therefore maintain a physical creative office or a fully compliant co-working lease in a commercial or municipal creative industry park to satisfy SAMR, tax, and bank KYB requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus and the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the legal taxpayer identification number for all Chinese tax purposes. Step 2: SAMR automatically shares registration data with the STA under the multi-certificate integration reform; within 30 days of incorporation, the WFOE must access the local STA e-Tax Bureau, complete supplementary tax registration information, appoint financial and tax principals, file the accounting/bookkeeping policy, and record bank deposit accounts used for tax payments. Step 3: The STA activates the taxpayer account using the USCC, and the electronic taxpayer profile becomes the sole TIN/VAT ID credential; no separate paper TIN is issued, but the USCC is encoded in all e-filings and e-invoices. Step 4: The WFOE must elect VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general), register for the fully digitalized e-invoice system, and set up corporate income tax, VAT, stamp duty, and social insurance registrations as applicable. Step 5: Non-resident shareholders do not require a separate Chinese TIN for the WFOE; a non-resident enterprise with a permanent establishment in China must separately register for a non-resident tax code with the local STA, while a non-resident without a permanent establishment is generally satisfied by domestic withholding at source.

None. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete tax registration and receive the Unified Social Credit Code-based taxpayer ID upon incorporation. For VAT, mandatory general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status for input VAT crediting and special VAT invoice issuance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods widely qualify for 0% VAT where they are physically exported and cleared by the customs authority. For services, 0% VAT or VAT exemption is available for cross-border taxable services supplied to an overseas entity where the service is within the STA cross-border services zero-rating/exemption catalogue, the service recipient is outside China, the service is not wholly consumed in China, and the consideration is received in foreign currency through bank settlement. Commercial 3D animation/VFX production for an overseas client may qualify under the design/cultural-creative or broadcasting/film service definitions if the documentation proves the overseas recipient and offshore use; the same VAT rules also exempt input-VAT-clearable export goods, subject to export-refund filing.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (China Customs release/clearance record)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other international transport document evidencing export movement
  • Foreign exchange settlement voucher and bank receipt from the designated foreign exchange bank
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-sanctioned export proceeds verification record
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • For zero-rated services: signed service agreement, overseas corporate registration/residency evidence, written evidence that the service is consumed outside China, zero-rated VAT invoice, and foreign-currency bank credit advice

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV real-time VAT control system. Under the national fully digitalized electronic invoice reform, VAT taxpayers issue fully digitalized electronic invoices through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau; each invoice is generated with a unique invoice number and digital signature/QR verification code and the issuance record is transmitted to the STA in real time. Special VAT invoices used for input credit must be authenticated on the national VAT invoice verification platform. Taxpayers using automated billing must connect their accounting/ERP systems to the STA-authorized e-invoice API; all invoice data, red-letter reverse invoices, and export invoices are subject to STA automated risk monitoring and anti-fraud digital stamping.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by the STA against customs, transportation, and foreign-exchange receipts. Failure to substantiate the claim with the required bundle triggers retroactive clawback of the VAT rebate/exemption, supplementary VAT assessment at the applicable domestic rate, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the original due date, and administrative penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid where false invoices or fraudulent documents are involved. Verified false issuance or use of special VAT invoices in export zero-rating cases also carries criminal liability for tax fraud, suspensions of export-tax-refund status, and blacklisting; non-resident service exports with missing documentation are reclassified as domestic taxable supplies at 6% VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime where the RMB is convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties) under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supervised through the SAFE monitoring system. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, are subject to a separate approval or filing process under the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration and Capital Account Settlement framework. A WFOE may receive overseas equity capital into a Capital Account at a designated bank, and such funds can be converted to RMB under the 'paid-up capital actual use' principle, but cannot be used for prohibited purposes such as securities investment or repayment of RMB loans. Current account receipts, including service fees for animation production, must be repatriated to a domestic bank account and are subject to the Export Receipts Watch List and the Post-Verification System. Profit remittances by the WFOE require an audited financial statement, a tax clearance certificate, and the bank's verification of the foreign exchange registration record. Surrender requirements are no longer mandatory for current account proceeds; however, the converting bank must ensure the underlying transaction has been completed and documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service trade export proceeds (including 3D animation production services), the statutory repatriation period is 180 days from the contracted payment due date or the invoice date, whichever is later. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to RMB under the current SAFE liberalization; export proceeds may be retained as foreign currency deposits in the WFOE's settlement account. If the WFOE opts to convert service receipts into RMB, it must present the service contract, the commercial invoice, and the proof of performance (deliverables acceptance) to the designated settlement bank, which will complete the verification and settlement. For goods exports under related instruments, the requirement is that all export foreign exchange receipts must be received within the customs-verified export period, typically 180 days, unless a longer period is approved. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers an automatic suspension of the entity's automatic verification registration and may result in a penalty under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, under the aegis of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not feasible for direct local onboarding. Wise, Airwallex, and similar cross-border EMIs that operate as financial technology companies do not hold a Chinese banking license or a PBOC-issued third-party payment license that would permit them to open a corporate bank account, accept FX settlements, or provide clearing rails in RMB within mainland China. Cross-border collections initiated via Wise B2B are ultimately cleared through local partner banks (e.g., ICBC, BOC) into a domestic RMB/CNY account, but the EMI cannot serve as the WFOE's primary banking partner, cannot custody the capital account, and cannot satisfy SAFE foreign exchange registration or capital verification requirements. Any use of such EMIs must be strictly limited to pre-paid disbursements to international vendors with a fully documented underlying contract; the bulk of treasury operations must be executed through a Tier-1 local bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated capital account at the Tier-1 bank, using the SAFE 'Foreign Exchange Registration Code' and the applicable purpose code (typically '601' for capital injection under the current SAFE coding system). The remittance instruction should include the legal name of the non-resident shareholder, the registered address, the reference to the FDI approval or filing, and the long-form invoice / capital subscription notice. The receiving bank will validate the foreign exchange registration record, screen the transaction against the 'Check & Monitor' list, and upon compliance, convert the foreign currency to RMB under the 'capital settlement upfront' mechanism (the so-called '4-in-1' process). After settlement, the bank is required to upload the transaction to SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administration Platform within the statutory T+1/T+2 window. A Chinese certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report and the WFOE's bookkeeper files the updated legal capital with the Administration for Market Regulation.For regular trade settlements, the WFOE will initiate SWIFT MT103 for individual vendor payments (e.g., software license fees, cloud rendering costs) and may use documentary collections (DP/DA) for large project-based milestones. Commercial letters of credit (LC) are generally not suited for 3D animation production and are only recommended if the buyer overseas requires one as a trade guarantee; however, for services, a standby LC or a bank guarantee is more practical. For all outbound RMB settlements, the bank will use China's Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for immediate clearing and settlement. The WFOE must present the underlying contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, and the tax certificate (if applicable) to the bank. Payments above the threshold (e.g., USD 500,000) may require prior verification by the bank's head-office disbursement desk.Onshore forward contracts (FX forwards) for current account transactions with a real underlying economic exposure, executed through a designated domestic bankOnshore FX swaps (same-currency or cross-currency) for temporary liquidity adjustment with a two-way legForeign currency options (plain vanilla calls or puts) for current account hedging, subject to SAFE's real demand verificationProhibited onshore: non-deliverable forwards (NDFs); offshore NDFs cannot be settled by onshore parties due to SAFE restrictions. Cross-currency interest rate swaps are available only with specific regulatory approval for foreign debt hedging.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not feasible for direct local onboarding. Wise, Airwallex, and similar cross-border EMIs that operate as financial technology companies do not hold a Chinese banking license or a PBOC-issued third-party payment license that would permit them to open a corporate bank account, accept FX settlements, or provide clearing rails in RMB within mainland China. Cross-border collections initiated via Wise B2B are ultimately cleared through local partner banks (e.g., ICBC, BOC) into a domestic RMB/CNY account, but the EMI cannot serve as the WFOE's primary banking partner, cannot custody the capital account, and cannot satisfy SAFE foreign exchange registration or capital verification requirements. Any use of such EMIs must be strictly limited to pre-paid disbursements to international vendors with a fully documented underlying contract; the bulk of treasury operations must be executed through a Tier-1 local bank.
