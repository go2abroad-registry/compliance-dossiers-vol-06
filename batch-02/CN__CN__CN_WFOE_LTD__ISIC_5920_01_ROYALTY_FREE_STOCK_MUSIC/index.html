Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

China imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company engaged in digital music licensing. Registered capital is recorded in the Articles of Association; contributions may be cash, technology, or other lawful property, and must be fully paid within five years from incorporation unless a shorter period is specified. No escrow or blocked account is required; the declared capital must be remitted through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported to SAFE under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial civil-defense fire permits because there is no factory, storage tank, chemical installation, or assembly line. The company must still comply with general Fire Protection Law obligations for its leased office: install fire extinguishers suitable for electrical fires, maintain unobstructed evacuation routes, post evacuation diagrams, and ensure the landlord has a valid fire-safety acceptance certificate for the building. Workplace safety approval is limited to a voluntary internal self-assessment and, where the office exceeds one hundred persons, appointment of a workplace-safety manager; no separate statutory certificate is triggered for office-only staffing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: a WFOE incorporated in China is a resident enterprise taxed by the State Taxation Administration on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no pass-through treatment is available for a limited liability WFOE and it is not eligible for territorial-only taxation.

Available election forms

None for enterprise income tax; no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under Chinese law. For VAT, an eligible taxpayer may elect to register as a General VAT taxpayer before exceeding the statutory turnover threshold, and qualifying cross-border service providers may elect to apply zero-rating or exemption where the statutory conditions are fully evidenced.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Any entity with annual aggregate taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 must register as a General VAT taxpayer; all WFOEs are subject to tax registration from incorporation, and below RMB 5,000,000 they may remain small-scale taxpayers unless they elect General VAT taxpayer status. Small-scale taxpayers are eligible for a VAT exemption for monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000, subject to prevailing policy conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: exported goods qualify for the zero VAT rate and export VAT refund when the exporter is a registered General VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported through China Customs, customs export procedures are completed, and the export proceeds are repatriated in foreign exchange. For royalty-free stock music licensing as a cross-border digital service: zero-rating or VAT exemption applies where the service is provided to an overseas customer, the license fee is paid in foreign exchange, and the digital audio content is wholly consumed outside China; the taxpayer must evidence the export service contract, overseas customer status, and delivery or access outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form generated by the China Customs integrated system, with matching customs release records for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading / air waybill / rail waybill evidencing transport of goods out of China when the royalty-free stock music is delivered as tangible media or packaged products
  • Bank-recorded foreign exchange receipt voucher or receipt record in the SAFE cross-border foreign exchange monitoring system evidencing inward remittance and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export service contract and zero-rated VAT invoice (for pure digital audio licensing) with overseas counterparty identification and evidence that the license fee is denominated and settled in a freely convertible foreign currency

Invoicing must be executed through the STA VAT Invoice Management System and the Electronic Invoice Service Platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. All Special VAT Invoices and Full Digital Electronic Invoices are issued with unique invoice codes, encrypted fiscal stamps, and tax-control serial numbers; invoice data are transmitted to the STA in real time, enabling automated fiscal clearance and anti-fraud digital stamping. B2B transactions and cross-border service invoices require digital electronic invoices or state-approved fiscal invoices, and the STA Golden Tax IV risk-control engine monitors anomalous invoicing, refund claims, and related-party transaction patterns.

The STA and China Customs jointly scrutinize export declarations, logistics records, foreign exchange receipts, service delivery evidence, and supporting contracts. If an export zero-rating or VAT refund claim cannot be substantiated, the taxpayer must restore and pay the applicable VAT at the statutory rate, plus late-payment surcharge, and may face an administrative penalty ranging from 50% up to 500% of the evaded tax. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion or export tax fraud under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, and may result in criminal prosecution, clawback of refunds, customs penalties, suspension of export refund rights, and downgrading in the tax credit classification system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under PBOC/SAFE oversight. The RMB is broadly convertible on the current account, including service trade proceeds from digital music licensing and subscription revenue, provided the underlying transactions are genuine and properly documented. The capital account, however, remains tightly controlled: a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for foreign direct investment before any shareholder capital injection can be received. Injected funds land in a dedicated FDI capital account and may be settled into RMB only for approved business purposes; cross-border profit remittance is permitted only out of audited after-tax distributable profits, supported by a CPA audit report, tax clearance documentation, board resolution, and a SAFE-verified purpose code. The WFOE must also keep foreign exchange receipts under current-account monitoring, report international balance-of-payments statistics, and ensure that every foreign currency transaction has traceable underlying commercial documentation. In practice, the system is workable for low-risk digital royalty licensing, but it requires rigorous documentary discipline and the use of designated onshore banks; offshore digital-only financial institutions cannot provide the local capital custody, SAFE registration, or foreign exchange settlement functions mandated by Chinese regulation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds for general trade and service receipts; the WFOE may retain qualifying current-account foreign exchange income in a designated onshore foreign currency account or convert it into RMB voluntarily through a domestic bank. However, current-account foreign exchange receipts from overseas clients for music licensing and digital subscription services must be repatriated to an onshore account within 180 days from the date of service performance or invoice, unless an explicit SAFE dispensation or approved time extension is obtained. The bank will verify the service contract, invoice, proof of delivery/customer access, and the corresponding international balance-of-payments declaration. Any unrepatriated or falsely declared export/licensing proceeds constitute a foreign exchange violation and may trigger SAFE sanctions, monetary penalties, suspension of FX business eligibility, or inclusion on the bank's internal AML risk list.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), including its local branches acting as the onshore foreign exchange regulatory enforcement organ

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail; conditional as an ancillary receivable collection channel. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, PayPal, Airwallex, and Currenxie are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE as domestic deposit-taking banks and cannot legally open the FDI capital account or complete SAFE foreign exchange registration for a mainland WFOE. They also cannot issue Chinese capital verification receipts. The WFOE may integrate PayPal or Stripe as online payment aggregators to collect low-value royalty-free music subscription fees from global customers, but those funds must be settled into the WFOE's onshore bank account through a licensed cross-border payment institution or by direct contractual arrangement with a domestic acquiring bank. The offshore EMI cannot hold the WFOE's permanent operational liquidity or act as the jurisdiction of profit retention. Any attempt to use an offshore EMI to bypass SAFE reporting, under-declare service export income, or avoid repatriation timelines will create an immediate regulatory and AML red flag.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File, 同期资料本地文档) is mandatory if for any year the Chinese-resident enterprise has: (i) related-party transfers of tangible assets above RMB 200 million; (ii) related-party transfers of financial assets above RMB 100 million; (iii) related-party transfers of intangible assets or license/royalty payments above RMB 40 million; or (iv) other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when total related-party transactions in a year reach RMB 1 billion or when the group meets global documentation thresholds. In all cases a Chinese Local File is also required if the entity is a member of a multinational group and its operations fall within the specified sectoral thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China applies its own economic-substance equivalents under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), the substance-for-treaty-benefits doctrine, and the preferential-regime substantial-operation statutes. A WFOE must demonstrate that it carries out Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in mainland China for the audio licensing business: contract negotiation and execution, server or hosting decisions, revenue recognition, and royalty collection. The beneficial-owner and business-purpose tests are applied to reject shell conduits used solely to obtain DTA reductions. In a transfer-pricing audit, the tax authority will recharacterize arrangements lacking economic substance, imputing income to the WFOE and denying treaty benefits retroactively with interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement of Cross-Border Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Administration Law Article 63, fraudulent understatement triggers a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and is over 10% of the taxable tax, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years in standard cases, or 3 to 7 years where the amount is huge, plus fines.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the director personally directed billing redirects or maintained a secondary ledger. CEO/CFO personal assets can be seized for unpaid tax via civil asset restitution actions. The director is placed on the tax exit-control blacklist (限制出境), prohibiting overseas business travel, and may be criminally prosecuted for ‘escape of tax’ if the evasion was knowing and material. Directors of WFOEs are jointly and severally liable if they knowingly ignore the fraudulent conduct and benefit from it.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin-Capitalized Royalty Drain

Statutory sanction: The Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 123 and the GAAR allow the tax authority to make a special tax adjustment, disallow the excessive royalty or interest deduction, and impose interest at the base lending rate plus five percentage points from the year the adjustment applies. If the taxpayer fails to provide required transfer-pricing documentation, penalties under the Law on Special Tax Adjustments apply, including fines of up to RMB 50,000 for incomplete documents, and the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they signed the related-party contract knowingly underpinning a sham transaction; they are exposed to civil asset restitution for the additional tax, interest and penalties recovered from the WFOE. A repeat failure to comply with the Tax Administration Law can lead to blacklisting of the legal representative, denial of exit permits, and, where the arrangement is a criminal tax fraud conspiracy, imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Shell Company or Conduit Company for Abusive Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Under the Beneficial Owner Notice (Public Notice 2018 No. 9), treaty benefits are denied for conduit entities. The tax authority can retroactively deny the reduced rate, reassess the full 10% withholding tax, impose a 50%-500% fine for non-compliance, and apply GAAR to disregard the legal form and recharacterize the income as taxable to the Chinese resident enterprise at the 25% headline rate. Repeat or fraudulent conduit planning may constitute illegal financial crime under the Anti-Money Laundering Office framework.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is accepted where the company is ‘merely a puppet’ of the parent. Directors face civil liability for restitution of the avoided tax and penalties. Criminal exposure includes up to 7 years' imprisonment for tax fraud; additionally, a travel ban/blacklisting (出境限制) is automatically issued if the company has a material undisclosed tax amount outstanding, and personal bank accounts of the directors can be frozen for tax recovery in asset-conservation procedures.
