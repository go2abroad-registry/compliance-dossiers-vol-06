Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is imposed for a WFOE engaged in ISIC 6201 B2B enterprise SaaS under the PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law. Registered capital is set freely in the Articles of Association, but the AOA must specify a subscription term not exceeding 5 years unless sector-specific FDI rules require otherwise. Capital contributions may be made in cash or in-kind; if any paid-in amount is contributed, it must be received through the FDI capital account and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). No blocked escrow account is required for this digital service category, and the capital need not be fully paid at incorporation; however, the subscribed amount must be sufficient to cover the contemplated business operations and the shareholder bears liability within the subscribed capital limit.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as an industrial clearance for the pure digital SaaS business. For office premises, the landlord must provide a valid fire safety inspection certificate or fire safety acceptance filing issued by the local fire and rescue authority before occupancy; the company must register in the emergency management system, maintain fire extinguishers and evacuation signage, and conduct routine fire drills. No special civil defense construction permit or hazardous production safety license is required because the enterprise has no physical factory, chemical process, or warehouse operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) system, accessible at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a newly established Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with a foreign non-resident shareholder, the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise. The Tax Identification Number (TIN) is obtained through the following procedure: (1) The foreign investor registers the WFOE with the local office of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The issued Business License contains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the enterprise's legal and TIN identifier. (2) Within 30 days of receiving the Business License, the entity must complete activation of its tax registration through the local Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal, using the USCC as the initial login credential. (3) The WFOE uploads statutory documentation: Articles of Association, stamped Business License, legal representative identification, financial controller identification, tax accountant appointment letter, and bank account details. (4) The e-Tax system formally assigns the taxpayer's national numeric TIN (issued by the STA) and electronically records the entity's tax status, including corporate income tax, VAT registration type, and withholding obligations. (5) Any foreign non-resident shareholder receiving China-source dividends, interest, or royalties must separately apply for a Chinese TIN via the same e-Tax platform to support treaty relief or withholding requirements, using original identity documents and proof of beneficial ownership.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent STA announcements, cross-border exported goods and specified offshore services, including software services, information technology services and data processing services supplied to overseas clients, may qualify for VAT zero-rating (0%) if the services are consumed entirely outside mainland China, contracted and collected from an overseas party, and the supplier maintains complete evidential records. For physical goods, a verified Customs export declaration is required. For the B2B Enterprise SaaS business model (ISIC 6201), the qualifying digital service must be provided to a non-resident enterprise, with contracts, service level agreements, workload records, and server access logs demonstrating that the service is rendered and consumed outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单) issued by the General Administration of Customs of China confirming export clearance for physical goods; for pure digital SaaS services, a registered Cross-Border Digital Service Export Declaration (digital service export contract with STA filing reference) is used instead
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing physical cross-border transport of goods; for digital services, an Offshore Service Consumption Certificate signed by the overseas customer, together with server logs or usage analytics proving consumption outside China
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (or Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent) issued by a Chinese designated foreign exchange bank, evidencing repatriation of settlement proceeds from the overseas buyer to the WFOE's bank account

China operates the All-Digital Electronic Invoice (also known as Full Digital e-Invoice) system under the State Taxation Administration. Taxpayers must issue sales invoices through the national electronic invoice clearance platform, which validates each invoice real-time via an anti-fraud cryptographic engine and affixes a digital signature and unique QR code. System-to-system integration through the official e-Tax API, or via STA-approved third-party electronic invoice service providers, is mandatory for digital invoice reporting. Traditional physical tax-control invoicing still exists on a transitional basis but is being phased out. All invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time, supporting VAT reconciliation and anti-fraud digital stamping requirements.

If the claimed 0% export VAT treatment is unsupported by the required documentation or found to be fraudulent, the tax authority will deny the zero-rating, claw back any VAT refund already granted, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the unpaid tax, and assess an administrative penalty ranging from 50% up to five times the tax amount. In cases of willful evasion or fraud, criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China may be pursued. Additionally, the taxpayer will suffer downgrade of its export tax credit rating, suspension of simplified refund procedures, increased audit scrutiny, and potential inclusion on the national tax integrity blacklist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a dual-track foreign exchange system. The RMB is fully convertible for current account items including software export service receipts, royalty income, and operating expense payments, without prior SAFE approval provided the underlying trade documentation is complete; banks perform a post-transaction authenticity check. Capital account transactions remain strictly controlled: foreign direct investment into a WFOE requires SAFE registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and corresponding settlement supervision. Profit remittance is permitted only after the annual audited financial statements are filed, corporate income tax is settled, and the bank verifies the board resolution and tax payment certificate. Surplus foreign currency may be retained or converted into RMB; there is no mandatory surrender ratio. However, the WFOE must maintain a documented payment baseline with its designated bank, retain contracts and invoices for every repatriation, and respect the negative list for RMB settlement of capital funds, which prohibits settled RMB from being used for securities investment, wealth management products, loans to non-related parties, or real estate purchases outside the approved business scope. Service trade foreign exchange receipts such as B2B SaaS subscription fees are processed as current account settlements, but the WFOE must be able to demonstrate genuine business substance and a clear audit trail between the foreign payer, the contractual customer, and the invoiced service.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods trade, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign exchange bank within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration. For service trade, the repatriation timeline is governed by the contractual settlement terms, but proceeds must be brought onshore promptly upon collection and reported to SAFE's service trade income/expense verification system; there is no mandatory conversion percentage. SAFE abolished the mandatory export surrender requirement in 2012, so the WFOE may retain foreign currency in its export proceeds settlement account or convert to RMB at prevailing market rates based on operational needs.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, administered under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility for use as a primary corporate treasury account. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business or Stripe Treasury are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open domestic banking or RMB settlement accounts for China-registered legal entities. They can only act as a collection gateway for cross-border SaaS subscription receivables in foreign currency, but all funds must ultimately be repatriated into a licensed onshore commercial bank account under SAFE monitoring. Using such EMIs for domestic disbursements, payroll, or intercompany RMB flows is prohibited and would create an unlicensed fund transmission exposure. The WFOE should therefore maintain its statutory bank accounts at a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank and treat offshore EMIs merely as a supported invoicing and collection tool within a fully documented current-account baseline.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign equity capital must be registered with SAFE through the receiving domestic bank via the FDI registration process. The offshore parent should remit capital in USD or EUR from its own corporate banking account using the SAFE-compliant payment purpose code for equity investment in a WFOE and must reference the FDI registration number in the wire message. The receiving bank will credit the proceeds to a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account and then require a licensed Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report before any RMB settlement is allowed. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed by the resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder when no DTA reduction applies; most Chinese DTAs reduce this to 5% where the beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the share capital, with the remaining treaty rate often at 10%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42, a Local File must be prepared when annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed CNY 200,000,000, intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers exceed CNY 100,000,000, or related-party service transactions exceed CNY 40,000,000; the Master File threshold is triggered where the multinational group's prior-year consolidated revenue is at least CNY 2,000,000,000. Even below these numeric thresholds, all cross-border related-party payments relevant to a B2B Enterprise SaaS WFOE must be reported on the annual related-party disclosure forms.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the same substance principle is embedded in the beneficial ownership test, the general anti-abuse rule of Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, and the principal purpose test of the Multilateral Instrument. A compliant WFOE must execute its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China: software research and development, contracting and billing enterprise customers, technical support, board governance and employment. If the WFOE has no office, no employees, no decision-making and exists only to route cloud software royalties or dividends to an offshore parent, the tax authorities may disregard the corporate form, deny DTA benefits, recharacterize the income as Chinese-source, and impose a 10% final withholding tax together with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Unreported Cross-Border SaaS Revenue

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 6 and 37 combined with Tax Administration Law Article 63; the tax authority will assess the omitted taxable income, add a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and impose a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax; criminal referral follows where intentional concealment exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents more than 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and controlling directors can be subject to exit restriction and blacklisting under Tax Administration Law Article 45; if they ordered the concealment or signed false accounts, they face criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, including imprisonment up to seven years, fines, and confiscation of illegal proceeds; Company Law Article 20 allows courts to pierce the WFOE veil and hold shareholders and actual controllers jointly liable for the unpaid tax.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping / Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9, Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 45-48, and the Multilateral Instrument principal purpose test allow the tax authorities to deny the DTA benefit, disregard the shell, reassess the income as Chinese-source, impose the statutory 10% withholding tax on gross payments, and add interest and penalties of 50%-500% under Tax Administration Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who created or continued the conduit structure can be personally pursued for the recaptured withholding tax under Company Law Article 20(3) when the corporate form is used to evade tax; asset tracing may attach their personal property; travel bans and blacklisting apply to the legal representative; criminal fraud exposure arises under Criminal Law Articles 224 and 266 if forged Tax Residency Certificates, fake contracts, or sham board resolutions are used.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's Length Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41, SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42, and Tax Administration Law Article 36 allow the special tax adjustment to recharacterize the intercompany charge, compute the arm's length taxable income, impose a ten-year lookback under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 48, add benchmark lending rate interest plus five percentage points, and impose a 50%-500% false-reporting penalty.

Director personal liability: The CFO and legal representative who certified false transfer pricing documentation can be held jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and interest; courts may pierce the corporate veil where intercompany payment abuse is deliberate; travel bans and blacklisting apply to directors in major tax evasion cases; criminal liability can arise under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205-1 if false invoices or false related-party declarations are executed.
