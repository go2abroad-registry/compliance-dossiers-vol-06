Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory paid-up minimum applies to a WFOE limited liability company engaged in ISIC 6201 software publishing and SaaS services. The governing capital rule is the full subscription and five-year payment-in obligation under Article 47 of the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision). The shareholders must set the registered capital in the Articles of Association, pay subscriptions in RMB through a proper capital account, obtain capital contribution certificates, and no blocked escrow account or deposit certificate is required for information-technology service activities.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with electronic filing through the National Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax Portal) administered by the local competent tax office in the registered jurisdiction of the WFOE.

E-government filing portal

A WFOE is a PRC resident limited liability company. Step 1: incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License, which carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), the sole statutory Tax Identification Number used for both EIT and VAT. Step 2: complete tax registration within 30 days of the Business License issuance through the e-Tax Portal; the SAMR data-sharing mechanism automatically creates the taxpayer record. Step 3: choose VAT registrant status; if expected annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer treatment is the default, though voluntary general taxpayer registration is permitted. Step 4: for ancillary non-resident withholding obligations (e.g., cross-border royalty payments), register as a withholding agent under the same USCC. Step 5: foreign non-resident suppliers with no PRC establishment may separately apply for a Non-Resident Taxpayer Number at the competent tax office for VAT withholding or direct registration, but that status does not apply to the WFOE itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax at the statutory 25% rate on its worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent treatment is available under Chinese law. For VAT, the WFOE is either a small-scale taxpayer or a general taxpayer depending on annual turnover.

Available election forms

China does not operate a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election comparable to the US or OECD-style hybrid rules. A WFOE may elect to be registered as a VAT general taxpayer even below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, and may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment if it qualifies. No consolidated corporate income tax filing election is available for PRC subsidiaries.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (annual taxable sales). Exceeding this threshold makes VAT general taxpayer registration mandatory; below the threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer and obtain simplified VAT treatment. For cross-border digital services supplied into China by foreign sellers, separate registration or collection duties may apply under the e-commerce VAT collection regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, zero-rating applies when goods are lawfully exported from China and the export customs declaration is matched to sales invoices, payment receipts, and foreign exchange collection. For the SaaS service, zero-rating or exemption applies under the PRC cross-border taxable services policy when the software service is supplied to an overseas non-resident enterprise or individual, the service is wholly consumed outside China, and consideration is received from outside China. Payment orchestration by Paddle or Lemon Squeezy does not by itself establish eligibility; the controlling evidence is the subscription contract, overseas consumer or business identity, IP or service delivery logs, and the recorded foreign exchange receipt.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical exports
  • Foreign exchange collection receipt from the settling bank (functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate / FIRC)
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list (for goods)
  • Cross-border software subscription agreement or service contract (for SaaS)
  • Proof of overseas service recipient and service consumption outside mainland China
  • Payment gateway settlement reports from Paddle or Lemon Squeezy matched to bank remittance records

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA-controlled Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system or the traditional tax-control invoice system. Digital invoices are uploaded in near real-time to the STA database through the e-Tax Portal API; each invoice bears a unique invoice code and inspection number, enabling anti-fraud verification. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp for electronic invoices; paper special VAT invoices require tax-control equipment and must be recorded in the VAT return. All sales and export documents must be reconciled to the invoicing system and the SAFE foreign exchange reporting database before export zero-rating is claimed.

Claims of export zero-rating are subject to strict STA and SAFE audit. The taxpayer must preserve all underlying export and foreign exchange records for at least five years. If the tax authority determines the zero-rated or refunded VAT was claimed fraudulently or without adequate substantiation, it will claw back the refunded VAT, impose late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, and apply administrative penalties of between 50% and 500% of the tax evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. In serious cases involving false export documents or fictitious cross-border services, criminal liability for VAT fraud and export tax fraud may attach under the Criminal Law, alongside retrospective reclassification of the supply as taxable at 6% or 13% VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed limited convertibility regime. Current account transactions are generally free of prior approval and legally convertible through designated foreign exchange banks, subject to genuine trade or service background verification and SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment monitoring. Capital account transactions remain strictly controlled: foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise requires SAFE registration through the bank, a foreign exchange capital account, and compliance with the negative list access regime. Profit remittances by a WFOE are permitted after annual audit and tax clearance, provided the enterprise has completed capital verification and has no outstanding foreign debt repayment obligations; remittances are processed under current account items as investment income, but banks require board resolutions, tax payment certificates, audited financial statements, and a FATCA/CRS declaration. Surrender requirements for export proceeds have been largely liberalised; enterprises may retain foreign currency in domestic foreign exchange accounts, but receipts must be repatriated into China and, when settled into Renminbi, must be supported by verified trade documents within the reporting timeline. Practical integration with the central bank's cross-border payment and anti-money laundering systems is mandatory, and any discrepancy triggers a bank suspension and SAFE penalty. Therefore, although the current account is de jure convertible, bank-level compliance under the know-your-customer, know-your-business and know-your-documents doctrine creates a quasi-approval process.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account in China; there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary current account proceeds, as the enterprise may decide to retain the funds in foreign currency or voluntarily settle them into Renminbi with a designated bank. The bank must verify the export declaration, invoice, bill of lading and the China International Trade Single Window data; export receipts should normally be credited within 90 days of goods declaration in the goods trade foreign exchange administration system, and if not matched within the bank's stipulated grace period, a written explanation and supporting logistics evidence are required. Mandatory settlement applies only to specific capital account revenues such as foreign debt drawdowns and foreign direct investment injection quotas, where the registered capital settlement proceeds may be converted at the account bank after capital verification, subject to applicable SAFE pilot zone rules. In practice, for a micro-SaaS exporter, each inbound subscription payment must be reported with a service trade code, and banks apply a one-to-one, item-by-item document matching rule for cross-border service receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the delegated foreign exchange control and enforcement authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs, including UK- and Hong Kong-licensed e-money institutions, are legally barred from opening domestic bank accounts or holding client funds in Mainland China without a payment institution licence issued by the PBOC. They cannot act as the depository institution for the WFOE's registered capital, cannot obtain SAFE foreign exchange registration, and cannot issue domestic account numbers for Chinese tax invoices. Their only permissible function is as a payment facilitation layer for cross-border platform collections, and even that requires partnership with a licensed Chinese bank or an established e-commerce cross-border payment institution. For treasury purposes, a micro-SaaS WFOE should maintain its primary liquidity and capital accounts in a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank and use EMIs only for merchant-of-record settlement disbursements where the underlying platform is a foreign acquiring entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent remits registered capital from its own account to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account via SWIFT, using purpose code Foreign Direct Investment or Construction of Enterprises in China in accordance with SAFE's balance-of-payments classification. The remittance must be filed with the PRC bank against the SAFE foreign exchange registration certificate, the company's business licence, the foreign investment information report entry, and the equity subscription documents. Upon arrival, a Chinese certified public accountant performs a capital verification and issues a capital verification report before the enterprise may apply for conversion of the registered capital into Renminbi under the settlement-in-account model. The settlement of registered capital must be used within the permitted business scope, and funds cannot be diverted to entrusted loans, securities investments, or real estate unless expressly permitted by pilot zone rules. For operational funding beyond capital, the WFOE may borrow foreign debt under the macro-prudential formula published by SAFE, with registration and bank-based settlement control.Cross-border trade settlements for the micro-SaaS business are executed primarily through SWIFT MT103 messages for service fees, software royalties, cloud hosting charges and affiliate payouts, with supporting contracts, invoices, service delivery reports and tax withholding vouchers attached to the bank's trade document system. For physical goods ancillary to the software licence, such as hardware API devices or promotional merchandise, the enterprise may use documentary collections or irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued by its Tier-1 bank; however, the most efficient clearing rail for recurring consumer subscription revenue is direct merchant-of-record settlement into the WFOE's foreign currency current account, followed by bank-settled conversion into Renminbi at the prevailing spot rate or a pre-booked forward rate. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 4)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends paid by a China-resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder (Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3 and 27, and Implementing Regulations Article 91). Treaty rates may reduce the WHT to 5% in many Chinese DTAs when the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the paid-in capital, provided substance requirements are satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: related-party tangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; related-party intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; and other related-party transactions (services, interest, royalties, commissions) exceeding RMB 40 million in a single tax year. Master File: mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions in the prior tax year exceeded RMB 1 billion. Transactions below thresholds still require an Annual Related Party Transaction Report with the annual CIT filling.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, but the general anti-avoidance rule (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47) and the substance-over-form doctrine (Tax Administration Law Article 35) require genuine business purpose and actual economic activity in China. For treaty relief, the OECD-derived beneficial ownership and principal purpose test applies. A WFOE engaged in micro-SaaS must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities - software development, price setting, risk management, customer contracting, and strategic decision-making - are performed or controlled in China, not merely held as passive ownership in a low-tax shell. Any offshore entity claiming income must be able to show real employees, office premises, and operational decision-making consistent with its claimed CIGA.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law: recovery of the underpaid tax plus late-payment interest; fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Article 201 of the Criminal Law: where the amount evaded is relatively large and exceeds 10% of taxable income, imprisonment up to 3 years and a fine; where the amount evaded is huge and exceeds 30%, imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and a fine.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 (and its successor Article 21 in the revised Company Law) if the director abuses the WFOE's separate legal personality to evade tax debts or defraud creditors. Directors who order or knowingly participate in the fraud are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax; their personal assets may be frozen, they may be placed on the tax blacklist, subjected to travel/exit bans, and criminally prosecuted.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Articles 41, 44, and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 authorize the tax authority to assess tax on a deemed basis, adjust related-party income, add interest (with additional adjustment interest if documentation is defective or incomplete), and impose administrative fines for failure to comply with filing obligations. Failure to submit the related-party report may result in a fine of RMB 10,000 or more, and serious cases may be treated as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing documentation or intentionally hide related-party transactions may be held personally responsible for the resulting tax underpayment under the abuse-of-in-personality doctrine. The tax authority may ask courts to blacklist the director, impose exit restrictions, and attach personal assets. If the TP omission is part of a fraudulent scheme, criminal liability under Article 201 (tax evasion) or Article 162 (collusion to evade tax) could lead to imprisonment.
Shell Company / Abusive Treaty Shopping / Artificial Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test (OECD BEPS Action 6) and SAT Announcement [2018] No. 9 beneficial ownership rules. The full 10% statutory WHT will be recovered from the non-resident, and the WFOE as withholding agent will be held liable for underwithheld tax plus daily interest under the Tax Administration Law. A fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed if the arrangement is deemed fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or structure the abusive shell arrangement can be personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and for assisting in tax evasion, particularly if they control the offshore conduit and cause the WFOE to pay dividends into it. Personal civil asset restitution, blacklisting, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for defrauding the revenue under the Criminal Law are all available sanctions. The corporate veil will be pierced where the shell arrangement lacks economic substance and is used to evade mandatory tax obligations.
