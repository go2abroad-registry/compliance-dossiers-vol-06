Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Taxation platform) accessible through the STA national portal at https://www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the statutory TIN equivalence is established through the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon business license issuance. The WFOE must complete initial tax registration with the competent local STA office within 30 days of incorporation; the tax registration is completed automatically in the STA taxpayer master database using the USCC as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identifier. The entity must subsequently complete tax-type recognition, invoice-printing/quota authorization, and VAT general taxpayer registration or small-scale taxpayer election. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no registered Chinese establishment but derives China-source income, the non-resident entity or its Chinese withholding agent must apply for a non-resident enterprise tax code with the competent STA office at the place where the payer is located; this code is used exclusively for corporate income tax withholding and VAT/surrogate collection obligations. The WFOE itself, as a Chinese-resident limited liability company, cannot elect to be treated as a disregarded branch and must obtain its Chinese TIN/USCC through the SAMR-ST integrated registration process.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese legal entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining their business license; there is no turnover threshold for taxpayer registration. The mandatory economic turnover threshold for VAT general taxpayer classification is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD equivalent); once this threshold is exceeded, the WFOE must register as a VAT general taxpayer and may not retroactively remain a small-scale taxpayer. For enterprises with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000, small-scale taxpayer treatment applies at the current reduced levy rate (historically 3%, with temporary preferential rates at 1% applicable from time to time), but the WFOE may elect general taxpayer status if permanent establishment, export refund, or input VAT credit requirements justify doing so.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to VAT general taxpayer exported goods: the goods must be customs-cleared as exports, physically leave Chinese customs territory, and the export proceeds must be repatriated into China through lawful foreign exchange channels; the VAT rate is set at 0% with corresponding refund of input VAT. For qualifying cross-border services and intangible products, including offshore software and mobile application licensing services, zero-rating eligibility requires that the recipient is a non-resident enterprise or individual, the service is consumed wholly outside mainland China, the sales contract, payment flow, and foreign exchange settlement documentation prove offshore delivery, and the service does not involve domestic use in China. The WFOE must maintain a complete export/offshore transaction dossier to substantiate the zero-rated treatment and must declare the zero-rated export sales in the VAT return under the applicable export tax refund/zero-rating line item. If an app sale through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing is structured as a distribution of digital software to foreign end users, the WFOE must evidence the offshore recipient identity, the place of consumption, and repatriation of net proceeds to China through the designated foreign exchange bank before applying the 0% rate or the export VAT refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Goods Declaration Report) duly stamped and released by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / International Express Waybill evidencing physical departure of exported goods from Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial invoices and export contract/purchase order showing the foreign buyer and delivery basis
  • SAFE Export Proceeds Verification Certificate or bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipt and Settlement Confirmation evidencing lawful repatriation of export consideration into the Chinese WFOE bank account
  • Customs-approved inspection/quarantine or commodity inspection documents when the exported goods are subject to statutory inspections
  • Digital electronic VAT special invoice or export invoice linkage record connecting the customs declaration to the VAT general taxpayer account
  • Packing list, insurance certificate, and freight documentation aligned with incoterms and cargo value declared in the customs export declaration

China enforces a national all-digital electronic invoice regime through the STA's Full-Link Digital Electronic Invoice system; all VAT invoices must be issued through the Electronic Tax Administration platform, and invoice data are transmitted in real time to the STA Golden Tax System (Golden Tax IV) for central validation, digital stamping, and allocation of a tax control number. Taxpayers must integrate e-invoicing APIs with their ERP/billing systems for special VAT invoices where required. Physical paper invoices are being phased out in favor of digital invoices; all invoice issuance, red-letter reversals, and statement filing obligations are subject to real-time fiscal clearance and anti-fraud matching against the STA's central tax-control database.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export tax refund and zero-rating claims using cross-matching of customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, invoice data, and VAT return filings. If any mandatory customs document, export proceeds remittance record, or offshore consumption evidentiary document is missing, unverified, inconsistent, or falsified, the claimed zero rate is revoked and all previously refunded or credited VAT is clawed back with statutory late-payment surcharge. Fraudulent export VAT claims are penalized under Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection with an administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the refunded tax amount, and the taxpayer may be stripped of export tax refund qualifications and listed in the tax integrity blacklist. Where the false export refund constitutes a crime, Criminal Law Article 204 for fraudulent export VAT refunds imposes severe criminal liability, including imprisonment of up to life imprisonment in aggravated cases, forfeiture of the illegal refunded amount, and monetary fines. The WFOE must therefore retain its export/offshore transaction file for at least five years and be prepared to produce original customs declarations, banking/SAFE receipts, and foreign exchange repatriation evidence during a tax audit.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with convertibility achieved for current account items but strict oversight over capital account items. The Renminbi is freely convertible for trade in goods and services, profit remittance, and current operational payments provided that a genuine underlying transaction evidencing the PRC bank is obtained and that the bank performs the 'real-instruction and real-deal' compliance review. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, cross-border loans, bond issuance, and outbound investments, require SAFE registration, approval, or post-event reporting. A WFOE is permitted to convert registered capital into local currency via a capital account using the Onshore Capital Account system, but such funds cannot be used outside the approved business scope. Profit distribution, dividends, and royalties remitted abroad are considered current account items; still, a corporate income tax withholding certificate, audited financial statements for the distributable profit, and a board resolution are mandatory, and banks transfer remittances after verifying that the amount does not exceed the distributable surplus. There is no blanket surrender requirement on export proceeds; enterprises may retain foreign exchange in their current account foreign currency deposits or settle in RMB according to commercial needs. However, all receipts must be repatriated and duly recorded in the bank's balance of payments system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, the mandatory export surrender ratio has been abolished. A WFOE receiving export proceeds arising from digital app sales is not required to convert any mandated percentage into RMB; the entity may retain foreign currency in its current account foreign exchange deposit account or sell it to a licensed onshore bank. Nevertheless, export proceeds must be repatriated to the PRC within the regulatory collection and verification timeline, generally not exceeding 180 days from the date of export declaration or the relevant payment trigger, unless an extension is approved by the bank and SAFE through the cross-border collection verification system. All export receipts must be reported to SAFE under the balance of payments statistical reporting rules with the proper trade or service item code.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the direct supervision of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally permitted to open corporate accounts or execute foreign exchange transactions on behalf of a mainland China WFOE. Chinese banking law and SAFE regulations require all onshore corporate foreign receipts and payments to be settled through licensed commercial banks operating within the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system. An unauthorized offshore EMI would expose the entity to penalties under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, the Anti-Money Laundering Law, and the PBOC's Rules on the Administration of Payment Services. Some licensed PBOC payment institutions may offer cross-border e-commerce collection solutions, but these institutions must hold a valid Cross-Border RMB Settlement qualification and a Payment Business License. The viability of using a pure fintech EMI is therefore Very Low, and the WFOE should structure its collection rails through a licensed domestic commercial bank or a qualified third-party payment institution engaged by the bank.

Root cause: Regular rapid disbursements to foreign cloud hosting providers, marketing platforms, and software developers create an outbound RMB to foreign currency velocity pattern that could be mistaken for trade mis-invoicing or capital flight.

Defense evidence: Deliver a board-approved treasury policy and payment calendar, original contracts or service-level agreements for cloud hosting, advertising and development services, invoices and delivery acceptances, tax withholding certificates as required, SAFE service trade registration documentation, and confirmation letters from the ultimate service providers explaining the necessity, scope, and remuneration basis of each cross-border payment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally permitted to open corporate accounts or execute foreign exchange transactions on behalf of a mainland China WFOE. Chinese banking law and SAFE regulations require all onshore corporate foreign receipts and payments to be settled through licensed commercial banks operating within the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system. An unauthorized offshore EMI would expose the entity to penalties under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, the Anti-Money Laundering Law, and the PBOC's Rules on the Administration of Payment Services. Some licensed PBOC payment institutions may offer cross-border e-commerce collection solutions, but these institutions must hold a valid Cross-Border RMB Settlement qualification and a Payment Business License. The viability of using a pure fintech EMI is therefore Very Low, and the WFOE should structure its collection rails through a licensed domestic commercial bank or a qualified third-party payment institution engaged by the bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A China Local File must be prepared by a WFOE whose annual related-party transactions any single category exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible-property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible-property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers; RMB 40 million for related-party services transfers; or RMB 20 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is additionally required when the taxpayer's annual aggregate related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 10 billion. The related-party transaction disclosure schedules must be filed annually without any threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies the economic substance doctrine through EITL Articles 46-47, Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations, and tax-treaty anti-abuse rules. The GAAR permits the tax authority to recharacterise structures with no reasonable commercial purpose and to deny treaty benefits to conduit companies. For the WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the mobile utility app must take place in China: software source-code development, server management, data analytics, user-experience decision-making, pricing strategy, and operational risk control. If these functions are actually performed in mainland China while profits are booked to a Hong Kong or Singapore affiliate lacking substance, the Chinese tax authority will reallocate income back to the WFOE and may apply the GAAR.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via sham transfer pricing and offshore profit stripping

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may apply EITL Article 41 to recompute the revenue at an arm's length price, impose a TP adjustment with 5% annual interest, levy a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Collection Law, and refer the case to the criminal system under Criminal Law Article 201 when the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors and the de facto controller are jointly and severally liable for the restituted tax and penalties if they directed the sham TP arrangement. Tax authorities may blacklist each executive for travel control under Article 44 of the Tax Administration Collection Law, preventing departure from mainland China until taxes and penalties are settled. Civil judgments can attach personal bank accounts and equity interests. Willful participation carries imprisonment of up to seven years and a personal criminal fine.
Willful failure to withhold and remit non-resident withholding tax on outbound dividends, royalties, and interest

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Collection Law, the WFOE as withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax, a penalty from 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount, and daily late surcharges at 0.05%. If the unremitted sum exceeds RMB 50,000, the case may be prosecuted as tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law with imprisonment for up to seven years and fines.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the remittance and instructed the accountant not to withhold is personally liable to pay the under-withheld tax and penalties out of personal assets. The court may freeze personal bank accounts, attach personal property, and issue a travel ban (blacklisting) against the director and the finance officer. A criminal conviction results in imprisonment from five to seven years for aggravated fraud and disqualification from acting as director in any PRC company.
Operating as an economic-substance shell and claiming DTA relief through a conduit shareholder

Statutory sanction: The tax authority applies the GAAR under EITL Articles 47 and 120, disregards the shell/conduit arrangement, reclassifies the income as China-source income taxable at 25%, and denies treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership tests and anti-abuse provisions of tax conventions. Financial penalties include the full reassessed tax, 5% annual interest on overdue amounts, and a 50%-500% evasive penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Collection Law. Criminal exposure arises if the taxpayer files false treaty-benefit declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors face corporate-veil piercing under Company Law principles where they abuse limited-liability status to evade compulsory tax obligations. Personal civil liability includes restitution of the entire underpaid State tax plus interest and penalties, enforced against personal bank accounts, shares, and real estate. The director is also placed on the tax exit-control blacklist, and the criminal path under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of up to seven years and an individual criminal fine.
