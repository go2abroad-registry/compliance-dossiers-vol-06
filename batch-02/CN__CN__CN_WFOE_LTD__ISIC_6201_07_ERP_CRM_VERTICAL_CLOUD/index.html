Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected for WFOE business license registration, tax registration, social insurance registration, and corporate bank account opening in China. Local tax authorities and banks require a verifiable physical address in China that is not merely a mail-forwarding box. In designated Free Trade Zones and government-approved incubators, cluster registration (concentrated registration) is available for software and technology enterprises, under which a government-designated commercial address may be used for the business license. However, the tax authority and the bank will still expect actual operational substance, such as office chairs, workstations, staff payroll, social insurance records, and ERP/CRM infrastructure. A cluster address should be supported by a written address-service agreement with the incubator or park operator; a standalone commercial mailbox outside such a scheme will be rejected by PBOC-regulated banks under customer due-diligence rules and by the tax bureau as an empty-shell indicator.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) constitutes the statutory taxpayer identification number for both corporate income tax and VAT; no separate TIN application is required. Following incorporation, the WFOE must complete consolidated tax registration through the integrated business license and tax registration workflow, and must finalise its tax identity profile with the competent local STA office via the e-Tax portal within 30 days, including details of legal representation, accounting standards, and intended business scope. The competent tax authority then confirms the WFOE's tax registration under the USCC. Foreign non-resident enterprises without a Chinese permanent establishment that conduct taxable activities in mainland China must instead apply for non-resident taxpayer registration with the STA office of the payer jurisdiction, submitting the home-jurisdiction tax identification number, certificate of residence, and supporting documentation for China-source taxable income, which facilitates Chinese payers in fulfilling withholding obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer may claim the 0% export VAT rate on goods that physically leave mainland China through a reported customs export declaration, and on qualifying cross-border services (including offshore ERP/CRM cloud services) supplied to a recipient located outside mainland China where the service is wholly consumed abroad. Statutory conditions include having VAT general taxpayer status, issuing the export invoice at 0% VAT, evidencing export with a customs declaration and international transport documents, and demonstrating receipt and repatriation of the foreign currency through designated banking channels within the statutory deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form as accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or equivalent international transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Export sales contract or written purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds receipt confirming foreign-currency repatriation
  • Export proceeds collection record retrievable through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) system

China mandates issuance of STA-controlled electronic tax invoices through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform for fully digitalized e-invoices. All VAT invoices must be generated using the taxpayer's digital certificate and transmitted to the STA system, where the invoice receives a unique control number and digital signature before it is considered valid for VAT deduction. For higher-volume issuers, approved invoicing software may integrate via STA-certified APIs; the anti-fraud framework operates through digital signatures, real-time or near-real-time issuance monitoring, and cross-matching of special VAT invoices in the national invoice verification platform.

Upon audit, the tax authorities in conjunction with Customs and SAFE may examine the complete export dossier and cross-check declarations against logistics and foreign-exchange records. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the STA will claw back the VAT refund, impose a daily late-payment surcharge, and, where a deliberate false export declaration is found, impose a fine of not less than one time and not more than five times the fraudulently obtained refund. The WFOE's export tax refund eligibility may be suspended or revoked, its taxpayer credit rating downgraded, and individuals involved may face criminal liability under PRC law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict bifurcation between current account and capital account transactions. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in vertical ERP/CRM cloud software services, the primary capital account transaction is the inbound equity investment. SAFE requires the WFOE to complete a Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) foreign exchange registration through its designated bank before any overseas equity funds are remitted. Once registered, the capital injection must be credited to a dedicated FDI foreign exchange capital account and then settled into RMB for permitted operating expenditures such as salaries, software development costs, cloud hosting fees, and office rent. Capital account convertibility for such injections remains relatively administrable but is not fully free: the use of foreign exchange capital is restricted to the approved business scope, and speculation, equity investment outside the scope, or investment in discretionary financial products is prohibited. On the current account side, software license fees, subscription charges, distributed profits, royalties, and operating expenses related to cross-border service trade are generally convertible after the bank verifies genuine underlying transactions through contracts, invoices, tax payment certificates, and payment purpose declarations. Profit remittance for a WFOE is legally permitted, but the bank will require an audited annual financial statement, board resolution approving the dividend distribution, an income tax settlement certificate, and a CRS/beneficial ownership declaration. In practice, the main hurdle is not the absence of convertibility but the highly documentation-intensive verification process and the residual risk that a bank may decline a remittance if the transaction purpose is ambiguous or the supporting contract lacks a clear economic substance nexus. Overall, the RMB is convertible on current account for trade and service payments, while capital account flows are open under a regulated foreign direct investment framework with no automatic market-based convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export proceeds earned by a Chinese WFOE from software licensing, SaaS subscriptions, implementation fees, or other cross-border service revenues, the statutory rule requires prompt repatriation to a SAFE-registered domestic foreign exchange receipt account. Specifically, export proceeds must be collected and repatriated to China through a designated bank within 90 days from the date of customs declaration for goods, or within the contractually stipulated settlement period for services provided to non-residents. Any delayed collection beyond 90 days must be reported to SAFE by the bank under the trade foreign exchange monitoring rules. There is no mandatory currency surrender ratio in the modern regime: the WFOE may either retain the foreign currency in its settlement account or voluntarily convert it into RMB at the market exchange rate. All export income must be reconciled against the declared transaction purpose code and supported by contracts, invoices from the Chinese entity, and evidence that the service was actually rendered to the overseas customer. SAFE conducts post-event verification through its cross-border payment monitoring system, and any mismatch between the declared purpose and the actual flow may result in a penalty and freezing of the foreign exchange account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Domestic banking regulation in China does not permit an unlicensed foreign fintech EMI to open a standalone corporate current account in RMB or to perform CNY clearing on behalf of a WFOE. The only legal clearing channels are licensed Chinese commercial banks which operates under PBOC and SAFE supervision. Therefore, pure online EMIs such as Revolut, Wise Business, or Payoneer, unless operating through a licensed Chinese bank partnership or holding a Chinese payment business licence with cross-border renminbi eligibility, cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for the mainland WFOE. A cross-border fintech may however be used for offshore collection of subscription fees from overseas customers, provided the funds are ultimately swept into the WFOE's domestic bank account via the proper export receipt declaration. The viability rating is low as a primary solution and supplemental-only in an offshore collection agency role. Any such arrangement must be contractually structured so that the foreign EMI acts solely as a payment processor, not as the legal settlement account holder, with reporting duties retained by the WFOE through its onshore bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound capital injection begins with SAFE foreign exchange registration for the WFOE. After the business license is issued, the WFOE must apply to a designated domestic bank for the FDI foreign exchange capital account, submitting the SAFE registration code, the Articles of Association, the investment agreement, and the certificate of approval or filing from the Ministry of Commerce. The overseas shareholder then remits the capital via SWIFT MT103, using the standard remittance purpose code for FDI equity investment from the parent jurisdiction to the Chinese bank's intermediate and final account. The bank will verify the remittance instruction, match it to the SAFE registration, and record the funds as foreign exchange capital. The WFOE may subsequently convert the foreign exchange into RMB at the bank's on-market rate for permitted operating expenses. After the contribution is made, the WFOE must engage a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report confirming the funds have been injected under the amended articles of association and the approval documents. The bank will also file the transaction with SAFE through the cross-border payment information system. No disbursement of the capital may occur before the CPA capital verification is completed and the bank receives the corresponding verification report.For outbound operating payments such as overseas cloud infrastructure procurement, international subcontractor fees, software licenses from third-party vendors, dividends to the foreign shareholder, or intercompany service charges from the parent, the WFOE must use the SWIFT MT103 message over the standard banking corridor. Each outbound transaction must be supported by a valid commercial contract, an invoice from the overseas counterparty, proof of performance, and the relevant tax withholding certificates such as a corporate income tax payment receipt for the non-resident counterpart. For service trade disbursements above the deduplication threshold, the bank will require the transaction to bear a SAFE payment purpose code and may require the submission of an tax completion certificate issued by the Chinese tax authority. Where the payment is a dividend distribution, the supporting file bundle additionally includes an audited financial report, the shareholder board resolution, the Chinese corporate income tax settlement documents, and a bank dividend remittance declaration. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Domestic banking regulation in China does not permit an unlicensed foreign fintech EMI to open a standalone corporate current account in RMB or to perform CNY clearing on behalf of a WFOE. The viability rating is low as a primary solution and supplemental-only in an offshore collection agency role. Any such arrangement must be contractually structured so that the foreign EMI acts solely as a payment processor, not as the legal settlement account holder, with reporting duties retained by the WFOE through its onshore bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Announcement (2016) No. 42, a Local File must be prepared when any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds is exceeded in a single year: (i) tangible asset transfers (purchase or sale) in excess of CNY 200 million; (ii) intercompany financial asset transfers above CNY 100 million; (iii) intangible property transfers above CNY 100 million; and (iv) other related-party transactions (including management, software services, cloud hosting, marketing, and administrative services) in excess of CNY 40 million. A Master File is mandatory if the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue in the immediately preceding tax year exceeds CNY 5.5 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting applies at the same CNY 5.5 billion group revenue threshold; annual related-party declaration (A100000-level schedules) is required for all WFOEs regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not maintain a separate offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code akin to certain island jurisdictions, but the CIT Law’s General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 47) and the Special Tax Adjustment rules validate the ‘substance over form’ doctrine. A cloud-software WFOE must perform its actual core income-generating activities (CIGA) within China: strategic development of vertical ERP/CRM features, user-data localization, hosting/vulnerability remediation, sales team presence, customer support, and risk management. If the WFOE is a paper entity merely recording profits while substantial procurement, R&D, and the key decision-making functions occur offshore, the SAT may re-attribute income to the non-resident entity under the premise of lack of economic substance, deny treaty benefits, and apply withholding tax for disguised distributions. Inbound cross-border services must likewise be tested for CIGA of the foreign provider under the permanent-establishment and anti-abuse provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false CIT declarations

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus interest and confiscation of evaded tax. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law triggers imprisonment from 3 to 7 years and monetary fines where the tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and is at least 10% of the amount payable. The State Taxation Administration will simultaneously confiscate any improper VAT credits and CIT deductions.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20(3) of the PRC Company Law where directorial abuse of the separate WFOE personality evades tax debts and seriously harms creditors; directors face unlimited personal asset restitution and may become jointly and severally liable. The tax authority may apply for an exit ban (blacklisting), denying the director international travel until the tax liability, penalties, and interest are fully settled. A criminal conviction leads to detention, hard-labor reeducation, and prohibition on serving as a director of any Chinese enterprise.
Failure to withhold and remit withholding tax on outbound dividend, royalty, or interest

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law orders the payer to pay the unwithheld tax and to be fined 50% to 300% of the amount not withheld and remitted. Article 40 and the Non-resident Enterprise Taxation Measures provide that cross-border fund remittance will be blocked by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange unless the withholding certificate is shown. Persistent failure to remit withheld tax may face criminal referral for misappropriation of tax funds.

Director personal liability: Company Law Article 147 imposes a duty of loyalty and diligence; the court will hold directors personally accountable for authorizing dividend remittances without tax clearance. The non-resident shareholder plus the WFOE's legal representative and financial controller face joint personal liens, seizure of personal bank accounts, and asset freezing. Travel bans are routinely issued against legal representatives for unpaid withholding tax. If the WFOE is declared an empty shell, the director is exposed to rescue capital contributions and restitution of the principal.
Transfer pricing sham / lack of economic substance with abusive intercompany license and service agreements

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law allow the SAT to make a transfer-pricing adjustment to apply the arm's length principle and re-characterize the transaction. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection and the Special Tax Adjustment Implementation Measures, the adjustment may carry a 5-point-per-year interest surcharge (bare minimum), plus additional penalty up to 50%-250% for concealed and unsubstantiated related-party transactions. The result is a denied deduction for the royalty, additional dividend-withholding tax, and possible reclassification to a taxable distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or assent to a fabricated intercompany cost structure are treated as accomplices in tax evasion. They can be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties. Chinese law in this context applies economic substance doctrine to pierce the nominal separate existence of the WFOE: the foreign shareholder and the WFOE director’s personal assets can be attached to satisfy the corporate tax debt. In severe abusive-scheme cases, directors face imprisonment under Article 201 of the Criminal Law and an exit ban, while the offshore IP holding company may be blacklisted and stripped of treaty benefits.
