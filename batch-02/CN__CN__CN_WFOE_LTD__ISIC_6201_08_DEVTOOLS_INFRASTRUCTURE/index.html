Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA). Online electronic portal: STA Electronic Tax Service Platform (China E-Tax), the unified national online tax filing, invoice issuance, and tax clearance portal for all PRC legal entities, including wholly foreign-owned enterprises.

E-government filing portal

For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) formed as a Chinese limited liability company, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) upon issuance of the corporate business license. Procedure: (1) incorporate the WFOE through the AMR online or counters services and obtain the Business License containing the 18-digit USCC; (2) complete mandatory tax registration with the competent STA office within 30 days of business license issuance, using the USCC as the tax identification number; (3) register for corporate income tax, value added tax (VAT), stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax, payroll withholding tax, and social insurance via the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform; (4) apply for value added tax taxpayer status classification (small-scale or general taxpayer) and electronic invoice quota; (5) complete open-bank transaction reporting under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) system f necessary for cross-border receipts. No separate physical TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is the sole state-recognized tax account identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC enterprises, including WFOEs, must complete VAT registration at formation regardless of historical or expected turnover. The mandatory economic turnover threshold triggering mandatory general taxpayer registration is annual taxable sales in excess of RMB 5,000,000 (Renminbi five million), at which point the taxpayer must apply for VAT general taxpayer status. Entities with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may remain small-scale taxpayers, subject to a reduced collection methodology and invoicing restrictions. The monthly taxable-sales threshold below which no VAT is actually payable for small-scale taxpayers is RMB 100,000, but this is a collection threshold and does not exempt an operating WFOE from registration, archiving, and monthly/quarterly filing obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT treatment is available to a WFOE for qualifying cross-border supplies under the PRC VAT pilot regulations. For physical goods, the WFOE must hold a valid Customs Export Declaration evidencing actual departure of goods from mainland China and raising foreign exchange revenue from a non-PRC buyer; such exports are entitled to a 0% VAT rate with refund of associated input VAT. For qualifying software, developer infrastructure, database, and API monitoring cloud services supplied to overseas recipients, the WFOE may apply the 0% rate or exemption if the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the recipient is an offshore entity, the service contract indicates a foreign currency consideration or approved RMB settlement, and the transaction qualifies as a cross-border sale in the modern-services category under the STA's cross-border tax administration rules. Services rendered to domestic Chinese users through local data centers or with significant performance in mainland China do not qualify for zero-rating and remain taxable at the standard modern-services rate of 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Customs Bill of Entry) generated from the electronic customs clearance system
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of exported goods from mainland China (for goods exports)
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher showing inward remittance from the offshore buyer and matching the export invoice value
  • Cross-border service contract or commercial invoice identifying the offshore recipient, the nature of the cloud/software service, and the foreign territory of consumption
  • Zero-rated VAT e-invoice issued and cleared through the STA e-invoice platform for the underlying export transaction
  • Working papers and internal substantiation logs demonstrating that no portion of the service is performed or consumed within mainland China

China operates a mandatory national digital e-invoice administration regime known as the fully digitalized electronic invoice ('e-invoice') system, administered through the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform. Newly established WFOEs must obtain an electronic invoice quota from the competent STA office, issue e-invoices through the official e-invoice module, stamp each invoice with the taxpayer's unique digital certificate, and transmit the invoice data instantaneously to the national STA fiscal database in real time. No paper invoice may substitute for the mandatory digital clearance process once the taxpayer is incorporated and registered as a digital e-invoice issuer. Sale transactions, VAT input credit claims, and export zero-rating substantiation are all linked to the digital invoice records archived by the STA; any discrepancy between issued e-invoice data, customs declarations, and foreign exchange receipts may trigger automated anti-fraud scrutiny.

Export zero-rating positions are subject to retrospective STA, local tax bureau, customs, and SAFE audit scrutiny. If the WFOE cannot substantiate the export with the full customs, e-invoice, service-contract, and foreign-exchange remittance documentation within the statutorily required 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation window, or if the STA determines that a zero-rated transaction was fictitious, inflated, or ineligible, the tax authority will claw back the entire VAT refund plus input-VAT credits taken, impose late-payment surcharge interest accruing daily, and apply administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Where export tax rebate fraud is found to be intentional, criminal liability may attach under Chinese criminal law, with imprisonment, confiscation of illegal gains, permanent loss of export-tax-refund qualifications, and reputational debarment from cross-border trade facilitation programs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with significant administrative controls over cross-border capital flows. The Renminbi is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, provided transactions are supported by genuine underlying trade or service contracts and are reported through the SAFE monitoring system. Capital account transactions, however, remain heavily regulated and require case-by-case approval, filing, or registration. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must register its foreign direct investment with SAFE through a designated bank before receiving inbound equity capital, and all subsequent capital account movements are tracked under the capital account information system. Profit remittances are permitted for lawful after-tax earnings, but require audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the local tax authority, and proof that the capital contribution has been fully paid and verified. Dividend distributions must be reported under the current account and are convertibility-approved by the settlement bank, but the withholding tax must be paid first. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds, but all receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days and either retained in foreign currency accounts or settled into RMB via designated banks. The practical limitation for foreign treasury managers is not formal inconvertibility but the layered documentation, bank-level verification, and tax clearance procedures that can delay repatriation and liquidity deployment. Businesses must therefore maintain tight coordination between the Shanghai treasury team, the local settlement bank, and SAFE reporting channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless SAFE approves an extension. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: China abolished the compulsory surrender system, so the WFOE may retain export foreign exchange in its foreign currency settlement account or sell it to a designated bank at market rates. All export receipts must still be matched against validated customs declarations and invoicing data in the trade monitoring system, and any significant date or amount mismatch will trigger SAFE scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Stripe Treasury, or European e-money institutions have Low direct viability for serving a mainland China WFOE. These EMIs do not hold a banking license from the PBOC and cannot open domestic settlement accounts or capital accounts in China. They may support inbound cross-border collections from overseas customers through partner banks, but any funds received outside China must still be brought into the mainland via lawful SAFE-registered channels, which requires a domestic bank relationship. Chinese regulations limit payment institutions to licensed domestic third-party payment platforms with SAFE cross-border settlement approvals; an unlicensed offshore EMI would be legally barred from acting as the primary treasury rail. The practical architecture is to use the offshore EMI as a treasury visibility tool for overseas receivables, while the Chinese WFOE maintains its primary operational and regulatory accounts at a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. All direct inward capital injections and trade settlements must be cleared through the domestic banking system to satisfy SAFE reporting, tax withholding, and capital verification requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital must be routed through a SAFE-designated domestic bank using the WFOE's capital account. Before remittance, the WFOE must complete FDI registration at the local SAFE branch or its delegated bank, and obtain a Foreign Direct Investment Registration Notice. The overseas parent should initiate a SWIFT MT103 with remittance purpose clearly coded as direct investment equity injection, and the receiving bank must be notified in advance so the funds can be credited to the capital settlement account under the correct SAFE transaction code. After the inbound funds are credited, a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report, confirming that the contribution amount was received and recorded in RMB or foreign currency in compliance with the registered investment schedule. Disbursement of capital account funds is limited to lawful operating purposes and must be supported by corresponding contracts, invoices, and tax declarations. The same routing protocol applies to subsequent capital increases, which require an updated SAFE filing and a new bank account designation where necessary.Outbound payments from the WFOE are cleared through the current account settlement system at the Tier-1 domestic bank. Standard SWIFT MT103 transfers are used for operating expenses, vendor payments, software licensing fees, and overseas payroll, provided the corresponding SAFE trade code is stated and the underlying contract, invoices, and tax certificates are submitted. For physical goods purchases, commercial letters of credit issued by the domestic bank are the preferred rail, allowing the WFOE to open LCs in favor of overseas suppliers with documents such as bills of lading, commercial invoices, packing lists, and inspection certificates. Documentary collections are acceptable for low-risk repeat transactions, but the bank will still require full trade documentation to confirm the transaction is genuine. All outbound disbursements require the payment purpose code to be accurately populated and must be consistent with the registered business scope. For service imports such as cloud infrastructure licensing or external developer tool subscriptions, the bank may require proof of service consumption, the tax withholding certificate, and a statement verifying that the transaction is not a disguised capital outflow.Onshore deliverable forward FX contracts with a maturity matched to a real trade or investment exposure, executed at a SAFE-designated bank under the real demand principleCross-currency swaps for balance-sheet hedging of foreign-currency denominated loans or intercompany capital obligationsFX options with a real underlying revenue or cost exposure, purchased from a licensed domestic bank with full underlying documentationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for onshore RMB settlement by a mainland WFOE; offshore NDF tenors cannot be booked through the domestic banking system and any attempt to do so would violate SAFE derivative supervision rules

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs such as Mercury, Stripe Treasury, or European e-money institutions have Low direct viability for serving a mainland China WFOE. These EMIs do not hold a banking license from the PBOC and cannot open domestic settlement accounts or capital accounts in China. They may support inbound cross-border collections from overseas customers through partner banks, but any funds received outside China must still be brought into the mainland via lawful SAFE-registered channels, which requires a domestic bank relationship. Chinese regulations limit payment institutions to licensed domestic third-party payment platforms with SAFE cross-border settlement approvals; an unlicensed offshore EMI would be legally barred from acting as the primary treasury rail. The practical architecture is to use the offshore EMI as a treasury visibility tool for overseas receivables, while the Chinese WFOE maintains its primary operational and regulatory accounts at a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. All direct inward capital injections and trade settlements must be cleared through the domestic banking system to satisfy SAFE reporting, tax withholding, and capital verification requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous documentation) mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property, RMB 100 million for intangible property, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (excluding services under specified exemptions). Master File is mandatory if consolidated related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion. The WFOE must also submit Special Tax Areas (ST) schedules for connected-party transactions irrespective of threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the CIT Law. The STA may disregard transactions and re-attribute income where the WFOE is considered a shell lacking core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as strategic decision-making, risk assumption, and key operational functions. Although China has not adopted standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore regimes, a China-resident WFOE must maintain a physical office, local management, payroll, and decision-making capacity; otherwise DTA benefits and the CIT residence status may be challenged as being located purely on paper but without substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Invoices and Fabricated Costs

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration, the taxpayer is ordered to pay the underpaid tax within the prescribed timeframe and monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. The use of forged VAT invoices further triggers Criminal Law Articles 205 and 206; for issuing false special VAT invoices, fines and imprisonment up to life sentence may be imposed on the entity and responsible persons.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors knowingly sign or direct the false invoicing scheme. Directors face personal civil liability for the shortfall, inflation-adjusted restitution, and exclusion from exit (出境限制) via the STA's blacklisting mechanism. Criminal conviction under Article 201 for tax evasion can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years if the evaded tax exceeds RMB 500,000 and exceeds 10% of taxable income; aggravated circumstances above RMB 1,000,000 may result in imprisonment of 7-10 years plus property confiscation.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing and Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the CIT Law, the STA may make an arm's length adjustment and also impose late payment interest of 0.05% per day. Under Article 47 (GAAR), the STA may disregard the intercompany transaction and recharacterize the entire arrangement, applying a 10% penalty on the adjusted amount if a punitive adjustment is made. Implementation measures in SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016) require contemporaneous documentation and filing of related-party transaction forms.

Director personal liability: If the transfer pricing could constitute willful evasion, personal liability attaches to directors who were involved in pricing decisions. The directors may be subject to civil liability for the unpaid corporate tax plus interest, and the STA may impose exit bans under the Exit Control List (Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law). For deliberate transfer pricing fraud without reasonable business rationale, the criminal offence of tax evasion under Article 201 may be charged, leading to imprisonment and restriction of personal liberty. Directors are also exposed to civil law claims from minority shareholders and joint-venture partners for breach of fiduciary duty.
Shell Company Abuse and Denial of DTA Treaty Benefits

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 and the general prevention of treaty abuse rules, the STA may re-characterize the WFOE as a non-resident permanent establishment of its foreign parent, impose the full 10% non-treaty WHT on all profit distributions, and levy a 50% penalty on the under-collected WHT (Article 63 TCAL). Additional sanctions include imposition of late filing penalties for non-filing of non-resident withholding returns.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitate treaty abuse may be jointly and severally liable for the shortfall in withholding taxes; the STA may blacklist directors and prohibit departure from China until taxes are settled. In cases of fraudulent misrepresentation to tax authorities, criminal liability can arise under Article 201 of the Criminal Law. The director could face imprisonment up to 7 years, confinement, and confiscation of personal assets derived from the evasion scheme. If the abuse involves foreign investment regulatory approvals, civil sanctions from the foreign exchange authority and market regulator may further block the transfer of funds.
