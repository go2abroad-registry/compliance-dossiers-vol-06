A bare virtual office or commercial mailbox address is generally rejected by Chinese tax authorities and banks for a cybersecurity SaaS WFOE. The State Taxation Administration requires a verifiable operating address for tax registration, VAT invoice control, and inspections, and local commercial banks require physical premises, an executed lease contract, utility invoices, and on-site management staff before opening a settlement account. For cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration testing SaaS, the company should maintain a physical office or a serviced office with a real lease and local employees, because the tax authority and the bank will treat a mail-forwarding address as a red flag indicating non-compliance or shell-company risk.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account is required for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company in China. The foreign investor must specify the registered capital in the Articles of Association and must ensure the full subscribed capital is paid in within five years after the business license is issued as required by Company Law Article 47, unless a shorter period is stated in the Articles or by law. Paid-in foreign currency capital must be remitted through a duly licensed bank and recorded in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) reporting system; funds may not be withdrawn or distributed prior to lawful capital verification and financial statement preparation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; the official online electronic portal is the China Electronic Tax Administration e-Tax platform, accessible through the national e-Tax web portal and the local State Tax Bureau e-Tax client, with taxpayer authentication based on the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code and legal representative identity.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company in China Mainland, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the Administration for Market Regulation on the business license automatically becomes the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number; a separate TIN application is not required. The initial tax identity is recorded through the One-Set-Up multi-department registration procedure, and the e-Tax account is activated with the competent State tax bureau. Required supporting material includes the business license, articles of association, office lease or place of business proof, legal representative and finance officer identification, passport or corporate certificate of the ultimate foreign investor, bank account information, authorisation letters, and a statement of expected tax and invoice affairs. A foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese establishment that makes taxable supplies in China must complete non-resident taxpayer registration with the same local tax authority and may be required to appoint a withholding agent.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not recognise check-the-box elections or hybrid entity elections; the WFOE limited liability company is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise at the entity level on worldwide income and is a VAT taxpayer. The only available statutory status election is VAT taxpayer status: small-scale taxpayer versus general VAT taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayers may use the 3 percent levy rate (with transitional reductions where applicable), while general VAT taxpayer status is compulsory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory in RMB when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold an entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, the exported products must be declared to Chinese customs and physically leave the customs territory under a valid Chinese export customs declaration; title, risk and commercial benefit must pass to the overseas buyer; the sale must be arm's length; and payment must be received in foreign currency from the overseas party within the foreign exchange repatriation period. For qualifying offshore digital services such as cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration-testing SaaS supplied to non-resident enterprises, the service recipient must have no Chinese establishment or place of business, the service must be wholly consumed outside China, the supply must fall within the State Taxation Administration catalogue of zero-rated cross-border services, and revenue records must demonstrate that the service is separately supplied for offshore use with no domestic utilisation in China.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (customs Bill of Entry / export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt and repatriation voucher
  • Export contract and commercial invoice with packing list
  • Customs valuation and tariff classification documentation for exported goods
  • For SaaS cross-border services: service agreement with the overseas recipient, technical scope and user-access evidence proving offshore consumption, and the STA cross-border services zero-rating statement forms

China requires digital invoicing through the national Electronic Invoice System and the Golden Tax Project Phase IV infrastructure. The Fully Digitized Electronic Invoice (FD-EI) standard is being applied nationwide; invoices must be issued from the electronic invoice service platform using the tax authority digital cryptographic stamp and transmitted to the State Taxation Administration in real time. No manual offline invoices are recognised for VAT proof. Enterprise billing systems must connect through certified fiscal interfaces or APIs to obtain invoice serial numbers, apply the uniform national invoice layout, and enable anti-fraud deduction and credit matching through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform and the electronic invoice ledgers.

The State Taxation Administration and China Customs cross-match export declarations, logistics records, foreign-exchange receipts and VAT refund claims. Unverified, unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating positions are subject to retrospective clawback of input VAT credits and export tax refunds, late-payment charges of 0.05 percent per day, penalties ranging from 50 percent to five times the tax involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, and criminal referral for false export declaration, tax evasion or fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law. The entire historical export zero-rating chain may be reopened for audit when documentation is missing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a tightly managed foreign exchange system anchored by the PBOC and enforced through SAFE. Under this regime, current-account transactions such as trade in goods and services, ordinary profit repatriation, and royalty/licensing fees are freely convertible after presentation of authentic underlying documentation and a bank-level 'real need' review. Capital-account items, by contrast, require case-by-case approval and are generally subject to mandatory registration, quota thresholds, and earmarking. A China WFOE must obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate (FDI) from SAFE before receiving shareholder equity injections. Capital contributions in foreign currency enter a dedicated capital escrow account ('Capital Account'), and the funds can only be converted into RMB after the bank verifies its declared purpose, typically for payment of operating expenses, purchasing cybersecurity equipment, software royalties, or salaries. Profit remittance to the non-resident parent is permitted only after annual statutory audit, tax filing and full settlement of corporate income tax (including withholding tax on dividends), and must be presented as a 'dividend remittance application' with the bank. Recent circulars have simplified the process, but the practical hurdles remain banks' internal compliance review, a full beneficial-ownership review, and the absence of a fully free cross-border capital market. In summary, current-account conversion is open but documentary-heavy; capital-account conversion remains liberalized for greenfield FDI but the use of proceeds is strictly monitored via the SAFE system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE-monitored regime, export proceeds received for products and services must be repatriated into China within 30 calendar days from the date of export declaration. Following the 2012 foreign-exchange settlement reform, mandatory conversion ratios were abolished; enterprises may retain up to 100% of export receipts in foreign currency in an approved foreign-currency current account. However, they must complete the transaction's declaration within 5 working days of receipt in the Bank's Exchange Reporting System. In practice, a bank may restrict retention where the beneficial owner is in a low-tax jurisdiction or where the proceeds exceed a prudential threshold; in such cases, the bank may require an enhanced documentary dossier consisting of the underlying commercial invoice, an export goods declaration, a customs verification form, and a written explanation of the retained-currency purpose.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

The direct use of offshore fintech electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut as principal clearing accounts for a WFOE in mainland China is legally barred. Chinese regulations require all local-currency and foreign-currency banking transactions to be conducted through domestically licensed banks that maintain direct connection to the PBOC's National Advance Clearing System (CNAPS) and the Foreign Exchange Payment System (CFXPS). Offshore EMIs that do not hold a banking license issued by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (now NFRA, under PBOC supervision) cannot obtain a RMB account, receive capital-injection funds, or execute capital account conversions. Their practical use is limited to ancillary cross-border collection from non-Chinese customers, provided the WFOE has completed SAFE's service trade/current account registration and routes the funds via a local designated bank. Therefore, the financial viability of relying on an offshore EMI as a standalone treasury rail is Low; a WFOE must engage a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all statutory foreign-exchange and capital transactions. The EMI may only act as a technology front-end that consolidates invoices and payment records, with settlement captured by the domestic bank's SWIFT/MT103 inbound instructions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: The direct use of offshore fintech electronic-money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Airwallex, or Revolut as principal clearing accounts for a WFOE in mainland China is legally barred. Chinese regulations require all local-currency and foreign-currency banking transactions to be conducted through domestically licensed banks that maintain direct connection to the PBOC's National Advance Clearing System (CNAPS) and the Foreign Exchange Payment System (CFXPS). Offshore EMIs that do not hold a banking license issued by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (now NFRA, under PBOC supervision) cannot obtain a RMB account, receive capital-injection funds, or execute capital account conversions. Their practical use is limited to ancillary cross-border collection from non-Chinese customers, provided the WFOE has completed SAFE's service trade/current account registration and routes the funds via a local designated bank. Therefore, the financial viability of relying on an offshore EMI as a standalone treasury rail is Low; a WFOE must engage a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all statutory foreign-exchange and capital transactions. The EMI may only act as a technology front-end that consolidates invoices and payment records, with settlement captured by the domestic bank's SWIFT/MT103 inbound instructions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. Master File: an enterprise with total revenue of RMB 1 billion or more plus related-party transactions, or whose ultimate parent group has consolidated revenue above RMB 2 billion, must prepare a Master File.

Economic substance scope: PRC CIT has no Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations label, but the PRC general anti-avoidance rule (CIT Law Art. 47), the Beneficial Ownership test (SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9), and BEPS minimum standards impose substantive requirements. A WFOE in the cybersecurity SaaS business must still have its CIGA in China: physical office, employees, technical infrastructure, risk management, R&D decisions, contracts with customers, and client onboarding. An offshore shell that merely holds shares and routes profits cannot claim treaty benefits; the SAT may recharacterize the entire structure and impose 10% WHT plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing and contemporaneous documentation failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 41 and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42; the SAT may make a transfer pricing adjustment and impose interest plus a penalty from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 63.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who approve fraudulent pricing may be jointly and severally liable through veil-piercing under Company Law Art. 20; the legal representative is named on the tax authority blacklist and subject to an exit travel ban under Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 44; knowing evasion engages Criminal Law Art. 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
Shell-company abuse and DTA treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities deny treaty relief under the beneficial ownership requirement of SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the PRC general anti-avoidance rule (CIT Law Art. 47), reimpose 10% statutory WHT, add 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and impose a 50%-500% penalty for tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: The ultimate beneficial owner and directors of the WFOE cannot hide behind the corporate form; Company Law Art. 20 exposure, personal restitution for diverted tax payments, blacklisting, and a travel ban apply. Criminal liability for fraudulent investment/tax fraud may be charged under Criminal Law Art. 204 where false documents are used to obtain treaty refunds.
Thin capitalization and disguised equity distribution

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 46 and the PRC judicial explanation on thin capitalization disallow the excessive interest deduction; the disallowed amount is recharacterized as a deemed dividend and is subject to 10% dividend WHT, plus daily surcharge and a penalty of 50%-500% of the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham loan agreements, backdated resolutions, or false foreign-exchange filings are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall; the court may pierce the veil and order restitution of corporate assets, while the legal representative is placed under exit control and blacklisted; repeated deliberate evasion may result in criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Art. 201.
