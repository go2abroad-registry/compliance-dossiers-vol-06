Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE under Chinese law; however, the registered capital must be fully subscribed within the time limit specified in the Articles of Association (typically 30 years from incorporation). The capital may be contributed in cash or in-kind, and must be deposited into a designated bank account in China. No escrow or blocked account requirement applies, but the capital must be verifiable and used for legitimate business purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable – The company operates in a standard office environment; however, the office must comply with the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China (2019) and obtain a fire safety inspection certificate from the local fire department. The company must also ensure workplace safety per the Occupational Health and Safety Law, but no special industrial clearance is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with electronic filing through the STA-administered National Electronic Tax Office (ETax) online portal.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identity is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically issued and shared by the State Administration for Market Regulation with the STA under the multi-license integration system. This USCC functions as the TIN and VAT registration number; no separate TIN procurement application is needed for the locally incorporated entity. A foreign non-resident enterprise with no Chinese establishment must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration through the ETax non-resident taxpayer module before earning income from China source, providing its home-country registration certificate, foreign board-and-director details, address, contract meeting the source-of-income rule, and designated bank relationship. The competent tax authority then records a non-resident registration code for withholding and indirect-tax compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections for entity classification; a WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise. The only relevant commercial election is that a small-scale VAT taxpayer may voluntarily be registered as a general VAT taxpayer upon satisfying accounting and compliance conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for initial VAT registration; the WFOE is VAT-registered from incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. The WFOE's EdTech subscription service is an information technology / educational service subject to the 6% VAT rate for domestic supplies, while the 13% statutory headline rate applies to goods sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of goods from the PRC customs territory qualifies for 0% VAT upon verified customs departure and foreign exchange receipt. For the WFOE's digital EdTech language-learning services, 0% VAT applies to qualifying cross-border services delivered to foreign non-resident users and consumed entirely outside China; eligibility requires clear evidence of the non-resident customer, offshore consumption, foreign payment remittance, and no substantial domestic user base or local consumption in China.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration captured electronically in the China Customs export declaration system
  • Original bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical exit of merchandise from China
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank inward remittance advice or State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-monitoring record evidencing receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • For digital EdTech subscription sales: non-resident customer agreement, IP/geolocation evidence, app-store settlement reports from Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, and cross-border foreign-currency payment receipts

VAT invoices must be issued only through the STA electronic invoice system controlled by Golden Tax Phase IV. Fully digitalized electronic invoices carry STA-assigned digital fingerprints, and issuance data is transmitted in near-real-time to the STA clearance platform through the National Electronic Tax Office APIs. The anti-fraud mandate requires three-way matching among invoice, export/customs event, and foreign exchange receipt for zero-rated exports; cross-border app-store sales must be reconciliable to Apple IAP and Google Play Billing settlement statements, with e-invoices issued to overseas B2B buyers or aggregate reporting accepted for foreign B2C consumers.

The STA and China Customs jointly mine Golden Tax Phase IV data to verify export declarations, invoice issuance, foreign exchange collection, and payment settlement. A zero-rating claim that fails audit triggers retrospective clawback of the VAT refund or zero-rating benefit plus additional VAT liability, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax amount under the PRC Law on Tax Collection and Administration. Intentional fabrication of exports, forged bills of lading, or fictitious app-store receipts is referred to the criminal courts under the PRC Criminal Law, where fraudulent export refunds can attract imprisonment up to life; fraudulent assessments are not statute-barred, while ordinary documentation defects remain open for retrospective reassessment for up to five years.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated convertibility framework. Current account transactions, including software licensing fees, EdTech subscription commissions, and payments for services, are largely convertible after the settlement bank completes a real-need authenticity review of contract, invoice, tax payment, and SAFE service trade registration records. Capital account items are not freely convertible by ministerial decree: each equity injection, shareholder loan, or liquidation remittance requires a SAFE foreign exchange registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and actual-use verification. The old mandatory export surrender system has been replaced by a retention-and-settle model: export proceeds must be repatriated through a designated bank and may be held or voluntarily converted into RMB; there is no fixed statutory conversion rate for ordinary software and service exports. Profit repatriation hurdles remain material: an audited statutory financial report, proper dividend resolution, verified cumulative distributable profits, corporate income tax and withholding tax clearance, SAFE capital project reporting, and bank-level ultimate beneficial ownership and sanctions clearance are all required before the dividend is wired out. EdTech firms must further separate Apple and Google billing receipts as cross-border service trade claims and maintain matching platform settlements and royalty calculations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For physical goods exports, proceeds must be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange account within 180 calendar days after the export customs declaration date and matched by the bank in the SAFE trade monitoring system; no uniform mandatory conversion ratio is imposed at the national level. For EdTech cross-border service exports, no customs declaration clock applies; instead, the bank must match each Apple/Google consolidated payout to the underlying service invoices, and any receivable still unpaid 90 days beyond the referenced invoice due date triggers a SAFE service trade authenticity review. The WFOE may retain foreign currency or voluntarily sell it to a domestic bank at the prevailing market rate, although a bank may require an explanation for unusually large retained export balances.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as a primary liquidity rail. Domestic corporate RMB and foreign exchange current and capital account services are reserved for licensed Chinese banks and cannot be provided by offshore EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex unless those EMIs hold a PBOC and SAFE payment institution license and operate through a Chinese domestic bank partner. For a WFOE requiring statutory capital injection and SAFE registration, only a Chinese bank can maintain the regulated capital account records; an EMI can act only as a supplementary collection conduit for low-value international card payments, and even then it must comply with the PBOC non-bank payment institution rules, which currently limit currency conversion and require a Chinese custodian bank. The practical viability is Low for primary treasury and Medium only for ancillary e-commerce disbursements if a licensed domestic partner is used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete SAFE foreign-investment information registration and open a foreign exchange capital account. The non-resident shareholder should wire capital through SWIFT MT103 to that account; the remitter name and bank account must exactly match the registered shareholder, and the reference field should contain the SAFE foreign exchange registration number and the Chinese balance-of-payments purpose code for equity capital from direct investment. After receipt, the bank reports the transaction into the SAFE capital project information system, and any conversion into RMB is done on a real-need basis after the bank verifies the intended use of funds; a Chinese certified public accountant should then perform capital verification and issue a report for registration and subsequent remittance compliance. The resulting RMB amount must be credited into the domestic basic settlement account and spent only on the WFOE operating scope; prohibited uses include equity investment, real estate purchase outside pilot rules, wealth management, and speculative securities transactions. For larger receipts the bank may also require a board resolution, share subscription price evidence, and a source-of-funds confirmation.For EdTech operating payments, use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border wire transfers of royalties, software licensing, teacher-side payments, cloud service fees, and marketing expenses, supported by contract, invoice, tax voucher, and tax withholding proof; use clearing rails such as China International Payment System (CIPS) when the beneficiary accepts cross-border RMB. For physical goods such as course accessories or hardware imports, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are available through the issuing Chinese bank, but software-heavy EdTech flows should rely primarily on MT103 and CIPS. Every outgoing payment must also be integrated with SAFE trade in services reporting and the bank AML transaction monitoring system.Onshore CNY/USD, CNY/EUR, and CNY/HKD deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts against genuine future CNY or FX cash flows, supported by underlying contracts or forecast exposure, entered under a master agreement with the settlement bank and subject to margin collateral requirements.Non-deliverable forwards are not permitted for the onshore WFOE as its own hedge where domestic CNY is deliverable; NDFs are generally restricted to offshore counterparties without an onshore CNY funding base, and any group-level NDF strategy must be structured outside the WFOE balance sheet.Cross-currency swaps for one to five year capital and dividend exposures may be used only for documented principal, interest, or dividend cash-flow hedging of the WFOE equity capital or shareholder loans, and must comply with SAFE real-demand derivatives principle; speculative naked positions are prohibited and the bank will demand ISDA and credit support annex documentation plus SAFE reporting.
