Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the National Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) electronic portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with provincial and municipal tax service branches administering VAT and enterprise income tax filings on the same portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license. Procedure: (1) the foreign investors file for enterprise name pre-approval and WFOE incorporation through the State Administration for Market Regulation online system; (2) after SAMR approves the establishment and issues the business license bearing the USCC, tax registration is automatically completed under the 'Multiple Certificates in One' reform because the STA receives the incorporation data electronically; no separate TIN application is required; (3) the legal representative completes real-name authentication on E-Tax and files supplementary taxpayer information, including accounting standard, bank account details, VAT taxpayer type preference, and invoice issuance entitlement; (4) the E-Tax system then activates the WFOE as either a general VAT taxpayer or small-scale VAT taxpayer and authorises the issuance of full digital e-invoices. A foreign non-resident parent entity has no automatic PRC TIN; if it derives China-source income independently without a permanent establishment, it must register with the competent tax authority through a domestic withholding agent or obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration limited to that China-source activity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for every enterprise upon SAMR business licensing; it is not triggered by turnover. For general VAT taxpayer classification, the mandatory threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; a below-threshold taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general status. Small-scale taxpayers currently benefit from the RMB 100,000 monthly VAT exemption policy but remain registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies when goods are legally exported from mainland China under a customs export declaration and the payments are received in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB from non-resident buyers. For the no-code website builder service (ISIC 6201 software and IT services), 0% VAT and associated exemption apply under Ministry of Finance and STA Circular 36 (2016) when: (i) the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual located outside mainland China; (ii) the service is wholly consumed outside the customs territory; (iii) the consideration is received in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB from abroad; and (iv) the taxpayer files the cross-border taxable service zero-rating/exemption declaration and retains the contract, service specification, issued zero-rated e-invoice, payment receipt, and evidence of overseas delivery and use. Pure online software-as-a-service delivery does not require customs clearance, but the documentary bundle must prove foreign consumption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) for physical export shipments
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing export of goods from mainland China
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Voucher (statutory export proceeds receipt, equivalent to an inward remittance certificate)
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Zero-rated full digital e-invoice or cross-border taxable service zero-rating declaration accepted by the STA
  • Foreign recipient tax identification and service delivery/consumption evidence for offshore digital services

China operates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (full digital e-invoice) regime under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoices must be issued through the National Electronic Tax Bureau or an STA-approved e-invoice service platform; the system assigns a unique electronic invoice number and a tax-control verification stamp in near real time, transmits the invoice record to the national VAT invoice electronic database, and enables transaction-level comparison with customs, forex, bank settlement, and enterprise income tax data. Taxpayers using enterprise resource planning systems may connect through API to the e-invoice issuance platform; paper VAT invoices remain only for legacy scenarios where full digital e-invoicing is not yet applied.

The STA Golden Tax Phase IV system cross-matches VAT invoices, customs export declarations, foreign exchange collection data, and bank settlement records. A zero-rated export declaration will be rejected and output VAT assessed at the applicable domestic rate (6% for IT/no-code services; 13% for goods) where the documentation is incomplete, the foreign-exchange proceeds are not received within 180 days, or the service is found to be consumed in mainland China. Recovery adds daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05%, an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, and a specific penalty of one to five times the refund obtained under Article 66. If the false export or zero-rating claim constitutes fraudulent export tax-refund crime under the PRC Criminal Law, responsible persons are subject to imprisonment of up to life imprisonment and monetary fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a modern, structurally controlled foreign exchange regime. The Chinese Renminbi is fully convertible on current account transactions, covering bona fide trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties and ordinary operating expenses, subject to authenticity verification and tax compliance. Capital account transactions, however, remain tightly supervised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Inbound direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is permitted after completion of the FDI registration and establishment of a dedicated foreign exchange capital account. The WFOE may settle foreign currency capital into Renminbi only for genuine business expenditures within the permitted negative list framework; speculative investment in domestic equity or real estate is prohibited. Profit remittance to the overseas parent is treated as a current account item and is generally allowed, but the paying bank must receive a certified Chinese audit report, corporate income tax return, tax payment confirmation, board resolution declaring the dividend, and a valid SAFE registration. Surrender obligations for export proceeds have been abolished since 2012; enterprises may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts or settle voluntarily. Nevertheless, all receipts must be repatriated to China within prescribed timing, reported through the banking system, and supported by underlying transaction documents, invoices, and customs records. The practical effect for a low-code software exporter is that cash flow can move with relative efficiency, but every cross-border leap requires documentary discipline and alignment with SAFE circulars.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage conversion in current law. Export foreign currency receipts generated by the WFOE must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account through approved channels within 120 days of shipment or service delivery, unless an extension is approved by the local SAFE branch. The enterprise may voluntarily choose between retaining the proceeds in foreign currency and converting to Renminbi, but all transactions must be reported using the correct trade and settlement purpose codes and matched with underlying contracts, invoices, and bank statements.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China and its wholly administered State Administration of Foreign Exchange

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions, including digital EMI platforms promoted by Hong Kong or Singapore-licensed providers, possess only very limited legal viability for a mainland WFOE. Because the RMB is restricted to domestic financial institutions holding PBOC banking licenses, any payment or settlement solution must eventually clean through a Chinese bank. Offshore EMIs cannot issue a primary onshore bank account number for a WFOE, cannot participate in SAFE FDI registration, and cannot act as the sole treasury operator. Their practical utility is confined to ancillary collection channels for overseas customers, often working as merchant-of-record payment facilitators. Legally, they are barred from providing core bank account services in mainland China, and any attempt to bypass domestic clearing rails would violate the Administration of Foreign Exchange and Payment Institution regulations. Therefore the onboarding viability is rated Low. The WFOE must maintain its primary operational currency account with a licensed domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas shareholder must effect the transfer exclusively through SWIFT MT103 from a recognized correspondent bank into the WFOE's designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account opened with the Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The remittance instruction must clearly reflect a capital injection purpose code compatible with SAFE's direct investment classification, and the remitter's KYC file must support the identity and ownership of the company. Immediately after receipt, the WFOE must complete the SAFE FDI registration if not already finalized, file the Foreign Investment Information Report with the local market supervision authority, obtain a capital verification audit report from a Chinese certified public accountant, and deposit settlement proceeds in a RMB settlement account linked to the capital account. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is statutorily required under CIT Law Article 42 and the SAT related-party transaction filing rules if the annual related-party transaction amount reaches any of the following thresholds: RMB 200,000,000 for tangible property transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for intangible property transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for related-party services; RMB 100,000,000 for financial asset transfers; or RMB 100,000,000 for related-party debt/investment arrangements. A Master File is additionally required when the aggregate annual related-party transaction amount reaches RMB 1,000,000,000 or more.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations as a separate statute, but the general anti-abuse rule under CIT Law Article 47, the arm's length principle under Article 41, and the beneficial ownership and Principal Purpose Test contained in applicable tax treaties operate together to require genuine economic substance. The WFOE must demonstrate that profits earned from no-code website builder software and related services correspond to functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed in China. Core Income-Generating Activities such as product development, customer contracting, pricing, billing, support, and risk management should occur in China through actual employees, premises, and decision-making. If these activities are performed outside China but the WFOE invoices for them, there may be a permanent establishment risk abroad; if the WFOE is a shell that merely invoices while the foreign parent performs all substantive functions, China can disregard its separate existence, reattribute profits, and deny treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The competent tax authority may invoke CIT Law Article 47 and Tax Collection Law Article 35 to recharacterize the arrangement, disregard the conduit entity, deny treaty benefits, and impose the full 10% statutory withholding tax or, if there is no treaty entitlement, 10% WHT plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%. If the scheme constitutes intentional tax evasion, a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax may be imposed under Tax Collection Law Article 63, and the entity may be included in the Major Tax Violation Case blacklist for public disclosure and administrative discipline.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law if the directors or shareholders abuse the separate legal personality of the WFOE to evade tax and harm creditors. Directors can be held personally and jointly liable for restitution of diverted assets, subjected to tax enforcement travel bans and exit restrictions if the company has unpaid tax, and blacklisted as de facto controllers. Where the arrangement is criminal tax evasion or fraud, the directors and the legal representative may face investigation under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years, fines, and disqualification from corporate management.
Tax Evasion and False Dividend/Withholding Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Law Article 63, the tax authority will collect the unpaid tax and late-payment surcharge and impose a monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax. Article 64 applies where a false tax return is filed and the taxpayer fails to pay tax; serious fraud triggers criminal referral. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 provides imprisonment of up to 3 years or criminal detention, plus fines, where the evaded tax amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable; imprisonment of 3 to 7 years and fines apply where the amount is gigantic and exceeds 30%. A unit crime also makes the directly responsible supervisor and CFO liable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false tax returns, instruct concealment of revenue, or authorize undisclosed profit distributions are exposed to personal civil liability through corporate veil piercing. The tax authority may freeze the company's bank accounts, seize assets, and impose credit blacklisting on the legal representative. In criminal cases, the responsible directors and the person in charge may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to 7 years, receive criminal fines, and be ordered to make restitution. Travel bans and high-consumption restrictions operate under tax enforcement blacklisting mechanisms while the tax deficiency remains unpaid.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Sham Intercompany Financing/IP Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Article 41 and the SAT transfer pricing documentation rules, the tax authority may apply the best method, make a transfer pricing adjustment, and impose special tax adjustment interest at the PBOC benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points for related-party adjustments. Under Tax Collection Law, an underpaid tax arising from false documentation or concealment can attract a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax due, and the failure to provide required related-party documentation may support a re-estimation of taxable income and the denial of deductions. In egregious cases, the conduct is criminalized as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute sham intellectual property licenses or artificial debt push-downs without commercial purpose may be liable when the company cannot pay its CIT deficiency because profits have been stripped out to related non-residents. PRC courts may lift the corporate veil and hold directors and controlling shareholders jointly liable to the extent of their abuse. Unpaid corporate tax can trigger asset preservation, travel bans, and blacklisting of the legal representative or de facto controller. If the sham arrangement is proven to be intentional concealment of taxable income, the directors face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years, fines, and restitution of the evaded tax.
