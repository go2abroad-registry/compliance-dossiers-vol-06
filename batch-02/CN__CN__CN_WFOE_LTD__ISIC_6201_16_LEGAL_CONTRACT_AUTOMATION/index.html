Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under PRC Company Law or Foreign Investment Law for an ordinary WFOE; there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. The sole shareholder must determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association, state the contribution method and schedule, and pay the subscribed capital by the final contribution deadline. Under the revised Company Law, capital contributed in cash or in kind must be fully paid in accordance with the AoA and, in practice, the stated capital must be reasonably sufficient to support the announced business scope and operating needs; delayed or fictitious capital contribution subjects the shareholder to rectification orders and administrative penalties. The foreign investor may remit funds into a foreign-currency capital account only after the business license is issued and the account has been filed with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through the bank.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable for a manufacturing Civil Defence fire permit or storage-related civil defence rating. The leased office must nonetheless comply with the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China through a fire-safety inspection or filing with the local fire and rescue department, and the employer must register staff for work-injury insurance and comply with the Work Safety Law. No industrial structural-load certificate or hazardous-chemical site clearance is required for a pure digital-services WFOE.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

The State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the national unified Electronic Tax Service Portal (e-Tax) and the STA VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform; the e-Tax portal handles VAT, corporate income tax, stamp duty, payroll and export tax refund filings, with provincial STA offices administering the same centralized portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE (limited liability company) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the legal-person business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN and VAT ID for Chinese tax filings. Step 2: SAMR registration is pushed electronically to the STA through the one-stop inter-agency registration mechanism, automatically establishing taxpayer status; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Step 3: Activate e-Tax access with the USCC and complete taxpayer identity, financial accounting setup, tax category registration (enterprise income tax, VAT, stamp duty, payroll withholding, urban maintenance and construction tax) within 30 days, and select small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status. Step 4: Complete FDI foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the designated bank, open a capital account and RMB settlement account, and record paid-in capital. Step 5: A foreign non-resident parent is not generally required to obtain a separate Chinese VAT ID; cross-border payments to the parent are subject to withholding by the WFOE under the WFOE's USCC, with treaty relief substantiated by a Certificate of Tax Residence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable turnover is the compulsory VAT general-taxpayer registration threshold; tax registration itself is mandatory upon business license issuance. Below RMB 5,000,000, the entity is normally treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer (3% collection rate, with temporary 1% relief currently available) unless it elects general taxpayer status. The WFOE's core modern services (SaaS, legal document automation, e-signature authentication) fall under China's information technology services category subject to VAT at 6% for general taxpayers, whereas physical goods sales are at 13%, and qualifying cross-border supplies may be zero-rated or exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Eligibility for zero-rating or VAT exemption is determined under the PRC VAT export provisions. For tangible goods, actual customs exit from Chinese customs territory to an overseas destination substantiates a 0% VAT export supply with input-VAT refund. For the WFOE's qualifying offshore digital services (cloud document automation, e-signature authentication, legal contract SaaS), the supply is zero-rated or VAT-exempt when: (i) the service buyer is a non-resident enterprise with no Chinese permanent establishment using the service; (ii) the service is wholly rendered from China but consumed outside Chinese customs territory; (iii) there is no domestic end-user access or consumption in China; (iv) the contract and commercial invoice are issued to the overseas customer; and (v) the consideration is received in freely convertible foreign currency and recorded in the SAFE cross-border settlement system. Zero-rated services allow input-VAT credit or refund; exempt cross-border services may not require output VAT but generally do not allow input credit.

  • For physical goods export: Customs Export Goods Declaration (export bill of entry equivalent) bearing the Chinese export declaration number and customs release mark.
  • For physical goods export: Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Air Waybill (air freight) showing departure from a Chinese customs port and destination outside China.
  • For all exports: export commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract and, where relevant, the digital electronic invoice with a zero-tax-rate notation.
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation evidence: bank receipt and SAFE cross-border settlement/export proceeds collection voucher showing receipt of the export consideration in convertible foreign currency.
  • For digital or offshore services: signed master service agreement with the non-resident customer, e-signature audit trails, delivery and usage logs, overseas access/consumption evidence, and a written statement from the overseas customer confirming no Chinese consumption.
  • For export tax rebate filing: the STA e-Tax-platform-generated export tax rebate application with cross-linked e-invoice and Customs data.

China operates the full-process digital electronic invoice (DEI) system and the Golden Tax IV central fiscal-monitoring architecture. Taxpayers must generate invoices exclusively through the national e-Tax portal or STA-certified API integration, with real-time transmission to the STA central fiscal database. Every invoice is protected by a unique electronic serial number, digital signature and QR anti-fraud stamp; the buyer's input-VAT credit is available only after the central platform validates the seller, buyer, tax rate and amount. No shadow invoicing or off-platform clearing is permitted, and invoice data are continuously reconciled with VAT returns, customs declarations and foreign-exchange receipts.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to automated risk profiling and continuous cross-checks among the STA Golden Tax IV system, China Customs, the VAT invoice database and the SAFE foreign-exchange monitoring database. Failure to retain the mandatory documentation bundle, failure to repatriate or substantiate export proceeds within the statutory period (including the 180-day repatriation benchmark), or any false or unsupported statement can trigger: (i) retrospective clawback of the VAT refund or zero-rating benefit obtained, with interest; (ii) re-characterization of the export as a domestic taxable sale or service with VAT assessed at the applicable rate plus urban maintenance and construction tax and education surcharge; (iii) a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date; (iv) administrative penalties under Articles 63-66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Administration of Tax Collection, usually one to five times the tax or refund unlawfully obtained; (v) cancellation or suspension of the taxpayer's export tax refund qualification and inclusion in the tax dishonesty blacklist; and (vi) criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates, carrying penalties up to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict bifurcation between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are generally freely convertible subject to truthful declaration and submission of supporting customs, invoice, and contract documentation. Capital account transactions, however, remain heavily controlled and require SAFE registration, approval, or filing depending on the nature of the flow. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must complete Foreign Investment (FDI) FX registration with SAFE before injecting foreign currency share capital; the converted RMB may then be used only within the approved business scope. Profit remittances to offshore shareholders are permitted after annual audit, tax clearance, and submission of board resolutions, but the bank will verify the legitimacy of underlying income and the completeness of corporate income tax and withholding tax filings. Export proceeds must be repatriated onshore through designated bank channels; although full forced surrender was phased out, enterprises are required to monitor trade collection under the goods trade monitoring system and may retain funds in foreign currency accounts or settle into RMB. Surrender timelines are formally governed by the thirty-day receipt window for export collection under SAFE circulars, with penalties for overdue unrepatriated amounts. The WFOE should therefore maintain a robust treasury calendar that aligns invoicing, customs declaration, collection, settlement, and outward remittance cycles with SAFE reporting deadlines to preserve full convertibility compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations on goods trade foreign exchange management, export proceeds for general goods must be repatriated to China within the statutory collection period, normally 90 days from the export date, followed by mandatory reconciliation through the Bank's SAFE Monitoring System; enterprises may choose either to retain the foreign currency in a Foreign Currency Settlement Account or convert it to RMB voluntarily. No fixed mandatory conversion ratio applies, but 100% repatriation is compulsory, and failure to repatriate within the 90-day window without a prior SAFE-approved extension triggers delinquency classification, penalties, and downgrading of the entity's trade facilitation status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is very low for any China-domiciled WFOE lacking a local banking license. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex or Currenxie cannot act as the primary treasury rail in China because SAFE and PBOC require all capital account transactions, FDI injections, export collections, and profit repatriations to route through domestic banks under the Regulatory Information Sharing platform. No unlicensed foreign EMI is permitted to open domestic yuan accounts or process corporate payments in mainland China; therefore, the WFOE may only use such EMIs for incidental offshore spending outside China, while all onshore compliance flows must remain with tier-1 domestic banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed through a designated domestic bank that is SAFE-authorized to process FDI. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI cross-border viability is very low for any China-domiciled WFOE lacking a local banking license. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex or Currenxie cannot act as the primary treasury rail in China because SAFE and PBOC require all capital account transactions, FDI injections, export collections, and profit repatriations to route through domestic banks under the Regulatory Information Sharing platform. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is generally triggered when total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or where group-level entry conditions in the annual related-party report require consolidated disclosure. Quarterly/annual related-party transaction tables must be filed together with the annual CIT return.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate an Economic Substance Regulation modeled on offshore jurisdictions, but the economic-substance doctrine is embedded in the general anti-avoidance rule (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47) and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (State Taxation Administration Announcement 2017 No. 6). The tax authority will disregard a WFOE or contract structure lacking real decision-making, personnel, assets, and risks, and will recharacterize the arrangement based on its commercial substance. For treaty relief, the OECD Beneficial Ownership commentary and China's treaty practice require the income recipient to exercise substantive powers over the income, so a conduit WFOE holding intangible rights without core income-generating activities will lose treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Abuse of corporate form and treaty shopping through a substance-less conduit WFOE

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits and disallowance of the intermediary company; beneficial owner recharacterization under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6; the tax authority may reconstruct taxable income using a deemed profit margin and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under the Company Law where the director intentionally uses the company to evade tax and abuses limited liability; directors and actual controllers become jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Blacklisting through the tax credit reporting system imposes travel restrictions on legal representatives, and egregious, intentional non-payment may trigger criminal liability for tax evasion.
Fabricated cost scheme and false invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, tax evasion is punished by recovery of the unpaid tax, a late-payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to five times the tax underpaid. False issuance of special VAT invoices violates Criminal Law Article 205 and may lead to criminal fines, confiscation, and imprisonment up to life for responsible persons in severe cases.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance director, and tax responsible persons who signed or approved false invoices are personally exposed to criminal prosecution; if the WFOE lacks assets, directors personally compensate the tax authority through asset restitution and freezing orders. Directors also face a personal fine and a statutory ban on serving as directors in any Chinese company.
Omission of transfer pricing documentation and cross-border payment disclosures

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disregard undisclosed related-party transactions, adjust taxable income by the deemed profit method, and impose the standard understatement penalty of 50%-500% of the adjustment plus daily surcharges. Refusal to produce documentation permits the authority to make a unilateral assessment and accelerates a special audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false annual related-party disclosures are jointly liable for the resulting tax deficiency under civil recovery procedures; intentional concealment can be prosecuted as tax evasion, leading to confiscation of assets, travel bans, and up to seven years imprisonment depending on the amount evaded.
