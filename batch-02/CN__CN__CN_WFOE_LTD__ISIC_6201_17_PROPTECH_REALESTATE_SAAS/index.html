Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise and no blocked escrow account rule. The registered capital is set by the sole shareholder in the Articles of Association. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, contributions must be fully paid within five years from incorporation, unless a special industry rule imposes immediate paid-in capital. For ISIC 6201 SaaS/PropTech, a modest registered capital is acceptable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online taxpayer service is through the official Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) portal operated by the STA and provincial/local tax service centers.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE, the foreign non-resident shareholder first incorporates the PRC limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtains a business license bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the one-stop market registration data-sharing process. The WFOE then completes the mandatory taxpayer registration confirmation through the Electronic Tax Administration portal, confirming the legal representative, financial officer, accounting system, bank account, and the ISIC 6201 business scope. The STA uses the USCC as the sole tax identification number for both enterprise income tax and VAT; no separate VAT certificate is issued. If the WFOE expects annual taxable service turnover to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or if it needs to credit input VAT and claim export zero-rating, it must also apply for general VAT taxpayer status. Tax registration is completed without a separate physical visit except when bank-account and on-site verification are selected by the local tax service center.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification. The PRC does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election; the WFOE is a resident enterprise subject to entity-level enterprise income tax on worldwide income. Under VAT, the small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer status is a registration/administrative status, not an entity classification, and is tested by annual turnover; no hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.06%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover of services exceeds RMB 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million); below this ceiling the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it applies voluntarily, and export zero-rating/input-credit recovery generally requires general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for VAT zero-rating where the export is physically declared to customs and the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer. Cross-border software services, including software rental/SaaS supplied to a non-resident client and consumed wholly outside mainland China, qualify for VAT zero-rating under the cross-border taxable services rules (Ministry of Finance/STA policies, including Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 as updated). Eligibility requires the WFOE to be a VAT general taxpayer, the recipient to be an overseas entity, the service not to be consumed or performed for the benefit of a Chinese resident in China, and the consideration to be paid from outside China and booked as an export/service-trade FX receipt. The claim is made through the STA export/zero-rating filing module and supported by a contract, invoice, service-delivery records, and foreign-exchange receipt evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Export) for physical goods; not applicable to a pure digital SaaS service unless hardware, embedded devices, or physical media are bundled with the software platform.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or carrier cargo receipt for physical goods exports; for SaaS export, a system-generated digital service delivery or usage record is used in place of a transport document.
  • Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Income Declaration issued by a SAFE-designated bank as the PRC statutory substitute for an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); FIRC is not a Chinese statutory form and should not be requested for a PRC WFOE.

VAT invoices including special VAT invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices are issued through the STA electronic invoice platforms controlled by Golden Tax Phase IV. Issuance data is cleared to the STA in real time; special VAT invoices carry a 20-digit invoice code, invoice number, encrypted anti-fraud validation code, and are subject to automatic anti-counterfeiting checks. The WFOE must use only STA-authorized billing software/APIs for e-invoice issuance and must maintain the XML/OFD data payload of each issued digital e-invoice for audit.

The STA automatically cross-matches VAT zero-rating claims with Customs export declarations, SAFE foreign-exchange collection records, and bank payment records. If the supporting bundle is incomplete or inconsistent, the STA reclassifies the supply as domestic taxable and claws back the VAT input credits or refunds, with overdue tax interest at 0.05% per day and a fine of between 50% and 500% of the underpaid tax under the PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection. False declaration of exports or fabricated cross-border services for the purpose of obtaining export VAT refunds/zero-rating can also constitute criminal tax fraud, exposing the legal representative and finance officers to criminal liability, and triggers downgrade of the WFOE's taxpayer credit rating with loss of export facilitation status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China is a jurisdiction with stringent foreign exchange controls on capital account transactions. The CNY is convertible for current account trade and service transactions in the ordinary course of business, but capital account items require case-by-case SAFE registration, approval, or filing. A WFOE may convert its registered capital from USD to CNY only after completing foreign exchange registration with SAFE and opening an FDI capital FX settlement account at a licensed domestic bank. The resulting RMB funds are held in a restricted capital settlement account and must be used for business purposes consistent with the approved registered scope; diversion into securities investments, entrusted loans, or speculative real estate equity is generally prohibited except under narrowly defined SAFE pilot programs. Profit remittances to the overseas parent are permitted but require audited PRC financial statements, a board resolution approving the dividend distribution, tax filing receipts proving payment of withholding tax, and CRS/FATCA declarations. Service trade outbound payments, including royalties to affiliated technology partners, trigger additional tax settlement certificates and transfer pricing documentation. The overall assessment is that CNY is not freely convertible; operational liquidity can be managed efficiently through domestic commercial bank FX products, but all cross-border capital movements are monitored, verified, and constrained by SAFE compliance infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE has abolished any forced surrender percentage for export proceeds. The current legal framework requires that export receipts, whether from goods or services, be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account unless an offshore retention exemption is formally approved by SAFE. Under the prevailing trade verification system, goods export proceeds must be collected and verified within the time limit in the underlying commercial contract, generally not exceeding 180 days from the date of customs declaration. For cross-border service trade, including SaaS licensing and PropTech platform subscription revenue, the repatriation deadline follows the contractual payment maturity plus a 30-day administrative grace period. The WFOE may freely choose to keep the foreign currency in its designated FX account, or sell it to a commercial bank at the market rate; no statutory conversion ratio or surrender ratio is currently imposed.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Revolut, and comparable virtual account platforms do not possess a PBOC banking license or SAFE cross-border payment institution license for Mainland China. They are therefore legally barred from opening domestic CNY clearing accounts, issuing CNAPS-capable local account identifiers, or acting as the primary treasury banking layer for a China WFOE. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary collection wallets for overseas market receivables, but all onshore receipts, capital injections, FX settlement, and regulatory reporting must route through a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. Relying on an EMI as principal clearing rail would violate PBOC AML requirements and expose the WFOE to frozen funds, non-bank counterparty risk, and SAFE cross-border reconciliation failure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The oversea shareholder remits the registered capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital FX settlement account at an approved Tier-1 commercial bank. The remittance instruction must include the SAFE-accepted BOP purpose code for inbound direct investment capital injection and the WFOE's SAFE foreign exchange registration identifier. Upon credit, the receiving bank performs a SAFE funds-in verification, converts the USD into CNY at the market rate, and deposits the funds into a restricted RMB capital account. A bank-issued capital verification statement and a PRC CPA capital verification report will document the paid-in capital. The RMB capital is then disbursed only against qualified underlying contracts, operational invoices, payroll, and registered business expenses. The bank monitors the end-use to ensure that the proceeds do not flow into equity investments, entrusted loans, real estate purchases, or securities trading.The WFOE will use domestic bank SWIFT MT103 outbound remittances for cross-border service trade settlements, including software licensing, cloud infrastructure, overseas marketing, and professional fees. Each outbound payment requires the signed underlying service agreement, valid foreign invoice, proof of service receipt, and a tax clearance certificate issued by the PRC tax authority. Royalty and affiliated-party payments are subject to transfer pricing documentation and, where applicable, withholding tax certification. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are considered secondary rails and will only be deployed when the transaction involves physical equipment, infrastructure procurement, or bank-supported trade under import contracts. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the PRC Company Law and the Organic Measures for Financial Reporting, every Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (limited liability company) is statutorily required to engage a licensed Certified Public Accountant (CPA) firm to audit its annual financial statements, irrespective of turnover, balance-sheet size, or headcount, because the WFOE is typically a single-member limited liability company and is an exercise of foreign investment subject to annual independent audit. Additional explicit audit triggers include: (a) total assets exceeding RMB 50 million, (b) annual revenue exceeding RMB 30 million, (c) a headcount of more than 200 employees, or (d) listing, bond issuance, or financial institution status. Audited financial statements must be submitted to the SAMR annual report system and tax authority upon request.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a China-resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder. Under many comprehensive Double Tax Agreements, the rate is reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE; otherwise, standard DTA dividend rates are generally 10% or the domestic residual rate.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed and the tax settled within five months after the fiscal year-end; for calendar-year WFOEs the deadline is May 31. Quarterly provisional returns are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where annual intercompany tangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, intangible property transfers exceed RMB 40 million, or other related-party transactions such as service fees and SaaS licensing exceed RMB 100 million. Master File obligations arise where group revenue is RMB 1 billion or more and controlled transactions are present. Country-by-Country Reporting applies where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue is at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations law, but economic substance is enforced through the beneficial ownership test under SAT Notice 2018 No. 9 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. For a PropTech SaaS WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities must include software engineering, cloud server management, data processing, contract negotiation, customer billing, technical support, and strategic decision-making performed in China. If these CIGA are artificially relocated to a low-tax jurisdiction or an empty intermediary holding company, the STA may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the arrangement, and restate taxable income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Annual Return

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law imposes a penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid tax and the collection of overdue tax; Article 32 imposes a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion by fines and imprisonment of up to three years where the amount evaded is relatively large and exceeds 10% of tax payable, and three to seven years where the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who organize, approve, or sign fraudulent returns can have the corporate veil pierced under Company Law principles: personal bank assets and real estate may be frozen and seized for restitution, the director may be placed on the tax blacklist and restricted from leaving China, and the director faces criminal detention and imprisonment for tax evasion.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse and Conduit Financing

Statutory sanction: The STA may apply GAAR under Article 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law to disregards the abusive arrangement, deny treaty benefits, and reapply the domestic 10% withholding tax. Under Notice 2018 No. 9, the foreign shareholder must prove beneficial ownership; failure results in recovery of the under-withheld tax, daily late surcharge, and a penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law if the denial is based on false declarations.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs treaty-benefit declarations for a conduit shareholder can be held jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax and non-compensated losses; criminal liability may be imposed for fraudulent evasion or aiding tax evasion, and the director's personal assets can be attached and departure from China prohibited.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The STA is authorized under Articles 41-44 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Notice 2016 No. 42 to adjust taxable income using prescribed transfer pricing methods and to assess tax by deemed profit where documentation is absent. The adjusted underpaid tax bears a 0.05% daily late surcharge under Article 32 of the Tax Collection Law; failure to provide related-party information can lead to a penalty under Article 44 of the CIT Law, and the intentional concealment of controlled transactions may be prosecuted under Tax Collection Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors bear personal responsibility for the completeness of transfer pricing documentation and board-level approval of intercompany pricing. Where deliberate profit shifting is proven, the corporate veil may be pierced to recover the tax deficiency from the directors' personal estates, a blacklist travel ban may be imposed, and the directors may face imprisonment for criminal tax evasion if the adjusted amount reaches the statutory threshold.
