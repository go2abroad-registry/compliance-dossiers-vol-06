Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered address for a logistics technology SaaS WFOE. Chinese Company Law requires a company to maintain a domicile that is its principal place of business. Local SAMR authorities normally demand a lease agreement, property ownership certificate, or landlord's permit matching the registered address. Tax authorities and banks reject mail-forwarding arrangements, and for industries involving telematics and fleet dispatch, the tax authority expects the entity to maintain books, contracts, and physical meeting capability at the registered site. Some free trade zones allow flexible address filing, but an actual physical site will still be required for tax inspection, bank account opening, and commercial substance verification.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None for a general software/SaaS WFOE outside the Negative List. The 2023 Company Law Article 47 requires the full registered capital subscribed in the Articles to be contributed within five years from incorporation; there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: STA National Electronic Tax Service Platform, accessed via the official STA website www.chinatax.gov.cn and through provincial electronic tax bureaus.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Prepare foreign investor corporate documents with notarization and legalization/apostille, WFOE articles of association, registered address certificate, and board/shareholder resolutions. Step 2: Submit the WFOE establishment application through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) one-stop enterprise startup service to obtain a business license carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Step 3: Complete supplementary tax registration within 30 days after business license issuance through the electronic tax platform: confirm legal entity resident status, ISIC 6201/NAICS 488490 industry classification, accounting and inventory policies, legal representative, bank accounts, shareholders, and expected annual turnover. Step 4: Determine VAT status; if expected annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory; below the threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or elect general VAT taxpayer status if accounting standards allow. Step 5: Apply for e-invoice issuance capability, obtain the tax control U-key/digital certificate, and configure the electronic invoice platform with the financial system. Step 6: Complete State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) registration at a designated bank for foreign-currency export proceeds. Step 7: For an actual non-resident foreign enterprise without a Chinese permanent establishment supplying taxable services into China, register for non-resident VAT withholding/tax registration through the competent provincial STA or rely on the Chinese domestic counterparty's withholding obligation; the WFOE itself, as a Chinese tax-resident legal entity, requires only the USCC/TIN rather than a separate foreign ID.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None with respect to pass-through or check-the-box elections; China has no entity classification election or hybrid mismatch election under general tax law. The available statutory election is VAT taxpayer status: a new WFOE below the RMB 5,000,000 general VAT registration threshold may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment with simplified levy, or elect general VAT taxpayer status to recover input VAT and claim export zero-rating; once annual turnover exceeds the threshold, general VAT taxpayer status is compulsory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual VAT-taxable turnover (excluding VAT) for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all incorporated WFOEs must complete tax registration and obtain a Unified Social Credit Code/TIN upon establishment regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Eligibility for 0% VAT export treatment arises for physical goods exported from China under the PRC VAT export exemption/refund regime and for specified cross-border services consumed entirely outside mainland China. For this logistics fleet-dispatch SaaS, the WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer, the service must be supplied to an offshore recipient, the service must be rendered and consumed outside China, the contract and payment evidence must demonstrate foreign-currency settlement, and the export zero-rating declaration must be filed through the STA e-taxation platform; physical hardware exports accompanying the SaaS require an export customs declaration.

  • Customs Export Declaration of the People's Republic of China issued by the General Administration of Customs for any physical goods or hardware exported
  • Original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing outbound shipment from a PRC port to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent SAFE-designated bank foreign-exchange receipt and settlement voucher proving the full foreign-currency export proceeds were repatriated to the WFOE's PRC account
  • Digital zero-rated VAT e-invoice, export sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and for SaaS delivery: service agreement, server/telematics usage logs, and other evidence proving offshore consumption

The STA mandates the Full Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime for general VAT taxpayers; invoices must be issued through the STA electronic invoice service platform using the Unified Social Credit Code, digitally signed with the tax-control U-key, include the anti-fraud QR code, and transmit invoice data to the STA in near-real-time. High-volume issuers must integrate via the STA-authorized e-invoice API; no private paper invoice production is permitted for digitalized taxpayers, and general VAT taxpayers must use Special VAT Invoices for B2B taxable supplies.

If export zero-rating is unsubstantiated or fraudulent, the STA will claw back the zero-rated output VAT, disallow any refund/export rebate, impose daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, assess administrative penalties between 50% and 500% of the tax amount under the Law of the PRC on the Administration of Tax Collection, downgrade the taxpayer credit rating, suspend e-invoice issuance and export tax-refund eligibility, and refer serious cases for criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law; customs authorities may separately impose penalties and confiscate any wrongfully refunded amounts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi operates under a managed floating exchange-rate system rather than full free convertibility. Current-account transactions are broadly convertible, but each payment or receipt must be supported by genuine underlying commercial documentation and pass the bank's authenticity and matching review. The capital account remains materially controlled. A WFOE receiving foreign equity capital must first complete SAFE foreign-investment registration, then open a dedicated foreign-currency capital account at a licensed Chinese bank. Withdrawal and conversion of the capital into RMB are done through the bank under SAFE capital-account payment-convertibility mechanisms with post-registration monitoring. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are lawful after the annual audit, statutory tax settlement, and filing of the financial statements with the authorities; the bank verifies that the distribution is drawn from verified accumulated profits and that tax has been cleared. Export proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system and held in a current-account foreign-currency deposit or voluntarily sold for RMB; mandatory surrender has been abolished, but the enterprise remains subject to verification of the trade receipts and reporting through the cross-border balance-of-payments system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods-export foreign-exchange income must be repatriated to a PRC bank within 90 days from the customs export declaration date, or within the due date under the contract if a later payment term has been formally applied for with SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; 100% of the export proceeds may remain in a foreign-exchange current account, and conversion into RMB is optional at the bank's prevailing interbank market rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low - not legally viable as a primary bank. Cross-border digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Stripe, or similar non-bank payment providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China and cannot open a PRC basic deposit account on behalf of the WFOE. They cannot access CNAPS or CIPS as a substitute clearing member for the entity's domestic treasury operations. Such platforms may be used only as auxiliary collection rails for overseas or e-commerce card transactions, provided the provider holds the required SAFE cross-border payment institution license. The WFOE must maintain its primary operational account, capital account, and RMB basic deposit account at a licensed Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent remits the FDI capital via SWIFT MT103, or cross-border RMB via CIPS, into the WFOE's designated foreign-currency capital account at the domestic bank. The remittance instruction must cite the SAFE FDI registration number and use the appropriate cross-border remittance purpose code for FDI equity injection. Upon receipt, the bank performs an authenticity and compliance review, converts the proceeds into RMB at the prevailing market rate, and transfers the converted funds into the RMB capital fund account. A PRC licensed CPA thereafter issues a capital verification report evidencing the injection. All capital funds must be used strictly within the approved business scope; they may not be used for securities investment, entrusted loans, real-estate speculation, or non-business expenditures.For software licensing, cloud hosting, logistics subcontracting, and overseas vendor payments, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for direct credit transfers, commercial letters of credit for physical hardware or fleet-vehicle procurement, and documentary collections for moderate-risk supplier settlement. Cross-border RMB payments with Chinese counterparties or qualified foreign beneficiaries should be routed via CIPS. Every outbound payment must be supported by valid contracts, invoices, customs declarations where applicable, and a SAFE trade-verification record. Such platforms may be used only as auxiliary collection rails for overseas or e-commerce card transactions, provided the provider holds the required SAFE cross-border payment institution license. The WFOE must maintain its primary operational account, capital account, and RMB basic deposit account at a licensed Chinese commercial bank.
