Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum amount is imposed on a general WFOE under the 2023 Company Law. The registered capital is set by the shareholder and may be paid in instalments, but the unpaid balance must be fully paid within five years from incorporation (Article 47). No blocked escrow account is required for a standard digital-services WFOE. Any capital contribution in the form of technology or intellectual property must be valued by an independent appraiser and reported to the market regulator. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic filing and registration through the China National E-Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau, etax.chinatax.gov.cn) with provincial tax service modules

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor forms the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a limited liability company under the Foreign Investment Law and registers its legal personality with the State Administration for Market Regulation. Under China's multi-registration integration reform, the tax registration is completed simultaneously with the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), and the USCC functions as the taxpayer identification number for the WFOE for enterprise income tax, VAT, and other national taxes. No separate TIN application is required for mainland-incorporated companies. The WFOE must then complete its post-registration fiscal profile in the e-Tax Bureau, select small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status, register payroll and withholding programmes, and activate digital invoice issuance. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that has not established a legal person or branch in mainland China, the Chinese withholding agent must complete non-resident tax registration through the e-Tax Bureau before the first China-sourced payment is made; where the non-resident requires a direct taxpayer identity, the competent tax authority will assign a non-resident taxpayer identification number upon completion of the non-resident enterprise tax registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification: China does not offer a check-the-box pass-through election; all domestically incorporated enterprises, including WFOEs, are taxed at the entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The only material statutory elections are (i) VAT status as small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer, with mandatory general taxpayer registration once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, and (ii) special tax treatment election for qualified enterprise reorganizations, subject to STA filing; no hybrid-mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is compulsory within 30 days after business registration without a turnover threshold; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, while persons with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may be classified as small-scale taxpayers; cross-border digital/IT services are taxable at 6% when supplied domestically and may qualify for 0% or exemption when exported.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for a 0% VAT rate where the goods are actually declared to customs and exported from mainland China to an overseas purchaser, with title passing abroad, and the exporter has filed the export VAT refund/exemption procedure. For cross-border offshore digital services such as EMR SaaS, the zero-rate or VAT exemption with input credit/refund eligibility requires the service provider to be a Chinese resident enterprise, the recipient to be an overseas enterprise or individual, the service to be wholly consumed outside mainland China, no mainland domestic customers to receive the same service, the contract and invoice to be denominated in foreign currency, and the consideration to be paid from outside China and repatriated to mainland China. The SaaS service must be classified as a cross-border IT/software service or offshore outsourcing service under STA guidance; the supplier must file the cross-border taxable service zero-rating/exemption declaration through the e-Tax Bureau, maintain the underlying export service contract, foreign-currency invoice, and foreign recipient documentation, and preserve server access/delivery logs showing the service was accessed and used overseas. Domestic EMR SaaS supplied to hospitals or clinics in mainland China cannot be zero-rated and is subject to VAT at 6%.

  • Export Goods Declaration Form (customs export declaration with the pre-assigned customs declaration number) for physical software carriers or hardware; not applicable to pure SaaS exports
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list consistent with the customs declaration and foreign exchange receipt
  • Bank inbound remittance receipt / Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Confirmation issued by a designated foreign exchange bank or SAFE-approved electronic payment confirmation
  • For cross-border SaaS with no physical customs clearance: signed export software/service contract, foreign-currency commercial invoice, proof of the overseas recipient's legal existence, and service delivery/output logs demonstrating consumption outside mainland China

China operates a mandatory unified electronic invoice system administered by the STA. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the tax-control system or the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform; invoice data are transmitted online to the tax authority in near real time, and FDEI issuance is integrated with the e-Tax Bureau through statutory APIs and interface-based clearance. Each invoice carries an anti-fraud digital stamp, a unique invoice code, and a verification QR code; invoices that fail online verification cannot be used to claim input VAT credits. General VAT taxpayers must also use the VAT invoice verification module to authenticate counterparty special invoices, and the tax authority may impose tax-control restrictions on abnormal taxpayers.

The STA, customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) conduct cross-matching audits of export declarations, invoice data, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. A false or unsubstantiated export zero-rating claim triggers reversal of the 0% VAT treatment, recovery of unpaid VAT at the domestic rate, mandatory repayment of any input VAT already refunded, and late-payment surcharge at the statutory daily rate of 0.05% per day. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, tax evasion is subject to an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax underpaid; serious cases attract criminal liability under the Criminal Law, customs fines and confiscation for false export declarations, suspension or revocation of export tax refund/exemption qualification, downgrading of the taxpayer's tax credit status, blocking of digital invoice issuance, and recovery of the tax without a limitation period where fraud, evasion, or refusal to pay is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under the PBOC with a bifurcated convertibility framework. Current account transactions, including export proceeds from software and SaaS services, are generally freely convertible and require only bank-level verification and SAFE reporting. Capital account transactions remain heavily regulated and require prior approval, registration, or filing from SAFE for foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and profit repatriation. For a WFOE, inbound equity capital injections must be converted through a dedicated foreign exchange capital account and used within the approved business scope. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permissible for current-year after-tax earnings, subject to audit report, tax clearance certificate, board resolution, and SAFE registration. Dividend remittance is processed via the bank's SAFE-mandated verification of the outbound remittance application and supporting documents. Surrender of export proceeds is not mandatory as China abolished mandatory surrender requirements in 2012, but all current account receipts must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign currency settlement account or converted to RMB via a designated bank. Deferred payment or indirect routing of receivables through offshore accounts is prohibited unless specifically approved by SAFE. The overall convertibility for a medical EMR SaaS exporter is favorable on current account, but any capital structure involving offshore debt, IP license fees, or stock-based compensation creates capital account constraints requiring detailed regulatory filings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from service trade (including SaaS subscriptions) must be received within the statutory repatriation timeframe of 180 days from the performance date or as otherwise agreed in the commercial contract, but must be brought onshore before the annual FX supervision and balance report deadline. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain foreign currency in its current account Foreign Currency Settlement Account or sell it to a domestic bank on a voluntary basis. All export proceeds must be recorded in the SAFE Digital Foreign Exchange Platform and matched against the service trade contract, invoice, and customs or service quality endorsements where applicable.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under PBOC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers such as Neo, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not recognized as licensed banks or payment institutions by PBOC or SAFE. They cannot legally open account facilities for a mainland China WFOE or execute onshore RMB settlement, capital account conversion, or foreign exchange hedge transactions under Chinese law. Any attempt to route collection through such EMIs would violate currency control regulations and trigger shutdown, fines, or account freezing. The only permissible path for cross-border liquidity is a domestic Chinese bank integrated with SAFE's reporting system; the WFOE should rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic banks for all collections, disbursements, and FX hedging. Fintech solutions can be used only as non-accounting ancillary billing layers that funnel to a licensed Chinese bank custody account, not as standalone settlement rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital for the WFOE must be routed through a bank that has received the FDI registration information from SAFE. The foreign shareholder wires funds using the MT103 SWIFT standard, clearly indicating the purpose as 'Equity Investment for Registered Capital' and quoting the SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate number. The receiving Chinese bank deposits funds into the Foreign Exchange Capital Account, verifies the registration against SAFE's digital platform, and then converts them to RMB only upon submission of a board resolution, a capital contribution schedule, and a conversion use plan. After conversion, RMB must be placed in a Capital Settlement Account and cannot be used for equity investment, securities, or real estate purchases outside the approved business scope. Before settlement, the bank performs a final check on the capital source, the shareholder's proof of funding, and the WFOE's operating license. Documentary Collections are acceptable for low-risk subscription license payments where the documents are simple and no store-of-value guarantee is needed. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI providers such as Neo, Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not recognized as licensed banks or payment institutions by PBOC or SAFE. The only permissible path for cross-border liquidity is a domestic Chinese bank integrated with SAFE's reporting system; the WFOE should rely exclusively on Tier-1 domestic banks for all collections, disbursements, and FX hedging. Fintech solutions can be used only as non-accounting ancillary billing layers that funnel to a licensed Chinese bank custody account, not as standalone settlement rails.
