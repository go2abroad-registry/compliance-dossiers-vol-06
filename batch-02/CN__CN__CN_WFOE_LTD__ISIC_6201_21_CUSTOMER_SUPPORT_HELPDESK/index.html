Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum registered capital is prescribed for a general-technology WFOE limited liability company, and no escrowed blocked capital account is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; official online electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company, the first step is incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The Business License issued by SAMR contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number for the entity. Within 30 days of issuance of the Business License, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent State Taxation Administration office through the Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting the Business License, articles of association, legal representative identity documents, registered-address proof, bank account settlement certificate, and foreign investor information. Tax registration is integrated and no separate TIN is issued beyond the USCC. The WFOE then confirms or applies for its VAT taxpayer classification through the e-tax portal; if general taxpayer status is selected, the approval is recorded in the tax registration profile. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment is registered separately in the withholding-tax administration module for Chinese-source income, using its home-country tax identification number, but does not obtain the WFOE's USCC; all local tax obligations of the WFOE are administered under the USCC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete tax registration regardless of turnover. Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily registers as a general taxpayer. China is a unitary tax jurisdiction, so there is no separate sub-national VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported physical goods are eligible for zero-rate VAT with input VAT refund/rebate upon actual export through Chinese customs if the exporter holds complete customs and foreign-exchange repatriation records. Qualifying offshore digital services, including helpdesk/SaaS/customer-support cloud services, are eligible for VAT zero-rating or exemption if the service recipient is a non-resident, the service is consumed wholly outside China, and the service falls within the State Taxation Administration's published list of cross-border taxable services eligible for zero-rating or exemption. The WFOE must retain evidence of the overseas place of consumption and the absence of domestic Chinese customer usage; mixed supplies are zero-rated only to the extent the service is consumed outside China.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export customs release form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original transport document)
  • Foreign Exchange Proceeds Collection Confirmation issued by the settlement bank (bank-issued export proceeds repatriation certificate equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs declaration
  • Export sales contract with the overseas customer or, for digital services, a cross-border service agreement and evidence of overseas consumption
  • Zero-rated VAT electronic export invoice issued through the STA-approved e-invoice system

China requires real-time fiscal clearance through State Taxation Administration systems, principally the Digital Electronic Invoice (DEI) platform and the Electronic Tax Bureau. VAT invoices, including special and ordinary invoices, must be issued through STA-approved tax-control invoicing software or e-invoice service providers, and issuance metadata is transmitted to the tax authority in real time through STA-approved APIs. Fully digital electronic invoices bear a unified anti-fraud digital code and do not require physical seals. All invoice issuance, red-notice credit notes, and voided invoices must be reported and cleared electronically; handwritten or unregistered invoice templates are prohibited.

Tax authorities may retrospectively audit export zero-rating and VAT refund claims and claw back any refunded VAT if the goods were not actually exported, the digital services were consumed in China, the foreign exchange proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or the supporting customs, transport, and payment documentation is missing or false. Penalties include repayment of the wrongfully refunded VAT plus interest, administrative fines under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection of between one and five times the fraudulently obtained export tax refund, and criminal liability for export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law, including fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system under which current-account transactions for genuine trade in services are largely convertible, while capital-account transactions remain subject to strict registration, purpose verification, and bank-level supervision. The WFOE's foreign investor capital injection must enter through an FDI-registered foreign-exchange capital account at an onshore designated bank; conversion from foreign currency to RMB is permitted on a 'use-based settlement' principle, meaning the bank will convert only those funds actually needed for permitted operating expenses within the company's registered business scope. The WFOE may retain unconverted foreign currencies in the account but cannot channel those funds into capital-account prohibited uses such as equity investments, securities, or real estate outside the negative-list exemptions. Revenues from overseas customer-support and helpdesk SaaS subscriptions are classified under current-account service trade; the bank handles settlement or retention after reviewing the customer contract, service invoice, proof of delivery, and payment notification. Profit remittance to the foreign parent is a current-account distribution requiring an audited statutory financial report, board resolution, withholding tax payment voucher, and SAFE filing for dividends; the bank must verify no round-tripping or illegal cross-border arbitrage. Thus RMB is not freely convertible under the capital account, but the genuine operational and distribution flows of a compliant WFOE are achievable through a licensed domestic bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion percentage on current-account export-service receipts for a standard WFOE; the effective mandatory surrender ratio is zero. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to mainland China and credited to the WFOE's domestic foreign-exchange current account promptly upon realization, and the receiving bank must report the receipt to SAFE in the cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system within the prescribed operational cycle, generally the next business day. If the WFOE requires RMB for salaries, rent, and domestic operations, it may settle the foreign currency at a licensed bank on a voluntary and genuine-transaction basis. For trade in goods, export units must complete export collection reconciliation within 90 days of the customs declaration date. For service-trade exports, the documentary package—contract, invoice, support tickets or service log, and bank statement—must be retained for at least five years. A clean A-category current-account WFOE should operate under zero mandatory surrender and bank-monitored retention; B/C-rated entities may face accelerated settlement and heightened document review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally viable as the primary banking relationship for a mainland WFOE. Chinese financial regulations require corporate foreign-exchange accounts, FDI registration, bank reporting, capital verification, and tax withholding evidence; an offshore EMI cannot satisfy these obligations or open a regulated onshore RMB account in the WFOE's name. EMIs such as Airwallex or Currencycloud can only support downstream collection and payment flows through licensed partner banks and cannot replace the domestic tier-1 relationship needed for SAFE reporting, capital injection, and dividend remittance. The use of Stripe Billing or PayPal as customer acquisition rails is acceptable only if the payment settlement is made from the third-party merchant provider into the WFOE's onshore bank current account with appropriate service-trade declarations; the offshore EMI cannot execute the capital-account conversion or act as the FX settlement venue for FDI capital.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must wire the capital contribution from its own corporate bank account to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI foreign-exchange capital account. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration through the designated bank and obtain a unique Foreign Exchange Business Registration Number; the bank will open the capital account and connect it to the foreign capital project information system. The SWIFT MT103 payment must include the purpose code for direct investment, the WFOE name, and the registered capital reference. After the funds arrive, the WFOE converts such amounts into RMB strictly on a payment-based settlement basis, presenting correspondences, lease agreements, payroll, procurement invoices, or tax receipts. A domestic licensed CPA then issues a capital verification report after reconciling the foreign-currency receipt, bank conversion, and RMB disbursements; this report is filed with the local market-regulation authority and serves as proof of contribution completion. No funds may be transferred to a third party or converted into a long-dated RMB deposit without a genuine operating purpose.For operating disbursements related to overseas cloud hosting, software licensing, subcontractors, and corporate services, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 payments evidenced by service contracts, invoices, and, where applicable, a Chinese tax withholding clearance certificate for VAT and enterprise income tax paid on cross-border service fees. For larger physical or managed trade flows, commercial letters of credit issued through the WFOE's tier-1 bank, and confirmed or advised by the counterparty's bank, provide safer settlement; documentary collections such as D/P and D/A are acceptable for low-risk, long-standing counterparties. The bank will require the WFOE to complete a cross-border RMB and FX payment declaration under SAFE's current-account monitoring system and to keep an audit trail for at least five years. Where the WFOE receives Stripe or PayPal settlement funds, the remitting entity must be the licensed payment processor and the underlying reason code must identify the SaaS subscription or helpdesk service; aggregated payout structures that obscure the underlying customer invoices may be rejected by the Chinese receiving bank.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forwards with a SAFE-registered tier-1 bank, limited to hedging real current-account or confirmed service-trade cash flows and requiring underlying contract documentationVanilla European-style FX options for risk management of the WFOE's service-trade receivables, with strikes and tenors aligned to the estimated contract settlement calendarCross-currency swaps with a licensed Chinese bank for foreign-currency debt or FDI-related maturities, provided the exposure is matched and the transaction is reported to SAFE for approval or filingNon-deliverable forwards are not permissible for the mainland WFOE on an onshore settlement basis; offshore NDFs can only be accessed by an offshore treasury affiliate and cannot be settled, rolled, or netted through the WFOE's mainland bank accounts

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs are not legally viable as the primary banking relationship for a mainland WFOE. Chinese financial regulations require corporate foreign-exchange accounts, FDI registration, bank reporting, capital verification, and tax withholding evidence; an offshore EMI cannot satisfy these obligations or open a regulated onshore RMB account in the WFOE's name. EMIs such as Airwallex or Currencycloud can only support downstream collection and payment flows through licensed partner banks and cannot replace the domestic tier-1 relationship needed for SAFE reporting, capital injection, and dividend remittance. The use of Stripe Billing or PayPal as customer acquisition rails is acceptable only if the payment settlement is made from the third-party merchant provider into the WFOE's onshore bank current account with appropriate service-trade declarations; the offshore EMI cannot execute the capital-account conversion or act as the FX settlement venue for FDI capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual aggregate related-party tangible-asset transfers (local file); RMB 200 million annual aggregate related-party financial-asset transfers; RMB 100 million annual aggregate related-party intangible-asset or service transactions; RMB 400 million annual aggregate related-party transactions for master file; however, if any single related-party transaction with one counterparty exceeds RMB 200 million, the local file must be prepared for that transaction; the related-party disclosure form A105050 must always be filed even if below threshold; a Country-by-Country Report filing threshold of RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group revenue applies where the ultimate parent files in China.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in China are not codified as a separate statute, but the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 empowers the tax authority to recharacterize arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose; a WFOE must demonstrate substantive business operations, actual employees, decision-making in China, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) for software development, hosting, customer support, and contract conclusion; under the OECD BEPS Action 8-10 and China's transfer pricing rules, the WFOE assuming limited-risk distribution or routine helpdesk functions is entitled only to a routine return (e.g., 3%-5% net margin under TNMM) unless it owns economically significant intangibles, bears meaningful risks, and exercises control over those risks; failure to satisfy economic substance may lead to profit reattribution, deemed dividend recharacterization, and a 10% WHT on the adjusted amount.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Overstatement of Deductible Costs

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 63, a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax is imposed; under the VAT Invoice Administration Measures and Criminal Law Article 205, issuing or using fraudulent special VAT invoices carries a fine of 1-5 times the defrauded amount and criminal imprisonment; under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, non-payment of tax due to falsification is punishable by a 50%-500% fine, and where the criminal threshold is met (underpaid tax exceeding RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of taxable income), the entity is fined and the responsible person is subject to up to seven years' imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201; the tax authority will also disallow the inflated deductions, recharacterize the payment as a deemed dividend, and assess 10% WHT plus late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Chinese courts and tax authorities may pierce the corporate veil under EIT Law Article 46 and Company Law Article 20/63 where the director caused the company to evade tax and commingled assets; directors face personal joint and several liability for the unpaid corporate tax, restitution of diverted funds, inclusion on the tax blacklist (重大税收违法失信案件) under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 54, which triggers travel bans, exit restrictions, and credit blacklisting; criminal liability for tax evasion includes imprisonment of up to seven years and fines for the directly responsible supervisor and legal representative; a director who knowingly signs false tax filings may be prosecuted as an accomplice or principal under Criminal Law Article 201.
Shell Company / Substance Failure and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6, the tax authority may apply the GAAR to disregard the WFOE's separate entity status, reattribute 100% of the global helpdesk income to China, and impose tax at 25% plus a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 for intentional understatement; the indefinite deferral of Chinese tax by keeping profits offshore is denied, and assessed income is treated as a deemed dividend distribution subject to 10% WHT; a failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation attracts a RMB 100,000-500,000 fine under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42; the entity may be deregistered or struck off under Measures for Registration Administration, and the legal representative is blacklisted personally and prohibited from serving as director in any new company.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are jointly and severally liable for enterprise income tax, surcharges, and penalties where the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20(3) if the director's abuse of the corporate form caused creditor or tax losses; directors face civil asset seizure and restitution of the shifted profits; the tax blacklist under Announcement 2018 No. 54 imposes exit-restriction (出境限制) and high-consumption restrictions on the legal representative and direct responsible managers; criminal exposure includes fraud or tax evasion offenses, with imprisonment of three to ten years for fraudulent tax filings under Criminal Law Article 201; for cross-border profit stripping involving false documentation, the director may be held liable for organizing a tax evasion crime, leading to a sentence of up to seven years and confiscation of personal property.
AML / Beneficial Ownership Non-Disclosure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, the treaty benefit is denied and the payment is recharacterized as a dividend taxed at 10% statutory rate plus a 50%-500% penalty under EIT Law Article 63; under Article 6 of the Anti-Money Laundering Law, the WFOE faces fines of RMB 200,000-2,000,000 for failure to perform client due diligence and report suspicious transactions; the criminal offense of money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191 applies where the tax-evaded proceeds are laundered, with imprisonment of up to ten years and fines of 5%-20% of the laundered amount; the fake TRC constitutes document forgery under Criminal Law Article 280, punishable by up to three years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or approve false treaty documents are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69, which imposes a fine of 50%-300% of the tax not withheld, and the director may be jointly and severally liable where they directed the non-withholding; the court may impose a travel ban, freeze personal bank accounts, and restrict property disposal; criminal liability for forging tax documents and money laundering can lead to imprisonment of three to ten years and disqualification from corporate managerial positions; the director's personal assets may be attached to satisfy the corporate tax debt under the 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine where the company is a mere alter ego.
