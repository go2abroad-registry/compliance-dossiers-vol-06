Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn), together with the fully digitalised e-invoice service platform; the local STA office for the WFOE's registered address is the routine competent authority.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a China-incorporated resident company, so the foreign investor does not apply separately for a Chinese TIN. After the incorporation application is approved by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the SAMR-generated 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically pushed to the STA as the taxpayer's statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is also the VAT registration number. The legal representative or authorised tax agent must then access the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and complete the supplementary taxpayer information filing: register the finance/accounting policy, bank account, business scope (including ISIC 6201 / email newsletter infrastructure services), investor and beneficial-owner information, legal representative ID and contact details, and the desired VAT invoice type/quota. The tax bureau will activate the electronic tax account and assign a VAT taxpayer type; a newly formed WFOE is ordinarily treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer until it elects or is statutorily required to become a VAT general taxpayer (mandatory once annual taxable sales reach RMB 5,000,000; voluntary registration is available below that threshold if certain turnover/accounting conditions are met). The TIN/USCC is used for enterprise income tax, VAT, payroll withholding and social contribution reporting. If the foreign investor is acting as a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment and derives China-source income, a separate non-resident tax registration or withholding procedure may apply, but that is not the default for this resident WFOE vehicle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese enterprise income tax resident and is taxed on worldwide income at the 25% enterprise income tax rate, subject to applicable concessions; it is not a pass-through entity. Distributions to foreign shareholders are generally subject to withholding tax at the statutory rate of 10%, reduced under an applicable income tax treaty.

Available election forms

None for entity classification purposes. The People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box election or an elective hybrid-mismatch regime for WFOEs; anti-hybrid principles, if applicable, are mandatory statutory anti-avoidance rules. The only relevant status election is VAT-related: a WFOE may apply voluntarily for general VAT taxpayer status if it satisfies the accounting and turnover conditions, or it may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to the compulsory RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover registration point. That election does not change the enterprise income tax entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB. There is no threshold for basic VAT registration because the WFOE is compulsorily registered as a VAT taxpayer upon incorporation. The mandatory registration point for VAT general taxpayer status is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. A small-scale taxpayer may currently benefit from a temporary VAT exemption for monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000, or RMB 300,000 per quarter, subject to the prevailing policy at the time of supply. Cross-border zero-rated supplies are excluded from domestic taxable turnover for threshold purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the WFOE's email newsletter infrastructure and SaaS supply, zero-rating at 0% VAT can be claimed only for a cross-border taxable service supplied to a non-resident enterprise or person whose consumption of the service occurs entirely outside mainland China, where the remuneration is paid from outside mainland China, and where the service is not physically performed or used within mainland China. The service must fall within the STA and Ministry of Finance zero-rated cross-border service list under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and subsequent notices, or must otherwise qualify as an exempt cross-border service where zero-rating is not listed. The WFOE must file the cross-border service declaration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau, issue a zero-rated export e-invoice or an exempt-export invoice where applicable, and substantiate the offshore nature of the supply with contract, payment, and delivery evidence. If the offshore-consumption test is not satisfied, the standard 6% VAT is chargeable. For physical goods exported from China, zero-rating is available where the goods are lawfully declared for export, leave the customs territory, the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, and the export is settled with the authorities in accordance with customs and SAFE rules; the exporter may then claim an export VAT refund for attributable input VAT through the official export tax rebate system.

China is implementing fully digitalised e-invoices (FDEI) nationwide. The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA-approved electronic tax invoice platform; each FDEI receives a tax-authority-issued digital invoice number and an official digital stamp generated by the STA system, and the invoice record is transmitted to the STA in real time at issuance. The taxpayer must retain the system-generated XML and cannot issue a PDF without a corresponding valid e-invoice record. For high-volume API-driven invoicing, the STA's e-invoice interface endorsed by the Electronic Tax Bureau may be used to submit invoice data from the taxpayer's billing system, but the invoice remains subject to the anti-fraud digital stamping requirement. VAT special invoices used to claim input VAT credits must be verified or filed through the VAT Invoice Integrated Service Platform within the statutory credit-claim window, generally 360 days for paper special invoices, with FDEI credit claims following the current platform rules. Export zero-rated supplies must be declared in the VAT return using the cross-border service and international trade reporting screens in the Electronic Tax Bureau, with supporting documents retained for audit.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with statutory RMB convertibility only on current account transactions; the capital account remains materially restricted and requires prior SAFE registration or bank-mediated verification for each flow. For a mainland-domiciled Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the software-as-a-service and email-infrastructure sector, subscription and API usage fees received from overseas clients constitute service-trade current account receipts and must be repatriated into China through a domestic designated bank. The WFOE may lawfully retain those receipts in a foreign currency current account or settle them into RMB at the spot rate, but every transaction must be supported by an invoice, contract, and SAFE-compliant purpose code. The bulk SaaS revenue model, however, creates an operational mismatch: foreign payment gateways such as Stripe or direct card acquirers do not, as a practical matter, onboard mainland WFOE merchants, and non-accredited shell collections through offshore card processors expose the entity to suspicion of unlicensed cross-border payment activity. Dividend remittance to foreign shareholders is treated as a current account flow only after completion of the annual statutory audit, CIT settlement, and submission of board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax payment proofs, and the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate to the settlement bank. In contrast, capital injections, shareholder loans, and intra-group funding are capital account items: they must be registered through the SAFE foreign exchange system as Foreign Direct Investment, held in a dedicated capital account, and restricted to the approved business scope. Any hedging or treasury operation must be grounded in real transaction exposure; speculative derivatives and artificial round-tripping are expressly prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory surrender of current account export proceeds; however, all service-trade export receipts, including software and email-infrastructure subscription fees, must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign currency account within the contractual payment terms and in any event no later than the applicable SAFE verification deadline generally applied to outstanding collections. For goods-related export proceeds, the statutory collection-inward period is normally 90 days from the date of export declaration, extendable to 180 days for deferred-payment transactions, and each receipt must be matched to the underlying declaration through SAFE's Trade in Goods Monitoring System within the required working-day window. For service trade, receipts must be matched against the service contract and invoice using the SAFE Service Trade portal, and conversion into RMB is permitted only after the bank completes the real-transaction background check and obtains a valid service trade purpose code.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Not Viable. Foreign digital-only banks and electronic money institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to maintain domestic deposit accounts or provide CNY clearing through CNAPS for a mainland-registered WFOE. Their use for a mainland WFOE's principal treasury function would constitute unlicensed cross-border payment activity; banks routinely reject inward RMB transfers arriving through these EMIs because the remittance lacks a SAFE-compliant purpose code and the correspondent bank identifiers do not match a registered Chinese beneficiary. These EMIs may be used only where the WFOE establishes a properly registered offshore holding or operating entity and maintains offshore arm's-length invoices; the mainland WFOE still must bring revenue onshore via qualifying cross-border payment institutions licensed by PBOC, such as LianLian Pay or PingPong, or via ordinary SWIFT remittance from the client's account. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent or ultimate investor must remit equity consideration from an offshore account in the investor's own name into the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a licensed domestic settlement bank. Before remittance, the WFOE must obtain its FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate through the bank or directly through SAFE and submit the investment contract, identity documents of the investor, and an explanatory statement. The bank then records the inflow into the EXCVS system with a capital account entry code; upon arrival, the WFOE may convert the foreign currency into RMB for permitted business expenses, but it cannot use the capital account to purchase wealth-management products, invest in real estate, or lend within the banking system except as specifically permitted. The registered paid-in capital must be verified against the bank's capital-confirmation statement and company registration records. If the investor instead injects through a cross-border RMB route, the RCPMIS declaration must identify the purpose as direct investment with a valid OFAC/SWIFT purpose code and the investor's SPV credentials.Operational and trade disbursements for the WFOE's email-infrastructure business, including overseas cloud hosting, bandwidth, sub-processor licenses, external legal fees, and third-party SaaS administration tools, should be executed in foreign currency via SWIFT MT103 with a clear SAFE purpose code and a documented real transaction. The bank requires the invoice, contract, proof of service performance, tax withholding certificates for cross-border payments of royalties or services, and evidence that the beneficiary is the actual counterparty. For physical infrastructure procurement, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Documentary Collection may be used for hardware imports, but for pure software-dependent settlements, MT103 direct transfer is the standard clearing rail. All outbound disbursements must be matched to the bank's payment-verification system, and expenses outside the registered business scope will be rejected.Onshore RMB-USD forward contracts executed with the settlement bank under SAFE's real-need principle, with underlying receivable or payable exposure demonstrable from invoices, service contracts, and revenue forecasts.Enterprise FX swaps between RMB and USD for current account settlement and capital account working capital conversion, subject to bank approval and transaction-background validation.Purchased currency options where the WFOE is the buyer to cap adverse USD movement; writing naked options is prohibited for non-financial enterprises.Currency swaps with the bank solely for matching long-term cash-flow exposure on registered cross-border loan or equity financing, if permitted under SAFE's cross-border financing macro-prudential framework.Offshore NDFs are prohibited for the mainland WFOE's onshore treasury account; any NDF hedging may be considered only in an affiliated offshore vehicle with separate governance, not as the domestic entity's clearing rail.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Not Viable. Foreign digital-only banks and electronic money institutions such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to maintain domestic deposit accounts or provide CNY clearing through CNAPS for a mainland-registered WFOE. Their use for a mainland WFOE's principal treasury function would constitute unlicensed cross-border payment activity; banks routinely reject inward RMB transfers arriving through these EMIs because the remittance lacks a SAFE-compliant purpose code and the correspondent bank identifiers do not match a registered Chinese beneficiary. These EMIs may be used only where the WFOE establishes a properly registered offshore holding or operating entity and maintains offshore arm's-length invoices; the mainland WFOE still must bring revenue onshore via qualifying cross-border payment institutions licensed by PBOC, such as LianLian Pay or PingPong, or via ordinary SWIFT remittance from the client's account. Stripe Billing and card acquiring cannot serve as the WFOE's direct receipts rail onshore.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is required when the multinational group's aggregate annual related-party transaction amount reaches RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered when the group's consolidated revenue in the prior year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine through the general anti-avoidance rule in EIT Law Article 47 and related anti-abuse measures. Although China does not prescribe a conventional Economic Substances Regulations CIGA schedule, the Chinese tax authority will evaluate whether the WFOE performs the core income-generating activities underlying the email-infrastructure software: development and enhancement of the software, maintenance and protection of the platform, employment of skilled engineers and support staff, management of customer contracts, assumption of associated risks, and deployment of physical and intangible assets. Treaty protection and transfer-pricing allocation will be denied where the alleged legal entity lacks real economic substance and the parties' conduct contradicts their written agreements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Under-Declaration of Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Administration and Collection, the tax authority recovers the underpaid tax, imposes a daily late surcharge of 0.05%, and assesses an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Where the evaded amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the taxable income due, Criminal Law Article 201 punishes the responsible actors with imprisonment of up to three years for the ordinary tier and three to seven years for especially large amounts exceeding 30% of due tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative, and the financial officer who directed, approved, or knowingly tolerated the concealment are jointly and severally liable under PRC tort and company law principles to restore lost assets and compensate the tax revenue shortfall. Chinese tax authorities may list such directors on the Serious Dishonest Taxpayers blacklist, impose exit bans and departure restrictions, restrict banking and financing activities, and initiate criminal prosecution leading to personal imprisonment.
DTA Relief Abuse and Shell Company Beneficial Ownership Failure

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership rules and apply the GAAR under EIT Law Article 47, ignoring the conduit and re-characterizing the dividend for the true owner. The withholding tax is recalculated at the statutory 10% rate, with late surcharge and administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the short-paid tax under Article 63; criminal charges may follow where the arrangement is accompanied by forged board minutes or false documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign back-dated board resolutions, false beneficial-ownership declarations, or fabricated intercompany documents are personally exposed to civil restitution claims for the extra tax and penalties. Under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine, personal assets of directors can be attached where the corporate form is used to defraud creditors or the tax system; travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal liability for fraudulent document-making can culminate in imprisonment for up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41, the tax authority may make an arm's-length adjustment and reallocate profit to the Chinese WFOE; under Article 44 and the related-party documentation rules, failure to produce documentation permits deemed assessment and an increased interest burden on the underpayment, often benchmark PBOC lending rate plus five percentage points. Reporting violations also trigger administrative penalties up to RMB 10,000, and misleading or fraudulent documentation may expose the taxpayer to the 50% to 500% Article 63 penalty and criminal tax-fraud provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors and transfer-pricing officers who approved or signed inflated offshore invoices without legitimate service substance may be held personally liable for the resulting tax, interest, and penalties under PRC company law negligence and fiduciary principles. The responsible individuals may be blacklisted, restricted from leaving China, disqualified from serving as directors or senior officers, and in cases of deliberate fraud, sentenced to criminal imprisonment for tax evasion or document forgery.
