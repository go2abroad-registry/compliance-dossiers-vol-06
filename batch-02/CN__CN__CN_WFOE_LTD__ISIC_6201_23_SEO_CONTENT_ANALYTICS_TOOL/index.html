Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its provincial/municipal tax service bureaus; online portal: National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The WFOE obtains its business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation; the license bears the statutory 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the sole Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all Chinese resident legal entities and also functions as the VAT taxpayer identification number. No separate VAT registration number is issued. Step 2: Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE's legal representative and financial officer must complete real-name authentication and tax entity information reporting on the National Electronic Tax Administration portal, thereby opening the corporate tax file with the local STA. Step 3: The WFOE must select VAT taxpayer status: small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer; general status is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period, and voluntary general registration is available below that threshold. Step 4: The WFOE must execute a three-party tax payment agreement among the company, bank, and tax authority to enable automatic electronic payment of corporate income tax, VAT, and other national taxes. Step 5: The WFOE must apply for the fully digitalized electronic invoice quota and digital signature through the STA e-invoice platform, together with a tax control device or digital U-Key if required. Step 6: For a pure foreign non-resident enterprise with no establishment in China, no WFOE USCC is issued; instead, the domestic payer must complete non-resident withholding tax registration and contract filing through the e-tax portal, and the STA assigns a special non-resident tax code for withholding purposes only.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No pass-through election and no check-the-box election exist under PRC tax law; a WFOE is always a PRC resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax at 25% on worldwide income. For VAT, a small-scale taxpayer may voluntarily elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer if it has sound accounting records and a fixed place of business, even if annual turnover is below RMB 5,000,000; once elected, the taxpayer generally cannot revert to small-scale status for 36 months. No hybrid mismatch elections are recognized under PRC law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must register for VAT at incorporation regardless of turnover; there is no minimum turnover exemption for corporate registration. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 within any consecutive 12-month period. Below RMB 5,000,000, the WFOE remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects to become a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Chinese WFOE supplying SEO data crawler, keyword analytics, or other qualifying software and information system services to an overseas recipient may claim the 0% VAT export rate if the services fall within the zero-rated cross-border services categories under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement [2016] No. 29, namely software services, information system services, business process management services, offshore service outsourcing, or related technology services. The services must be supplied to an overseas unit or individual and must be wholly consumed outside the People's Republic of China, meaning they have no direct connection to PRC real estate, domestic goods, or domestic personnel, and are not used for a domestic business operation. The entire consideration must be received from outside China and the WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer; small-scale taxpayers may only use cross-border VAT exemption, not zero-rating with input-VAT refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration) - required only when tangible hardware or a physical data carrier is exported; pure online SaaS services do not generate a customs Bill of Entry
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform, clearly stating 0% VAT
  • Cross-border service sales contract with the overseas customer, specifying the service scope, place of supply, and confirmation that the service is consumed wholly outside China
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher or service trade income declaration evidencing actual receipt of the consideration in foreign currency, functionally equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate
  • Overseas customer identity and residence proof, such as an overseas business registration certificate or other foreign entity documentation
  • WFOE self-certification that the service is wholly consumed outside China and is not related to PRC land, buildings, goods, or personnel
  • Service trade export filing or Ministry of Commerce registration record if the transaction qualifies as offshore service outsourcing

All VAT invoices must be issued through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform managed by the STA. The WFOE must use fully digitalized electronic invoices, which are generated with an STA-verified electronic signature and streamed to the Golden Tax System Phase IV in real time. Invoice data is cleared, archived, and cross-matched by the STA against VAT returns, corporate income tax returns, customs declarations, and foreign exchange receipt records. Special VAT invoices used by buyers to claim input VAT credit must be authenticated and verified in the VAT invoice verification system before deduction. There is no separate paper fiscal stamp for digital e-invoices; the anti-fraud architecture relies on real-time one-invoice-one-number reporting, artificial intelligence anomaly detection, and the centralized National Tax Data Warehouse.

The STA uses Golden Tax System Phase IV to cross-match zero-rated VAT declarations with e-invoice data, customs export declarations, service trade contracts, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If the WFOE cannot substantiate that the service was wholly consumed outside China, that the customer is genuinely non-resident, or that the export proceeds were received in foreign currency within the prescribed repatriation window, the tax authority will claw back the zero-rated input VAT refund and assess VAT at the applicable rate together with late payment interest at 0.05% per day. Penalties may range from 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax for false or fraudulent claims under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Knowingly issuing false zero-rated invoices, fabricating export documentation, or fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds may constitute a criminal tax offense under the PRC Criminal Law, with penalties including heavy fines, confiscation, and imprisonment up to life imprisonment. The entity's tax credit rating will be downgraded and customs, SAFE, and market supervision enforcement actions may be simultaneously initiated.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's FX regime is stringent in structure: the RMB is convertible for current account transactions, but the capital account remains heavily regulated. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) delegates daily verification to designated foreign exchange banks, requiring corroborated economic substance behind every remittance. A WFOE may open foreign currency capital accounts and receive registered FDI equity injections without special approval, but only after completing SAFE foreign investment registration and acquiring a bank identification code. Once the capital arrives, it must be booked to the capital settlement account and may then be settled into RMB for approved operating purposes, but cannot be diverted to equity investments, loans, or securities without prior approval. Profit repatriation to overseas shareholders is permitted after annual audit and corporate income tax settlement, but banks must see audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and board resolutions approving distribution. Current account outflows for royalties, software licensing fees, and trade settlements require valid contracts, invoices, and in many cases tax registration certificates for withholding tax. Overall, treasury liquidity is not freely convertible; all cross-border moves require purpose, evidence, and recordkeeping, making bank onboarding and AML/KYC compliance the real operational gate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Administration of Export Proceeds and Monitoring, export foreign exchange income must be brought back to China via designated banks within 180 days after customs declaration or contract settlement date. The mandatory conversion ratio currently stands at zero: export proceeds can be fully retained in foreign exchange settlement accounts, fully sold into RMB, or split. However, all export receipts must be reconciled to customs declaration data in the SAFE monitoring system, and retention beyond the prescribed period or unexplained discrepancies can trigger investigation and penalties. For software/technology export, providers may need the relevant SAFE service-trade filing to verify delivery and commercial authenticity.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Infeasible as a standalone local clearing rail. Stripe and Paddle are payment service providers, not licensed banks in China; they cannot issue CNY clearing accounts, accept export proceeds under SAFE, or conduct foreign exchange settlement. Offshore digital EMIs may offer virtual accounts, but PRC banks will not remit regulated capital or trade funds to unlicensed EMI accounts. Domestic use of third-party payment channels requires a PBOC-licensed institution with a cross-border payment license. For this WFOE, all treasury flows must route through a SAFE-designated domestic bank with proper FX registration and compliance documentation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital must be wired to the bank identified in the SAFE foreign investment registration after completing the Foreign Investment Registration and opening a Capital Settlement Account. Remittance clearing uses SWIFT MT103 with the SAFE-approved BOP transaction code for FDI equity capital contribution. On receipt, the local bank verifies remitter identity, purpose code, and matching registration. A licensed PRC accounting firm then issues a capital verification report, which is submitted to the bank and retained for SAFE audit. The capital may be settled into RMB only for genuine operating purposes such as payroll, procurement, and rent, and cannot be used for speculative financial investments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Infeasible as a standalone local clearing rail. Stripe and Paddle are payment service providers, not licensed banks in China; they cannot issue CNY clearing accounts, accept export proceeds under SAFE, or conduct foreign exchange settlement. Offshore digital EMIs may offer virtual accounts, but PRC banks will not remit regulated capital or trade funds to unlicensed EMI accounts. Domestic use of third-party payment channels requires a PBOC-licensed institution with a cross-border payment license. For this WFOE, all treasury flows must route through a SAFE-designated domestic bank with proper FX registration and compliance documentation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends distributed to a non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China; distributions to non-resident individuals are subject to individual income tax at 20%, with treaty relief potentially reducing the rate to 10%, and many treaties reduce the corporate dividend rate to 5% where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China applies transaction-type thresholds under SAT Bulletin [2016] No. 42: local File is required when prior-year related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000, intangible-property transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000; the Master File generally is required when related-party transactions with overseas affiliates exceed RMB 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but SAT applies the substance-over-form doctrine through CIT Article 47, general anti-avoidance rules, and beneficial ownership requirements for treaty relief; a WFOE must maintain actual core income-generating activities in China, including strategic decision-making, employees, office facilities, contract execution, and technical support for the SEO analytics platform.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false invoicing or fraudulent cost inflation

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 63 and 69: 50% to five times the evaded tax, daily late surcharge of 0.05%, and withheld-amount penalties of 50% to three times. Criminal Law Article 201 provides imprisonment of up to seven years and fines for major tax evasion; Criminal Law Article 205 provides up to life imprisonment and property confiscation for false special VAT invoicing.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 or the 2023 Company Law Article 23 where shareholders abuse limited liability; directors who caused the false filings face joint and several liability for tax, fines, and civil restitution, company disqualification or blacklisting on the SAT serious tax violation list, exit control at the border, and criminal liability as principals or accomplices.
Thin capitalization and disguised equity through excessive intercompany interest

Statutory sanction: CIT Article 46 and Implementation Regulation Article 119 disallow interest exceeding the 2:1 general debt-equity ratio (5:1 for financial enterprises); SAT may recharacterize the excess interest as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding tax and a transfer pricing adjustment under CIT Article 41, with penalties of 50% to five times the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the artificial debt structure can be jointly liable if they used the corporate form to evade tax or defraud creditors; the legal representative and finance officers may be blacklisted, prohibited from leaving China until tax is settled, and subject to civil damages; if the scheme is proven fraudulent, directors face imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax avoidance or Article 161 for false statement during liquidation if it becomes insolvent.
Shell entity arrangement or treaty-shopping abuse lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: CIT Article 47 and SAT Announcement [2017] No. 6 empower SAT to disregard the shell structure, recharacterize the transactions, deny treaty benefits, impose the full 10% withholding tax, and assess interest plus penalties of 50% to five times under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63; the beneficial owner test under the DTA prevents treaty protection for a conduit company.

Director personal liability: The individual directors and ultimate shareholders who established or controlled the sham structure may be jointly and severally liable for the aggregate tax, interest, and penalties; their names may be placed on the serious tax violation blacklist, causing travel restrictions and reputational damages; participation in a cross-border fraud or tax evasion scheme exposes them to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201, including imprisonment of three to seven years for serious evasion.
