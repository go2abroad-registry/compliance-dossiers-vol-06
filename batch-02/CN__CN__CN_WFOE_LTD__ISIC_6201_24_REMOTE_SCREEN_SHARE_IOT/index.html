Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online enterprise tax portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) available at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and through provincial electronic tax bureau accounts.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory taxpayer identification number and VAT taxpayer identification number. After SAMR incorporation, the tax registration is automatically synchronized under the multi-license integration regime; the WFOE must activate its tax account through the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days after obtaining the business license, authenticate the legal representative and financial officer, complete the taxpayer classification (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer), and formalize invoice issuance capabilities. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment but deriving Chinese-source income is assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number by the competent STA upon the initial withholding or self-filing procedure; this does not alter the non-resident status of that foreign enterprise.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT export zero-rating for remote desktop access and unattended machine telemetry software services, the WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer and the supply must qualify as software services or information system services provided to an overseas customer and wholly consumed outside China. The statutory criteria require that the service is supplied to an overseas institution or individual, the customer is not a Chinese taxable entity, the service is not used or performed within China, and the foreign-exchange proceeds are received in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. The legal basis includes Caishui [2016] No. 36 and applicable STA announcements on cross-border taxable acts zero-rating; small-scale VAT taxpayers may apply the VAT exemption regime instead of the zero-rating refund regime.

  • Signed cross-border service contract, statement of work, and technical specification evidencing remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software services delivered from China to the overseas customer.
  • Commercial VAT invoice issued in foreign currency or English, clearly referencing the zero-rated service and the tax rate of 0%.
  • Bank receipt and foreign exchange income declaration/report issued by the Chinese settlement bank evidencing receipt of the overseas customer's remittance; in China this is the bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipt Declaration rather than an Inward Remittance Certificate, together with the corresponding SAFE transaction report.
  • Proof of overseas consumption: customer due diligence records, IP address and geo-location evidence, remote access server logs, unattended machine telemetry endpoint locations, and documentation establishing that no party in China consumes the software service.
  • If physical goods are exported alongside the software (for example hardware-embedded IoT devices), the Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, export invoice, and export tax-refund declaration filed with the STA export tax-refund system are also mandatory.

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-certified VAT invoice tax-control system or, for fully digitalized e-invoices, through the State Taxation Administration fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform. Invoice data are transmitted in real time to the STA VAT invoice electronic base ledger; each invoice must carry a valid tax-control digital stamp/verification code generated by tax-control equipment or the national e-invoice platform before it can be used for input VAT credit. Monthly VAT filing must reconcile issued invoices, received special VAT invoices, and the electronic invoice ledger, with structured invoice data (OFD/XML) retained for audit.

The STA and SAFE may audit the zero-rating claim, require full substantiation, and disallow or reverse the 0% VAT treatment and associated input VAT refunds where the documentation is incomplete, fraudulent, or the foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the required period. Clawback actions include recovery of the short-paid output VAT and refunded input VAT, daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of unpaid or underpaid tax and from 100% to 500% of fraudulently obtained export tax refunds, and referral for criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export tax refund or false VAT invoice issuance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime administered by the PBOC and SAFE. Current account items, including software subscriptions, remote support service fees and profit remittances, are broadly convertible once the bank is satisfied that the transaction is genuine, the underlying contract/invoice is present, and tax liabilities are settled. Capital account items are not freely convertible. A foreign investor may inject registered paid-in capital under the FDI framework after completing enterprise establishment and foreign exchange registration, and only through a licensed bank in China; the converted RMB funds from the foreign exchange capital account must be used strictly for the WFOE's operating purposes and may not be channeled into securities investment, entrusted loans, real estate outside business use, or non-business loan repayment. There is no mandatory surrender requirement for export proceeds; retained foreign currency may be held in a current-account foreign exchange deposit. Profit remittance by a WFOE to its non-resident parent is treated as a current-account outward remittance after audited financial statements, enterprise income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax assessment, and a shareholder profit-distribution resolution. The remitting bank will verify the audited financials, tax clearance evidence and corporate authorization; if the WFOE has insufficient foreign currency balances, it may purchase foreign exchange with proper documentation. Practical hurdles include the need for annual statutory audit, local tax clearance, updated beneficial ownership details and a legitimate profit-distribution resolution that matches the paid-in capital account records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's administration of trade in goods and services, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic bank account of the exporting WFOE. Goods export proceeds must be received and reported to SAFE through the bank within 90 days after the export customs declaration, unless a delayed-receipt filing is approved. Service exports, including remote desktop access, IoT telemetry and screen-share software subscriptions, must be collected within the contractual payment date and typically no later than 90 days after invoice issuance or service completion; the bank performs single-transaction verification through the SAFE digital platform. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. There is no statutory obligation to convert any portion of export proceeds into CNY; the WFOE may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts in a current-account foreign exchange deposit or voluntarily convert at the commercial rate, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and consistent with tax/customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury or clearing rail. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully open a corporate foreign exchange capital account for a China-domiciled WFOE, cannot obtain SAFE registration codes for inbound FDI, and cannot act as the settlement bank for statutory capital contributions. Using an offshore EMI as the main treasury account would create a liquidity, verification and AML/CFT gap that Chinese banks and SAFE will not accept. The only acceptable use is as a merchant payment gateway for collecting foreign B2B/B2C SaaS subscription revenues, with settlement routed into a licensed Chinese commercial bank account. Even then, the EMI must operate through a licensed local cross-border payment partner; independent EMI custody of WFOE operating funds is legally barred. Overall viability rating: 2/5 for merchant collection, 1/5 for treasury banking, 0/5 for FDI capital custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder routes investment funds via SWIFT MT103 into the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE must first complete SAFE/MOFCOM registration and obtain a banking foreign-exchange establishment/temporary admission code. The remittance must include the exact beneficial-owner name, purpose code for FDI capital contribution, and SAFE registration reference. After the bank effects inward checking and capital registration, the funds may be converted into RMB under bank supervision, credited to a restricted capital-account RMB payment account, and then transferred to the WFOE's ordinary RMB basic deposit account for operating disbursements. A certified public accountant in China then issues a capital verification report confirming the actual paid-in capital received, to be filed with the corporate registry and used for statutory reporting.For pure software and remote-support trade, the default clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for supplier and subcontractor payments after verification of contract, invoice, tax clearance and purpose-of-payment declaration. For IoT hardware units bundled with software, commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700 and MT720) are accepted for physical-goods settlement, and documentary collections (SWIFT MT400) may be used for smaller trade flows with trusted buyers. All outbound disbursements must be supported by real commercial documentation, must pass bank anti-fraud review, and must be reported to SAFE under the current-account payment verification framework.Delivery-based RMB/USD forward contracts executed onshore with a licensed Chinese bank based on genuine current-account trade or FDI-related exposureRMB/USD FX swaps for matching short-term liquidity in both currencies, limited to tenor of up to one year and backed by registered cross-border trade flowsCross-currency interest rate swaps for long-term shareholder loans or registered foreign debt where both principal and interest are subject to SAFE debt registrationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) strictly prohibited for onshore WFOE treasury; only offshore group treasury entities may use NDFs, and any NDF hedge for the China entity must be nullified by onshore deliverable hedging to avoid violation of SAFE derivatives rules

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility as a primary treasury or clearing rail. An offshore digital EMI cannot lawfully open a corporate foreign exchange capital account for a China-domiciled WFOE, cannot obtain SAFE registration codes for inbound FDI, and cannot act as the settlement bank for statutory capital contributions. Using an offshore EMI as the main treasury account would create a liquidity, verification and AML/CFT gap that Chinese banks and SAFE will not accept. The only acceptable use is as a merchant payment gateway for collecting foreign B2B/B2C SaaS subscription revenues, with settlement routed into a licensed Chinese commercial bank account. Even then, the EMI must operate through a licensed local cross-border payment partner; independent EMI custody of WFOE operating funds is legally barred. Overall viability rating: 2/5 for merchant collection, 1/5 for treasury banking, 0/5 for FDI capital custody.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required if annual related-party transactions reach any of: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File is required when total annual related-party transaction value reaches RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country reporting is required for resident ultimate parent groups with consolidated group revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion in the previous fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime of the UAE type, but the EIT Law general anti-avoidance rule (article 47 and implementation rules) and the substance requirements of SAT Announcement 2015 No. 60 for treaty benefits allow the tax authority to re-characterize a WFOE that has no recruitment, no local R&D, no board decisions, no operating authority, or no core income-generating activities in Mainland China. The WFOE's remote software and IoT business profits must be economically attributable to personnel, assets and functions located in China; if the Chinese entity is a mere riskless shell, the tax authority may impute profit, deny treaty benefits, disregard the entity, and apply CFC rules to the non-resident parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell entity and lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law article 47, the State Taxation Administration may disregard the entity, re-characterize transactions, and adjust taxable income; under Tax Collection Administration Law article 63 the adjustment may be reclassified as tax evasion with a penalty of 50% to 500% of unpaid tax, plus daily interest and late-payment surcharges; the entity may also be blacklisted in the State Taxation Administration major tax violation case publication system.

Director personal liability: A Chinese court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law article 23 and hold shareholders and directors jointly and severally liable when the entity is abused to evade taxes or harm creditors; directors can face travel bans, asset freezes, personal enforcement of restitution orders, and personal criminal liability under Criminal Law article 201 for willful tax evasion, including imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer pricing manipulation of intercompany royalties and service fees

Statutory sanction: EIT Law articles 41 and 44 authorize the tax authority to make a re-determination on an arm's length basis, adjust taxable income, and impose interest under article 48; Tax Collection Administration Law article 44 permits collection security measures; the adjustment can be treated as tax evasion and liable to a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; criminal exposure arises under Criminal Law article 201 where false invoices or fictitious agreements are used.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party agreements or knowingly prepare false transfer pricing documentation are liable for civil damages to creditors and the tax authority; the court may order personal restitution from the directors' assets, impose travel bans and blacklisting, and convict them for tax evasion or false issuance of invoices, with imprisonment up to seven years.
Failure to withhold and remit WHT on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Administration Law article 69, the withholding agent must pay the amount that should have been withheld and is subject to a fine of 50% to 300% of the unrecovered withholding tax; under article 32, a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge applies; the unpaid WHT is not time-barred in cases of malicious concealment and interest continues to accrue.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance officer, and actual controller may be personally liable under the piercing doctrine when the WFOE is left without assets after paying dividends; the tax authority can seize personal assets, terminate export tax refund eligibility, and impose a prohibition on exit; directors who knowingly aided the under-withholding or diversion of tax funds can be prosecuted under Criminal Law article 201 with criminal fines and imprisonment for up to seven years.
