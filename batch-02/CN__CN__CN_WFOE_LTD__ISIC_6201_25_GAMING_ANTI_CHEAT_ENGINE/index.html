Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Ordinary WFOE limited companies have no statutory minimum paid-up capital under PRC Company Law. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the online electronic portal is the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform (E-Tax), the unified national system for tax registration, VAT filing, invoice issuance and fiscal clearance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which under China's multi-certificate integration reform is the statutory Tax Identification Number for tax, VAT, customs and social insurance. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days after issuance of the business license, complete consolidated tax registration with the competent local STA tax bureau via the E-Tax platform by confirming legal entity particulars, registered address, bank account details, business scope and intended tax types. Step 3: File the tax type determination application, including VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty and other local taxes as applicable; the bureau then assigns the VAT filing cycle and payment calendar. Step 4: Elect or be assigned the VAT taxpayer status: small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable turnover is below CNY 5,000,000, or general VAT taxpayer where turnover exceeds that threshold or where the entity voluntarily opts in; apply for the electronic invoice quota through E-Tax. Step 5: Complete the Foreign Investment Information Reporting required by the Foreign Investment Law on SAMR and banking channels. The USCC alone is used as the VAT registration number; no separate VAT ID is issued. Any change in capital, shareholder, legal representative, address or business scope must be reported to STA within 30 days.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The mandatory VAT registration threshold is the requirement to register as a general VAT taxpayer when annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000. Below that threshold a WFOE may maintain small-scale taxpayer status, but there is no VAT registration exemption for taxable supplies made in China; any non-resident seller with taxable digital services consumed in China must also obtain the relevant registration and file VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the cross-border service VAT zero-rating rules (including software services, information technology services and transfer of technology), the WFOE may claim the 0% VAT rate for its anti-cheat engine middleware if: (1) the software, license or information technology service is supplied to an enterprise or individual outside mainland China; (2) the service is wholly consumed or used outside mainland China; (3) the contractual counterparty, payment source and beneficial user are all offshore; (4) the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer filing under the ordinary calculation method; and (5) the offshore supply is separately declared in the VAT return and supported by contemporaneous documentation of cross-border character and offshore consumption. For software delivered as physical goods or embedded hardware, zero-rating is achieved through the customs goods-export process with an export declaration. For pure digital services (downloads, SaaS, licensing), no customs export declaration is required, but the service must be demonstrably consumed outside China; the normal domestic rate for software services is 6% and the zero-rating mechanism reduces that rate to 0%.

  • Cross-border software license or service agreement providing that the anti-cheat middleware is supplied to an overseas customer and is wholly consumed outside mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and zero-rated VAT invoice generated through the E-Tax platform with the offshore export sale indication and 0% tax rate line
  • E-Tax platform zero-rated cross-border service export record and reconciliation schedule filed with the VAT return
  • Bank foreign-exchange settlement receipt or export proceeds collection receipt issued by a designated Chinese foreign-exchange bank (equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing receipt of offshore payment in accordance with SAFE rules
  • If the middleware is exported as physical goods or embedded hardware: Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), packing list, Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, and customs declaration number recorded on the VAT return
  • Destination and consumption evidence: overseas server or IP logs, end-user attestation, or the overseas client's statement confirming that no taxable consumption took place in mainland China

China mandates real-time digitalized electronic invoicing through the nationwide VAT invoice system integrated into the STA E-Tax platform. A WFOE must apply for an electronic invoice quota (monthly invoice amount and per-invoice caps) online. Every invoice is generated through the State Taxation Administration's digital invoice service, stamped with a unique national invoice number and a tax authority cryptographic signature, cleared in real time and stored centrally. Taxpayers may connect their billing or ERP systems through the official E-Tax API integration pathway; invoices issued outside this digital clearance channel are unlawful. The E-Tax system automatically pre-fills VAT returns from cleared invoice data, blocks issuance above the approved quota, and reconciles invoice flows across purchasers and sellers under the Golden Tax System Phase IV.

STA conducts post-export audit review by cross-checking the VAT zero-rate declaration, E-Tax invoice data, bank settlement receipts and customs data under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. If the zero-rating is found unsubstantiated or fraudulent, STA will disallow the 0% rate and assess VAT at the applicable domestic rate (6% for software services or 13% for goods) together with late-payment interest at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties range from 50% to five times the tax undercharged under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law. Cases involving forged documents, fake invoices or circular offshore transactions are treated as export fraud; the taxpayer may be barred from export tax zero-rating and refund for 12 to 36 months, and criminal liability may be pursued under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border trade in services such as software licensing, maintenance fees, and anti-cheat middleware subscriptions, provided the commercial transaction is bona fide and supported by genuine contracts and invoices. Capital account items, however, remain heavily regulated: foreign direct investment into a WFOE must be routed through a SAFE-registered capital account, followed by bank-level verification, and cannot be freely deployed into domestic loans, equity investments, or non-business-purpose fund transfers without specific SAFE approvals. Profit remittances to offshore shareholders are permissible under current account rules once the WFOE has completed its annual audit, verified its distributable reserves, and evidenced that all taxes, in particular corporate income tax and withholding tax on dividends, have been settled. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds of services, but all receipts must be repatriated to a domestic bank account and reconciled against the reporting system; proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at market rates. Practical hurdles include purpose-code integrity, documentation of beneficial ownership, and the prohibition on commingling personal and corporate funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service export receipts, China imposes no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory surrender percentage; however, the WFOE must confirm receipt through its domestic operating bank within the applicable customs and SAFE data-reporting windows, usually no more than five working days after the funds enter the account system. The bank will verify the trade-in-services contract, invoice, and electronic customs declaration or service trade registration; excess proceeds above the contracted amount may be frozen for review, and retained foreign currency balances must be reported in the SAFE balance-of-payments system. Profit-derived repatriation requires dividend approval documents, audit report, tax clearance certificates, and a local bank application using the appropriate current account exchange code.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers, such as those operating under the European E-Money Directive or a Singapore MPI license, are not legally authorized to provide deposit-taking, settlement, or custody services to a Chinese mainland entity in RMB. They cannot issue local account numbers on the domestic CNAPS or CHIPS network, cannot access SAFE's direct reporting channels, and cannot substitute for a licensed Chinese commercial bank for capital account registration or foreign exchange verification. Any attempt to use an EMI as the primary treasury conduit would be deemed non-compliant and pose a severe AML risk. A plausible ancillary use is limited to facilitating inbound wire references before funds are transmitted into the designated domestic bank account, but the WFOE's statutory books and SAFE filings must always reflect the licensed Chinese bank as the settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the offshore parent must be routed through a SAFE-registered basic foreign exchange account at a licensed Chinese bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when any related-party transaction category exceeds the following annual RMB thresholds: tangible property transfers RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers RMB 100 million; service transactions RMB 40 million (SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42). Master File is generally required for an MNE group whose aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or whose consolidated revenue meets the CbC reporting threshold; the WFOE must also file the Annual Related-Party Transaction Reporting forms (including schedules for services, royalties, interest, and cost-sharing) in the e-Tax portal.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not have a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation statute, but its general anti-avoidance rule (EIT Law Article 47), special anti-treaty-abuse rules, and the OECD-influenced special tax adjustment chapter require that the WFOE possess the actual functions, assets, risks, and control over Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA). For an anti-cheat engine company, CIGA includes software engineering, kernel-level algorithm development, threat-response, server operations, client/developer support, and product commercialization decisions. A WFOE that is a bare licensor while an offshore parent retains beneficial ownership of the intangible and risks will be recharacterized, with income reattributed to the offshore party and, if that party has a Chinese PE, the 25% rate applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through false R&D super-deductions and under-declared software revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of unpaid tax, interest, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may result in imprisonment up to seven years plus fines if the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and shadow directors who sign false returns can be personally liable for civil restitution when corporate assets are insufficient, subject to asset freezes and travel bans (exit restrictions) under the tax administration law, and may face imprisonment as principal offenders of a unit crime.
Transfer pricing manipulation and failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 48 allow a transfer pricing adjustment with an imputed recharacterization of profit, interest at the benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points, and penalties up to 500% of the underpaid tax if fraud is found; SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 authorizes adverse inference when documentation is missing.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve circular cash flows, backdated agreements, or artificial pricing may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax under the corporate-veil piercing doctrine of the Company Law; they can be blacklisted for tax-related exit bans and, if the scheme is found to be intentional tax evasion, imprisoned for up to seven years.
Improper outbound withholding: non-withheld dividends/interest/royalties and sham beneficial ownership

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 69 punishes failure to withhold with an order to make good the unwithheld tax and a fine of 50% to 300% of that amount; in addition, EIT Law Article 39 allows collection from the non-resident taxpayer and the withholding agent may be held secondarily liable, with interest and administrative fines.

Director personal liability: The director who authorizes the payment without withholding can be made personally liable if company assets have been stripped or the limited liability veil is abused; tax commissioners may issue travel bans/blacklisting against the legal representative until the tax is settled, and intentional evasion may lead to criminal prosecution for theft of state tax revenue with imprisonment up to seven years plus fines.
