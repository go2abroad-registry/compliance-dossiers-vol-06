Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected as the sole registered address for a China WFOE. SAMR requires a physical lease agreement and evidence of exclusive use rights for the registered premises. Tax authorities reject virtual-only addresses for VAT registration and invoice issuance, and commercial banks require an on-site verification visit with a visible company nameplate, staff, and equipment. For an offshore development center engaged in software engineering, a virtual address creates a material risk of a finding of no commercial substance, bank account refusal, and administrative penalty. The minimum acceptable structure is a serviced office with actual desks, or a dedicated leased office in a commercial building.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a WFOE whose business scope is limited to ISIC 6202 computer programming, consultancy and related services. However, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, each shareholder must fully pay its subscribed capital within five years after establishment unless a shorter period is required by the State Council; the contribution amount and schedule must be written in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow account is required for a standard offshore development center, but cash contributions must be deposited into a bank account in the company's own name and adequately evidenced in the capital contribution ledger.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic filing and registration portal: the Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain commercial registration as a WFOE from the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR); the AMR issues an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory PRC Taxpayer Identification Number for all legal entities. Step 2: Within 30 days of business license issuance, complete tax registration with the STA local office through the Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax portal using the USCC, articles of association, legal representative identity documents, registered address and domestic bank account details. Step 3: Select VAT status: small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer; general status is compulsory if assessed annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, but the WFOE may elect general status after satisfying accounting and invoice-control requirements. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident entity with no PRC establishment, no separate TIN is required solely for equity ownership; if it receives Chinese-source income and no withholding agent exists, it must register with the STA for withholding administration through the local tax service hall or e-Tax to obtain a non-resident taxpayer ID. Step 5: Apply for export zero-rating filing status and VAT invoice issuance permissions under the WFOE's USCC.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for a WFOE; a WFOE is automatically a PRC resident enterprise taxable at entity level on worldwide income. Voluntary general VAT taxpayer status can be elected only after statutory turnover and accounting-system conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period; a foreign seller with no PRC establishment must register directly or appoint a withholding agent if it makes taxable sales into or within China unless a statutory cross-border service exemption or zero-rating rule applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physical exports from mainland China to destinations outside the customs territory qualify for zero-rated VAT, provided the export customs declaration is filed, goods leave China, and foreign exchange is received. Services: software engineering, development, maintenance and related offshore services provided to an overseas enterprise may qualify for zero-rated VAT if the services are supplied by the WFOE, consumed wholly outside China, the offshore recipient is not a PRC resident, payment originates from outside China and contemporaneous contracts, performance and delivery evidence demonstrate foreign use. Pure domestic use, resale to PRC parties or onshore consumption disqualifies zero-rating and triggers VAT at 6% or 13%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration) submitted to the General Administration of Customs via the China International Trade Single Window for physical goods exports.
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (original or electronic surrogate) evidencing movement of goods out of mainland China.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-approved bank foreign-exchange settlement record proving receipt of export consideration.
  • Cross-border service substantiation package for zero-rated software services: signed offshore software development and maintenance contract, zero-rated export VAT invoice, certificate of overseas tax registration/residence of the foreign recipient, and delivery and payment records showing that the service is wholly consumed outside China.

The STA mandates electronic VAT invoicing through its national electronic invoice platform. All VAT invoices must be issued digitally or through tax-control equipment and transmitted to the STA in near-real time; invoice serial numbers and anti-counterfeit codes are centrally allocated. General taxpayers must use API-based or web-based invoice issuance integrated with the e-Tax platform. High-volume exporters reconcile their zero-rated invoice data with customs declarations and foreign-exchange settlement records through the Golden Tax Phase IV system, which performs automatic risk screening; red-credit notes and voided invoices also require digital reporting.

Export zero-rating is audited against STA import/export data, GACC customs declarations, banking FX settlement records and invoice/input-VAT logs. If the zero-rating is unsubstantiated, the claimed refund is clawed back, output VAT is assessed at the applicable rate (13% for goods or 6% for software services) with late-payment surcharge. Deliberate fraud including falsified customs or banking evidence is punishable by recovery plus an administrative penalty of one to five times the fraudulent refund, and criminal referral for tax evasion; repeated deficiencies can suspend export-tax-refund facilitation and downgrade the taxpayer credit rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls, while the current account is largely convertible under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the rules of SAFE. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in offshore software development and maintenance (ISIC 6202), current account transactions such as payment for services, employee salary remittances, and routine operating expenses can be settled in RMB or foreign currency upon presentation of genuine supporting documentation and a valid contract. However, capital account items remain heavily regulated. Foreign direct investment capital injections into a WFOE require cross-border inbound remittance declarations, and the RMB converted from such capital must be placed in a designated pair account (foreign exchange capital account and RMB capital account). Surplus funds from capital contributions cannot be freely used for domestic equity investment or on-lending without SAFE approval, and profit remittances to overseas shareholders are subject to audited financial statements, corporate income tax payment certificates, and a board resolution declaring dividends. Profit repatriation is generally permitted after tax but must be routed through a domestic bank that verifies compliance with SAFE's circulars, including the 2019 'Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Cross-Border Trade and Investment' and the 2020 'Notice on strengthening the management of current account foreign exchange revenue and expenditure'. In practice, conversion of export proceeds from software services must be monitored: although China has formally abolished the mandatory surrender of export proceeds, banks must verify that foreign exchange receipts are booked into a current account and converted to RMB only upon matching against customs or service trade declarations. The WFOE is fully exposed to RMB/USD volatility, and hedging is only permitted for real underlying exposures via qualified domestic banks under SAFE's derivatives rules. NDFs for RMB are generally prohibited onshore, but onshore forward contracts and swaps are available for cross-border trade and investment flows. The practical convertibility for the ODC is therefore high for current account operations, moderate for capital account moves such as debt or equity restructuring, and constrained by strict document-based verification and anti-round-tripping rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline and mandatory conversion ratios for export foreign currency receipts: No mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may keep 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency, but all such proceeds must be settled into a domestic current account within 30 days of receipt and reported via the SAFE digital platform. Failure to report and match against underlying service contracts may trigger suspension of foreign exchange settlement capabilities.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) lack legal authorization to provide multi-currency local settlement accounts or FX conversion services to wholly foreign-owned enterprises in mainland China. The People's Bank of China and SAFE have not issued any license permitting foreign EMIs to interface directly with the RMB clearing system or to act as settlement agents for domestic WFOEs. Cross-border receipts must flow through a local bank with a SAFE-approved 'signature bank' status; using an EMI to collect client receivables prior to sweeps into China creates a tax compliance and FX violation risk, as the revenue trail is opaque to SAFE. The only permissible use of an EMI is as a non-recourse invoicing convenience for overseas clients, provided that funds are ultimately remitted to the WFOE's domestic bank account via SWIFT under a proper service trade declaration. However, given the risk of a 'false transaction' determination and the impossibility of obtaining formal SAFE approval for such a route, fintech EMI viability is rated 'Low' for the WFOE's corporate treasury. The WFOE should rely exclusively on its domestic Tier-1 banks for transactional FX and legal clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For an inbound capital injection from a foreign shareholder, the WFOE must first complete business registration with SAMR and obtain a Unique Social Credit Code. The shareholder remits foreign currency (usually USD) from an overseas bank directly to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account (外汇资本金账户) in a designated Tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance must include a remittance purpose code consistent with 'Direct Investment - Initial Capital Contribution'. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the funds against the FDI Declaration, converts the foreign currency to RMB at the bank's spot rate (or at the opening of a forward transaction if the WFOE has a valid hedging need), and transfers the RMB into a separate RMB Capital Account. The bank then submits the capital detailed report to SAFE within 10 business days. Subsequently, the WFOE must conduct a capital verification (验资) through a licensed Chinese CPA firm, which issues a Capital Verification Report to be filed with the Market Regulation Department. The total capital can be used for approved operational expenses (rent, salaries, local procurement) but cannot be invested in equity or bank wealth management products without special SAFE permission. For regular working capital from real business income (customers' payments), the inbound credits are routed through the Current Account (经常项目外汇账户), which requires a service trade declaration and tax registration. The WFOE must maintain a 30-day report cycle on SAFE's platform; every transaction must be tagged with the appropriate service code (e.g., '221000' for computer/information services). Wire protocol: SWIFT MT103 with a 'Purpose of Payment' field carefully describing the actual service rendered, e.g., 'Offshore software development outsourcing - monthly fixed fee' to avoid mismatches. The bank may issue a Payment Instruction to the exporter (出口收汇) if the amount exceeds USD 50,000, requiring an invoice and proof of delivery.Outbound disbursements from the WFOE to overseas vendors or subsidiaries (e.g., subcontracted cloud infrastructure costs, hardware licensing, or external consultants) are cleared through the same Tier-1 domestic bank using SWIFT MT103 for working capital transfers, provided that the underlying reason is a current account item. For trade settlements, if the ODC purchases software licenses from foreign suppliers or makes periodic royalty payments, the bank will settle via SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate foreign exchange transaction code. For larger-scale procurement where the transaction is part of a physical goods trade (e.g., buying hardware for the ODC's internal data center), an Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit (L/C) may be issued in favor of the supplier, with the bank acting as issuing bank. Documents required under the L/C include commercial invoice, transport document (bill of lading or air waybill), and a certificate of insurance, if applicable. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are also available but riskier; due to the service nature of the business, most ODC outflows will be simple MT103 transfers for consultancy fees, licensing, and cloud service subscriptions, which must be supported by service contracts and invoices to the domestic bank's cross-border compliance department. For recurring monthly subscriptions, the WFOE can set up a 'reserve instruction' with the bank to execute a batch MT103 monthly, subject to automatic documentary verification. All outbound wires must be assigned an outbound purpose code in the SAFE system, and any payment exceeding USD 50,000 will trigger a check of the recipient's business background and the beneficiary's tax identification number.Onshore RMB/USD Forward Contracts (with actual delivery) for hedging known future revenue streams from overseas clients, up to 12 months tenor, requiring a signed underlying contract. No cash settlement. Permitted by SAFE under the 'Guidelines for Derivatives Trading of Banks'. Available from Tier-1 banks on a best-effort basis.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are strictly prohibited for onshore clients in mainland China. The WFOE cannot transact RMB NDFs domestically. Offshore NDFs would violate the PBOC's circular on RMB forwards; hence the WFOE must rely on deliverable forwards (DF) or cross-currency swaps.Currency Swaps (FX Swap / Cross-Currency Swap) allowed for legitimate capital account operations, such as swapping the WFOE's RMB capital to USD for overseas acquisition or lending, with a maximum tenor aligned to the maturity of the underlying investment or the shareholder loan. Requires SAFE approval for the associated capital transaction.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Wise, Payoneer, Revolut Business) lack legal authorization to provide multi-currency local settlement accounts or FX conversion services to wholly foreign-owned enterprises in mainland China. The People's Bank of China and SAFE have not issued any license permitting foreign EMIs to interface directly with the RMB clearing system or to act as settlement agents for domestic WFOEs. Cross-border receipts must flow through a local bank with a SAFE-approved 'signature bank' status; using an EMI to collect client receivables prior to sweeps into China creates a tax compliance and FX violation risk, as the revenue trail is opaque to SAFE. The only permissible use of an EMI is as a non-recourse invoicing convenience for overseas clients, provided that funds are ultimately remitted to the WFOE's domestic bank account via SWIFT under a proper service trade declaration. However, given the risk of a 'false transaction' determination and the impossibility of obtaining formal SAFE approval for such a route, fintech EMI viability is rated 'Low' for the WFOE's corporate treasury. The WFOE should rely exclusively on its domestic Tier-1 banks for transactional FX and legal clearing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is compulsory when annual related-party tangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible property-related transactions exceed RMB 100 million, related-party services exceed RMB 40 million, or related-party loan balances exceed RMB 50 million. Master File is mandatory when the related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion or the group meets the consolidated revenue test under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016). Even below these thresholds, contemporaneous transfer-pricing analysis should be prepared to defend arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: China has no Gulf-state Economic Substance Regulations, but the tax authorities apply substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, business purpose and the general anti-abuse rule under CIT Law Article 47. A WFOE offshore development center must demonstrate real employees, office premises, management decisions, entrepreneurial risk assumption and Core Income-Generating Activities in China. If the WFOE is a mere shell, the tax authority can recharacterize the structure, allocate profit to the overseas principal, deny DTA benefits and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing and False Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 48; SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016); Tax Administration Law Article 63. The tax authority may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impose interest at the RMB benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points, add a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax if fraud or deliberate concealment is found.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who deliberately approved the false pricing or stripped the WFOE of assets may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 23, making them jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and creditor claims. Intentional tax evasion exposes directors to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201, with fines and imprisonment up to 3 years for ordinary tax evasion and up to 7 years for serious cases.
DTA Abuse through Shell or Conduit Holding Structure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and the SAT general anti-abuse rule; SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018). The tax authority may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the conduit, collect the full 10% statutory withholding plus interest and daily surcharges, and impose tax administrative penalties. Serious cases result in publication on the serious-tax-violation blacklist and joint punishment by multiple government agencies.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial-ownership attestations or knowingly participated in the conduit scheme face personal civil liability for the underwithheld tax, asset preservation orders, travel restrictions, prohibition on leaving China, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for using deceptive means to evade taxes, with imprisonment up to 7 years and fines.
False VAT Invoicing and Criminal Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. The Criminal Law Article 205 punishes false VAT invoicing, and Article 201 punishes tax evasion. The tax authority must transfer the case to the public security authorities when the underpaid tax exceeds the criminal threshold; enterprises may be deregistered, blacklisted and have their export or payment facilitation capabilities suspended.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial controller and directors who organized or knowingly approved the false invoicing and revenue diversion may be arrested and criminally prosecuted. Responsible officers under Article 205 face imprisonment up to life imprisonment in cases involving especially huge amounts; under Article 201 they face imprisonment up to 7 years and fines. Civil and administrative joint-punishment measures include travel bans, exit restrictions, high-consumption restriction, disqualification from serving as directors, asset freeze and full restitution of the tax loss.
