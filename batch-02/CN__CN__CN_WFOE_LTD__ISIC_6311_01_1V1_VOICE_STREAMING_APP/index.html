Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a general wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company under the PRC Company Law 2023 and Foreign Investment Law 2020. The shareholders may fix the registered capital in the Articles of Association and must pay the subscribed capital in full by the agreed contribution deadline; cash contributions are subject to bank deposit verification. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, exercised through provincial, municipal and district tax service bureaus; electronic compliance is conducted through the official e-Tax Bureau portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the STA Golden Tax Phase IV centralized digital tax-control system.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE): (1) complete incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the integrated national enterprise registration platform and obtain the Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which becomes the WFOE's Chinese Tax Identification Number; (2) within 30 days of license issuance, complete tax registration with the competent local tax service bureau through the e-Tax Bureau portal or in-person counter, confirming registration with the provincial STA and filing the financial accounting system, bank account, legal representative and controlling foreign investor details; (3) determine VAT taxpayer status and, where annual taxable turnover reaches RMB 5,000,000 or where input VAT credit is desired, register as a VAT general taxpayer with the competent tax authority and obtain approval for official VAT fapiao issuance; (4) for a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment, the non-resident entity must separately apply for a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Identification Number with the STA tax service bureau where the payor or property is located, or file through its authorized withholding agent, submitting the non-resident registration application, evidence of incorporation, contract evidencing China-source income, and withholding agent appointment documents; (5) complete foreign exchange registration with the relevant designated foreign exchange bank or through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) digital system for future export/import receipts and cross-border payment evidence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the statutory threshold for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; all enterprises engaged in taxable sales must complete basic tax registration upon establishment irrespective of turnover, and taxpayers below RMB 5,000,000 may voluntarily register as VAT general taxpayers. A WFOE with predominantly app-store digital services should register as a VAT general taxpayer to preserve input VAT credit eligibility and export/service zero-rating or exemption rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: physical exports from China customs territory qualify for the VAT zero rate and export tax rebate only when there is a formal outward customs declaration, actual departure of goods evidenced by a transport document, an export sale to a non-resident buyer, and receipt of foreign exchange proceeds by the WFOE; the WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer with properly credited input VAT. Qualifying offshore digital services: the voice streaming and companion social application services may qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption if they fall within the STA's cross-border taxable services rules, including under Notice Caishui [2016] No. 36 and the State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 53 of 2016 on cross-border taxable services exemption, provided the service is supplied to an overseas recipient, is completely consumed outside the PRC, does not relate to domestic persons, real estate or enterprises, and payment is received from outside China. For app-store distribution through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the WFOE must maintain platform store settlement reports, non-resident user download/geo-location evidence, and interface evidence showing the service is made available to offshore territories; services consumed by users located within the PRC are domestic supplies subject to VAT at the applicable rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outward export customs declaration form with customs release endorsement, export license where applicable, and customs-assigned examination code)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal/air transport document proving physical export and offshore destination of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, under current SAFE and bank digitalization practice, a designated foreign exchange bank receipt/export proceeds collection and settlement voucher evidencing the non-resident buyer's remittance and timely FX repatriation

China does not operate a traditional per-transaction fiscal clearance device regime; however, the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (digital e-invoice) reform require all VAT invoices to be issued through the official e-Tax Bureau digital invoice platform using STA-controlled invoice codes, 20-digit invoice numbers and a digitally stamped anti-fraud two-dimensional code. Transaction-level invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time upon issuance. Large enterprises and certified electronic invoice service providers may use approved electronic invoice APIs, but all data ultimately clears through the STA e-Tax Bureau. Special VAT invoices for input crediting, export invoices and zero-rated/exempt service invoices must be issued within the statutory time limits and reported in the VAT filing return; no separate paper fiscal treasury stamp is used.

The STA and customs/SAFE authorities jointly audit export VAT zero-rating and rebate claims. If a claim is unsupported by the required customs declaration, transport documents or foreign exchange receipt evidence, the tax authority will disallow the zero rate, claw back any refund already paid, and recharacterize the transaction as a domestic taxable supply. The taxpayer must then pay output VAT at the applicable rate plus late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date. Under Article 66 of the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds by false export declarations or other deceptive means triggers recovery of the refund, a fine of 100% to 500% of the refund obtained, and suspension of the right to apply for export tax rebates for a prescribed period; where criminal liability is established, Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China imposes fines and imprisonment ranging up to life imprisonment for especially large fraud amounts. There is no limitation period for recovery of fraudulent export refunds, tax evasion or refusal to pay tax under Article 52 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and retrospective clawback can therefore reach back beyond the ordinary five-year record retention period where fraud is proven.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime for the RMB with current account convertibility but strict capital account controls. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in voice streaming services must obtain all inbound foreign direct investment through a lawful FDI capital account, complete SAFE registration at the bank, and use a capital settlement account for RMB conversion purposes. Revenue derived from overseas app stores, including Apple App Store and Google Play, is treated as current account service income and can be received in a domestic foreign exchange current account, settled into RMB, or retained in FX, provided the transaction is genuine and backed by commercial agreements, invoices, and periodic settlement statements. Profit remittance to the foreign parent company is permitted once the WFOE has paid corporate income tax, prepared audited financial statements, obtained a tax payment certificate, and submitted a board resolution approving the dividend distribution. The bank will verify the authenticity, tax compliance, and beneficial ownership background before effecting the cross-border remittance. Capital account transactions, such as shareholder loans, equity transfers, or offshore guarantees, require additional SAFE approval and cannot be freely settled. Therefore, while service-related earnings are convertible on the current account, capital flows remain tightly controlled, and any attempt to route capital injection, intercompany loans, or profit repatriation without the required regulatory filings will be rejected by the relevant clearing bank and may trigger an SAFE penalty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current account administration, export and cross-border service proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within the verification window established by the bank, generally within 90 days from the date of service completion or contractual billing for service exports, and within 90 days from the customs declaration for goods exports, unless an extension is approved by the bank under the trade financing supervision system. Mandatory conversion ratio is 0% - China no longer imposes compulsory surrender of foreign export proceeds to the central bank; however, 100% of export/service receipts must be received into a SAFE-supervised FX account and either retained or settled voluntarily through a designated licensed bank based on genuine underlying trade and service documents.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally authorized to hold client funds, open domestic settlement accounts, or perform foreign exchange conversion directly for a mainland China WFOE. Such EMIs lack a SAFE payment institution license and cannot act as a local clearing rail. They may technically collect app store proceeds overseas if they hold appropriate licenses in the merchant’s offshore intermediate jurisdiction, but the funds must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed Chinese commercial bank and reported to SAFE. Therefore, the WFOE must rely on a tier-1 domestic bank for all onshore clearing, capital verification, and FX settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the direct offshore shareholder’s bank account to the WFOE’s FDI Capital Account at a SAFE-designated tier-1 bank. The payment instruction must clearly reference the purpose code for foreign direct investment, the shareholder name, the WFOE name, and the SAFE FDI registration number. Upon receipt, the bank will conduct authenticity screening; the funds are then placed in the capital settlement account for conversion into RMB at prevailing rates. A Chinese certified public accountant performs capital verification and issues a capital verification report, which is filed with SAFE and the market regulation authority. No capital injection may be converted to RMB until the capital verification and SAFE filing are completed. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not legally authorized to hold client funds, open domestic settlement accounts, or perform foreign exchange conversion directly for a mainland China WFOE. Such EMIs lack a SAFE payment institution license and cannot act as a local clearing rail. They may technically collect app store proceeds overseas if they hold appropriate licenses in the merchant’s offshore intermediate jurisdiction, but the funds must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed Chinese commercial bank and reported to SAFE. Therefore, the WFOE must rely on a tier-1 domestic bank for all onshore clearing, capital verification, and FX settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare Local File documentation if its annual related-party transactions reach any of these thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services (SAT Announcement No. 42 [2016]). Master File documentation is additionally mandated when cross-border related-party revenue or aggregate related-party transactions exceed the statutory RMB 1 billion threshold or when the enterprise is selected by the tax authority.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply a standalone zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but it enforces economic substance through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47, the 2018 Beneficial Ownership Rules, and treaty abuse doctrines. For a WFOE, the tax authorities will test whether the entity performs entrepreneurial functions, owns or controls unique intangibles, manages commercial risks, and conducts its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) by having management, employees, physical assets, and board decisions in China. A shell WFOE that merely signs paper contracts while the foreign parent makes all strategic and operational decisions risks being denied treaty benefits, having intercompany royalties disregarded, and facing permanent establishment scenarios in the architecturally high-tax jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Unreported Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Tax Collection Law Article 32, late tax bears a daily surcharge of 0.05%; Article 63 imposes recovery of unpaid tax and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the amount evaded. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion: imprisonment up to 3 years with a fine where the evaded amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, or imprisonment of 3 to 7 years where the amount is enormous and exceeds 30%. Criminal Law Article 205 adds criminal liability for falsely issued VAT invoices.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil where directors or shareholders commingle assets, create a mere façade, or grossly abuse the limited-liability structure to evade taxes. The responsible legal representative and directors face personal civil asset restitution, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans and blacklisting by the tax authority, and potential criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205. If the corporate form has been abused to shirk tax obligations, shareholders may be held jointly and severally liable for all unpaid CIT, WHT, penalties, and surcharges.
Treaty Abuse and Artificial Shell-Holding Structure

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and its implementing rules grant the tax authority the power to recharacterize or disregard transactions lacking reasonable commercial purpose; Article 48 imposes interest on the underpaid tax. SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018) denies treaty benefits where the beneficial owner is not substantive. The applicable sanction is an upward CIT adjustment, restoration of the 10% WHT, late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, and, where false documents were submitted, a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Law Article 64.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who personally designed the sham holding chain may be personally liable for the WFOE's under-withheld tax when the entity is treated as their alter ego. Remedies include civil asset tracing and restitution, seizure of personal assets for corporate tax debts, restrictions on exit, denial of visa status or tax-residence certification, and criminal liability if the treaty abuse was a deliberate scheme to evade tax. A director found guilty of tax evasion through the shell arrangement may receive imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.
Anti-Money Laundering and Payment-Channel Abuse in In-App Voice Commerce

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes rectification orders, confiscation of illegal gains, and financial penalties on institutions, with aggravating sanctions including fines up to RMB 5 million for severe violations and licence revocations; directly responsible directors and AML compliance officers face personal fines. Criminal Law Article 191 (money laundering) punishes conduct that conceals or disguises the criminal origin of proceeds with imprisonment for up to 10 years and criminal fines. Ancillary liability also arises under the Cybersecurity Law and Data Security Law for unlawful processing of user-financial data without consent.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly permit the app to be used as a money-laundering conduit cannot hide behind the corporate limited-liability shield. Courts may pierce the veil and order restitution of laundered assets from personal holdings; authorities may impose travel bans, asset freezes, and exit controls. Individuals who facilitate laundering, use shell treasury accounts, or approve anonymous settlement transfers face personal criminal imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 191, disqualification from acting as directors, and civil joint liability for damages suffered by the company or defrauded users.
