Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) / National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

A newly incorporated WFOE automatically obtains its tax identifier in the form of the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation. Within 30 calendar days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration on the e-Tax portal by linking the USCC to the local STA tax service office for its registered address, submitting the business license, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, and bank account information. The STA then activates the taxpayer profile, assigns tax categories, filing frequency, VAT invoice quota, and withholding obligations. A foreign non-resident entity without a PE or establishment that earns China-source income must either be registered directly with STA for non-resident enterprise tax or have its Chinese payer/WFOE act as withholding agent to obtain a non-resident tax code; the WFOE itself remains a resident taxpayer with the USCC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election; the WFOE is a per se resident enterprise taxed at entity level. The only relevant election is VAT status: a taxpayer with annual taxable turnover not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may elect general VAT taxpayer status in order to claim input VAT credits, while turnover above that threshold requires general taxpayer status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for tax registration: every WFOE must register for VAT immediately after incorporation. For general VAT taxpayer status, the annual taxable turnover threshold is CNY 5,000,000; above this threshold the WFOE must be a general taxpayer. The WFOE's digital app services are generally subject to the 6% VAT rate for modern/information services, not the 13% headline goods rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported through PRC customs are subject to 0% VAT when the exporter holds the customs export declaration, conveys title to the overseas buyer, and receives the export proceeds in accordance with SAFE. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas parties and wholly consumed outside mainland China, including software/information-system services, may be zero-rated under Caishui [2016] No. 36, provided the contract is with a non-resident, payment originates from abroad, actual service consumption occurs outside China, and the taxpayer files the statutory VAT export zero-rate/exemption return with a complete documentary file. If the app's video-matching service is reclassified by STA as a value-added telecommunications service rather than an information-system service, the supply may be VAT-exempt instead of zero-rated; the WFOE must secure the correct tax classification before claiming refunds.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (the PRC customs export goods declaration), required only for physical goods movement; not applicable to a pure digital service because no goods cross the customs frontier.
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / express logistics proof, required for physical exports; for digital downloads, replace with service delivery logs and remote-server/network access records.
  • Cross-border service contract or agreement with the overseas contracting party, identifying the non-resident counterparty, service scope, delivery mechanism, and pricing.
  • Foreign exchange income declaration / inward remittance certificate (FIRC) issued by a PRC designated foreign-exchange bank, evidencing collection of the export sales proceeds from overseas.
  • Commercial invoice and app-store payout statements (Apple IAP / Paymentwall) reconciling reported turnover and showing settlement from the overseas distribution channel.
  • Overseas consumption evidence such as IP geolocation logs, user registration country data, and service usage records demonstrating that the service is completely consumed outside mainland China.

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA National Electronic Invoice Platform (Digital Electronic Invoice system) and reported to the Golden Tax Phase IV system in real time. Issuance is controlled by the STA's tax-control document allocation; high-volume issuers may connect through the official APIs/EDI interfaces. VAT special invoices carry a unique electronic invoice number and tax-control seal; there is no separate physical fiscal stamping process. Monthly VAT returns must reconcile with platform invoice data and bank/Golden Tax records.

STA cross-matches Golden Tax IV invoice data, customs export declarations, SAFE foreign-exchange income declarations, and app-store payout reports. Any discrepancy—missing foreign-currency receipt, false invoices, unpaid/repatriated proceeds, or proof that the service was consumed in China—will invalidate the zero-rating. The tax authorities will claw back all VAT refunded, charge daily late-payment interest at 0.05% of the overdue tax, and impose administrative penalties from 50% up to 500% of the tax involved for false export-refund or tax-evasion conduct. Fraudulent export-refund cases are referred for criminal prosecution; recovery is not subject to a limitation period in cases involving evasion, fraud, or refusal to pay.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is conditionally convertible under a genuine transaction verification regime. Current account items such as cross-border service fees, software licenses, advertising payments, dividends, and employee compensation are generally convertible through licensed banks provided the underlying transaction is supported by contracts, invoices, and customs or tax proof where applicable. Capital account items are materially restricted. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may receive foreign currency equity investment from its overseas shareholder only after completing Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) foreign exchange registration with SAFE and opening a designated capital account at a locally licensed bank. The inbound foreign currency must subsequently be converted to RMB within the registered capital scope, subject to capital verification by a Certified Public Accountant (CPA) and permissible use under China's negative list framework. The converted RMB cannot be used for domestic equity investment, entrusted loans, speculative securities transactions, or non-core real estate purchases without separate regulatory approval. Dividend remittance to the offshore parent is permitted after the annual statutory audit certifies distributable profits and the 10% PRC dividend withholding tax has been settled, subject to any reduced rate available under the applicable double tax treaty. Foreign currency loans, cross-border guarantee arrangements, and equity transfers involving non-residents require additional SAFE registration or filing. In addition, China has removed the historical mandatory export proceeds surrender requirement, but export-related foreign exchange income must be repatriated into China through designated banks within the regulated time limit and may be either retained in foreign currency or settled at market rates. Treasury and liquidity management must therefore be built around SAFE compliance, documentary proof of each remittance, and continuous monitoring of policy updates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency proceeds under the current SAFE framework. Exporters and service providers may keep export receipts in foreign currency accounts at designated banks or convert them into RMB at market rates. However, the proceeds must be repatriated into China and may not be retained offshore indefinitely; trade-related foreign exchange receipts must be collected within the time limit agreed in the contract and reported to SAFE, with goods exporters generally expected to complete the relevant foreign exchange verification promptly after customs declaration. For cross-border service export, the service contract and actual invoice must support the timing of repatriation. Any falsification of export value, delayed repatriation without valid justification, or use of shell intermediary accounts will trigger administrative sanctions,AML scrutiny, and potential revocation of the bank's foreign exchange settlement status.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms are not viable as a primary banking channel for a China-based WFOE. They are not licensed banks or deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot provide a domestic RMB account, a SAFE-registered capital account, or access to CIPS and the domestic clearing system. While an offshore EMI could support a parent company's outward remittance through its correspondent banking partners, the WFOE itself must maintain its core treasury accounts at a PRC-licensed commercial bank to receive FDI, pay taxes, settle FX, and disburse operating expenditures. Attempting to use an unlicensed EMI for domestic RMB collection or payroll could result in payment freezes, judicial blockages, and AML penalties. EMIs may be used only for incidental cross-border overhead payments by the overseas parent outside China and must never act as the settlement rail for Chinese entity cash flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a WFOE to a non-resident foreign shareholder (CIT Law Art. 37); a tax treaty can reduce this to 5% if the beneficial owner holds directly at least 25% of the WFOE's capital for 12 continuous months, or can reduce it to 10% for portfolio shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare and submit a China Local File if any related-party transaction category exceeds: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset or fund transfers; or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions in a single tax year. Master File is mandatory when the multinational group achieved consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5 billion in the preceding fiscal year. Annual related-party transaction schedules must be filed electronically with the national CIT return.

Economic substance scope: Although mainland China does not issue separate Economic Substance Regulations similar to the UAE, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule of Article 47 CIT Law, Article 6 substance-over-form principle, and SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018 require the legal owner and material operator of income-generating activity to be identical. A shell WFOE with no office, no payroll and no China-based decision-making cannot claim treaty benefits; CIGA analysis is performed to locate the actual place where income-producing functions are performed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, a fraudulent tax declaration results in a tax authority order to repay the evaded tax within the set period and a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid amount. Where unpaid tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and is accompanied by concealment of income or false accounting, Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law imposes criminal fines and imprisonment of up to seven years for serious volume.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives who knowingly instructed the concealment are jointly and severally liable for the evaded tax and penalties under Company Law Article 20 for abuse of limited liability; asset freezes and exit bans are issued by SAT and the Immigration Department; the individual is listed in the tax dishonesty blacklist, loses airline and high-speed rail travel privileges, is barred from serving as a director for up to three years, and faces imprisonment for criminal offenses.
Shell Company and General Anti-Avoidance Recharacterization

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Articles 120 to 121 of the Implementing Regulations, the tax authority may recharacterize the business arrangement, disregard the offshore shell structure, reattribute profits to the actual operating entity, impose CIT at 25%, and charge interest at the benchmark rate plus five percentage points; a person may also face a false registration fine of up to RMB 200,000.

Director personal liability: Equitable lifting of the corporate veil makes directors answerable with their personal assets for the reissued corporate tax plus interest; individuals on the serious tax violation list face travel restrictions, bank account asset preservation, disqualification from representing new enterprises, and criminal responsibility under Criminal Law Article 205 for fabricating contracts or invoices if the recharacterization is triggered by forged documentation.
Transfer Pricing Base Erosion, Thin Capitalization and Sham Royalties

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Special Tax Adjustment rules, the tax authority can adjust the interest and royalty to the arm's length amount, disallow the over-deducted expense, impose CIT on the shifted profit, and require payment of unpaid tax plus interest and a penalty of up to five times the underpaid amount; thin capitalization deeming rules under Article 55 may reclassify excess interest as dividends subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors and civil officers who signed the biased intercompany loan and royalty agreements can be personally held liable for the unpaid corporate tax and WHT primary obligations if the court finds intentional disregard; their personal bank accounts may be frozen, international travel banned through exit authentication, and criminal conviction under Article 201 is available if the diversion exceeded the RMB 50,000 threshold; the blacklist entry further blocks future board seats in any Chinese-incorporated entity.
