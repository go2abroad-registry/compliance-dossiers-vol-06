Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a WFOE structured as a pure equity holding SPV, virtual offices, commercial mailboxes, or registered-agent-only addresses are categorically rejected by SAMR for company registration and by the tax authorities (State Taxation Administration) for tax registration. The WFOE must lease or own a physical office space within the registered administrative district, evidenced by a valid lease agreement filed with SAMR and the tax bureau. The address must be a private or commercial property with a valid real estate ownership certificate that can receive postal mail and be the seat of management. Banks also require the physical address to be the actual business premises and often conduct a site visit before opening a corporate bank account. While a serviced office or co-working space with a dedicated desk and office equipment may satisfy the requirement, a pure virtual mailbox with no executive staff will not.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None as a statutory minimum; PRC Company Law abolished the minimum registered capital requirement for ordinary limited liability companies. However, the registered capital stated in the Articles of Association must be a firm commitment of the sole shareholder, must be paid in within the contribution period set forth in the Articles of Association (which may be up to 30 years for non-wholly-funded foreign investment projects, but generally subject to a 5-year period for cash contributions unless extended), and must be subscribed in a lawful currency. The contributed capital must be remitted into a capital verification account or regular foreign-exchange settlement account, documented with the bank's FX receipt, and reported to the local MSAB and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the designated bank. If the shareholder initially chooses to declare capital by a self-certification and contribution schedule, the authorities can impose revocation, rectification, or fines for false declaration under Article 198 of PRC Company Law.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - The WFOE operates from a Class-A or commercial office address exclusively for managerial and treasury functions; there is no civil defense fire safety license, structural load inspection, or workplace-safety approval required for pure equity holding. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; statutory e-filing portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online platform at etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a WFOE, the unified social credit code (USCC) issued at business registration serves as the sole statutory taxpayer identification number. The Administration for Market Regulation automatically shares incorporation data with the STA under the multi-license integration reform, and the company completes VAT and CIT registration through the e-Tax portal using its USCC. For foreign non-resident entities with China-source income but no Chinese permanent establishment, the entity must apply to the local STA branch for a non-resident taxpayer identification number (TIN); the application requires the home-country business registration certificate, tax residence certificate, and a documented contract evidencing China-source income, plus appointment of a domestic withholding agent or domestic taxpayer identification. The STA issues a 15-digit TIN enabling the foreign entity to meet withholding, VAT reverse-charge, and e-invoice issuance obligations.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Chinese tax law does not permit check-the-box entity classification elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The WFOE is mandatorily treated as a Chinese tax-resident enterprise subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income; no pass-through election exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; mandatory VAT registration for limited liability entities at incorporation without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT-registered exporter can zero-rate exported goods if the goods are physically exported from China, declared through the China International Trade Single Window under the exporter's customs registration, sold to an overseas buyer and paid for in a foreign convertible currency that is repatriated into China under the goods-trade foreign exchange monitoring system. For services, zero-rating applies to services supplied to non-resident recipients where the benefits are wholly received and consumed outside China, including qualifying cross-border digital services. A pure equity holding SPV normally has no export turnover; if it later carries trading activity, it must satisfy the same documentation and FX repatriation tests.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration with official electronic release record
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Rail Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Bank Export Proceeds Collection Certificate evidencing inward remittance of foreign exchange
  • Commercial export invoice and underlying foreign sales contract
  • Special VAT Invoice issued by suppliers for exported goods (required for input VAT rebate claims)

The STA operates a compulsory national electronic invoicing and online clearance regime. All VAT invoices are issued in real-time through the e-Tax platform or an STA-approved service provider under the comprehensive electronic invoice model. Each invoice carries a unique encrypted code, is digitally signed with the taxpayer's e-tax encryption key, and is transmitted instantly to the national tax clearing database. Buyer-side input VAT credit is conditioned on successful online matching and validation of the seller-issued invoice. Use of paper anti-fraud stamps is abolished; failure to comply invalidates the invoice, disallows input-VAT deduction, and imposes late-filing penalties.

The STA may audit export zero-rating claims within the statutory five-year tax assessment window, extended to ten years in cases of fraud. If a claimed zero-rating is not fully substantiated by the customs, transport, and foreign-exchange records, the VAT refunds are clawed back and the underlying exports are re-characterized as taxable domestic sales subject to 13% VAT plus late interest at 0.05% per day. Under Article 66 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law, fraudulent export tax refunds are punishable by an administrative penalty of one to five times the refund obtained, revocation of export tax refund filing status, and blacklisting on the tax credit system. Serious fraudulent export refunds exceeding the criminal threshold are prosecuted under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, exposing responsible persons to fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment from five years up to life imprisonment where the amount is especially huge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial currency convertibility. The renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible on current account items, including trade in goods and services, while capital account items remain subject to SAFE licensing, registration, and post-event filing. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) established for pure equity holding may receive registered capital contributions from non-resident shareholders in a foreign currency or RMB, but the associated RMB conversion must be undertaken through a designated domestic bank that has SAFE-approved foreign exchange business qualifications. Surplus funds and undistributed profits can be repatriated as dividends only after annual statutory audit, tax settlement, and submission of a board resolution confirming distributable profits. The WFOE must not commingle capital account and current account funds; each transaction requires a legitimate business background and supporting documentation such as contracts, invoices, customs declarations, or tax certificates. Since the holding SPV itself generates minimal operating cash flow, the bank will scrutinize the source of funds for capital injection and the downstream dividend flow to ensure that FX conversion is not used for speculative balance-sheet arbitrage. Profit remittance is subject to withholding tax, and the domestic bank will require the tax bureau settlement certificate or electronic withholding voucher before effecting the transfer. In practice, proceeds from registered capital can be settled under the foreign exchange capital settlement framework, but disbursement must conform to the registered business scope and negative-list restrictions that prohibit settlement funds from being directed into equity or securities investments unless specific SAFE waivers apply. There is no free market convertibility for capital account purposes; all material FX changes must be reported by the bank through the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Chinese entity engaged in goods export, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into the domestic banking system within 90 days from the date of customs declaration unless an approved deferred-payment period applies. Since the foreign exchange reform, there is no mandatory conversion ratio requiring all proceeds to be sold to the central bank; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a settlement account or convert it into RMB at its own discretion, subject to current account authenticity review. The relevant SAFE form is the trade in goods payment and receipt record; a failure to repatriate within the statutory deadline gives rise to the bank's obligation to file non-compliance and potential amendment of the exporter's trade credit classification.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are effectively barred from serving as primary banking rails for an onshore Chinese WFOE. Under the PBOC licensing framework, only commercial banks and a narrow set of licensed non-bank payment institutions may operate in domestic clearing and settlement; offshore EMIs are not authorized to maintain RMB account classes, process capital account registrations, or provide the SAFE reconciliation reports expected by regulators. An offshore EMI might be used only as an ancillary provider for travelers or cross-border e-commerce payouts, never for statutory capital injection, dividend repatriation, or trade settlement. Therefore the practical viability rating is low and the compliance maximum is to keep all treasury activity inside licensed domestic Tier-1 institutions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The non-resident shareholder must effect an SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the WFOE's designated SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at a Tier-1 commercial bank. The remittance narrative must specify the purpose as capital contribution or investment in registered capital, using the standard inward remittance purpose declaration code relevant to foreign direct investment in China. The domestic receiving bank will verify that the amount matches the registered capital with the Market Regulation Administration and will file the transaction with SAFE. After receipt and conversion into RMB, a certified public accountant issues a capital verification report, which is required for further commercial-registration filings and for subsequent remittance of dividends. All settlement disbursements must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, and board resolutions and must not violate the negative-list restrictions on capital account settlement funds.For the holding SPV and its downstream operating subsidiaries, outbound payments should be cleared through licensed domestic bank channels using SWIFT MT103 for direct cash transfers, commercial letters of credit (L/C) for documentary trade settlements, and documentary collections where the seller ships under a bill of lading and the bank handles documents through an advised collection. As the SPV is primarily passive and not itself a goods trader, the most relevant outbound flows are dividends and loan repayments to the foreign parent, which require audited profit calculations, tax withholding certificates, board minutes, and SAFE current-account payment declarations. Any trade-related payment executed by an underlying subsidiary must maintain matching customs declarations and invoice evidence to preserve the bank's allowed trade credit status.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD and RMB/HKD forward contracts entered into for real existing current-account or capital-account exposures with a SAFE-licensed bankFX swaps between spot and forward contracts for temporary RMB liquidity conversion when the WFOE has verified underlying foreign-currency receipts or obligationsCross-currency interest rate swaps in RMB and USD where the notional amount is supported by the WFOE's actual borrowings or intercompany loans and where the bank confirms SAFE derivative qualificationOffshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts concluded only by the foreign parent or an offshore treasury center in Hong Kong or another acceptable jurisdiction, not by the onshore WFOE, because onshore enterprises are not permitted speculative NDF transactions without real physical settlement eligibility

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are effectively barred from serving as primary banking rails for an onshore Chinese WFOE. Under the PBOC licensing framework, only commercial banks and a narrow set of licensed non-bank payment institutions may operate in domestic clearing and settlement; offshore EMIs are not authorized to maintain RMB account classes, process capital account registrations, or provide the SAFE reconciliation reports expected by regulators. An offshore EMI might be used only as an ancillary provider for travelers or cross-border e-commerce payouts, never for statutory capital injection, dividend repatriation, or trade settlement. Therefore the practical viability rating is low and the compliance maximum is to keep all treasury activity inside licensed domestic Tier-1 institutions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 20,000,000 for annual related-party tangible-asset transactions; RMB 10,000,000 for annual related-party intangible-asset transfers; RMB 10,000,000 for annual related-party financial-asset transfers; and other thresholds under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42. Master File preparation may also apply where group-wide related-party turnover exceeds RMB 1,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply British-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the substance principle is embedded in the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and in the Chinese beneficial ownership doctrine. A pure equity holding SPV must demonstrate actual management decision-making, board meetings in China, real employees, physical or shared office premises, independently controlled bank accounts, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China. Absent such substance, the SPV may be recharacterized as a conduit, treaty benefits will be denied, underpaying income will be re-assessed at 25% CIT, and further deemed remittances may be subject to 10% WHT, plus surcharges, penalties, and blacklisting.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63: recovery of the evaded tax, daily late-payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Criminal Law Article 201: where evasion exceeds RMB 100,000 and 10% of the tax due, the responsible person can receive imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced where the director signed a false return and controlled the concealment; the director then bears joint and several civil liability for the State's tax loss. The director may be placed on the Serious Tax Dishonesty Blacklist, triggering a travel ban, denial of exit at the border, prohibition on serving as a legal representative elsewhere, and exposure to criminal imprisonment for a coordinated fraud.
Abusive DTA and conduit structures lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the SAT's anti-avoidance rules: the tax authority may recharacterize the conduit, deny treaty benefits, reassess 25% CIT on the underlying profits, impose 10% WHT on deemed distributions, and charge interest for late payment. If the concealment is fraudulent, Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law may additionally impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall, and GAAR reassessment can extend 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign beneficial ownership declarations while knowing the company is a conduit can be personally liable for the additionally assessed tax, interest, and penalties. The courts may disregard the corporate entity and attach personal assets of the directors. A permanent exit ban and blacklisting apply to directors of companies found to be instruments of tax fraud, and the forging or fraudulent use of treaty-related certificates could lead to criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years under criminal fraud provisions.
Failure to withhold non-resident income tax on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 69 requires the Chinese resident payer to withhold and remit the tax; failure to do so triggers a fine of 50% to 300% of the un-withheld amount and the withholding agent remains liable for any uncollected tax. Late daily surcharge of 0.05% accrues from the statutory due date, and false or omitted declarations can cross criminal fraud thresholds under Article 201 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize outbound dividend, interest, or royalty remittances without proper withholding expose the company to tax losses for which they can be held civilly liable. Once the company is blacklisted under the joint enforcement memorandum, the responsible director becomes subject to a travel ban and exit control, cannot open new enterprises, and may face personal confiscation of assets. Where the absence of withholding is part of a larger scheme to strip profits and evade tax, the director is also exposed to personal criminal prosecution and imprisonment.
