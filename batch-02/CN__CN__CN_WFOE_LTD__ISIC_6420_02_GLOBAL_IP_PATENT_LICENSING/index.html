Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is impermissible as the registered address of a WFOE engaged in global IP licensing. The Company Law and SAMR market-subject registration require a registered address that is a real, existing, and lawfully leased or owned premise within the relevant municipal area, capable of receiving summons, tax notices and regulatory correspondence, with complete title or lease documents and a place-of-business certificate. Tax authorities will reject a purely virtual address because economic substance rules for IP holding companies demand that core business decisions, accounting books, management functions and personnel be situated in China. Banks will also refuse to open a settlement account at a virtual address, as the Anti-Money Laundering provisions require face-to-face due diligence and verification of physical business premises through on-site visits when justified by risk. For an IP sub-licensing holdco, a co-working or serviced office with a dedicated private room and a genuine lease is the minimum acceptable substance.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital and no blocked escrow account are required for a WFOE engaged solely in intellectual-property patent and trademark license holding under ISIC 6420. The Articles of Association must state a registered capital, and each shareholder must contribute the subscribed amount within five years from establishment under Article 47 of the Company Law. A foreign investor may contribute in foreign currency through the WFOE's foreign-exchange capital account, subject to SAFE and designated-bank registration for currency conversion and capital controls.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing and registration through the National Electronic Taxation Administration Platform (e-tax system) and the China International Trade Single Window for customs-related tax declarations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: After incorporation of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation, the one-stop integration procedure automatically issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) which functions as the enterprise's national Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for direct tax, VAT, and customs duties. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration particulars with the competent local State Taxation Administration office through the electronic tax system, supplying the USCC, registered address, legal representative identity, bank account details, business scope, and expected tax categories. Step 3: VAT registration is automatic under the USCC if the entity engages in taxable sales; the entity may be classified as a small-scale taxpayer until its annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, after which mandatory general taxpayer registration applies. Step 4: Where the foreign non-resident licensor of IP to the Chinese WFOE does not maintain a Chinese establishment, it is not required to obtain a Chinese TIN for indirect tax purposes; instead, the WFOE, as the withholding agent, must perform VAT and withholding income tax obligations at source using the reverse-charge mechanism. Step 5: For the WFOE's own export activities, the USCC and the foreign exchange receipt record are registered with customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); no separate TIN is necessary beyond the USCC.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for small-scale taxpayer status and compulsory general VAT registration; below this threshold, voluntary general registration is available, but foreign suppliers without a Chinese establishment are subject to withholding VAT regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China are zero-rated upon physical crossing of the Chinese border, provided the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, receives foreign currency consideration, and submits a customs export declaration with an approved customs record. Qualifying offshore IP, patent, and franchise licensing services may also be zero-rated or exempt if the services are provided entirely to a non-resident enterprise, are consumed wholly outside China, do not generate taxable consumption in mainland China, and are not connected with a permanent establishment in China; the main statutory basis is the cross-border taxable services provisions under Caishui [2016] No. 36, with supplementary guidance on exempt/zero-rated international transport and offshore service transactions.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight), Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical exportation of goods to the foreign recipient
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange inward remittance voucher demonstrating receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Export commodity inspection certificate if applicable under China's inbound/outbound commodity inspection catalogue
  • Service transaction contract, IP licence agreement or royalty schedule for cross-border digital and intellectual property services, showing the foreign recipient and offshore consumption
  • Bank requisition or foreign exchange payment record evidencing the full repatriation of export proceeds within statutory deadlines

China operates a mandatory VAT invoice regime under the Golden Tax IV system. All full-digitized electronic invoices (fapiao) must be generated through the State Taxation Administration's invoice management platform; real-time fiscal clearance applies to suspicious or high-risk categories, requiring transmission of invoice data to the tax authorities at the moment of issuance. The platform enforces anti-fraud digital stamping, tax control encryption devices or secure electronic signature keys, and centralized reconciliation of input/output VAT through the VAT invoice service platform. Cross-border licensing transactions must be documented through the specified 'service trade or foreign exchange payment' forms and be declared for withholding VAT within the time limit.

If an export zero-rating claim fails substantive verification, the tax authorities will claw back all zero-rated output VAT not recognised, require payment of the applicable VAT plus late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day from the due date, and impose a civil penalty ranging from 50% to five times the tax underpaid for fraudulent or intentional misdecoaration. False customs declarations may trigger customs penalties, revocation of export tax rebate qualification, and retrospective reassessments extending up to ten years in fraud or tax evasion cases, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export-tax refunds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertibility differentiated between current account and capital account. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, wages, and intellectual property royalties, are generally freely convertible provided the underlying transaction is genuine and verified by a designated foreign exchange bank. A WFOE receiving global IP patent licensing royalties can convert foreign currency into RMB through its domestic settlement account without SAFE pre-approval for current account items, as long as the license agreement, invoices, tax payment certificates, and payment instructions are submitted to the bank. Capital account items, however, remain tightly controlled. FDI equity injections require SAFE registration either directly at a bank or through the local SAFE branch before remittance, and the resulting RMB settlement funds are subject to a 'negative list' restricting their use in equity investments, entrusted loans, and securities speculation. Profit remittances abroad are permitted but conditioned on audited financial statements, prior year tax clearance, and a certificate of foreign exchange payment for dividends. Withholding tax and VAT on cross-border royalties must be filed and settled before remittance. There is no mandatory export surrender requirement for current account receipts, but all cross-border receipts must be repatriated to China through a designated bank, and payments must be reported in the SAFE monitoring system. Consequently, treasury planning must emphasize documentary readiness and bank relationship management rather than reliance on unrestricted capital mobility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE framework effective after the 2012 reform, China no longer imposes mandatory foreign-exchange surrender ratios or a statutory conversion deadline for export proceeds. Exporters and licensing entities may retain 100% of their foreign-currency receipts in an onshore foreign exchange account or voluntarily convert into RMB at market rates through a designated bank. However, all current-account receipts, including IP royalty and license fees, must be repatriated into China through designated banks and reported in the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system within the time frame established in the underlying collection schedule; and any settlement into RMB requires the presenting bank to complete authenticity verification using contracts, invoices, customs declarations (for goods) or tax payment vouchers (for services/royalties).

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for domestic legal entity servicing. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions ('EMIs') and digital-only banks are not licensed banks or designated foreign exchange banks under PBOC and SAFE regulations, and they cannot open RMB/foreign currency settlement accounts, perform current-account collection, or process SAFE registrations for a China-based WFOE. Domestic technology-enabled banks such as MYbank and WeBank can provide basic deposit accounts, but they do not replace the statutory role of a designated bank in cross-border FX authenticity review and regulatory reporting. Therefore, all cross-border treasury and royalty flows must be routed through a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with SAFE-authorized direct foreign exchange operation capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For WFOE equity capital, the foreign investor first completes SAFE FDI registration (bank-direct or local SAFE branch) after the business license is issued. The remittance is then wired via SWIFT MT103 from the investor's corporate account to the WFOE's foreign currency capital account at the domestic Tier-1 bank, using the approved FDI remittance purpose code. The bank will debit a foreign exchange settlement monitoring system entry and require the WFOE to submit the FDI business registration certificate, board resolution, joint venture/incorporation documents, and a capital verification report from a PRC certified public accountant before the capital can be settled into RMB and used for permitted operating purposes. All receipts must be matched to the SAFE capital account information system within the prescribed reporting timeliness, and any future increase in registered capital needs a separate SAFE modification registration.Royalty payments by the WFOE to offshore licensors ride on the current account and are processed through SWIFT MT103 messages after submission of the licence agreement, tax payment certificates (VAT and withholding tax), non-trade and pro forma invoices, and a bank-issued payment application with a valid SAFE purpose code for royalties and licence fees. For physical-goods-backed distribution, the preferred rails are an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) against a full set of clean-on-board bills of lading and inspection certificates, or Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) where the WFOE has strong receivables history and lower risk appetite. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for domestic legal entity servicing. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions ('EMIs') and digital-only banks are not licensed banks or designated foreign exchange banks under PBOC and SAFE regulations, and they cannot open RMB/foreign currency settlement accounts, perform current-account collection, or process SAFE registrations for a China-based WFOE. Domestic technology-enabled banks such as MYbank and WeBank can provide basic deposit accounts, but they do not replace the statutory role of a designated bank in cross-border FX authenticity review and regulatory reporting. Therefore, all cross-border treasury and royalty flows must be routed through a licensed Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with SAFE-authorized direct foreign exchange operation capability.
