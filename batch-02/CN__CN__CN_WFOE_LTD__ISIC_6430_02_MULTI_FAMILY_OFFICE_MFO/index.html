Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox-only registered address is generally impermissible for a WFOE whose business scope covers ISIC 6430 discretionary portfolio advisory and multi-family office services. Under SAMR registration rules, the registered address (住所) must be a fixed, concrete office location confirmed as lawfully usable and must match the actual place of business operations. Local tax authorities and banks treat a purely virtual mailbox as a high-risk indicator of shell or diversion activities. For a licensed asset-management-type or family-office-type business, the local financial or market regulator may require an actual office with adequate physical records custody, segregated client-asset custody arrangements, and dual-control access. A virtual address may only temporarily support a pre-license mailing or administrative contact, but it will not satisfy commercial substance requirements. Therefore, a physical office lease with a term of at least one to two years, a local business license or commercial lease registration, and a visible place of management in the same prefecture are mandatory.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: State Taxation Administration National Unified Standard Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax), accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

The Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is incorporated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) one-stop registration window. SAMR automatically forwards the company's establishment data to the STA under the multi-certificate integration reform. The STA then issues the 18-character Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally serves as the Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes; no separate paper tax registration certificate is issued. The legal representative or authorized financial officer must access the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau through real-name authentication and complete the pre-starting tax registration information report within 30 days after issuance of the business license. The filing must include corporate bank account details, accounting records and bookkeeping arrangements, shareholder information, and legal representative identity. The E-Tax system automatically constructs the VAT taxpayer profile, and the taxpayer must determine whether it is a small-scale VAT taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China, the payer or withholding agent completes the Non-Resident Enterprise tax registration through the E-Tax platform, obtains a non-resident taxpayer identification code, and performs withholding registration under the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law and the Tax Collection Administration Law.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The WFOE is a Chinese tax resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on its worldwide income at the standard 25% rate; no pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

None for entity classification purposes; the PRC does not operate a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch entity election. The only relevant election is the VAT general taxpayer registration election: an enterprise with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may elect general VAT taxpayer status, while an enterprise exceeding that threshold must register as a general VAT taxpayer. The general VAT taxpayer status is required to claim input VAT credits and cross-border zero-rating refunds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales (expected to be predominantly taxable services for a multi-family office advisory business); taxpayers below this threshold are small-scale VAT taxpayers with simplified taxation, while those above must register as general VAT taxpayers. Basic VAT registration is triggered by any taxable business activity with no de minimis turnover threshold for general tax liability.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods may claim the 0% VAT rate only when the goods are physically declared to and leave PRC customs territory, the export sale is genuine, title and risk have passed to the overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated through authorized banking channels. For cross-border services, including discretionary portfolio advisory services supplied by a multi-family office to non-resident private clients, the services can qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption only if the services are consumed wholly outside China, the client is a non-resident, the benefit and economic enjoyment occur outside China, the services are not resupplied into China, and the WFOE maintains contemporaneous evidence of the foreign client's identity, the cross-border contract, service delivery, invoices, and foreign exchange receipts. Advisory services that are directly connected to assets, investments, or taxable activities within China are ineligible for zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration) evidencing the physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill as proof of cross-border transport of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt confirmation evidencing export proceeds repatriation
  • Cross-border service agreement or engagement letter with the non-resident multi-family office client
  • Detailed VAT invoice issued for the cross-border service
  • Overseas client tax residency certificate or legal existence evidence
  • Service completion and delivery documentation evidencing the provision of the advisory services outside China

China operates the National Unified Standard Electronic Tax Bureau invoice platform and the VAT Invoice Management System. Fully digitalized electronic invoices (digital e-invoices) are the mandatory standard for newly registered taxpayers and are being rolled out nationwide. Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA through the electronic invoice service platform, and each invoice carries a unique anti-fraud encryption code, QR code, and digital tax-control stamp. VAT special invoices are reconciled against the STA invoice verification system before downstream customers may claim input VAT credits or before exporters may apply for zero-rating refunds. This is a centralized real-time invoice clearance and anti-fraud digital stamping regime rather than a distributed physical fiscal cash-register sign-off system.

The STA may revoke zero-rating or exemption, claw back any input VAT credit or refund, and impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on underpaid VAT where export substantiation is missing or defective. A claim supported by false documentation is treated as fraudulent export tax refund or tax evasion under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, exposing the WFOE to recapture of the full tax benefit, a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved, and criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund, which can lead to imprisonment up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the 180-day deadline without a recognized SAFE-approval extension may also trigger State Administration of Foreign Exchange penalties, loss of customs administrative facilitations, and automatic reversal of the zero-rating treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a clear bifurcation between current-account and capital-account convertibility. RMB is fully convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, meaning WFOE invoice settlement, service fees, royalty and profit remittances can clear through designated banks after routine documentary review. Capital-account items remain subject to strict prior approval/registration by SAFE: FDI contributions, external loans, equity transfers and repatriation of liquidation proceeds all require a specific SAFE registration or filing, a dedicated onshore foreign-exchange capital account and an underlying transaction that passes a genuine-entity and reasonableness review. Profit remittances are allowed but are not an automatic right; they must be substantiated by an annual audit report, tax clearance certificate, board resolution and confirmation that the registered paid-in capital has been fully verified. The bank will also apply anti-round-tripping checks to ensure that the funds are not in substance moving Chinese domestic wealth offshore. In practice, convertibility is available but accompanied by heavy documentary burden, purpose-code verification, transaction-by-transaction monitoring, and strict post-verification checks by SAFE. Any attempt to use internal netting or sweeping without SAFE approval would be treated as illegal cross-border settlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; a WFOE may retain 100% of goods-export FX receipts in a domestic foreign-exchange current-account or choose to settle into RMB through a designated bank. All export proceeds must be repatriated onshore within the deadline set by SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring system (normally 90 days from the date of customs declaration; earlier or later collection must be justified). The retained funds may be used for current-account payments or settled at spot/forward rates, but any capital-account use remains restricted.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) with delegated foreign-exchange administration to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital electronic-money institutions and EMI-style neobanks are not permitted to open deposit accounts, maintain FX accounts or provide payment services to a mainland-incorporated WFOE without a PBoC payment institution license. The WFOE must bank with a SAFE-designated onshore commercial bank for all capital-account flows; any fintech EMI can only operate as a white-label technology vendor reporting to a licensed domestic bank, and cannot serve as an independent clearing or settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The parent must first complete SAFE's Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration with the local SAFE branch or a designated bank, obtain a Foreign-Exchange Registration Code, and open a dedicated FDI capital account in a licensed domestic designated bank. Incoming injection wires must be sent via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary bank with China's SWIFT/CNAPS details, using SAFE's capital-account purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise equity contribution (typically 621011 for FDI settlement arising from equity investment, subject to bank-specific mapping). The remittance advice must identify the overseas payee name, the WFOE's registration code and the underlying contract. After arrival, the bank will verify the ownership/control line, screen the funds for sanction/PEP matches, and record the debit in the Foreign Exchange Capital Project Settlement and Remittance system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 400 million for related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible asset transfers and related-party financial transactions; RMB 40 million for other related-party services. Master File contemporaneous documentation is required where the multinational group's annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. These thresholds are expressed in renminbi and must be monitored using gross principal amounts for loans and gross contractual amounts for services.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime of the Gulf Cooperation Council type, but applies a substantive economic-substance doctrine under the Enterprise Income Tax Law general anti-abuse rule (Article 47), SAT Bulletin 2017 No. 6, and the beneficial-ownership tests in SAT Bulletin 2015 No. 60. A multi-family office claiming treaty benefits or intercompany fee deductions must demonstrate core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China: real offices, qualified investment staff, client-facing portfolio risk and performance committees, board decision-making records, payroll, local licenses, and custody/trade execution functions. A booking entity with no CIGA is exposed to recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, deemed withholding, and penalty-driven transfer pricing adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Corporate Income Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes knowingly filing a false return or failing to file where taxes are evaded: the threshold is generally triggered when the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable; penalties include imprisonment up to 3 years for relatively large evasion and 3 to 7 years for extraordinarily large evasion, together with a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors, supervisors, and senior managers who direct or participate in the false filing are subject to criminal prosecution as directly responsible persons. The Chinese tax authority may freeze personal bank accounts, seize properties where the corporate form is abused, and place the legal representative on an exit-control blacklist under the Tax Collection and Administration Law before the case is adjudicated. If the court pierces the corporate veil for fraudulent tax avoidance, directors and de facto controlling shareholders bear joint and several restitution liability for the tax, interest, and penalties out of personal assets.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and SAT Bulletin 2017 No. 6, disregards arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose, recharacterizes the income, and denies treaty relief under the beneficial-ownership rules. The adjusted tax is collectible with interest, generally at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; the associated related-party return penalties under Article 63 can add 50% to 500% of the re-determined tax. Treaty denial means the 10% statutory withholding rate applies on the total gross payment instead of the reduced treaty rate.

Director personal liability: Where shareholders and directors abuse the corporate legal personality to avoid withholding tax, the PRC Company Law doctrine of veil-piercing makes controlling shareholders and directors joint and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax to the extent the corporate form was used to evade statutory obligations. Directors can be made parties to tax enforcement proceedings, their personal assets can be preserved, and their exit from China can be blocked. If the scheme also produces an intentionally false tax return, Criminal Law Article 201 liability extends to the individual director who originated and implemented the paper structure.
Unlicensed Financial Activity / Money Laundering and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed asset-management or wealth-management activity is an illegal business operation under Criminal Law Article 225, punishable with confiscation of illegal gains, fines, and imprisonment of up to 5 years for serious conduct and more than 5 years in especially serious cases. Conduct that knowingly channels proceeds of predicate crime through the WFOE accounts constitutes money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191, carrying imprisonment of 5 to 10 years plus fines in severe cases. The anti-money laundering regulator may simultaneously impose administrative fines, suspension of activities, removal of the license, and blacklisting of the institution and its responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors, compliance officers, and de facto owners cannot escape criminal responsibility by relying on a nominee board; any director who executes or approves the payment-rail scheme can be named as a directly responsible person under Criminal Law Articles 191 and 225. Personal sanctions include asset seizure and criminal confiscation, monetary fines, imprisonment, civil restitution to defrauded family clients, travel restrictions, and prohibition from serving as a director of any licensed Chinese financial institution. The corporate veil is pierced where the managing director operates the WFOE as a mere instrumentality for the foreign principal.
