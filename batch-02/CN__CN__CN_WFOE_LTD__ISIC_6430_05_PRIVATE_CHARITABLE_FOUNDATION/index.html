Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filings are processed through the National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax / Electronic Tax Bureau) platform at the STA branch covering the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the foreign-invested WFOE under the Foreign Investment Law of the PRC and the PRC Company Law; if licensed as a charitable endowment entity, first obtain the statutory charitable foundation license from the competent civil affairs authority. Step 2 - Register with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receive the Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is automatically the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all national taxes, including VAT. Step 3 - Complete integrated tax registration via the STA e-Tax platform or through SAMR/STA data sharing by filing legal representative details, financial institution account information, accounting software/policy, and tax practitioner contact. No separate VAT certificate or TIN is issued because the USCC serves both enterprise income tax and VAT identification. For a foreign non-resident entity without a PRC establishment, tax obligations are generally satisfied by withholding at source; if the non-resident itself creates a taxable presence through a fixed place, service establishment, or digital supply platform in China, it must complete tax registration with the competent STA office and obtain an STA-assigned taxpayer identification number through the e-Tax system. Step 4 - If recognized as a charitable/non-profit foundation, apply for the state tax-exemption qualification with the fiscal and tax authorities and file the charitable status certificate with the STA. Step 5 - Determine VAT taxpayer status: below RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover the WFOE is a small-scale VAT taxpayer; upon exceeding the threshold it must be classified as a general VAT taxpayer, which is also required to export goods under zero-rating and claim input VAT refunds.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; the PRC applies entity-level taxation to WFOEs. VAT status elections are administrative: a small-scale VAT taxpayer may voluntarily apply for general VAT taxpayer status below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, and mandatory general status applies above it. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales (turnover) is the mandatory transition threshold for general VAT taxpayer status; all licensed enterprises are automatically registered for VAT from incorporation with no minimum monetary threshold. Overseas remote sellers providing taxable digital services or goods to PRC consumers may have VAT registration or collection obligations when their economic nexus meets the conditions under the E-Commerce Law and cross-border VAT rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% export VAT applies when the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from China and cleared by Customs, title and risk are transferred to an overseas purchaser, the export sales price is supported by contract and invoices, and the proceeds are paid in foreign currency and repatriated to China within the foreign-exchange collection period. For cross-border services, including qualifying offshore endowment or fund management services, zero-rating is available only when the service recipient is located outside China, the service is performed wholly outside China, the contract and invoice are properly executed, and foreign-currency remittance proves the offshore commercial nature. Domestic charitable grant distribution or domestic fund administration does not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for the exported goods, with commodity code, quantity, value, destination and exporter details matching the VAT refund claim
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, air waybill or other internationally recognized transport document proving physical export from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank-generated proof of foreign-currency repatriation from the overseas buyer into the exporter's PRC bank account
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice signed with the overseas customer
  • Customs inspection/quarantine certificate or product compliance certificate where the exported goods are subject to statutory inspection
  • Special VAT invoice or export invoice data transmitted to the STA electronic tax control and export refund platform
  • Any additional packing list, insurance certificate, or freight evidence required by China Customs or STA to verify the export transaction

China mandates VAT invoice issuance exclusively through STA-controlled tax control and e-invoice systems. The fully digitalized e-invoice reform requires issuance, transmission, acceptance, and red-letter reversal through the National Electronic Tax Administration e-invoice service platform, with real-time reporting to the STA and a unique anti-fraud digital stamp or QR code on each invoice. Large taxpayers integrate their ERP systems with the e-invoice platform using certified APIs or third-party e-invoice service providers; invoice allocation, data integrity, and reconciliation are supervised under Golden Tax Phase IV, and any mismatch between invoice data and VAT returns can suspend invoice quotas or trigger penalties.

Export VAT refunds are audited through cross-matching of Customs export declarations, e-invoice data, logistics records, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export, without valid foreign-currency receipt, after the 180-day repatriation deadline, or with forged documents, the refund is clawed back with interest, the export transaction is recharacterized as taxable supply, and penalties are assessed under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Tax Collection at 50% to 5 times the defrauded refund amount. Criminal exposure for fraudulent export tax refund under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law includes imprisonment up to life, fines, and confiscation of property; responsible officers and the WFOE may also face collection enforcement, loss of export refund eligibility, and cancellation of charitable tax-exempt status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with limited capital account convertibility. The RMB is freely convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, and interest, provided that genuine underlying business documentation is presented. Capital account transactions require prior approval or registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). A wholly foreign-owned enterprise must complete foreign investment registration under the FDI system; each inbound capital injection is reported via the SAFE capital registration platform and reconciled at the domestic bank through a Foreign Exchange Capital Account. Funds credited as registered paid-in capital may be converted into RMB upon submission of a capital verification report prepared by a licensed PRC accounting firm, and the RMB proceeds must comply with the negative list and business scope. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is classified as a current-account item after corporate income tax settlement, but the remittance bank will require audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a shareholders resolution approving the dividend. For a private charitable foundation, cross-border donations and grants are subject to the Overseas Donations Regulation and the PRC Charity Law, and must flow through a qualified domestic charitable trust or charitable institution. There is no free repatriation of the original endowment principal; liquidation proceeds are strictly controlled and need SAFE and civil affairs approval. The entire FX architecture is therefore transaction-based, product-specific, and highly fact-driven, with no umbrella convertibility entitlement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 reform, no mandatory conversion ratio applies to export foreign currency receipts. Exporters must repatriate the full amount of proceeds into China within 180 days after the date of receipt, unless an extension is approved by SAFE. The exporter may retain up to 100% of the foreign currency in an export settlement account with a licensed bank, or sell the proceeds to a designated bank as RMB. Each receipt must be matched to the customs declaration and the trade contract in the Cross-Border Receipt and Payment Verification System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) in conjunction with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital fintech EMI providers, such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer, do not hold a mainland China banking licence and are legally barred from providing domestic settlement, clearing, or deposit services to PRC-incorporated entities. A WFOE must maintain its primary treasury and clearing infrastructure with a SAFE-licensed domestic commercial bank. Foreign EMIs can only provide ancillary offshore collection accounts for inbound donations before funds are wired into the WFOE's onshore capital account; they cannot serve as the primary clearing rail or hold RMB balances for the entity. Therefore, the viability of fintech EMIs is rated Low and their use is restricted to off-China currency pools.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection for the WFOE must flow from the direct offshore shareholder via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated domestic bank. The remittance instruction must carry SAFE purpose code 622 - Foreign Investment Capital, plus the unique FDI registration code. On receipt, the bank updates the SAFE system and issues a receipt voucher. The entity then appoints a PRC licensed accounting firm to produce a capital verification report, which is filed with the Administration of Market Regulation and the bank before funds may be converted to RMB. Conversion is executed under the bank's converted settlement account, and every utilisation of the converted RMB must be checked against the negative list, under the supervision of the People's Bank of China.For trade disbursements, the WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 as the standard customer credit transfer for service fees and licensing royalties, complemented by commercially issued letters of credit (MT700) and documentary collections (MT400/MT420) for physical goods. All outbound grant distributions to foreign charitable beneficiaries must be prefaced by an application for cross-border payment under the service/trade item, and the bank will require the relevant contract, invoice, approval certificate, and grant resolution. A WFOE must maintain its primary treasury and clearing infrastructure with a SAFE-licensed domestic commercial bank. Foreign EMIs can only provide ancillary offshore collection accounts for inbound donations before funds are wired into the WFOE's onshore capital account; they cannot serve as the primary clearing rail or hold RMB balances for the entity. Therefore, the viability of fintech EMIs is rated Low and their use is restricted to off-China currency pools.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 200 million for tangible property transactions, CNY 100 million for intangible property transfers and financial transactions, CNY 40 million for other related-party transactions; a master file is required when combined annual controlled transactions exceed CNY 1 billion (or consolidated group revenue exceeds CNY 5.5 billion for country-by-country reporting).

Economic substance scope: China has no formal ESR regime, but the PRC GAAR (CIT Art. 47), the anti-beneficial-ownership rules in Notice [2018] No. 9, and the statutory substance-over-form principle require every cross-border arrangement to demonstrate genuine Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in the PRC. For a charitable foundation WFOE, economic substance is evidenced by regular board meetings, actual grant-making processes, charitable program staffing, local bank accounts, and public-benefit activity; a shell entity with passive foreign assets and no PRC operational substance will be ignored for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax on Cross-Border Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Arts. 37-39 and Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 69 allow the tax authority to collect the unpaid WHT directly from the WFOE as withholding agent and impose a fine of 50% to 300% of the unremitted amount; late payment triggers a daily 0.05% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who order the payment ignore signed compliance obligations and are treated as directly accountable persons: they face administrative penalties, a personal tax blacklisting with travel bans, potential personal restitution of the uncollected tax, and criminal prosecution if the deliberate concealment exceeds RMB 1 million, leading to imprisonment of up to 7 years under Art. 201.
Sham Charitable Foundation / Shell Non-Profit Structure

Statutory sanction: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Art. 47) empowers the tax authority to disregard the foundation's legal form, recharacterize all earnings as fully taxable profits at 25%, and impose a fine of 50%-500% under TCAL Art. 63; the Ministry of Civil Affairs may revoke the foundation's registration under Charity Law and issue a RMB 20,000-200,000 fine for operating without genuine charitable purpose.

Director personal liability: Courts pierce the corporate veil because the foundation form is used as a fraud device: directors are jointly liable for misapplied endowment assets, forced to repatriate diverted charitable funds, placed on the tax and civil-society blacklist with travel and conscription bans, and in cases of major charitable asset fraud are subject to up to 10 years in prison under Criminal Law articles 224 and 266.
