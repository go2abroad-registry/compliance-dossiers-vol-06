Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

Under the PRC Company Law as revised in 2023, no statutory minimum registered capital is prescribed for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, and formation registration may be completed on a subscribed capital basis. However, because the target scope includes commercial factoring, the Local Financial Regulatory Bureau generally requires paid-in registered capital of at least RMB 50,000,000, or such lower threshold as may apply under a pilot free trade zone, together with continuous net assets above that amount, before the commercial factoring filing is effective. No escrow blocking account is required by the registration authority; all paid-in funds must be deposited into a dedicated bank capital contribution account, evidenced by a capital verification report issued by a licensed PRC accounting firm, and must not be withdrawn outside lawful business use.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification and civil defense clearance for ordinary commercial office occupancy is required from the district fire and rescue authority, including a fire inspection certificate, emergency evacuation plan, fire extinguisher inspection and fire alarm system verification. Structural load inspection is applicable only if the office premises will hold heavy equipment or file storage systems above the building's design load; a weight certification from a licensed structural engineer must be filed. Workplace safety approval from the local emergency management bureau is required for the occupational safety management system, electrical safety inspection and employee emergency training documentation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus, with electronic filing and administration via the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-taxation platform). Customs matters are administered by the General Administration of Customs through the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) formed as a Chinese limited liability company, the statutory taxpayer identification number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at incorporation. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau using its USCC, authenticate the legal representative through real-name verification, designate the tax jurisdiction, provide banking and accounting system details, and select its VAT taxpayer identity. STA then finalizes the enterprise income tax registration as a Chinese resident enterprise and the VAT registration as either a general taxpayer or a small-scale taxpayer where applicable. The WFOE may then apply for electronic invoice quota and, where needed, the general taxpayer special VAT invoice issuance entitlement. Foreign non-resident shareholders do not receive a separate Chinese VAT registration; their Chinese-source income is handled through withholding filing with the tax authorities and evidenced by a Tax Payment Certificate issued by STA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification: China does not provide check-the-box elections, hybrid mismatch elections, or pass-through treatment for a WFOE; it is automatically taxable as a resident enterprise on its worldwide income. A separate VAT status election is available: if annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5 million, the entity may elect small-scale taxpayer status at a simplified rate, but election is lost if the threshold is exceeded or if it issues special VAT invoices.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no economic turnover threshold for VAT registration; any entity engaged in taxable activities must register with STA within 30 days of SAMR license issuance, regardless of revenue. The RMB 5 million annual taxable sales threshold determines the mandatory classification as a general VAT taxpayer, with the small-scale taxpayer election available only below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the 0% VAT rate applies only where the goods are actually physically exported, the export transaction is reported in a valid Export Goods Customs Declaration, the sale is made to an overseas purchaser, and the export proceeds are repatriated into China through the banking system. For services, zero-rating is available only for those cross-border taxable services expressly listed in the Caishui [2016] No. 36 catalogue, such as R&D, design, software, and certain digital/information services, where the service recipient is overseas and the service is consumed overseas. Factoring and accounts-receivable trade finance services are not generally included in the zero-rated services catalogue; they are domestic VAT-taxable financial services at 6% unless a specific cross-border financial service exemption applies with full documentation proving offshore supply and offshore consumption.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration issued by the General Administration of Customs, containing the export customs record number and evidencing actual export
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or equivalent international transport document evidencing physical movement of goods out of China
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract, and packing list
  • Bank foreign exchange collection record or bank-issued collecting advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds, reconciled in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System
  • Where applicable, the original special VAT invoice used to support input VAT refunds on exported goods

Mandatory real-time fiscal invoice clearance is implemented through the Golden Tax Phase IV architecture and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices are issued through STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform, transmitted to STA in real time, and authenticated with a unique invoice code and digital signature. Taxpayers with large issuance volumes may integrate via STA's official application programming interfaces for centralized issuance. There is no separate analogue fiscal stamping; invoice data clearance is automated at issuance, and recipients must verify and accept digital invoices in the STA system before claiming input VAT credits. The use of legacy paper special VAT invoices is restricted by quota and tax-control U-key requirements.

STA and China Customs may jointly audit export declarations, physical logistics, VAT invoice chains, and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is disallowed because export or repatriation documentation is fraudulent or incomplete, the exporter is required to claw back all refunded and exempted VAT, treat the offshore sale as a domestic taxable supply, pay output VAT, and remit late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties range from 50% to five times the tax amount evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent issuance of special VAT invoices or deliberate deception to obtain export tax refunds constitutes the crime of defrauding export tax rebates under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, carrying imprisonment, fines, and mandatory confiscation of unlawfully obtained refunds, in addition to downgrading or suspension of the enterprise's export tax-refund classification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but the capital account remains heavily regulated. A WFOE whose scope includes accounts-receivable factoring and reverse factoring is treated as a financial-service activity subject to enhanced foreign-exchange (FX) registration and supervisory review by SAFE. Current-account items, such as factoring fees, commissions, and trade-related interest, enjoy liberalized conversion once authenticity has been verified through SAFE’s transaction-certification regime at a domestic designated bank. However, capital-account items, including foreign-currency equity injections, shareholder loans, external guarantees, and any profit repatriation that exceeds the registered capital, require prior SAFE registration or a separate flling. Profit remittance hurdles include audited statutory financial statements, a complete tax clearance certificate covering withholding tax at the treaty rate (usually 10% absent a double-tax-agreement reduction), proof that all underlying receipts were booked through a SAFE-supervised bank account, and documentary evidence of compliance with the registered business scope. The RMB is not freely convertible for capital-account purposes; every cross-border flow must be settled through a local bank under an explicitly approved purpose code. Export proceeds surrender requirements have evolved from forced conversion to a repatriation-plus-retention model, but convertibility is conditional on verifiable trade documents and absence of artificial trade structuring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, and may be retained in their original foreign currency or sold for RMB at the exporter’s discretion. There is no statutory minimum surrender ratio under normal circumstances; however, SAFE’s risk-based supervision may impose temporary mandatory settlement orders on high-risk exporters or those with prior compliance violations. Every receipt must be matched with the corresponding customs declaration and reported to SAFE’s Trade in Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated policy execution by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. Under PRC banking and FX rules, only PBOC/CBIRC-licensed domestic banks may open capital accounts, perform FX settlement, and clear cross-border RMB and foreign-currency transactions. Offshore fintech EMIs are neither licensed banks in China nor qualified SAFE intermediaries and cannot be used for WFOE account-opening, capital verification, or trade settlement. Attempts to route cross-border funds through an EMI would violate SAFE regulations, fail the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission’s prudence standards, and prevent the WFOE from obtaining the required capital verification report or tax filing support.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Injection must follow the SAFE-registered FDI route: after securing the business license and completing MOFCOM filing, the WFOE opens a RMB/FX capital account at a SAFE-designated bank. The overseas parent remits the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code BOP 6011 (Foreign Direct Investment – Equity Capital). The receiving bank authenticates the credit, updates the SAFE Foreign-Invested Enterprise System, and allows inward conversion into RMB for the valid operational purposes listed in the registered business scope. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation is statutorily required when annual related-party transactions exceed CNY 200 million for tangible assets, CNY 100 million for intangible asset transfers, CNY 100 million for financial asset transactions, or CNY 40 million for related-party services. Large-group Master File requirements and related-party disclosure thresholds must also be tested annually.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations registration regime, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the OECD Principal Purpose Test under each applicable treaty require genuine economic substance. A factoring WFOE must conduct its core income-generating activities in China through local employees, offices, credit analysis, underwriting, collections, risk management, financing decisions, and board oversight. A shell WFOE with no local decision-making and with the entire financial substance conducted offshore can be recharacterized, lose treaty benefits, and have income re-attributed to the entity that actually performs the substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, the underpaid Enterprise Income Tax is collected with a penalty of 50% to 500% of the deficiency; false invoicing triggers additional administrative fines, suspension or revocation of licenses, tax blacklisting, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law tax crime provisions when the evaded amount exceeds the criminal threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or participate in the fraudulent scheme can be personally liable for restitution of tax debts after the court pierces the corporate veil under the PRC Company Law; the legal representative or responsible managing director may be subjected to a travel ban and exit control through the tax blacklisting mechanism; criminal conviction for tax evasion can result in fixed-term imprisonment of up to seven years, substantial fines, and confiscation of personal assets.
Thin Capitalization and Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the thin-capitalization rules disallow related-party interest exceeding the regulatory debt-to-equity ratio, generally 2:1 for non-bank enterprises. The disallowed interest is recharacterized as a distribution or adjusted as a transfer-pricing matter, yielding a 10% withholding tax on the deemed dividend, plus daily late-payment surcharge and penalties between 50% and 500% of the tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the excessive debt structure can face personal liability for the unpaid tax in a veil-piercing or fraudulent-transfer action; responsible officers may be blacklisted and barred from exit; and where the scheme is intentional and concealed, criminal liability for accessory tax evasion can lead to imprisonment and asset forfeiture.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse or False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the Principal Purpose Test of the applicable Double Tax Treaty, treaty benefits are denied; the income is recharacterized and taxed at the domestic 10% withholding rate or adjusted as a transfer-pricing matter; penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, daily surcharges, information-exchange reporting, and administrative blacklisting apply.

Director personal liability: Directors using the WFOE as a facade can be personally liable when the corporate veil is pierced, including restitution of tax, penalties, and creditor losses; legal representatives and directors face travel bans and exit restrictions through the joint punishment system for serious tax violations; criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion or financial fraud may impose imprisonment terms, monetary penalties, and confiscation of personal assets.
