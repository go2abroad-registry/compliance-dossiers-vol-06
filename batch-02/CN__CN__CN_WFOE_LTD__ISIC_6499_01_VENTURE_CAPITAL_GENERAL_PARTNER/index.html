Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up registered capital is imposed on an ordinary WFOE limited liability company under the 2023 Company Law. However, Article 47 requires shareholders to pay subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter period is stated in the articles. Capital can be contributed in cash, equipment, intellectual property or equity if lawfully valued. No blocked escrow account is mandated by SAMR for formation. For private fund manager registration, the Asset Management Association of China (AMAC) generally expects paid-up registered capital of at least RMB 2 million, together with a demonstrably adequate office and compliance infrastructure.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required for the office premises: the local Fire and Rescue Department must issue a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate or Fire Safety Acceptance Record before the office is occupied under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. No civil defense permit, dangerous goods storage approval or structural load inspection is triggered for an office-only financial company. The WFOE must maintain evacuation signage, fire extinguishers and records of fire drills. Workplace safety approval for industrial workshops is Not Applicable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; the official online fiscal portal is the National Unified Electronic Taxpayer Portal (e-Tax), operated by STA and provincial-level tax authorities for registration, filing, assessment, invoice administration and export tax refund processes.

E-government filing portal

A Mainland China WFOE is formed through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Upon issuance of the business license, the WFOE receives a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all Chinese taxes, including enterprise income tax, VAT, stamp duty and withholding taxes. Tax data is automatically transmitted from SAMR to STA, making a separate tax registration certificate unnecessary. Within 30 calendar days after the business license date, the WFOE must complete the tax information registration report through the e-Tax portal, confirming the entity name, USCC, registered address, business scope, legal representative, shareholders, industry classification (ISIC 6499/NAICS 525910), accounting standards, bank account details, authorized agents, and forecast of taxable turnover. The WFOE must also register as a VAT taxpayer: as a general taxpayer if forecast annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, or as a small-scale taxpayer below that threshold, with an elective general-taxpayer application if proper accounting and premises are established. In addition, because the WFOE is a venture-capital general partner making fee, carry and dividend payments to non-resident funds, it must complete withholding-agent registration for Chinese-source payments to non-residents; the non-resident fund may separately be required to obtain a Chinese non-resident enterprise TIN through the withholding or direct filing route. Finally, the WFOE must apply through e-Tax for invoice types and quota, and obtain the digital tax-control certificate needed to issue VAT invoices.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a resident Chinese limited liability company taxed under the Enterprise Income Tax Law on worldwide income, with no pass-through treatment. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a resident Chinese limited liability company taxed under the Enterprise Income Tax Law on worldwide income, with no pass-through treatment. It is also a VAT taxable person; its VAT status is either general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer depending on turnover.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; China has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election that changes a WFOE's status to pass-through. The only material election is VAT: a small-scale taxpayer may voluntarily elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer if it can demonstrate fixed business premises, sound books, and reliable accounting, and may not revert without approval. Enterprise income tax group consolidation is not available for separate WFOEs; no partnership-style pass-through is permitted for a WFOE itself.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 generally requires mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayer status applies to annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000, and small-scale taxpayers enjoy a monthly VAT exemption of RMB 100,000 subject to STA policy updates. Voluntary general taxpayer registration is available below the threshold in limited cases. For a venture-capital GP, management fees and fund-advisory fees are taxable at 6%, while carried interest is generally not VAT-taxable if it is an investment-return allocation rather than remuneration for services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: 0% VAT, i.e. exemption with refund, is available only where the goods are actually exported through a customs supervision place; the exporter is a VAT taxpayer; the export is evidenced by a valid customs export declaration with the correct HS code and declared value; and the export sales proceeds are repatriated to China in accordance with SAFE rules, normally within 180 days, with supporting bank foreign-exchange receipts. For cross-border services: zero-rating is limited to enumerated cross-border services under STA regulations; a venture-capital GP's separately charged fund-administration or management services supplied to an overseas fund may qualify for cross-border VAT exemption rather than true zero-rating if the service is rendered entirely outside Mainland China, the recipient is an overseas entity, the benefit of the service is enjoyed outside China, and the taxpayer has filed the cross-border taxable service exemption record with the competent tax authority. Carried interest that represents an allocation of investment returns is not consideration for a taxable service and is outside the VAT base.

  • Export Customs Declaration Form (Customs Bill of Entry) with customs electronic release, export customs code, HS classification, quantity, FOB value and shipping marks; must be consistent with the export sales contract and invoice.
  • Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill, international rail or road consignment note, evidencing despatch and physical exit from China through the named customs port.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher issued by the settlement bank, or SAFE system export-collection record, showing receipt of the export sales proceeds from the overseas buyer.
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice detailing the overseas buyer, delivery terms, currency, unit price, total value, and payment milestones.
  • Packing list, customs inspection or weight list and, where applicable, the STA export tax-refund application data package electronically matching the customs clearance data and VAT invoice details.

China operates a mandatory digital invoice system under the STA's national electronic invoice platform. General VAT taxpayers must issue VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, through the tax-control digital invoicing system; all invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA central tax database, with automatic validation of taxpayer IDs, invoice numbers, tax codes, rates and amounts. The system applies anti-fraud digital stamping using the taxpayer's tax-control digital seal, and dynamic invoice quota management is used to prevent fictitious issuance. After the Golden Tax System Phase IV rollout, invoice data is reconciled with SAMR registration, corporate bank account settlement and customs clearance data. Large taxpayers or enterprises issuing a high volume of invoices are required or encouraged to connect their ERP systems to the electronic invoice platform through certified APIs to obtain invoice numbers and perform real-time invoice clearance. Digital invoices are the default; paper special VAT invoices are restricted to transitional cases and must be reported through the same tax-control system.

Export zero-rating claims are examined jointly by STA and Customs using cross-referenced electronic data, bank settlement data and invoice data. If the goods are not proven to have left China, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, or the documents are inconsistent, the zero-rating is reversed and output VAT becomes payable at the applicable statutory rate, 13% for goods or 6% for services, from the date of export, plus late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. A false export declaration, forged customs declaration, Bill of Lading, FIRC or fictitious invoice for the purpose of obtaining an export VAT refund is treated as fraudulent export tax-refund activity. Under Article 66 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, the tax authority will recover the refunded VAT and impose a penalty of 1 to 5 times the amount fraudulently obtained. Under Article 63, evasion of VAT through false zero-rating is punishable by a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. Criminal liability, including imprisonment or criminal fines, may follow under the Criminal Law where the fraud amount exceeds the criminal threshold; the enterprise may also be placed on the tax-related serious untrustworthy entity list, with restrictions on directors, legal representatives, invoicing, financing and cross-border clearance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility largely available for trade and service transactions, while the capital account remains closely regulated by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise formed as a venture capital general partner, the core distinction is that RMB converted from registered capital or operating receipts may be used for lawful current-item payments such as salaries, rent, management fees, professional fees and approved operational expenses without separate SAFE approval. However, capital-item movements, including investor equity injections, profit remittances, cross-border guarantees, intercompany loans, secondary equity transfers and outbound investments, require verifiable real transactions, purpose codes, supporting contracts and compliance under the Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit remittance is allowed only after audited statutory accounts, full tax settlement and administrative verification of distributable retained earnings; the bank requires a board resolution, tax payment certificates, audited financial statements and a detailed purpose statement. The venture capital GP will face enhanced scrutiny because capital calls, management fees and investment proceeds can be commingled if treasury controls are not transaction-mapped. Therefore, every cross-border flow must be referenced to a legal agreement, an arm's-length pricing rationale and a precise SAFE purpose classification, and the bank must be treated as a first-line compliance validator rather than a passive clearing gateway.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC regulations, there is no mandatory percentage of export foreign-exchange receipts that must be sold to banks; exporters may retain 100 percent of repatriated export proceeds in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntarily settle them into RMB at a designated bank. Nevertheless, all export performance and collection proceeds must be repatriated into the PRC banking system, verified through SAFE's electronic export monitoring framework, and reconciled with customs declarations. The standard statutory verification period for export receipts is generally 180 days from the customs export declaration date; where collection exceeds that window, the exporter must make a deferred collection registration or apply for an extension through the bank and SAFE's digital administration before the deadline lapses. For the WFOE GP, any incidental export or service-trade receipts must also be tagged with valid transaction background; capital account receipts from investors and fund-related events are treated under a different purpose-code matrix and are not subject to export surrender protocol.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), delegated through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally non-viable as a primary servicing bank. Offshore digital fintech EMIs are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to operate RMB clearing, act as designated foreign-exchange banks, access CIPS or hold a WFOE's capital account. Such EMIs are barred from performing FDI registration, capital verification, tax proofing and regulatory reporting that a licensed PRC bank must complete. An overseas EMI may only serve as an auxiliary receivable-collection vehicle outside China for ancillary purposes, and only if its due diligence standards are compatible; it can never replace a tier-1 domestic commercial bank for the regulated treasury and liquidity rail of a Chinese venture capital GP.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Multi-layer offshore ownership involving Cayman or BVI holding vehicles obscures direct and indirect beneficial ownership of the WFOE GP.

Root cause: Multi-layer offshore ownership involving Cayman or BVI holding vehicles obscures direct and indirect beneficial ownership of the WFOE GP.

Defense evidence: Provide a signed full-chain beneficial ownership map from the WFOE to every ultimate natural person; notarized certificates of incumbency and certificate of good standing from each offshore registered agent; constitutional documents and share register for each intermediate entity; trust deeds and protector letters where applicable; passport copies of all ultimate beneficial owners; source-of-wealth statements; and a formal corporate board resolution approving the establishment of the WFOE as general partner.
Capital calls drawn from numerous offshore limited partners create unexpected payor names, high transaction velocity and potential commingling of investor funds with operating capital.

Root cause: Capital calls drawn from numerous offshore limited partners create unexpected payor names, high transaction velocity and potential commingling of investor funds with operating capital.

Defense evidence: Submit the fund limited partnership agreement, all executed subscription documents and side letters, investor capital commitment schedules, official capital call notices, investor AML/KYC certificates from the fund administrator, audited fund financial statements, independent bank statements showing the funding chain, and a compliance undertaking that every inbound wire must identify the paying investor, reference a unique capital call number and be traceable to the matching commitment ledger.
Management fees, carried interest or expense reimbursements paid to affiliated offshore entities may be recharacterized as disguised profit remittances or abusive transfer pricing.

Root cause: Management fees, carried interest or expense reimbursements paid to affiliated offshore entities may be recharacterized as disguised profit remittances or abusive transfer pricing.

Defense evidence: Supply the investment management or advisory agreement, fee schedule and calculation model, itemized time and expense allocations, true invoices for each fee payment, meaningful evidence of services rendered, an audit trail showing portfolio monitoring deliverables, transfer pricing documentation aligned with Chinese rules and OECD principles, and a Chinese tax withholding certificate issued by the tax bureau if a cross-border service payment is made.
Use of FX-settled registered capital for domestic securities, entrusted loans, real estate or non-business investments could breach PRC Negative List and capital account payment restrictions for a venture capital GP.

Root cause: Use of FX-settled registered capital for domestic securities, entrusted loans, real estate or non-business investments could breach PRC Negative List and capital account payment restrictions for a venture capital GP.

Defense evidence: Provide a SAFE compliance undertaking, an annual operating and investment budget, the fund mandate and investment policy documents, executed term sheets and investment agreements, proof that the proposed target is not on the prohibited Negative List, capital payment instruction forms with the correct purpose code, and an external PRC legal opinion confirming that the deployment falls within the WFOE's business scope and is not a prohibited capital-account movement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign shareholders must transfer the subscribed registered capital via SWIFT MT103 from their overseas bank account to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account held at a licensed tier-1 PRC bank. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete electronic FDI foreign exchange registration through the local SAFE framework and obtain the unique FDI security/verification code from the bank. The wire instruction must include the full name of the investor, the full name of the WFOE, the capital contribution reference and a foreign exchange purpose code corresponding to foreign direct investment equity injection. Upon receipt, the receiving bank will verify name, amount, currency, tax registration and paid-in capital schedule; if conversion into RMB is requested, the bank will credit the RMB capital settlement account and require the WFOE to submit a capital utilization plan. A Chinese certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report after each paid-in injection, and subsequent use of the capital, including investment into portfolio companies or payment of operating expenses, must be supported by signed agreements and purpose-specific documentation to satisfy the SAFE real-transaction and Negative List requirements.For service-trade disbursements, including management fees, investment advisory fees, professional fees or licensing payments, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 through its licensed bank, attaching the underlying service contract, genuine invoice, evidence of completion, tax withholding certificate and the correct SAFE service-trade purpose code. For physical trade settlement, which is less typical for a venture capital GP, commercial letters of credit issued through the bank via SWIFT MT700/MT710 or documentary collections may be used when the WFOE or a portfolio company is importing equipment or purchasing goods. For the core venture capital treasury activities, outbound flows will normally be domestic RMB bank transfers to local target companies after FX settlement for equity investments, or MT103 wires to offshore service providers and intermediate fund structures; all such payments must be matched to a signed transaction, maintained in the bank's capital account payment log and submitted for SAFE cross-border transaction reporting where applicable.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts underwritten by a licensed onshore bank, subject to SAFE real-demand verification and supported by underlying current or capital account exposurePlain vanilla CNY FX options purchased from a licensed bank, with premium-only settlement and no speculative writing of options by the WFOEOnshore CNY/USD FX swaps for cash flow timing adjustments, provided the purpose is genuine and documentedCross-currency swaps for long-term USD-denominated capital commitments and RMB operating needs, after obtaining bank risk approval and SAFE registration if requiredNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible as onshore hedging instruments for the WFOE; they may be used only by an offshore affiliate with no CNY delivery obligation and with a separate legal opinion

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally non-viable as a primary servicing bank. Offshore digital fintech EMIs are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to operate RMB clearing, act as designated foreign-exchange banks, access CIPS or hold a WFOE's capital account. Such EMIs are barred from performing FDI registration, capital verification, tax proofing and regulatory reporting that a licensed PRC bank must complete. An overseas EMI may only serve as an auxiliary receivable-collection vehicle outside China for ancillary purposes, and only if its due diligence standards are compatible; it can never replace a tier-1 domestic commercial bank for the regulated treasury and liquidity rail of a Chinese venture capital GP.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when related-party transactions in the prior fiscal year exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible-asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is required when the MNE group's consolidated revenue in the prior year exceeds RMB 2 billion, and Country-by-Country Reporting is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply an OECD-style Bahamas/Dubai Economic Substance Regulation regime, but the PRC tax law requires economic substance through the general anti-avoidance rules (EIT Law Articles 47-48), the beneficial ownership doctrine under SAT Public Notice No. 9 of 2018, and the substance-over-form principle. For a VC GP, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include investment sourcing, due diligence, valuation, negotiation, capital calls, portfolio management, risk monitoring, and carried-interest decision-making. If those activities are actually performed by personnel in the WFOE while profits are booked or shifted offshore, the SAT may attribute income to a permanent establishment, recharacterize the arrangement, impose transfer pricing adjustments, and deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Fee Misallocation

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Articles 41 and 48 empower the SAT to make an arm's length recharacterization, increase taxable income, and collect unpaid tax plus benchmark-rate-plus interest. Under TCAL Article 63, intentional evasion attracts a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharges, and, if the amount is sufficiently large, criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors, CFOs, and legal representatives who sign false returns or direct the booking of sham fees risk being named as directly responsible persons under Criminal Law Article 201 and Article 211, with imprisonment up to 3 years for relatively large evasion and up to 7 years for especially large evasion, plus fines. Civilly, if corporate assets are stripped to avoid tax, courts may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 23, causing directors/shareholders to return extracted assets and make good unpaid taxes. The tax authority may also blacklist the legal representative and impose travel restrictions/exit bans under the outstanding-tax enforcement measures.
Shell-Company / Treaty Abuse / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The claim for treaty relief is rejected under SAT Public Notice No. 9 of 2018 for failure to satisfy the beneficial ownership test. EIT Law Articles 47-48 allow the SAT to apply GAAR to disregard the intermediary, recharacterize the income, impose the statutory 10% WHT, add interest, and apply TCAL Article 63 penalties of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create a sham conduit may lose the protection of limited liability: under Company Law Article 23, if abuse of the company's legal personality is used to evade tax, the controlling shareholder and those acting in concert are jointly and severally liable for company debts and tax arrears. The legal representative can be placed on the tax blacklist, banned from travel, and debarred from new senior management roles; criminal liability can arise if the arrangement is part of fraudulent tax evasion, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment.
Withholding Tax Failure / Unreported Outbound Distributions

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 69, the withholding agent that fails to withhold is ordered to remit the unpaid tax and is fined 50% to 300% of the failed withholding; the foreign payee may also be pursued for the tax. If the withholding agent fails to pay after demand, daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05% accrue under TCAL Article 32. Criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 can apply where the failure is intentional and large.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who order or authorize dividend remittances without WHT face civil liability for the unpaid collection if they are found to have abused the corporate form or acted outside corporate interests; the company may be required to withhold from subsequent distributions. The legal representative and CFO may be blacklisted, denied exit, and have personal assets traced where funds are commingled or transferred for personal benefit. In egregious cases, personal criminal penalties of imprisonment for tax evasion of up to 3-7 years and monetary fines are imposed under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 211.
