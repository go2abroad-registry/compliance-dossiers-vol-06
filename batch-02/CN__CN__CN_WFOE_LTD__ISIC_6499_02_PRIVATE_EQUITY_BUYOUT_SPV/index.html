Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a general WFOE under the current Company Law, and no blocked escrow account is required by the SAMR or by the Foreign Investment Law. The registered capital amount, currency, contribution form, and payment schedule must be specified in the Articles of Association, and all subscribed capital must be actually paid within the statutory maximum contribution period of five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter period is expressly stated in the Articles. The shareholder may not evade full subscription liability by claiming the capital is a mere clearing deposit.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to the financial holding SPV except for ordinary office occupancy. The commercial leasehold must have passed the building fire safety acceptance inspection, and the lessee must maintain fire escape access, portable extinguishers and emergency evacuation signage as required by national fire safety regulations. No Civil Defense construction approval, structural load inspection or industrial workplace safety clearance is required because the company will not install manufacturing equipment or store hazardous materials. If the SPV makes any leasehold alterations, the landlord or tenant must apply for fire safety alteration review through the local Fire and Rescue Brigade before renovation, and civil defense obligations may arise only if the alteration changes the building structure or underground spaces.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China are eligible for VAT zero-rating (0%) provided the export is genuine and the exporter retains customs records, export contracts, and proof of foreign exchange collection; qualifying offshore services rendered to non-resident clients may also be zero-rated or exempt under the STA's cross-border service rules. The WFOE must maintain complete documentation and repatriate proceeds within the statutory deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Export Contract
  • VAT invoice (if issued for export)
  • Export tax rebate declaration form and customs verification report

China enforces the Golden Tax Phase IV (Jin Shui Phase IV) system. VAT special invoices must be issued through STA-approved tax control equipment or the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform. Invoice data is transmitted in real-time via API to the STA's e-Invoice Service Platform; each invoice carries a unique anti-counterfeit tax control code and digital stamp; on-the-fly fiscal clearance and cross-matching with customs and treasury systems are mandatory.

Any export zero-rating or tax refund claim that fails documentary verification is retroactively clawed back with late-payment surcharges; under the Law on Tax Administration and Tax Collection, tax evasion penalties range from 50% to 5 times the tax involved, and fraudulent export refunds may trigger criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (crime of fraudulent export tax rebate), Customs Law penalties, downgrading of tax refund classification, and enhanced audit scrutiny for up to 3 years. If foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days without valid justification, the zero-rating is invalidated and export tax refund is denied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with strong administrative oversight. Current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are broadly convertible through bank-based verification and SAFE monitoring, and genuine trade-related foreign exchange receipts and payments can generally proceed without an individual SAFE approval. Capital-account transactions remain restricted and require case-by-case SAFE registration, quotas, or pre-clearance under China's negative-list rules. For a WFOE private equity buyout SPV, the critical convertibility bottlenecks are: (i) inbound FDI equity capital must first be recorded under the SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration system, the foreign-currency amount must be deposited in a designated capital foreign-exchange account, and conversion into RMB may only occur after bank review of the lawful purpose and positive-list compliance; (ii) dividend and profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted only from after-tax profits, supported by audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and a formal board resolution; (iii) any onward outbound investment or acquisition-related cross-border payments by the WFOE may require additional FDI/ODI filing or SAFE approval depending on the deal structure; and (iv) proceeds from export trade, if any, must be repatriated to China within the statutory trade-monitoring window and are reportable through the bank's SAFE-connected system. Therefore, currency convertibility is liberalized for real-economy current-account flows but remains tightly gated for capital-account flows, requiring every treasury movement to be tied to an evidenced, registered and auditable transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE goods-trade foreign exchange administration framework, export proceeds must be repatriated into a bank account in China within 180 days from the date of export customs clearance; if the 180-day timeline cannot be met, the exporter must provide a written explanation and supporting evidence to the receiving bank and local SAFE office. Under normal conditions, SAFE does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio on lawful export proceeds, so the WFOE may retain the receipts in a designated foreign-currency account or voluntarily convert them into RMB at the bank's prevailing rate. However, when an exporter enters SAFE's abnormal-list or key-monitoring category, or during balance-of-payments stress, SAFE may direct banks to demand mandatory conversion into RMB and to prohibit retention without an approved special reason.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), which serves as China's delegated foreign exchange regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Mainland China does not recognize offshore digital EMIs or electronic-money licenses as a lawful substitute for a bank account. Cross-border payment institutions licensed by the PBoC may provide ancillary collection and payment services, but they cannot hold WFOE client funds in a regulated deposit account, cannot issue lawful foreign-exchange settlement, and cannot act as the depositary bank for SAFE registration or capital verification. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI would create serious AML and KYC gaps, expose the WFOE to violation of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, and ruin any future capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Therefore, the WFOE must use a licensed domestic tier-1 commercial bank as its primary clearing bank and treat any fintech arrangement only as a supportive e-payment front-end.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

After the WFOE is incorporated, it must complete SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration through the receiving bank and obtain the required registration identifier. The foreign investor then wires funds via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated capital foreign-exchange account using the SAFE-registered remittance purpose for direct investment equity contribution; the receiving bank must verify that the remitter is the registered foreign investor and that the stated purpose matches the FDI registration. Upon arrival, the bank performs anti-money-laundering screening, checks the negative-list industry restrictions, and permits conversion into RMB only when the funds are needed for lawful business expenditures. A Chinese certified public accountant must subsequently issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, and the foreign-exchange purchase and use of the converted RMB must be recorded and traceable.For lawful current-account disbursements, the WFOE may instruct its domestic bank to execute SWIFT MT103 payments in foreign currency or RMB after the bank verifies the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration or tax filing, as required by SAFE. For import of goods, the common clearing rails are SWIFT MT700 documentary letters of credit, where the WFOE's bank issues or confirms the credit to the overseas beneficiary, and documentary collections such as D/P or D/A, where the bank acts as collecting or presenting agent against shipping documents. Dividend repatriation and intragroup service fees are cleared through SWIFT MT103 only after tax withholding and foreign-exchange compliance documents are lodged, while any buyout-related overseas payments must be checked against the WFOE's approved business scope and relevant SAFE registrations.Deliverable FX forwards: onshore RMB against USD, EUR, HKD and other trade currencies with SAFE-designated banks are permissible when the WFOE can evidence genuine payments or receipts with a specific maturity; no speculative open positions are allowed.FX swaps and cross-currency basis swaps: permitted for matched conversion and reconversion of current-account and capital-account flows, including import settlement, debt service, and planned dividend conversion, with the swap tenor limited to the maturity of the underlying exposure.FX options: permitted as hedging instruments for real contingent exposures, such as a possible acquisition completion payment or a trade-related tender requirement, provided the bank completes a risk-orientation test and the option is not used to create a naked speculative position.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not directly permissible for the onshore WFOE as a treasury contract because SAFE requires deliverable underlying exposure and onshore banks generally cannot book NDFs for domestic corporate clients; any offshore NDF hedge would need to be executed by a separate offshore group company through an offshore bank and cannot impose settlement obligations on the WFOE.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Mainland China does not recognize offshore digital EMIs or electronic-money licenses as a lawful substitute for a bank account. Cross-border payment institutions licensed by the PBoC may provide ancillary collection and payment services, but they cannot hold WFOE client funds in a regulated deposit account, cannot issue lawful foreign-exchange settlement, and cannot act as the depositary bank for SAFE registration or capital verification. Using an unlicensed offshore EMI would create serious AML and KYC gaps, expose the WFOE to violation of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, and ruin any future capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Therefore, the WFOE must use a licensed domestic tier-1 commercial bank as its primary clearing bank and treat any fintech arrangement only as a supportive e-payment front-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (Special Tax Investigation and TP Documentation) must be prepared when the enterprise's related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceed RMB 20 million for controlled transactions of tangible property; RMB 40 million for financial assets/equity transfers; RMB 10 million for intangible property; RMB 10 million for financial transactions; and RMB 5 million for other transactions. Master File must be prepared when the enterprise's total related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion annually or when the enterprise has been cross-border related-party transactions and the total transaction amount exceeds RMB 400 million (or meets the definitions in the EIT Law Article 114). The WFOE LBO SPV must also prepare Country-by-Country (CbC) Report if combined group revenue of the ultimate parent exceeds RMB 5.5 billion (≈€750 million).

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not applicable in mainland China as a separate statutory regime, but the enterprise income tax law applies 'substance over form' and 'business purpose' doctrines. For a private equity buyout SPV, the Chinese tax authorities scrutinize whether the WFOE has adequate economic substance to justify its profit attribution, management functions, and assumption of risks. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is relevant when evaluating the place of effective management, permanent establishment risk, and eligibility for treaty benefits. A thin-capitalized SPV with limited employees and no substantive decision-making in China may face income recharacterization, interest disallowance under the 3:1 debt-to-equity ratio, denial of treaty benefits, and potential attribution of profits to the offshore parent under the 'place of effective management' rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Falsification of Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law (PRC), the tax authority shall recover the evaded tax, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and require late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day. If the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes 10% or more of the taxable income, criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law may lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate or participate in tax evasion face joint and several civil liability for the enterprise's unpaid taxes and penalties under the Civil Code (Article 75), potential asset freezing, and travel restrictions by the tax authority. Criminal prosecution can result in personal fines and imprisonment up to 7 years for the responsible directors, officers, and actual controllers.
Transfer Pricing Misrepresentation and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 121 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Special Tax Adjustments Measures (SAT Announcement 2017-6), the tax authority may make an adjustment to taxable income and impose an interest surcharge of the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points on the additional tax resulting from the adjustment. A penalty of 50% to 500% applies to the underpaid tax if there is fraud or gross negligence. For thin capitalization, loans exceeding the 3:1 debt-to-equity ratio cause disallowance of interest deduction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign inaccurate transfer pricing documentation or direct fraudulent intercompany transactions can be held personally liable for the tax due if there is evidence of intentional evasion or abusing the legal personality. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, compelling directors to personally repay taxes, penalties, and interest. Persistent non-compliance can lead to blacklisting in the tax enforcement record, travel bans, and criminal charges for tax evasion under the Criminal Law.
Money Laundering and Illegal Fund Remittance (AML)

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law (AML) and Administrative Measures for the Reporting of Large-Value and Suspicious Transactions, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and SAFE can impose fines of up to RMB 5 million on the entity, suspend or revoke business licenses, and prohibit directors from holding management positions. Criminal prosecution under Article 191 of the Criminal Law (Money Laundering Crime) may impose imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are subject to personal asset seizure, freezing of bank accounts, travel bans, and visa revocation. Civil courts may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors personally liable for ill-gotten proceeds. Criminal imprisonment of up to 10 years and aggravated penalties apply where the laundering amount exceeds RMB 10 million or involves organized crime. Directors also face personal fines of 50% to 100% of the laundered amount and mandatory disqualification from serving as directors.
