Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); mandatory online filing through the STA E-Tax Bureau / Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform, with local administration by the registered competent state tax office.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a WFOE must first obtain a Business License from the local Administration for Market Regulation. Under the 'one license with five certificates' integration reform, the 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License is simultaneously the enterprise taxpayer identification number (TIN) and is automatically entered into the STA tax registry; no separate TIN application is made. Within 30 days of the Business License issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration confirmation through the STA E-Tax Bureau or at the competent tax office, electing VAT activities, Enterprise Income Tax, stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax, and education surcharges. For the VAT ledger, the WFOE must determine whether it is a small-scale or general VAT taxpayer; if projected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5 million it must register as a general VAT taxpayer, obtain invoice-issuing authorization, and procure the STA digital invoicing credentials and e-invoice quota through the electronic tax bureau. A foreign non-resident entity that does not establish a permanent establishment in China does not obtain a mainland TIN solely by holding equity; any China-source withholding is administered on the non-resident entity and the payer's E-Tax registration, and the non-resident may apply for treaty benefits through the STA 'non-resident treaty benefit procedure' when required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Tax_Exempt_Zone: Not applicable. The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise (tax resident under China's Enterprise Income Tax Law) and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income at 25% standard CIT (reduced to 15% only if qualified high-and-new-tech status is granted). No pass-through or transparent election is available in China.

Available election forms

None under Enterprise Income Tax Law (China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections). For VAT, a taxpayer below the RMB 5 million annual threshold may elect to be a general VAT taxpayer rather than a small-scale taxpayer; small-scale VAT taxpayers are automatically subject to simplified collection, not an elective entity-classification regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer once this threshold is reached. The newly incorporated WFOE must complete tax registration within 30 days of its Business License regardless of turnover; below the threshold it may be a small-scale VAT taxpayer at the collection rate of 3% (or such preferential rate as STA prescribes). Foreign non-resident suppliers of digital services with mainland consumers may also have registration or withholding requirements under China's VAT collection rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods is zero-rated/refundable when the goods are lawfully exported under customs supervision and the eventual VAT is not consumed in China. Cross-border services are zero-rated only if they are explicitly listed as zero-rated cross-border services (e.g., certain R&D, design, software, information technology, or consulting services) and are supplied to an overseas entity/individual with the service wholly consumed outside mainland China. An equity-crowdfunding portal that provides regulated financial intermediation or settlement services is generally treated as a 6% taxable financial/modern service unless the specific service is removed from the financial-services classification and meets the statutory consumption-outside-China test; offshore clients alone do not create zero-rating entitlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export goods declaration) issued through the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by the General Administration of Customs
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading / multimodal transport document evidencing exit of the goods from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and underlying export sales contract
  • VAT invoice for the export sale issued through the STA digital invoice system where required
  • Foreign-exchange receipt evidence: bank collection advice or settlement voucher recorded in the SAFE Trade in Goods foreign-exchange monitoring system (not an FIRC, which is not a statutory Chinese document)
  • Export tax refund/exemption declaration filed online through the STA export tax rebate module, where a refund is claimed

Mandatory use of the STA's Digital Invoice Service Platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV architecture. VAT invoices must be issued via STA-certified software or the E-Tax portal, with fully digitalized e-invoices (dynamic anti-fraud QR code, unique invoice number, near-real-time upload to the STA) as the default. Invoice data is reconciled with VAT filing, a risk-control engine blocks anomalous issuances, and paper or manual invoicing outside the approved national platform is unlawful.

The STA and Customs jointly audit export declarations, logistics, invoices, and SAFE forex receipts. If zero-rating or refund is claimed without actual export, actual receipt of proceeds, or proof of offshore consumption, the claimed VAT is clawed back with daily interest; under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection the tax authority may impose a penalty of between 50% and five times the underpaid tax or refund obtained. Fraudulent export-rebate claims may also constitute the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under the Criminal Law, with imprisonment up to life imprisonment and asset confiscation; the assessment and claw-back period is unlimited where fraud is established. For cross-border services, retrospective adjustment will apply if the service-consumption evidence or the non-resident status of the recipient is false or unsupported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated foreign exchange system. Current account items are broadly convertible for bona fide trade and service transactions, subject to bank verification of contracts, invoices, customs declarations and tax records. Capital account transactions remain heavily administered: FDI contributions, shareholder loans, dividends, and capital repatriation require SAFE registration, a designated foreign exchange capital account, and compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List. RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account; banks execute payments only after checking the underlying transaction authenticity and regulatory filings. For a WFOE operating an equity crowdfunding portal, the licensing status under CSRC regulation is a primary gating item; foreign capital may enter as registered capital only after the SAFE foreign exchange registration is completed. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, corporate income tax completion certificates, board resolutions, and proof of no outstanding SAFE reporting violations. Surplus capital or liquidation proceeds can be repatriated only after clearing all tax liabilities and closing the account through SAFE. In practice, convertibility is conditional, paper-intensive and subject to local SAFE branch discretion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through a domestic designated bank within 180 days from the customs export declaration date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a current account or convert to RMB at market rates. All receipts must be reported through the SAFE cross-border income and payment system, and any special trade model must be documented to avoid false trade or delayed repatriation penalties.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) exercising foreign exchange administration through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low legal viability as a primary account provider. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed to hold or clear domestic RMB current accounts for an onshore WFOE. The Chinese banking regulatory framework requires depository and settlement functions to be performed by licensed domestic commercial banks; foreign EMIs can only provide ancillary payment interfaces through partnership with a licensed Chinese bank. For a high-risk equity crowdfunding portal, the bank park account, escrow account and operational disbursement account must remain inside the domestic banking system to satisfy PBOC, SAFE and CSRC supervision.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For related-party transactions in a Chinese fiscal year, the Local File is mandatory where: tangible property transfers with related parties exceed RMB 100 million; intangible property transfers exceed RMB 10 million; financial asset transfers or intercompany loans/advances exceed RMB 10 million; or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 10 million. The Master File must be prepared when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The Annual Related-Party Transaction Report must be filed with the annual CIT return for any year in which related-party transactions occur, regardless of amount. Failure to prepare or submit contemporaneous documentation exposes the WFOE to TP adjustments, interest, and penalties.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Gulf-state style Economic Substance Regulations as a standalone code, but the STA applies substance-over-form and the Chinese General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and STA Public Notice 2017 No. 6. A WFOE claiming treaty benefits, tax-treaty reduced rates, or non-recognition of Chinese profit must demonstrate the Core Income-Generating Activities are in China: licensed platform decision-making, investor KYC, AML screening, investment suitability review, escrow account direction, risk warnings, deal-book preparation, complaint handling, and regulatory filing. If these functions are performed in Hong Kong or a tax haven while employees in China only sign documents, the STA may recharacterise the structure, reallocate profit to China, deny treaty benefits, and impose a penalty for lack of economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing profit shifting through offshore commission network

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 47; STA Public Notice 2017 No. 6; Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Enforcement Article 63. The tax authority may disregard the offshore booking company, impute the missing revenue to the WFOE, apply the arm's-length standard, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid CIT, and charge a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge. If the concealment exceeds the criminal threshold under Criminal Law Article 201, the taxpayer faces criminal fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 23 where the shareholder-director abuses WFOE limited liability to shift assets offshore. The director is personally liable for the unpaid CIT, interest, and penalties. Assets such as personal bank accounts, transfers to family members, and Hong Kong bank balances are subject to civil asset restitution. The legal representative is placed on the STA exit blacklist and prohibited from leaving China until the tax debt is settled. Directors who knowingly organise the profit shift can be sentenced to up to seven years' imprisonment.
Withholding tax evasion on outbound dividends, interest, and royalties

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37-40; Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Enforcement Article 69. A withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is liable for a penalty of 50% to 300% of the amount of tax that should have been withheld, plus daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05%. The expense paid is non-deductible to the extent the withholding is not performed. Recalcitrant withholding agents or their directors may also be blacklisted as serious tax violators.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dividend or royalty remittance instructions while knowing the tax was not withheld are treated as controlling officers of a non-compliant enterprise. Piercing the corporate veil allows creditors and tax authorities to recover the unwithheld tax from the director's personal assets. The board member is subject to civil asset restitution, travel-ban blacklisting at international border controls, and under Criminal Law Article 201, imprisonment and fines where the evasion is intentional and large. The same conduct may trigger criminal liability under Article 201 for fraudulent withholding returns.
Shell company, unlicensed equity crowdfunding, and AML failure

Statutory sanction: Securities Law Article 197 punishes operating a securities business without a licence by confiscating illegal income and imposing a fine of one to ten times the illegal income, or RMB 1,000,000 to RMB 10,000,000 where illegal income is absent. Criminal Law Article 176 penalises illegal absorption of public deposits with imprisonment of three to ten years and a fine for serious cases. Anti-Money-Laundering Law enforcement may impose fines on the entity and responsible directors for failing to maintain AML procedures, transaction monitoring, and customer identification. The regulated equity crowdfunding licence is revoked and the platform is shut down.

Director personal liability: Because the company lacks the economic substance supporting limited liability, the court will pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 23. Directors and the actual controlling shareholder are jointly and severally liable for restitution of investor principal, unpaid taxes, escrow shortages, and penalties. The responsible individual is subject to civil asset seizure, travel ban or blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment up to ten years for illegal fundraising or AML failure. Even a nominee director who lends his name to the shell may be banned from being a director and held personally accountable for consenting to illegal distributions.
