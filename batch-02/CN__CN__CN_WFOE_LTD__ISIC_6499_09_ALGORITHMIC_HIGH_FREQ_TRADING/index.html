Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The PRC Company Law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up registered capital and no mandatory escrow or blocked account requirement for ordinary wholly foreign-owned limited liability companies. The amount and contribution schedule are set in the Articles of Association and must be fully paid within the agreed term. However, if the WFOE intends to conduct regulated securities or futures market access for proprietary ultra-low-latency algorithmic high-frequency trading, the applicable China Securities Regulatory Commission or exchange licence may separately prescribe higher paid-up capital, net capital, and client asset segregation requirements; those are regulatory licence conditions, not incorporation escrow rules.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — no civil defense industrial fire permit, structural load inspection, or hazardous material storage approval is required for non-industrial office use. Before occupation, the leased office must obtain a standard fire safety completion filing or fire safety inspection certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department, and the Company must register work safety rules, provide ergonomic risk assessments, and complete occupational disease hazard notification under the Work Safety Law. For any backup server cabinet or battery room, a dedicated fire suppression and electrical safety inspection may be required by the building fire safety authority, but no special civil defense industrial clearance applies.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; taxpayer portal is the nationwide Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax) online platform operated by provincial-level STA branches.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the entity must first be incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation, which issues the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code. This code is automatically the taxpayer identification number and VAT taxpayer identification number used for all tax filings. Within 30 calendar days after the business license is issued, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local State Taxation Administration office through the e-Tax portal. Required data and documents include the business license, legal representative passport or ID, articles of association, registered address proof, bank account registration, and information on anticipated business scope and invoicing needs. After verification, the system activates taxpayer status, VAT registration, and the electronic invoice quota.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese-established entities must complete tax registration without an economic threshold; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000, while below that threshold the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer unless voluntary general taxpayer registration is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of physical goods from China qualify for the 0% VAT rate under the export exemption and refund mechanism, provided the goods physically leave China through customs control and the exporter retains formal export documentation. Qualifying cross-border services, including certain trading, market data, clearing, and financial software services supplied to an offshore recipient and consumed wholly outside China, may also be zero-rated or exempt from VAT. The taxpayer must demonstrate offshore recipient status, genuine cross-border supply, and economic substance through contracts, service descriptions, delivery records, and underlying trade and settlement evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading (original or telex release)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank export proceeds receipt record

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's fully digitalized electronic invoice platform or tax-control invoice equipment integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Special VAT invoices require receipt of a cryptographic tax-control stamp and real-time transmission for fiscal clearance through API integration. Invoice issuance can be blocked for non-compliant taxpayers or unreconciled filings, and tamper-evidence is maintained by centralized digital tax authority authentication.

Audit scrutiny is performed through Golden Tax data matching between the customs export declaration system, VAT invoice filings, foreign exchange receipt records, and corporate income tax filings. Unverified or fraudulent zero-rating claims are subject to mandatory export tax refund clawback, daily late-payment surcharges, administrative fines ranging from 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid or refunded VAT, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Renminbi is convertible for current account items such as trade settlement, service fees, royalty payments and dividends, but is still managed through a transaction-level documentary review by SAFE. Capital account items remain under strict control and require prior regulatory filing or approval. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, inbound equity registered capital enters via a SAFE-registered FX capital account and can be converted into RMB only through a designated bank under SAFE capital account rules. The converted RMB may be deployed solely within the approved business scope, and any subsequent change in shareholding, registered capital, or business purpose triggers fresh SAFE reregistration. Profit remittance to the overseas parent is permitted after annual statutory audit, tax clearance, and a bank review of board resolutions and balance-sheet substance; delays occur when the cumulative registered capital has not been fully contributed, when offshore shareholders are in high-risk jurisdictions, or when the WFOE shows persistent loss or mismatched cash flows. There is no blanket surrender ratio for current account export proceeds, but banks are required to keep receipt records and reconcile export customs and foreign exchange data. Commodity hedging flows and proprietary trading income are further restricted by license scope and product eligibility, and speculative cross-border NDF or offshore CNH hedging is not allowed for the onshore WFOE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated FX account of the WFOE in the exporting entity's own name; the default expected collection window is 90 days from the date of export customs declaration unless a longer period is registered with SAFE. Since 2012, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the WFOE may voluntarily settle into RMB or retain the FX in its settlement account, subject to ongoing SAFE reconciliation and bank examination of the underlying goods movement, customs invoice, bill of lading and import/export business license.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary or regulatory settlement channel. SAFE and PBOC rules require corporate foreign exchange receipts, capital account injections, RMB conversion, and cross-border settlements to occur through SAFE-designated domestic banks with a valid banking business license. Overseas fintech EMIs are not recognized as eligible holders or remitters of WFOE registered capital and cannot operate a SAFE foreign exchange capital account, issue an RMB settlement directive to CNAPS/CIPS on the WFOE's behalf, or perform the tax and FX purpose-code verification required for outbound profit remittance. An offshore EMI may be used only as a supplementary ancillary card or expense tool outside the regulated treasury perimeter, and even then any funding must pass through a licensed Tier-1 onshore custodian.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital injection should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's own bank account directly to the WFOE's SAFE-designated foreign exchange capital account. The wire must use the SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment capital injection and include the WFOE name and SAFE FDI registration number in the payment reference. The foreign shareholder must first complete SAFE FDI initial registration, obtain the Foreign Investor Qualified Certificate or equivalent record, and deposit capital according to the contribution schedule in the constitutional documents. After receipt, the designated Chinese bank converts the FX into RMB using the real-time market exchange rate or the bank's quoted price, and the funds are credited to the RMB capital account. A local statutory accounting firm or certified public auditor issues a capital verification report confirming contribution. The remitter, amount and reference must exactly match the approved FDI registration; third-party remittance is not acceptable without a notarised agency mandate.Cross-border settlements for operational and financial obligations should be executed as SWIFT MT103 payments with the correct SAFE income and payment purpose code, supported by invoices, contracts and regulatory approvals. Outbound dividend remittance to the offshore parent requires the audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificate, board resolution on profit distribution, dividend withholding tax payment proof, and the WFOE's foreign exchange business registration. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File is mandatory if in one tax year the related-party transactions exceed any of the following thresholds: related-party tangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; related-party financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. A Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China Mainland does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT rules impose a strict substance-over-form and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR). Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Public Notice 2015/45, transactions lacking a reasonable commercial purpose can be recharacterized. A WFOE engaged in algorithmic HFT must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are actually conducted in China: algorithm development, trade execution, market risk management, systems monitoring, compliance, and senior strategic decision-making. If the WFOE is a bare certificate-holder with no employees, no servers, no trading P&L, and no management, it will be treated as a tax conduit, treaty benefits will be denied, and profits will be reallocated to the jurisdiction where the economic substance truly resides.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fraudulent Reporting of Algorithmic Trading Income

Statutory sanction: Article 201 Criminal Law of the PRC imposes a fine and imprisonment for tax evasion; Article 63 Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%. The SAT may also blacklist the company as a serious tax violator and impose exit restrictions on the legal representative.

Director personal liability: A director or compliance officer who knowingly directed the evasion can be prosecuted as a directly responsible person with imprisonment of up to 7 years and an additional fine; the court may confiscate proceeds under asset recovery powers. If the WFOE is operated as the mere instrument of the shareholder and the abuse of corporate limited liability is established under Article 20 of the Company Law, the corporate veil will be pierced and the director/shareholder will be jointly and personally liable for the unpaid taxes, civil penalties, and restitution.
Non-Arm's-Length Intercompany Financing and Intellectual Property Migration

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorizes the SAT to make transfer pricing adjustments; Article 46 and the debt-to-equity safe harbor disallow interest on excessive related-party debt; Article 44 allows penalties and interest on undocumented transfer pricing. The Tax Collection Administration Law permits a 50% to 500% misreporting penalty on the adjusted tax and the SAT can make an upward adjustment with late-payment interest from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign sham related-party agreements and knowingly cause false tax filings can be subject to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201; if the extracted profits are moved out of the WFOE and the company cannot satisfy its tax debt, a court may pierce the veil and order personal reimbursement, freezing personal assets and restricting departure from China. In egregious cases, the management team may be investigated for document forgery and fraudulent invoicing, with imprisonment terms of up to 7 years.
Shell WFOE Structure and Treaty/Base Erosion Abuse

Statutory sanction: The GAAR in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) in China's tax treaties allow the SAT to deny treaty benefits, impose the full 10% withholding tax, and recharacterize the WFOE as having a taxable presence or direct ownership in the underlying income. False treaty claims are punishable under Article 52 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and public credit blacklisting; where shell arrangements involve fraud, criminal law sanctions for tax evasion and money laundering can be sought.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell WFOE may be personally liable if they use the corporate personality to evade tax and assets have been commingled with shareholder assets or intentionally removed from creditor reach. Courts can apply veil-piercing under Company Law Article 20 and order the director to repay the WFOE's tax debts with personal assets. Tax authorities can partner with border-control authorities to impose a departure/travel ban where the legal representative has serious unpaid tax liabilities. Criminal prosecution for knowing misuse of treaty arrangements can lead to fixed-term imprisonment of up to 7 years for the responsible officers and monetary fines imposed on both the entity and the individual.
