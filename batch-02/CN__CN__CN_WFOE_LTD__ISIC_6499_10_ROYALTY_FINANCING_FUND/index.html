Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investing entity establishing a WFOE: Step 1 - incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation to obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the entity's statutory tax identification number (TIN) for enterprise income tax and VAT. Step 2 - within 30 calendar days after issuance of the business license, complete national tax registration in the provincial e-Tax platform by uploading the business license, articles of association, registered address certificate, foreign investor identity documents, capital contribution bank receipt, and authorized legal representative's ID; Step 3 - obtain a tax registration record with the systemic taxpayer file number and register VAT taxpayer status (General or Small-Scale), including invoice quota and issuance authorization; Step 4 - complete export-tax registration and open a current account with an SAFE-authorized bank; Step 5 - for a true non-resident enterprise without Chinese permanent establishment, the non-resident should not obtain a Chinese VAT ID; rather, the Chinese payer must record the non-resident's home-country tax ID and perform withholding registration in the e-Tax withholding module, followed by issuance of a tax payment certificate for treaty clearance where applicable.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) generally triggers mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration; however, every WFOE is required to complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining the business license irrespective of turnover. Below the threshold, the taxpayer remains a Small-Scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects General VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rated VAT is available where goods are physically exported from China customs territory under an export declaration, sold to a non-resident buyer, and export proceeds are repatriated through SAFE-authorized banking channels. Services: zero-rating is restricted to qualifying offshore services explicitly listed by the STA, such as offshore R&D, software development, design, consulting, and IP royalties supplied to and wholly consumed by non-resident enterprises outside China. The WFOE's revenue-based financing activity (ISIC 6499) is a financial service normally taxable at 6% and is not automatically zero-rated; cross-border royalty components may qualify only if the royalty is tied to a licensable IP asset and is supported by documentary evidence that the royalty is supplied to a non-resident without economic use inside China.

China mandates real-time fiscal clearance through the STA's National Unified Electronic Invoice Platform (digital electronic invoices) and the e-Tax electronic taxation system. General VAT taxpayers must issue invoices via the approved digital U-Key/certificate environment; each invoice must carry the STA-issued anti-fraud digital stamp with a mandatory 2D QR code before issuance. Invoice issuance data, including voids and corrections, is transmitted to the STA in near real time using API/EDI machine-to-machine interfaces; the STA's rules engine performs automated cross-check of invoice inventory, buyer identity, and applicable VAT rates before a valid invoice can be delivered. Special VAT invoices used for input credits are reconciled through the STA's nationwide invoice check-and-verify database.

The STA matches customs release data, logistics information, electronic VAT invoice flow, and foreign-exchange repatriation records; any mismatch in exporter identity, consignee, invoice amount, or receipt timing is escalated for audit. Where export zero-rating is unsubstantiated or found fraudulent, the STA disallows the zero-rate, recomputes VAT at the applicable 6% or 13% rate, demands repayment of refunded VAT plus overdue interest, and imposes administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Falsified customs declarations or forged export documents constitute tax fraud or customs smuggling triggering criminal liability for the legal representative and responsible persons, forfeiture of export-tax-rebate qualification, inclusion in the tax risk blacklist, closure of special VAT invoice issuance, and retrospective clawback for the full statutory tax assessment period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with separated current account and capital account convertibility. Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade, services, royalties, and profit remittances, subject to genuine transaction verification through domestic banks and SAFE reporting. Capital account transactions remain restricted; foreign direct investment in a WFOE requires FDI registration with SAFE or delegated banks, and the foreign currency capital 注入 must be tracked through a capital account in specialized settlement supervision. The conversion of registered capital into RMB is permitted but must be used in a true commercial context and cannot be used for unauthorized equity investments, on-lending to third parties, or speculative FX activity. Profit remittances to the offshore parent are permitted after annual audited financial statements, tax filing, and a tax clearance certificate have been obtained, with the dividend payment remitted through a current account and supported by board resolutions and distribution documentation. Non-resident investors must retain evidence of source of funds and maintain a clear audit trail from the overseas bank through the capital escrow account to the operational RMB account. Overall, the system favours legitimate commercial flows but demands robust documentary discipline, and any deviation from approved scope or purpose codes triggers enhanced review by the domestic custodian bank and may be escalated to SAFE for anti-money-laundering and cross-border data verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished the mandatory export proceeds surrender system in 2012; therefore there is no statutory conversion ratio requiring enterprises to sell a fixed portion of foreign currency receipts. However, export related proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system within 180 days from the date of customs declaration, unless SAFE specifically approves an extended overseas retention period. Once repatriated, the WFOE may either retain the foreign currency in an export proceeds FX account or voluntarily convert it into RMB at a licensed bank in accordance with current account authenticity rules. The bank will verify contracts, customs declarations, invoices, and logistics documentation to ensure the timing and amount are consistent with the declared trade flow. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline can lead to SAFE penalties, suspended FX business qualification, and compulsory audit of the enterprise's cross-border receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility for pure-play offshore fintech EMI is Low / Not Viable onshore. PBOC and SAFE do not license foreign or unregulated electronic money institutions to maintain deposit-taking or cross-border clearing accounts for domestic Chinese legal entities. A WFOE must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with a licenced domestic commercial bank within China. Offshore EMIs cannot legally accept the WFOE's CNY operating receipts, provide CNH clearing, or perform the mandatory SAFE reporting and capital verification functions. At best, offshore EMIs may act as ancillary distribution rails for overseas payments outside mainland China, but such usage is unattractive for this treasury structure because it creates audit gaps, transfer pricing complexity, and heightened AML scrutiny at the domestic custodian bank. The prudent approach is to direct all inbound capital injections and major cross-border trade settlements through the Tier-1 domestic bank network, with EMI usage limited to non-core foreign vendor payments only if fully disclosed and documented.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound share capital contributions must be remitted through SWIFT MT103 from the offshore investor's bank to the WFOE's dedicated capital escrow account at a SAFE-licensed domestic bank. The payment reference must state the FDI purpose code established at the time of SAFE registration, together with the WFOE name and the contract or filing number. On receipt, the bank will verify the remitter identity, the stated purpose code, and the documentation evidencing the investment contract. A PRC registered certified public accountant must then issue a capital verification report confirming the full paid-in amount and currency conversion. Where the investor contributes in a non-RMB currency, the bank carries out conversion to RMB through the domestic interbank FX market at a managed price, and the funds are subsequently transferred to the Basic Deposit Account only for genuine working capital and operating expenditure. Any delay between capital entry and deployment must be reconciled with the submitted business plan and cannot be left unfunded or repatriated without specific SAFE approval.For trade-related settlements, the WFOE should use standard bank-mediated clearing rails: direct SWIFT MT103 transfers for service contracts, stewarded documentary collections for open-account trade, and commercial letters of credit where shipment finance is required. Each disbursement must be matched against the underlying contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration, and logistics evidence, with the purpose code accurately reflecting the underlying transaction. Royalty and financing return payments to the overseas parent must be supported by tax withholding receipts, transfer pricing documentation, and audited computation statements. The domestic bank will execute the payment only after authenticity verification and may require pre-approval from the SAFE or a qualified financial institution if the remittance is unusual in amount or frequency. For large or repetitive flows, the WFOE should maintain an approved annual payment plan with its relationship bank to reduce friction and avoid triggering systemic AML alerts.Deliverable FX forward contracts with a SAFE-licensed domestic bank are permissible for genuine current account or verified capital account exposures, but only where the WFOE can provide underlying transaction documentary evidence and no speculative position is created.Cross-currency swaps are permissible where they hedge the currency risk of an underlying cross-border borrowing or investment exposure and the transaction is registered or reported to SAFE by the domestic bank under the relevant foreign debt or capital account framework.FX options can be used for hedging of projected royalty and trade flows, with additional documentation required to show a reasonable linkage to the company's operational currency mismatch and expected cash flow volumes.Offshore non-deliverable forwards are not permissible onshore for the WFOE as the domestic RMB market does not allow direct NDF settlement; should the parent attempt to hedge through CNH NDFs offshore, the onshore entity must clearly isolate that hedge from the operational WFOE treasury and document that it does not create an unapproved synthetic offshore derivative for the Chinese entity.Any structured solution involving both forward and swap components must first be reviewed by the relation bank's compliance and valuation team to confirm it violates no SAFE prohibition on speculative derivative participation and is consistent with the company's business model under ISIC 6499.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility for pure-play offshore fintech EMI is Low / Not Viable onshore. PBOC and SAFE do not license foreign or unregulated electronic money institutions to maintain deposit-taking or cross-border clearing accounts for domestic Chinese legal entities. A WFOE must maintain its primary operational and capital accounts with a licenced domestic commercial bank within China. Offshore EMIs cannot legally accept the WFOE's CNY operating receipts, provide CNH clearing, or perform the mandatory SAFE reporting and capital verification functions. At best, offshore EMIs may act as ancillary distribution rails for overseas payments outside mainland China, but such usage is unattractive for this treasury structure because it creates audit gaps, transfer pricing complexity, and heightened AML scrutiny at the domestic custodian bank. The prudent approach is to direct all inbound capital injections and major cross-border trade settlements through the Tier-1 domestic bank network, with EMI usage limited to non-core foreign vendor payments only if fully disclosed and documented.

10% statutory withholding income tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise without a China permanent establishment, unless a bilateral tax treaty reduces the rate (e.g., often 5% for qualifying shareholders holding at least 25% of the WFOE capital).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is statutorily required when annual related-party transactions in a single category exceed: (i) RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; (ii) RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers such as royalties and license fees; (iii) RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers such as loans and financing instruments; and (iv) RMB 40 million for services or other transactions. Master File is additionally required for groups with one-year related-party transactions or combined related-party gross assets/revenue of RMB 550 million or more; Country-by-Country reporting applies to parent groups with consolidated group revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime like offshore financial centres, but the tax authorities enforce substance-over-form through the General Anti-Abuse Rule under CIT Law Article 47 and its Implementing Regulations, together with transfer pricing rules and DTA anti-abuse / Principal Purpose Test provisions. A royalty financing WFOE must conduct Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, including credit underwriting, investment decision-making, funding negotiation, risk management, treasury management, accounting, and substantive intellectual property and financial asset ownership, with real personnel, office, board functions, and records in China. If the WFOE is a booking centre without CIGA, tax authorities may recharacterize its transactions, deny treaty benefits, and deem non-resident recipients taxable on Chinese-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False CIT Reporting of Understated Revenue / Inflated Cross-Border Deductions

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 63, the tax authority orders payment of the evaded tax, interest, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax; false invoicing cases attract Criminal Law Article 205 penalties; under CIT Law Articles 41, 48 and General Anti-Abuse Rule Article 47, transfer pricing and recharacterization adjustments increase taxable income; criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and financial officers who directed or knowingly participated in false reporting may be jointly and severally liable to restore corporate assets and pay the tax, interest, and penalties under PRC Company Law Article 23 and tort principles. Courts may pierce the corporate veil, attach directors' personal assets, impose exit restrictions/exit bans under TCAL Article 44, place the entity and the director on the serious-tax-violation blacklist, and impose criminal imprisonment for up to seven years for tax evasion.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Dividend, Interest and Royalty WHT / Unapproved DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 69, the withholding agent (the WFOE) is fined 50% to 300% of the unwithheld or unhidden tax; Article 32 imposes 0.05%/day late surcharge; tax authorities may recover the tax from the non-resident payee, recharacterize the payment as a dividend, disallow the deduction, and apply CIT Law Article 47 anti-abuse adjustments. Criminal exposure is possible where failure to withhold is a component of systematic tax fraud under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 203.

Director personal liability: Directors and chief financial officers who deliberately omitted withholding or authorized unlawful treaty claims may be held personally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties under piercing-the-veil theory; they may suffer civil asset restitution, being placed on the tax black list, exit/prohibition from leaving China under TCAL Article 44, and criminal imprisonment up to 7 years if evasion or fraudulent means are proven.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Conduit Financing and AML Risk Enhancement

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule under CIT Law Article 47 to recharacterize purported financing and royalty agreements, deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, and impose domestic WHT of 10% on deemed dividends; the WFOE may also face TP adjustments of 50% to 300% penalties; the AML supervisor can impose administrative fines and revoke financial licenses under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law; if the structure facilitates concealment, the criminal provisions under Criminal Law Articles 191 and 312 carry confiscation and imprisonment up to ten years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly liable to creditors and tax authorities for abuse of corporate form, with civil recovery against personal assets under PRC Company Law Article 23; travel bans, asset freezes, and blacklisting may be applied; if a director knowingly uses a shell/conduit to move disguised tax-evaded funds, he or she faces criminal liability for money laundering or tax evasion with imprisonment from 3 to 10 years, in severe cases up to life for extraordinarily large amounts.
