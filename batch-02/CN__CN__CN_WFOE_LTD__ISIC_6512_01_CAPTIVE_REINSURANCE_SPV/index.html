Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office, commercial mailbox, or shared coworking address will be rejected for the captive reinsurance SPV because the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the local tax authorities treat the registered address as a substantive establishment indicator. The WFOE must lease or own a physical office address in China that is distinct from any post-office box. Local tax (State Taxation Administration) and SAMR authorities require formal lease agreements, property ownership certificates, or landlord permission letters and will reject mailbox addresses as insufficient business substance. Banks will likewise decline to open a basic deposit account or foreign-exchange capital account for a captive reinsurance entity registered at a virtual office, due to the money-laundering risk of letterbox companies. The registered address must be maintained continuously; any change requires filing with SAMR and updating the business license and the tax registration within 30 days.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No mandatory minimum paid-up capital for the general WFOE shell under the Company Law, but the captive reinsurance license under the Insurance Law requires the Company to have a minimum paid-in registered capital of RMB 200,000,000, fully paid in cash and maintained in a dedicated regulatory escrow/solvency account. The capital must be deposited in a PRC-licensed bank acceptable to NFRA and cannot be withdrawn, released, pledged or applied except for reinsurance reserves, claim payments and regulatory solvency requirements with prior NFRA approval.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to industrial operations; however, the company must obtain a fire safety acceptance certificate and occupancy safety certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department for its leased office premises; employee occupational exposure limits are irrelevant because no physical work process exists, but fire evacuation drills and business continuity planning are mandatory for NFRA licensing.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing through the official E-Tax Bureau portal (Electronic Tax Bureau) of the State Taxation Administration

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the WFOE at the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the SAMR issues a business license containing an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax filings. Step 2 - The SAMR incorporation data is automatically shared with the competent local tax bureau, completing the basic tax registration without a separate physical application. Step 3 - Within 30 calendar days of incorporation, the WFOE must log into the E-Tax Bureau portal, complete supplementary taxpayer records (bank settlement account, accounting standards, ISIC industry code, and VAT-regime selection), and confirm whether it is a general or small-scale VAT taxpayer. Step 4 - For a foreign non-resident enterprise that is not incorporated in China but earns China-source reinsurance premiums or other income, the foreign entity must complete a one-time non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority where the payer or withholding agent is located, submitting its foreign certificate of incorporation, board resolution, power of attorney, reinsurance agreement, and a substantive economic-benefit explanation. Step 5 - Upon successful review, the tax authority issues the electronic tax registration record; thereafter the WFOE uses its USCC for all VAT and corporate income tax compliance, including quarterly and annual filings through the electronic portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China's VAT zero-rating (0%) applies to goods physically exported out of Chinese customs territory and to the narrow statutory list of cross-border services enumerated in the Catalogue of Cross-Border Services Zero-Rated or Exempt from VAT (Caishui [2016] No. 36, as amended). For goods, the exporter must produce an export customs declaration, evidence of actual shipment by a licensed transport carrier, matching commercial documents, and verified receipt of foreign currency from abroad; input VAT on the export supply is refunded. Captive reinsurance and risk-underwriting services are not included in the zero-rating catalogue; captive reinsurance premiums sourced from mainland Chinese cedants are subject to VAT at the standard 6% rate, while reinsurance services supplied to an overseas recipient or for purely offshore risks are generally VAT-exempt (not zero-rated), with no entitlement to input VAT refund. The captive SPV cannot therefore zero-rate its premiums; the only export benefit available is the VAT exemption, which blocks input credits, unless the transaction falls within an approved offshore digital-services or technology-based risk-management category supplied to a foreign affiliate.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form generated through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Authenticated bill of lading or airway bill showing physical discharge from Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher issued by the designated foreign-exchange bank (inward remittance receipt equivalent)
  • SAFE Trade-in-Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System transaction record proving timely repatriation of export proceeds

The State Taxation Administration operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV with mandatory full digital electronic invoices for all registered VAT taxpayers. VAT invoices must be issued through the official E-Invoice Platform, which assigns each invoice an encrypted universal invoice code, a digitally signed QR code, and clears invoice data in near real time to the central tax authority database. Large taxpayers, including financial services entities, must integrate their ERP or accounting systems with the E-Invoice Platform either through an approved tax-control device or through the official full-digital e-invoice API. Only fully authenticated and cleared e-invoices give the recipient the right to deduct input VAT; the digital signature and QR code constitute the government-mandated anti-fraud stamp, and the Golden Tax engine automatically flags mismatches between invoice issuance, payment flows, and VAT returns.

The State Taxation Administration and China Customs conduct joint desk-based and on-site audits comparing customs export declarations, bill of lading dates, freight manifests, bank foreign-exchange receipts, and VAT invoice flows. If any document is missing, contradictory, or the data analytics engine flags a circular or non-genuine export transaction, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating and retrospectively claw back the entire refunded VAT amount, plus late-payment interest calculated from the original refund date. Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law, fraudulent export refund claims are subject to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the amount involved; under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, deliberate export-refund fraud is a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment for responsible individuals. Additionally, the exporter loses its favorable tax-risk rating and may be suspended from the export VAT refund regime for up to 36 months, with all future refund claims subjected to mandatory physical document review before any payment is released.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade and service payments, dividends for approved outward remittances, and interest payments on foreign debt registered with SAFE. Capital account items, however, remain heavily administered. Foreign direct investment into a WFOE must be registered via SAFE or through banks under the SAFE delegated system, with the foreign exchange capital injection made in RMB or a foreign currency under the capital account. Capital contributed in excess of the registered amount generally cannot be credited unless the registration is amended. Profit remittances are permitted after annual audit and tax clearance, subject to a bank-provided verification of board resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and SAFE records. There are no confiscatory surrender requirements for export receipts, but enterprises must repatriate current account FX into China and either retain it in a foreign currency account or convert it at a bank at the prevailing market rate. A captive reinsurance SPV will also require a NFRA license and its premium collections and reserve balances must be held in designated accounts, reinforcing the classification of RMB as current-account free but capital-account conditional.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of collection as reported to SAFE; no fixed percentage is surrendered, but the bank may settle at market rate or hold in a foreign currency account until it is used. Mandatory conversion ratios are not applied in the current account operation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Not viable. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding income tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise. Under certain bilateral Double Tax Agreements (DTAs), the rate may be reduced to 5% when the non-resident shareholder directly holds at least 25% of the Chinese company's shares and satisfies beneficial ownership conditions. A 5% reduced treaty rate is common under China's treaties with Hong Kong, Singapore, Luxembourg, and the United Kingdom (subject to limitation-on-benefits clauses).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Quarterly provisional CIT returns and prepayments are due within 15 days after the end of each calendar quarter. The annual CIT settlement return is due within 5 months after the fiscal year-end (for a calendar year taxpayer, by May 31 of the following year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A related-party transaction Local File is mandatory in China when annual related-party transactions exceed any of the following thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (including reinsurance services, commission, claims management, and allocation of shared costs). A Master File is required when the entire group's related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion in the tax year. The captive reinsurance entity will almost certainly be required to prepare both the Chinese Local File and a Country-by-Country Report if the group is a G20-headquartered MNE with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the UAE or OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a separate statutory instrument; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 45 (Controlled Foreign Company rules) allow the tax authority to recharacterise a captive reinsurer lacking real economic substance. The taxpayer must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China: active underwriting decision-making, risk assessment and pricing, claims adjustment, reinsurance reserve management, actuarial modelling, asset-liability management, and investment of reserves by locally employed risk professionals. If these activities are performed in the offshore parent or a Hong Kong agent, the Chinese captive's profit allocation will be adjusted and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unauthorised Insurance / Reinsurance Business (Regulatory Red Flag)

Statutory sanction: Article 159 of the Insurance Law of the PRC: confiscation of illegal premium income, closure of the illegal business, and a fine of 1 to 3 times the illegal income; if the illegal income is less than RMB 1 million, a fine of RMB 100,000 to RMB 1 million is imposed. The NFRA may additionally blacklist the entity and disqualify officers from holding senior insurance positions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve unlicensed underwriting may be held personally civilly liable for the unpaid claims of insured parties. Under Article 225 of the Criminal Law of the PRC (Illegal Business Operations), the responsible persons may be sentenced to up to 5 years' imprisonment and a fine, or up to 10 years plus fines if held for particularly serious circumstances. Directors are subject to travel bans (exit denial) and asset freezing by the cross-border financial enforcement authorities.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Cross-Border Profit Stripping (Tax Evasion)

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 114 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law: the tax authority recharacterises the transaction, adjusts taxable income, and imposes an additional tax of 10%-25% on the shifted profit. Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, the underpaid tax is subject to a penalty of 50% to 500% plus daily surcharge of 0.05%. The adjustment can also trigger withholding tax on deemed dividends to the non-resident parent.

Director personal liability: The directors who executed the transfer pricing scheme may be personally liable for restitution of the underpaid tax under the 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine in Article 20 of the Company Law. Criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law (Tax Evasion) is triggered when the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax due, leading to imprisonment up to 7 years and fines equal to 1 to 5 times the evaded amount. Directors are placed on the STA's 'Unreliable List' and are prohibited from leaving China until the tax is settled.
Shell Company / Empty Substance and Treaty Abuse (AML And Anti-Treaty Shopping Red Flag)

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (GAAR) permits the tax authority to disregard the entity entirely, deny the treaty benefits, reallocate income to the real risk taker, and impose a 25% CIT on the deemed Chinese-source profit. Additionally, the beneficial ownership defence fails, and the statutory 10% withholding tax is applied to all outbound payment without treaty reduction. If the shell is used for cross-border money laundering, the AML Law of the PRC imposes heavy fines up to RMB 10 million and revocation of the financial licence.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow a shell entity to mask ownership and avoid tax are subject to unlimited civil liability for the company's tax debts when the corporate veil is pierced under Article 20 of the Company Law. Criminal liability under Article 191 of the Criminal Law (Money Laundering) carries up to 10 years' imprisonment for proceeds of crime, and under Article 201 of the Criminal Law (Tax Evasion) up to 7 years' imprisonment. The directors, in practice, are blacklisted by the STA, face a cross-border travel ban, and their personal assets in China and abroad may be frozen through rogatory letters under the OECD Convention on Mutual Administrative Assistance in Tax Matters.
