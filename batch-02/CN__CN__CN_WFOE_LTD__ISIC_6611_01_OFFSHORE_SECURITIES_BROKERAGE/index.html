Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on the WFOE as an incorporation matter under the Company Law and Foreign Investment Law, and there is no blocked escrow account requirement. However, the licensed securities brokerage activity under ISIC 6611 involves the PRC Securities Law and CSRC administrative licensing rules, which require paid-in registered capital of at least RMB 50 million for brokerage-only authorization and higher capital where underwriting, proprietary trading, or asset management is added. The equity contribution must be remitted from abroad into a Chinese bank capital account, registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and fully paid-in before CSRC license certification.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) and its electronic tax bureau at the E-Tax portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn); the STA local branch of the WFOE's registered address exercises territorial jurisdiction and the national VAT invoice service platform administers e-invoicing clearing.

E-government filing portal

For the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is legally the taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the STA through the E-Tax portal by submitting the articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identity materials, investor certificate of incorporation or passport, and bank account details. After real-name authentication and STA review, the USCC is activated in the taxpayer database and the VAT taxpayer status (General VAT Taxpayer versus Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer) is determined. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment is instead registered through the STA non-resident tax registration process and, where no permanent establishment exists, is represented by a withholding agent rather than receiving a USCC.

Available election forms

None. China does not allow check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections; the WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a Chinese resident enterprise at entity level, although VAT general versus small-scale taxpayer registration is elective within the statutory turnover thresholds.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every established entity must obtain VAT registration immediately; annual taxable turnover of RMB 5,000,000 or more triggers mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration, while below that threshold the WFOE may remain a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer or elect General VAT Taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A WFOE may claim the 0% export VAT rate on offshore securities brokerage and qualifying digital financial services only if the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual, the service is performed for and consumed entirely outside China, no Chinese taxable service element is embedded, and the brokerage consideration is received in foreign currency. General VAT taxpayer registration, a written offshore service agreement, client offshore status evidence and foreign exchange receipt are mandatory; otherwise the 6% VAT rate applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) — required only where the transaction includes physical export of media, documents or tangible deliverables; pure offshore brokerage services do not require customs clearance.
  • Bill of Lading or airway waybill — required only for physical exports; not applicable to purely electronic or intangible securities brokerage services.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange repatriation voucher showing the offshore brokerage fee was received in foreign currency onshore.
  • Offshore service contract or master agreement clearly identifying the non-resident client, the fee structure, and the overseas place of performance and consumption.
  • Certificate or evidential file proving the client's non-resident legal status (overseas business registration, tax residence certificate, or passport for individuals).
  • Internal service completion and consumption log demonstrating that the brokerage execution, trade matching and advisory elements were carried out outside China.

Mandatory use of the STA fully digitalized e-invoice system and Golden Tax Phase IV controls. VAT invoices for brokerage services must be issued online through the E-Tax portal, carry a unique national invoice number and anti-counterfeiting QR/digital stamp, and be transmitted in real time to the STA for fiscal clearance before being delivered to the client; offline manual invoicing and private invoice printing are prohibited for this sector.

Upon audit, the STA may retrospectively disallow the 0% export treatment if documentation is incomplete, the client is determined to be a Chinese tax resident, or the service is deemed consumed in China. The consequences are immediate reversal to 6% output VAT, clawback of any input VAT refunded, daily late-payment interest of 0.05% on the unpaid tax, and fines from 50% to 500% of the unpaid or fraudulently refunded amount under the Law on Tax Collection and Administration (including Article 66 export refund fraud penalty of one to five times the refund fraudulently obtained). Deliberate falsification of export documents exposes the entity and responsible officers to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law; in fraud cases the statute of limitations is unlimited, whereas ordinary under-assessment is generally limited to five years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated foreign exchange system. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties, and interest remitted to nonresidents, are generally open and convertible upon presentation of authentic underlying commercial documents and compliance documents such as invoices, contracts, tax payment certificates, and audited financial statements. The capital account, however, remains heavily controlled: inbound foreign direct investment requires SAFE filing or registration, and the WFOE must open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a designated onshore bank. Foreign share capital can be converted into Renminbi through the capital account for approved operating purposes, but cannot be used freely for equity investment, entrusted loans, or speculative investment outside the registered business scope. Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days of the export declaration, but there is no general mandatory conversion ratio, allowing the WFOE to retain foreign currency or convert at market rates. The main profit remittance hurdles are the bank's necessity to conduct enhanced due diligence on the distribution of profits, require proof that corporate income tax on the distributed profits has been settled, verify that the audited net profits are genuine, and ensure that dividends booked by a securities brokerage entity are not a disguised capital flight vehicle. In practice, SAFE and the onshore bank will contest any remittance where the WFOE shows volatility in revenues inconsistent with licensed brokerage activities or where the offshore beneficial owners cannot provide a proven source of wealth and funds. Tax treaty claims require a Chinese tax residency certificate and beneficial ownership documentation before reduced withholding rates are applied. Overall, current account convertibility is workable, but capital account transactions demand rigorous pre-registration, purpose coding, and post-remittance documentation reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's foreign exchange administration regulations, export foreign currency receipts must be credited to the domestic foreign exchange settlement account of the WFOE within 180 days of the export customs declaration. No statutory fixed conversion ratio applies, so 100% of received export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at the market rate through the designated onshore bank. An extension of the 180-day collection period is only available for legitimate commercial reasons and requires bank documentation and SAFE reporting through the trade monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with operational foreign exchange authority delegated to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions are legally underwhelming for this jurisdiction and entity type. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Wise, and Payoneer are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to maintain RMB clearing accounts, issue domestic bank account numbers, or provide statutory CNY settlement services to a Mainland WFOE. These providers are also prohibited from completing SAFE foreign exchange registrations, collecting export proceeds, or acting as the receiving bank for FDI capital injections and profit remittances. A foreign EMI can only support non-core ancillary flows such as customer conversion at an offshore holding level, and only where the foreign EMI itself is licensed in the jurisdiction where the WFOE's parent operates. As the primary treasury and clearing rail, an EMI is infeasible because the WFOE will fail the regulator's requirement that all current and capital account FX transactions be cleared through financial institutions subject to PBOC supervision and SAFE data reporting. Viability rating: Low from the Mainland WFOE perspective; medium only for offshore payment acceptance at group treasury level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For annual related-party transactions > RMB 10 million (approximately USD 1.4 million) for tangible goods, > RMB 10 million for financial transfers, > RMB 40 million for other transactions (e.g., services, royalties, interest), and > RMB 2 billion for total related-party turnover (the latter triggers Master File and Country-by-Country Report); local file required above RMB 100 million in related-party income or > RMB 10 million in one category. Even below thresholds, a contemporaneous TP policy is recommended for a securities brokerage due to high-value intangibles (customer lists, algorithms, brand) and intercompany payments.

Economic substance scope: China applies a general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under EITL Article 47 and the Special Tax Adjustments (Circular 6, 2016) requiring that a non-resident affiliate or offshore intermediate company has substantial business activity (e.g., actual employees, premises, risks substantiated). For the WFOE itself, Chinese tax authorities will challenge entities with no real business in China—merely holding licenses or contracts—under the 'substance over form' doctrine. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of a securities brokerage include client onboarding, order execution, risk management, and portfolio management; if such activities are performed outside China, the China-sourced income allocation may be reduced, but the WFOE must demonstrate actual decision-making and risk control in China to justify its taxable profit. For offshore tax-jurisdiction parent companies, Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) from the jurisdiction (e.g., BVI, Cayman) may also apply, but in China the focus is on preventing artificial shifting of profit to low-taxed affiliates; the WFOE is expected to file Schedule A105090 and support the arm's length allocation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under EITL Article 6 and Article 109; the tax authority may assess additional tax of up to 25% plus 0.05% daily interest; administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law (PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, 2021 Revision).

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil where the WFOE is used as a personal vehicle to divert profits: directors and actual controllers are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under Article 20 of the Company Law (if fraud or misuse of corporate form is proved). Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may include up to 7 years' imprisonment and unpaid fines; in severe cases, the director's personal assets may be frozen and seized; a travel ban (‘出境限制’) may be imposed under Article 28 of the Exit-Entry Administration Law if the unpaid tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and enforcement is frustrated; a blacklisting by the State Administration for Market Regulation also bars the director from serving in any new enterprise for 3 years.
Failure to Withhold or Remit Non-Resident WHT (Dividend, Interest, or Royalty)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 37 of EITL and Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law: a penalty of 50%-300% of the amount of tax not withheld, and the WFOE is ordered to remit the unpaid amount; the non-resident may be chased for the tax as a secondary liability; daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% accrues from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve the remittance without withholding are deemed personally liable for the shortfall under Article 14 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law (joint liability where the enterprise is dissolved or stripped of assets). If the director signed a false declaration of treaty eligibility, criminal liability for tax fraud under Article 201 may apply: imprisonment of up to 7 years plus confiscation of assets. A court may issue a property preservation order against the director's personal bank accounts, and a restriction on exit may be imposed until full payment is secured.
Illegal Cross-Border Securities Brokerage / Shell Company / AML Violation

Statutory sanction: For unlicensed securities dealings: the CSRC may confiscate illegal gains and impose a fine of 1-10 times the illegal gain (Article 202 Securities Law); if the revenue is deemed illegal, the tax authorities may reclassify it and charge CIT plus penalties of up to 5 times the evaded tax. For AML failure: the People's Bank of China may impose a regulatory penalty up to RMB 5 million on the entity and RMB 1 million on responsible individuals, plus suspension of business under Article 53 AML Law.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is available under Article 20 Company Law where the WFOE is a sham: directors and actual controllers bear unlimited personal liability for all debts, including tax and regulatory penalties. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 175 (illegal operations) may lead to 5-10 years' imprisonment for serious cases; Article 191 (money laundering) carries up to 10 years' imprisonment and a fine. Seizure of personal assets, freezing of personal bank accounts, and a travel ban are standard enforcement tools. Individuals found guilty of securities fraud can be blacklisted from any listed company directorship for life, and are restricted from leaving China.
