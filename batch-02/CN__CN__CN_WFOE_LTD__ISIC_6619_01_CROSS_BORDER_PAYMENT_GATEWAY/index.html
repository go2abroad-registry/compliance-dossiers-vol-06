Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and through provincial-level electronic tax bureaus; local competent tax authority is the district/county STA office for the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign shareholders incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License and Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the national legal entity identifier. Step 2: Within 30 days of business license issuance, complete tax identification registration by using the USCC and legal representative credentials on the provincial e-Tax portal or at the competent STA office; this registration creates the taxpayer profile and binds the USCC as the TIN for all STA filings. Step 3: Complete VAT taxpayer registration, selecting status under the VAT regime (initially small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), electronic invoice quota, and invoice issuance method. Step 4: Register for payroll/social insurance withholding, corporate income tax, withholding income tax on non-resident shareholders, stamp duty, and any service-related taxes. Step 5: Report bank account details to the STA within 15 days after account opening. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxed on worldwide net income at a 25% statutory CIT rate; there is no pass-through treatment; dividends distributed to offshore shareholders are separately subject to Chinese withholding at 10%, subject to applicable double tax treaty relief.

Available election forms

None for check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. China has no elective pass-through regime for a limited liability company; a WFOE is mandatorily classified as a Chinese resident entity-level taxpayer. Under tax treaties, the WFOE may apply for treaty benefits as a PRC resident and beneficial owner with respect to its foreign parent, but this is a treaty eligibility determination, not a domestic entity-classification election. No consolidated or fiscal unity election is available for related limited liability companies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.06%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold; however, any Chinese entity carrying on taxable activities is required to complete VAT basic registration from commencement of business regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers with current monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 or quarterly sales up to RMB 300,000 may qualify for a VAT exemption, but that temporary exemption does not displace the registration obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For cross-border services, including payment gateway and merchant acquiring services, zero-rating meaning a 0% VAT rate with input VAT credit or refund is available only where the service falls within the STA cross-border zero-rate catalogue, including certain international transportation, research and development, technology, software, and information services, and all statutory conditions are satisfied. For payment and clearing services supplied to an overseas bank, acquirer, payment network, or foreign financial institution and wholly consumed outside mainland China, the usual treatment is cross-border VAT exemption without input-VAT refund, rather than zero-rating, unless the WFOE can establish that the service qualifies under the zero-rated catalogue provision for its specific service code. To claim zero-rating or cross-border exemption, the WFOE must demonstrate that: (a) the service recipient is a non-resident enterprise or individual located outside China; (b) the service is not used for domestic onshore settlement, domestic payment acceptance, or domestic trade facilitation; (c) the cross-border service agreement, transaction logs, payment gateway fee statements, and invoices clearly identify the offshore principal and the foreign-currency consideration; (d) the revenue is booked as cross-border service export revenue and included in SAFE trade-in-services monitoring; and (e) the digital e-invoice is issued with the correct cross-border zero-rate or exemption label and the relevant statutory contract number where required.

  • Customs Export Declaration is not applicable to cross-border payment gateway services; no physical goods Bill of Entry or Bill of Lading is required for service exports.
  • Signed cross-border payment gateway service agreement between the WFOE and the overseas bank, acquirer, payment network, or foreign financial institution, clearly defining service scope, fees, and destination of service consumption.
  • Foreign exchange collection voucher from a Chinese bank, equivalent to an inward remittance certificate, or the SAFE trade-in-services export collection record evidencing the actual receipt of offshore fees from the foreign counterparty.
  • Commercial invoice or full digital electronic invoice carrying the cross-border zero-rate or exemption status label and the relevant cross-border service code, where exemption or zero-rating is claimed.
  • Payment transaction logs and monthly merchant settlement reports showing that the gateway services are provided to foreign financial institutions for offshore transactions and are not facilitating domestic Chinese payment acceptance.
  • Certificate of foreign residence or identity of the non-resident service recipient, and, if treaty benefits are claimed, a beneficial ownership declaration or tax-residence certificate of the foreign party.

Under Golden Tax Phase IV (Jin Shui IV) and the national e-Invoice Service Platform, all VAT invoices, including fully digital electronic invoices, must be issued in real time through the STA-controlled e-invoice system using API or web-based issuance; invoices carry a unique 20-digit invoice code, a dynamic QR code, and an encrypted digital signature. VAT special invoices are subject to quota and must be matched and reconciled through the STA VAT Invoice Management System before the buyer can claim input VAT credit. The system performs real-time invoice data upload, fiscal clearance, purchase and sale anomaly scanning, and anti-fraud controls on high-risk taxpayers; pre-printed paper blank invoices are no longer permissible for fully digitalized taxpayers.

The STA will scrutinize zero-rating and cross-border exemption claims through Golden Tax Phase IV data analytics, matching the cross-border service contract, digital e-invoice labels, SAFE foreign exchange receipts, bank settlement records, and the non-resident status of the overseas beneficiary. Failure to substantiate the export nature of the service or any material misrepresentation triggers: (i) immediate removal of the zero-rate or exemption treatment and reclassification as a domestic taxable payment service at 6% VAT; (ii) mandatory repayment of all VAT input credits claimed or refunded, plus late-payment interest from the original filing date; (iii) a civil penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law; (iv) criminal exposure for fraudulent export tax refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law where wilful deception or fictitious cross-border contracts are established; and (v) SAFE monetary penalties and potential revocation of cross-border payment or foreign-exchange qualification. The statutory clawback period may extend to 10 years for fraudulent and false export-refund and zero-rating claims.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with a dual-track currency control framework. Renminbi is unconditionally convertible on current account items such as trade in goods and services, dividends, and interest, provided the underlying transaction is commercial and backed by valid documentation. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment (FDI), intercompany loans, equity transfers, and outward remittances for acquisitions, are subject to filing, registration, or approval with SAFE. A foreign-owned payment gateway entity will experience friction when moving large inbound equity injections and outbound service fee settlements: every remittance must be matched to a registered contract, invoice, and tax clearance certificate. Surrender or repatriation of export proceeds is no longer mandatory; however, all foreign currency receipts must be lodged with a designated domestic bank in the foreign exchange account regime and can be retained, converted to RMB on demand, or used to pay imported inputs within the verified scope of the trade credit system. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders requires a prior year audited financial statement, corporate income tax settlement certificate, and board resolution declaring dividends, and the entire process is subject to anti-money laundering and sanctions screening by the settlement bank. The SAFE system also applies periodic dynamic verification of business registers, and any deviation from the filed business scope will block funds flows. For a high-risk ISIC 6619 payment company, the bank will review the legal basis of each capital movement under the Payment Services Measures and the Cross-Border Payment Business guidelines, creating stringent know-your-transaction gateways before any conversion or disbursement is executed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 90 days of the stated shipment date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the statutory surrender requirement was abolished in 2007. However, all foreign exchange receipts must be reported and deposited into the exporter's designated foreign exchange settlement account, and any conversion into Renminbi must follow SAFE's real-transaction-background rules.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, or Currencycloud) are legally barred from directly onboarding a Chinese Mainland corporate entity, as non-bank payment service providers must hold a Payment Business Administrative License issued by the PBOC and join the NetsUnion or UnionPay clearing network. Any cross-border service must be delivered through a licensed Chinese bank or a licensed third-party payment institution (e.g., Alibaba's Alipay or Tencent's Tenpay) acting as a gateway. An offshore EMI may only provide ancillary technology or reporting solutions, never hold segregated client funds inside China. Feasibility rating: Low for direct vaulting and settlement; Medium for indirect API integration into a Chinese bank's foreign exchange and clearing infrastructure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation must be prepared when annual related-party tangible property transfers exceed CNY 200 million, intangible property or financial asset transfers exceed CNY 100 million, or other related-party transactions (service fees, royalties, interest) exceed CNY 40 million; Master File documentation is required where the MNE group's prior-year consolidated revenue exceeds CNY 2 billion and the Chinese entity has related-party transactions, or where annual related-party transactions reported by the Chinese entity exceed CNY 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the State Taxation Administration applies economic substance doctrine through the beneficial ownership test, substance-over-form principles, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in EIT Law Article 47. For a cross-border payment gateway WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in China, including merchant underwriting, KYC/AML controls, transaction monitoring, currency conversion decisions, settlement instructions, system risk management, and board-level decision-making; the entity must maintain local employees, office premises, business licences, bank settlement accounts, and payment scheme approvals sufficient to demonstrate that profits are economically connected to local activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 6, 41 and 47 permit the tax authority to recharacterise the arrangement under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, adjust taxable income on an arm's length basis, impose a 10% deemed dividend WHT, and apply late-payment interest. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes an additional penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and 0.05% daily late payment surcharges accrue on any adjusted tax.

Director personal liability: Chinese courts may pierce the corporate veil where directors deliberately operated the WFOE as a mere instrumentality and stripped assets; directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and creditor losses, subjected to asset seizure and travel restriction/blacklisting orders, and criminally indicted for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment in serious cases.
Transfer Pricing Fraud and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 empowers the tax authority to make a transfer pricing adjustment based on the best available data and to add interest; SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 requires contemporaneous documentation at the statutory thresholds. Under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 62 and 63, failure to provide required related-party documentation carries administrative fines of CNY 2,000 to CNY 10,000, while deliberate concealment of related-party transactions can be treated as tax evasion with penalties of 50% to 500% of the understated tax and criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party declaration filings may be personally investigated for aiding tax evasion, face civil restitution to the WFOE and minority shareholders, have their personal bank accounts frozen and exit visas blocked through the tax credit blacklisting system, and be imprisoned for up to 7 years if the scheme was used to deliberately defraud the state of material tax amounts.
Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation and Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The SAT will deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership rules in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in EIT Law Article 47; the under-withheld difference is collected as a 10% WHT deficiency plus daily interest. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 permits assessment of a 50% to 500% penalty on the tax evaded, and Article 201 of the Criminal Law applies where intentional non-withholding and false beneficial ownership certifications are used as a revenue fraud scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and the conduit entity may be jointly liable for the uncollected WHT, ordered to restore distributed assets, subjected to personal travel bans and blacklisting in China's tax credit system, and criminally prosecuted for fraudulent tax filings and false treaty claims; conviction can lead to serious criminal sanctions including imprisonment for tax evasion offenses, plus civil damages to the Chinese revenue authority and ultimate shareholders who suffered exposure from the abusive structure.
