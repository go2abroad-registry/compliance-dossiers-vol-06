Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The Company Law of the PRC imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE limited liability company. However, because the WFOE's ISIC 6619 electronic money institution business requires a People's Bank of China payment license, the company must satisfy the PBOC's special capital rule: a minimum registered paid-up capital of RMB 30,000,000 (approx. USD 4.2 million), deposited into a blocked escrow capital verification account. These funds must remain frozen and may only be released upon PBOC written consent or after the license application has been finally and lawfully settled. The foreign shareholder may otherwise subscribe for capital on a no-call basis, and the capital contribution timetable shall be set out in the Articles of Association to comply with the five-year statutory contribution rule for limited companies.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial/civil defense fire permit; however, the registered office must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate for office premises from the local Fire and Rescue Department, plus workplace occupancy certificate and routine occupational safety inspection. In exceptional cases, if the company maintains a server room, the server room must comply with NFPA/GB 50174 fire codes and obtain a Data Center Class A certification; otherwise, no civil defense permit is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing is conducted through the STA's national Electronic Tax Administration e-filing portal (e-Tax platform) and the local in-charge tax authority portals.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE entity, the tax identifier is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), an 18-digit number automatically assigned by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at incorporation; the one-stop SAMR-Tax registration service completes the initial tax registration, within 30 days after which the company must activate its tax file at the in-charge tax authority, apply for a tax UKey for VAT invoice issuance, confirm taxpayer categorization, and register payroll/social-security withholding. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available in the PRC. A WFOE is taxed as a resident enterprise on worldwide income at the statutory 25% corporate income tax rate. The only material status election is the application under the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law for a non-resident enterprise whose actual place of effective management is in China to be treated as a PRC resident enterprise; hybrid mismatch outcomes remain subject to general anti-avoidance rules, not elective regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers registration for a domestic WFOE, as all SAMR-incorporated entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of incorporation. The VAT structural threshold is only for taxpayer status: an entity is required to register as a general VAT taxpayer when its annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, and may voluntarily register below that threshold. A foreign non-resident supplier deriving China-sourced VAT-taxable turnover must be registered or have a withholding agent appointed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China may be zero-rated when they are physically declared to customs for export under the PRC Customs export formalities and the exporter subsequently obtains the tax authority's export VAT rebate. Cross-border services, including qualifying financial services provided to overseas counterparties, are zero-rated or VAT-exempt only when the service is clearly provided to a non-resident recipient, is wholly consumed outside China, and is supported by the place-of-supply and actual-delivery evidence contemplated in the cross-border service VAT rules. Domestic digital wallet reserve management, domestic payment initiation, and virtual IBAN issuance linked to China-resident users or China-sourced transactions are not zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration for Exported Goods completed and validated by the PRC Customs authorities
  • Bill of Lading or other transport evidence naming the exporter as shipper/consignor
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract or purchase order with the foreign buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds settlement receipt demonstrating receipt of foreign currency
  • For cross-border services: cross-border service contract, overseas recipient's certificate of residency, service completion/consumption reports, and bank foreign-exchange remittance documents

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled VAT Invoice Management System under the Golden Tax architecture, using the certified tax UKey. For the fully digitalized electronic invoice regime, invoice data are transmitted in near real time to the national electronic tax data platform through certified APIs or tax-authorized software; digital encryption and anti-fraud stamping are applied by the tax authority before an invoice is valid. Special VAT invoices, settlement vouchers, and payment clearing invoices issued by the licensed e-money institution must satisfy the same fiscal clearance and real-time reporting requirements.

Export zero-rating claims are audited by the STA in connection with China Customs export declarations, the VAT invoice database, and SAFE foreign-exchange monitoring. Any claim found to be unsupported, fraudulent, or failing to satisfy the documentation and foreign-exchange repatriation requirements is voided, the VAT refund or credit is clawed back, and a late payment surcharge applies at 0.05% per day from the due date. A false export or false invoice claim may trigger administrative penalties of 0.5 to 5 times the tax evaded, joint personal liability, inclusion in the national tax integrity blacklist, exit restrictions, and criminal prosecution under the PRC Criminal Law.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the People's Bank of China through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Under the current account, foreign exchange conversion for trade in goods and services is freely permitted provided the underlying transaction is genuine and documentary evidence is presented. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled and require prior registration or approval for foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and securities investment. Foreign exchange receipts from export trade generally are not subject to a mandatory conversion ratio; however, retained foreign currency above a bank-approved current account balance quota must be converted into RMB. Repatriation of export proceeds is statutorily required within 180 days of customs declaration. Profit remittance by a wholly foreign-owned enterprise is permissible, but in practice it demands a completed annual audit, tax clearance certificates from the State Taxation Administration, verification of capital contribution, and approval or filing under SAFE procedures. Therefore, while current account convertibility exists, the capital account remains restricted, and all cross-border transactions must be routed through a designated forex bank and supported by the underlying transaction documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days after the customs declaration date. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may retain foreign currency up to the limit approved by SAFE and the designated bank based on verified current account income, and any amount exceeding that limit must be settled to the bank in RMB. All settlement must be supported by genuine trade documentation and customs declaration data.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore and foreign digital EMI platforms are not licensed to issue payment instruments or hold client funds on behalf of an entity resident in Mainland China without a non-bank payment institution license issued by the People's Bank of China. Such platforms cannot be used as the primary operational account for treasury or liquidity purposes. At best, a foreign EMI can function as an ancillary payment gateway only through a PBOC-licensed Chinese partner, while the underlying WFOE treasury must remain anchored in a locally licensed commercial bank or a licensed third-party payment institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers to a SAFE-designated capital account maintained with a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The remittance shall state the foreign exchange purpose code for FDI capital injection and carry the foreign-invested enterprise registration information from SAFE. After arrival, the capital verification report must be prepared and issued by an independent Chinese certified public accountant, confirming the capital contribution against the approved business scope and registered capital amount. Proceeds can then be settled into RMB for permitted operational use subject to SAFE restrictions on capital account conversion.Outbound trade settlements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for straightforward wire transfers, commercial letters of credit for structured, high-value trade requiring documentary compliance, and documentary collections for lower-value or trust-based trade relationships. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. Offshore and foreign digital EMI platforms are not licensed to issue payment instruments or hold client funds on behalf of an entity resident in Mainland China without a non-bank payment institution license issued by the People's Bank of China. Such platforms cannot be used as the primary operational account for treasury or liquidity purposes. At best, a foreign EMI can function as an ancillary payment gateway only through a PBOC-licensed Chinese partner, while the underlying WFOE treasury must remain anchored in a locally licensed commercial bank or a licensed third-party payment institution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File is required when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File is required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although Mainland China does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, the Corporate Income Tax Law and the BEPS-influenced General Anti-Avoidance Rule require substantive economic activity. For a licensed electronic money institution, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) comprise reserve management, liquidity and foreign-exchange risk management, AML/KYC functions, transaction monitoring, and payment system control. If these activities are performed by the foreign parent without local direction and control, the Chinese tax authority may disregard the WFOE as a shell, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize intercompany arrangements, and impose TP adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent under-declaration of corporate income tax

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax plus daily interest at 0.05%; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment up to three years for relatively large evasion and up to seven years for especially large amounts; false invoice creation triggers additional criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 205.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under the PRC Company Law where the entity is a mere façade; the legal representative and finance director can be held personally liable to pay the evaded tax, penalties, and interest out of personal assets. Directors may be blacklisted under the STA Major Tax Violation Information Release Measures, resulting in exit and travel bans, restriction from senior management positions in financial institutions, and criminal imprisonment upon conviction.
Shell company and treaty abuse through lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law allows the tax authority to recharacterize the corporate form, disregard the abusive arrangement, and impose the tax that would have been payable absent the scheme, with interest and penalties; the People's Bank of China may revoke the payment institution or e-money license under the Administrative Measures for Payment Services.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false substance declarations are subject to market-entry bans for financial institutions, disqualification from good-standing certificates, and seizure of personal assets if corporate assets are insufficient; travel bans can be enforced through tax enforcement measures, and criminal liability may arise for fraud or obstruction of tax administration.
Anti-Money Laundering and unauthorized financial operations

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and payment services regulations authorize the People's Bank of China to impose monetary penalties, confiscate illegal gains, suspend operations, and revoke the license; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with imprisonment of up to ten years; Article 176 punishes illegal absorption of public deposits where wallet float is used as disguised deposit-taking.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers who knowingly or negligently allow AML failures face personal fines, removal from office, industry bans, and civil restitution of laundered assets; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and forfeiture of personal assets; shareholders who misuse corporate form to shield criminal proceeds can have the corporate veil pierced to recover assets.
