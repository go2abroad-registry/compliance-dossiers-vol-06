Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement apply at formation stage under the PRC Company Law or the Foreign Investment Law for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise. The Articles of Association must state the subscribed registered capital and contribution schedule, and shareholders must pay contributions in full within the statutory period (generally not exceeding five years from establishment). Sectoral authorities may impose significantly higher paid-in capital, net capital and surety bond requirements for a corporate virtual card issuer or non-bank payment institution; those requirements are enforced through the separate financial license procedure and are not prerequisites to SAMR registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online administration through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (eTax portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and local municipal/district tax service offices where the WFOE is registered.

E-government filing portal

Because the operating entity is a China mainland WFOE, it is a domestic resident taxpayer rather than a non-resident enterprise. The foreign shareholder does not obtain a separate PRC TIN. The WFOE's Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), is the mandatory taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number. After incorporation, SAMR automatically transmits formation data to the competent local tax office of the STA. The legal representative then accesses the national eTax portal, completes supplementary tax registration information, confirms the taxpayer classification, and activates the electronic tax registration certificate. No physical TIN card is issued. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxable on worldwide income at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate; no partnership-style or check-the-box pass-through treatment is available.

Available election forms

None for check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; PRC tax law does not permit entity classification elections. For VAT, a newly established WFOE may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer even if annual taxable sales are below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold; otherwise, below that threshold, small-scale VAT taxpayer status is the default.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales; exceeding this mandatory economic turnover threshold requires registration as a general VAT taxpayer. Below RMB 5,000,000, the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is not automatic. For goods, the WFOE must be a registered exporter, the goods must physically leave PRC customs territory under an accepted export declaration, title must pass to an overseas non-resident buyer, and proceeds must be repatriated through a designated bank. For services, only services expressly listed in the STA's current cross-border taxable services zero-rating catalogue and consumed wholly outside China can be zero-rated; corporate virtual-card issuing and payment-credit services are not, by themselves, in that catalogue and may instead be VAT-exempt cross-border services with no input credit, or taxable at 6% if consumed in China, unless a specific statutory exemption or zero-rating rule applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by the General Administration of Customs; no domestic Bill of Entry is used for exports)
  • Original Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (Air Waybill, rail waybill, multimodal transport document, or postal/courier receipt) showing shipment from the PRC to the foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed export sales contract between the WFOE and the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is not issued in mainland China; the lawful Chinese equivalent is the International Income Declaration and the SAFE export-proceeds collection/settlement record issued by the designated foreign-exchange bank
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice/e-invoice and the electronic export tax refund/exemption declaration where input VAT is claimed
  • Foreign-trade operator filing and GACC exporter registration certificates, plus any export licence required for regulated goods

Golden Tax IV requires fully digitalized electronic invoices to be issued through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each invoice carries a unique electronic serial number, QR code, cryptographic signature, and tax-authority digital seal. Issuance data is cleared and transmitted to the STA in real time; private or unauthenticated invoice issuance is not recognized. B2B taxpayers with high issuance volumes must integrate with the official platform through the required APIs or service-provider interfaces, and buyers and sellers must confirm invoice content on the national platform.

The STA, GACC, and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating claims through Golden Tax IV and cross-ministry data matching. If the customs declaration, transport document, foreign-exchange receipt, or sales contract is missing, contradictory, or false, the zero-rating is retrospectively revoked, output VAT is recomputed at the applicable rate, any refunded input VAT is clawed back, and daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% accrues from the original filing date. Administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the underpaid VAT may be imposed under the Law on Administration of Taxation of the People's Republic of China; repeated or fraudulent export-refund schemes can lead to suspension or revocation of export-tax-refund qualifications, customs penalties, referral to public security authorities, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a controlled floating exchange-rate regime in which current-account foreign exchange is conditionally convertible while capital-account foreign exchange remains strictly supervised. For a WFOE engaged in B2B virtual card issuance, the operational foreign exchange lifecycle - receipt of card settlement proceeds, payment for Mastercard/Visa program management and technology licensing, intercompany service fees, and dividend remittance - must be reconciled to underlying genuine commercial transactions. Current-account settlement is executed by designated direct banks under SAFE's know-your-client, know-your-business, and know-your-purpose documentary review framework rather than by open market convertibility. Every remittance must be linked to contracts, invoices, customs declarations for goods, tax certifications for services, or audited profit evidence for dividends. Capital-account convertibility remains largely pre-approval or registration based: FDI equity injections require SAFE FDI registration, a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account at a qualified domestic bank, and post-remittance capital verification. The WFOE cannot simply move offshore funds in and out interchangeably; each RMB conversion from its capital account must satisfy the SAFE Negative List and the designated bank's four-eyes internal control review. Retained trade proceeds must be repatriated to China and sit within the entity's approved current-account foreign-exchange settlement account, with conversion from foreign currency to RMB at the prevailing market price. Dividend remittance is generally permitted once income tax is withheld and bank documents are verified, but round-tripping or conversion structuring without commercial substance is treated as evasion and can trigger SAFE penalties and downgrading to a restricted verification classification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods-trade foreign exchange supervision framework, the default statutory benchmark is that post-shipment export proceeds must be received within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration for open-account and cash-against-goods settlements; more complex payment terms such as letters of credit or documentary collections may follow the due date of the instrument, but the receipt must still be reconciled in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within the bank's mandatory verification cycle. The WFOE must repatriate export foreign currency receipts to mainland China and cannot leave settlement proceeds in offshore personal or corporate shell accounts. There is no fixed statutory percentage that must be converted to RMB; however, the approved current-account foreign-exchange settlement account has a ceiling calibrated by the bank based on the enterprise's rolling trade-flow and foreign-currency receipt volume. Any balance above that ceiling must be disposed of by conversion to RMB or by verifiable foreign-currency current-account payments before the bank's daily transaction deadline. Overdue receipts exceeding 90 days or unexplained export collection delays are red flags that can downgrade the enterprise from Class A to Class B/C verification status, forcing piece-by-piece SAFE case handling and denying automated FX settlement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment institutions are not licensed banks in mainland China and cannot maintain domestic RMB or foreign-currency settlement accounts, cannot issue RMB-denominated cards directly, and cannot clear through CIPS or the PBOC clearing house under their foreign EMI license. China's payment institution rules require a PBOC non-bank payment license, a mainland-incorporated operating entity, and SAFE cross-border foreign-exchange payment institution registration. A WFOE may use an offshore EMI only as a software or spend-management interface, but the actual clearing and card sponsorship must sit with a Chinese commercial bank or a licensed card scheme member. An offshore EMI account cannot legally receive export proceeds or serve as the WFOE's operational collection account for domestic virtual card reimbursements; doing so would violate capital controls, payment regulations, and taxpayer disclosure duties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing is as follows: first, the foreign investor completes SAFE FDI Registration through the local SAFE branch and obtains the FDI registration certificate and unique registration code for the WFOE. Second, the WFOE opens a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated domestic bank, and the bank registers the account in SAFE's capital-account system. Third, the investor sends an SWIFT MT103 from its foreign bank to the WFOE's capital account, with the SAFE FDI registration code in field 70, the investor's legal name, and the payment purpose code for FDI equity injection for establishment of a foreign-invested enterprise under the capital and financial account category. Fourth, the receiving bank performs the trade-and-settlement authenticity check, completes the cross-border receipt and payment declaration, and issues the Capital Inflow Confirmation after funds are credited. Fifth, the WFOE may convert the foreign currency into RMB through the same bank into its RMB Capital Settlement Account, and the bank verifies that the use falls within the approved negative-list category. Sixth, a certified public accountant issues the Capital Verification Report based on the bank balance confirmation; the WFOE cannot on-lend the capital, change its use, or return it offshore without further SAFE approval.Primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for cross-border service fees, software licensing, card network fees, marketing, and intercompany current-account payments, supported by a contract, invoice, tax withholding certificate or payment certificate, and SAFE current-account payment purpose declaration. CIPS is used for cross-border CNY-denominated settlements with overseas counterparties and sponsor banks, with SWIFT messaging retained for cover or ancillary information. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility Rating: Low. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank payment institutions are not licensed banks in mainland China and cannot maintain domestic RMB or foreign-currency settlement accounts, cannot issue RMB-denominated cards directly, and cannot clear through CIPS or the PBOC clearing house under their foreign EMI license. China's payment institution rules require a PBOC non-bank payment license, a mainland-incorporated operating entity, and SAFE cross-border foreign-exchange payment institution registration. A WFOE may use an offshore EMI only as a software or spend-management interface, but the actual clearing and card sponsorship must sit with a Chinese commercial bank or a licensed card scheme member. An offshore EMI account cannot legally receive export proceeds or serve as the WFOE's operational collection account for domestic virtual card reimbursements; doing so would violate capital controls, payment regulations, and taxpayer disclosure duties.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder, withheld at source under PRC CIT Law Articles 3, 37, and 38. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China Public Notice [2016] No. 42 triggers Local File preparation when the annual aggregate of related-party transactions reaches: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transactions; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is required when aggregate annual revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exist; Country-by-Country Report threshold is RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group revenue.

Economic substance scope: China's Economic Substance Doctrine is expressed through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in CIT Law Article 47 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 120, combined with the beneficial ownership requirement under DTA implementation. A virtual card issuer WFOE must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed economically and commercially in China: merchant underwriting, transaction authorization, fraud monitoring, cardholder dispute resolution, compliance oversight, and own-risk assumption. If those functions are located in an offshore parent while China books only intercompany fees, tax authorities may disregard the WFOE for treaty purposes, reallocate profit under profit split, and impose special tax adjustment interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Bookkeeping and Fabricated Costs

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: the taxpayer is ordered to pay the unpaid tax and late interest, plus a monetary penalty of 50% up to 5 times the evaded tax. PRC Criminal Law Article 201: conviction for tax evasion can lead to imprisonment up to 3 years for ordinary evasion and 3 to 7 years for large-scale evasion, concurrent with criminal fines and confiscation of illegal gains.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorized, instructed, or concealed the fraudulent invoicing are treated as dual actors with the company. The corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law (2023) Article 23 when corporate personality is abused to evade debts or defraud creditors; personal bank accounts and assets of the legal representative and relevant directors may be frozen, personal restitution is owed for unpaid tax and fines, exit bans will be imposed by the tax authority, and directors face individual criminal imprisonment for intentionally evading tax.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividends

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69: the withholding agent shall be ordered to withhold the unpaid tax, and subject to a penalty of 50% up to 3 times the amount of the under-withheld tax. The tax authority may also impose late collection interest and treat the non-resident shareholder as having derived taxable income in China on the gross remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to pay dividends without withholding are personally liable for the shortfall to the extent they participated in the decision; the director of the WFOE is also subject to an individual administrative penalty. Where the act is formulated as a fraudulent cross-border scheme, the Chinese tax bureau can blacklist the legal representative, impose a travel ban, and refer the case for criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for evasion, with imprisonment up to 7 years and restitution of the tax with interest.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Absence of Mandatory Documentation

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 41 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 permit re-characterization of the controlled transaction under the arm's length standard. The tax authority may make a special tax adjustment to increase taxable income by the full excessive royalty; interest is charged at the benchmark interest rate plus 5 percentage points for the period of under-taxation, and no corresponding deduction or foreign tax credit is granted. A separate administrative fine for non-compliance with documentation obligations (Public Notice [2016] No. 42) may be issued.

Director personal liability: If the director signed the offshore royalty agreement knowing that the recipient lacked substance and that the rate was inflated, the PRC Company Law veil-piercing doctrine imposes joint and several asset liability on the director for the tax deficit and interest. The director may also be held criminally liable for intentional tax evasion, face exclusion from serving as a director of any Chinese company, suffer personal asset seizure, and be prohibited from leaving China until the tax deficiency is settled.
