Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No general minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE limited liability company under the Company Law; the registered capital is subscribed and must be fully paid within five years of incorporation. However, because the business scope is a licensed cross-border remittance and payment institution, the PBOC Payment Business License imposes minimum paid-in registered capital thresholds (commonly RMB 100 million for a nationwide payment license and RMB 30 million for a regional license) or such other threshold as PBOC prescribes for cross-border remittance agencies. No blocked escrow account is required at formation; paid-in capital must be deposited in the dedicated FDI capital settlement account and used in compliance with SAFE foreign exchange control regulations.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises require a Certificate of Fire Safety Acceptance under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China, covering fire exits, alarm systems, emergency lighting, and extinguishing equipment, together with an annual fire safety self-inspection filing. If the remittance platform houses an in-house data center or server room, the facility must additionally meet the data center structural and fire protection requirements of the PRC standard GB 50174, including fire suppression, temperature control, and emergency power testing. A workplace safety inspection and an occupational health assessment must be completed under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China. No civil defense permit applicable to heavy industry or warehousing is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and administrative processing are conducted through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online system, the STA's unified electronic tax administration portal for taxpayer registration, VAT filing, invoice management, and export-taxation declarations.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_WFOE_LTD, which is a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise organized as a PRC resident limited liability company, the TIN and VAT registration number are unified into the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Procedure: (1) Submit the WFOE incorporation dossier to SAMR and obtain the business license; under the five-in-one integrated registration reform, the USCC on the business license is automatically the tax registration identifier in the STA taxpayer database. (2) Complete the post-registration activation in the STA e-Tax portal by confirming legal representative identity, financial officer real-name identity, accounting system, domicile, bank account, and tax filing period. (3) Register the VAT tax-type classification and request general VAT taxpayer status where required; the WFOE must be included in the VAT tax registration at formation and cannot defer registration because the cross-border remittance licence itself mandates tax registration. (4) Activate VAT invoice capability in the STA VAT Invoice Management System and obtain digital e-invoice encryption keys and invoice quotas. (5) For non-resident foreign shareholders or non-resident service providers that do not have a PRC permanent establishment, separate tax registration is not required for the WFOE itself; instead, the WFOE as withholding agent registers the non-resident counterparty on e-Tax for withholding obligations stemming from any PRC-sourced payments.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system does not provide a check-the-box, pass-through, partnership-style, or hybrid-mismatch elective classification for a foreign-owned limited liability company. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise at entity level for corporate income tax and is a VAT taxpayer. The only administrative choice available is the VAT small-scale versus general taxpayer method, selected through the mandatory turnover threshold and not through a tax classification election; hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed by the STA under general anti-avoidance and special tax adjustment rules rather than by statutory elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; however, due to the integrated SAMR-STA registration mechanism, all licensed WFOEs, including the remittance entity, must obtain tax registration at incorporation regardless of turnover. Below RMB 5 million of annual taxable sales, a small-scale VAT taxpayer method is available, but the WFOE may elect general VAT status to preserve input VAT credits and to substantiate export zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim a VAT zero rate only for cross-border taxable services that fall within the statutory zero-rating or exemption catalogue for services supplied to non-resident enterprises or individuals and wholly consumed outside the PRC. For the cross-border money transmission and remittance-rail service, the WFOE must demonstrate through contract and operating records that: (i) the counterparty or end-recipient is outside China; (ii) the service is not consumed or employed within China; (iii) the remittance processing, clearing, and payment rails are executed as an offshore service; and (iv) the consideration is paid in foreign currency by a non-resident payer into the WFOE's designated foreign exchange account. Qualifying zero-rating produces a refundable input VAT credit; if the service does not satisfy the cross-border zero-rating conditions, the domestic portion is subject to VAT at the standard 6% financial-services rate, and any China-domestic payout or collection leg must be separately analysed. The WFOE should obtain an STA ruling or written confirmation where the remittance service is not explicitly listed in the cross-border zero-rating/exemption catalogue.

  • WFOE business license and U.S.C.C. tax registration proof
  • STA-recognized cross-border taxable service contract or platform agreement identifying the remittance service, the remittance rails (SWIFT and local cash pickup clearing), the non-resident counterparty, the place of performance and consumption, and the foreign-currency payment terms
  • Commercial invoice issued through the VAT Invoice Management System, annotated with a zero-rate line for the exported service and, if any, a separate 6% line for China domestic clearing services
  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, required only where physical payment cards, hardware, data carriers, or supporting equipment are necessarily exported as part of the cross-border service; not applicable to a pure intangible money-transmission service
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for any physical cargo movement, and/or the SWIFT MT103 or equivalent remittance-rail clearing record proving that the transfer instructions and funds crossed the PRC border
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued foreign exchange receipt confirmation from a PRC designated foreign exchange bank, accompanied by the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment declaration for trade in services showing settlement into the WFOE's domestic bank account
  • Service performance evidence consisting of offshore settlement logs, counterparty completion certificates, non-resident payroll or beneficiary statements, and the e-Tax declaration record for cross-border taxable service exemption or zero-rating, as required by STA practice

China does not operate a transaction-by-transaction real-time fiscal clearance stamp as in some Latin American VAT systems; instead, the PRC employs a state-controlled digital invoice clearance framework. All VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA VAT Invoice Management System and the national E-Invoice Service Platform. Before issuance, the system validates the seller's USCC, buyer's USCC, invoice amount, tax rate, and the unique invoice number with tax-control ciphertext. Issuance is transmitted to the STA in near real-time; the WFOE must connect its billing or ERP system to the e-invoice API or use the e-Tax portal's electronic invoice module. For zero-rated export supplies, the invoice must carry a zero-rate annotation on the relevant line, and the corresponding transaction must be declared in the VAT return; the same system serves as the anti-fraud audit trail for the STA.

The STA cross-matches e-invoice data, bank foreign-exchange receipts, SAFE service trade declarations, and customs declarations in real time. If a zero-rating claim is not supported by the mandatory documentation bundle, the STA will disallow the zero rate, reclassify the supply as taxable at the standard 6% VAT rate, claw back any input VAT credits or refunds already released, and assess late interest at 0.05% per day from the original due date. Under the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, an underpaid VAT attributable to false or unsupported documentation attracts an administrative penalty of between 50% and 500% of the tax shortfall; fraudulent export-refund claims or counterfeit invoice activity may trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including restitution, monetary penalties, confiscation, and imprisonment of responsible officers. Adverse findings also lower the taxpayer's public tax-credit rating and may lead to suspension or revocation of the cross-border remittance licence and disqualification from simplified cross-border payment rails.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with bifurcated current-account and capital-account convertibility. Current-account transactions, such as trade in goods and services, remittance of salaries, and dividends earned from legitimate foreign investment, are generally convertible and require only supporting documentation and SAFE reporting. Capital-account transactions, including foreign direct investment repatriation, cross-border loans, and investment in RMB-denominated financial products, are subject to strict prior approval or registration with SAFE. WFOEs must register foreign investment capital, open a capital account under SAFE supervision, and each capital injection must be verified against the registered amount. Surrender requirements oblige exporters to bring foreign exchange earnings into the China Interbank Foreign Exchange Market and settle through designated banks. Profit remittance hurdles include audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the State Taxation Administration, proof that registered capital is fully contributed, and a SAFE-verified foreign exchange declaration; dividends cannot be remitted before annual tax reconciliation. Effective compliance requires maintaining a robust paper trail for every FX transaction, central bank balance sheet reporting, and adherence to trade and payment background verification rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds for goods must be collected, repatriated, and reported to the designated foreign-exchange bank no later than the contractual settlement maturity or, in the absence of a contract term, within 90 days after customs declaration; the amount may be retained in a SAFE-quota foreign-currency account, but any balance exceeding the quota must be surrendered and converted to RMB at the bank's mid-market rate. Newly established WFOEs in high-risk remittance corridors are subject to a 100% conversion coverage requirement for the first operating year unless a written SAFE exemption is obtained.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its foreign-exchange administration arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and fintech payment firms are not granted the legal status of domestic deposit-taking banks in Mainland China, and SAFE has not authorized any unlicensed foreign digital EMI to open bank accounts or clear CNY obligations on behalf of a WFOE. Such providers can only potentially act as white-label remittance-origination technology vendors behind a licensed Chinese payment institution; direct account maintenance or foreign currency clearing is a high-risk unlicensed banking activity and will not withstand PBOC/SAFE examinations. Viability rating: low/restricted, permitted only in collaboration with a licensed Chinese payment processor under a cross-border RMB clearing arrangement approved by PBOC.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial registered capital must be wired from the foreign parent through a recognized SWIFT corridor to a SAFE-designated capital account at the tier-1 bank. The wire must carry Foreign Exchange (FX) purpose code '1102' (Foreign Direct Investment) and, upon receipt, the bank obtains a transaction confirmation from SAFE via the Cross-border Payment and Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Capital verification by a PRC certified public accountant is required before the funds can be transferred from the capital escrow account to the operating RMB account; funds must be deployed strictly per the approved permitted use framework, and post-settlement conversion must be validated against the contract, customs declaration, and expense evidence.For trade settlements, WFOE disbursement requests must be supported by customs declarations, commercial invoices, packing lists and bills of lading; settlement is executed via SWIFT MT103 for open-account payments, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) for documentary trade, or a Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A) when bank charges are to be controlled. All outgoing RMB or foreign-currency payments must be filed with SAFE under the designated trade or payment code, with the underlying contract attached in the bank's remittance system before execution, and any currency conversion beyond the retained foreign-currency quota requires a prior SAFE application or pre-approved current-account quota.RMB/USD spot settlement and forward outright contracts (delivery-based) entered into with domestic banks approved by SAFE for current-account exposureCross-currency swaps (FX swaps) on residual current-account cash flows, where both legs are connected to verified trade receivables or payables and approved bank linesOffshore NDF (non-deliverable forward) contracts are permitted only for offshore counterparties of the foreign parent, not for the onshore WFOE's own books, due to SAFE capital-account prohibitionsForeign-exchange options purchased from onshore banks to hedge committed trade and remittance pipelines, provided the premium is documented as a treasury cost

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI and fintech payment firms are not granted the legal status of domestic deposit-taking banks in Mainland China, and SAFE has not authorized any unlicensed foreign digital EMI to open bank accounts or clear CNY obligations on behalf of a WFOE. Such providers can only potentially act as white-label remittance-origination technology vendors behind a licensed Chinese payment institution; direct account maintenance or foreign currency clearing is a high-risk unlicensed banking activity and will not withstand PBOC/SAFE examinations. Viability rating: low/restricted, permitted only in collaboration with a licensed Chinese payment processor under a cross-border RMB clearing arrangement approved by PBOC.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required if annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000; intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000; financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000; or receipt/payment for services exceeds RMB 40,000,000 each; Master File required when annual related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1,000,000,000 (Bulletin 2016 No.42)

Economic substance scope: China applies the Economic Substance Doctrine by enabling tax authorities to disregard transactions or recharacterize them under General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Article 47 EIT Law and Article 120 of Implementing Regulations) and the Special Tax Adjustment chapters of the EIT Law. Enterprises must have actual business functions, assets, and risks in mainland China; the core income-generating activities (CIGA) of the remittance platform - licensing, server hosting, customer contract negotiation, and payment processing - must be physically conducted by the WFOE with adequate employees. A pure 'post-box' shell company with no personnel or facilities would fail substance review and be treated as having no economic substance for treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed Money Remittance / Illegal Business Operation

Statutory sanction: Article 225 of the Criminal Law (illegal business operation) imposes confiscation of illegal income and fines equivalent to 1-5 times the illegal proceeds, and in serious cases, imprisonment for not less than 5 years; additionally, PBOC may impose an administrative penalty of RMB 1 million or more and revoke the company's licenses under the Payment Rules, while SAFE penalty for violation of foreign exchange controls is 0.1%-0.5% of the transaction amount for institutions.

Director personal liability: Under the Doctrine of Piercing the Corporate Veil (Company Law Article 20), directors who misuse the limited liability structure to conceal the unlicensed business or channel illegal remittance flows are jointly and severally liable for the company's debts. Personal civil liabilities include asset attachment and restitution of wrongfully obtained remittance fees. Directors may face a travel ban (personal departure restriction) and be placed on the list of dishonest judgment debtors. Criminal imprisonment: the legal representative and compliance officer can be prosecuted personally and sentenced to imprisonment of 5 to 10 years with hefty fines if the illegal business amount is especially substantial.
Tax Evasion through Underreporting Cross-Border Fees / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law: taxpayers who withhold or report less tax through fraudulent act shall be liable to pay the tax, 50%-500% penalty and late surcharge of 0.05% per day; if the underpaid tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and exceeds 10% of the amount of tax due, criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 applies: a fine equal to 1-5 times the amount of tax and imprisonment up to 7 years for the directly responsible personnel.

Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil when the illegal tax evasion is committed by the actual controller through the corporation to obtain tax benefits; directors and financial officers must personally bear the unpaid tax and penalties. Restitution orders attach their personal bank accounts, real estate, and equity interests; a personal asset freezing and seizure order can be issued; the offending director will be blacklisted by the tax bureau, and criminal conviction will suspend their management rights and prohibit them from serving as a director in any Chinese company.
Abusive Transfer Pricing / Shell-Company Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Articles 41-44 EIT Law authorize the tax authority to make a primary adjusted tax assessment: any income under-reported because of related-party non-arm's-length pricing is reallocated and taxed at 25%; Article 46 imposes thin-capitalization disallowance; interest on overdue primary adjustment is 110% of the market rate (a 10-percentage-point penalty) and a fine of up to 50% of the tax underpaid under Article 44 of Tax Administration Enforcement Rules; documentation failure triggers RMB 10,000-50,000 fines and concurrent production of ex-post comparability analysis.

Director personal liability: When the transfer pricing arrangements are sham, the veil-piercing doctrine applies: directors who sign the pricing policy and who knowingly direct or cause the misleading documentation are equally liable for the adjusted corporate tax. Personal civil liability includes payment of amounts equal to the tax and interest recovered from the company; their personal assets are at risk. Under the Chinese social-credit system, the director may be blacklisted, prohibited from leaving the country, ineligible to take trains or flights, and have property seized. Criminal charges for fraudulent concealment of taxable income may result in imprisonment for a maximum of 7 years (Criminal Law Article 201).
