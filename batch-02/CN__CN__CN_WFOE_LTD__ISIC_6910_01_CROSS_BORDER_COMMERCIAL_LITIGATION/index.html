Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. PRC Company Law, as amended by the 2023 Revision, abolishes any statutory minimum paid-up capital for an ordinary wholly foreign-owned service enterprise. The registered capital is set by the shareholder in the Articles of Association and must be reasonably adapted to the business scale. Contributions are made by wire transfer into a dedicated capital contribution account at a licensed bank; the bank completes State Administration of Foreign Exchange reporting, and no blocked escrow account is required. Paid-in capital must be published in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System within 20 working days after contribution.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to physical civil-defense permits for factories; nevertheless, the leased office premises must obtain a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department before first occupation, pass structural load clearance for the intended seating and archival storage, and obtain workplace safety and occupational health clearance under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform

E-government filing portal

For the CN_WFOE_LTD, incorporation through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) generates the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the integrated one-stop SAMR-STA data-sharing process at the competent local tax authority, submitting the articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, investor documents, capital contribution schedule, and bank account information. The local tax authority then activates the taxpayer master file and e-Tax account, enabling electronic VAT invoice issuance and filing. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment that derives China-source income must separately obtain a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number through the withholding agent or by direct application to the competent tax authority before performing satisfaction of withholding reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or pass-through elections for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise; entity-level Corporate Income Tax applies by statute. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and specific anti-avoidance measures govern instead.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales; all VAT taxpayers must register for tax, and annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer status, while taxpayers below the threshold may be registered as small-scale VAT taxpayers or may voluntarily apply for general VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT treatment for goods requires the goods to be physically exported from mainland China under customs supervision by a registered general VAT taxpayer, with the export customs declaration matching the sales contract and commercial invoice, transfer of title and risk to the overseas buyer, and export proceeds repatriated and received through designated foreign exchange banks. For qualifying cross-border services, including offshore commercial litigation and arbitration services, zero-rating or exemption applies where the service recipient is an overseas entity, the services are performed entirely outside mainland China or delivered for use outside China, and the consideration is received from outside China; comprehensive supporting contracts, delivery records, and evidence of overseas consumption must be retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation from a designated bank
  • Export tax rebate or exemption application package and electronic data file
  • VAT special invoices and input VAT deduction certificates supporting the rebate computation

China operates the All-Digital Electronic Invoice (ADEI) system under the National Electronic Tax Administration. VAT invoices are issued in real time through the STA electronic invoice service platform; invoice data including the unique digital code, dynamic anti-fraud elements, and digital signature is transmitted to the tax authority at the moment of issuance. The STA platform applies real-time risk-control clearance, automated data matching, and blocking of suspicious issuers. Although a government-issued API connection is not universally compulsory, enterprises must use e-Tax approved clients or STA-compatible interface integrations for high-volume digital issuance; paper invoices have been progressively phased out, and physical stamping is not the controlling authentication method for digital electronic invoices.

The STA and customs authorities may jointly audit export zero-rating and VAT rebate claims. If the export declaration is false, goods are not physically exported, invoices are inflated, or foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, the tax authority will claw back the refunded VAT, impose a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05 percent, and impose a penalty of 50 percent to 500 percent of the falsely refunded or evaded amount under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Serious cases constitute fraudulent export tax rebate crime under the Criminal Law, exposing responsible individuals and entities to heavy fines and imprisonment; additionally, the taxpayer may be suspended from export rebate processing, downgraded in tax and customs credit ratings, and subjected to enhanced future audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates in Chinese yuan (CNY) under a managed floating exchange rate regime. For current account items, convertibility is effective: once a genuine service trade contract, invoice and supporting documentation exist, foreign currency received by a WFOE can be converted into RMB at a designated bank without SAFE's prior case-by-case approval. Capital account items remain restricted and require SAFE registration, admission to the negative list, and in many cases a qualified domestic institutional investor arrangement or regulatory filing. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account and are no longer subject to a fixed statutory surrender ratio; the enterprise may retain the currency or convert it at market rates. Profit remittance is treated as a current account item if the WFOE has paid the required corporate income tax and distributed actual dividends, but banks require audited financial statements, resolutions of shareholders and proof of tax payment, and any remittance of pre-export or capital-derived funds will be reclassified as capital account and trigger SAFE review. Practical hurdles include strict genuineness checks, beneficiary and purpose code validation, and PBOC's anti-money laundering monitoring of unusual retainer flows from litigation clients.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE export proceeds verification rules, export receipts and eligible service trade receipts must be repatriated to a bank account in China within 180 calendar days from the date of customs declaration for goods, or from the service completion or invoice date for services, unless SAFE has granted a specific deferral for approved e-commerce or bonded logistics models. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the old mandatory surrender regime is replaced by a repatriation and verification duty, with the enterprise free to retain foreign currency or settle at the onshore market rate. The receiving bank matches the proceeds against electronic port records and may require supporting contracts, customs declarations and tax invoices. Failure to repatriate in time triggers verification errors, regulatory ranking downgrades and tightened access to future foreign currency purchases.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for domestic clearing and account hosting; medium only as an ancillary offshore collection gateway. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer and similar non-bank providers are not licensed by PBOC as Chinese deposit-taking institutions, cannot open corporate settlement accounts in mainland China, and cannot clear retainer receipts directly into the WFOE's domestic banking infrastructure without a PBOC-licensed Chinese partner institution. SAFE will only recognise capital injection and export proceeds that arrive through a domestic designated bank under the proper FX purpose code. Treasury should therefore keep the Tier-1 domestic bank as the principal relationship and use an EMI only for small foreign client convenience payment collection, never for registered capital, dividend remittance or statutory capital verification flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For equity capital, the WFOE must first obtain SAFE FDI registration and a foreign exchange business number, open a capital account at a domestic designated bank, receive the offshore remittance by SWIFT MT103 with the payment purpose code for FDI equity investment, pass the foreign investment negative-list screening, and complete a capital verification report by a licensed PRC CPA before converting RMB. For litigation client retainers classified as service trade, the WFOE must execute a service trade contract with the offshore litigant, register the contract with the designated bank where required, and receive funds through the basic operational current account with the retainer agreement and invoice attached; every wire must state the exact SAFE purpose code and the full legal name of the WFOE to prevent rejection.Domestic court filing fees, arbitration registration fees and local expert witness fees should be settled through the WFOE's RMB basic account using standard CHIPS-equivalent CNAPS or RMB electronic clearing. International disbursements to overseas counsel, arbitral institutions, expert witnesses and foreign court registries must be executed by SWIFT MT103 from a foreign currency current account, using the applicable SAFE service trade purpose code for legal services, and supported by the underlying award, settlement agreement, invoice and matter reference. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not the natural rail for professional litigation services; they are acceptable only where a trade-damages award or escrow arrangement requires settlement through trade finance instruments, in which case the commercial bank's trade finance desk must approve the LC opening or documentary presentation under existing trade control and sanctions policies.Onshore CNY/USD forward contracts with a domestic designated bank under the strict real-demand principle and a demonstrable litigation fee exposureCross-currency swaps (CNY/USD) matched to the expected duration of retainer liabilities and foreseeable damage awards, subject to SAFE prohibition on speculative swapsForeign exchange options with two-way premium for contingent legal costs and uncertain timing of case disbursementsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the mainland WFOE as a primary hedge instrument; NDFs may only be used by an offshore parent or investor hedging CNY exposure, and cannot be booked through the WFOE's domestic treasury without violating SAFE hedge-real-demand rules

Turnover: All Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprises (WFOEs) are subject to an unconditional statutory audit: the PRC Company Law requires every limited liability company to prepare audited annual financial statements at the end of each fiscal year. There is no balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption for an ordinary WFOE. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 400 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 200 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services; a Master File must be prepared if the MNE group's consolidated revenue is above RMB 5.5 billion or related-party transaction amounts reach RMB 1 billion, and the Country-by-Country Report is required only for groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the EIT Law, tax-treaty beneficial-ownership clauses and the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI require genuine economic substance. A PRC WFOE in commercial litigation must exercise its Core Income-Generating Activities (decision-making, case-management, risk-bearing, client contracting, and performance of legal services) in China; if the WFOE is a mere shell with no real staff, premises or functions, SAT may recharacterise its profit allocation under LATC Art. 47 GAAR, deny treaty benefits, impute the income back to the foreign principal, and impose a special tax adjustment interest from the due tax date.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion / false invoicing

Statutory sanction: LATC Art. 63 demands payment of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest (0.05% per day) and a civil penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid amount; where the amount of evaded tax reaches statutory thresholds, Criminal Law Art. 201 applies with a maximum of seven years' imprisonment for the perpetrator and a fine.

Director personal liability: If the director is the person who designed or signed the fraudulent return, he/she is directly criminally liable and may also be ordered to make restitution of the evaded tax through court judgment. The legal representative and financial director will be placed on the SAT Tax-Related Violation Blacklist, resulting in a PRC travel ban (exit restriction), restrictions on high-consumption and business travel, and disqualification as a company director.
Failure to withhold tax on outbound profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: LATC Art. 69 provides that a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit is liable to be ordered to remit the short-collected tax and is punishable by a fine of 50%-300% of the tax not withheld; daily late interest under LATC Art. 32 continues to accrue and the SAT may recover the funds from the WFOE's bank accounts, including the trust account only where permissible and not causing client-funds breach.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly orders the repatriation without WHT is jointly and severally responsible for the enterprise tax debt if the court lifts the veil because the director used the company as a sham vehicle or diverted corporate funds. The legal representative is additionally blacklisted and prohibited from exiting China until the WHT, penalties and interest are settled, and may face criminal liability as a co-principal or accomplice in tax evasion.
Sham treaty shopping / lacking economic substance

Statutory sanction: The SAT will disregard the entity under EIT Law Art. 47 and the general anti-abuse rule, recalculate taxable income at 25%, and impose a special tax adjustment interest equal to the benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points (EIT Law Art. 48). The treaty benefit is denied and the underpaid withholding tax is collected with a 50%-300% withholding-agent penalty (LATC Art. 69).

Director personal liability: Directors who conceal the sham structure may be sued in civil proceedings for unjust enrichment and restitution of diverted profits; the traveling ban is imposed on responsible officers for the punishment period, and repeated tax-treaty abuse constitutes punishable tax evasion or fraud. If the scheme involves forged TRC or fake residency certificates, Criminal Law Art. 280 (forgery of official documents) allows a sentence of up to ten years, and the director's personal assets may be attached to satisfy the company's tax restitution.
