Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered address for a WFOE engaged in outsourced virtual CFO services. PRC company law and tax regulations require an actual business address shown on the business licence, used for service of process, tax registration, payroll, and invoice management. State Taxation Administration inspectors and commercial banks reject a bare virtual address because bank KYC and AML controls require physical verification of the operating premises and evidence of real business substance. A shared office or government-designated incubator seat providing a genuine workstation, meeting room, registered address, and documented lease may be accepted in some provinces; however, the authorities will still require a valid lease agreement, rental invoices, and an on-site manager. Therefore, any virtual office arrangement must be paired with a physical presence sufficient to pass verification by the AMR, tax authorities, and the opening bank.

Competent corporate registry

Governing statute

Company Law of the People's Republic of China (2023 Revision), effective 1 July 2024, particularly Articles 23-37 (formation and registration), Article 47 (five-year capital contribution period for limited liability companies), Articles 58-68 (shareholders' powers and governance organs), and Article 93 (incorporation of wholly foreign-owned enterprises as limited liability companies); together with the Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (2019) and the Regulations on the Implementation of the Foreign Investment Law; and the Measures for the Registration of Market Entities (2021).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a general-purpose WFOE providing outsourcing, accounting, or virtual CFO advisory services under ISIC 6920. The registered capital amount is self-declared and must be reasonably consistent with the announced business scale. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, all shareholders must pay the full subscribed capital contribution within five years from the date of incorporation. There is no blocked escrow account requirement under PRC law; however, the foreign investor's capital contribution must be remitted through a licensed bank into a foreign exchange settlement capital account opened in the name of the WFOE and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the FDI foreign exchange registration procedure.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable as a special Civil Defense fire permit because no manufacturing or high-risk physical operation is present. The Company must nevertheless ensure the leased commercial premises have a valid fire safety acceptance certificate under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China, and must make a simple workplace safety registration with the local emergency management authority as an employer of office-based staff.

WHEREAS the Company is a wholly foreign-owned enterprise organized as a limited liability company under the Company Law of the People's Republic of China and the Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China; and WHEREAS the Board of Directors is authorized by the Articles of Association to take corporate formation actions on behalf of the Company;

NOW, THEREFORE, BE IT RESOLVED as follows:

1. ARTICLES OF ASSOCIATION: The Articles of Association of CN_WFOE_LTD, as submitted for registration, are hereby approved, ratified, and adopted as the supreme internal governing instrument of the Company. The registered name, registered address, business scope, organizational structure, capital contribution rules, and governance procedures contained in the Articles are confirmed in all respects.

2. SHARE CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION: The sole foreign shareholder of the Company is hereby acknowledged as subscribing to one hundred percent of the registered capital of the Company. The legal representative and the chief financial officer are authorized to open a foreign exchange settlement account for the Company, receive the subscribed capital contribution, and cause such capital to be recorded in the Company's statutory books. All contributions shall be paid in full within the statutory five-year period, and no distribution or dividend may be made before the paid-in capital obligations are fulfilled in accordance with the Articles and applicable law.

3. REGISTERED OFFICE: The registered office of the Company shall be the address stated in the application for establishment registration. The legal representative is irrevocably authorized to execute the office lease, accept service of process, file all address-related amendments, and take all steps necessary to maintain the registered office in good standing.

4. BANKING AUTHORIZATION: The Company shall maintain banking relationships with licensed commercial banks in the People's Republic of China. The Chairman, the General Manager, the Chief Financial Officer, and the legal representative are each authorized, in such combinations as the banking mandates require, to open and operate the basic deposit account, foreign exchange settlement account, and any auxiliary accounts necessary for the Company's business. The board ratifies any banking forms, signature cards, mandates, and account agreements executed by or on behalf of the Company in connection with this authorization.

5. STATUTORY OFFICERS: The following officers are appointed with statutory effect: the Chairman and Legal Representative shall have the executive authority to represent the Company and sign all binding corporate documents; the General Manager shall supervise the daily operations of the Company; the Chief Financial Officer shall oversee bookkeeping, mult  icurrency ledgers, tax filings, and treasury management; and the Supervisor shall exercise independent audit and monitoring powers as required by law. Each officer shall act within the delegated authority and in accordance with the Articles of Association.

6. COMPANY SEALS: The Company shall cause the engraving of the official company seal, the financial seal, the legal representative seal, and the invoice seal through a Public Security Bureau-licensed engraving enterprise. The use of any seal shall be recorded in the Company's seal-use ledger.

7. ACCOUNTING AND FISCAL YEAR: The Company's financial books shall be maintained in RMB, on the accrual basis, under PRC Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises. The fiscal year shall begin on January 1 and end on December 31 of each calendar year. The Chief Financial Officer is authorized to select and contract with qualified licensed accountants and tax advisors as required.

8. RATIFICATION AND EFFECTIVENESS: All actions taken by the founders, attorneys, and agents in furtherance of the incorporation, registration, licensing, and operational setup of the Company prior to the date of this resolution are hereby ratified, confirmed, and approved as acts of the Company. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); official electronic filing portal is the National Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax@China), accessible through www.chinatax.gov.cn, covering tax registration, VAT filing, invoice issuance, export tax refund/zero-rating applications, and corporate income tax compliance.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), no separate Tax Identification Number application is required; the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code printed on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the legal Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT registration number. The incorporation dossier is automatically transmitted from SAMR to the local STA office, completing tax registration on an ex officio basis. After incorporation, the legal representative and finance officer must activate the taxpayer account on the e-Tax@China portal using the registered mobile phone, real-name authentication, and electronic tax device authorization; they must then verify or update enterprise tax registration information, banking and capital account details, VAT payer type election, electronic invoice quotas, and applicable accounting/filing frequency. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that does not have a Chinese establishment but derives Chinese-source income, the non-resident entity or its withholding agent must apply for non-resident tax identification registration with the competent STA office for the location of the payer or place of income derivation, submitting the foreign enterprise registration certificate, board resolution or appointment document, contract or agreement, and beneficial ownership self-certification; the STA issues a non-resident tax registration record that is used for withholding tax, VAT, and stamp duty compliance.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None, because China maintains a unitary corporate income tax system with no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections; however, for indirect tax purposes the WFOE may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer status at the inception of operations, or apply to become a General VAT Payer, and the General VAT Payer designation allows input VAT credit recovery and eligibility for export zero-rating.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in taxable turnover measured over any trailing consecutive 12-month period is the mandatory threshold for registration as a General VAT Payer; all Chinese WFOEs are automatically tax-registered at incorporation, so no separate VAT entity registration threshold applies, but a taxpayer whose annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 must file a General VAT Payer registration with the STA and comply with special VAT invoice issuance, input VAT crediting, and monthly/quarterly filing rules, while the current small-scale taxpayer administrative exemption for turnover not exceeding RMB 100,000 per month may apply where the taxpayer has not elected General VAT Payer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are eligible for 0% VAT zero-rating when the WFOE is a General VAT Payer, the goods have actually departed from China under a valid export customs declaration filed with the General Administration of Customs, the exporter retains the export sales contract and transport documents, and the export proceeds are received from an overseas buyer and repatriated through a Chinese bank. Cross-border services, including qualifying outsourced virtual CFO and bookkeeping/consulting services delivered to non-resident clients, may qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption when the service recipient is located outside China, the services are actually rendered outside China or consumed entirely offshore, the payment originates from abroad and is received in foreign currency, and the service falls within the STA cross-border taxable services guidance; full 0% zero-rating is specifically available for certain statutory offshore services such as technology/IT, software, R&D, and specified business process outsourcing, while other cross-border advisory services may be subject to VAT exemption rather than zero-rating unless the taxpayer obtains the relevant STA confirmation or administrative ruling.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) issued and electronically confirmed by the General Administration of Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or International Consignment Note proving physical export movement from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice issued in the name of the overseas buyer and packing list describing the exported goods
  • Original or electronically authenticated export sales contract or purchase order with the offshore customer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued Foreign Exchange Receipts and Payments Transaction Certificate from a SAFE-designated Chinese bank proving receipt of export proceeds from abroad
  • Export rebate/zero-rating claim record generated in the e-Tax@China export tax system
  • For cross-border services: signed offshore service agreement, service delivery report, performance or completion certificate, evidence of the non-resident client's foreign tax residence, and bank SWIFT credit confirmation showing receipt of foreign-currency consideration

China mandates the Fapiao VAT invoice system with real-time fiscal supervision through the STA electronic invoice platform. All VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the official e-Tax channel or STA-approved software/API endpoint, with each invoice carrying a unique digital monitoring code, a digital tax-control signature, and encrypted transaction data uploaded to the STA at issuance. The fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) framework eliminates the need for physical pre-printed invoice stock and requires the seller to obtain an approved electronic invoice quota, maintain a synchronous invoice-data reconciliation file, and include the correct commodity or service classification code, VAT rate, buyer Unified Social Credit Code, and tax amount. Failure to clear invoice data through the STA anti-fraud digital control environment can render an invoice invalid for VAT deduction, for export refund purposes, or as a valid corporate expense voucher.

The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly audit export zero-rating and VAT refund claims through documentary, logistics, payment-trail, and enterprise-accounting cross-checks. Any zero-rating or export refund claim not supported by the mandatory customs, transport, invoicing, and foreign-exchange repatriation documentation is reversed, with the output VAT recaptured at the applicable domestic rate, interest and late payment surcharges imposed, and a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Administration and Tax Collection. Where zero-rating is obtained by fabricating export declarations, false bills of lading, or fake remittance certificates, the taxpayer is additionally liable for confiscation of the wrongly refunded tax, customs penalties, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export-tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which may result in imprisonment and severe fines; the responsible legal representative, financial officer, and any accomplice may also be subject to personal liability, travel restrictions, and market-entry prohibitions in aggravated egregious cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China achieves current account convertibility under IMF Article VIII while maintaining a managed floating exchange rate subject to SAFE oversight. Operating revenues in RMB arising from exported CFO services can be converted and remitted through an authorized bank if supported by genuine commercial invoices and the bank's real-business-needs review. Inbound equity capital injections, however, are capital account transactions requiring FDI registration with SAFE or its delegated bank, payment into a designated foreign currency capital account, and use of converted RMB strictly in accordance with the Foreign Investment Negative List and the approved business scope. Profit remittances are current account investment income: the WFOE must submit audited financial statements, corporate income tax payment certificates, a shareholder distribution resolution, and historical audit evidence to the remitting bank, which then performs a documentary check and reports to SAFE. Export or service-export proceeds do not have a mandatory conversion percentage, but they must be repatriated to China within the statutory period and settled only through licensed banks at market rates. Capital account items such as cross-border loans, guarantees, foreign exchange derivatives, and overseas investment remain subject to quotas, filings, or case-by-case SAFE approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished blanket mandatory surrender requirements for foreign exchange export proceeds under the 2012 reform package. For goods trade, export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration or the date the overseas collection right becomes due, subject to verification under SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment system. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain proceeds in a current account foreign exchange settlement account or convert them to RMB at the prevailing market rate through an authorized domestic bank. For service exports such as outsourced CFO advisory fees, proceeds must be repatriated in accordance with the underlying service contract and reported to SAFE through the collecting bank using the appropriate balance-of-payments transaction code before any conversion or retention decision is executed.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Enterprise Income Tax rate under Article 4 of the EIT Law)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed to non-resident corporate shareholders, unless a lower rate is available under an applicable Double Tax Agreement/treaty (e.g., 5% for a Hong Kong resident company holding at least 25% of the WFOE's capital).

Annual return must be filed within 5 months after each calendar year-end (i.e., by May 31 of the following year). Quarterly provisional returns are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter (April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15 for Q4).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: annual related-party transactions of other types (including service fees) exceeding RMB 40 million (approx. USD 5.5 million) require preparation of a Local File. Master File: required when the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion (approx. USD 700 million) and the entity is part of the group. Thresholds per STA Bulletin No. 42 of 2016. Additionally, a Country-by-Country Report is triggered if the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion (approx. USD 750 million).

Economic substance scope: China does not impose an Economic Substance Regulations regime in the offshore sense, but the substance-over-form doctrine applies under the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) and Article 26 of the EIT Law. For a WFOE with a virtual CFO business, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) – contracting, team management, billing, and accounting – must be performed in China with real personnel and decision-making substance. A shell WFOE that merely routes income to a related offshore entity without Chinese substance may be recharacterized, and treaty benefits may be denied for lack of beneficial ownership. Directors must ensure that the board meetings, books, and employment records reflect actual operations in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Understated Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law (TACL) and Article 201 of the Criminal Law. Administrative sanction: recovery of the unpaid tax plus a penalty of 50% to 5 times (500%) of the evaded tax. Where the evasion exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, criminal liability arises.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or instructed the concealment may be held personally liable for the civil restitution of the evaded tax and penalties. They also face a travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from China until tax liabilities are settled (Art. 44 TACL). Under Criminal Law Art. 201 and 211, the responsible director may be sentenced to up to 3 years imprisonment (or 3–7 years if the amount is huge) and criminal fines.
Tax Fraud – Fabricated Deductions / Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Violates Article 63 TACL (falsification of accounting vouchers) and may constitute 'fraudulent tax filing'. Sanctions include a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, disallowance of the deduction, and potential criminal exposure under Criminal Law Art. 201. If fictitious VAT special invoices are involved, criminal liability for issuing false invoices under Criminal Law Art. 205 may apply (for ordinary invoices, Art. 205-1).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced if the director used the WFOE as an instrument to commit fraud. Directors must restore the amount of tax avoided with personal assets. A court may impose an exit ban and freeze personal bank accounts. Criminal imprisonment of 3–7 years for tax evasion or up to 10 years for issuing false invoices may be imposed on the 'directly responsible supervisor and other directly responsible persons'.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse (Beneficial Ownership Failure)

Statutory sanction: Violates the General Anti-Abuse Rule under Article 47 EIT Law and the specific anti-treaty-shopping provisions in STA Annual Announcement 2009 No. 601 and Public Notice 2018 No. 9 (Beneficial Ownership). The tax authority may deny treaty benefits and re-characterize the dividend as fully taxable at 10% (or higher if no treaty), impose an additional 10% WHT difference plus a 50%–500% penalty for under-withholding, and assess interest on the shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly arranged the artificial structure are jointly liable for the corporate tax debts to the extent they participated in the abuse. They can be placed on a 'non-trustworthy taxpayer' list (黑名单), which triggers travel bans, restrictions on high consumption (e.g., air travel, luxurious hotels), and credit disabilities. Criminal charges for fraudulent tax refund or evasion may follow if the arrangement concealed taxable income. The corporate veil will be pierced to hold the directors personally responsible for the unpaid taxes and penalties.
