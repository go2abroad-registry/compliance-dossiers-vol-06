Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - China imposes no statutory minimum registered capital for a consulting WFOE. The shareholders determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association, but under the 2023 Company Law the initial contribution must be at least 30% within six months and the balance within five years. No escrowed blocked account is mandated; however, contributed capital must be remitted in compliance with PRC foreign exchange rules and reported to the local State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) branch.

If the local district fire service requires a simple annual fire inspection for any commercial office, the company shall cooperate and obtain the certificate of conformity before full operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) with local municipal/provincial tax service bureaus; online portal is the State Taxation Administration Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and corresponding provincial e-Tax portals.

E-government filing portal

Because the WFOE is a China-resident enterprise, the tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Procedure: (1) complete name pre-approval and foreign investment admission filings (the consultancy activity is not on the foreign investment negative list); (2) file incorporation documents with SAMR and obtain the Business License containing the USCC, which is the statutory TIN for tax, customs, social insurance and banking purposes; (3) complete tax registration within 30 days through the e-Tax portal by authenticating the legal representative's real-name identity and filing supplementary taxpayer information, accounting standards, bank account details and contact persons with the competent local STA tax service bureau; (4) activate VAT taxpayer status and Digital Electronic Invoice issuance in the national VAT invoice system, and apply for general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or electively when below that threshold; (5) register employee payroll withholding and social insurance. A non-resident foreign enterprise without a Chinese entity does not receive a full TIN; it obtains a non-resident tax reference record from the STA for withholding and filing obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold triggers basic VAT registration for a China-established WFOE; any entity with taxable business activities must register upon establishment. For general VAT taxpayer status, the statutory registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; taxpayers below that threshold may elect general status or remain small-scale.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from China qualify for zero-rated VAT treatment under the export tax rebate regime once the goods are actually declared to customs and leave the PRC. For this WFOE's international tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services, zero-rating is not the governing treatment; instead, the services fall within the cross-border VAT exemption category for consulting services supplied to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China. The exemption requires that (i) the recipient is an overseas unit without a China establishment using the service for its China business, (ii) the service is performed and consumed entirely outside China, (iii) the WFOE files the cross-border taxable service exemption record with the STA through the e-Tax portal, and (iv) the WFOE retains evidence of actual offshore service delivery and foreign currency repatriation.

  • Customs Export Declaration of the People's Republic of China (export bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or international transport document issued by the carrier
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract and purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt advice evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Cross-border service contract and detailed service delivery report (for cross-border services)
  • STA e-Tax portal record of the cross-border taxable service exemption filing (for cross-border services)

China requires all VAT taxpayers to use the STA-controlled national VAT invoice system, now operated as the fully digitalized Digital Electronic Invoice system under Golden Tax Phase IV. Invoices are issued through the e-Tax platform using tax-authority-allocated invoice numbers and encrypted anti-fraud digital stamping, with real-time data clearing that cross-matches issued invoices, VAT credit claims and filed VAT returns. Approved enterprises may integrate issuance through authorized ERP/API channels, but all invoices are subject to STA clearance and risk-based issuance quotas.

Export zero-rating and cross-border service exemption claims are subject to documentary audit by the STA and customs/tax police coordination. If the underlying documents do not substantiate the export or offshore consumption, the tax authority retroactively reverses the tax benefit, requires output VAT to be declared and paid together with statutory late-payment surcharge, disallows the corresponding input VAT credits, and may impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid VAT under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent use of forged, falsified or recycled documents to obtain an export tax rebate can constitute criminal tax fraud under PRC Criminal Law, with penalties including imprisonment up to life for large amounts, confiscation of property, and an unlimited or extended retrospective assessment period for confirmed fraud or concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system with RMB convertible on current account for genuine trade and service transactions but subject to a regulated capital account. The WFOE may legally receive equity investment and pay international tax advisory fees abroad if the underlying contracts, invoices, and tax residency certificates are presented to the bank; the Foreign Exchange Administration verifies the transaction before release. Dividend and profit remittance requires an annual audit by a Chinese CPA, a tax clearance or withholding tax payment certificate, board resolution on profit distribution, and a SAFE-registered capital account; remittance is permitted after corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax are settled. Surrender requirements are currently settlement-based: export and service proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic FX settlement account and may be retained in foreign currency or voluntarily converted into RMB; there is no fixed mandatory percentage conversion. However, the bank is obliged to verify the nature of funds under the cross-border receipt and payment verification system and monitor consistency with declared purpose. Capital inflows require SAFE FDI registration and an equity entry record; conversion of capital is strictly purpose-bound and cannot be used for securities or speculative activities. Current account freedom exists while capital account movement requires pre-registration, documentary compliance, and post-verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE settlement and sale of foreign exchange rules, goods export proceeds must be repatriated into China through a licensed bank and credited to the entity's Foreign Exchange Settlement Account within 180 days from customs declaration unless a SAFE-approved extension is obtained; there is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage since the abolition of forced surrender, so the exporter may voluntarily convert to RMB or retain foreign currency. Service trade income, including tax structuring consultancy fees, must be received by the contractual maturity date and verified with the service trade contract, commercial invoice, proof of service delivery, and tax payment documentation where applicable.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not legally viable as the primary banking infrastructure. Under Chinese banking regulations, only licensed onshore commercial banks may open RMB current accounts, foreign currency capital accounts, and settlement accounts for a WFOE. Overseas fintech EMIs and digital banks licensed in other jurisdictions do not possess a Chinese banking license and are prohibited from maintaining onshore RMB or foreign currency accounts, performing SAFE registrations, completing capital verification, or clearing statutory cross-border tax and trade payments. Such platforms may serve only as an offshore treasury visibility layer; the statutory corporate banking, FDI capital injection, tax remittance, and regulatory reporting functions must remain with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity funds should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the overseas parent to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign currency capital account at the chosen Tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance instruction must contain the beneficiary WFOE legal name, the SAFE-registered capital account number, and the foreign exchange payment purpose code corresponding to direct investment capital injection. The bank requires the FDI registration certificate, shareholders' resolution, articles of association, and a written explanation of the investment purpose to be submitted before or simultaneously with the incoming wire. Upon receipt, the bank performs post-verification in the SAFE system, records the capital entry, and permits conversion to RMB only for expenditures stated in the approved business plan. A Chinese certified public accountant must then issue a capital verification report confirming paid-in capital. The WFOE may not use capital funds for entrusted loans, securities investments, speculative real estate acquisition, or any other purpose outside the approved business scope.Outbound cross-border trade and service disbursements are executed predominantly through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with full supporting documentation attached under bank advice: for tax structuring consultancy services, the bank requires a service trade contract, commercial invoice, delivered work product evidence, and tax withholding or payment certificates where foreign service providers are subject to Chinese taxes. If the WFOE also handles physical goods as an ancillary trade activity, Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) and Documentary Collections may be used, but services should not be routed through LCs because no transport documents exist. Dividend and profit repatriation uses MT103 with a board distribution resolution, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and the SAFE-registered remittance basis. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not legally viable as the primary banking infrastructure. Under Chinese banking regulations, only licensed onshore commercial banks may open RMB current accounts, foreign currency capital accounts, and settlement accounts for a WFOE. Overseas fintech EMIs and digital banks licensed in other jurisdictions do not possess a Chinese banking license and are prohibited from maintaining onshore RMB or foreign currency accounts, performing SAFE registrations, completing capital verification, or clearing statutory cross-border tax and trade payments. Such platforms may serve only as an offshore treasury visibility layer; the statutory corporate banking, FDI capital injection, tax remittance, and regulatory reporting functions must remain with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-property transactions exceed RMB 100 million, intercompany financial transactions exceed RMB 50 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion or total cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China has no formal Economic Substance Regulations label, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in Articles 47 and 48 of the CIT Law and the substance-over-form doctrine mandate that a WFOE must conduct its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) inside China, including strategic decision-making, employee deployment, contract negotiation, risk-taking, and invoice issuance. A WFOE that exists only on paper and channeling profits abroad is liable to be recharacterised or disregarded for tax purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Thin capitalisation and disguised profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 46 of the CIT Law denies the deduction for excess interest and Article 37 of the CIT Law recharacterises the disallowed interest as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding tax; Article 47 of the TCAL adds a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% and a penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham debt structure are personally liable under the veil-piercing rule in Article 20(3) of the Company Law, exposing their personal assets to restitution for the unpaid corporate tax; the legal representative may be placed on the tax blacklist and subjected to exit-control measures preventing travel abroad, and criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law carries up to 7 years of imprisonment.
Shell company and absence of economic substance

Statutory sanction: The STA may invoke Article 47 of the CIT Law to disallow the arrangement and recast income, and Article 48 permits tax authorities to make a comprehensive income adjustment; Article 63 of the TCAL imposes a 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid tax, plus possible daily surcharges from the due date.

Director personal liability: Under Company Law Article 20, a director who caused or benefited from the artificial shell structure is jointly and severally liable for the shortfall, subject to civil asset restitution and freezing; the legal representative is subject to an exit ban until settlement is completed, and intentional evasion through false substance representations constitutes a criminal offence under Article 201 of the Criminal Law with penalties up to 7 years of imprisonment.
Manipulated transfer pricing and fabricated documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the CIT Law authorises the STA to reassess taxable income on the arm's length basis; Article 44 of the TCAL allows imputed taxable income in the absence of documentation; Article 62 imposes fines of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 for non-filing, and Article 63 imposes a 50% to 500% fraud penalty with no statute of limitations for fraudulent conduct.

Director personal liability: The director who knowingly certified false transfer pricing documentation is personally responsible for the restitution of the underpaid tax after veil-piercing under the Company Law, can be blacklisted and denied cross-border travel by the exit control court mechanism, and, because deliberate document fabrication is a concealment strategy, Criminal Law Article 201 provides for imprisonment of up to 3 years where the evasion is substantial and up to 7 years for exceptionally large amounts.
