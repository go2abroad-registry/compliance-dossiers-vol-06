Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox (e.g., a serviced desk address not physically occupied by the WFOE staff) is generally rejected by the State Taxation Administration and by Chinese commercial banks for an ISIC 7020 consultancy entity. The local SAMR branch requires a registered office to be a physical street address with a valid rental contract or property ownership certificate; business parks and registered-office service providers are acceptable only if they can issue an official lease and provide actual occupied meeting space, mailroom facilities accessible to the tax bureau, and evidence of a permanent operational presence. Banks conducting entity KYC will, in most jurisdictions, refuse to open a corporate current account for a WFOE using a virtual address because the address is used as the tax invoicing seat and service place; instead they mandate a genuine office lease with a minimum term of one year, unless the entity leases from a government-approved incubator that holds an explicit municipal recognition for foreign-invested low-risk consultancy. It is highly probable that tax authorities will trigger a first-site inspection within six months if the address shows no utility bills, no staff presence, or no fixed telephone line. Consequently, a commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered address; it may only supplement an actual physical office.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company under the PRC Companies Law (2024 Revision). Registered capital is determined by the shareholder's election in the Articles of Association. Contributions must be paid in full within five years from establishment unless a shorter period is required by law or administrative regulation. There is no blocked escrow account requirement for a consulting business, but the foreign shareholder must remit subscribed capital through a licensed foreign exchange bank account and record the paid-in amount with the local Administration for Market Regulation.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to industrial licensing models. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online taxpayer portal: the Unified Electronic Taxpayer Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) through the STA website, with export and customs related data integrated via the China International Trade Single Window operated by the General Administration of Customs.

E-government filing portal

For a newly formed Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), after incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number. The WFOE must complete tax registration in the Unified Electronic Taxpayer Bureau within 30 days from the date of the business license, supplement shareholder and controller identification, file accounting system information, file bank account records, and verify the legal representative identity. A foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment or place of business in China obtains a non-resident taxpayer registration code through withholding registration at the competent tax authority of the Chinese payer; if the foreign enterprise leases or owns real property in China, it must complete a separate temporary taxpayer registration for property-related taxes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC VAT law requires every entity engaged in taxable supplies to complete tax registration upon establishment irrespective of turnover. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer (applicable simplified collection rate is generally 3%, subject to grace-period reductions), while the standard VAT rates for general taxpayers are 13% for goods, 9% for certain specified supplies, and 6% for modern services such as market-entry consultancy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the 0% VAT rate is available where the goods physically leave China under a customs export declaration and the exporter retains evidence of physical export and foreign currency settlement. For cross-border services, zero-rating is restricted to services expressly listed in the STA cross-border zero-rating catalogue, including international transportation, R&D and technical services, software services, information system services and business-process outsourcing supplied to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China. A market-entry consultancy service may be eligible for VAT exemption rather than zero-rating if classified as consulting, unless it falls within a qualifying technical or R&D service; in all cross-border service cases, the service must be provided to an overseas entity, have no domestic consumption relation, and be evidenced by contract, service outcome records and foreign exchange receipt.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form filed through the China International Trade Single Window (the Chinese export bill of entry)
  • Carrier-issued ocean Bill of Lading, air waybill, or rail waybill evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice with matching description and value to the customs declaration
  • Packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation from a bank authorized by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) evidencing receipt from the overseas buyer
  • Customs release or clearance record from the General Administration of Customs
  • VAT export invoice and matching internal accounting record

China mandates fully digitalised electronic invoicing for newly registered enterprises through the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Invoices must be issued using tax-control equipment or the platform API, with invoice data transmitted in near-real-time to the STA Golden Tax System for anti-fraud matching; each invoice carries a unique statistical digital stamp and structured metadata for fiscal clearance. There is no discretionary paper-based clearance, and all invoice data is subject to centralised tax authority reconciliation and automated exception screening.

The STA and customs conduct risk-based audits of zero-rated export transactions, including matching customs declarations with VAT returns, logistics documents, bank receipts and contract terms. If zero-rating is disallowed for lack of substantiation or misdescription, the taxpayer is reassessed at the applicable VAT rate (13% for goods or 6% for services) with late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and may face an administrative penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax deficiency under the PRC Tax Administration Law. A fraudulent export refund claim is a separate criminal offense under the PRC Criminal Law, with penalties including fines, imprisonment, and up to life imprisonment in aggravated cases, and the tax authority may reassess without time limit where fraud or wilful evasion is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a qualified current-account convertibility regime and a heavily supervised capital account. Current-account payments, including consulting fees, royalties, freight, and distributable dividends after tax, may be settled through a designated bank upon submission of authentic underlying documents and tax payment proof. Capital-account movements, such as foreign direct investment equity injections, shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, and escrow deposits for M&A, require SAFE FDI registration before transfer and sometimes PBOC approval. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender percentage: a WFOE may retain them in a foreign exchange account or convert them into RMB at the prevailing market rate, but they must be repatriated through a domestic designated bank and reconciled with customs and SAFE trade data. Profit remittance is allowed once the statutory annual audit is completed, corporate income tax is settled, and a board or corporate resolution approving the dividend is passed. The practical hurdles are documentary rigor, genuine-transaction verification, tax clearance certificates, capital account scrutiny, and the waiting time for bank compliance review, rather than an absolute prohibition.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 2012, China no longer enforces a fixed surrender ratio for export proceeds. All export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to mainland China through a designated foreign exchange bank unless SAFE-approved offshore retention applies. The exporter may either keep the proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert them to RMB at market rate. Banks wire-credit and verify the funds against customs declaration data, invoices, and the SAFE trade monitoring system. In practice, after the customs export date, undelivered receipts that are not reported within the 90-day monitoring period cause the WFOE to be flagged for delayed export proceeds, restricting subsequent foreign exchange transactions until supporting receipts or rectification are submitted.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and onshore account opening. An offshore fintech EMI such as Wise or Airwallex cannot lawfully open a domestic RMB settlement account for a mainland-incorporated WFOE or provide CIPS clearing on behalf of the entity. Under the PBOC and SAFE administrative systems, foreign payment institutions do not have the direct license to service a Chinese domestic corporate entity's cross-border trade settlements in RMB unless through a licensed Chinese bank as the local clearing and KYC agent. A WFOE may use such EMI rails only as a non-core collection channel for small overseas customer payments that ultimately route into the onshore bank, and even then AML enhanced scrutiny attaches because the payment chain is less transparent. The legal optimum is onshore Tier-1 bank rails with SWIFT and CIPS, and fintech rails at most for last-mile remittance to overseas consultants when accompanied by full reconciliation.

Initial registered capital or operating funds arrive from a bank account not belonging to the registered direct investor, or via layered payments inconsistent with the FDI registration.

Root cause: Initial registered capital or operating funds arrive from a bank account not belonging to the registered direct investor, or via layered payments inconsistent with the FDI registration.

Defense evidence: SAFE foreign direct investment registration certificate and the bank-stamped FDI application form; board resolutions approving the amount and currency of the capital injection; original SWIFT MT103 from the direct investor's named corporate account; CPA interim capital verification report after the funds are booked; source-of-funds declaration and parent bank statement for the prior two quarters; and an undertaking letter confirming no cryptocurrency or third-party anonymous funding was used.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore parent must inject equity into the WFOE's foreign currency capital account opened at a Tier-1 bank that accepts SAFE FDI registration. Before the first remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration and obtain the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate or the bank-generated registration number. The remittance must be sent from the direct investor's named corporate account through SWIFT MT103, with the transaction purpose code referring to foreign direct investment capital contribution and remittance information citing the equity subscription agreement. On receipt, the bank performs genuine-transaction screening, and a Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report after the funds are booked and converted into RMB. For offshore investors injecting RMB, an FDI RMB settlement account under PBOC rules is required. No crypto, third-party payer, or anonymous wallet can be used; funds must be directly traceable to the registered direct investor.Use SWIFT MT103 for straightforward service fee remittances, with full remitter and beneficiary details plus invoice and contract references. For goods-related settlement, Commercial Letters of Credit are common: the WFOE's issuing bank opens the LC in favor of an overseas beneficiary, and documents include commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, certificate of origin, and insurance policy. Documentary collections such as D/P and D/A can be used for lower-risk trade with the remitting bank acting as intermediary. Service trade outbound payments require tax clearance and a service trade foreign exchange payment application; goods imports require customs declarations and SAFE trade monitoring data. All outbound RMB should use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System where the counterparty bank supports it, while USD and EUR disbursements use SWIFT.Onshore deliverable FX forwards on current account exposure, booked through a SAFE-licensed domestic bank and supported by genuine trade or service contracts.FX swaps for short-term liquidity matching between injected foreign currency and RMB operating expenditures, limited to real repayment and delivery capacity.Cross-currency swaps for approved long-term cross-border financing or lease liabilities, with SAFE registration or approval where the underlying is a capital account transaction.Offshore non-deliverable forwards for RMB are not acceptable hedging products for a mainland-resident WFOE; derivatives must be transacted onshore with real exposure and under SAFE's real-need principle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury and onshore account opening. An offshore fintech EMI such as Wise or Airwallex cannot lawfully open a domestic RMB settlement account for a mainland-incorporated WFOE or provide CIPS clearing on behalf of the entity. Under the PBOC and SAFE administrative systems, foreign payment institutions do not have the direct license to service a Chinese domestic corporate entity's cross-border trade settlements in RMB unless through a licensed Chinese bank as the local clearing and KYC agent. A WFOE may use such EMI rails only as a non-core collection channel for small overseas customer payments that ultimately route into the onshore bank, and even then AML enhanced scrutiny attaches because the payment chain is less transparent. The legal optimum is onshore Tier-1 bank rails with SWIFT and CIPS, and fintech rails at most for last-mile remittance to overseas consultants when accompanied by full reconciliation.
