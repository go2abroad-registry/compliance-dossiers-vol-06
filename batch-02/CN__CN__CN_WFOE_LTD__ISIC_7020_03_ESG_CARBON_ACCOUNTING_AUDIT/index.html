Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under PRC Company Law and SAMR registration rules, a WFOE must maintain a lawful registered domicile in Mainland China where official documents can be served and where the company's actual business premises can be inspected. A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally rejected by SAMR and municipal tax authorities because the WFOE must provide a lease contract, property ownership certificate or notarized landlord consent, and because tax registration and bank account opening trigger physical KYC visits. For ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage, the activity is treated as a professional services company and still requires substantive premises or at least a fully licensed serviced office with dedicated desks, reception capacity and utility evidence. A commercial mailbox is not permissible as the sole registered address; banks will almost always reject it for corporate account opening.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law (2023 revision) does not impose a statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE LLC engaged in management consulting, ESG carbon accounting and offset brokerage. There is no blocked escrow account rule. The Articles of Association must state a registered capital amount, and the shareholders must pay the subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of incorporation unless the Articles prescribe a shorter term. For foreign exchange integrity, paid-in capital must enter a dedicated capital account at a permitted bank, and the bank may issue a capital declaration; this is not a statutory escrow but a custodial banking control.

The WFOE must still observe ordinary workplace fire safety obligations under the lease and the national work-safety rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the WFOE, the SAMR issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The WFOE then files tax registration on the STA's e-Tax platform within 30 days of business license issuance; the USCC becomes the entity's TIN and VAT registration number. For a foreign non-resident enterprise with China-source income but no permanent establishment, the payer or withholding agent files the non-resident tax registration on the e-Tax platform and obtains a temporary taxpayer identification number linked to the foreign entity, presenting the foreign certificate of incorporation and a tax residency certificate; for foreign enterprises with a permanent establishment, tax registration is completed via the e-Tax platform at the PE location and a TIN is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Corporate Income Tax Law does not offer check-the-box elections or elective pass-through status. All limited liability companies, including WFOEs, are automatically treated as resident enterprises taxable at entity level. The STA applies anti-hybrid mismatch rules under the special tax adjustment measures to deny deductions attributable to hybrid instruments or hybrid entities, but no elective mechanism is available.

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory general taxpayer VAT status; VAT registration is compulsory upon issuance of the business license regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports are eligible for VAT zero-rating (export tax rebate) when the exporter is a registered general taxpayer, the goods leave Chinese customs through an Export Goods Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed in the China International Trade Single Window, and the export transaction is fully completed within the statutory period. Qualifying offshore services (including ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage services) may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt when the services are supplied to a non-resident entity, consumed entirely outside China, and the WFOE files the prescribed zero-rating/exemption application with the STA plus the underlying service contract, export invoice and proof of foreign exchange receipt. The exact classification follows Caishui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4 for cross-border services, subject to the 'fully outside China' consumption test.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed via China Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or Express Courier Receipt
  • Commercial invoice containing a zero-rated VAT statement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Settlement and Sale Report
  • Service contract and work order evidencing cross-border supply to a non-resident party
  • Tax status certificate of the foreign receiver (tax residence certificate) if required
  • STA cross-border service zero-rating/exemption declaration package (submitted electronically through the e-Tax platform)

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax IV system using certified tax-control equipment or the STA e-invoice platform. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time at the point of issue; each invoice is embedded with a digital QR code, electronic signature and unique invoice code for anti-fraud digital stamping. There is no public API; integration requires approved invoicing software connected via encrypted data exchange to the STA's tax-control servers. Special VAT invoices subject to input tax deduction are additionally verified through the STA's online anti-counterfeiting check.

The STA and SAFE jointly verify export zero-rating claims using Golden Tax IV data and SAFE foreign exchange records. Failure to substantiate the customs declaration, delivery evidence, or FX repatriation within the 180-day statutory window reverses the zero-rating, triggers output VAT at the standard rate together with 0.05% daily overdue surcharge, and exposes the taxpayer to administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall. Submission of forged documents constitutes criminal activity under the PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, carrying penalties of up to life imprisonment, mandatory clawback of all refunded VAT, and unlimited look-back in cases of intentional fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) operates under a current-account convertible, capital-account administratively controlled framework administered by SAFE. For a WFOE engaged in cross-border ESG auditing, current account flows such as service fees, licence payments, and opex-related remittances can generally be converted at market rates through a designated onshore bank without central approval, provided bona fide documentation is presented. Capital account flows, however, including direct equity injections, capital reduction, cross-border financing, and outbound repatriation of after-tax profits, require SAFE registration or bank verification through the capital account information system. There is no mandatory expropriation-style conversion; export proceeds are repatriated and may be held in foreign-exchange settlement accounts or converted, subject to SAFE monitoring. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificates, and evidence of paid-in capital, and they are blocked until the WFOE has complied with China's foreign debt ratio and negative-list requirements. The practical convertibility hurdle lies in proving the real commercial substance of each transaction and securing the required coding and vouchers, rather than rationing hard currency. No formal monthly surrender ratio applies today, but banks monitor export receipts under the trade monitoring system and require identification of trade counterparties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer enforces mandatory foreign-currency surrender. For goods exports, export proceeds must be repatriated to a mainland bank, with the standard monitoring window being 90 days from customs declaration (extended up to 210 days for approved documentary-credit and post-shipment financing structures). For service exports such as ESG carbon auditing, receivable collection follows the contractual payment schedule and must be declared to SAFE as service-trade income; the bank will verify underlying invoices and contracts. Conversion of repatriated proceeds is at the enterprise's discretion, either retaining eligible funds in a foreign-exchange settlement account or selling them for RMB at the market rate, with all flows captured in SAFE's trade monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low / effectively unsupported. Foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to provide mainland deposit or FX settlement accounts to a WFOE. An offshore EMI cannot execute SAFE capital account registration, obtain payment codes, or evidence export/service receivable matching, and banks might reject funds traceable to an EMI as the remitter because it obscures the underlying beneficial owner. The WFOE should maintain local treasury through a licensed domestic Tier-1 bank and use global EMIs only in upstream supplier jurisdictions if the EMI is locally regulated and the flows are documentary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign parent wires the equity injection through SWIFT to the WFOE's FDI capital settlement account at a licensed Tier-1 bank, using the bank's SAFE-referenced surplus capital business transaction code for foreign direct investment. The bank checks the FDI registration, clears the funds into RMB under the capital account rules, and a Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report that is filed with SAMR and used for the foreign-exchange registration certificate. The remittance instructions must include the legal entity name, the FDI registration number, the precise purpose code, and the underlying equity investment agreement.For cross-border service fees (carbon audits, offset brokerage, consultancy), the WFOE uses SWIFT MT103 with underlying service contracts, tax settlement certificates and SAFE current-account declaration codes. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory if any annual related-party transaction threshold is exceeded: tangible asset transfer above RMB 200 million; intangible property transfer above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfer above RMB 100 million; related-party service transactions above RMB 40 million; or other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. Master File is generally required where the multinational group has related-party transactions and prior-year consolidated group revenue is above RMB 5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not administer a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime as in offshore jurisdictions, but the WFOE must evidence genuine economic substance through its Core Income-Generating Activities: contract negotiation, pricing decisions, client risk assumption, audit methodology, credit and operational risk, and management of carbon offset trades. The Chinese tax authorities apply the beneficial ownership doctrine, Special Tax Adjustment rules, CFC rules, permanent establishment attribution and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law to recharacterise arrangements that lack commercial substance. An ESG carbon advisory WFOE with no real staff or decision-making authority and whose profits are routed to a shell parent will face recharacterisation, transfer pricing adjustment and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Invoicing and Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes recovery of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. False invoice offences are separately punishable under the Criminal Law with fines and imprisonment. Major cases trigger public blacklisting and revocation of credit status.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and directly responsible directors may be stripped of limited-liability protection where they personally directed the invoicing scheme. They may be pursued for restitution of the Company's underpaid tax, subjected to exit bans under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, blacklisted from holding management positions, and criminally convicted under Article 201 of the Criminal Law with imprisonment up to seven years, plus criminal fines and asset confiscation.
Transfer Pricing and Offshore Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41, 47 and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, the tax authority may adjust the transactions to arm's length, deny deductions, recharacterise excessive remittances as hidden dividends, and apply interest surcharge at the benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points. The adjustment may also reduce or eliminate DTA relief and trigger secondary adjustments on deemed dividend distributions.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign artificially structured intra-group fee arrangements may be civilly liable for losses caused to the Company under director fiduciary duties. Persistent and deliberate profit stripping is treated as unit crime tax evasion, exposing responsible directors and the legal representative to criminal prosecution, personal fines, travel bans and asset recovery against their personal wealth where the corporate form is found to be a mere façade.
Abusive Treaty Relief and Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% on the amount the withholding agent failed to withhold. The tax authority can deny treaty benefits under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, collect the entire differential tax plus interest, and recover the unremitted tax from the non-resident shareholder or the WFOE as withholding agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement conduit arrangements for treaty shopping face personal civil liability for the Company's tax losses, exit restrictions, blacklisting, and prosecution as directly responsible persons of a unit crime where the arrangement is fraudulent. Criminal conviction under tax evasion provisions can lead to imprisonment up to seven years, personal fines, confiscation of the tax benefit, and piercing of the corporate veil when the WFOE is treated as a fraudulent vehicle.
