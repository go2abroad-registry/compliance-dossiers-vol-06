Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE engaged in government affairs advisory services. The shareholders must set the registered capital amount and contribution schedule in the Articles of Association and contribute by the agreed date (default maximum term of not more than five years unless otherwise approved). No blocked escrow account is legally required; however, foreign shareholders must transfer subscribed capital into a foreign-invested enterprise capital account established at a licensed bank, subject to SAFE foreign exchange registration, and the bank may impose a holding period before funds are available for operational use.

Funding & unlock path: The office premises must be covered by a building fire safety acceptance certificate or a landlord-provided fire safety clearance letter under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. The WFOE must maintain portable dry-chemical fire extinguishers, illuminated emergency exit signs, and an evacuation plan, and arrange an annual inspection of the fire safety installation. No Civil Defense construction approval or occupational hazard evaluation is required for an office-based professional services company; general workplace safety duties under the Work Safety Law apply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for zero-rating when customs export formalities are completed, the goods physically leave China, the sale is supported by genuine export documentation, and export proceeds are collected through designated banks and repatriated within the statutory timeframe. Cross-border qualifying services (including certain consulting and advisory services) may be zero-rated if supplied to an overseas client, wholly consumed outside China, and paid in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB. For this entity, exported government-affairs advisory and lobbying services can qualify only if no domestic stakeholder benefit, no in-China performance, and full offshore consumption is evidenced; otherwise domestic lobbying/consulting services are taxable at 6% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill / railway consignment note) showing the goods left China
  • Commercial export invoice and packing list
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance / Export Proceeds Receipt Certificate (FIRC-equivalent foreign exchange receipt declaration)
  • Signed export contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Export VAT e-invoice as required for refund/export records
  • Customs release or port data record cross-matching the declaration

China requires the issuance of fully digitalized electronic e-invoices through the STA e-Tax Bureau platform using STA-certified invoicing systems or national data exchange APIs. Real-time invoice data upload to the Golden Tax IV anti-fraud system and approval/clearance before issue is mandatory; each invoice is protected by a unique digital invoice number and the national VAT invoice verification system. Special VAT invoices must be reconciled at the invoice level through the e-Tax governance system.

Tax authorities cross-check customs declarations, logistics data, e-invoices, contract documents and bank foreign-exchange receipts under the Golden Tax IV system. Any zero-rating claim that is fraudulent, unverified, or filed without full repatriation documentation will be denied; refunded VAT input credits are clawed back with interest and daily late-payment surcharge (0.05% per day). Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the PRC, false export refund claims are tax evasion punishable by a fine of 50% to 500% of the refunded amount, confiscation of illegal gains, and criminal liability. The taxpayer's export-refund facilitation status may also be suspended or revoked, credit rating downgraded, and joint disciplinary 'blacklist' measures may restrict customs clearance, financing and foreign-trade privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime in which RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions but remains tightly controlled on the capital account. A WFOE may lawfully receive foreign equity capital through a SAFE-registered FDI capital account, and it may settle those foreign currency funds into RMB after the depositary bank verifies the underlying investment contract, business scope, and negative-list compliance. Export receipts, service fees, and retainer revenue fall within current account convertibility; conversion and outward remittance are permitted once the bank obtains documentary evidence of the genuine transaction, including contracts, invoices, tax settlement vouchers, and BOP reporting codes. Profit remittance and dividend distribution are treated as current account payments after corporate income tax is met, but they still require an audited balance sheet, board resolution, tax payment certificate, and SAFE/current-account declaration. Capital account outflows, such as repayment of cross-border shareholder loans or offshore investments, require separate SAFE approval and are not automatically convertible. The absence of forced surrender does not remove compliance duties: all foreign exchange receipts are monitored through the Trade Monitoring System, and unverified conversion, round-tripping, or false purpose code declarations attract severe administrative penalties. For a cross-border lobbying advisory WFOE, the treasury blueprint must maintain a strict documentary trail for every inward and outward remittance because regulators scrutinise any flow tied to government relations, foreign stakeholders, and retained earnings distributions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account and matched with the corresponding customs declaration through the SAFE Trade Monitoring System; receipts arriving more than 90 days after customs export must be pre-registered with SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the current post-2012 regime: the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency in an approved current account or voluntarily sell them to a designated tier-1 bank for RMB. Late receipt, non-receipt, or mismatched payment beyond the statutory tolerance must be reported and corrected to avoid wrongful non-repatriation charges.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex are not domestic banking licence holders in mainland China and cannot open the statutory FDI capital account or RMB basic deposit account required for a WFOE. They cannot perform capital verification, cannot accept SAFE FDI registration, and cannot lawfully settle equity capital into RMB. A SAFE-licensed domestic third-party payment institution may support small trade-related cross-border payments, but it is not a substitute for a tier-1 commercial bank account. For Stage 4 treasury purposes, the WFOE must maintain its primary clearing relationship with a licensed Chinese commercial bank; EMIs may only be used as ancillary collection processors for low-value advisory retainers where permitted.
