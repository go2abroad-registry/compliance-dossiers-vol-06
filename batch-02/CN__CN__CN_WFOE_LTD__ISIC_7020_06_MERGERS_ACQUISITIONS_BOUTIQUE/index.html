Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the locally competent state tax office; online filing and taxpayer services are performed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) digital taxpayer portal (https://tpass.chinatax.gov.cn) and the interconnected provincial electronic tax bureaus.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the WFOE business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or the local market regulator; the license displays the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number for all PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Tax registration is integrated into the SAMR license issuance through the multi-certificate integration mechanism; the competent tax authority automatically receives the incorporation data. Step 3: Within 30 days of the business license issue, the legal representative or financial officer must log into the STA Electronic Tax Bureau, complete the tax-related information reporting, identify VAT classification (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), select the financial accounting system, appoint a chief accountant, and confirm tax filing periods. Step 4: Register the bank account for tax payment by signing a tripartite electronic tax payment agreement among taxpayer, bank, and tax authority. Step 5: The USCC acts as the VAT registration number and corporate income tax taxpayer number; no separate TIN certificate is issued. Step 6: For any foreign non-resident enterprise rendering taxable services to the WFOE, the WFOE must register in the e-Tax Bureau as withholding agent for VAT and corporate income tax and obtain the overseas taxpayer reference number from the contract party for withholding tax documentation. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China, the competent tax authority may assign a temporary tax code for withholding case management, but the WFOE itself is not required to obtain a separate non-resident VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not have a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election. A WFOE is automatically a PRC resident enterprise and is taxed at the entity level on worldwide income. VAT classification as a general taxpayer is mandatory once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; a below-threshold taxpayer may optionally register as a VAT general taxpayer if adequate accounting is maintained, but this is an invoicing-rate election rather than a statutory entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration; every licensed WFOE is automatically subject to basic VAT tax registration regardless of turnover. Before reaching the threshold, an entity may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer paying VAT at 3% with simplified invoicing, or may voluntarily register as a general taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT rules, exported goods generally carry a 0% VAT rate with input VAT refund. For cross-border services, only explicitly enumerated zero-rated modern services, including certain research and development, contract energy management, integrated circuit design, and software-related technical services, are eligible for 0% VAT and input VAT refund. A middle-market cross-border M&A advisory boutique is more likely to be within the cross-border taxable services VAT exemption catalogue rather than the 0% refund regime because the service is a consulting or financial advisory service rather than an enumerated zero-rated technical service. To claim the favourable treatment, the WFOE must prove that the service recipient is located outside mainland China and provides proof of non-resident identity; the advisory service is actually supplied from China to that offshore recipient; the benefits of the service are wholly received and consumed outside China; the consideration is paid from outside China by the non-resident client; and the service does not generate economic effects inside China, such as advice used by a PRC target, a PRC subsidiary, or a PRC deal site. If these criteria are satisfied, the export service can be treated as VAT-exempt. If the service is instead characterised as one of the enumerated zero-rated modern services, the output VAT can be zero-rated and the corresponding input VAT may be refunded. If the criteria are not met, 6% VAT is charged on the service fee.

  • Customs export declaration only if physical goods are exported: the People's Republic of China Export Goods Declaration Form issued by China Customs; for pure cross-border M&A advisory services, no customs declaration is required because no merchandise crosses the PRC frontier.
  • Signed cross-border service agreement in English or Chinese identifying the non-resident client, scope of advisory services, service fee, payment schedule, and a contractual declaration that the service is consumed outside China.
  • Statutory VAT invoice or all-digital electronic invoice issued for the cross-border service, clearly marked as zero-rated or exempt service with reference to the supporting legal basis where the tax system permits such statement.
  • Official certificate of non-resident status: overseas business registration certificate, tax residence certificate, or CRS self-certification from the client.
  • Bank inward remittance receipt, being the PRC equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), issued by a designated foreign exchange bank under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) administration.
  • Full accounting working files: board-approved transaction memorandum, engagement letter, invoices, bank statements, evidence of payment settlement through SWIFT wire, and internal revenue-recognition records tracing the foreign currency into China.

VAT invoices are statutory tax documents issued through the national electronic invoicing system. The STA has deployed fully digitalized electronic invoices (all-digital e-invoices) on the Electronic Tax Bureau platform, with real-time structured invoice data transmitted to tax authorities at issuance. Taxpayers using traditional self-issued VAT invoice systems must report invoice usage and data on a monthly basis; special VAT invoices used for input-credit claims are subject to certificate verification and comparison through the national VAT invoice checking system. For cross-border export services, the taxpayer must maintain a contemporaneous documentation file linking the invoice to the overseas service contract and the incoming foreign-exchange payment; no separate fiscal stamping machine is required in the current all-digital e-invoice environment.

The tax authority will scrutinise all zero-rated or exempt export claims using the STA's invoice, customs, foreign-exchange, and bank data. If the supporting documents are absent, inconsistent, or the foreign-exchange payment is not repatriated within the prescribed period, generally 180 days from invoice date or service completion as applicable, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating or exemption, assess output VAT at 6%, impose late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day from the original due date, and may impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the amount of tax underpaid under the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where false invoices, fake export contracts, counterfeit FIRC receipts, or circular financing are used to claim refunds, the case may be referred for criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund or tax evasion under the PRC Criminal Law, with penalties including confiscation, heavy fines, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account; current-account transactions are broadly convertible after genuineness verification. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise, the core funding event is inward FDI: the foreign shareholder must complete SAFE's FDI business registration (generally digitally via a designated bank), open a foreign-exchange capital account, and remit capital against a designated purpose code; the bank will then monitor the settlement of the proceeds into RMB. The capital account remains restricted: funds cannot be used for prohibited purposes such as short-term entrusted loans, speculative equity investment outside the WFOE's approved business scope, or repayment of domestic RMB loans unless special regulatory rules apply. Profit remittance is possible under the current-account framework, but the intended dividend distribution from after-tax retained earnings is subject to a statutory audit, tax clearance, and bank verification of the profit remittance declaration; arrears cannot be set off against foreign debt. The overall balance-of-payments monitoring means the WFOE needs a bank that maintains direct SAFE connectivity and a fully documented deal chain to convert and remit funds. Strategic capital flows continue to require case-by-case approval or filing under SAFE and PBOC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 2012, the People's Republic of China has maintained a no-compulsory-surrender regime for foreign exchange. All trade-related export proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system and routed through the bank's foreign-exchange receipts monitoring workflow before being available in the enterprise's ordinary current account; the receiving bank verifies the electronic customs declaration and the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System record. Once the matching is completed, the WFOE may keep 100% of the proceeds in foreign currency, convert into RMB, or use them for approved payments; there is no mandated conversion ratio. In practice, export receivables remaining outstanding more than 90 days after the declared shipment date must be logged and explained to SAFE, and overdue entries can be rejected for conversion or settlement. Service-export fees, which are the WFOE's principal receivable, require only invoice and contract verification and may be retained without any conversion ratio, but if the bank suspects round-tripping, the full beneficiary and economic substance dossier will be demanded.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated regulator of foreign exchange and cross-border payments

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: low / not viable as a primary statutory treasury rail. Under PRC FX law, a WFOE's registered capital injection, capital-settlement monitoring, and dividend repatriation must be executed through banks that are authorized by SAFE and connected to the PBOC's CNAPS/CIPS and the SAFE data-exchange platform. Offshore digital EMIs operating under Hong Kong, Singapore, UK, or EU licenses are not authorized to open RMB local clearing accounts, issue a PRC business account, or perform the required SAFE FDI registration. Using such an EMI for the WFOE's main ledger would create a prohibited unlicensed payment operation and undermine audit evidence; therefore no fintech EMI should be proposed. An offshore finance licensing company may use an EMI only for incidental foreign-operating expenses, and never for capital account transactions or dividends.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign shareholder must first ensure the WFOE has completed SAFE's foreign-exchange registration for direct investment; the bank issues an FDI registration/verification code and opens a foreign-exchange capital account in the WFOE's name. Inbound remittances should be sent via SWIFT MT103 with the beneficiary bank and branch code, the WFOE's exact name and unified social credit code, and the SAFE-recognized capital purpose code; for a capital contribution to a WFOE, the remitter on the wire must be the registered foreign shareholder or a fully disclosed wholly-owned intermediate under SAFE direct-investment rules. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the amount against the registered FDI amount and the remitter identity, and it credits the funds to the pending verification position. Conversion into RMB takes place only after the company submits the capital-use declaration and board resolution describing the use of proceeds; the bank marks the related SAFE code and monitors subsequent RMB payments. Capital verification at the legal level is satisfied by the bank's receipt record and SAFE registration code; if the shareholder's subscription agreement or later financing requires it, a PRC certified public accountant may issue a capital verification report.Outbound payments for the WFOE's M&A advisory trade disbursements are processed as current-account service payments, not as goods trade. The company must submit the underlying engagement agreement, a tax-compliant invoice, proof of service delivery, and the SAFE service trade reporting form. The clearing rails available are (i) SWIFT MT103 for advisory fees, referral fees, and operating disbursements settled in USD, EUR, or HKD after conversion of RMB through the designated bank; (ii) cross-border RMB transfers through CIPS for contracts priced in RMB; and (iii) commercial letters of credit or documentary collections only if the M&A boutique is financing or facilitating a physical trade transaction as part of a deal, as they cannot be used for pure advisory fees. Where the beneficiary is an overseas affiliate of the founder, the bank will treat it as a related-party payment and require arm's-length transfer pricing documentation plus board approval.Onshore CNY forward contracts with a domestic bank under a real trade or balance-sheet exposure; the hedge must be evidenced by the underlying M&A advisory agreement or signed deal timeline, and tenors cannot exceed the expected invoice settlement date.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for the conversion and re-conversion of capital or funding flows, permitted only if the underlying debt or transaction has been registered with SAFE and the bank's credit and documentation committee has approved the derivative limit.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permitted as an onshore instrument for a PRC WFOE; offshore NDFs are not an acceptable substitute because using an offshore NDF to hedge onshore RMB cash flow would be considered an evasion of SAFE controls.Currency swap boundaries: no speculative layering is allowed; derivative gains and losses must be recognized in ordinary profit and loss, and all settlements must flow through the designated domestic clearing bank without bypassing the mandatory SAFE verification rails.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: low / not viable as a primary statutory treasury rail. Under PRC FX law, a WFOE's registered capital injection, capital-settlement monitoring, and dividend repatriation must be executed through banks that are authorized by SAFE and connected to the PBOC's CNAPS/CIPS and the SAFE data-exchange platform. Offshore digital EMIs operating under Hong Kong, Singapore, UK, or EU licenses are not authorized to open RMB local clearing accounts, issue a PRC business account, or perform the required SAFE FDI registration. Using such an EMI for the WFOE's main ledger would create a prohibited unlicensed payment operation and undermine audit evidence; therefore no fintech EMI should be proposed. An offshore finance licensing company may use an EMI only for incidental foreign-operating expenses, and never for capital account transactions or dividends.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory for any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds: (a) tangible property transactions above RMB 200 million; (b) intangible property transactions above RMB 100 million; (c) financial asset transactions above RMB 100 million; (d) other transactions (including management, consulting, and M&A advisory services) above RMB 40 million. Master File is required when the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in a year exceeds RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country (CbC) Report is required for a multinational enterprise group with previous-year consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China enforces a substance-over-form doctrine and general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR). For DTA relief and beneficial-ownership determinations, the tax authority examines whether the foreign service or royalty recipient has real personnel, assets, and decision-making capacity in the claimed jurisdiction; if the offshore entity is a mere conduit with no Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as deal sourcing, negotiation, risk assumption, or senior managerial decisions, the treaty benefit is denied. While China does not operate UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, the concept of CIGA is embedded in Public Notice [2018] No. 9 on Beneficial Ownership and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. An M&A advisory boutique must ensure that foreign parent entities receiving success fees, referral fees, or commission fees perform actual services rather than simply routing invoices through tax-haven jurisdictions; a thin capital or shell operation will trigger recharacterization, full 10% withholding on profits deemed repatriated, and the risk of criminal tax fraud sanctions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Invoicing and Expense Fabrication

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law (PRC) demands recovery of the underpaid tax with a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded amount and a daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05%. Issuing false special VAT invoices violates Article 205 of the Criminal Law, carrying a fine of 2-5 times the invoice amount and imprisonment up to and including the death penalty for exceptionally serious cases; ordinary falsification under Article 201 carries up to seven years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors, senior managers, and the actual controller are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the corporate assets misappropriated or the tax loss suffered where they directed the invoicing scheme. Under Article 20 of the Company Law, abuse of the corporate personality to evade taxes allows creditors and the tax authority to pierce the corporate veil and attach directors' personal property. The director is listed in the 'Serious Tax Violation Trust Violation Blacklist', resulting in restrictions on taking trains and planes, a travel ban (相关出境限制) for the relevant director, banking rejections, and disqualification from serving as a senior officer of any enterprise for several years. Upon criminal conviction, the responsible director faces 3-7 years imprisonment, criminal fines, and confiscation of proceeds.
Transfer Pricing Mis-Pricing and Profit Shifting to Related Parties

Statutory sanction: Articles 41, 44, and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law permit the tax authority to adjust income by applying the arm's length principle; Article 114 of the Implementation Regulations imposes interest on the additional tax at the base rate plus 5 percentage points, compounded; Article 6 of the Measures for Special Tax Adjustments (Announcement No. 6 of 2017) adds a penalty of 10%-20% of the underpaid tax if documentation is not provided, and Article 64 of the Tax Collection Law imposes a 50%-500% penalty for intentional understatement.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the intercompany fee policy without an economic analysis can be held personally liable for the additional CIT, interest, and penalties under the 'deepening the veil' doctrine when the arrangement is found to be a fraud on the tax authority and creditors. Chinese law allows the tax bureau to apply to the court to freeze personal assets of directors if corporate assets are insufficient and the director is proven to have orchestrated the asset stripping. The director may be blacklisted for tax non-compliance (travel ban, high-consumption limit) and may face criminal charges under Article 201 of the Criminal Law as a co-conspirator of tax evasion, risking up to 7 years in prison, restitution of all shifted profits, and court-ordered disqualification from any board position in China for up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Public Notice [2018] No. 9 and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law deny treaty benefits where the recipient is not the beneficial owner or where the arrangement has as its main purpose the obtaining of treaty benefits. The tax authority will impose the statutory 10% withholding on dividends (or 20% on royalties/interest recharacterized as dividends), plus a surcharge of 0.05% per day, a penalty of 50%-500% of the under-withheld tax, and interest. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44, the enterprise may be ordered to provide a bank guarantee or have its export/import and outbound remittance functions restricted.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally exposed to creditor and tax claims by piercing of the corporate veil if they acted in bad faith to misuse the corporate form; they must restore all corporate assets and pay the shortfall with personal funds. The director is placed on the serious-tax-violation blacklist: travel restrictions are imposed including prohibition from leaving mainland China at the exit border, denial of first-class travel, and restriction from high-consumption activities. If the arrangement is prosecuted as fraudulent treaty abuse, the director faces criminal investigation for tax fraud using deception (Criminal Law Art. 201), with imprisonment up to 7 years, criminal fines, and confiscation of improperly transferred dividends. Personal asset tracing and freezing orders can extend to offshore bank accounts controlled by the director, and the director may be subject to a 10-year ban from corporate management under the Company Law and Market Supervision disciplinary rules.
