Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Administration Bureau (e-Tax), accessible through the provincial electronic tax bureau login on the STA official website.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the local Administration for Market Regulation. The WFOE must, within 30 days of business license issuance, complete post-licensing tax registration through the e-Tax portal: file taxpayer basic information, legal representative and responsible person details, registered address, scope of business, bank deposit account reporting, accounting/financial system filing, and VAT taxpayer type selection (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer). For a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China, the non-resident entity must either apply for tax registration with the competent tax authority in charge of the Chinese payer or withholding agent, or be registered through a withholding agent using the non-resident tax registration application; the tax authority will issue a Tax Identification Number (TIN) for treaty relief, withholding filing, and settlement of China-source income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

As a Chinese legal entity, the WFOE is required to register for VAT upon establishment. The economic threshold that triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below that amount the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer (levy rate 3%, with certain preferential reductions). A small-scale taxpayer with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 is currently exempt from VAT. For cross-border inbound digital services supplied by a non-resident without an establishment, the Chinese recipient is required to withhold and pay VAT on a reverse-charge basis; there is no turnover threshold for that withholding obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border supply of services qualifies for VAT zero-rating only when the supply is made to an entity or individual outside China and the service is completely consumed outside China. For the WFOE's virtual performance hosting, metaverse access, and digital ticket tokenization services, zero-rating may be claimed if the WFOE maintains the position that such services constitute cross-border information technology or platform services, the customer or audience is outside China, the performance and platform access are rendered through the internet without any place of consumption in China, payment is received from abroad in foreign exchange, and the service falls within the published catalogue of cross-border taxable services eligible for zero-rating or exemption. If any part of the virtual concert is consumed by persons located in China, or the supply is made to a domestic establishment, that portion remains taxable at the 6% VAT rate. If the services are recharacterized as cultural or lifestyle services, only VAT exemption rather than zero-rating may be available, and input VAT recovery would be correspondingly restricted.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (the official export declaration for physical goods) - mandatory only where physical goods, merchandise, or physical deliverables are embedded in the virtual concert package; pure digital services do not require a customs declaration, but if goods are bundled, the export declaration and commodity classification code are required
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - mandatory only for the physical-goods component; for pure digital services, the platform delivery logs, IP geolocation records, and service delivery timestamps serve as the digital equivalent
  • Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form (the current statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by the designated foreign exchange bank evidencing inward remittance of export consideration from the overseas customer in foreign currency
  • Zero-rated export VAT invoice issued through the e-invoice platform with the cross-border services tax code, plus the underlying service export contract, customer identity and location documentation, and proof of performance of the virtual event outside China

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Under the nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice system, each invoice receives a single tax-control code and is transmitted to the tax authority in real time; there is no separate physical fiscal hardware clearance. Legacy tax-controlled invoices, if permitted, must still be uploaded daily through tax-controlled invoice software. The e-invoice system applies automated anti-fraud controls including invoice amount quotas, frequency limits, invoice reason-code matching, and automatic cross-matching with VAT returns, customs export records, and foreign exchange inflow data.

Where the zero-rating or exemption is claimed without sufficient documentation, the STA may disallow the zero rate and assess VAT at 6% retroactively, impose a late-payment surcharge (currently 0.05% per day), and impose an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. Cases involving forged customs declarations, false foreign-exchange receipts, fabricated digital delivery logs, or intentional evasion may trigger export tax rebate fraud liability under the Criminal Law (fraudulent export rebate and tax evasion provisions), resulting in criminal penalties against responsible persons. The tax authorities routinely cross-match e-invoice data with bank foreign-exchange receipts, SAFE foreign-exchange reporting data, and digital platform logs; accordingly, supporting records and documentation should be retained for at least 10 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese yuan is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, subject to genuine transaction verification and documentary compliance. Capital account transactions, however, remain heavily regulated and require SAFE registration or approval. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate from SAFE before accepting any inbound equity or loan capital. Registered capital in foreign currency may only be settled under approved capital account payments capped by the disclosed investment amount and actual business needs. Export proceeds must be repatriated through designated banks but may be retained in foreign currency current accounts or converted to RMB voluntarily; there is no mandatory conversion ratio under the post-2012 administration rules. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders requires a full audit of the WFOE financial statements, corporate income tax clearance certificates, board dividend resolutions, and verification that the underlying profits were actually earned and not sourced from speculative FX gains. Remittance is processed through the bank under current account Article 3 codes after ensuring the entity holds an annual inspection certificate and no unresolved foreign debt exposure. In practice, banks exercise bank-level soft controls requiring underlying contracts and invoices for every material remittance, creating timing hurdles but not absolute barriers. Crypto-related proceeds from metaverse ticketing are not recognized as lawful current account receipts unless converted to fiat via licensed payment institutions and documented with a valid trade nexus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days after the export customs declaration date, unless earlier settlement is agreed in the trade contract. No mandatory surrender ratio is currently imposed; a WFOE may retain 100% of foreign exchange earnings in its current account or convert them to RMB at the prevailing onshore bank rate. All export receipts must be matched against the China Customs electronic verification system and supporting invoices, bills of lading, and clean payment orders before bankers accept or release the funds.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Foreign fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are effectively barred from servicing Chinese-resident legal entities without an onshore payment institution license and a non-bank payment business permit from the People's Bank of China. No standalone EMI licensing regime exists for offshore digital banks; therefore cross-border digital payment intermediaries such as Stripe and USDC settlement rails are not permitted to facilitate direct onshore treasury operations. The WFOE must open domestic bank accounts and clear inbound and outbound flows through the licensed commercial banking network. Fintech solutions may only be used as ancillary front-end payment gateways if the underlying settlement is funneled through an onshore licensed bank or through an international card network that contracts with a licensed acquiring bank. Direct crypto settlement into the WFOE bank account is prohibited, and virtual concert ticket sales collected in USDC offshore must be liquidated outside mainland China and brought in through compliant trade or service remittance channels, subject to all prevailing SAFE and anti-money laundering checks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be executed through a SWIFT telegraphic transfer from the foreign parent company's bank to the WFOE's USD capital escrow account opened with a Chinese commercial bank. Prior to the wire, the WFOE must complete SAFE foreign exchange business registration with the bank acting as a designated agent, and obtain the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate (FDI). The wire must include a clear payment narrative referencing the registered foreign investment code and contract number. Upon receipt, the bank will verify the payment matched with the approved FDI information and effect capital settlement to RMB either entirely or as required for the enterprise's actual admitted expenditures, following the Negative List sectors and the payment expense authenticity rules. Capital verification must be performed by a licensed Chinese accountant, who issues a capital verification report based on the bank stamped inflow notification, Safe registration information, and the signed articles of association. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million in aggregate controlled transactions triggers preparation and retention of contemporaneous Local File documentation; a Master File is additionally required for large groups meeting the consolidated turnover and transaction filing thresholds under China's administrative measures. The annual related-party transaction report filing is mandatory independent of the documentation threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form under CIT Law Article 41 and Article 47. For DTA relief, the non-resident beneficial owner must prove substantive activities and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in its residence jurisdiction. A WFOE or offshore intermediate lacking staff, office, business risk, and decision-making authority cannot claim treaty benefits; the income may be recharacterised by the Chinese tax authority to ensure the economically relevant business activity is taxed where it actually occurs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Digital Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 for false VAT special invoices carries penalties ranging from fixed-term imprisonment up to life imprisonment with property confiscation. Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax plus daily 0.05% overdue interest and compulsory recovery of all underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors, de facto officers, and legal representatives may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 when the corporate form is abused to evade tax. They can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts, suffer asset freezes, be placed on the state exit-control blacklist with travel restrictions, and be subject to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment from three to seven years for aggravated tax evasion, in addition to criminal fines.
Transfer Pricing, Thin Capitalization and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration is empowered to make transfer pricing adjustments under EIT Law Articles 41, 44, and 46, imposing additional tax plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%. Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation may entail administrative fines up to RMB 50,000 and may shift the burden of proof, causing unilateral adjustments. If the mispricing is accompanied by fraudulent documentation, Tax Collection Law Article 63 penalties of 50% to five times the tax shortfall apply.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or executing artificial profit-shifting arrangements may be required to appear for tax audits, may face personal liability if assets are distributed to the offshore parent while tax obligations remain unpaid, and may be subject to travel bans and blacklisting by the tax and migration authorities. In severe false-documentation cases, criminal tax evasion charges can result in imprisonment of three to seven years and personal fines.
Withholding Tax Default and Treaty Abuse Through Shell Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 69: the withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable to be ordered to recover the tax and to pay a fine of 50% to three times the amount of tax that should have been withheld. Where treaty benefits have been obtained based on fraudulent substance claims, the EIT Law Article 47 anti-avoidance rule reverses the treaty rate, imposes additional tax, and adds late-payment interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the WFOE to make outbound distributions while deliberately avoiding withholding can be personally liable for the unremitted withholding tax when corporate assets are stripped. They may be subjected to civil asset restitution, travel bans, exit-control blacklisting under the joint punishment mechanism, disqualification as directors, and criminal liability for tax evasion or money laundering where cross-border movement of digital assets is involved, potentially leading to imprisonment and forfeiture of assets.
