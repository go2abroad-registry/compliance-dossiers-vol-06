Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) limited liability company engaged in digital engineering and design services. The registered capital must be determined by the shareholders and stated in the Articles of Association. Under Article 47 of the Company Law, subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from incorporation unless a shorter period is required by an applicable regulatory regime. No blocked escrow account is mandated; capital contributions are remitted directly to the company's RMB or foreign-currency capital account opened with a SAFE-designated commercial bank after completion of foreign investment and foreign exchange registration.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online service platform accessible via the official STA website (https://www.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China, the tax identification number (TIN) is procured as follows: (1) Complete company incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining the Business License which carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC automatically constitutes the statutory taxpayer identification number for tax purposes. (2) Within 30 days of business license issuance, file supplementary tax information with the competent local tax authority (sub-bureau of STA) through the 'multi-certificate integration' channel, submitting the company charter, legal representative identity documents, business address proof, and financial/accounting system details. (3) The tax authority will formally register the entity, determine applicable taxes and filing periods, and assess VAT taxpayer status (small-scale vs general taxpayer). (4) Obtain a tax-control U-Key or cloud-based e-tax account to enable invoice issuance and electronic filing. The WFOE's USCC (18-digit code) serves as the VAT and CIT registration number, valid for both domestic and cross-border transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic VAT registration is mandatory for all enterprises upon incorporation regardless of turnover. For general VAT taxpayer status, the mandatory economic turnover threshold is RMB 5,000,000 (inclusive of VAT-exclusive taxable sales) over any rolling 12-month period; exceeding this threshold obligates the taxpayer to register as a general VAT taxpayer. Smaller taxpayers may choose simplified status below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE (registered as a general VAT taxpayer) may claim 0% VAT on offshore engineering CAD/BIM services if the services are exported to an overseas customer, the services are wholly consumed outside China, the income is paid from abroad in foreign currency, and the services are not connected to goods or immovable property located in China. Design services, including building information modeling and structural drafting, fall under the PRC VAT zero-rated service categories for offshore services (财税〔2016〕36号 Annex 4). The taxpayer must maintain a written contract evidencing the cross-border nature of the supply and file a zero-rated export VAT declaration in the STA e-tax system.

  • Signed export service contract between the WFOE and the overseas client, specifying the type and scope of CAD/BIM services
  • Foreign currency inward remittance certificate (银行收汇水单) or bank credit advice proving receipt of payment from overseas
  • Foreign exchange income declaration (涉外收入申报单) filed with the designated foreign exchange bank under SAFE regulations
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice (零税率增值税普通发票) issued through the e-tax platform, marked as export service
  • Service delivery evidence (e.g., BIM model/drawing delivery record, electronic acceptance certificate) demonstrating execution outside China

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's digital e-invoice platform (全电发票/数电票) or STA-certified tax-control equipment (tax U-key or cloud e-invoicing). Invoice data is transmitted in near-real-time to the STA's Golden Tax System Phase IV (Jin-Si), which performs automated validation, matches invoice data against reported VAT returns, and affixes a unique digital anti-fraud stamp. Bulk invoice issuance requires system-to-system API integration or U-key authentication. Physical fiscal stamping is abolished; digital authentication and real-time clearance apply to all VAT invoices.

Fraudulent or unsubstantiated claims of export zero-rating trigger retrospective clawback of all zero-rated input tax refunds and the application of interest surcharges calculated from the original refund date. Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (Articles 63 and 66), a taxpayer fabricating export supplies or falsely claiming export VAT refunds is liable to repay the fraudulently obtained refund and to a penalty of 1–5 times the amount of tax evaded; criminal liability for tax evasion may be pursued where the amount exceeds statutory thresholds. The STA's Golden Tax System deploys automated risk screening for export transactions, including mismatch checks between contracts, foreign exchange receipts, service timelines, and invoice records, and may initiate full tax audits. Failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days without an approved exception results in mandatory reversal of zero-rated treatment and demand for payment of VAT on the previously zero-rated consideration, plus late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls. The current account is broadly convertible for genuine trade in goods and services after banks complete an authenticity and uniqueness review, while the capital account remains heavily managed and liberalized only through qualified SAFE registration, approval, or pilot schemes. An onshore wholly foreign-owned enterprise receiving foreign equity capital must use a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated domestic bank. Conversion of that foreign currency into RMB is permitted only for approved operational purposes such as wages, rent, taxes, procurement, and ordinary current account expenses; speculative investments, on-lending to related parties, and securities trading are prohibited without specific SAFE authority. Each conversion from the capital account requires a capital account payment confirmation and documentary proof of use of proceeds. Profit remittances to overseas shareholders are treated as current account payments and do not require a standalone SAFE approval, but the bank will require annual audited financial statements, accumulated distributable profits, board resolutions, and a foreign enterprise income tax withholding and payment certificate. The old compulsory export surrender regime has been abolished, but repatriation requirements remain. Overall, a WFOE can execute legitimate cross-border treasury flows if every underlying transaction is real, documented, and correctly coded in the bank's SAFE reporting system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may retain 100% of qualifying export proceeds in a domestic foreign currency account or voluntarily settle them into RMB at the interbank exchange rate. However, repatriation is mandatory. For physical goods exports, proceeds must be collected and repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days of the customs declaration date. For BIM/CAD engineering and structural drafting services falling under ISIC 7110, proceeds must be repatriated within the contractual payment terms and generally no later than 180 days from the relevant invoice date; service contracts must support the timing. The WFOE must credit all service billing to its designated domestic foreign currency current account and provide contracts, invoices, deliverables evidence, and bank verification for any conversion. Failure to repatriate on time triggers SAFE monitoring, penalty exposure, and bank account restriction.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a primary onshore clearing rail. Wise and similar fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking banks or corporate account providers by the PBOC or the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission. A PRC-registered WFOE must route foreign currency receipts through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE reporting. Wise may only be used in the offshore treasury structure of the overseas parent, not as the operating account of the Chinese entity. Wise is legally barred from providing local RMB settlement accounts to mainland-resident commercial entities for their domestic operations, and any attempt to use Wise to mask the China settlement trail would violate payment institution regulations and anti-money laundering requirements. For this entity, the compliant route is a SWIFT wire to a domestic bank current account with correct service-trade purpose codes, not an EMI payout rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas shareholder must wire the equity injection in USD, EUR, or HKD using a corresponding bank instruction with the SAFE BOP trade/investment declaration category for direct investment equity into China, typically coded in the bank system as FDI equity investment (for example SAFE declaration code 621011 for establishment or increase of equity). The remittance must be directed to the WFOE's dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the chosen designated bank. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete the SAFE FDI registration and obtain the bank-approved foreign exchange registration record. Upon receipt, the bank will credit the capital account, report the inflow to SAFE, and issue a capital account payment confirmation. Conversion into RMB for payroll, rent, taxes, and collective operating expenses must be supported by invoices and contracts. The WFOE should also file a capital verification schedule with a licensed Chinese CPA when the bank or articles of association require it; in all cases the bank's FDI data and confirmation records are the primary compliance evidence.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary onshore clearing rail. Wise and similar fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking banks or corporate account providers by the PBOC or the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission. A PRC-registered WFOE must route foreign currency receipts through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE reporting. Wise may only be used in the offshore treasury structure of the overseas parent, not as the operating account of the Chinese entity. Wise is legally barred from providing local RMB settlement accounts to mainland-resident commercial entities for their domestic operations, and any attempt to use Wise to mask the China settlement trail would violate payment institution regulations and anti-money laundering requirements. For this entity, the compliant route is a SWIFT wire to a domestic bank current account with correct service-trade purpose codes, not an EMI payout rail.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 / Line 1 - Sales Revenue (Ying Ye Shou Ru) Gross revenue from civil infrastructure CAD/BIM drafting, offshore building information modeling services, software licensing, and related engineering outsourcing contracts. A WFOE is a resident taxpayer in mainland China and must include worldwide revenue in this line; it is not itself a non-resident enterprise. For cross-border CAD/BIM work performed in China for overseas clients, revenue must be recognized under the completed-contract or input-taxed method actually implemented. Any related-party service fee recharged to an offshore group company must be arm's length, supported by a written agreement, and consistent with the transfer pricing documentation; failing to book inbound/outbound service revenue from or to foreign related parties is a classic audit red flag.
Box 2 / Line 2 - Operating Costs (Ying Ye Cheng Ben) Direct costs of providing BIM/CAD services, including BIM engineer salaries, cloud subscription fees, software amortization, drafting subcontract costs, rendering hardware depreciation, and direct project overhead. Only costs connected with actual China-source services are deductible. If part of the BIM workflow is subcontracted to a foreign related party or a group shared-service center, the WFOE must obtain contemporaneous evidence of the arm's length consideration and must confirm that the foreign subcontractor has complied with Chinese WHT obligations in respect of any CAD/BIM royalties or technical service fees. Deductions for a 'notional' allocation of foreign head-office costs should be rejected unless a valid cost-sharing agreement exists.
Box 3 / Line 3 - Taxes and Surcharges (Shui Jin Ji Fu Jia) Deductible business taxes and surcharges paid during the tax year, including urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, and local surcharges related to VAT. The WFOE must reconcile this line with the VAT return and bank payment records. For non-resident suppliers, the WFOE as withholding agent must withhold VAT and enterprise income tax when paying royalties or interest; failure to report the withholding in the proper filing period invalidates the corresponding deduction and triggers interest and penalties.
Box 4 / Line 4 - Selling Expenses (Xiao Shou Fei Yong) Marketing, bid costs, international exhibition costs, overseas client business trips, and commissions paid to sales agents or business development partners. If commissions are paid to a non-resident sales agent that has no China PE, the WFOE must treat the payment as a fee paid to a non-resident and comply with the withholding filing requirements where the service is deemed sourced in China. A sales agent structured as a mailbox company in a tax-friendly jurisdiction with no real office, staff, or risk-bearing functions will not satisfy the beneficial ownership test and could be recharacterized, disallow the deduction, and trigger WHT.
Box 5 / Line 5 - Administrative Expenses (Guan Li Fei Yong) Office rental, local administrative salaries, professional fees, directors' fees, insurance, and general corporate overhead of the Chinese WFOE. This line is the primary evidence of economic substance. For a WFOE providing CAD/BIM services, tax authorities will expect to see local physical office space, full-time design engineers, project managers, decision-makers, and accounting staff in China. Excessive management fees charged by the foreign parent without actual services are not deductible and may be adjusted as disguised profit repatriation; the parent fee must be supported by agreements, time allocation, deliverables, and beneficial ownership.
Box 6 / Line 6 - Financial Expenses (Cai Wu Fei Yong) Interest expense on bank loans and intercompany borrowings, bank charges, guarantee fees, and net foreign-exchange losses. Interest paid to a non-resident lender or foreign shareholder is subject to thin-capitalization rules: deductible interest is generally limited to a 2:1 debt-to-equity safe harbor for non-financial enterprises (4:1 for financial enterprises). The WFOE must withhold 10% enterprise income tax on outbound interest unless a tax treaty reduces the rate; a tax residence certificate and beneficial ownership documentation must be obtained before applying a reduced treaty rate. Foreign-exchange losses must be realized and booked; unrealized FX losses may be added back.
Box 7 / Line 7 - Asset Impairment Losses (Zi Chan Jian Zhi Sun Shi) Provision for bad debts, impairment of contract assets, receivable loss provisions, and write-downs of idle equipment or software. Tax law allows deductions for actual losses only; general bad-debt provisions are not deductible for CIT. A difference between accounting impairment and taxable write-off must be reported in the tax adjustment schedule. If the receivable is from an offshore related party, the WFOE must prove actual exhaustion and commercial purpose; abandoned related-party receivables are a red flag for hidden profit repatriation and can result in deemed dividend WHT plus penalties.
Box 8 / Line 15 - Total Profit (Li Run Zong E) Accounting pre-tax profit computed under the PRC accounting standards or acceptable financial reporting framework, before adjustments in the tax return. For a WFOE, total profit must agree with the audited financial statements. The WFOE must disclose any non-resident shareholder loans, leases, royalties, services, and dividend payments in the related-party transaction schedules. An artificially low total profit caused by shifting income to a low-tax jurisdiction or booking revenue abroad while costs and employees remain in China will trigger transfer pricing adjustment, interest, penalties, and possible Criminal Law exposure for the representative person.
