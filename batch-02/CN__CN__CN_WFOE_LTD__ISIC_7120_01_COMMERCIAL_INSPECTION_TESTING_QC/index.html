Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law (2023) and the Foreign Investment Law impose no mandatory minimum paid-in capital for a WFOE LLC; however, the shareholders shall determine and state the amount of registered capital in the Articles of Association, and unless otherwise provided by specific industry rules (e.g., licensed inspection services may require sufficient working capital under CMA qualification rules), the company may adopt a subscription regime. No blocked escrow account is required for a WFOE LLC in the general commercial testing and inspection sector, except that the subscribed capital must be contributed within five years of incorporation in accordance with Articles 47 and 55 of the Company Law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and occupational clearance are Not Applicable for the principal WFOE activity because the company operates as an inspection and audit agency at third-party client factories, with no bilateral fixed premises of a high-risk type. The registered office (standard commercial office) must nonetheless obtain a Fire Safety Self-Check Certificate issued by the local Fire and Rescue Department (or the building management’s fire protection certificate) on the basis of a low-hazard occupancy classification. If the company later installs an in-house laboratory, it must obtain a Fire Safety Acceptance Report (建设工程消防验收备案) for any renovation exceeding 300 m2, and complete a workplace safety facility registration with the Emergency Management Bureau, including installation of fire extinguishers (minimum 1 per 50 m2), emergency exit signage, and an annual fire drill record. Additionally, all field inspectors must be covered under occupational injury insurance and a personal protective equipment (PPE) safety protocol in accordance with the Work Safety Law of the PRC, and the company must appoint a safety manager if it employs more than 100 people or if the inspection scope involves hazardous environments at client sites.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through provincial, municipal, and county-level tax offices; online electronic portal is the Electronic Tax Administration platform (e-Tax), accessed at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with registration data exchanged through the State Administration for Market Regulation one-window system and customs trade registration through the General Administration of Customs.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese WFOE: Step 1, the foreign investor incorporates the WFOE with the local Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued Business License carries the 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for Chinese resident enterprises. Step 2, SAMR transmits the incorporation record to the STA, and the WFOE must complete tax registration details through the e-Tax portal within 30 calendar days from the license date, including accounting basis, business scope, competent tax office, and VAT taxpayer status; no separate TIN certificate is issued because the USCC is the TIN. Step 3, the WFOE activates the full digital electronic invoice system and, if it will export goods or supply zero-rated services, the VAT export refund filing preference in the e-Tax refund module. Step 4, the WFOE completes customs registration for import/export enterprise using the USCC and the foreign exchange current-account registration with an authorised bank under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no Chinese legal entity: the foreign entity must file a non-resident tax registration with the competent STA office for the Chinese payer, agent, or permanent establishment; the dossier must include the foreign certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate, board resolution authorizing the Chinese applicant, power of attorney, identity documents of the appointed representative, and the relevant service or trade agreement. The STA assigns a non-resident taxpayer identification number in the Non-Resident Taxpayer Administration module. Any VAT due on services supplied by an unregistered non-resident seller to a Chinese payer is generally withheld by the Chinese payer through the e-Tax withholding module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box or entity classification election for enterprise income tax; legal entity form determines taxation. There is no hybrid mismatch election. The only election relevant here is VAT status: a small-scale VAT taxpayer below the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales threshold may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status if it has sound accounting and a fixed place of business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration nexus is established immediately upon incorporation or upon a permanent establishment in China; there is no turnover threshold for tax registration itself. For VAT classification, compulsory general taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; taxpayers below this threshold remain small-scale VAT taxpayers using simplified VAT treatment unless they voluntarily elect general taxpayer status. For this inspection/testing business, the applicable VAT rate for the service supply is 6%; China's principal goods rate is 13% and lower rates of 9% and 6% apply to other categories.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of exported goods requires the goods to be physically exported from China with a recorded Customs Export Declaration, the exporter to hold the corresponding input VAT documentation, and the export proceeds to be received in foreign currency and repatriated through an authorised bank within the statutory SAFE period. Zero-rating of cross-border services is limited to the MOF/STA cross-border taxable services zero-rating and exemption catalogue, requires the service to be supplied to an overseas entity, the service to be wholly consumed outside China, and the consideration to be received from the foreign counterparty. On-site factory pre-shipment inspection, testing, and audit agency work physically performed in China does not automatically qualify as zero-rated and is normally subject to VAT at 6%; a specific exemption or zero-rating entry must be established for the exact service category, otherwise the supplier cannot claim a 0% rate and refund of input VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (the China Customs export cargo declaration bearing the unique customs bill number and exporter name matching the VAT taxpayer and bank collection records) as proof of physical export for goods.
  • Transport document proving border crossing: original ocean or multimodal Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or rail/road customs-transport waybill, as applicable; for international express, the customs-registered parcel or mail list.
  • Commercial export invoice and export sales contract or purchase order identifying the overseas buyer, together with the packing list and any product inspection or quality certificate required by the commodity category.
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: the Cross-Border Receipt and Payment Declaration issued by the settling bank, which is China's statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate, plus SAFE trade-foreign-exchange monitoring records showing receipt of the export proceeds within the statutory deadline and the bank settlement or sale voucher.
  • Input VAT documentation used to compute the refund: Special VAT Invoices from domestic suppliers and the Customs Import VAT Special Payment Certificate for imported inputs; for a service export, the cross-border service contract, service delivery and acceptance report, foreign-currency invoice, and proof of payment from the overseas service recipient.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's FX administration, current account items are largely convertible on a genuine transparency basis. Legitimate trade and service receipts and payments, including pre-shipment inspection fees, laboratory testing charges, and technical service fees, can be cleared through designated banks after ordinary authenticity review, without case-by-case SAFE approval. Capital account items are stringently controlled. Inbound FDI must be registered with SAFE under the Direct Investment registration regime before a domestic designated bank can open a foreign exchange capital account for the WFOE. Funds may enter under an approved capital quota and then be settled into RMB only for permitted business uses, such as payroll, rent, inspection equipment, consumables, third-party laboratory subcontracting, and tax liabilities. Profit remittance to a foreign parent is treated as a current-account distribution but requires post-tax proof: annual statutory audit, CIT/VAT settlement sheets, withholding tax filing for passive income, board dividend resolution, and a clean SAFE declaration. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation and overseas retention restrictions, but there is no mandatory surrender ratio; proceeds may be retained in foreign exchange or settled into RMB at the entity's discretion. Remittance bottlenecks typically arise when the supporting contract, invoice, customs or service verification, and tax paperwork do not reconcile with the declared payment code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic bank foreign exchange account within 180 days of the customs export declaration or completion of the underlying service export under SAFE monitoring. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; WFOEs may retain the foreign currency receivables or voluntarily convert them into RMB. SAFE applies aggregate dynamic verification and may request documentary proof of the service contract, invoice, inspection certificate, and payment receipt if the cash flow does not match the declared economic substance.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); foreign exchange regulatory administration delegated to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to nil as a primary banking solution. Overseas fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, and Currencycloud do not hold PBOC/CBIRC banking or payment institution licenses in mainland China and are legally barred from opening domestic CNY or FX enterprise accounts, maintaining branch deposit-taking operations, or honoring SAFE controls on behalf of a Chinese WFOE. They may appear only as a software layer integrated with a licensed Chinese sponsor bank, but they cannot replace the FDI capital escrow, SAFE registration, tax withholding, or cross-border payment rails. The WFOE must maintain its account relationships with a licensed domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under China's Accounting Law and financial reporting rules, every enterprise with limited liability, including a WFOE, is legally required to prepare annual financial statements in accordance with PRC Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises or the applicable Small Business Accounting Standards. Independent audit is compulsory for: (1) all companies that are subsidiaries of listed groups or that are themselves listed, or those whose annual financial statements must be audited under applicable securities regulation; (2) enterprises with a total asset scale, annual revenue, or workforce that meet thresholds established by sector regulators, particularly foreign-invested enterprises whose regulatory certificate, banking, or foreign-exchange compliance requires certified financial statements; (3) enterprises whose local Market Supervision Administration or tax authority conducts a statutory inspection and demands an audited annual report; (4) enterprises applying for a government subsidy, bidding, or a public procurement; (5) enterprises that meet sector-specific threshold requirements, for example under the Measures for Foreign Investment Information Reporting, where audited statements are required if total investment or operating turnover exceeds the amounts stipulated by the competent authority or if the enterprise has cross-border investment and financing. Quantitative thresholds commonly applied by Chinese banks, tax authorities, and foreign-exchange authorities are: total assets above RMB 30 million; annual operating revenue above RMB 30 million; or 50 or more employees on a full-time equivalent basis. However, because the PRC Rules for Enterprise Financial Reporting do not carve out a universal audited threshold for all unlisted WFOEs, a WFOE engaged in commercial inspection and testing must obtain a CPA audit whenever: revenue exceeds RMB 20 million; total assets exceed RMB 20 million; headcount exceeds 30 persons on an annual average basis; the enterprise applies for a class of foreign-exchange current-account annual reconciliation requiring a certified auditor's report; or the enterprise is selected by the tax authority for a major tax-case investigation and an independent audit is required under the evidence-collection rules. The governing accounting framework is the PRC Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises (CAS/ASBE), which converge with IFRS in almost all material respects; Small-Scale Enterprises may apply the Simplified Accounting Standards for Small Enterprises (EAS) but, once an independent audit is triggered, the auditor must assess whether the financial statements fairly present under the CAS framework. The statutory audit must be performed by a licensed Chinese CPA firm registered with the Ministry of Finance and the Chinese Institute of Certified Public Accountants (CICPA), and the audit report must be issued in Chinese.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax rate under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law; non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment are taxed at 10% on China-source dividends, interest, royalties, and gains

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% of the gross dividend paid by a Chinese WFOE to its non-resident corporate shareholder, withheld at the time of distribution under Article 37 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; treaty relief may reduce the rate to 5% for a beneficial owner that holds at least 25% of the WFOE's capital for a continued minimum holding period under the applicable DTA or Mainland-HK/Macau arrangement

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File is required when the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in the tax year reaches or exceeds RMB 1 billion; Local File contemporaneous documentation is required when any related-party transaction category exceeds the prescribed threshold: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions, and RMB 30 million for equity transfers; the Annual Related-Party Transaction Report must be filed with the annual CIT return when any threshold is met

Economic substance scope: China does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the State Taxation Administration enforces a substance-over-form doctrine under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the general anti-avoidance rule in the Implementation Regulations; for a WFOE classified as ISIC 7120, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in China, including physical on-site inspections, laboratory testing, client contracting, quality control decision-making, employment of qualified technical personnel, maintenance of inspection equipment, and scientific analysis; a WFOE that merely holds contracts while the foreign parent or an offshore affiliate performs all substantive work, or that exists only to re-invoice services and shift profits, is vulnerable to recharacterization, transfer pricing adjustment, and denial of treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership doctrine.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 63 allows the tax authority to recover the unpaid tax and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount; late payment interest accrues at 0.05% per day; Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion with fines and imprisonment up to 3 years for relatively large evasion and up to 7 years for huge evasion with a high percentage of underpaid tax; Criminal Law Article 205 imposes severe penalties, including life imprisonment, for issuing false special VAT invoices.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who approved the false invoicing scheme face joint and several civil liability for restitution of the diverted corporate assets under the Company Law principle that abuse of corporate form to evade debts or taxes pierces the corporate veil; personal bank accounts and assets may be frozen and seized; the legal representative is subject to an exit ban and blacklisting through the tax authorities' cross-departmental enforcement mechanism; criminal convictions carry imprisonment up to 7 years for ordinary evasion and up to life imprisonment where false special VAT invoices were created and used.
Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Shell Intercompany Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 empowers the State Taxation Administration to make primary transfer pricing adjustments according to the arm's length principle; Article 48 requires interest on adjusted tax with an additional 5 percentage point interest margin for non-compliant documentation; Article 47 and the general anti-avoidance rule allow the tax authority to recharacterize the entire arrangement; penalties for falsified documentation and underpaid tax can reach 50% of the underpaid tax, with daily interest of 0.05%, and the adjustment period can extend back through the full statutory audit window of 10 years for tax avoidance arrangements.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or direct fabricated intercompany service contracts, false cost allocation statements, or knowingly incomplete transfer pricing documents can be held personally responsible when the corporate veil is pierced because the transferee entity is a sham and the WFOE is left with no separate financial substance; personal civil liability includes restitution of the full amount of shifted profits plus interest; the legal representative may be placed on the tax blacklist and prohibited from leaving China; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to 7 years if the arrangement is used to conceal taxable income from the Chinese tax authorities.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance and Unlawful Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 69 provides that a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit tax must pay a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount; the tax authority will also collect the unpaid tax from the WFOE as the statutory withholding agent unless it proves that the payee has already paid the tax; Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 37 requires the WFOE to withhold at source, and Article 47 permits the tax authority to recharacterize disguised dividends, loans, or royalties; late payment interest runs from the date on which the dividend was actually paid.

Director personal liability: Directors who vote for or execute a dividend distribution while intentionally ignoring the withholding obligation, or who disguise a dividend through a fake interest or royalty arrangement, are personally exposed to civil restitution to the tax authority for the unremitted withholding tax and penalties; the legal representative can be blacklisted and banned from leaving China while the tax arrears remain unpaid; participation in a scheme that knowingly uses shell contracts to conceal a dividend distribution constitutes tax evasion and can lead to imprisonment of responsible directors for up to 7 years, together with confiscation of personal assets used in the offense.
