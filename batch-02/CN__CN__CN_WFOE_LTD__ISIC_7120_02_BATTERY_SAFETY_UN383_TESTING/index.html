Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital; no blocked escrow account requirement under general Chinese company law. However, under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, a limited liability company must fully pay its subscribed registered capital within five years of incorporation, and the contribution schedule must be stated in the Articles of Association. The sole foreign shareholder may contribute in convertible foreign currency remitted into a licensed China domestic bank account; the paid-in capital must be reported to the local Administration for Market Regulation and to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through the bank account registration procedure.

Funding & unlock path: The laboratory must obtain the Fire Safety Acceptance Certificate or Completion Filing Certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Division under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China. The premises must pass a structural load inspection for drop-test machines and EMC shielding chambers. Occupational health clearance is required for radio-frequency radiation exposure in EMC test areas, mechanical injury risks from drop-test apparatus, and dangerous goods handling; the employer must conduct occupational hazard assessment, provide training, label dangerous goods, maintain material safety data sheets, and register special equipment such as air compressors or lifting devices with the special-equipment safety supervision authority.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA). Electronic filing is conducted through the China Electronic Tax Administration portal (e-Tax) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with VAT invoice clearance administered through the STA's national electronic invoice service platform and Golden Tax Phase IV system.

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Business formation: The foreign shareholder registers the WFOE as a Chinese limited liability company with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). Where the activity is not on the Foreign Investment Negative List, only filing is required. The AMR issues a Business License carrying an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which simultaneously functions as the legal entity identifier, National Tax Identification Number (TIN), and VAT taxpayer code. Step 2 - Automatic tax registration: Under the multi-certificate integration system, the AMR transmits incorporation data to the STA in real time; no separate tax registration application is generally required. The WFOE must nonetheless log in to the e-Tax portal, complete real-name authentication for the legal representative and responsible financial person, confirm the tax registration details, and verify the assigned taxpayer type. Step 3 - Tax profile activation: The STA automatically assigns statutory tax types (Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, Stamp Duty, Urban Maintenance and Construction Tax, and Education Surcharges) based on the registered business scope. The WFOE must activate its tax filing account, complete the tripartite bank-tax payment agreement, and enable the e-invoice issuance function. Step 4 - VAT invoice enablement: If the WFOE will issue VAT special invoices, it must apply for the electronic invoice quota and obtain the tax UKey via the e-Tax portal. VAT general taxpayer status may be elected in advance or is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Step 5 - Non-resident TIN procedure (if applicable): A foreign non-resident enterprise without a mainland entity does not automatically obtain a Chinese VAT ID. It must complete non-resident tax registration with the competent STA, usually in the jurisdiction of the Chinese withholding agent, through the e-Tax non-resident tax affairs module. Upon approval, the STA issues a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number used for withholding of VAT and Enterprise Income Tax on Chinese-source income. Step 6 - Customs registration for exports: If the WFOE exports goods, including returned or sample battery units, it must separately register at the Customs authority through the China International Trade Single Window and obtain a 10-digit customs registration code. Dangerous-goods and lithium-battery shipment authorizations must be obtained before the first export declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC direct tax code does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election for a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise. A Chinese-incorporated WFOE is automatically treated as a Chinese tax resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate. No hybrid mismatch election is available under domestic law; treaty tie-breaker rules apply based on facts rather than taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover-based exemption from VAT registration for an incorporated WFOE; tax registration is mandatory within 30 days of issuance of the Business License. The mandatory economic threshold governing VAT general taxpayer classification is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below that threshold the taxpayer may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer and pay at the simplified levy rate, unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status in order to issue special VAT invoices and claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim a 0% VAT rate or export VAT refund/exemption only where it satisfies the statutory 'export' test. For goods, the WFOE must be the customs exporter, the goods must be physically exported from mainland China, the export must be declared under the WFOE's own customs registration code, and the goods must be wholly delivered outside China. For the WFOE's battery safety testing services, including UN38.3 drop testing and CE-RED electromagnetic laboratory services supplied to an offshore principal, the services are eligible for zero-rating/exemption as cross-border taxable services only if the services are supplied to an enterprise or individual located outside China, are completely consumed outside China, are not connected with real estate or movable property located in China, and are not excluded by the cross-border service negative list. In addition, the contract must be executed with the overseas customer, the payment must be received in foreign currency through a Chinese bank and eventually repatriated to the WFOE's capital account or current account within the monitored period, and all export/foreign-exchange documentation must be retained to prove the cross-border nature of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form bearing the unique customs declaration number and the WFOE's 10-digit customs registration code
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the shipment of goods out of the customs territory of China
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and the underlying export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank Export Proceeds Collection Certificate, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), or the SAFE Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form issued by the receiving bank confirming the credit of the foreign-currency remittance
  • Dangerous goods transport documentation and UN38.3 test summary/certificate for battery shipments, where applicable to the export customs process

  • Any applicable inspection, quarantine, certification, or license document required by the customs procedure for the specific export goods

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice clearance regime. All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices and export-related invoices, must be issued through the STA-controlled electronic invoice service platform, which transmits invoice data in real time to the tax authority. Fully digital electronic invoices carry a 19-digit e-invoice number and an anti-counterfeit QR code. Special VAT invoices are monitored through the Golden Tax Phase IV system, with automatic matching between the seller's invoice issuance, the buyer's input VAT credit, customs export data, and VAT return filings. Any locally generated or unregistered invoice document is not acceptable for input VAT credit or export zero-rating substantiation. Anti-fraud digital stamping applies to every issued invoice, and risk-scoring algorithms flag invoices that are split, backdated, or issued without underlying export documentation.

The STA audits export zero-rating claims using Golden Tax Phase IV risk analytics, customs data matching, and bank foreign-exchange data. If the export declaration, transport document, or foreign-exchange receipt is false, missing, or inconsistent, the tax authority will deny the zero-rating treatment, claw back the refunded VAT, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and may impose a fine ranging from one to five times the amount of tax fraudulently obtained under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. The STA may also suspend or disqualify the WFOE's export VAT refund registration and refer the matter for criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, which carries penalties including imprisonment, fines, and, in aggravated cases, life imprisonment. Customs penalties for false export declarations may be imposed separately.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains full current-account convertibility for bona fide trade and service transactions, but capital-account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, approval, and post-verification requirements. The WFOE may receive equity capital injections under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) framework only after completing SAFE foreign-exchange registration and opening a designated capital-foreign-exchange account with a domestic bank. Once injected, registered capital in foreign currency may be settled into RMB for operational purposes within approved business scope, but capital withdrawals, equity transfers, or both-nation repatriations require audited financial statements, tax discharge certificates, and board resolutions demonstrating after-tax distributable profits. Export receipts must be repatriated to China within statutory timelines, and although the mandatory surrender ratio has been abolished, banks apply documentary verification and electronic monitoring through SAFE's digital cross-border foreign-exchange platform. Profit remittance by a WFOE is permitted for genuinely distributed after-tax profits, but SAFE and the tax bureau require tax clearance certificates confirming that corporate income tax and any applicable withholding taxes on dividends have been settled. The battery safety laboratory's cross-border service fees, equipment import payments, and testing-related royalties are treated as current-account items and are freely settleable provided the underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and service completion evidence are filed with the designated bank. Overall, the regime is administratively restrictive rather than market-excluded, with high compliance burdens for capital movement but no parallel-market convertibility constraint for legitimate trade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic resident foreign-exchange current account within 180 days of the customs export declaration date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the SAFE 2019 reform; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in a current-account balance or sell it to a designated commercial bank at market rate, but every transaction must be matched with customs data, invoices, and shipping evidence in the SAFE verification system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the delegated foreign exchange administrative authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for the WFOE's domestic banking needs. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise hold no PBoC-issued payment institution license, no banking license under the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (CBIRC), and no SAFE clearing membership. They cannot open RMB basic deposit accounts, clear payroll, or act as receiving banks for FDI capital injections for a China-domiciled entity. Their permissible role is limited to offshore remittance initiation from a foreign parent or from non-resident employees using a foreign-issued wallet; any direct onshore treasury clearing, RMB settlement, or SAFE reporting function is legally barred. The WFOE must therefore use a licensed domestic commercial bank for all operational, capital, and trade clearing rails, with the EMI relegated strictly to a front-end payment instruction channel routed to a corresponding domestic bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for the WFOE's domestic banking needs. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise hold no PBoC-issued payment institution license, no banking license under the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission (CBIRC), and no SAFE clearing membership. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200,000,000 for related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for related-party intangible asset transfers and for financial asset transfers; RMB 40,000,000 for related-party service transactions (Local File). Master File is mandatory when the MNE group's consolidated annual revenue for the prior year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies the economic substance doctrine primarily through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Articles 47 and 48. A WFOE performing UN38.3 battery safety testing must genuinely execute the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, including strategic management, laboratory staff, equipment ownership, calibration protocols, and risk assumption. If the profit is booked in China for invoicing purposes but all substantive testing is physically outsourced to an offshore affiliate, the Chinese tax authority may disregard the WFOE's legal form, deny treaty benefits, and reassess taxable profit to the real economic activity. Official notice or blacklisting under the 'serious tax violation' measures may accompany such a determination.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsification of Accounts

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, intentional tax evasion committed by a company can lead to a fine up to 1 level of the evaded tax and imprisonment of up to 3 years for relatively serious cases (evaded tax exceeding RMB 100,000 and 10% of tax due), or 3 to 7 years where the amount is huge. Administratively, Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes 50% to 500% fines of the evaded amount and daily interest surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally approve or arrange false invoicing are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts via lifting the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20. Individual criminal prosecution follows for the director(s) designated as persons directly responsible; they may face criminal detention, asset freezing, travel restrictions through 'tax dishonesty blacklisting', and entry into the BMX (Bonded Security and Enforcement) system resulting in an exit ban.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47, the tax authority may adjust the company's taxable income by re-characterizing the arrangement as one having no reasonable commercial purpose. The Company Law Article 20 allows creditors and the tax authority to disregard the separate legal personality. Official denial of treaty benefits may result in a 10% dividend WHT being applied to the entire profit repatriation, and the failure to file contemporaneous TP documentation triggers additional penalties under SAT Bulletin [2016] No. 42.

Director personal liability: Shareholders and directors of a shell entity are personally liable for unpaid corporate tax and debts to the extent of the abuse. Travel restrictions and blacklisting apply to legal representatives and actual controllers under the 'Measures on Joint Disciplinary Actions for Parties Subject to Enforcement for Tax-Related Dishonesty'. In aggravated cases of cross-border profit stripping disguised as dividends, the responsible directors may be exposed to criminal investigation for organising a 'tax evasion' under Criminal Law Article 201 or 'illegal business operations' under Article 225.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Law Article 36 and EIT Law Article 41, the tax authority will make a transfer pricing adjustment and may impose a 5% yearly interest charge on the underpaid tax under Article 48. Article 114 of the same law imposes administrative penalties, and intentional non-compliance with TP documentation can result in a penalty of up to RMB 100,000. Repeated or egregious profit shifting may trigger re-characterization of the payment as a dividend, yielding a 10% withholding tax in addition to local tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: A director who actively negotiates and executes a clearly non-arm's-length agreement with an offshore affiliate is implicated as a 'directly responsible person'. Under Company Law Article 147, the director's breach of fiduciary duty creates personal liability for restitution of the diverted company assets. Under judicial interpretations of Criminal Law Article 201, such actions can amount to 'other deceptions such as concealing income or claiming false deductions', leading to criminal penalties for the responsible director, including imprisonment, personal fines, and exclusion from serving as a company director for up to five years after conviction. The court may also grant civil judgment allowing creditors to pierce the WFOE's veil against the director's personal assets.
