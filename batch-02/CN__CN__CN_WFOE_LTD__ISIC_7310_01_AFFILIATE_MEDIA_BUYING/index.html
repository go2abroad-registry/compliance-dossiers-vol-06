Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online tax affairs are handled through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal administered by the local STA office with jurisdiction over the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE's statutory taxpayer identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) during incorporation; there is no separate VAT ID. The procurement/registration sequence for a foreign-owned limited company is: (1) incorporate with SAMR and obtain the Business License; (2) rely on the multi-certificate integrated registration process under which SAMR pushes formation data to STA to create the initial tax registration; (3) log into the National Electronic Tax Bureau with the USCC, complete real-name registration of the legal representative, financial officer and tax handler, and authenticate the authorized overseas controllers/ownership details as applicable; (4) complete taxpayer status identification, selecting VAT, enterprise income tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge and local education surcharge, stamp tax, and payroll/individual income withholding as applicable; (5) file the enterprise accounting system and inventory/cost accounting policy for the advertising/media-buying business; (6) link bank account details and sign the electronic tax collection payment agreement; (7) apply for VAT invoice issuing entitlement, including the tax-control UKey and the fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform; (8) file the general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer classification determination with STA; and (9) for non-resident investors and counterparties relying on tax treaty relief, document withholding tax identity through a Certificate of Tax Residence from the relevant jurisdiction and the applicable PRC claim procedure. The WFOE itself is a resident taxpayer, not a pass-through vehicle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for a WFOE; a WFOE is automatically an entity-level resident taxpayer. The only statutory election relevant in this regime is VAT general taxpayer registration for an enterprise whose annual taxable turnover is above the general taxpayer threshold; small-scale taxpayer status below RMB 5 million is a turnover-based administrative classification, not a separate entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) of annual taxable turnover is the mandatory general taxpayer registration threshold. An enterprise with annual taxable turnover at or below RMB 5 million may remain a small-scale taxpayer; an enterprise exceeding RMB 5 million must apply for VAT general taxpayer status. For goods the standard VAT headline rate is 13%; for the target digital advertising and media buying services the applicable domestic VAT rate is 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT and associated export tax rebate only where: the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer; the goods are actually released from Chinese customs under an approved Customs Export Declaration; the sale is to an overseas purchaser; the goods leave China within the declared export window; the exporter retains the original transport documents; export proceeds are repatriated through regulated bank channels within the statutory deadline; and the export rebate claim is filed through the Export Tax Refund Management System. Qualifying cross-border digital advertising and media buying services can qualify for zero-rating (or the statutory VAT exemption category, as applicable) where the service recipient is established overseas, the service is fully performed and consumed outside China, the advertising placement/audience is geographically external to the PRC, no PRC property or domestic consumer receives the benefit, consideration is paid from outside China in a foreign currency or invoiced in a freely convertible currency, and the contract, service-delivery evidence and bank receipt trail prove a genuine offshore transaction. If the media buying directs traffic to PRC-based advertisers or is consumed by PRC users, the zero-rating claim fails and domestic 6% VAT applies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic Customs Declaration for Export Goods with customs release/permit record filed through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Transport documentation: original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or combined transport document) evidencing export shipment from the PRC
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the customs declaration and shipment
  • Export Sales Contract or overseas Purchase Order with Incoterms and payment terms
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt evidence: SAFE Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form and the bank credit voucher for the export invoice currency (the PRC statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate)
  • Export Tax Refund Claim package submitted through the STA Export Tax Refund Management System / Electronic Tax Bureau, including all VAT invoice or export invoice records and customs-electronically matched data
  • Any export certificate of origin, product inspection certificate, or destination-market compliance document required by PRC customs or the destination import authority

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-authorised invoice systems, currently the nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice (electronic invoice service platform) and the tax-control UKey/electronic tax control equipment. Invoices must be uploaded in real time to the STA invoice system with a unique invoice code, serial number and QR code; no unregistered handwritten or non-cleared invoices are permitted. Input VAT deduction is supported through the VAT invoice selection-and-confirmation platform and the VAT declaration system; any discrepancy between issued invoices and declared output VAT is automatically flagged for anti-fraud review and can lead to invoice supply lockout, tax collection order, and penalty.

On denial of zero-rating, the tax authority will claw back any VAT refund already paid, require the WFOE to reissue the supply with domestic VAT at 6% or 13% as applicable, add a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day for the underpaid VAT, and impose administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. The tax authority may also assess penalties for failure to keep export supporting documents, and customs may impose separate penalties for inaccurate export declarations. Where zero-rating was supported by forged documents, sham offshore counterparties, circular or non-repatriated foreign exchange, fictitious advertising placements, or fabricated logistics records, the case is handled as tax evasion or export-tax fraud; this can trigger retrospective clawback for up to the full statutory audit look-back period, personal liability of responsible officers, customs administrative penalties, revocation of the general taxpayer qualification and export tax rebate registration, and criminal prosecution for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under the Criminal Law, with confiscation of assets and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with careful separation of current-account and capital-account convertibility. Current-account items, including trade in goods, services, and intra-affiliate royalties, are generally freely convertible once the underlying transaction is proven through the SAFE-registered bank. The RMB, however, is not fully convertible on the capital account: foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is permitted under a dedicated foreign exchange registration (FDI registration), but each capital injection must be linked to a real equity contribution, settled only after verification, and cannot be used for uncontrolled cross-border securities speculation. Profit remittances by a WFOE represent an operating expense and are lawful via the current-account route, yet the bank will require the audited financials, tax filing evidence, and board-approved distribution resolution before wiring dividends abroad. Capital repatriation upon liquidation or equity transfer remains tightly controlled and requires SAFE approval along with a complete exit tax clearances file. The practical effect is that onshore liquidity is trapped in a three-vector matrix: entity-level tax settlement, bank-side authenticity checks, and SAFE electronic records. For an affiliate-media-buying WFOE, this means the RMB funding accumulated from domestic clients cannot simply be converted into USD and swept offshore; every intra-group disbursement must be justified by a real service contract, invoice, or licensing arrangement. CFO-level forecasting is therefore mandatory because liquidity planning must embrace the SAFE pre-registration lead time, the bank's compliance review cycle, and the 5-business-day conversion window often applied to capital-account settlement using the 'pay before settlement' principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds are generally required to be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of receipt, unless the enterprise has obtained valid SAFE approval for an offshore retention arrangement. Once repatriated, funds are credited to the enterprise's foreign-currency account and may be retained in their original currency without any statutory fixed conversion ratio; there is no mandatory surrender of export foreign exchange to the state, but the proceeds must be recorded against the customs declaration within the electronic goods-trade monitoring window. Any plan to leave trade proceeds outside China for more than 180 days must be pre-approved by SAFE and supported by the underlying non-payment risk documentation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Legal viability is Low for treating fintech EMI platforms such as Wise, Mercury, or Airwallex as primary treasury rails in China Mainland. Under PRC banking and payment regulations, non-bank e-money institutions are not permitted to open current accounts for WFOEs or to provide onshore lending or SAFE-regulated foreign exchange settlement. The only compliant architecture is to use a licensed domestic commercial bank as the anchor institution and, where necessary, to contract the EMI as a purely offshore treasury management tool for the overseas holding company side; onshore receipts must never be routed through an unlicensed foreign digital bank. Fintech platforms can be used upstream for foreign investor capital aggregation, or downstream for the WFOE's own foreign business development, but every RMB-to-foreign-currency conversion and every domestic payment leg must occur through a PRC-regulated bank. For an affiliate-media-buying WFOE, reliance on EMI rails for salary, publisher payouts, or vendor settlements in mainland China is prohibited and will trigger bank de-risking, SAFE penalties, and potential anti-money-laundering regulatory action.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under State Taxation Administration Public Notice 2016 No. 42, a contemporaneous Local File is required where annual related-party transaction amounts exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for services and related party transactions; a Master File is required where total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion in a tax year; Country-by-Country reporting is required for multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not label its regime as Economic Substance Regulations, but the tax administration applies substance-over-form principles under the Corporate Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules. A WFOE engaged in digital advertising media arbitrage must have real decision-making, employees, contracts, risk assumption, and core income-generating activities within China. CIGA for affiliate media buying includes sourcing traffic, negotiating with media publishers, pricing bids, optimizing campaigns, settling payments, maintaining customer relationships, and bearing credit and performance risk. A WFOE that merely records revenue and immediately distributes profits to an offshore affiliate without local substance will be denied treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as a shell company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent VAT Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection Article 63, the taxpayer must pay the underpaid tax plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; under Article 205 of the Criminal Law, issuing or using false VAT Special Invoices may result in fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment for up to life imprisonment for newly committed offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve, prepare, or knowingly use false tax documentation face personal civil liability for restitution of evaded taxes, fines, and unpaid surcharges; the corporate veil may be pierced under Company Law principles where the director abuses the limited liability entity; directors may be placed on exit-control blacklists, have travel restricted, face asset freezes, and in criminal cases receive fixed-term imprisonment, fines, and disqualification from acting as directors.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Treaty Shopping Through Shell Entities

Statutory sanction: Under the Corporate Income Tax Law Article 47 and the State Taxation Administration General Anti-Avoidance Measures, the tax authority may recharacterize the arrangement, deny the deducted fees, deny treaty benefits, impose withholding tax on deemed dividends, apply special tax adjustment interest, and impose penalties under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law where there is intentional concealment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly operate or control the shell structure may be held personally liable as responsible parties for the underpaid tax; courts can pierce the corporate veil and order civil restitution of profits shifted offshore; directors may face travel bans, bank account freezes, blacklisting, and criminal tax evasion charges under Article 201 of the Criminal Law if the court finds fraudulent intent and concealment of taxable income.
AML, Illegal Cross-Border Fund Movement, and Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines on organizations and responsible personnel for failure to establish or implement AML controls; under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, unauthorized cross-border transfer of funds is subject to confiscation of illegal gains and fines of between 30% and 500%? In practice SAFE may impose fines of up to five times the illegal amount and cancel foreign exchange registration; Article 191 of the Criminal Law punishes money laundering with imprisonment for up to ten years and fines where the predicate proceeds involve tax fraud, fraud, or illegal foreign exchange activity.
