Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under current law. The PRC Company Law removes any minimum paid-in registered capital for an ordinary foreign-invested limited liability company, and shareholders determine the amount in the Articles of Association. However, every shareholder must contribute the full subscribed capital within five years after the company obtains its business license. There is no statutory blocked escrow account or bank capital verification requirement for a general ISIC 7310 creator talent and advertising agency. Inbound share capital must be remitted through lawful foreign exchange channels via a SAFE-designated bank and reported under the Foreign Investment Information Report and the enterprise credit information system.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through local STA branches and the national online e-Tax portal (Electronic Tax Bureau; https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn). Customs-related export declarations are filed through the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the business license automatically carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for the entity. Step 2: Within 30 days after business registration, the WFOE must complete taxpayer registration and tax type filing through the STA e-Tax portal or in person at the competent local STA branch. No separate TIN certificate is issued because the USCC is the TIN; the electronic tax registration record activates the obligations for corporate income tax, VAT, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, individual income tax withholding, and stamp duty. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident entity without an establishment or place of business in China, the non-resident enterprise or its Chinese withholding agent must file a non-resident enterprise tax registration through the same e-Tax portal, submitting the foreign entity's incorporation documents, identification of the responsible person, and contracts evidencing China-source taxable transactions. The STA then assigns a non-resident TIN, which is used for contract/tax registration, filing, and obtaining withholding certificates. Step 4: All subsequent changes in address, legal representative, capital, or business scope must be updated in the e-Tax portal, and the USCC or non-resident TIN must be referenced consistently in VAT invoices, customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, tax filings, and export rebate submissions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on its worldwide income at the statutory 25% corporate income tax rate; pass-through treatment is not available. For VAT purposes, the entity is classified either as a small-scale VAT taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer depending on annual taxable turnover and its election, but the entity-level CIT classification remains the default.

Available election forms

None comparable to U.S. check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. China has no unilateral entity classification election for tax transparency. Available elections include: voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer if annual taxable sales are below the mandatory RMB 5,000,000 threshold; application of small-scale VAT taxpayer preferential treatment where eligible; election to claim zero-rating or VAT exemption for cross-border taxable services by filing the required transaction documentation with the STA; and election by a non-resident shareholder to claim treaty benefits under an applicable double tax agreement to reduce China withholding tax rates.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory for the WFOE at establishment with no turnover threshold. Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that amount, the taxpayer may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or elect general taxpayer status if its accounting records are sound. Small-scale VAT taxpayers are generally taxed at a reduced levy rate, while the general VAT rate applicable to the WFOE's advertising and MCN agency services is 6%; the headline China VAT rate for goods is 13% and the rate for certain transportation, construction, and real-property services is 9%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: zero-rating is available for physical exports from the PRC customs territory provided the goods are lawfully reported in a Customs Export Declaration, physically leave China, are not prohibited or restricted for export, the exporter has title and valid sales evidence, and the export proceeds are collected in foreign currency and repatriated to China. For services: zero-rating is available only for taxable services expressly listed as zero-rated cross-border services in Annex IV to Caishui [2016] No. 36, which includes categories such as international transportation, research and development, design, software and information technology services, and other specifically enumerated offshore services supplied to an overseas entity and wholly used or consumed outside China. General MCN creator representation and advertising agency services are ordinarily treated as cross-border taxable services that may qualify for VAT exemption rather than 0% zero-rating, unless a separately priced and separately documented line item falls within the enumerated zero-rated service catalogue. In all cases, the taxpayer must prove the offshore recipient identity, genuine cross-border supply, offshore consumption or destination, contractual chain, and economic substance of the transaction.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form filed through the China International Trade Single Window and released by customs)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration data
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international consignment note evidencing physical export from China
  • Export proceeds collection receipt (Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt / Inward Remittance Certificate) issued by a designated foreign exchange bank, or the electronic receipt record in the SAFE trade in goods/foreign exchange administration platform
  • Cross-border service agreement, statement of work, delivery records, and evidence of offshore use or consumption for zero-rated or exempt cross-border services

China operates a fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) regime. Taxable enterprises must issue VAT invoices through the STA e-invoice platform or through STA-certified commercial software connected by application programming interface (API). Each FDEI is transmitted in real time to the STA Golden Tax System and carries a unique invoice code, invoice number, QR code, and tax-control digital signature, enabling real-time authenticity verification, duplicate detection, and cross-matching with customs declaration data, bank payment data, and VAT returns. Paper VAT special invoices, where still used, are tax-control invoices generated from the VAT invoice management system and must be reconciled with the STA-ledger database. There is no separate municipal physical fiscal stamp; compliance is enforced through real-time digital transmission and anti-fraud validation.

The STA customs and foreign-exchange authorities cross-match every zero-rated export claim against the VAT invoice database, Customs Export Declaration, logistics records, bills of lading, foreign-exchange collection receipts, banking records, and the underlying commercial contracts. If the zero-rating or export refund is unsupported, reversed, or found fraudulent, the STA will claw back the VAT refunded or exempted, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the underpaid tax, and may impose a fine of between 50% and 500% of the tax involved under the Law of the People's Republic on the Administration of Tax Collection. The tax authority may also suspend the taxpayer's export VAT refund rights and require retrospective adjustment for prior periods. Fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds constitutes a crime under the PRC Criminal Law, carrying possible imprisonment and criminal fines, and the limitation period for serious fiscal fraud is extended or unlimited while the matter remains concealed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertibility split between current and capital accounts. Current account transactions, including trade-related service fees and creator remittances, are generally freely convertible if supported by authentic commercial documentation under SAFE's principle of 'real need' verification. However, capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment capital injections, cross-border shareholder loans, and outward portfolio investments, remain subject to pre-approval, registration, and quantitative controls. Foreign exchange collected from export activities and cross-border service revenue must be repatriated to China and may be retained in foreign currency accounts or settled into RMB; the ability to remit profits offshore is allowed only after annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, and SAFE verification of tax payment certificates. Practical hurdles include the requirement for verified underlying contracts, invoices, and safe custody of evidence; any mismatch in the declared purpose code or settlement pattern triggers compliance review. Profit remittances require clearing through a designated bank in China, with the tax authorities' Certificate of Tax Payment or tax withholding statement, and cannot be effected via unlicensed offshore fintech channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts from goods and services must be repatriated into China within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration or the service completion date, and must be credited to a current account foreign exchange account at a designated domestic bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current managed regime, but the proceeds may be converted into RMB upon settlement; all transactions must be verified under SAFE's trade authenticity review, and any retained foreign currency must be reported in the enterprise's foreign exchange receipts and payments monitoring system. Certain special categories such as processing trade or advance receipts may be subject to earlier repatriation deadlines or additional verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation arises when related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset purchases/sales, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers or financial transactions (loans), RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions in the fiscal year. Master File required where total related-party transactions exceed RMB 3 billion (or revenue > RMB 5 billion and related-party percentage material).

Economic substance scope: PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and Public Notice 2009 No. 2 apply; although no formal ESR framework, the tax authorities may apply substance-over-form and economic substance principles to recharacterize transactions. For a WFOE managing TikTok creators, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must include strategic and operational management, contract negotiation, creator development, and risk-bearing functions within China to avoid PE risk and to support the arm's-length allocation of profits. The effective management and control of the WFOE must remain in China; otherwise Article 8(2) and Article 45 of EIT Law could attribute profits to the offshore parent.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting Domestic Revenue

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Article 49, Implementation Regulations Article 133, and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of the underpaid tax, daily surcharge of 0.05% per day, and additional monetary penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 if the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 (imprisonment up to 7 years plus an unlimited fine).

Director personal liability: Directors and the responsible tax officer are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax, and assets may be seized for restitution; personal travel bans (exit restrictions) may be imposed by the tax authority (Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44). In the event of intentional evasion, the director as the decision-maker will face criminal prosecution and imprisonment; corporate veil-piercing can be invoked to hold the foreign parent liable if the subsidiary is entirely a puppet and the parent controls funding and revenue.
Shell Company – Inadequate Economic Substance and sham proportionate allocation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and Public Notice 2009 No. 2 (GAAR): discretionary re-characterization of the WFOE as a pure shell, denial of treaty benefits, and reallocation of profits to the higher-taxing jurisdiction; penalties under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 for false declarations.

Director personal liability: Where the shell structure enables tax abuse, directors who sign the sham contracts and authorise tax filings may be held personally liable for the superior amount of tax, interest, and penalties; an exit ban can be issued and the director's personal assets attached. The onus lies on the director to demonstrate legitimate business purpose; failure leads to criminal liability for fraudulent tax refund or evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation – Inflated Offshore Service Fees and Royalties

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 (arm's length principle), Article 46 (thin capitalization), Public Notice 2016 No. 42 (transfer pricing documentation), and Public Notice 2017 No. 6 (special tax investigation adjustment and mutual agreement procedures). The tax authority can make a transfer pricing adjustment, charge interest on the adjusted tax, and impose a surcharge; intentional evasion triggers a 50%-500% fine. Criminal enforcement can extend to imprisonment for tax evasion above RMB 50,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable if they approve the intercompany fraud and the WFOE becomes insolvent when the adjustment is imposed; the corporate veil can be pierced to recover the tax from the director's private assets. Additionally, the offshore affiliate's directors may be criminally liable as joint offenders, and the responsible Chinese director can face a travel ban and extradition issues under the OECD/BEPS framework.
