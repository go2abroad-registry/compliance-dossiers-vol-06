Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online taxpayer portal: the 'Electronic Taxpayer' (e-Tax) environment accessible through the national tax service platform at www.chinatax.gov.cn and through each provincial-level electronic tax bureau portal under the STA's unified identity authentication system.

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in Mainland China must first incorporate the entity with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The business license issued by SAMR contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier accepted by the STA. The WFOE must then complete tax registration in the provincial electronic tax bureau within 30 days of obtaining the business license; the data is synchronized from SAMR through the national 'one-time license and one-time tax registration' information-sharing mechanism. The WFOE must verify its tax types, filing period, corporate income tax regime, withholding obligations, and VAT taxpayer status in the e-Tax system using its legal representative's digital certificate or e-tax login. For a foreign non-resident enterprise with no permanent establishment in China that earns China-source income, the non-resident enterprise obtains a TIN by having the Chinese payer or withholding agent register the non-resident enterprise in the STA's non-resident tax management module and withholding registration system. The resident WFOE, once registered, uses the same USCC for all corporate income tax, VAT, customs, and cross-border tax filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election under its corporate income tax law. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a Chinese tax resident enterprise and taxed at entity level on its worldwide income. The only relevant statutory election is the VAT administrative election: a WFOE with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to become a General VAT Taxpayer, and a Small-Scale Taxpayer may elect simplified levy treatment; these are indirect tax administrative elections, not entity classification or hybrid mismatch elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic VAT/tax registration; every entity engaged in taxable supplies must register for tax. Mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in local currency. Taxpayers with annual turnover below RMB 5,000,000 may remain Small-Scale Taxpayers, subject to simplified collection methods, unless they voluntarily elect General VAT Taxpayer status. For cross-border programmatic advertising services supplied within China to domestic customers, the output VAT rate is generally 6%; export zero-rating eligibility is governed by cross-border service rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rated VAT applies to goods exported from the customs territory of China, provided the export is actually declared, shipped, and cleared by customs. For services, zero-rated VAT applies to qualifying cross-border taxable services supplied by a Chinese entity to an overseas recipient where the service is wholly consumed outside China. Programmatic real-time ad exchange and SSP services may qualify as zero-rated cross-border services under CaiShui (2016) No. 36 and related clarifying circulars if the DSP/SSP platform supplies advertising decisioning, ad exchange, and monetization infrastructure to foreign ad platforms, foreign advertisers, or foreign publishers, and if the advertising inventory and audience data are not directed at China-sourced impressions or Chinese domestic advertisers. The service contract must show the overseas recipient and foreign consumption; the commercial invoice, payment receipt, and evidence that the service is rendered and used outside China are required. If the platform serves Chinese domestic advertisers or delivers advertising to audiences located in China, the service is taxable at the standard services rate of 6%, and zero-rating is unavailable.

China mandates invoice issuance through the STA-controlled VAT invoice management framework, now operated under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. The fully digitalized electronic invoice regime requires taxpayers to issue electronic VAT invoices through the national electronic invoice platform; issuance data is transmitted to the STA in near-real time. Third-party invoice software providers must integrate with the STA through authorized APIs. Anti-fraud enforcement uses unique electronic invoice numbers, digital tax control codes, the taxpayer's digital certificate, and the electronic tax seal; there is no separate physical fiscal stamp, but the invoice data is subject to statutory audit and intelligent risk monitoring. Special VAT invoices for input credit must strictly comply with the STA's invoice data structural requirements and cannot be manually fabricated.

The STA may audit zero-rating claims retrospectively and deny the zero rate where the statutory documentation package is incomplete, the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the prescribed 180-day period, or the services are found to be consumed inside China. In a clawback scenario, all VAT credits previously refunded or offset are recovered, statutory late-payment surcharge accrues at 0.05% per day from the date the tax was due, and administrative penalties range from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export refund conduct, including forged customs declarations or fabricated foreign exchange receipts, is subject to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may also impose penalties for failure to repatriate export proceeds in accordance with the Administration of Settlement, Sale, and Payment of Foreign Exchange Provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese Renminbi is not fully convertible. Current account items, including cross-border digital advertising service fees, are generally freely convertible for trade in services once the transaction is validated by the designated bank and the amount is within the approved service trade scope. Capital account items, including inbound FDI equity contributions, debt financing, and external guarantees, remain subject to registration, approval, and investment-purpose verification. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must handle its capital injection through an FDI registration with SAFE and must not divert registered capital into treasury products or offset losses without bank supervision. Profit remittance is permitted once annual statutory audit is completed, taxes on dividends are withheld, and the bank verifies the audited financial statements and tax payment certificates. Exchange-rate fluctuations may affect capital conversion because settlement of foreign currency capital into RMB follows the onshore spot rate at the bank's designated price; there is no free offshore convertibility. To achieve a borderless AdTech treasury, the WFOE must combine onshore bank accounts with foreign currency accounts for collection from overseas programmatic buyers and clearing through SWIFT. The entire treasury structure must be supported by underlying contracts, invoices, and audit trails to avoid being characterised as speculative cross-border gambling or trade misinvoicing, which could lead to SAFE penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current PRC foreign exchange administration framework, there is no mandatory export-proceeds conversion ratio and no forced surrender requirement to the State for service trade receipts. However, all cross-border current account receipts must be repatriated into China and collected through a designated domestic bank account in a timely manner; the bank must verify the authenticity of the transaction before accepting the deposit. For goods trade, export proceeds are required to be collected within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless an approved extension exists. For digital advertising service receipts under ISIC 7310, the WFOE should invoice within the agreed billing cycle and receive the foreign exchange proceeds through the bank under the service trade verification system, submitting the service contract, commercial invoice, evidence of DSP/SSP delivery, and tax payment certificates. The bank will not apply a fixed conversion ratio, but the WFOE may voluntarily convert into RMB or hold the proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account; if retained in foreign currency, the account balance must be supported by eligible current account receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange regulator

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not a compliant primary banking channel for the WFOE in Mainland China. The PBOC and SAFE have not opened the local RMB clearing system (CNAPS/CIPS) to unlicensed fintech EMIs; therefore an offshore EMI cannot legally maintain the WFOE's capital verification account, obtain the FDI registration, or complete annual tax remittance filings. An EMI can be used only in a non-establishment support capacity, such as collecting advertising receivables from selected markets before funds are swept into the onshore bank, provided the EMI does not hold any IBAN or RMB account in China and the WFOE has segregated deposit accounts. If the EMI has a licensed domestic banking partner, that partner must still be the account-holder bank of record for all capital and tax-related transactions. The overall viability is Low as a primary rail and Medium as a secondary collection tool, but it must never replace the tier-1 domestic bank for statutory capital or dividend repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE shall first complete the SAFE Foreign-Invested Enterprise (FDI) registration and foreign exchange investment reporting through a tier-1 domestic bank. The overseas investor must wire the equity subscription in the original currency using a SWIFT MT103 from the remitter's own banking account, not from a third-party source. The SWIFT remittance information must clearly identify the remitter, account number, relationship to the WFOE, and a purpose code matching the SAFE Balance of Payments classification, such as 601011 for inbound FDI capital contribution; the receiving bank will perform capital verification, record the sum in the FDI Capital Account, and then permit conversion into RMB for approved uses. All capital utilisation must be made from the settled RMB compliance account and can be used for wages, materials, office expenses, and genuine advertising infrastructure costs within the registered business scope, but cannot be used for secondary project investment or domestic equity acquisition without SAFE approval. The WFOE must file the capital settlement conversion report to SAFE within 30 days of each settlement transaction and obtain a capital account bank confirmation for the annual audit.Outbound settlements for advertising inventory, SSP inventory purchases, data enrichment, or overseas publisher payments should be transmitted primarily by SWIFT MT103 with full-payment instructions from the WFOE's onshore settlement account. For high-value recurring invoices to foreign technology vendors, the WFOE may open a documentary collection or commercial letter of credit if the vendor's treasury requires the protection of trade documents, although digital AdTech services generally do not have bills of lading and are better served by clean wire transfer. Domestic publisher payouts in RMB can be executed through CNAPS or ACH-like batch clearing, while cross-border payouts to independent third-party app stores, measurement partners, and ad verification firms should be sent via the domestic bank's cross-border wire channel using the contract and invoice as supporting documents. The bank will require the exact funds purpose code for service trade, withholding tax certificate for royalties or software charges if any, and the VAT invoice when processing outward payments; the WFOE should keep a treasury dashboard linking each SWIFT confirmation to the source DSP/SSP transaction ID to avoid duplication.Onshore RMB forward contracts (deliverable) with genuine current account exposure under the SAFE real-need principleCurrency swaps and common FX swaps between RMB and USD/EUR for matching the timing of capital settlement, dividend repatriation, and vendor disbursementsFX options on small notional amounts for adverse-movement coverage where a tier-1 bank approves the required trade-background documentsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for onshore WFOE hedging, and onshore banks will require separated underlying transactions rather than index-only speculation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not a compliant primary banking channel for the WFOE in Mainland China. The PBOC and SAFE have not opened the local RMB clearing system (CNAPS/CIPS) to unlicensed fintech EMIs; therefore an offshore EMI cannot legally maintain the WFOE's capital verification account, obtain the FDI registration, or complete annual tax remittance filings. An EMI can be used only in a non-establishment support capacity, such as collecting advertising receivables from selected markets before funds are swept into the onshore bank, provided the EMI does not hold any IBAN or RMB account in China and the WFOE has segregated deposit accounts. If the EMI has a licensed domestic banking partner, that partner must still be the account-holder bank of record for all capital and tax-related transactions. The overall viability is Low as a primary rail and Medium as a secondary collection tool, but it must never replace the tier-1 domestic bank for statutory capital or dividend repatriation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is mandatory if annual aggregated related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China enforces an economic substance doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and the Beneficial Ownership test under SAT Public Notice [2018] No.9. A WFOE must demonstrate substantive business activities, actual employees, premises, decision-making authority, and risk assumption to support intercompany contracts and to claim treaty benefits; otherwise, treaty benefits are denied and profits are re-characterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, evaded taxes are recovered with late-payment surcharge and a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or directly participated in the fraudulent arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes and fines. They may face exit restriction (blacklisting), asset seizure, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No.42 and Article 44 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may reassess taxable income using the appropriate transfer-pricing method and impose additional interest at the one-year loan base rate plus 5 percentage points per annum on the underpaid tax. Administrative fines under Article 64 of the Tax Collection Administration Law range from RMB 2,000 to RMB 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the company's failure to implement proper transfer-pricing policies. They may be subject to civil claims by the company, administrative exit bans, and, in aggravated cases, criminal liability for aiding tax evasion.
Treaty Abuse / False Beneficial Ownership Claim

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under SAT Public Notice [2018] No.9 and Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. The tax authority collects the underpaid withholding tax plus daily late-payment interest (0.05% per day) and imposes a penalty of up to 5 times the shortfall. If fraud is established, criminal tax evasion charges apply under the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or approved the conduit arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the short-paid withholding tax. They may face travel bans, asset freezes, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent tax practices, with imprisonment of 3 to 10 years depending on the amount defrauded.
