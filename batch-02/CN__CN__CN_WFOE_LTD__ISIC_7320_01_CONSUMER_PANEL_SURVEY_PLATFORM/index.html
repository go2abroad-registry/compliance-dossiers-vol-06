Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For an ISIC 7320 consumer polling and market research SaaS platform structured as a WFOE, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not acceptable as the sole registered address. Chinese tax authorities require the registered office to correspond with the actual place of tax collection, and commercial banks reject virtual addresses during account-opening due diligence because the site inspection and anti-money-laundering interview cannot be completed. A commercial mailbox may be used only as an auxiliary correspondence address after a physical office has been established. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary threshold for initial tax registration: every business-licensed entity must complete tax registration and obtain the USCC/taxpayer ID immediately upon incorporation. For VAT classification, an entity with annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (or monthly sales exceeding CNY 500,000) MUST register as a general VAT taxpayer; an entity below that threshold is treated as a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general-taxpayer treatment under the applicable simplified rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: export sales by a VAT general taxpayer qualify for the 0% VAT export rate if the goods are declared to customs as exports, physically leave the customs territory of China, the exporter obtains and retains the customs export declaration, transport documents and foreign-exchange receipts, and the export is reported on the VAT export-rebate/zero-rating return. For services/digital services: cross-border services in the statutory zero-rating positive list (including software services, information-system services, business-process management services, R&D services, design services and technology transfer) qualify for 0% VAT only if the service is supplied to an overseas non-resident entity or individual, is wholly consumed outside China, the consideration is received in foreign currency from outside China, and the service contract, e-invoice, payment receipts and offshore-consumption evidence are maintained. A consumer-panel market-research SaaS platform may qualify only if the STA classifies the supply as software/information-system/BPM service; if classified instead as market-research or consulting services, the offshore supply is generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, with no refund of input VAT.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (China customs export declaration) for goods exports
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or other transport evidence proving that the goods left Chinese customs territory
  • Foreign-currency Receipt Certificate (FIRC-equivalent export collection verification) issued by a Chinese designated foreign-exchange bank
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice (for goods)
  • Cross-border service contract, English/Chinese service agreement and evidence of offshore consumption (for services/digital services)
  • E-invoice issued through the STA e-invoice platform (Special VAT Invoice for zero-rated goods export or ordinary invoice for services exemption/zero-rate)

China mandates fully digitalized e-invoicing through the STA national e-invoice platform and the VAT tax-control system. Special VAT invoices and ordinary VAT invoices must be issued through STA-certified e-invoice channels or tax-control devices (Tax UKey). Invoice data are reported to the STA in real time or near-real time, carrying a unique 20-digit invoice number, anti-counterfeit QR code, and tax-authority digital verification. Large-enterprise ERP/API integration is supported; export zero-rating and refund claims require valid e-invoices to be issued, reported, and cancelled/red-flagged correctly.

If the STA or customs finds that the export declaration is false, the services were not wholly consumed outside China, foreign exchange was not repatriated within the statutory deadline, or required documents are missing/e-invoices are invalid, the zero-rate claim is denied and the taxpayer must repay the uncollected VAT and any input-VAT refund received, with a late-payment surcharge accruing daily at 0.05% from the original due date. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levy and Collection, administrative penalties range from 50% to five times the amount of any evaded or underpaid tax, with credit downgrade and restriction of export-rebate facilitation. Fraudulent export zero-rating or false export-refund claims may also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China (fraudulent export tax refund crimes), and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) sanctions including fines and prohibition on designated foreign-exchange business for failure to repatriate or properly verify export proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a regulated but progressive current-account convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations and SAFE implementing rules. The renminbi is not freely convertible on capital account, but current-account items, including cross-border market research fees, royalties, and export service proceeds, are generally convertible once genuine transaction documents are provided to a licensed bank. The WFOE must complete SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration before receiving inbound equity capital; thereafter, each inward remittance is credited to the FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account under a recognized capital-item purpose code. Subsequent use is strictly monitored: converted RMB may fund operating expenses, salaries, rents and permitted procurement, but may not be used for unauthorized securities investment, entrusted loans, or real estate speculation. Dividend remittance to the foreign shareholder is an approved current-account outflow if the WFOE submits an audited balance sheet, tax clearance certificate, board resolution, and proof that dividends originated from genuine undistributed profits. The bank files the remittance under SAFE's monitoring system, and no separate foreign-exchange quota is required for legal profit repatriation. Hedging of current-account exposures is available, while speculative derivative trades are prohibited.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods-export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into a Chinese bank account within the monitoring period, normally 90 days from the customs export declaration, unless SAFE approves a later collection date with underwriting contract and customs evidence. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: since the abolition of compulsory surrender, the WFOE may retain 0-100% of the export proceeds in foreign currency or sell them to a licensed bank at market rate. For the WFOE's ISIC 7320 service receipts, the same current-account convertibility principle applies but no goods-export surrender timeline applies; service fees must be collected and reported under SAFE service trade regulations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and PayPal Business have Low viability as primary corporate banking rails in China. They hold no PBOC banking license, cannot issue onshore CNY bank accounts, and cannot participate in CNAPS or the SAFE corporate forex current-account system. Chinese licences for non-bank payment institutions restrict them to escrow-style payment services, not deposit-taking or trade settlement. The WFOE must maintain its regulated onshore account at a Tier-1 commercial bank and may use PayPal/Wise only as auxiliary disbursement pipes for cross-border panel incentives, funded by compliant onshore remittances. Any use must be supported by documented payout contracts, records of beneficial recipients, and service-trade filings to SAFE to avoid red flags.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital is routed via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's overseas bank to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank. For RMB-denominated equity investment, the remittance can clear through CIPS. Prior to receipt, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration, and the investor must reference the SAFE registration number and the approved capital contribution purpose code in the wire instruction. Upon receival, the bank verifies the amount against the registration, updates the SAFE monitoring system, and may credit the account. RMB settlement of the capital account is allowed for eligible operating uses, but prohibited for unauthorized securities investment, entrusted loans, non-operating real estate, or equity investments into other Chinese entities beyond legal limits. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a Local File is mandatory for the WFOE if its related-party transactions in the prior tax year exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services and other transactions (including SaaS and market-research platform services); a Master File is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion and a Country-by-Country Report is required when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent reaches RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the offshore-centre sense, but it applies substance-over-form under Article 47 CIT Law and SAT Announcement No. 2 of 2017; the WFOE must show real Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, including survey design, panel data collection, analytics, client contracting, pricing decisions, and support staff. If CIGA are performed by a foreign related entity without Chinese substance, the tax authority may reallocate residual profits to the WFOE, assess 25% CIT, add interest and surcharges, and impose 10% WHT on deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Deduction

Statutory sanction: Article 63 TCAL requires recovery of the underpaid tax plus a fine of 50% to five times the tax shortfall; Article 201 Criminal Law criminalises evasion of RMB 50,000 or more exceeding 10% of the total tax payable, with fines and imprisonment up to seven years where the evaded amount is RMB 500,000 or more and exceeds 30% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: Article 23 of the PRC Company Law (2023 revision) allows courts to pierce the corporate veil where a director abuses the limited-liability structure; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax and penalties, may face asset seizure and travel bans under civil enforcement blacklisting, and may be personally sentenced to imprisonment for up to seven years if found to have directed the false tax filings.
Shell Company and General Anti-Avoidance Rule

Statutory sanction: The PRC GAAR under Article 47 CIT Law and SAT Announcement No. 2 of 2017 authorises tax authorities to disregard the shell, recharacterise the transactions, assess the reallocated profit at 25%, add late-payment interest plus an additional GAAR surcharge, and impose 10% withholding on deemed dividends; beneficial-ownership denial under the DTA strips any treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure a phantom WFOE to defeat tax liabilities are individually liable under civil enforcement rules and can be pierced under the Company Law; courts may recover tax penalties from personal assets, impose travel bans and high-consumption blacklisting, and refer criminal indictment under Article 201 Criminal Law for tax evasion or Article 208 for unlawful tax-invoice use, carrying imprisonment up to seven years.
Foreign Exchange and AML Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Articles 39 and 40 impose administrative fines of 30%-70% of the illegal foreign-exchange amount for unauthorised cross-border settlement and compulsory closure of unlawful channels; the revised Anti-Money Laundering Law of the PRC (2024) imposes penalties on payment institutions failing to implement suspicious-transaction reporting, including fines up to RMB 5 million and confiscation of illegal gains.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct the WFOE to use non-compliant payment rails to hide taxable revenue or to bypass AML controls are personally exposed to SAFE fines, civil restitution through corporate-veil piercing, travel bans and asset restrictions, and criminal prosecution under Article 191 Criminal Law for money laundering, with fines and imprisonment up to ten years where the proceeds derive from tax evasion or fraud.
