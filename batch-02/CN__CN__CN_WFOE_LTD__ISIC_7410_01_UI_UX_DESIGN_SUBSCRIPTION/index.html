Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (Limited Liability Company) in China. The registered capital is set by the shareholder in the Articles of Association and may be in Renminbi or a freely convertible currency. Under the 2023 Company Law, shareholders must pay their subscribed contributions in full within five years from the date of establishment, unless a shorter period is required by law or specified in the Articles. Funds must be remitted from abroad into a designated foreign exchange capital account, and the foreign investor must complete foreign exchange registration through the bank acting on behalf of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). No blocked escrow account rule applies to a standard WFOE in the digital design services sector unless the business scope falls within a restricted or negative-list industry, which is not the case for ISIC 7410 UI/UX design subscription services.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (SAT); local tax service bureau for the WFOE's registered place; electronic filing through the national Electronic Tax Service Platform (electronic tax bureau) and the China 'Multi-Certification Integration' portal.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to enterprise income tax on worldwide income at the statutory CIT rate; it is not a pass-through entity and there is no territorial exemption for its global receipts.

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box entity classification election and no hybrid mismatch election for a WFOE. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a corporate resident enterprise. VAT general taxpayer/small-scale taxpayer status is an administrative registration category, not a corporate tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is mandatory upon incorporation with no turnover threshold. In the VAT system, annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 requires registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer but may also register voluntarily as a general taxpayer. The applicable VAT rate for domestic UI/UX design subscription services is generally 6% (modern services), while the 13% standard headline VAT rate applies to goods and other taxable supplies; exports of qualifying services or goods are subject to 0% VAT if statutory conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply 0% VAT on cross-border supplies of design/creative services where the services are provided to an overseas customer, the services are supplied and/or consumed wholly outside mainland China, the consideration is paid in foreign exchange and repatriated to China, and the export is supported by the statutory documentation. For physical exports, 0% VAT/final exemption is available where goods are actually exported from Chinese customs territory under an approved customs export declaration and the foreign exchange proceeds are collected within the prescribed period. Pure domestic design subscription customers are subject to VAT at 6% on each taxable supply; the WFOE must retain contemporaneous evidence of the overseas status of the recipient and the offshore consumption of the services.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form issued by China Customs (export declaration number, customs clearance records, and electronic customs data through the Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other shipping/transport document evidencing physical export of goods, where the supply is in goods form
  • Commercial invoice and signed export/service agreement with the overseas customer, describing the design subscription services or goods and the destination
  • Bank inward remittance record or settlement slip issued by a licensed bank in China evidencing collection of export proceeds in foreign exchange from the overseas customer
  • VAT zero-rated export invoice issued through the official electronic invoice platform with the 0% rate indicated
  • Statement/report filed with the tax authority in the export tax rebate declaration (where applicable) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) goods trade monitoring record confirming export foreign exchange receipt

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice and real-time fiscal data clearance regime. VAT invoices (including special VAT invoices and fully digital electronic invoices) must be issued using the official e-invoice platform integrated with the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System (Phase IV). Invoice data are transmitted electronically in real time; the WFOE must configure its accounting/billing system to interface with the national electronic invoice service platform, not a private API, and must comply with the anti-forgery rules for invoice serial numbers, invoice amount, VAT rate, buyer Unified Social Credit Code/tax ID, and zero/standard rate indicators. The tax authority automatically matches invoice data with VAT returns, customs export data, and foreign exchange collection records for risk control and audit selection.

Tax authorities cross-match the VAT zero-rated invoices with customs export declarations, SAFE foreign exchange collection data, bank settlement records, and the WFOE's corporate income tax reporting. If the required customs, transport, service-consumption, or remittance documentation is missing or inconsistent, or if the export proceeds are not collected/repatriated within the 180-day statutory period without a valid exception, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating and recompute VAT at the domestic rate (6% for design services), impose daily late payment surcharges at 0.05% of the underpaid tax, and may impose administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Deliberate fraudulent export invoicing or false export declarations may constitute tax evasion and be referred for criminal prosecution, and the WFOE may be removed from favorable export tax rebate classified-management status and subjected to enhanced investigation and retrospective clawback for prior zero-rated periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which RMB is fully convertible on the current account for bona fide trade in services and goods, but only conditionally convertible on the capital account. A WFOE may receive design-subscription fees from overseas clients and freely settle, retain, or remit current-account service revenues after completing bank verification and cross-border income reporting. However, capital-account transactions, including paid-in equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border financing, require SAFE FDI registration, dedicated capital accounts, negative-list compliance checks, and post-settlement monitoring. The foreign parent's equity capital must be remitted into a SAFE-registered FDI capital account in foreign currency, and the receiving bank will verify the transaction purpose and underlying contract before allowing RMB settlement. Profits and dividends are remittable as current-account items only after the WFOE produces audited financial statements, corporate income tax filings, and a tax clearance certificate showing that withholding taxes have been discharged. Daily operations remain subject to bank document review; funds may not be moved freely for unauthorized purposes such as fixed-asset speculation, portfolio investment, or unrelated intra-group lending without further SAFE approval. Convertibility is therefore real but procedural, bank-intermediated, and documentary, with material compliance gates surrounding every cross-border capital movement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory surrender ratio or forced foreign-currency conversion under the current SAFE framework. Export proceeds from both goods and services must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign-exchange account through a licensed bank and are subject to the trade-validation window, currently administered on a 90-day monitoring basis from the export or service-delivery date. Once credited, the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in its current-account FX settlement account or voluntarily sell it to a licensed bank for RMB. The bank must receive the cross-border income declaration, underlying client contract, invoice, and evidence of service delivery. Uncollected or overdue service-trade receivables beyond the monitoring threshold will trigger a SAFE counterparty query or on-site inspection, but no conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not viable as the WFOE's primary treasury or settlement bank in mainland China. They do not hold a Chinese banking license or a PBOC payment-institution license, cannot issue local Chinese account numbers, and cannot accept or settle RMB through domestic clearing on behalf of a mainland WFOE. A WFOE is legally required to bring cross-border service receipts onshore through a SAFE-regulated bank and to complete the corresponding cross-border income declaration. Treating an EMI as a collection bank would bypass statutory trade verification, create a false picture of ultimate receipts, and expose the entity to AML/tax non-compliance. Stripe and Wise may be referenced only as billing UX or payment-acquirer facilitators operated by the offshore commercial counterparty, never as the WFOE's clearing bank. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for corporate account holding is therefore rated as not permissible.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not viable as the WFOE's primary treasury or settlement bank in mainland China. They do not hold a Chinese banking license or a PBOC payment-institution license, cannot issue local Chinese account numbers, and cannot accept or settle RMB through domestic clearing on behalf of a mainland WFOE. A WFOE is legally required to bring cross-border service receipts onshore through a SAFE-regulated bank and to complete the corresponding cross-border income declaration. Treating an EMI as a collection bank would bypass statutory trade verification, create a false picture of ultimate receipts, and expose the entity to AML/tax non-compliance. Stripe and Wise may be referenced only as billing UX or payment-acquirer facilitators operated by the offshore commercial counterparty, never as the WFOE's clearing bank. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for corporate account holding is therefore rated as not permissible.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation arises when, in a single fiscal year, related-party transactions exceed any of the following thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; and RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions. A Master File is required when the consolidated group revenue in the prior year is RMB 5 billion or more and related-party transaction thresholds are met; Country-by-Country Report filing is required when group consolidated revenue in the prior year is approximately RMB 5.5 billion (roughly EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: China does not apply an Economic Substance Regulations code in the British sense, but the substance-over-form doctrine is embedded in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (GAAR), Article 6 of the Tax Administration Law, and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 on related-party services. For a UI/UX design-as-a-service WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities are the creative direction, user research, wireframing, prototyping, usability testing, visual design, digital subscription contract management, quality assurance, and credit-risk assumption. Those activities must be performed by WFOE employees in China with real office premises, payroll, governance minutes, and corporate bank accounts. The WFOE must earn and retain an arm's length share of the total profit for the functions it performs, assets it uses, and risks it bears; a mere pass-through invoicing vehicle or cost-plus shell with zero residual profit and no local economic substance is vulnerable to recharacterization and special tax adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Non-Booking of Digital Export Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 32 of the Tax Administration Law imposes daily 0.05% late-payment interest; Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax; the Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion with fines and fixed-term imprisonment of up to three years where the evasion is substantial, and three to seven years where the amount is huge and the evasion ratio is high.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and actual controller who directed the non-booking and false return can be held jointly civilly liable under the veil-piercing provision of Company Law Article 23, ordered to restore diverted corporate assets, placed on the dishonest taxpayer blacklist, subjected to high-consumption restrictions and a travel ban, and prosecuted personally under the PRC Criminal Law.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Failure

Statutory sanction: Article 44 of the Tax Administration Law permits deemed assessment; Articles 121 and 122 allow special-tax-adjustment interest at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points and a look-back period of 10 years for tax evasion cases. Failure to provide required documentation can trigger fines up to RMB 10,000, and Section 10 thousand? Destroying or falsifying supporting documents attracts criminal penalties for fraudulent accounting and tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party declarations, destroy timesheets and contracts, or use the WFOE as a façade to strip Chinese taxable profit may be found jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT and withholding taxes; they may be blacklisted, barred from exiting China, and prosecuted for deliberately providing false tax information.
DTA Treaty-Shopping and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: The beneficial-ownership requirement is denied under the applicable DTA and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; the tax authority recharacterizes the arrangement, imposes the domestic 10% withholding on the true economic beneficiary, and under Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law the WFOE as withholding agent may be fined 50% to three times of the amount that should have been withheld. Interest and penalties apply from the original distribution date.

Director personal liability: The directors and ultimate beneficial owners who engineered the conduit structure may be personally liable for the under-withheld tax where the corporate veil is used to commit fraud; the legal representative may be subject to criminal prosecution for tax evasion, public blacklisting, restricted consumption, and a travel ban, with the court able to enforce restitution from personal assets.
