Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally disfavored and frequently rejected for a WFOE in the stock photography and generative visual assets industry. The market regulation authority requires a physical registered address in China at which the enterprise can carry out business, accept legal process and be reached for inspection; the tax authority verifies the address separately during taxpayer registration; banks require a physical-office visit, doorplate, lease agreement, utility invoice and staff presence before accepting corporate account documentation. Registration at a virtual mailbox may be accepted only in a narrow pilot/free-trade-zone context under an approved hosting agreement, but most commercial banks will decline KYC approval for such an address. For a low-risk digital-media WFOE, the prudent structure is a dedicated office lease in the same administrative district as the registered address, with the lease filed and, if required, property ownership evidence in the file.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum or government-blocked escrow account is imposed for a permitted-service WFOE classified under ISIC 7420 / NAICS 541922. Under the 2023 Company Law, the registered capital stated in the Articles of Association is a binding obligation and must be fully paid by the shareholders within five (5) years from the date of issuance of the business license unless a shorter period is stated in the Articles. Capital must be remitted through a licensed bank foreign-exchange capital account, evidenced by injection receipts, and is subject to anti-money-laundering and foreign-exchange consistency screening by the receiving bank.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no Civil Defense fire permit, structural load inspection, or occupation-health clearance applies to a purely digital office/consulting operation. A standard office workplace must comply with general fire-safety measures of the building management and local fire-rescue rules, but no separate Civil Defense industrial permit is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; taxpayer services and filings are administered through the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax) operated by provincial-level STA offices, with cross-entity data exchanges among the STA, the General Administration of Customs (GACC), and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the WFOE, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN/VAT ID for all Chinese tax purposes. Tax registration is integrated into the one-stop market entity registration procedure; the WFOE must finalize its taxpayer profile with the competent local STA office within 30 days of license issuance, including general or small-scale VAT taxpayer classification, tax calculation method, banking information, and activation of the electronic invoice service. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that does not establish a Chinese legal entity but derives China-source income, the non-resident taxpayer identification number must be applied for through the competent STA office directly or through a withholding agent before filing, paying tax, or claiming treaty benefits; this limited registration does not create a permanent establishment solely by itself.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - China taxes a WFOE as a resident enterprise on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and does not permit pass-through treatment to foreign shareholders.

Available election forms

None - China does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification election. A WFOE cannot elect pass-through taxation; VAT general taxpayer status is a turnover-based registrable status, not an income tax entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold applies for basic tax registration of an incorporated WFOE; under the VAT regime, an enterprise must register as a General VAT taxpayer when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. A small-scale taxpayer with monthly taxable sales of RMB 100,000 or below may be eligible for the prevailing small-scale VAT exemption policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China zero-rates qualifying exports of goods and cross-border taxable services. For the stock-photo/generative visual-asset business, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available where the digital/licensed content is supplied to an overseas recipient, consumed entirely outside the People's Republic of China, and the service type is included in the STA published catalogue of zero-rated or VAT-exempt cross-border services. The WFOE must be a General VAT taxpayer, maintain a proper input VAT chain, evidence actual performance abroad, and repatriate or collect cross-border settlement proceeds within the statutory period; services not satisfying the consumption-outside-China test are subject to the domestic VAT service rate, which for this entity is generally 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) filed with the General Administration of Customs for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent transport document evidencing physical export
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation / Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (the China bank equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, FIRC) evidencing payment by the overseas buyer
  • Export invoice issued through the STA e-invoice platform
  • Export contract and commercial invoice
  • For digital stock-photo and licensing services: service agreement, licensing records, platform usage logs, and evidence that the service is consumed and used outside China
  • STA export tax refund / zero-rating declaration data transmitted through the e-Tax portal

China mandates electronic invoice issuance through the STA unified e-invoice platform. The WFOE must use the All-Electronic Invoice (Full Digital Invoice) system, which requires API-based submission to the STA e-invoice service platform, anti-counterfeit tax-control digital signatures, QR codes, and Golden Tax System verification. Special VAT invoices must be reported and cross-matched before a customer may claim input VAT credits; export-related invoices and customs export declarations are reconciled through the national customs-tax data exchange.

The STA, GACC, and SAFE cross-match customs declarations, e-invoices, foreign-exchange receipts, contracts, and service-consumption evidence. Export zero-rating claims with missing documentation, inconsistent customs data, unverified foreign-exchange repatriation, or false digital-service export characterizations are disallowed and clawed back, with VAT at the domestic rate re-asserted, a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or evaded under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious cases involving fraudulent export rebates or counterfeit VAT invoices may constitute criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China and may result in loss of export-tax-rebate eligibility, customs sanctions, and inclusion in the serious tax violation blacklist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's RMB is not fully freely convertible; the PBOC/SAFE maintain a managed floating exchange rate with a daily central parity band. Current account items such as trade in goods, royalty payments and dividend remittances are convertible on a bona fide basis, subject to the purpose and truthfulness examination performed by designated banks. Capital account transactions remain controlled. Inbound equity funding for a WFOE requires FDI registration with SAFE, an approved foreign exchange capital account, and compliance with the special-purpose use rules; foreign exchange cannot be used for domestic equity investment outside the negative list. Export earnings are repatriated into a domestic foreign-currency account and may be retained or converted, eliminating the old mandatory surrender regime, but the bank and SAFE monitor the trade flow for discrepancy. Dividend remittance to the foreign parent is permitted where the WFOE has accumulated distributable profits verified by audit, the relevant corporate income tax and 10% withholding tax are settled, a tax redemption certificate is produced, and no prior AML/SAFE penalty remains outstanding. In practice, the bank also demands board dividend resolutions, the latest annual audited statements, and evidence that the underlying revenues are derived from permissible licensed or unlicensed digital content transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be brought onshore within 180 days of the customs declaration date, or for cross-border digital services and licensed stock photo income, within the contractually agreed service performance window recognized by SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio currently imposed: the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign-currency receipts in a domestic foreign-currency account, or convert them into CNY at a designated bank. SAFE and the bank reconcile each receipt using the cross-border receipts and payments system, and any discrepancy exceeding 10% against the underlying invoice triggers submission of supplementary evidence.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

In-depth feasibility is Low for any foreign or offshore digital EMI that seeks to act as the WFOE's primary banking or domestic RMB clearing channel. Stripe, PayPal, and similar EMIs do not hold a PBOC payment institution license and are not legally permitted to open onshore RMB settlement accounts or complete CNY clearing in mainland China. These rails may be used only as an offshore collection conduit for inbound B2C earnings from overseas customers, with the funds periodically swept into a Tier-1 corporate foreign-currency account. AWFOE relying solely on such an EMI would create substantial KYC opacity, high transaction-splitting risk, and potential suspension under PBOC anti-money laundering sanctions. Therefore, the compliant blueprint is Tier-1 banking as the primary treasury spine, with EMI rails confined to foreign customer payment aggregation and limited by contractual terms acceptable to the Chinese settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The overseas parent must first complete SAFE Foreign Direct Investment registration. Capital should be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign-currency capital account at a Tier-1 bank, with the beneficiary reference including the WFOE registered name and tax identification number, and the payment purpose code expressly marked as FDI equity injection. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the FDI payment control table and may release the funds for permitted operating use after settlement into RMB. A licensed Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report within six months, and all disbursements must be retained for inspection. The WFOE may not use the injected capital for domestic securities, entrusted loans, or real estate unless the investment falls squarely within the applicable free-trade-zone negative-list exemption.For licensing fees, royalty distributions and micro-payments to overseas photographers and creators, the WFOE should issue SWIFT MT103 remittances through the Tier-1 bank after presenting invoice, license code, digital delivery confirmation and tax withholding certificate. Current-account payments are processed automatically after the bank completes authenticity screening, without a separate SAFE pre-approval. For high-value content acquisitions, a commercial letter of credit may be opened at the WFOE's bank with presentation of technical acceptance certificates, delivery manifests and invoice match. Documentary collections are acceptable where no LC is needed, provided the transaction is backed by electronic delivery records, order number and contract clauses. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: In-depth feasibility is Low for any foreign or offshore digital EMI that seeks to act as the WFOE's primary banking or domestic RMB clearing channel. Stripe, PayPal, and similar EMIs do not hold a PBOC payment institution license and are not legally permitted to open onshore RMB settlement accounts or complete CNY clearing in mainland China. These rails may be used only as an offshore collection conduit for inbound B2C earnings from overseas customers, with the funds periodically swept into a Tier-1 corporate foreign-currency account. AWFOE relying solely on such an EMI would create substantial KYC opacity, high transaction-splitting risk, and potential suspension under PBOC anti-money laundering sanctions. Therefore, the compliant blueprint is Tier-1 banking as the primary treasury spine, with EMI rails confined to foreign customer payment aggregation and limited by contractual terms acceptable to the Chinese settlement bank.
