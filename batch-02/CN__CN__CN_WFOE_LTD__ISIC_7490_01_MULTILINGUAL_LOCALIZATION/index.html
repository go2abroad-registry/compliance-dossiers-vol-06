Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a WFOE limited liability company formed for ISIC 7490. Under Company Law of the PRC (2023 Revision), shareholders must pay the subscribed capital in full within five years from establishment unless a shorter or longer period is required by law or administrative regulations. The Articles of Association must specify the amount and contribution schedule; capital contributions may be in cash or lawful non-cash assets, and foreign-currency equity contributions must be remitted through a licensed bank and recorded with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the National Electronic Tax Service Platform (chinatax.gov.cn) and the competent local district/county tax bureau electronic tax office; WFOE registration data is shared automatically from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the multi-license integration system.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise with the State Administration for Market Regulation; the issued business license will contain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings. 2. After SAMR approval, the WFOE must complete tax identification confirmation through the electronic tax service platform within 30 days, including taxpayer status confirmation, legal representative and financial officer real-person authentication, tax type identification (VAT, corporate income tax, urban construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty), filing period selection, and activation of the STA tax-control device (tax UKey). 3. No separate paper tax registration certificate is required because the five-in-one license integrates tax registration with SAMR registration. 4. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch pass-through elections. Under China's VAT system, a WFOE may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment if annual taxable sales do not exceed CNY 5,000,000, or may voluntarily register as a general taxpayer; once registered as a general taxpayer, reversal is generally restricted for at least 12 months. No other entity-level opt-out is available for WFOEs, which are always Chinese resident enterprises taxed at entity level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For a newly incorporated WFOE, there is no separate VAT registration threshold because the business license itself triggers tax registration; the small-scale taxpayer threshold is annual taxable sales of CNY 5,000,000 or less for services, and general taxpayer registration is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. For policy-monitoring purposes, small-scale taxpayers may also benefit from monthly sales exemption thresholds such as CNY 100,000 during applicable STA policy windows, but this does not remove the obligation to register and file. Pure non-resident suppliers without an establishment are generally not registered for VAT; instead, the Chinese buyer performs VAT withholding.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A Chinese WFOE may claim zero-rating or VAT exemption on cross-border taxable services when all of the following statutory conditions are met: the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual outside China; the service is wholly rendered outside China and the benefits are consumed entirely outside China; the contract, commercial invoice, and payment trail clearly evidence an offshore recipient; foreign exchange or equivalent proceeds are received from abroad; and the service falls within the STA/MOF cross-border taxable services catalogue. Translation, localization, and media dubbing services supplied to overseas clients are usually eligible under the professional/cultural services exemption or zero-rating category; the WFOE must confirm the specific classification with the competent tax authority to determine whether the rate is 0% (zero-rating) or exemption. Zero-rating eligibility requires a documented export declaration filed through the National Electronic Tax Service Platform in the cross-border taxable services exemption/zero-rating schedule, supported by contracts, invoices, overseas client status certificates, and foreign exchange receipts.

  • For pure cross-border services (translation/localization/dubbing), no customs bill of entry is required; the substantiation bundle must include: cross-border service contract with the overseas client, commercial invoice and full-digital electronic invoice, overseas client corporate registration evidence, service delivery and acceptance certificate, bank SWIFT/foreign exchange settlement receipt, and the STA cross-border service zero-rating/exemption declaration schedule.
  • If physical media containing localized content is exported (e.g., dubbed masters on hard drives), the WFOE must additionally retain the customs export declaration (Bill of Entry), bill of lading or airway bill, and the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt issued by a designated Chinese bank, together with the e-export manifest.
  • Where export proceeds are received from an overseas escrow or marketplace platform, the WFOE must retain the platform settlement statement and the corresponding underlying client contract in addition to the FIRC/payment receipt so that the tax authority can trace the proceeds to the stated cross-border service transaction.

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-controlled fiscal channels: either the Full-Digital Electronic Invoice system or the tax UKey-based special VAT invoice system. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system in near real-time, and each invoice receives a cryptographic invoice number and anti-counterfeiting code. There is no private-sector real-time fiscal clearance API; all e-invoicing APIs must use STA-certified third-party e-invoice platforms linked to the National Electronic Tax Service Platform. Special VAT invoices used for input credit by a buyer require exact USCC/TIN matching and correct tax classification; failure to upload or match invoice data exposes the WFOE to invoice verification rejection, VAT input denial, and anti-fraud investigation.

The STA and State Administration of Foreign Exchange cross-recommend export proceeds, invoice data, and customs records; any zero-rating or export refund claim that is unsupported by contract, invoice, service performance proof, or foreign exchange repatriation within 180 days will be reversed with retroactive VAT collection, late payment interest at the statutory daily rate, and an administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the involved tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on the Collection and Refund of Export Taxes. If the export zero-rating claim is found to be fraudulent or materially false, the input VAT previously credited can be clawed back, the WFOE may be downgraded to small-scale taxpayer status, and criminal liability for export tax refund fraud may be pursued under the Criminal Law, including penalties of imprisonment for related responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. The Renminbi is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border trade in services, royalties, and ordinary operating remittances, provided the underlying business documents are authentic and submitted to a designated bank. Capital-account transactions remain heavily controlled and are subject to case-by-case approval by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Foreign direct investment into a wholly foreign-owned enterprise is permitted under the Foreign Investment Negative List, but the subscribed capital must enter through a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, cannot be repurposed for securities or entrusted loans, and requires a capital verification report from a Chinese registered CPA. Profit repatriation to the offshore parent is conditional upon audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance certificates, and a bank-verified non-resident dividend remittance declaration. Export proceeds from localization services are not subject to forced surrender; however, the entire amount must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account and each receipt must be matched with invoice and delivery evidence under the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Speculative derivative positions are prohibited; all FX hedging must be grounded in genuine commercial need.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the WFOE's cross-border localization services must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange account within 90 days from the date of contractual payment maturity, or within 30 days after the agreed collection deadline, as monitored by SAFE through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. No mandatory fixed surrender ratio is applied; registered exporters may retain up to 100% of verified export proceeds in foreign currency in their accounts or voluntarily convert them into Renminbi at the then-prevailing market rate. Every receipt must pass the bank's authenticity and matching verification against the export service invoice and delivery evidence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Non-viable as a primary banking partner. Mainland China's banking regulatory and SAFE frameworks do not grant Wise, Payoneer, or similar offshore electronic money institutions any license to operate as deposit-taking financial institutions within the Mainland. Such EMIs cannot open domestic RMB or foreign-currency accounts for the WFOE, cannot receive foreign direct investment capital, and cannot pass SAFE capital verification or current-account authenticity checks. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign direct investment capital for the WFOE must be routed through the onshore foreign-exchange capital account at a licensed designated bank. The offshore parent executes a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer with the Foreign Investment Negative List legal-purpose code and the precise invoice or capital subscription reference. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable as a primary banking partner. Mainland China's banking regulatory and SAFE frameworks do not grant Wise, Payoneer, or similar offshore electronic money institutions any license to operate as deposit-taking financial institutions within the Mainland. Such EMIs cannot open domestic RMB or foreign-currency accounts for the WFOE, cannot receive foreign direct investment capital, and cannot pass SAFE capital verification or current-account authenticity checks. They may only be used as an auxiliary contractor-payout gadget for micro-amounts to overseas linguist vendors, provided the WFOE maintains a fully licensed domestic banking relationship as the lead treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, or where the aggregate of related-party intangible-property, service, or financial-asset transactions exceeds RMB 100 million; Master File is required where the multinational group meets the PRC CbC consolidated-revenue threshold (RMB 5.5 billion) or where the STA specifically issues a contemporaneous-documentation notice.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine in Treaty Abuse Prevention (Beneficial Ownership, SAT Announcements 2016 No. 42 and 2018 No. 9) and in Special Tax Adjustment Article 47 of the TAL. Although China has no identical Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code, the STA will disregard a WFOE lacking substantive direction, staff, premises, invoicing, bank controls and risk-bearing; Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a localization agency must be performed in China, including client contracting, editorial decisions, quality acceptance, foreign-exchange settlement, and director control of bank accounts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fabricated Intercompany Service Recharges

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and the outstanding tax remains collectable with daily 0.05% surcharge; Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law criminalizes false tax declarations and can lead to confiscation of assets and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the WFOE veil where directors signed fabricated service agreements or knowingly used the company to disguise dividends; directors may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties, placed on the tax-related dishonest taxpayer blacklist (exit-restriction/non-civil-air-travel), and sentenced to up to three years' imprisonment in ordinary cases or three to seven years for especially large amounts.
Shell WFOE / Treaty Shopping / Absence of Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 47 and Special Tax Adjustment measures allow the STA to disregard the conduit entity and recharacterize the payment; the dividend is re-taxed at 10% or the treaty benefit is fully denied, with late payment surcharges and a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63; false beneficial-ownership filings may trigger criminal liability for false tax declarations.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the conduit remittance structure face personal civil liability for the withheld shortfall, bank account freezes, travel restrictions on the tax dishonest list, and potential criminal indictment for organizing tax evasion; Chinese courts may ignore the corporate shell because it is a mere instrument of the controlling shareholder.
AML and Foreign-Currency / RMB Settlement Violations in Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Regulations impose confiscation of illegal forex earnings plus fines up to 30% of the illegal amount, while the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines up to RMB 1 million on enterprises and allows freezing of accounts; unpaid withholding tax triggers TAL penalties already described.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners who arrange forex-splitting or unapproved outbound transfers are personally debarred from corporate management, blacklisted for international travel, subject to asset recovery for unpaid tax, and may face imprisonment up to 10 years where money laundering is proven; the corporate veil is pierced where the WFOE exists as the director's alter ego.
