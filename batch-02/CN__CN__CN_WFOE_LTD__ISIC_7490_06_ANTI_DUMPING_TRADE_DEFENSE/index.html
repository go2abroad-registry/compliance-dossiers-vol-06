VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based exemption from VAT registration for incorporated legal persons: every WFOE must complete tax registration upon establishment, regardless of projected turnover. For general VAT taxpayer status, annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory registration as a general taxpayer; below that, the small-scale taxpayer levy rate of 3% may apply. Professional consulting output generally falls under the 6% VAT rate, while goods are generally 13% and specified transport/construction 9%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from PRC customs territory and sold to overseas customers are eligible for the 0% export VAT rate and associated export VAT rebate if the export declaration is genuine, the consideration is paid by an offshore party, the goods are released by Customs, and all export tax refund documentation is filed and verified. Cross-border services, including consulting services for anti-dumping trade defense, qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption only if supplied to an overseas recipient and wholly consumed outside China, evidenced by contract terms, offshore service-delivery records, and absence of domestic consumption in China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export clearance form) validated by the General Administration of Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing overseas collection
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Export packing list and customs release record
  • Export VAT ordinary invoice and export tax refund declaration record

China mandates e-invoicing through the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT invoices are generated and cleared electronically in the STA's National VAT Invoice System; fully digitalized electronic invoices are transmitted to STA in real time, validated against taxpayer registration and tax-control metadata, and returned with a unique invoice number, verification code and tax authority electronic seal before legal use. Special VAT Invoices are used for domestic taxable supplies to VAT-registered recipients; exports are documented with export invoices and customs-captured declaration data.

If the export zero-rating/refund cannot be substantiated within the statutory verification period, STA may recover the refunded VAT as unpaid tax, assess late-payment surcharge and impose administrative fines of 50% to five times the tax involved under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. False documentation, fraudulent export refunds, or forged customs declarations are referral matters for the Anti-Smuggling Bureau of the General Administration of Customs and public-security investigation, with criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for tax fraud offenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a partial convertibility regime distinctly bifurcated between current account and capital account transactions. Current account items, including trade in goods, services, and remittances related to the WFOE's anti-dumping legal defence consulting fees, are generally freely convertible once the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by compliant documentation. No mandatory foreign exchange surrender applies to receipts, but all foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to a bank-designated foreign exchange account within 180 days from the date of the underlying customs declaration or service completion, and the enterprise retains the right to keep the proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert into RMB at will. Capital account items, such as inbound equity injections from the foreign parent, loan principal repayments, or profit repatriation treated as dividends, require explicit SAFE registration, capital account opening approval, and bank verification. Profit remittance hurdles arise because dividends can only be distributed from audited after-tax profits, and remittances require an annual audit report, tax clearance certificate, shareholder resolution, and possibly a certificate from a tax resident of the jurisdiction of the foreign shareholder. For capital injections, the FIE must complete Foreign Investment (FDI) registration with SAFE through a domestic bank, and the paid-in capital must be verified by a registered Chinese CPA. Substantial documentary and compliance requirements effectively preclude free outbound movement of funds without a genuine trade or service underlying, and any forward FX hedging requires demonstration of real trade exposure. The convertibility regime remains stable but is intensely verification-heavy, with no availability of on-demand capital conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE reforms, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; however, all export-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a PRC-designated foreign exchange account at an authorised bank within 180 days from the date of customs export (or from the service completion date for service receipts). The enterprise may voluntarily convert the proceeds into RMB or retain them in a foreign currency account, but must present export declarations, invoices, and customs receipts to the bank for authenticity verification. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline triggers administrative penalties and potential inclusion in the SAFE non-compliance watchlist, which also degrades future cross-border banking approvals.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) in conjunction with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), which acts as the delegated foreign exchange regulator under PBOC authority for all convertibility and cross-border flow approvals applied to the mainland China jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital banking EMIs (e.g., multi-currency neobank platforms domiciled in the UK, Singapore, or Hong Kong) have negligibly low viability as the primary operating rails for a mainland China WFOE. A WFOE is legally required to maintain corporate banking relationships with a SAFE-authorised domestic bank for all RMB and FX settlement, capital verification, and tax remittance filings. Foreign EMIs cannot directly open domestic Chinese settlement accounts, nor can they issue local RMB accounts or perform SAFE registration architecture. At the ancillary level, a WFOE may use offshore EMIs only to receive funds from clients in foreign jurisdictions where the Chinese bank wiring grid has correspondent limitations; however, the final repatriation of such fees must pass through a formal Chinese bank account with full underlying contract evidence. Given China's cross-border data and banking licensing restrictions, fintech EMIs are either legally barred from servicing domestic entities or are functionally non-operational for primary treasury; if used at all, they serve merely as collection vaults subject to immediate downstream wire clearing to the domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital infusion from the foreign parent must be handled via the capital account channel: (a) execute a shareholder injection agreement and obtain a Foreign Investment Enterprise (FIE) establishment approval from the local branch of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the Ministry of Commerce; (b) complete SAFE FDI registration at the receiving Chinese bank, obtaining a Foreign Exchange Operational Business Number (FWBA); (c) open a dedicated Capital Escrow (FKTA) account at a SAFE-authorised bank; (d) request the parent to dispatch a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for the injection with purpose code 622 (actual capital contribution) or 623 (working capital loan), naming the WFOE as beneficiary; (e) upon credit, the bank will trigger capital verification requiring a Chinese certified public accountant (CPA) to issue a capital verification report (Confirmation of Registered Capital Paid-in). After verification, the funds may be transferred to the general RMB operating account only via the bank's capital conversion system, with a mandatory SWIFT message trail showing the original FX purpose code. No use of the capital escrow funds is permitted for payment of shareholder loans, RMB-denominated bonds, or investment in securities without further SAFE approval.For outbound payment of trade remedies, legal fees, and consultancy costs, SWIFT MT103 messages are the dominant clearing rail for single-point wire transfers to foreign appellate counsel, expert witnesses, and foreign law firms. For more granular trade settlement related to underlying goods or service contracts, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) opened by the WFOE's Chinese bank on behalf of a foreign supplier (or a standby L/C for dispute claims) provides the mandatory documentary mechanism. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) remain permissible but are lower in preference due to time-weighted exposure and need for manual presentation. Every outbound remittance requires an underlying invoice/contract, proof of actual service delivery, and a tax clearance statement showing Withholding Tax (WHT) compliance on cross-border service payments at the 10% rate (unless reduced by DTA). The bank will encode the payment under SAFE purpose category 228070 (legal and accounting services) or 226010 (filing fees), ensuring the payment route is traceable and defensible in post-transaction audits.Permissible RMB/USD and RMB/EUR forward contracts as a designated bank instrument, provided the WFOE can evidence underlying current or capital account exposure with factual invoice timing and a signed hedging mandate.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are only tradable offshore in the CNH market; domestic onshore regulated NDFs are prohibited for PRC residents. A WFOE cannot legally contract an offshore NDF without entering the scope of prohibited internal settlement mechanisms. Therefore, the WFOE must execute only deliverable FX forwards with its Tier-1 bank onshore.Permissible cross-currency swap instruments are allowed only for qualified current-account cash flow hedging, requiring each swap leg to be tied to a genuine commercial transaction between the WFOE and a foreign counterparty. The swap notional cannot exceed 1.5x the projected annual service fee inflows and outflows, and must be amortised according to a schedule matching the contractual milestones, with the bank verifying underlying documents quarterly.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital banking EMIs (e.g., multi-currency neobank platforms domiciled in the UK, Singapore, or Hong Kong) have negligibly low viability as the primary operating rails for a mainland China WFOE. A WFOE is legally required to maintain corporate banking relationships with a SAFE-authorised domestic bank for all RMB and FX settlement, capital verification, and tax remittance filings. Foreign EMIs cannot directly open domestic Chinese settlement accounts, nor can they issue local RMB accounts or perform SAFE registration architecture. At the ancillary level, a WFOE may use offshore EMIs only to receive funds from clients in foreign jurisdictions where the Chinese bank wiring grid has correspondent limitations; however, the final repatriation of such fees must pass through a formal Chinese bank account with full underlying contract evidence. Given China's cross-border data and banking licensing restrictions, fintech EMIs are either legally barred from servicing domestic entities or are functionally non-operational for primary treasury; if used at all, they serve merely as collection vaults subject to immediate downstream wire clearing to the domestic Tier-1 bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under State Taxation Administration Public Notice 2016 No.42, Local File is required when annual related-party transactions for tangible-asset transfers reach RMB 200 million, financial-asset transfers reach RMB 100 million, intangible-asset transfers reach RMB 50 million, related-party borrowing-lending transactions reach RMB 20 million, or other related-party transactions reach RMB 50 million; Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies a substance-over-form general anti-avoidance rule and beneficial ownership doctrine rather than an OECD-style ESR filing regime. A WFOE must demonstrate real Core Income-Generating Activities in China: local management, qualified personnel, physical office, active contract negotiation, material decision-making and actual performance of anti-dumping defense services. A shell company with nominal shareholding, no employees and no operating substance will be denied treaty relief, invoice deductions and PE benefits, and may be attacked under the DTA abuse rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion Through False Invoices

Statutory sanction: The tax authority imposes full tax recovery, a daily 0.05% late surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of evaded tax under Law on Tax Administration and Collection Article 63. Criminal Law Article 205 on false special VAT invoices carries prison sentences up to life imprisonment plus asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law when director conduct abuses limited liability; directors who signed or directed false invoicing are jointly and severally liable for tax debts, face personal asset freezing, exit-control and blacklisting orders, criminal arrest and imprisonment. Restitution orders extend to assets held through nominee vehicles.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Missing Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may make a transfer-pricing adjustment, impute taxable profit and apply late-payment interest under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 to 48 and the Special Tax Adjustment rules. Documentation failures attract administrative penalties up to RMB 20,000 under the Tax Collection Law; serious cases expose the taxpayer to fraud penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers who knowingly structure intercompany prices at artificial levels are treated as responsible persons for recovery, with joint civil liability for adjusted tax, surcharge and penalties. Courts can attach personal assets and impose travel bans through the Social Credit and Exit-Entry systems; criminal evasion charges apply where adjustments are coupled with intentional false books.
DTA and Withholding Tax Abuse Through Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: DTA benefits are denied retroactively under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 40 and STA Public Notice 2015 No.60; the tax authority recovers the full under-withheld 10% tax, plus a daily 0.05% late surcharge and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under Tax Collection Law Article 63. Criminal prosecution for tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 201 applies where treaty abuse is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for making false declarations; directors who signed treaty benefit forms and board minutes are personally liable for the unwithheld tax, interest and fines. Assets in China may be frozen, the director may be blacklisted and travel-restricted, and fraudulent conduct is subject to imprisonment up to 7 years plus confiscation of the proceeds of crime.
