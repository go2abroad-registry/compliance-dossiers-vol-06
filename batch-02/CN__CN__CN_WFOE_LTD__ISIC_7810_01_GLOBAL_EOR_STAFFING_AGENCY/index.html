Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital is applicable to an ordinary wholly foreign-owned enterprise limited liability company under PRC law as long as it does not enter a restricted or licensed sector; the registered capital amount is set by the shareholder in the Articles of Association. Under the 2023 Company Law, shareholders must fully pay subscribed capital before the expiry of five years from the date of incorporation, unless a more stringent sectoral rule applies. No escrow or blocked account requirement is imposed for a staffing agency under foreign investment law. However, if the WFOE provides labor dispatch services on the Chinese mainland, the Labor Dispatch Administrative License requires registered capital of not less than RMB 2,000,000 and a capital verification report confirming the paid-in amount. Foreign exchange capital contributions must be remitted through a designated SAFE-registered capital account and recorded in the Foreign Investment Comprehensive Reporting System.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); administered through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus and the national Electronic Tax Bureau platform at etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a China-established WFOE, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code issued by the Administration for Market Regulation is the statutory taxpayer identification number and VAT identification number. Purchase the business license through the integrated one-stop registration system; the tax authority obtains the registration record electronically. Within 30 days of the AMR business license issuance, complete taxpayer onboarding on the Electronic Tax Bureau by real-name authentication, confirmation of tax categories, VAT taxpayer status election, withholding registration, and banking information filing. Non-resident entities without a Chinese permanent establishment must register for a non-resident enterprise tax identification number at the competent tax authority through the withholding agent or by direct application under the State Taxation Administration's non-resident tax registration measures; the same number is used for Enterprise Income Tax withholding and VAT purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is a Chinese tax-resident limited liability company taxed on its worldwide income at entity level, with no pass-through recognition, branch attribution not available, and distributions to foreign shareholders subject to withholding tax under the applicable double tax treaty.

Available election forms

None under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; China does not provide US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. VAT elections are available: a small-scale taxpayer whose annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000 may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer with input credit entitlement; an entity with projected turnover above the statutory threshold must register as a general taxpayer. Hybrid mismatch treatment follows STA anti-avoidance circulars rather than taxpayer elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for WFOE VAT registration: every entity holding an AMR business license is automatically registered for VAT through the multi-license integration mechanism. The compulsory general VAT taxpayer threshold is annual taxable turnover above RMB 5,000,000. Below this amount, small-scale taxpayer status applies unless an election is made. The EOR/staffing platform services are generally subject to the 6% modern-services VAT rate; the 13% rate applies to the sale of goods and is the statutory headline rate; cross-border exports can be 0% where statutory conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, the VAT rate is zero when the goods are physically exported from mainland China through customs supervision and the export declaration is filed by the WFOE; the taxpayer is entitled to export tax rebate on the corresponding input VAT. For cross-border services, a 0% rate applies only to the specific zero-rated cross-border taxable services enumerated in Caishui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4 and subsequent STA announcements, such as offshore service outsourcing activities, where the recipient is an overseas entity and the relevant service is wholly consumed outside mainland China. EOR payroll administration and staffing agency services must be supported by evidence that the labour and payroll services are carried out overseas and not used in China; where not enumerated as zero-rated, they may be exempt from VAT rather than zero-rated, and an export refund of input VAT is not automatic.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Export Declaration with export supervision status and customs release chop) evidencing the physical export from mainland China
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document proving goods delivery at the foreign destination and the overseas consignee's receipt
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Remittance Certificate or bank receipt/SAFE foreign-exchange receipt record evidencing settlement of the sale proceeds in convertible foreign currency

The STA has rolled out fully digitalised electronic invoices through the national Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform. FDEI are issued, transmitted, and stored entirely electronically; each invoice carries a unified national invoice number, a dynamic anti-fraud QR code, and is cleared in real time through the STA central system. Taxpayers using conventional VAT invoice management must transmit invoice data through authorised tax-control equipment or the electronic tax bureau API at the time of issuance. Third-party invoicing software must use the STA-approved interfaces; real-time reporting failure or failure to pass anti-fraud verification can block invoice issuance and trigger tax audit alerts.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to post-clearance audit by the STA, customs, and SAFE. If the export declaration, logistics documents, or foreign-exchange receipts are false, missing, or inconsistent, the STA will disallow the repayment, claw back the refunded VAT, add late-payment surcharge, and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded under the Tax Administration Law. Fraudulent export-refund claims are criminal offences under the Criminal Law, and the taxpayer may lose export-tax-refund status, be downgraded in customs credit management, and face unlimited retroactive assessment for wilful misstatement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a regulated convertible currency system for residents. Current account items, including service trade fees and dividend remittances, are generally convertible and no longer require administrative approval; however, they remain subject to the SAFE trade authenticity verification framework, under which each bank transaction must be supported by commercial contracts, invoices, customs declarations where applicable, or relevant service completion evidence. Capital account items, including inbound foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, and overseas securities investment, are subject to registration, filing, or prior approval by SAFE or PBOC. Foreign-invested enterprises must complete FDI registration in the SAFE system before receiving equity proceeds. Once received, foreign currency capital cannot be freely deployed; conversion into Renminbi requires a capital settlement plan and requires matching each settled amount to a bona fide operating expense. Export proceeds from services such as global employer of record must be repatriated to a domestic designated bank account within the verification window; no mandatory surrender percentage is imposed, meaning the enterprise may retain the foreign currency balances at a licensed bank. Profit remittance to offshore shareholders is recognized as a current account item but is historically conditioned on audited financial statements, corporate resolution approval, proof of tax payment and a valid tax clearance certificate, and a bank review of the FDI registration. Therefore, treasury operations need to maintain a dual-track record-keeping system for SAFE and tax authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE current account verification procedures, export receipts and cross-border service fees including EOR payroll service income must be repatriated to the entity's domestic foreign exchange account within the generally applicable statutory collection window of 90 days from the underlying export declaration or the date on which the service is rendered and invoiced. The bank will match each receipt against export verification data via the SAFE system and may request supporting trade or service contracts. No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed by the current regulatory framework; the enterprise is free to retain the foreign currency in a foreign exchange settlement account or sell it to the designated bank. Any overdue receipt without an approved derogation will be flagged to SAFE, potentially leading to a downgrade of the enterprise's foreign exchange verification grade, suspension of automatic closing, or enhanced documentary inspection.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Not Viable. Mainland China is not within the Wise or Revolut direct license network; these institutions do not hold a PBOC payment institution license or SAFE cross-border RMB license. Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Initial equity injection from a low-tax jurisdiction parent with no operational substance

Root cause: Initial equity injection from a low-tax jurisdiction parent with no operational substance

Defense evidence: Full chain of corporate documents including parent certificate of incorporation, certificate of incumbency, audited financial statements for two years, source-of-funds report from an international CPA firm, proof that the parent has active EOR operations and at least ten full-time employees, a tax residency certificate from the parent jurisdiction, and an undertaking not to use any bearer share or nominee.
High-frequency cross-border disbursements to individual foreign contractors in multiple currencies

Root cause: High-frequency cross-border disbursements to individual foreign contractors in multiple currencies

Defense evidence: A complete EOR service agreement with each client, contractor KYC forms with passport copies, beneficial ownership declarations, signed contractor agreements confirming employment or independent contractor status, payroll register with date-stamped invoices, and documentary evidence that each distribution is supported by a specific employment contract and tax withholding obligation.
Capital account proceeds converted into RMB and later used for non-operating purposes

Root cause: Capital account proceeds converted into RMB and later used for non-operating purposes

Defense evidence: A committed capital settlement plan pre-approved by the bank's SAFE officer, general ledger and bank statements showing settlement moneys are only used for payroll, rent, tax payments, and platform software licenses, an annual capital-account verification report by a Chinese registered accountant, and confirmation from the external auditor that no settlement proceeds were applied to securities, real estate, or non-current asset purchases without SAFE approval.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital for the WFOE must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the foreign parent or its subsidiary to the WFOE's foreign-currency capital account at a designated tier-1 commercial bank in China. The remittance instructions must carry the SAFE FDI business file number generated on the Foreign Exchange Registration of Direct Investment, and the bank will upload the transaction to the SAFE Trade and Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. The corresponding SWIFT field must clearly state FDI capital contribution and the SAFE-recognized purpose code for foreign direct investment. After receipt, if the company requires Renminbi for operating purposes, it must complete a capital account settlement through the payment-in-X procedure, where each RMB payment is directly linked to an underlying invoice or salary schedule. The bank will require a non-resident tax clearance certificate or a shareholder loan confirmation to ensure that the contribution is not taxable. A Chinese-registered certified accountant must issue a capital verification report before the industry regulator acknowledges the paid-in capital.Outbound disbursements for EOR payroll and contractor settlements will be executed through SWIFT MT103 messages with purpose code for salaries and other remuneration of employees for wage remittances to non-resident individuals, and through documentary collections or commercial letters of credit when the counterparty requests binding trade documentation. Given the service nature of the EOR business, SWIFT MT103 issued by a direct participant in CNAPS or CIPS is the primary clearing rail. Each payment instruction must reference the connected service invoice, payroll register, and employment contract; the bank will verify the transaction against the WFOE's SAFE current account records. For settlement of local payroll in RMB, payments will clear through the People's Bank of China's high-value payment system and the local settlement network. For cross-border payments to agents, a trade-related letter of credit may be issued, but this is rarely used for pure payroll disbursement and is mainly reserved for bulk billing to enterprise clients.Forward foreign exchange contracts, being onshore Renminbi forward settlement and sale instruments executed through licensed Chinese designated banksForeign exchange swap transactions between CNY and USD or between CNY and EURCross-currency interest rate swaps for matched long-term foreign currency debt exposure, subject to prior SAFE or PBOC registrationCurrency options traded on the interbank market in the name of the WFOE for genuine hedging demand

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Not Viable. Mainland China is not within the Wise or Revolut direct license network; these institutions do not hold a PBOC payment institution license or SAFE cross-border RMB license. Chinese commercial entities are required to conduct cross-border collections and payments through designated banks that interface with the SAFE digital monitoring system. Using an offshore EMI for settlement of WFOE payroll or trade proceeds would constitute an unlawful foreign exchange channel, triggering administrative penalties of 20 to 30 percent of the illegal amount and potential loss of the enterprise's FDI verification status. A hybrid structure is possible only for payments to foreign contractors outside China where the WFOE establishes a separately licensed foreign subsidiary or co-employer in that jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation thresholds require a Local File when related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or services/other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million in a tax year. A Master File is required when total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion in the fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not impose Guernsey-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the tax authorities apply the broader substance-over-form doctrine, controlled-foreign-company rules and the general anti-avoidance rule under EIT Law Article 47. A WFOE operating a global EOR platform must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA), including client contracting, payroll administration, social insurance filings, invoicing, treasury operations and risk decisions, are performed and managed in China. The OECD Multilateral Instrument's Principal Purpose Test, applicable to China's covered tax agreements, allows denial of treaty benefits where the offshore parent or intermediate holding entity lacks real commercial substance and was interposed mainly to obtain treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Payroll Deductions

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63, tax evasion carries a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05%, and recovery of all overdue tax. Under Criminal Law Article 201, deliberate concealment of taxable income constituting a significant amount and more than 10% of payable tax is a criminal offence punishable by imprisonment up to three years; where the amount is enormous, imprisonment of three to seven years and fines may be imposed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the director manages the WFOE and shareholder assets indistinguishably, personally signs false invoices, or directs diversion of EOR payroll funds. Directors and de facto controllers may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax from personal assets, subjected to asset preservation orders, travel bans, restricted consumption orders and blacklisting as dishonest judgment debtors under Supreme People's Court rules, and may receive custodial sentences under the criminal tax provisions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Deficient TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 44, when contemporaneous documentation is absent, the tax authority may determine taxable income by deemed profit methods, resulting in additional tax, penalties and a five-year special-interest charge on underpaid tax. Tax Administration Law Article 45 imposes fines up to RMB 10,000 for documentation failures; where the mispricing amounts to evasion, the 50%-500% evasion penalty and Criminal Law Article 201 apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party transaction schedules, control non-arm's length pricing committees, or order incomplete books can be held personally liable for the resulting corporate tax and interest where the corporate form was misused. Courts may garnish the director's domestic bank accounts, prohibit departure from China, blacklist the director under civil enforcement measures, and in fraud cases impose imprisonment up to three years; repeated multi-entity schemes can attract longer custodial terms.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 47 and the Principal Purpose Test in China's covered tax treaties, the tax authority may disregard the intermediary entity, re-source the income to the beneficial owner, deny treaty benefits, impose the statutory 10% withholding tax plus interest and penalties under the Tax Administration Law. Where the operation conceals the identity of ultimate beneficial owners or processes illicit funds, the anti-money laundering regime permits fines against the entity, responsible officers and persons in charge, and Criminal Law Article 191 imposes fines and imprisonment for money laundering offences.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers of a zombie WFOE may have the corporate veil pierced if they commingle the entity's funds with parent assets, use the WFOE bank account to receive unlawful payments, or structure the entity to avoid consolidation. Under PRC Company Law the controlling shareholder who causes confusion with corporate assets is personally liable for company debts. The director may be subject to asset restitution, travel restrictions, entry of the name into the social credit blacklist, seizure of personal property and criminal imprisonment up to seven years for laundering the proceeds of substantive tax evasion.
