Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic tax filing and registration via the China Electronic Taxpayer (e-Tax) portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and provincial e-Tax bureaus, with official statutory information at https://www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the WFOE obtains a business license from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that functions as the taxpayer identification number (TIN). Tax registration is integrated with company registration under the multi-license consolidation system; the in-charge tax authority automatically activates the tax profile. Within 30 days of obtaining the license, the WFOE must complete the registration by verifying legal representative, financial and accounting policy, banking account, capital contribution, and expected taxpayer classification on the e-Tax portal. If the WFOE is treated as a general VAT taxpayer, the tax authority will issue a VAT taxpayer identification number distinct from the USCC for special VAT invoice management. A foreign non-resident entity without an establishment or place of business that earns China-source income must apply separately for a non-resident enterprise tax record through the e-Tax portal and appoint a payer to perform withholding; the USCC or a non-resident tax identification number is used for remittance and treaty relief applications.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None (China has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election for a WFOE). Available elections are limited to: voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer if accounting records and tax control systems meet the statutory standards; election to apply the deemed profit method for enterprise income tax where books are incomplete (rare for a licensed WFOE); and application for treaty-reduced withholding rates. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities must complete tax registration upon business license issuance. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer status arises when annual assessable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 (5 million yuan). Small-scale VAT taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (or RMB 300,000 per quarter) may be eligible for exemption; voluntary general taxpayer registration is available for enterprises with sound accounting and internal tax control systems.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border services supplied by a Chinese VAT taxpayer to non-resident clients may qualify for VAT zero-rating if the services fall within the statutory zero-rated cross-border services catalogue (e.g., offshore outsourcing, research and development, design, software, business process management, and international transport) and are wholly consumed outside China. For study-abroad placement and immigration/visa advisory services, the applicable regime is generally the VAT exemption catalogue for cross-border services sold to overseas entities or individuals and wholly consumed offshore; zero-rating may be claimed only where the service is a qualifying offshore digital or consulting service supported by an overseas-recipient contract, no domestic real property or goods are involved, and the non-resident recipient bears no relation to the taxable supply within China. The taxpayer must document the offshore recipient, place of consumption, commercial purpose, and absence of domestic connect.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - required only for physical goods; for pure service exports no customs declaration is applicable, but the export service contract and statement of work must be retained
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill - required only for physical goods; for service exports retain the signed overseas service agreement, invoices, and delivery records (e-mail or performance evidence)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt and settlement slip evidencing payment in convertible foreign currency from the overseas client
  • Export invoice (ordinary VAT invoice marked for cross-border supply), tax payment or non-taxation record, and any supply-chain proof required by the in-charge tax authority

The P.R.C. operates the Golden Tax Phase IV electronic invoicing system. Issuance of VAT invoices must be performed through the tax authority's Full Digital Electronic Invoice platform, which assigns real-time invoice quotas, applies a digital anti-counterfeiting seal and QR code, and transmits every invoice to the STA in real time. General VAT taxpayers issuing special VAT invoices must use the electronic special VAT invoice module and may integrate via API or tax-control UKey; there is no per-invoice government fiscal stamping step, but invoice data is cleared, validated, and monitored by the tax authority in real time for anti-fraud purposes.

The STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) jointly audit export zero-rating/exemption claims. Claimants who fail to substantiate offshore consumption, payment receipt, or repatriation within the applicable period are assessed clawback of the uncollected VAT, late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, and penalties of 50% to 5 times the unpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law. Fabricating customs or foreign-exchange documents to obtain a VAT refund constitutes tax fraud and can trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, export tax-rebate fraud prohibitions, loss of tax-payment credit rating, and suspension of VAT invoice privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII, meaning payments related to trade in services, tuition fees, and visa filing charges may generally be settled through designated banks upon presentation of authentic underlying documents. Capital-account transactions, including the injection of registered capital into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise and the subsequent repatriation of profits, remain subject to strict licensing, registration, and SAFE filing requirements. Export proceeds from eligible goods and services must be repatriated into China and either retained in a designated foreign-exchange account or converted into RMB through a licenced bank; there is no fixed statutory surrender percentage, but the real transaction background principle requires every cross-border remittance to be matched to a contract, invoice, tax filing, or capital verification report. For an international education and immigration consultancy operating as a WFOE, the key practical hurdles are the need for complete SAFE foreign investment information registration, the opening of a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account before equity injection, and the prohibition on collecting client monies or paying overseas universities through informal channels. Profits may be remitted abroad as CNY dividends converted into foreign currency only after the annual audit is completed, tax clearance certificates are obtained, and a SAFE dividend remittance filing is processed. The regime is therefore convertible on the current account but administratively intensive on the capital account, requiring the treasury function to maintain continuous compliance documentation and dedicated regulated banking channels for every inbound and outbound transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export service and goods proceeds generally must be repatriated into China through a licenced designated bank within 30 days from the relevant customs declaration date or contractual settlement date, unless the enterprise has obtained a SAFE-approved extension or an approved offshore retention arrangement. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may leave the foreign currency in a valid foreign-exchange account or convert 100 percent into RMB, in each case supported by the underlying export declaration, service contract, commercial invoice, and bank transaction records.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the delegated authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), the central bank of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers, including e-money institutions sometimes described as neobanks, are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to hold or settle onshore RMB current accounts, foreign-exchange capital accounts, payroll accounts, or tax accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. Their legal feasibility for primary corporate banking in China is Low. Such platforms may be used only as supplementary offshore disbursement tools outside the People's Republic of China if the WFOE operates through a separately established foreign subsidiary with locally regulated bank accounts; they cannot receive equity injection, cannot complete SAFE capital verification, and cannot satisfy the physical branch KYC mandates required by Chinese commercial banks. All onshore client collections and overseas institutional payments must be routed through regulated Chinese bank channels using SWIFT, CIPS, or licensed foreign-exchange settlement infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital for the WFOE must enter the People's Republic of China through a dedicated Foreign-Exchange Capital Account opened with a licenced bank after SAFE foreign-exchange registration. The overseas remitter should send a SWIFT MT103 using the Chinese payment purpose code for direct investment capital injection, and the bank will require the SAFE beneficiary reference number, the shareholder resolution, and the foreign-investment filing certificate. The capital must be verified by a certified public accountant after receipt, may be converted to RMB only for permitted business expenses, and is expressly prohibited from supporting securities trading, entrusted loans, or investment in restricted negative-list industries.For overseas tuition, university application fees, visa filing fees, partner commissions, and contractual advisory payments, the WFOE should initiate SWIFT MT103 transfers from its operational RMB or foreign-exchange account with the full underlying contract and invoice references, using SAFE/INN purpose codes aligned to educational services or professional consulting fees. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not the standard clearing rails for an immigration and study-abroad advisory business, but they may be used when a foreign partner or university requests a trade-based instrument; each LC or collection must then be supported by a documented service contract, invoice, proof of performance, and bank screening evidence. Cross-border RMB transactions can also be settled through CIPS with participating banks, while USD and other major currency payments rely on correspondent banking and CHIPS settlement via SWIFT MT103.Onshore CNY deliverable FX spot and forward contracts are permitted for hedging genuine current-account and capital-account exposure arising from tuition remittance, service fees, and capital commitments, but only when supported by a real transaction background and executed through a licenced domestic bank; speculative forwards without underlying exposure are prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are available only to offshore non-resident counterparties in the CNH market and cannot be booked by the onshore WFOE as its own hedging instrument; the WFOE should hedge using onshore deliverable forwards to maintain RMB convertibility compliance.Cross-currency swaps are permitted to hedge genuine long-term exposures such as a planned USD-denominated dividend remittance or a multi-year tuition disbursement programme, but the swap notional, maturity, and hedged ratio must be matched to the underlying obligation and must satisfy SAFE and PBOC restrictions on speculative derivative transactions.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI providers, including e-money institutions sometimes described as neobanks, are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to hold or settle onshore RMB current accounts, foreign-exchange capital accounts, payroll accounts, or tax accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. Their legal feasibility for primary corporate banking in China is Low. Such platforms may be used only as supplementary offshore disbursement tools outside the People's Republic of China if the WFOE operates through a separately established foreign subsidiary with locally regulated bank accounts; they cannot receive equity injection, cannot complete SAFE capital verification, and cannot satisfy the physical branch KYC mandates required by Chinese commercial banks. All onshore client collections and overseas institutional payments must be routed through regulated Chinese bank channels using SWIFT, CIPS, or licensed foreign-exchange settlement infrastructure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File where related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million or aggregate controlled transactions exceed RMB 20 million; Master File required where group consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and related-party transactions meet prescribed thresholds; annual related-party transaction reporting mandatory regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: China applies economic substance principles through Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and through treaty anti-abuse provisions (PPT / LOB). The WFOE must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) — client sourcing, counseling, visa dossier preparation, university admissions negotiations, post-arrival support — are performed by its own employees in China. Substantial decision-making and operational substance must exist; an invoice-only structure is at risk of recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, attribution of PE profits, and imposition of deemed withholding liabilities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Financial Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of unpaid tax plus daily surcharge of 0.05%, and penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; Criminal Law Article 201: tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of tax due is punishable by up to 3 years imprisonment or criminal detention; severe cases with large amount and over 20% of tax due are punishable by 3 to 7 years imprisonment, plus fines of 1 to 5 times the evaded tax; violation of invoice rules carries additional penalties under Criminal Law Article 205; joint punishment mechanisms impose credit blacklisting, bank account freezing, and exit denial.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative are subject to personal criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 if they directly organize or participate in fraudulent tax filings; civil liability may be imposed through corporate veil piercing under Company Law Article 20 where the company is used as a mere instrument for tax fraud, making directors jointly liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; tax authorities may issue exit-control and travel bans preventing overseas travel for the legal representative and responsible managers; the director may also be blacklisted and barred from serving as director/supervisor/senior executive of other enterprises.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41: tax authority may adjust taxable income to arm's length and impose interest equal to benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points for up to 3 years of underpayment; Article 46 and thin capitalization rules deny excess interest deductions; Article 47 GAAR permits recharacterization of the entire arrangement; Tax Collection Law Article 63 penalties apply for fraudulent underreporting, with penalties up to 500% of underpaid tax; failing to provide required documentation triggers penalty of RMB 2,000 and burden of proof shifts to taxpayer; false documentation may attract criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false intercompany agreements or documentation may face criminal liability for aiding tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201; courts may pierce the corporate veil if the WFOE is a sham and its funds are commingled with personal assets of directors, leading to unlimited personal liability for corporate tax debts; travel bans and exit controls can be imposed under joint disciplinary mechanisms for serious tax violations; in liquidation scenarios, directors who continue trading while insolvent may be personally liable for all unpaid withholding taxes and corporate tax obligations.
Illegal Cross-Border Fund Remittance and AML / Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Article 39: penalties of up to 30% of the illegal foreign exchange amount, confiscation of illegal gains, and mandatory closure of foreign exchange accounts; Anti-Money Laundering Law: fines of RMB 200,000 to RMB 2,000,000 for failing to implement AML obligations, possible license revocation, and personal fines on compliance staff; Criminal Law Article 191: money laundering punishable by up to 10 years imprisonment; Article 225: illegal business operation (conducting licensed services without license) carries imprisonment up to 5 years and confiscation of property; SAFE may blacklist the company and relevant persons, restricting foreign exchange and travel rights.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct or participate in payment-splitting schemes or use personal accounts to receive company revenue face criminal liability for illegal business operation or foreign exchange evasion; AML violations can trigger personal administrative fines for senior managers and the designated compliance officer; corporate veil may be pierced if the company is found to be a conduit for personal benefit or fraud, allowing clients to sue directors personally; directors may be prohibited from leaving China until foreign exchange penalties are settled, and immigration violation–related criminal convictions can result in imprisonment and permanent disbarment from holding managerial positions.
