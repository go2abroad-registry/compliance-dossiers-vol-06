Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum registered capital for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise in the permitted remote BPO/call center service sector. The sole shareholder may subscribe a reasonable registered capital denominated in RMB or a permitted foreign currency, which must be stated in the Articles of Association. Under Article 47 of the Company Law (2023 Revision), the subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years after establishment as provided in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow bank account, capital deposit certificate, prior capital verification report, or government escrow arrangement is required for this non-regulated service category; capital inflows are handled through a SAFE-authorized bank under the Foreign Capital Confirmation procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing via the STA National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) portal, accessible from the official STA website (www.chinatax.gov.cn) and administered by the local competent tax service offices.

E-government filing portal

Upon SAMR incorporation, the WFOE obtains a Business License bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN and VAT ID in China. The WFOE must complete tax registration through the e-Tax portal within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, uploading the USCC, Articles of Association, legal representative identification, and bank account details; the competent STA office then activates the taxpayer file, VAT identity, and invoice issuance quotas. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (for example the overseas parent) that receives Chinese-source withholding payments without a permanent establishment, the non-resident enterprise must be registered with the STA for withholding tax using its home-country TIN or certificate of incorporation, apostilled or officially translated; where required, the STA will allocate a non-resident enterprise identification number for withholding purposes. The WFOE also files its VAT general taxpayer status application (mandatory above CNY 5,000,000 annual turnover, or voluntary when the threshold is not yet met) and registers its export-service contracts and banking payment channels in the STA export-tax filing and record-keeping system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC corporate income tax resident by incorporation and is subject to entity-level CIT on worldwide income. No pass-through or flow-through treatment is available for the foreign shareholders.

Available election forms

None for corporate income tax; China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. For VAT only, an eligible small-scale taxpayer may elect general taxpayer status to claim input VAT credits, and a taxpayer may elect to waive an applicable VAT exemption so that special VAT invoices can be issued; once waived, the exemption cannot be re-claimed for 36 months.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the compulsory turnover threshold for VAT general taxpayer registration; all domestic taxable enterprises must complete tax registration upon establishment, and taxpayers below the threshold generally operate as small-scale taxpayers under the simplified 3% collection rate unless they elect general taxpayer status. Under current STA policy, small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding CNY 100,000 (or quarterly sales not exceeding CNY 300,000) may qualify for VAT exemption, where applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border BPO and remote call-center services qualify for 0% VAT only when supplied by a VAT general taxpayer to a non-resident enterprise or individual, the services are completely consumed outside China, the consideration is denominated and received in foreign currency, and the proceeds are repatriated through regulated banking channels. Qualifying service categories include business-process-management services, offshore outsourcing services, and information technology outsourcing services, which cover call-center helpdesk and tele-sales operations. The WFOE must document that the service recipient is overseas, the service is not resupplied or consumed within China, and the performance is not linked to any domestic customer; otherwise the supply is taxable at the standard 6% VAT rate.

  • N/A - Customs Export Declaration or Bill of Entry and Bill of Lading are not required for cross-border services; the customs clearance step does not apply to remote BPO/call-center service exports.
  • Export service contract with the overseas client, in English and/or Chinese, setting out the services, fee basis, service delivery location, and a clause confirming no domestic consumption and no domestic recipient.
  • Commercial invoice issued through the VAT Invoice Management System, reflecting zero-rate or exempt export service treatment.
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation and bank settlement evidence: Cross-Border Receipt and Payment Declaration Form issued by the receiving bank under SAFE, the bank's inward remittance advice or SWIFT credit confirmation, and the WFOE's foreign-exchange settlement record.
  • Service delivery substantiation: call detail records, VoIP or telephony provider usage logs, customer helpdesk tickets, tele-sales recordings or reports, and IP geolocation data demonstrating that the service is performed for and consumed by the overseas client.
  • Overseas client status evidence: certificate of incorporation, tax residence certificate or business registration extract, and beneficial owner certification.

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA VAT Invoice Management System (VMS). Taxpayers use STA-issued tax control devices or cloud-based digital certificates to issue machine-made ordinary or special VAT invoices; every invoice receives an anti-counterfeit tax control code and is transmitted to the STA for real-time electronic clearance. The national system is transitioning to the fully digitalized electronic invoice (DEI) on the STA Electronic Invoice Platform, under which invoices are automatically generated, transmitted, recognized, and archived without paper or physical stamping, and are cleared in real time through the taxpayer's electronic account. Export zero-rate invoicing for cross-border BPO services must be issued as ordinary VAT invoices or DEIs through the same system and must be reconciled with the VAT return and the export-service declaration.

Any zero-rating claim is subject to documentary audit by the STA and cross-checks with the SAFE foreign-exchange reporting system. If the taxpayer fails to substantiate the export service or the foreign-exchange proceeds are not received within the applicable repatriation deadline, the STA will reverse the 0% rate, assess 6% VAT on the gross supply, charge a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose a false-declaration penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded VAT under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Levies. Recovery is retrospective to the original supply date, may extend across the full statutory assessment period, and is unlimited in cases of fraud; criminal liability for tax evasion or export-tax fraud can be pursued under the Criminal Law, and SAFE may separately sanction false cross-border payment declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China observes IMF Article VIII current-account convertibility, so a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise may settle or remit foreign exchange for cross-border services, dividends, interest, royalties and wages after the underlying transaction is validated. The capital account, by contrast, is heavily regulated: every FDI contribution, foreign debt drawdown, equity change and liquidation distribution requires SAFE registration and bank-level verification. For this remote BPO call-center WFOE, the typical treasury closure will involve two separate wheels: settlement of receivable invoices for call-center services on the current account, and controlled conversion of foreign-investment capital for RMB operating expenditure. Profit remittance is permitted but not automatic; the WFOE must submit audited statutory accounts, enterprise income-tax settlement proof, a board dividend resolution, and a SAFE current-account remittance declaration. China does not currently impose daily surrender quotas on export or services proceeds, but all receipts must be repatriated to an onshore bank account and cannot be held indefinitely abroad. Banks therefore act as de facto first-line regulators, checking invoices and evidence of real service capacity against proposed SWIFT flows, especially for inbound and outbound payments that depart from a low-risk BPO profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For services exports under ISIC 7820, SAFE requires repatriation of every foreign-currency receivable into a domestic designated-bank account by the time the invoice is booked; there is no fixed statutory conversion ratio or forced sale window. The WFOE may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency account or voluntarily settle through the onshore interbank market at observable market rates. Export proceeds verification is done through SAFE's digital cross-border receipts and filing system using the relevant services-trade balance-of-payments code; any foreign-currency balance retained in excess of the licensed operating-currency ceiling, or parked offshore without justification, is treated as a foreign-exchange control violation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs are not legally viable as the corporate banking spine for a mainland China WFOE. PBOC digital-payment rules permit licensed non-bank payment institutions to collect or disburse funds for specific convenience scenarios, but they cannot issue an account capable of holding the WFOE's FDI capital, receiving export proceeds, or effecting direct SWIFT clearing as a bank. Wise and similar B2B platforms may be used only by the foreign parent as an offshore payment initiation tool, never as a substitute for the onshore tier-1 bank relationship. Operational feasibility for using a global EMI to route the WFOE's receivables is rated Low: statutorily barred from servicing mainland corporate entities as banks and incompatible with SAFE recording.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign investor must first establish the WFOE with China's market regulator, then complete SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration through the chosen designated bank. Once registration is obtained, the bank opens a Foreign Exchange Capital Account; capital is remitted by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 using the SAFE balance-of-payments capital-account purpose code for inbound equity investment. The WFOE must engage a Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report after each injection, and the bank will verify the report against the remittance before allowing the funds to be settled to RMB. RMB settlement is strictly pay-and-use: each drawdown must be matched by invoices for wages, office rent, telecom systems, cloud services or other operational expenses falling inside the business scope. The funds cannot be used for non-operating real estate, domestic equity purchases, unauthorized entrusted loans, or conversion into personal wealth.A pure BPO call-center WFOE should route most current-account disbursements through a corporate SWIFT MT103 wire transfer from the onshore RMB settlement account or the retained foreign-currency account. For trade in services, the bank requires an underlying contract, an invoice, and where applicable a tax withholding certificate for cross-border royalties or technical service fees. Commercial letters of credit are appropriate only if the BPO scope also imports physical IT hardware or telecom equipment; documentary collections are rarely used and inefficient for high-volume low-value service fees. Domestic RMB vendor and employee payments are cleared through CNAPS and IBPS, while foreign-currency supplier payments require SAFE current-account filing with the standard services-trade balance-of-payments code.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts executed with an onshore SAFE-designated bank, available only when the WFOE can evidence an underlying current-account or approved capital-account exposure and does not use the contract for speculative purposes.Onshore FX swaps between CNY and foreign currency for tenor-matched conversion of FDI capital or retained export proceeds, subject to the bank's internal counterparty approval and SAFE real-demand review.Cross-currency swaps for long-term economic exposure such as foreign-currency financing or committed dividend streams, permitted only with documented underlying exposure and timely transaction reporting.FX option structures as hedging overlays on wind-down or contract renewal cash flows, restricted to non-leveraged cost-bearing strategies and never sold short by the WFOE.Offshore non-deliverable forwards are not permissible as a WFOE hedging tool on the mainland; an NDF can be performed only by the offshore parent outside SAFE's onshore perimeter and must not create net cash settlement obligations inside the WFOE's bank accounts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs are not legally viable as the corporate banking spine for a mainland China WFOE. PBOC digital-payment rules permit licensed non-bank payment institutions to collect or disburse funds for specific convenience scenarios, but they cannot issue an account capable of holding the WFOE's FDI capital, receiving export proceeds, or effecting direct SWIFT clearing as a bank. Wise and similar B2B platforms may be used only by the foreign parent as an offshore payment initiation tool, never as a substitute for the onshore tier-1 bank relationship. Operational feasibility for using a global EMI to route the WFOE's receivables is rated Low: statutorily barred from servicing mainland corporate entities as banks and incompatible with SAFE recording.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file (contemporaneous TP documentation) required when any of the following related-party transaction thresholds are exceeded for the tax year: tangible asset transfers ≥ CNY 400 million; intangible asset transfers ≥ CNY 200 million; financial asset transfers including loans ≥ CNY 100 million; other related-party transactions (including service fee recharges and cost allocations) ≥ CNY 200 million; Master File required if aggregate annual related-party transactions ≥ CNY 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report notification required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ CNY 5.5 million? no, specifically for MNEs with total consolidated group revenue of at least CNY 1 billion? Need exact: China requires CbC reporting for ultimate parent groups of MNEs with consolidated revenue in the prior fiscal year of at least CNY 5.5 billion (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: Under the Chinese GAAR (Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law) and SAT Announcement 2015 No. 21, substance over form applies to any preferential tax treatment or treaty benefit; the BPO call center WFOE must display Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) actually performed in mainland China, including end-customer contract negotiation, service pricing, quality management, hiring, training, and risk assumption; a shell entity lacking real employees, premises, and business decision-making functions will be disregarded for tax purposes, and its income may be reattributed to the foreign parent or recharacterized as taxable dividend; Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) type requirements are not formally statutory in China, but China tax authorities increasingly apply the OECD principal purpose test (PPT) and beneficial ownership tests to deny treaty benefits to letterbox companies.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Underreporting

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; Article 201 of the Criminal Law punishes tax evasion with imprisonment up to 3 years plus fines, or 3-7 years for evaded tax amounts exceeding 30% of tax due and an evaded amount above RMB 100,000; additional surcharge of 0.05% per day on unpaid tax

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally liable for civil restitution if they signed false returns or directed fraudulent accounting; they face criminal liability as 'directly responsible persons' under the Criminal Law, including imprisonment, asset seizure, and unlimited fines; tax dishonesty blacklisting triggers an exit ban (no departure without clearing tax), publicity on the credit platform, and prohibition from serving as corporate directors in any jurisdiction; individual travel bans are enforced through Public Security exit-control measures.
Abuse of Treaty / Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under the GAAR (Article 47, EITL) and Article 121 of the Tax Administration Law, the tax authority may recharacterize the entity, deny treaty benefits, impose the standard 10% WHT on dividends retroactively, and add 0.05% daily delinquency interest; under Article 205 of the Criminal Law, creating false or forged documents in the treaty relief process constitutes criminal forgery/fraud, punishable by imprisonment up to 10 years and fines; the false TRC itself may be investigated by the foreign tax authority as a criminal act

Director personal liability: Directors who initiated or signed treaty benefit claims are jointly and severally liable for the under-taxed WHT plus penalties; they can be subject to a personal civil travel ban under Article 8, Law on Administration of Exit and Entry of Chinese Citizens, until the tax debt is settled; they face criminal fraud charges for issuing false certificates, leading to imprisonment and disqualification from management positions; the court may pierce the corporate veil and attach directors' personal bank accounts and properties if they deliberately transferred profits out of the WFOE to defeat tax claims.
False VAT Invoicing / Fabricated Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 205 of the Criminal Law punishes issuance of false VAT special invoices with imprisonment up to life, life or large fines, and confiscation of property; Article 206 punishes accepting forged special invoices; tax authorities impose a fine of CNY 50,000 to CNY 500,000 and suspend the enterprise VAT refunds; transfer pricing documentation fraud is penalized under Article 42 of the TAL with a fine of CNY 10,000-50,000 plus adjustment of taxable income and 0.05% daily late fee; willful falsification is treated as tax evasion under Article 201

Director personal liability: The director, finance manager, and legal representative may be criminally liable as 'principal or accomplice' for invoice crimes; penalties include arrest, criminal detention, imprisonment, and unlimited fines; civil asset restitution includes reimbursement of all evaded taxes and penalties from personal assets if corporate assets are insufficient; the individual is automatically listed on the 'serious tax violation blacklist' with consequences including non-renewal of passports, denial of visa applications, eligibility for public procurement, and prohibition from leaving mainland China; international asset tracing and anti-corruption authorities may cooperate through mutual legal assistance treaties to seize overseas properties.
