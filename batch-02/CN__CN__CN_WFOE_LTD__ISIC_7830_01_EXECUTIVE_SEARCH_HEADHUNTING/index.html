Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address is generally rejected for a WFOE in the executive search and headhunting industry. The Company Law of the PRC and the Regulations on the Administration of Company Registration require the registered address to be a physical premises actually used by the enterprise for its principal business or administrative management. The local SAMR office will cross-check the property ownership certificate or lease agreement, and the tax authority will conduct a verification of the business premises before issuing the tax registration certificate. For a headhunting agency, the local Human Resources and Social Security Bureau will, during the license review, require that the applicant has an independent office with dedicated workstations, a meeting room for confidential interviews, secure file storage, and evidence of normal business operation. Consequently, commercial mailbox addresses and co-working virtual addresses are admissible only if the co-working operator accepts a formal sub-lease for an actual assignable desk, the lease term is at least 12 months, and the operator provides a written business premises usage confirmation letter acceptable to SAMR, the HRSSB, the tax agency, and the commercial bank. A pure postal mailbox without a physical reception desk, meeting room, and staff presence will not satisfy the requirement and will be treated as false registration, subject to a fine of up to RMB 50,000 and deregistration. For the target payment rails, the receiving bank will also conduct a physical visit and require the company name sign at the door before opening the RMB basic bank account and drafting foreign exchange settlement instructions for retainer wires.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT registration threshold for business entities; every enterprise must register for VAT upon obtaining its business license. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable service revenue exceeds RMB 5,000,000, with voluntary election available for smaller service entities.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exports, the 0% VAT zero-rating applies automatically upon compliance with customs export procedures and foreign exchange repatriation rules. For executive search/headhunting services (classified as consultancy/modern services), Chinese VAT law under Ministry of Finance Circular Cai Shui [2016] No.36 generally provides VAT exemption rather than zero-rating for services rendered to overseas clients and wholly consumed outside mainland China. To qualify, the WFOE must prove: (i) the services are supplied to a non-resident client, (ii) the services are performed and consumed entirely outside China, (iii) the price is negotiated on a commercial arm's-length basis, and (iv) the foreign currency consideration is received and repatriated into China through a licensed bank. For services that fall within national zero-rating categories (e.g., R&D, design, international freight onward), the same documentary and repatriation requirements apply to claim a 0% output VAT rate.

  • Cross-border service contract (bilingual or English) specifying the headhunting deliverables and overseas consumer; commercial invoice issued by the WFOE; evidence of overseas consumption, including client engagement emails, meeting records, and final placement reports showing executive search output used entirely abroad.
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Income Declaration (FX income report issued by the Chinese receiving bank under SAFE reporting rules) evidencing the receipt of overseas payments and the currency conversion/remittance into the WFOE's domestic account.
  • For any incidental goods exports (e.g., recruitment marketing materials): Customs Export Goods Declaration (Chinese Customs Export Declaration Form), Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, and Export Collection Verification receipt from the remitting bank.

China employs a real-time fiscal invoice clearance mechanism under the Golden Tax System (GTS phase IV). VAT invoices, including both ordinary VAT invoices and Special VAT Invoices (SVIs), must be issued via the STA-approved electronic invoice service platform. Fully digitalized electronic invoices (FD-EI) are generated with a unique tax control code, transmitted to the STA instantaneously, and supported by mandatory API-based integration for high-volume issuers. There is no separate physical stamping requirement; the tax control code serves as the anti-fraud fiscal stamp. All invoice data is subject to continuous automated cross-matching between VAT output invoices, input credits, corporate income tax, and bank transaction data within the STA's tax digitisation infrastructure.

Unverified or fraudulent zero-rating/exemption claims attract comprehensive STA audit scrutiny. If the STA determines that the exported services were not wholly consumed abroad, or that the customs/FX documents are forged or non-authentic, it will retrospectively reverse the VAT exemption/zero-rating, claw back the unpaid VAT for up to 5 years (with no time limit in cases of fraud), charge daily late-payment interest of 0.05% of the unpaid amount, and impose administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the under-declared tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal exposure under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (fraudulent export tax rebates) applies where the WFOE uses false documentation or fictitious export transactions; penalties can include incarceration of responsible individuals and confiscation of assets. Additionally, SAFE and the State Administration for Market Regulation may impose separate foreign-exchange control penalties for unlawful cross-border receipt or retention of funds.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in services such as executive search and headhunting retainer fees, subject to genuine transaction documentation and standard bank verification. Capital account transactions, however, operate under a registered and supervised system: a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must register its foreign investment with SAFE or a delegated bank under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting regime, and only then may the foreign shareholder inject capital. Injected foreign currency is converted to RMB at a domestic bank under a single capital account settlement rule, and the RMB funds are credited to the WFOE's capital account or a renminbi capital escrow account. For profit repatriation, the WFOE must complete annual audit of financial statements, file corporate income tax returns, and verify that the distributable profits are derived from real operations; remittance abroad is processed by the bank against audited profit distribution resolutions and the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate. There is no general surrender requirement for export proceeds since the 2012 reform, but all foreign exchange receipts from current account activities must be repatriated and either retained in a current account or settled with a designated bank; a formal reconciliation against the State Administration of Foreign Exchange monitoring system is mandatory. In practice, structural pain points include the need for registered capital to be fully injected within the business term, strict use of the capital account prohibition on providing equity financing to third parties, and the difficulty of converting RMB retained earnings into hard currency without a complete statutory audit trail. For a headhunting WFOE with intangible service invoices, the bank will demand robust contracts, staff payroll evidence, and tax payment receipts for every remittance, as service trade is treated as high-impact for money laundering due to the absence of physical goods. Overall, RMB is convertible for operating income and remittances of after-tax profits, but every cross-border flow must be mapped to an approved purpose code and cannot be netted or arbitrarily offset.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The People's Republic of China does not mandate a fixed statutory conversion ratio for export proceeds. Under the current SAFE framework, a WFOE may retain foreign currency receipts in an export settlement current account or independently decide to settle them into RMB. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to China within the timeframe specified by the terms of the trade contract, generally no later than the payment due date, and must be received through a designated foreign exchange bank. The bank will require exhibition of customs declarations, invoices, and trade service contracts; if the receivable remains outstanding beyond the statutory reconciliation period (normally 90 days for general trade, extendable with justification), the WFOE must file a trade foreign exchange receivable deferral registration with SAFE. For executive search services, there is no physical customs declaration; the trigger documentary dossier includes the signed headhunting agreement, timesheets, candidate placement reports, and cross-border invoice; banks will treat such service export proceeds as current account income and allow retention or settlement at the WFOE's discretion, subject to anti-money laundering screening. The core legal obligation is timely repatriation, not conversion, and a failure to repatriate warrants penalties under the Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Trade.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Practically non-viable for a China domestic WFOE treasury. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Airwallex are not licensed to maintain local RMB capital accounts or deliver services against SAFE regulations. While Wise B2B can be used to submit certain outward remittance instructions in theory, the funds must first be domiciled in a duly licensed Chinese bank current account; the EMI itself cannot act as a receiving bank for foreign direct investment capital injections, cannot issue foreign exchange receipts for capital account verification, and cannot interpret Chinese statutory reporting obligations to the PBOC/SAFE. Furthermore, China's Rules for the Administration of RMB Settlement of Cross-Border Trade require that cross-border settlement be handled by a domestic designated bank with a direct CNAPS/SWIFT interface. An offshore EMI would merely be a payment instruction aggregator and would still require a corresponding domestic bank sponsor, which defeats the compliance purpose. The only allowable niche is for employee expense reimbursement or outbound service payments after the underlying RMB has already been settled in a domestic bank, but even then, the corporate bank's approval is required. Hence the underwriting recommendation is to rely exclusively on Tier-1 Chinese banks and treat the EMI as an auxiliary reporting tool only, never as the primary liquidity rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the WFOE must be routed as a direct SWIFT MT103 from the foreign shareholder's bank to the WFOE's designated capital account at the chosen Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must include the payment purpose code '621011 – foreigners' capital contribution to establish a foreign-invested enterprise' under the SAFE categorization, and the remitter must be the legal shareholder as recorded in the Foreign Investment Information Report. The receiving bank will wait for the transaction to hit its Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and then request the capital verification report from the statutory Chinese certified public accountant before approving the settlement of foreign currency into RMB. Once the RMB is settled, the funds must be placed into a capital deposit account with a restricted use: they can be used only for the WFOE's own operations such as payroll, rent, purchase of office equipment, and business expenses; they cannot be lent to affiliates or used to acquire real estate for non-operational purposes. For additional working capital, the WFOE may raise an intercompany loan subject to the 'macro-prudential adjustment coefficient' ratio set by the PBOC, whereby the borrowing limit is based on the enterprise's net assets; the intercompany loan must be registered with SAFE, and the inbound creditor's account must be identified via a full name and a purpose code '622 – foreign loans from non-residents'. Both capital injection and intercompany loans require a follow-up SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration update within 15 days of the change, and each disbursement to third parties must be supported by an invoice, a contract, and a tax withholding voucher where applicable. The critical interlock is that the bank's anti-money laundering monitoring will tag the WFOE's capital account if settlement exceeds the declared investment amount or if the source of funds does not match the registered beneficial owner; hence the treasury department must maintain a centralized register of all inbound MT103 messages and reconcile them to the SAFE system daily.For the executive search and headhunting business, outbound trade disbursements principally consist of retainer fee remittances to overseas subcontractors, referral partners, or international payroll agents, and dividend payments to the offshore parent. The preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for all single payment obligations under USD 500,000, using the appropriate SAFE purpose code: 'Services – Management Consulting and Talent Services' (322 for current account service fee payments) or 'Profit Distribution' (320) for dividends. The bank will require a complete documentary estate for each MT103: a synthetic invoice with the exact description of the executive search service, the underlying client contract, the candidate placement evidence, and a tax deduction declaration for the contractor if the payment is to a non-resident entity. For higher-value placements where the WFOE is the prime contractor and must ensure quality and delivery, a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) is permissible for the export of services? In practice, international service trade does not use shipping documents, so a banker's acceptance or a commercial letter of credit is rare; however, a standby letter of credit (SBLC) from the client's bank can be provided as a payment guarantee for large retained search mandates. The most operationally efficient route for recurring monthly retainer payments of five to ten accounts is to enforce a payment factory arrangement with the domestic bank: the WFOE uploads a bulk payments file in the bank's proprietary format, the bank screens the beneficiaries against OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions lists, and then releases the RTGS/CHIPS or SWIFT transactions in one batch. For outbound dividends, the bank must verify the audited financials, the tax completion certificate for withholding tax (WHT) paid on the dividend, the board resolution declaring the dividend, and the SAFE profit repatriation form; the funds are then settled directly without further review. The clearing rail is always MT103 through the domestic bank; no third-party netting or e-wallet settlement is permitted for a WFOE's regulated trade and dividend flows.Onshore RMB/USD foreign exchange spot transactions are available for current account service trade receipts and payments; the bank requires genuine transaction documents and does not permit speculative trading.Enterprises can enter into plain-vanilla forward contracts with the commercial bank for the purpose of hedging the WFOE's receivables or payables, with the underlying trade contract evidenced and the contract maturity aligned to the expected cash flow; banks usually require a 10-20% margin guarantee, but a corporate credit line can substitute.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are strictly prohibited for onshore China entities; the deliverable USD/RMB forward contract is the only permissible instrument, and settlement must be on a deliverable basis at maturity through the domestic bank;
