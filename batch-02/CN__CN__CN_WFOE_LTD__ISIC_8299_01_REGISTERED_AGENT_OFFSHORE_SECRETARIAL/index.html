Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual offices and commercial mailbox addresses are generally not permissible for a licensed company secretarial and registered agent WFOE. SAMR requires a registered address corresponding to a valid physical lease and genuine business premises; the State Taxation Administration and commercial banks reject a virtual office or remote mailbox for tax registration, VAT invoice control equipment installation, bank account opening and substantive money-laundering risk assessment, because the entity cannot demonstrate actual management, personnel or records at the declared address. Certain free-trade-zone cluster addresses may be tolerated for passive holding companies, but a business scope involving registered agent, offshore secretarial services and statutory company secretarial licenses requires a dedicated physical office, a visible name plate and original statutory books and records maintained within the administrative district.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the Company Law for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in registered agent and offshore secretarial services. The registered capital set out in the Articles of Association must be fully subscribed by the foreign shareholders, and the subscribed contributions must actually be paid within the capital contribution term specified in the Articles. No blocked escrow account or mandatory upfront capital deposit is required at formation, but the contributed capital must be genuine, proportionate to the stated business scope, and subject to normal foreign exchange administration rules for any subsequent repatriation.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable at the industrial level, but the registered office must hold a fire safety inspection acceptance certificate under the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China before commencing use. The workplace must receive a commercial lease fire safety compliance inspection, and general occupational safety obligations under the Work Safety Law apply to office employees. No separate civil defense permit or structural license is required because the premises are used solely for registered agent, corporate secretarial, and consulting services.

Competent tax authority

(2) The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License is the statutory Chinese Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID); no separate TIN application is needed. (3) Complete the automatic tax registration confirmation within 30 days of Business License issuance through the integrated one-stop registration window at the SAMR or through the National Electronic Tax Bureau; the tax authority receives corporate registration data electronically. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise for corporate income tax purposes and is taxable on its worldwide income at the general statutory rate of 25%; it is not a pass-through vehicle. For indirect tax purposes, it is registered as a VAT taxpayer.

Available election forms

None under China's corporate income tax classification rules; China has no check-the-box election for domestic limited liability companies. The WFOE may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer status only while annual taxable turnover remains below the RMB 5 million threshold, or it may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer upon meeting the conditions. China also applies anti-hybrid mismatch rules to deny double-deduction outcomes, but these are statutory anti-avoidance provisions, not elective classification mechanisms.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for tax registration because every WFOE is automatically registered upon issuance of its Business License; however, for VAT classification, a VAT taxpayer engaged in taxable sales with annual turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (CNY) must register as a general VAT taxpayer. A taxpayer below that threshold may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the simplified collection rate.

China mandates real-time fiscal clearance of invoices through the STA Golden Tax System Phase IV and the National Electronic Tax Bureau. Full Digital Electronic Invoices (FDEIs) must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform using the unified invoice code and cryptographic freshness code; issuance is transmitted electronically to the tax authority in near real-time, and each invoice carries an anti-fraud QR code and digital fiscal stamp. VAT special invoices must be uploaded to the tax authority's verification system, and any mismatch between issued invoices, received invoices, VAT returns, and customs data automatically triggers tax risk alerts. The relevant technical interface is the STA e-Tax system API, which supports electronic invoice issuance, red-letter reversal, and refund-tracking without paper stamps.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates as an Article VIII IMF member with full current-account convertibility for trade and services, meaning current-account transactions such as remitted service fees, royalties, and trade payments can generally be settled through designated banks without SAFE prior approval. Capital-account flows — including inbound equity investment, intra-group loans, and return of capital — remain subject to strict admission and verification controls. Foreign-invested enterprises enjoy the freedom to convert their registered capital into RMB at the market central parity rate, provided the conversion is for bona fide operational purposes; however, outbound profit remittance requires an audited financial statement, completed tax clearance, and submission of a foreign exchange remittance application to the bank. Surrender of exports is no longer mandatory on a fixed percentage basis since the 2012 reform, but export receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days of customs declaration, and SAFE supervises the truthfulness of each payment under the higher-level transaction documentation rule. In practice, the bank performs direct counterparty payment review, and while Chinese yuan has moved toward greater balance-of-payments flexibility, convertibility for capital transactions remains procedural and case-by-case.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Administration, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the Customs export declaration date. The mandatory conversion ratio is currently zero — enterprises may retain the full amount as foreign currency in an FEE account or sell at the market price. For high-risk classifications, SAFE may require earlier repatriation and stricter documentary verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low (statutory bar). Foreign digital EMI licenses do not authorise payment institutions to open accounts for Chinese mainland entities; under the PBOC/SAFE regulatory framework, only domestically licensed bank- or online-payment institutions (with payment license) can participate in cross-border FX settlement. Such EMIs have no access to SAFE's Foreign Exchange Code, cannot receive capital injections, and cannot conduct currency conversion legally for a WFOE. They may, in a limited offshore sales segment, facilitate collection of non-mainland client fees, but every transaction must be routed in compliance with the WFOE's onshore concessionary control; they cannot replace Tier-1 commercial banking rails.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Statutory audit is mandatory for all limited liability companies in mainland China, including every WFOE, under Article 164 of the PRC Company Law (as amended through 2023). No quantitative exemption is granted based on total assets, turnover, or headcount; irrespective of size, each company must engage a licensed CPA firm (approved by the Ministry of Finance) to audit its annual financial statements. In addition, a certified audit report is de facto required for (1) the annual corporate income tax settlement where the enterprise files a tax loss, claims carryforward losses, has intra-group or related-party cross-border transactions, or adopts transfer-pricing documentation, (2) bank credit facilities where the lender contractually requires an audited statement, and (3) foreign-invested enterprises applying for foreign exchange registration or capital market licences. Local taxation bureaus in some provinces require an audit report for enterprises with annual revenue above RMB 10 million or total assets above RMB 20 million, or with more than 50 employees, as part of tax risk-differential management.
