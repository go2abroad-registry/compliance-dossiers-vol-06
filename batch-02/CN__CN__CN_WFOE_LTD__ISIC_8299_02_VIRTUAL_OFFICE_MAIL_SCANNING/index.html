Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a WFOE LLC under the PRC Company Law (2023). The registered capital is a subscribed amount recorded in the Articles of Association; each shareholder must fully contribute within five years after incorporation unless the Articles provide a severer sequential distribution schedule. Capital verification reports are not mandatory for SAMR registration in a virtual-office/non-licensed sector, but banks and foreign-exchange authorities may require injection evidence. No blocked escrow capital account is legally prescribed for this service business; nonetheless, funds for registered capital must pass through the Company's designated capital account in China.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense clearance: the premises must have a completed building fire-protection acceptance record, a fire safety management system, portable extinguishers rated for office electrical fires, emergency lighting and evacuation signage, and, if the office or mailroom is open to clients as a public venue, a Fire Safety Inspection Certificate from the local fire rescue brigade. Occupational clearance: the company must carry out a workplace hazard assessment under the PRC Work Safety Law, register employees for work injury insurance, install anti-static grounding for sensitive scanning equipment, and provide sufficient load capacity certificates for shelving and storage; any basement mailroom must have a civil defense occupancy approval issued by the municipal civil defense office.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a WFOE LLC under the PRC Company Law (2023). The registered capital is a subscribed amount recorded in the Articles of Association; each shareholder must fully contribute within five years after incorporation unless the Articles provide a severer sequential distribution schedule. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is through the 'e-Tax' National Electronic Tax Service Portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the local Electronic Tax Bureau online service hall.

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer registration number. The WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA office within 30 days after the business license date, either via the e-Tax portal or at the tax service hall, and must file its bank account details, accounting professional, legal representative, and address information. No separate paper TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is used for CIT, VAT, payroll and withholding returns. A newly incorporated WFOE whose expected annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 should simultaneously apply for general VAT taxpayer status; otherwise it is initially a small-scale taxpayer and may later elect general VAT taxpayer status upon meeting statutory criteria. For a non-resident enterprise that does not establish a Chinese branch, no general VAT registration is required; the China-resident payer is statutorily obliged to withhold VAT and enterprise income tax on China-source taxable services, and the non-resident entity is identified in the withholding filing by its home-country TIN or by a temporary registration record created by the STA in the taxpayer master file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification; the People's Republic of China does not maintain a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch passthrough election. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise at entity level. Available statutory elections are limited to (i) small-scale VAT taxpayer election to become a general VAT taxpayer before the threshold, (ii) selection of the simplified VAT method where specifically authorized, and (iii) treaty-based relief claims for withholding tax, none of which alter the WFOE's entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; taxpayers below this threshold are normally classified as small-scale taxpayers. For foreign non-resident suppliers without an establishment in China, there is no local turnover threshold; VAT is collected by reverse-charge withholding from the Chinese recipient at the applicable rate, or the foreign supplier may elect direct VAT registration for cross-border e-commerce sales.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated if they are actually shipped across the PRC customs frontier and lawfully declared through the China International Trade Single Window. Cross-border services are zero-rated only if they are within the statutory positive list (including international transportation, certain research and development services, design services, software services, and certain information technology services) and are supplied to an overseas enterprise or individual and consumed entirely outside mainland China. The target virtual-office and mail scanning service (ISIC 8299) is not expressly listed on the 0% service positive list; therefore it will ordinarily be taxable at 6% as a modern service unless the taxpayer obtains confirmation that the service is wholly performed and consumed outside China and qualifies for cross-border exemption or zero-rating under Ministry of Finance and STA Notice Cai Shui 2016 No. 36 and subsequent rules. The taxpayer bears the burden of proving overseas recipient, offshore consumption, actual service delivery, and foreign payment remittance.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export customs declaration submitted via the China International Trade Single Window, with the customs bill number)
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, air waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the customs declaration value, quantity, and description
  • Export contract, purchase order, or frame services agreement containing the parties, Incoterms, payment terms, and destination
  • Bank receipt or credit advice evidencing foreign-currency export proceeds credited to the WFOE's domestic foreign-exchange account, recorded in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System
  • For cross-border services: service agreement, evidence of delivery/use outside China, overseas recipient's business existence documentation, foreign remittance voucher, and a taxpayer written declaration that no local service consumption occurred in mainland China

China operates the STA 'Golden Tax Phase IV' invoice administration system and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) service platform. Registered VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the official e-invoice platform, tax-control software, or approved APIs integrated with the STA; every invoice is transmitted to the STA invoicing database in real time upon issuance and carries a statutory anti-counterfeit tax-control code plus an electronic fiscal seal generated by the tax authority. Physical paper invoices, where still approved, must be processed through tax-control fiscal equipment and have the same digital clearance obligation. FDEIs require no physical stamp; authenticity is verified through the national unified electronic invoice service platform.

The STA and Customs will cross-check the consistency of contract flow, goods or service flow, invoice flow, and fund flow. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days without a legally approved extension may cause the export VAT zero-rating or exemption to be reversed, with the previously uncharged VAT collected together with late-payment surcharge. If fraudulent or over-declared export refunds are detected, the tax authority recovers the refunded tax and imposes a penalty of not less than 50% and not more than five times the amount refunded under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where the fraud constitutes a criminal offense, the case is referred for prosecution under Criminal Law Article 204 (crime of defrauding export tax rebates) and Article 205 (crime of falsely issuing VAT special invoices), carrying custodial sentences and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime under the People's Bank of China, with current-account RMB convertibility fundamentally in place. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, ordinary trade and service payments can be converted with supporting documents such as contracts, invoices, and customs declarations through a designated FX bank. The capital account, by contrast, remains heavily controlled: FDI equity injections, foreign debt, liquidation remittances, and offshore lending each require SAFE registration, bank-level credibility checks, and post-event reporting. Profit distributions to foreign shareholders are possible only after annual statutory audit, tax clearance, and verification of registered capital, with remittance routed via SAFE-sanctioned profit-repatriation procedures. Surplus capital settlement requires verified compliance with the negative-list industry restriction, and all settlement proceeds must be applied to bona fide operating expenditures. Surrender obligations have eased historically, but repatriation requirements persist for export proceeds. This asymmetry means treasury operations must distinguish operational RMB liquidity, which is highly convertible for current-account purposes, from structural capital movements, which are constrained and document-intensive. Any attempt to bypass registration, mis-designate settlement purpose, or use shell nominee accounts is likely to be rejected by domestic banks and to trigger SAFE enforcement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE administration, China no longer applies a fixed statutory ratio requiring export foreign-exchange receipts to be mandatorily surrendered to the state. However, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days of receipt unless an offshore retention exemption has been approved by SAFE. RMB settlement is permissible at the customer's bank spot rate after the bank verifies the underlying customs declaration, invoice, and logistics documents. There is no legally fixed minimum conversion ratio for export proceeds; enterprises may retain a portion as foreign-currency deposits or voluntarily convert to RMB. Compliance is evidenced through the bank's FX monitoring system and post-transaction reporting to SAFE.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank and ultimate monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as global neobanks and virtual-account providers are not viable as the primary banking layer for a PRC WFOE. They hold no Chinese mainland banking license, no direct access to CNAPS or CIPS, and are outside SAFE's direct regulatory perimeter. They cannot legally open a RMB basic deposit account, receive FDI capital under the capital account, or execute SAFE-regulated FX settlements. A WFOE may use an offshore EMI only as an ancillary collection and disbursement tool for foreign-currency operations outside China, but all onshore treasury flows, tax payments, payroll, and cross-border capital receipts must be cleared through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Any structure that routes FDI capital through such an EMI will be presumptively treated as non-compliant and will be blocked by SAFE and PBOC reporting systems.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The WFOE must first complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration through its designated domestic bank, obtaining the unique FDI business registration code. The foreign investor then wires equity capital from its own offshore corporate bank account to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed domestic Tier-1 bank. The SWIFT MT103 remittance instruction must use the approved transaction purpose code for capital injection, cite the FDI registration number and the investor's legal name, and include a reference to the capital contribution schedule in the company's articles. Only the beneficiary bank may release the funds into the capital account, after which the WFOE may settle foreign currency into RMB under the settlement-by-verification principle, strictly for approved operating purposes such as rent, salaries, procurement, and tax payments. The WFOE must engage a PRC certified public accountant to issue a capital-verification report confirming the receipt of subscribed capital, and all related paperwork must be filed with SAFE and the local market regulation authority within statutory deadlines.Outbound disbursements for goods and services are executed through SWIFT MT103 payment orders, commercial letters of credit issued under MT700, or documentary collections, all cleared via the domestic bank's current-account framework. Each disbursement requires a valid commercial contract, vendor invoice, customs declaration or service agreement, and, where applicable, transport documents and an inspection certificate. Banks will screen each transaction against the cross-border RMB/FX negative list, capital-flight indicators, and sanctions lists. Service-related payments may additionally require tax-related settlement certificates or withholding-tax proof. The WFOE must ensure that all outgoing foreign-currency payments are legitimate current-account transactions with full documentary support; treasury cannot use these rails to prepay intercompany trade obligations in excess of the underlying service, as banks will invoke enhanced due diligence and may suspend the payment if the counterparty or purpose code is ambiguous.Onshore deliverable CNY forward contracts, including USD/CNY and EUR/CNY, with genuine underlying trade, service, or debt-service exposure executing through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE real-demand rules.Cross-currency swaps for hedging WFOE foreign-currency balance-sheet positions or eligible cross-border loan exposure, subject to bank credit approval and SAFE registration or filing requirements.Vanilla FX options and option combinations involving premium payment and physical delivery, permitted only when the notional volume is proportioned to verified commercial exposure.FX swaps between onshore RMB and foreign currency for liquidity management, with strict documentation of the economic purpose and SAFE reporting through the counterparty bank.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMIs such as global neobanks and virtual-account providers are not viable as the primary banking layer for a PRC WFOE. They hold no Chinese mainland banking license, no direct access to CNAPS or CIPS, and are outside SAFE's direct regulatory perimeter. They cannot legally open a RMB basic deposit account, receive FDI capital under the capital account, or execute SAFE-regulated FX settlements. A WFOE may use an offshore EMI only as an ancillary collection and disbursement tool for foreign-currency operations outside China, but all onshore treasury flows, tax payments, payroll, and cross-border capital receipts must be cleared through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Any structure that routes FDI capital through such an EMI will be presumptively treated as non-compliant and will be blocked by SAFE and PBOC reporting systems.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is required where annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 400 million, intangible-property transfers, financing transactions or service transactions exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is mandatory where the enterprise's annual related-party transaction aggregate exceeds RMB 10 billion or the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds the locally announced threshold, currently in the multi-billion-RMB range. Schedule A109010 related-party disclosure applies to all enterprises with controlled transactions regardless of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the tax authorities apply substance-over-form, beneficial-ownership requirements and Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law as a general anti-avoidance rule. For a CMRA/virtual-office WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must occur in China, meaning personnel who negotiate and manage client relationships, physical or digital office facilities, operational equipment and accounting oversight must be present. A letterbox subsidiary with no real activity will be disregarded for tax and treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company and treaty-abusive dividend distributions

Statutory sanction: Articles 47 and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law permit re-characterization and back interest of up to 20% per annum; Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection imposes a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, impose full 10% withholding, and add late-payment surcharges and social-credit penalties.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Civil Code general provisions and Company Law fiduciary principles, a director who knowingly signs false declarations, receives or transfers stripped profits, or causes the company to continue trading while insolvent may be jointly and severally liable for restitution. Additional consequences include exit-control blacklisting prohibiting overseas travel, personal asset freezing, and criminal tax-evasion prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, punishable by up to seven years imprisonment plus fines.
Transfer pricing manipulation and unsubstantiated intercompany charges

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may re-determine taxable income and impose back interest and penalties; Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law exposes the enterprise to a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. The absence of required TP documentation triggers the tax authority's right to estimate taxable income and may lead to additional penalties against the entity as a serious documentation violation.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false pricing vouchers are exposed to de facto director liability and civil compensation if adjusted tax debts render the WFOE insolvent. In aggravated cases, deliberately fabricating an intercompany charge apparatus to shift Chinese profits is treated as criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, carrying fines and imprisonment up to seven years; exit-restriction blacklisting remains in force until all tax arrears are settled.
Undeclared digital revenue and AML/foreign-exchange violations

Statutory sanction: Article 6 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law includes all such revenue in taxable income; failure to account triggers Article 63 penalties of 50%-500% of omitted tax plus 0.05% daily surcharge. Operation without the required postal-licence registration violates PRC postal regulations and may lead to licence revocation and forfeiture of illegal gains. Foreign-exchange violations under SAFE rules can attract penalties of 30%-100% of the unlawful transaction amount and personal fines for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced where directors use the Stripe account as a personal operating account or actively conceal receipts. Directors face joint civil restitution, travel bans, inclusion on the customs and foreign-exchange blacklist, and, if fraud crosses criminal thresholds, prosecution under Articles 201 and 225 of the Criminal Law for tax evasion and illegal business operation, with imprisonment up to seven years and confiscation of assets.
