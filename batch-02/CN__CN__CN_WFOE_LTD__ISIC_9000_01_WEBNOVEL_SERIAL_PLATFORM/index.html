Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required as a registration precondition for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise under the PRC Company Law. No blocked escrow account or capital guarantee deposit is required at incorporation. The registered capital is determined in the articles of association and must be rationally matched to the proposed business scale. Under Article 47(1) of the 2023 Company Law, shareholders of a limited liability company must contribute their subscribed capital in full within five years after incorporation unless an applicable exemption or longer transitional schedule applies. Capital may be cash or permissible non-monetary property, and no percentage of the registered capital must be paid in before filing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a Chinese tax resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income, with no pass-through treatment for foreign shareholders; dividends distributed to non-resident shareholders are subject to withholding enterprise income tax at the statutory rate or reduced treaty rate, and the WFOE remains fully liable to VAT and enterprise income tax as a standalone corporate taxpayer.

Available election forms

None; the People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election, a hybrid mismatch election, or an optional transparent entity status for a Chinese limited liability WFOE. The only relevant election is the VAT registration status election: an enterprise whose annual taxable sales do not exceed the mandatory general taxpayer threshold may elect to be registered as a general VAT taxpayer, subject to sound accounting records and approval by the competent tax authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for mandatory VAT registration of an incorporated WFOE; registration is required as part of the USCC-based national tax registration upon establishment. For VAT taxpayer classification, annual taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000 over a rolling 12-month or four-quarter period makes general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory; a taxpayer at or below RMB 5,000,000 remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods exported from China, 0% VAT is applied to actual exports and the enterprise may claim export tax refund/exemption after customs clearance. For the WFOE's qualifying digital web-novel subscription and offshore platform services, VAT zero-rating or exemption is available only where the service is supplied to an overseas user or business, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, the transaction falls within the STA's statutory cross-border zero-rated/exempt positive list (covering, depending on the precise contractual description, cultural creative services, information services, copyright/licensing, or software services), and the supply is not rendered for domestic consumption. The taxpayer must hold conclusive evidence of offshore customer identity and location, offshore payment and foreign-exchange settlement, contractual delivery terms, and platform-side consumption logs demonstrating the rendering of the service outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) containing the goods description, HS classification, FOB value, consignee, and customs clearance date
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill issued to the overseas consignee for physical export shipments
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract or purchase order
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by the PRC bank, including an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank inbound remittance / export proceeds settlement voucher proving payment repatriation from the overseas buyer
  • For digital web-novel services: signed digital service or licensing agreement, overseas customer profile, IP address and access logs, content delivery/server metadata, and usage records showing that the platform content was accessed and consumed by users outside Mainland China

All VAT invoices must be issued and filed through the STA's National Electronic Invoice Platform or the tax-controlled special VAT invoice system; invoice data, including buyer's USCC, item classification, amount, VAT rate, and invoice serial number, is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV central data platform. The issuance of fully digitalised electronic invoices carries automated anti-fraud digital stamping, classification-specific verification, and no blank or out-of-system invoice issuance. Taxpayers using Special VAT Invoices must complete tax-bureau approval, obtain invoice quotas, and are subject to near real-time fiscal clearance; third-party invoicing/ERP systems must use STA-certified interfaces/APIs and cannot submit commercial invoices as fiscal VAT evidence.

The STA and foreign exchange authorities will audit the chain of export trading, beneficiary identity, service consumption location, invoice flow, payment flow and actual foreign exchange repatriation. Where zero-rating or export refund is claimed without the statutory documentation, the supply is re-characterised as a domestic taxable supply, VAT and late-payment surcharge are assessed, and administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed. Fraudulent claims in the export tax refund programme expose the taxpayer to recovery of any refunded VAT, fines of one to five times the refunded amount, loss of export refund qualifications, and criminal liability for tax/fraud offences where the evasion or cheating thresholds are exceeded.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed convertible current account and a heavily regulated capital account. Current account transactions, including receipts and payments for trade in goods and services, may generally be settled through designated domestic banks without case-by-case SAFE approval, but the banking systems require underlying supporting documents and genuine transaction verification. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment capital injections, shareholder loans, external debt, repatriation of profits, and outbound royalty or intercompany payments, are subject to SAFE registration, macroprudential limits, and in some cases prior filing. The RMB is fully convertible for current account purposes but only partially convertible for capital account items. Foreign-invested enterprises must open dedicated capital accounts for inbound equity funds and are subject to the Negative List for foreign investment. Dividend and profit remittances require audited financial statements, profit distribution resolutions from the board or shareholders, and full settlement of PRC corporate income tax and withholding tax. There is no fixed cap on current account conversion, but all cross-border receipts must be repatriated to China and channeled through designated foreign exchange banks. The convertibility framework favors operating cash flows while preserving enforcement tools for balance-of-payments risks, trade-based money laundering, and sudden outflows. Treasury planning should thus treat RMB conversion onshore as reliable for genuine operating needs, while capital account movements require earlier documentation and regulatory lead time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods export receipts, the exporting WFOE must repatriate foreign exchange proceeds to China through a designated domestic bank and complete the SAFE trade verification process within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless SAFE has approved a longer settlement period based on industry practice or trade contract terms. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the current regime permits export proceeds to be retained in foreign exchange settlement accounts up to the approved current-account receivable quota. Balances exceeding the quota must be converted to RMB promptly through the designated bank. The bank will apply the SAFE goods trade 'A/B/C' classification; a standard 'A' class enterprise receives expedited processing, whereas lower classifications impose stricter surrender and document-exception timelines.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its subsidiary State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very low and generally not viable. Offshore fintech e-money institutions and digital EMI providers are not licensed banks in China and are legally barred from offering PRC currency accounts, RMB clearing, deposit-taking facilities, or foreign exchange settlement services for onshore WFOEs. Chinese designated banks are exclusively permitted to perform cross-border foreign exchange settlement and trade verification under SAFE rules. An offshore EMI can only act as an ancillary payment collector for customer subscriptions outside China, but it cannot function as the treasury clearing bank for the WFOE's mainland corporate account. Attempting to route WFOE operating receipts through an offshore EMI creates regulatory red flags and AML/CFT concerns. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on Tier-1 domestic banks with SWIFT and RMB clearing capabilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity injections must be routed through the WFOE's SAFE-registered capital account at a designated domestic bank. The overseas shareholder remits via SWIFT using the SAFE foreign exchange declaration purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contribution. The remitter must match exactly the shareholder name recorded in the SAFE foreign investment registration filing. The bank verifies the amount against the approved capital registration and then permits conversion into RMB, either through discretionary capital settlement for operating needs or through same-currency retention in the capital account. After receipt and conversion, a PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report certifying the paid-in capital. The capital account cannot be used for non-business funds, and disbursements to suppliers or operating accounts must be made with supporting contracts, invoices, and board resolutions.For operating payments to overseas content licensors and platform service providers, the WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from its basic RMB or FX settlement account, supported by the SAFE service trade and other items payment documentation, tax withholding certificates, and digital licensing agreements. For physical merchandise or print distribution of novels where goods are involved, commercial letters of credit (MT700) or documentary collections are the preferred clearing rails, with a full set of documents including commercial invoice, bill of lading, insurance certificate, and inspection certificates. For recurring subscription pass-through payments to Apple and Google, proceeds are received from the platforms under standard platform settlement, after which treasury can apply for FX conversion only for genuine operating remittances such as royalties, search engine marketing, and cloud hosting fees.Deliverable onshore CNY forward contracts (CNY/USD, CNY/EUR) with a designated bank, supported by genuine current account or capital account underlying exposure and documented liquidity needs.Onshore FX swaps (CNY/USD) involving simultaneous spot or near-leg conversion and forward reversal, subject to underlying transaction verification and SAFE-compliant bank margin arrangements.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and foreign currencies for the WFOE's external debt or long-term payable obligations where the swap notional matches the outstanding registered loan and interest payment schedule.Onshore FX options are permissible for hedging genuine exchange rate risk for approved current account transactions, but offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not legally available to mainland entities because the onshore regime requires delivered funds and underlying trade or investment support.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very low and generally not viable. Offshore fintech e-money institutions and digital EMI providers are not licensed banks in China and are legally barred from offering PRC currency accounts, RMB clearing, deposit-taking facilities, or foreign exchange settlement services for onshore WFOEs. Chinese designated banks are exclusively permitted to perform cross-border foreign exchange settlement and trade verification under SAFE rules. An offshore EMI can only act as an ancillary payment collector for customer subscriptions outside China, but it cannot function as the treasury clearing bank for the WFOE's mainland corporate account. Attempting to route WFOE operating receipts through an offshore EMI creates regulatory red flags and AML/CFT concerns. The treasury blueprint should therefore rely on Tier-1 domestic banks with SWIFT and RMB clearing capabilities.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China imposes mandatory contemporaneous Local File documentation where related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services; a Master File is compulsory when total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion; in every case involving any related-party transaction, the enterprise must file the annual related-party transaction schedules with the CIT return under SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not operate an ESR regime equivalent to UAE or BVI regulations, but Articles 46 and 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the general anti-avoidance doctrine permit the tax authority to disregard entities that lack a reasonable commercial purpose and to recharacterize arrangements designed to avoid Chinese CIT. Beneficial ownership and CIGA analysis is mandatory when invoking a tax treaty: the foreign shareholder must have real staff, premises, decision-making power, and risk-bearing capacity in its state of residence, while the core income-generating activities of the webnovel platform, including content selection, editorial curation, feed algorithm, payment management, user acquisition, and server control, must be performed in China by the WFOE, without artificial displacement to an offshore shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 48 empower the tax authority to adjust taxable income to an arm's length amount and to impose special tax adjustment interest of the central bank benchmark rate plus five percentage points without a statute-of-limitations period. Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and daily overdue interest of 0.05%; where the tax evaded is above RMB 100,000 and over 10% of tax payable, criminal liability for tax evasion attaches under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize false transfer pricing contracts, sign misleading related-party transaction schedules, or direct the diversion of Chinese revenue to offshore entities may have the corporate veil pierced under the Company Law, making them personally liable for the tax arrears and civil damages to creditors. Chinese tax and court enforcement mechanisms allow freezing of personal assets, travel-ban supervision, and blacklisting of the legal representative; conviction on tax evasion or false invoicing carries imprisonment of up to three years for ordinary evasion and three to seven years for extremely large cases.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 69 requires the withholding agent to pay the unpaid withholding tax and subjects it to a fine of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld. The non-resident beneficiary remains assessed for full PRC CIT, with late payment interest and penalties. Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 46 and 47 permit the tax authority to recharacterize the transaction as a dividend distribution and apply the full 10% dividend withholding rate, while SAT Public Notice 2011 No.24 denies treaty benefits to rent-a-custodian structures that lack beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial director who sign remittance instructions after knowing a dividend was mischaracterized are treated as responsible persons for the unpaid withholding; the corporate veil may be pierced to seize their personal bank accounts and properties. Travel bans and border-exit restrictions under the serious-tax-dishonesty joint punishment mechanism apply to responsible directors, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion or money laundering can result in imprisonment of up to seven years together with confiscation of assets derived from the unlawful distribution.
Artificial Shell Company and Abusive Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: General anti-avoidance rules in Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 46 and 47, together with the anti-treaty-shopping provisions in SAT Public Notice 2015 No.7 and Notice 2019 No.25, allow the tax authority to disregard the artificial intermediary, tax the underlying profits as domestic-source income, and impose the statutory 10% withholding on the economic dividend. The arrangement is additionally subject to a 50% to 500% under-assessment penalty under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 where false documentation accompanied the treaty claim, with unlimited look-back for special tax adjustments.

Director personal liability: If a director knowingly forms or maintains the circular shell structure, the Chinese courts may pierce the company veil and impose personal liability for corporate tax debts, unpaid withholding taxes, and restitution of diverted assets. Blacklisting triggers travel-ban enforcement, restrictions on exit from China, denial of banking facilities, and seizure of personal property. Criminal exposure includes tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 and forgery or fraud in relation to the Tax Residency Certificate, which can lead to imprisonment terms of up to seven years, or longer when combined with cross-border money laundering.
