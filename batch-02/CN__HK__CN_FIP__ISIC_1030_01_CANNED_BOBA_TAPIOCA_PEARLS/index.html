Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Hong Kong foreign-invested partnership; minimum capital requirement is false. The partnership deed may set the committed and paid-up capital agreed by the general and limited partners, and no blocked escrow account is required or mandated by Hong Kong formation law. Any escrow or margin arrangement may be imposed by a trade finance bank under private agreement to support SWIFT LC or TT payment rails, and must be documented as a contractual bank facility condition, not as a statutory incorporation requirement. All capital contributions must be evidenced by capital account statements, bank receipts and source-of-funds records acceptable for AML/KYC compliance.

Funding & unlock path: Obtain a Fire Services Installations Certificate from a registered fire service contractor and have the annual inspection accepted by the Fire Services Department under the Fire Services Ordinance; apply for a Dangerous Goods Licence under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance if flammable solvents, fuel oil, refrigerants, compressed gases or chemical sterilants are stored above exempt quantities. The Buildings Department must approve and certify floor loading and structural bearing elements for production tanks, retorts, aseptic filling machines, elevators and racking, and issue an Occupation Permit or Certificate of Compliance for any new or materially altered industrial premises. The facility must be registered as a factory under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance, with certified pressure vessels and boilers, emergency evacuation plans, fire drills, first-aid provision, machine guarding and employees' compensation insurance; Labour Department occupational safety and health audits must be passed before full production capacity is authorised.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB) of the national China Tax Service platform, operated by the STA and provincial tax services.

E-government filing portal

A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete market entity registration with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Within 30 calendar days from AMR registration, the FIP must complete tax registration via the ETB new taxpayer onboarding flow, including verifying legal representative identity, partnership deed, registered address, bank settlement account, industry ISIC/NAICS mapping, and starting tax filing obligations. In the same portal the FIP selects VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general taxpayer) and applies for the VAT invoice issuance quota, digital certificate for e-filing, and the electronic invoice platform licence. The STA then issues the tax registration record linked to the USCC. A non-resident investor without a PRC establishment does not need an additional TIN for the partnership unless it separately earns China-source income; in that case a non-resident tax registration is completed in the ETB for withholding obligations.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (the FIP is statutorily transparent for Enterprise Income Tax; partners are taxed at their respective resident/non-resident level, while the partnership itself remains a registered VAT taxpayer for goods supply, export zero-rating, and input VAT rebate claims). — A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete market entity registration with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the FIP is statutorily transparent for Enterprise Income Tax; partners are taxed at their respective resident/non-resident level, while the partnership itself remains a registered VAT taxpayer for goods supply, export zero-rating, and input VAT rebate claims).

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under PRC tax law. Chinese law conclusively classifies a partnership as pass-through; the only elective decision is the VAT taxpayer status: a newly formed FIP may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer even if annual turnover is below the RMB 5 million threshold. No anti-hybrid election is available; anti-avoidance and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) provisions apply to artificial arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (approximately USD 700,000) is the compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold; below this, the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer at the 3% levy rate, subject to applicable exemption policies, unless it elects general status. All entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of tangible goods from the PRC customs territory, including canned boba tapioca pearls produced by the FIP, qualify for 0% VAT where the goods are physically exported to a foreign country or to Hong Kong SAR/Macau/Taiwan (each outside the PRC customs territory), the exporter is a PRC general VAT taxpayer, the sale is made to a non-PRC buyer, the customs declaration is settled by GACC, and the export proceeds are repatriated into China through a licensed bank within the statutory period. In such cases, input VAT on purchased materials and services is recoverable under the export-refund mechanism. Additional GACC food-export registration and health certificates are required for canned food export, but they do not replace the tax evidence set.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) released by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) with the export customs registration number
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international railway consignment note proving physical departure from a PRC customs port
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract/purchase order, packing list, and declared customs value statement
  • Bank-endorsed Export Proceeds Collection Certificate or Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)/electronic bank settlement record evidencing receipt and repatriation of the export sale proceeds in foreign currency
  • Electronic Export VAT Rebate Declaration submitted through the STA export-rebate module in the ETB system

All special VAT invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices must be issued, authenticated, and transmitted in near real time to the STA through the National Electronic Invoice Platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. The invoice file carries a cryptographic fiscal signature/tax-control stamp issued by the STA inventory system; no unsigned or external invoice is valid. Post-issuance modification is prohibited; corrections require a red-letter invoice in the same platform with reason code. For exports, the invoice, customs declaration, and foreign exchange collection data are linked between the STA and General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) for automated verification.

The STA and GACC jointly cross-check customs export data, invoice data, logistics documents, and foreign exchange repatriation records. If export zero-rating is claimed without the mandatory documentation bundle, or if the underlying documents are found false or inconsistent, the tax authority will claw back the VAT refund with statutory late-payment interest and impose a penalty ranging from 0.5 to 5 times the tax amount involved. Serious or fraudulent cases also carry criminal liability for export tax fraud under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, including confiscation of assets, fines, and imprisonment risk. The FIP may be additionally placed on the tax high-risk list, causing suspension of VAT export rebates, mandatory advance tax verification, intensified customs inspections, and disqualification from simplified export-refund procedures for a period determined by the STA.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime administered by PBOC and SAFE. Current-account payments are generally freely convertible after verification, including trade-related proceeds, while capital-account transactions remain subject to pre-approval, registration, quota, or authenticity checks. A mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership is allowed to open a foreign-currency capital account for FDI equity injection and a RMB/CNY basic settlement account for operating flows. The RMB is conditionally convertible on current account and prudently controlled on capital account. Profit remittance is lawful but not automatic: the FIP must first complete annual audit, pay all applicable taxes and withholding, submit tax payment certificates and audited financial statements, and then register or provide SAFE-related documents with the designated bank. The bank performs business authenticity review and files the outflow with SAFE. There is no blanket prohibition on remitting profits, but banks must verify trade/investment background, source-of-funds, and beneficial ownership before allowing payment. Surrender of export receipts to RMB is voluntary at market exchange rates, and FX sale/remittance will be constrained by the entity's compliance status and SAFE monitoring lists.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange account in China within the statutory trade-receipt timeline, generally 90 days after export customs declaration unless an extended deferred-payment schedule has been registered with SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%, meaning there is no compulsory surrender; the FIP may choose to retain foreign currency in its designated account or voluntarily settle it into RMB at the prevailing spot rate through a licensed designated foreign-exchange bank. Every receipt must be reported to SAFE through the bank using the applicable trade-in-goods collection code and matched to customs declarations, invoices, and bills of lading.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a substitute for bank accounts. In mainland China, fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks are not licensed by SAFE as designated foreign-exchange banks and cannot lawfully open an onshore capital or operating account for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. They may not handle FDI capital injection, profit remittance, or export surrender without intermediation through a SAFE-designated commercial bank. Some licensed Chinese third-party payment institutions can process small-value current-account payments, but only as payment facilitators forwarding funds to a regulated bank account; they do not provide the required FX clearing, capital verification, or SAFE reporting rails. Therefore, the FIP must use a Tier-1 domestic bank for all cross-border liquidity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Corporate Income Tax rate applied to the resident corporate partners to whom the fiscally transparent Foreign-Invested Partnership allocates taxable profit; no entity-level income tax is imposed on the FIP itself)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC withholding tax on China-source profit remittances and passive income allocable to a non-resident corporate partner; the FIP itself does not declare dividend tax, but the partner-level remittance bears the 10% final withholding unless a treaty claim is accepted.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for related-party financial transactions and intangible asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; RMB 1 billion aggregate related-party transactions requiring a Master File under STA Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese FIP must maintain real economic substance in mainland China by performing its core income-generating activities (CIGA) for manufacture and aseptic tubing: physical plant, food-safety licences, production operators, quality-control personnel, inventory risk, and management of the supply chain. If the Hong Kong entity is a shell or is merely an e-commerce invoice hub, the tax authority will deny the DTA benefits and apply the GAAR/special anti-avoidance rules to reallocate the full manufacturing margin to the PRC FIP. If the Hong Kong entity claims certain relevant activities, Hong Kong's own substance requirements may also apply, requiring an economic substance test and local CIGA presence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealment of Manufacturing Revenue

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a 50%-500% penalty on the evaded tax plus 0.05% daily late-payment interest; Criminal Law Article 201 criminalises evasion above RMB 50,000 and applies imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: The managing general partner and its directors may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and interest where partnership assets have been distributed before settlement; courts will pierce the partnership veil, freeze personal bank accounts, impose exit bans / blacklisting against the responsible officers, and impose criminal sentences on the actual management.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation Through Hong Kong Sales Hub

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will make a transfer pricing adjustment under the arm's length principle, levy a 50%-500% evasion penalty where negligence or wilfulness is shown, impose special adjustment interest at the benchmark one-year RMB lending rate plus 5 percentage points, and require a revised annual CIT return.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the below-market contracts or approved the sham margin split face personal liability for the adjusted tax, interest and penalties; asset restitution of shifted profits, joint liability of the Hong Kong shareholder under veil-piercing theory, and travel restrictions under the PRC enforcement blacklisting system may be ordered, with criminal prosecution for tax evasion where false transfer-pricing documentation is used.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and Shell Company Exploitation of Hong Kong DTA

Statutory sanction: The tax authority denies treaty benefits under the principal-purpose test and restates the statutory 10% withholding on all profit distributions, interest, and royalties; the deficiency is collected with daily surcharge, a 50%-500% fraud penalty is imposed for intentionally false declarations, and false TRC filings expose the taxpayer to criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Corporate-veil piercing extends to the Hong Kong shell shareholders and de facto controllers; directors are civilly liable for the full treaty-benefit shortfall, restitution, and penalty amounts; they may be blacklisted and prevented from leaving mainland China while the case is under enforcement; the managing director of the FIP and the Hong Kelly nominee directors face imprisonment up to 7 years for aiding criminal tax evasion.
