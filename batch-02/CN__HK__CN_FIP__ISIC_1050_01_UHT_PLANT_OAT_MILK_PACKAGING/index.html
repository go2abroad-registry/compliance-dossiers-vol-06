Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD), including its Business Registration Office; electronic filing and registration are accessed through the GovHK electronic portal and the IRD eTAX system. Cross-border trade declarations are administered by the Customs and Excise Department (C&ED) and the Census and Statistics Department through the Government Electronic Trading Services (GETS).

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a conventional Tax Identification Number for partnerships. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) structured with partnership-limited liability must first be registered with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) if it elects limited partnership status; general partnerships are governed by the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38). Within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, the FIP must lodge Form IRBR 1 (Application for Business Registration) with the IRD Business Registration Office via eTAX/GovHK, attaching the executed partnership agreement, evidence of overseas registration of any non-resident corporate partner, identity documents for individual partners, the Hong Kong registered address, and proof of appointment of an authorized Hong Kong resident representative or tax agent where the partnership is non-resident. The IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) containing a Business Registration Number (BRN) in the standard 8-digit format plus check digit. The BRN is the operative taxpayer reference for Profits Tax returns, employer returns, and correspondence with the IRD. There is no minimum capital requirement, and the partnership itself is not chargeable to Profits Tax; each partner receives their own tax file reference in their personal or corporate capacity for the assessment of partnership-source Hong Kong profits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box regime, deemed entity classification election, or hybrid mismatch election for partnerships. The FIP is mandatorily fiscally transparent: the partnership is not a separate assessable person for Profits Tax; income is allocated to partners in accordance with the partnership agreement and assessed on each partner under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. The only practical elections available are administrative in nature, such as the choice of accounting date subject to IRD approval and the allocation of profits among partners for tax accounting purposes under Part IV of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax. Hong Kong applies only a narrow excise regime under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, which does not apply to UHT oat milk products.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no broad-based indirect tax exists. Any person, including a Foreign-Invested Partnership, carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) from the date business commences, regardless of turnover level or profitability.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT or GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no input VAT refund mechanism to substantiate. Exports of aseptic UHT oat milk packaging from Hong Kong are outside the scope of Hong Kong domestic indirect taxation; no VAT is chargeable on the export, and no VAT is recovered by the exporter. The relevant mandatory obligations are statistical and customs declaration requirements rather than VAT zero-rating conditions. Furthermore, Hong Kong has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline for export proceeds because the territory has no exchange controls; if a 180-day benchmark is adopted for documentary discipline, it is a financing/banking convention, not a statutory tax requirement.

No real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, SAF-T reporting, or VAT e-invoicing mandate exists in Hong Kong. There is no tax-authority API for invoice stamping or anti-fraud digital stamping for general commercial invoices. For export cargo, the mandatory electronic declaration channel is GETS, used to lodge the statutory Export Declaration with the Census and Statistics Department and C&ED under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60A). Businesses must retain books and records under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and Cap. 310 for at least seven years.

Because Hong Kong has no VAT zero-rating regime, there is no VAT clawback or VAT refund recovery mechanism. However, a knowingly false or fraudulent export declaration is a criminal offence under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60A), punishable on conviction by fines and imprisonment, and the C&ED may detain and forfeit the goods. For Profits Tax, if a Foreign-Invested Partnership files an income allocation or additional tax return that is incorrect without reasonable excuse, or omits assessable partnership income, the IRD may raise additional assessments within six years of the end of the relevant assessment year, or within ten years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion, and may impose additional tax under Section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance of up to three times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also cross-match export declarations, bank remittance records, and invoices through its information-gathering powers when reviewing claims for offshore profit treatment or partnership loss allocations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system under which RMB is fully convertible for genuine current-account transactions such as trade settlement and profit remittance, but capital-account transactions remain strictly controlled. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is resident for exchange-control purposes and must use a SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account for inbound equity capital. Registered capital can be converted into RMB for permitted operational use, but each conversion must be tied to an actual expenditure and supported by an underlying invoice or contract. Profit distributions to the non-resident partners are remitted offshore only after the annual audit, tax clearance, a signed general-partner distribution resolution, and a CPAs verification that the profit is real and taxed. There is no blanket convertibility right: the bank will reject any remittance that lacks a traceable real economic substance. The Hong Kong node is unencumbered by exchange controls, allowing free conversion between RMB offshore (CNH), HKD and USD, but movement of funds into mainland China must still inbound through the FIP capital account and respect the differentiated current-account and capital-account treatment. Overall, the structure is viable with disciplined SAFE filings, but treasury policy must avoid discretionary capital arbitrage that could trigger anti-round-tripping inquiries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a PRC-onshore FIP exporting aseptic oat milk products, export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to the designated foreign-exchange account in China within the statutory monitoring period, normally 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. There is no mandatory percentage conversion to RMB; the FIP may retain all proceeds in foreign currency. The bank must verify the settlement against the SAFE monitoring system and customs declaration data, and the FIP must fully reconcile the invoiced equivalent, while any processing trade or bonded-zone-related export flow is subject to the aggregate ratio requirements of the customs processing manual.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the Hong Kong treasury clearing node.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low for the PRC-onshore FIP. Under the People's Bank of China and SAFE legal framework, non-bank payment institutions and foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed to open current accounts, accept deposits, or maintain business accounts in mainland China. Platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or XTransfer may be used by the Hong Kong treasury vehicle as payment facilitators for receivables and small-amount invoicing, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing rail or as a repository for export proceeds. Even where an EMI holds a Hong Kong Money Service Operator license or an SVF license, funds crossing into mainland China must still be routed through a licensed domestic commercial bank and subject to SAFE trade verification. Consequently, the FIP must open at least one Tier-1 Chinese bank account before any EMI API connectivity is considered, and the EMI should be limited to the HK side for collection of overseas receivables and payment of overseas suppliers when the corresponding trade documentation is preserved.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection flows are executed via SWIFT from a licensed Hong Kong bank to the FIP's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account. The remittance instruction must cite the SAFE FDI Registration Form number, the entity's eight-digit capital account notification code, and must use the balance-of-payments purpose code applicable to FDI equity injection, commonly 621 under the SAFE transaction code manual. The remitter must be the actual foreign partner listed in the partnership agreement, and the overseas remitting bank must be a bona fide licensed financial institution. Upon receipt, funds are lodged in USD or HKD and later converted into RMB for allowed uses such as the acquisition of UHT packaging machinery, raw materials, and operational expenditure; the settlement instruction must reference the corresponding payment purpose and invoice. Within 60 days, a PRC certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report confirming that the registered capital has been paid in cash or in kind; SAFE may periodically verify and freeze unsupported balances.Outbound trade payments from the FIP to overseas suppliers and service providers will be settled primarily through SWIFT MT103 messages for telegraphic transfer disbursements and through documentary letter of credit instruments for large UHT packaging and oat milk production equipment transactions. An irrevocable at sight commercial LC is the preferred rail for bulk raw-material imports, while documentary collections, such as documents against payment or documents against acceptance, can be used for low-value repetitive trade. The bank will require a signed commercial invoice, the bill of lading or air waybill, the customs declaration, and the corresponding contract in order to disburse the foreign exchange; for advance payments, the bank may cap the advance at a percentage of the total invoice and require a bank guarantee. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low for the PRC-onshore FIP. Under the People's Bank of China and SAFE legal framework, non-bank payment institutions and foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed to open current accounts, accept deposits, or maintain business accounts in mainland China. Platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or XTransfer may be used by the Hong Kong treasury vehicle as payment facilitators for receivables and small-amount invoicing, but they cannot serve as the primary treasury clearing rail or as a repository for export proceeds. Even where an EMI holds a Hong Kong Money Service Operator license or an SVF license, funds crossing into mainland China must still be routed through a licensed domestic commercial bank and subject to SAFE trade verification. Consequently, the FIP must open at least one Tier-1 Chinese bank account before any EMI API connectivity is considered, and the EMI should be limited to the HK side for collection of overseas receivables and payment of overseas suppliers when the corresponding trade documentation is preserved.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) for corporate partners on their allocable share; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) itself is tax-transparent with no entity-level EIT

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% for dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; 5% under the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement when the beneficial owner is an HK resident company that has directly held at least 25% of the paying company's capital for 365 days or more; 10% for all other HK shareholders

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if annual related-party transactions reach any of: tangible-property transfers CNY 200 million, intangible-property transfers CNY 100 million, financial-asset transfers CNY 100 million, or other related-party transactions CNY 40 million; Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed CNY 1 billion. Same thresholds apply in CNY.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form, beneficial ownership tests, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) to deny treaty benefits for conduit HK structures; the HK partner must maintain real business operations in Hong Kong or risk being treated as a shell under the Mainland's anti-treaty-abuse doctrine. If the HK entity also claims Hong Kong's Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE), it must satisfy economic substance requirements in Hong Kong for passive income by having sufficient employees, premises, and management activities in HK. The FIP plant must own the core income-generating assets (UHT lines, inventory, formulas, supply contracts) and bear principal commercial risks; the HK partner must perform core income-generating activities only for any passive income it claims to earn, and all manufacturing, sales negotiation, storage, order fulfillment and invoice issuance for the Chinese plant must occur in mainland China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion) and Art. 205 (false issuance of special VAT invoices); Tax Collection and Administration Law Art. 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; overdue tax interest up to 0.05% per day.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the FIP's limited-liability veil if the structure is used to evade tax or defraud creditors; directors and general partners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and fines; criminal conviction carries up to 7 years' imprisonment for Art. 201 and up to life imprisonment for severe Art. 205 false-invoice fraud; the individual may also be subject to departure restrictions (blacklisting) and asset freezing.
Conduit shell entity and DTA abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the China-HK DTA anti-abuse provisions and PRC GAAR (EIT Law Art. 120 and implementation rules), treaty benefits are denied; the entire payment is re-taxed at 25% (if recharacterized as PE business profit) or 10% statutory WHT, plus interest and a 50%-300% underpayment penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors of the HK conduit can be held personally liable for the evaded Chinese tax under the piercing analysis if the shell was used to perpetrate fraud; travel bans, blacklisting by tax authorities, and confiscation of repatriated funds are available; criminal false-declaration charges under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 may attach to the signatories of the treaty-benefit forms if they knowingly made false beneficial-ownership statements.
AML and beneficial ownership non-disclosure

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law Art. 33 imposes fines from CNY 200,000 to CNY 2,000,000 on the institution, with direct fines of CNY 20,000 to CNY 200,000 on responsible officers; repeat violations or serious non-compliance can trigger suspension of business licence and criminal liability under Art. 191 for money laundering (up to 10 years' imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally liable for AML failures, including joint and several compensation for losses; courts may order the freezing and confiscation of partnership assets; individuals involved in laundering proceeds of tax crimes face up to 10 years' imprisonment, and senior managers may be subject to an entry/exit ban and permanent disqualification from serving as directors in any PRC financial or commercial entity.
