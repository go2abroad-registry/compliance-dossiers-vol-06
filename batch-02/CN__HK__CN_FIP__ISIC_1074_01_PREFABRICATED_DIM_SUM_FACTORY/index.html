Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic tax registration and filing portal: the e-Tax Service Platform of the STA (etax.chinatax.gov.cn), plus the local STA electronic tax bureau used by the Foreign-Invested Partnership. The Hong Kong parent is administered separately by the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong SAR for any Hong Kong profits tax and business registration obligations.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: the Hong Kong investing entity must maintain a valid Business Registration Certificate (BRC) and Business Registration Number issued by the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Step 2: execute the partnership agreement for the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) and submit the formation application to the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Step 3: after approval, SAMR issues the Foreign-Invested Partnership Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification code for the PRC entity. Step 4: the USCC is automatically synchronized to the State Taxation Administration under the one-license-one-code integrated registration policy; no separate PRC TIN application is required. Step 5: the FIP legal representative and tax personnel must authenticate on the STA e-Tax Service Platform, complete the initial tax registration file, confirm applicable tax types and tax status, and apply for VAT invoice quotas. Step 6: if the FIP intends to export goods, it must register with the competent tax authority for the export tax rebate (zero-rate) regime. Step 7: a Hong Kong non-resident investor without a PRC establishment does not obtain a separate PRC TIN for the partnership; any PRC withholding tax on its partnership income is administered through the FIP tax file.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
No US-style check-the-box election exists under PRC law. Available elections include the voluntary election to register as a general VAT taxpayer before the turnover threshold is exceeded; the FIP itself is the VAT taxpayer while remaining pass-through for enterprise income tax purposes, with each partner assessed on its partnership share. Hybrid mismatch rules in the PRC Enterprise Income Tax framework do not create an entity classification election; they operate as anti-deferral adjustments. The Hong Kong parent may rely on the Mainland and Hong Kong Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation for the attribution and allocation of partnership profits.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
All PRC business entities are automatically registered for VAT upon issuance of the USCC. The mandatory statutory threshold for general VAT taxpayer classification is annual taxable turnover exceeding CNY 5,000,000. A Foreign-Invested Partnership with annual turnover at or below CNY 5,000,000 may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general VAT taxpayer status.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Exports of industrial quick-frozen steamed dim sum and dumplings from the PRC FIP qualify for the VAT zero-rate and export tax rebate regime only if: (a) the FIP is registered as a general VAT taxpayer; (b) the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory under a valid Customs export declaration and shipped to an overseas buyer or to Hong Kong or Macau for legitimate trade; (c) the FIP has borne input VAT on purchases attributable to the exported goods and submits an export tax rebate application through the STA export tax refund management system; (d) the sale is a genuine commercial transaction with title transferred to the overseas buyer; (e) export proceeds are repatriated through PRC designated foreign-exchange banks or otherwise verified under SAFE trade-in-goods regulations; and (f) all transport, customs, invoicing, and foreign-exchange records are retained for audit. Qualifying cross-border digital services are outside this product scope; this classification covers goods only.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible in Hong Kong, but the substantive treasury bottleneck for the FIP is mainland RMB convertibility. China operates a managed floating RMB regime under which the current account is convertible for bona fide trade in goods and services, while capital-account transactions remain controlled. A foreign-invested partnership established in mainland China may receive the Hong Kong partner's capital contribution only after completing the SAFE foreign direct investment filing or registration through the receiving bank or local SAFE. Once registered, the FIP can open a foreign exchange capital settlement account and convert the incoming funds into RMB for permitted working capital and investment purposes, subject to the negative-list restrictions on sectors that may not receive foreign investment. Export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China and brought into the electronic export verification system; banks verify each inflow against customs declarations, commercial invoices, and logistics documents. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is not a routine payment: the FIP must produce a statutory audit report, prove that the distribution is sourced from after-tax accumulated profits, submit the signed partnership distribution resolution, obtain tax clearance or payment receipts, and present the SAFE registration document; any inconsistency between the remitted amount and the registered partnership capital contribution table will cause the bank to reject the payment. In short, the RMB is convertible on the current account but every capital-item movement requires registration, supporting evidence, and beneficial-owner screening.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE export-proceeds collection rules, export receipts must be repatriated into the onshore banking system no later than 180 days after the date of customs declaration unless an extension is approved for documented long-term payment terms. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for non-financial exporters under the willing-settlement regime: the FIP may retain 100% of collected foreign currency in an Export Proceeds Retention Account or voluntarily convert receipts into RMB at the market exchange rate through its settlement bank. Every receipt must be matched to the Electronic Port Verification and Cross-border Receipts and Payments system, and the underlying trade purpose code must align with the frozen dim sum factory's export invoices and customs declarations.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acting as the parallel offshore banking regulator for the Hong Kong remitting entity

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Cross-border digital EMI rails are not a substitute for SAFE-regulated onshore banking in mainland China. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Airwallex or Currenxie are useful only for offshore collection and treasury outside China; they are not licensed banks in China and are barred from opening the statutory capital escrow account, performing FDI capital verification, or providing capital-account FX conversion. Licensed Chinese non-bank payment institutions can collect domestic e-commerce merchant funds but cannot issue corporate settlement accounts or act as the FIP's SAFE counterparty. Overall viability is LOW for the FIP's primary treasury and capital account, and MEDIUM only for ancillary overseas collection of export receivables before those funds are repatriated to the onshore settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The HK partner must remit from its own bank account to the FIP's onshore foreign exchange capital settlement account at a Tier-1 mainland bank using a SWIFT MT103. The remitter and beneficiary names, the amount, and the payment reference must match the SAFE foreign direct investment registration and the partnership agreement. The sending bank uses the inward direct investment capital injection category as the FX transaction purpose code; the exact SAFE numeric code is confirmed with the receiving bank and linked to the FDI registration certificate and the capital contribution schedule. Upon receipt, the receiving bank or a certified public accountant performs capital verification against the registered schedule and issues an FDI capital injection confirmation or capital verification report. The foreign currency cannot be spent directly on domestic operating costs until it is converted into RMB under the capital settlement negative list; conversion must be reported to SAFE by the receiving bank within the required reporting window.For operating and trade payments, the FIP should use SWIFT MT103 for TT wire disbursements, Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 for large perishable and commodity shipments, and Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) only for established, credit-rated counterparties under signed trade contracts. Each outbound payment must be supported by a trade contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration or import/export evidence, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, and applicable health or cold-chain certificate for frozen dim sum ingredients and packaging. The settlement bank will match the payment to the SAFE current-account verification system and will reject any payment whose purpose code, goods description, or recipient identity is inconsistent with the FIP's registered business scope.Onshore plain-vanilla deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts supported by genuine current-account trade exposure and full SAFE real-demand documentationOnshore RMB/HKD forward contracts for hedging Hong Kong dollar-denominated capital and trade cash flowsOnshore FX swaps between spot and forward for short-term working capital liquidity caused by trade timing mismatchesCross-currency swaps for medium-term matching of RMB operating income against foreign currency debt or trade finance, subject to bank credit approval and SAFE registrationFX options, including participating forwards and risk reversals, for hedging export receivables from frozen dim sum buyers, with no speculative laddered positionsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the onshore FIP; all hedging must be executed onshore with deliverable instruments and an underlying transaction
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border digital EMI rails are not a substitute for SAFE-regulated onshore banking in mainland China. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Airwallex or Currenxie are useful only for offshore collection and treasury outside China; they are not licensed banks in China and are barred from opening the statutory capital escrow account, performing FDI capital verification, or providing capital-account FX conversion. Licensed Chinese non-bank payment institutions can collect domestic e-commerce merchant funds but cannot issue corporate settlement accounts or act as the FIP's SAFE counterparty. Overall viability is LOW for the FIP's primary treasury and capital account, and MEDIUM only for ancillary overseas collection of export receivables before those funds are repatriated to the onshore settlement bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: HK$10,000,000 (per related-party transaction category) for mandatory Local File; HK$100,000,000 in related-party revenue for Master File; HK$5,000,000 for certain cross-border inter-company services (thresholds under RDTO, TP (D) Regulations and IRD Practice Note DIPN 58, 59, 60).

      
Economic substance scope: Applicability of Economic Substance Regulations (ESR): Hong Kong does not have an ESR regime equivalent to Caribbean jurisdictions; however, under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the 'operation test' / territorial source concept, an FIP must demonstrate adequate economic substance in Hong Kong (substance, premises, employees, management decisions and expenditure) to support the claim that profits are not sourced from Hong Kong; the two-tiered profits tax regime requires actual business operations in Hong Kong; unrelated shell FIPs with no Hong Kong substance face challenged claims to non-taxable offshore profits and can be assessed to full Hong Kong profits tax; also, the IRD’s transfer pricing rules require the entity to maintain sufficient functional and risk analysis and documentation (DIPN 58-60).

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / Incorrect Tax Returns
        
Statutory sanction: Under Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82A: upon conviction, a fine of HK$100,000 plus three times the amount of tax evaded or undercharged; imprisonment for 3 years; where tax is undercharged and there is no prosecution, penalties up to three times the omitted tax; default surcharge up to 10% of tax due.

        
Director personal liability: Individual partners and general partners (or directors of a corporate general partner) can be criminally prosecuted; the court may order civil recovery of unpaid tax and penalties; travel restrictions and exit bans may be imposed by the Commissary or Immigration Department pending determination; the Inland Revenue Department can bring proceedings against the individual for penalties personally; corporate veil pierced where the partnership is a facade or used to evade tax; imprisonment for up to 3 years; personal liability for tax, penalties, and legal costs.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure / Thin Capitalization
        
Statutory sanction: Under s.50A and s.51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance and Transfer Pricing (D) Rules: penalty of HK$50,000 plus three times the undercharged tax; the IRD may make transfer pricing adjustments and deem additional assessable profits; for failure to keep records, penalty up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months.

        
Director personal liability: General partners and managing partners who knowingly participated in transfer pricing misstatement can be held liable; civil claims for the amount of underpaid tax; the company general partner's directors may be personally liable for penalties if they actively and knowingly cause the FIP to fail; travel bans may be imposed, but imprisonment is generally limited to cases of dishonesty or tax evasion.

      

      
      

        Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance
        
Statutory sanction: Reclassification of the FIP as a resident in Hong Kong due to central management and control (if actual management is in Hong Kong) and assessment of full profits tax; denial of offshore claims; no special ESR penalties but potential IRD default assessments and penalties up to three times tax undercharged; refusal of the two-tiered profits tax concession if not satisfying the 'specified recipient' test.

        
Director personal liability: Where acts are done through the corporate general partner with no substance, courts may impose liability on its directors for conspiracy to defraud the IRD; personal liability for fines; the corporate veil can be pierced for a sham partnership; immigration restrictions exercised; criminal prosecution risk for fraud.

      

      
      

        AML / Money Laundering and Tax-Related Financial Crimes
        
Statutory sanction: Under Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO) s.25: money laundering offence punishable with fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years; Customs and Excise Duty Ordinance penalties for false declarations/importer-exporter offences: fine up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment for 2 years; failure to comply with AML/CFT requirements under Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO): fine up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for 2 years per offence.

        
Director personal liability: Partners and the management of the FIP may be prosecuted for money laundering; personal confiscation orders can seize proceeds of crime; travel restrictions and denial of bail; unlimited personal monetary penalties; for foreign-invested partners, extradition risk to Hong Kong possible; immigration travel bans are commonly imposed while investigations are pending; imprisonment for up to 14 years.
