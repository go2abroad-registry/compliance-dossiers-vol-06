Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax). The Hong Kong SAR Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the separate authority for any HK-side investor/offshore-profit elements.

E-government filing portal

1) Incorporate or register the foreign-invested partnership (FIP) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) local office and obtain the Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory tax identification number. 2) File the tax registration record-filing with the local STA branch through e-Tax within 30 days of the Business License date. 3) The partnership must elect or be assigned taxpayer status; register for Value Added Tax (VAT), Enterprise Income Tax pass-through reporting, stamp duty, city maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, and payroll/withholding obligations. 4) For export activities, complete China Customs registration, obtain the Customs Registration Code, and activate the export VAT rebate profile. 5) If the partnership has no registered address in mainland China, the local STA may require a Chinese tax resident representative or service agent; the foreign partners are not separately required to obtain a second VAT ID for partnership activities. 6) For the Hong Kong resident partner/investor, register with the IRD where applicable for profits tax and employer obligations, and obtain a Business Registration Certificate if conducted through an HK branch.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer in China. All taxable establishments must apply for tax registration and obtain the USCC/TIN regardless of turnover. A FIP may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer below the threshold to recover input VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% export VAT rate applies where the FIP is a VAT general taxpayer, the instant rice noodles are physically exported from the China customs territory under an export customs declaration, the exporter is the legal seller of record, the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, the export proceeds are collected through designated foreign exchange channels, and all supporting export and foreign-exchange settlement documents are retained. Qualifying offshore digital services are not the applicable category for this manufacturing scope; the statutory zero-rating mechanism for goods is the export VAT rebate (export tax refund).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry/Export Goods Declaration) with customs release verification
  • Clean bill of lading, multimodal transport document, or air waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / foreign exchange receipt confirmation from a PRC designated bank showing export proceeds settlement
  • Commercial invoice issued by the FIP to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list with marks, numbers, package weights, and HS 1902/1079 product description
  • Export sales contract or purchase order stating agreed Incoterms, price, and delivery terms
  • VAT special invoice for domestic input purchases of raw materials and processing services used in the exported goods if the rebate is claimed on input VAT
  • Export goods list or customs pre-clearance record if the goods move through a special customs supervision zone

China operates a fully digitalized electronic invoice (FD-E-invoice) regime through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Taxpayers must transmit invoice data in near-real time via the e-Tax portal or system-to-system APIs; the STA uses the Golden Tax Phase IV system to clear, validate, assign unique invoice numbers and QR codes, and enforce anti-fraud digital stamping. Physical tax-control equipment is being replaced by the fully digitalized electronic invoice, and no manual invoice alterations are permitted after fiscal clearance. Hong Kong has no e-invoicing VAT clearance requirement.

Export VAT refund/zero-rating is cross-validated by the STA, China Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through electronic customs data, logistics trajectory, bank FX receipts, and the digital VAT invoice chain. If the export is not substantiated or the required foreign exchange receipt is not obtained within 180 days, the zero rating is revoked, output VAT is recharged at the standard rate, the refund is clawed back with interest and a daily late-payment surcharge (0.05% per day), and administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax involved may be imposed under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where fraudulent export documents or fictitious cross-border trades are found, the matter is referred for penalties under the Customs Law and the PRC Criminal Law, including pecuniary punishment and imprisonment for the persons responsible.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a foreign exchange control-free zone: the HKD is fully convertible, and there are no capital account restrictions, surrender obligations, or profit remittance approvals imposed by the HKMA. However, because this Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) carries a Mainland China nexus through ISIC 1079 packaged-food manufacturing, the treasury plan must reconcile the fully convertible Hong Kong engine with the administered RMB foreign exchange system. On the mainland, current account items such as trade settlements for instant rice noodle and retort river snail noodle exports are conditionally convertible after SAFE-verified documentation, while capital account transactions require SAFE registration, filing, or approval depending on the payment nature. Profit distributions or partnership income remitted from mainland China to Hong Kong must be behind audited financial statements, tax payment vouchers, and an SAFE-approved foreign exchange settlement transaction, and the underlying funds must be booked to a foreign currency account with a licensed commercial bank. In Hong Kong, partnership distributions have no convertibility blockage and may flow out freely. Hence the prudent structure is to keep RMB receivables in mainland operating accounts, use Hong Kong as the hard-currency warehousing and clearing hub, and avoid commingling current account receipts with capital account settlement proceeds. The entity should maintain separate bank relationships for onshore RMB operating liquidity and offshore HKD/USD treasury clearing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China statutory regime: export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account maintained with a licensed bank within 180 days from the standard export declaration verification period unless SAFE approves an extended term under the trade-payment administration mechanism; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, and the exporter may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign currency or settle into RMB at market spot rates after passing real trade background verification. Hong Kong SAR regime: no surrender rule exists; all export proceeds may be retained, converted, or remitted freely in any currency.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for onshore RMB foreign exchange control; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the central banking institution and foreign exchange clearing supervisor for Hong Kong SAR cross-border flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to not viable as the primary treasury rail. Hong Kong-licensed digital banks and stored value facilities may technically onboard the Hong Kong entity, but they are not authorized to substitute for a fully licensed commercial bank in trade finance instruments such as commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, SAFE-related foreign exchange registration, or RMB cross-border clearing through CIPS. In Mainland China, pure fintech EMIs are legally barred from acting as corporate foreign exchange settlement institutions or cross-border payment clearing banks; only PBOC/SAFE-regulated commercial banks and licensed clearing institutions may perform the necessary FX, capital verification, and cross-border trade settlement roles. Therefore Tier-1 commercial banks must be used as the front-line clearing counterparty; fintech platforms may only be considered for ancillary expense payments, never for capital injections or export proceeds.

In Mainland China, pure fintech EMIs are legally barred from acting as corporate foreign exchange settlement institutions or cross-border payment clearing banks; only PBOC/SAFE-regulated commercial banks and licensed clearing institutions may perform the necessary FX, capital verification, and cross-border trade settlement roles. Therefore Tier-1 commercial banks must be used as the front-line clearing counterparty; fintech platforms may only be considered for ancillary expense payments, never for capital injections or export proceeds.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax (CIT); the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is a pass-through entity and is not itself a CIT taxpayer, so the 25% headline rate is applied on the taxable corporate partner's distributive share

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory domestic dividend WHT rate is 10% on China-source dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise; under the China-Hong Kong DTA the rate may be reduced to 5% only if the beneficial owner is the Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the paying Chinese company's capital and has sufficient substantive business in Hong Kong. For the FIP, periodic profit distributions are not legally 'dividends' because the FIP is a partnership; therefore the distribution is re-characterized as partner distributive income or PE business profit and must be analyzed under the business-profits and PE articles before any WHT reduction is claimed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: RMB 100 million for annual related-party tangible-property transfers; RMB 10 million for intangible-property transfers; RMB 10 million for financial-asset transfers; RMB 40 million for related-party services. Master File threshold: annual aggregate related-party transactions of RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report threshold: group consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion. Even below these thresholds, the FIP and its partners must file the Annual Related Party Transaction Report and maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing records if structured cross-border transactions involve the Hong Kong partner.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong ESR/CIGA and mainland PE substance principles apply concurrently. The HK partner must demonstrate genuine core income-generating activities in Hong Kong, such as strategic IP ownership, financing decisions, and commercial negotiation, to claim treaty benefits. The mainland FIP must demonstrate that the core rice-noodle manufacturing activities, including raw-material procurement, retort cooking, sterilization, quality control, packaging, warehousing, branding decisions, and logistics, are actually performed in mainland China by real personnel and assets. A letterbox HK partner or a mainland FIP that is a mere invoice mill, without employees and operating assets, will be recharacterized as a shell, the apparent corporate separation will be ignored, the HK partner may be deemed to have a mainland PE, and the entire mainland manufacturing profit will be taxed at 25% with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Hidden Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3 and 38; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of the full unpaid tax, daily late-payment interest of 0.05% per day, and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; where a false withholding certificate or forged bookkeeping record is used, the criminal threshold under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 is crossed.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is achieved through joint-tort liability under PRC Civil Code Article 1168; directors who knowingly caused the omission are jointly and severally liable for restitution of diverted profits, unpaid taxes, and penalties. The directors may be placed on the SAT serious-tax-violation blacklist, their passports may be restricted under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and imprisonment of up to 7 years can be imposed for aggravated tax evasion, together with civil asset tracing and forfeiture of personal property acquired through the evasion.
Transfer Pricing Fraud and Profit-Shifting Through Abusive Royalties

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 requires arm's-length pricing; Article 48 imposes additional annual special-tax-adjustment interest starting from the year the adjustment is assessed; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 adds a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42 imposes documentation penalties for failure to maintain contemporaneous records. The excessive royalties are disallowed, the profit is re-attributed to the manufacturing FIP, and the HK recipient's treaty protection is disregarded.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the re-assessed profit if they signed false intercompany agreements or directed inflated invoices; the corporate veil is pierced and personal assets may be seized to satisfy the tax debt. Repeated use of fictitious invoices to support the sham royalties constitutes criminal tax evasion or false-invoice crime under PRC Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205, carrying imprisonment plus monetary fines and a direct ban on leaving mainland China during the criminal investigation.
Shell Company Structure and Abuse of DTA Withholding Concessions

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures authorize general anti-abuse recharacterization: the DTA claim is denied, the income is re-sourced to mainland China, the full 10% WHT is collected, daily late-payment interest is imposed, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid is applied under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63; in Hong Kong, a false application for resident status or false profits tax declarations may breach the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the Crimes Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leaves the directors of the shell and the mainland FIP personally liable for the denied WHT, penalties, and interest; the directors are subject to the SAT blacklisting and travel-ban regime, asset-restitution orders, and personal bankruptcy exposure. In egregious fraud schemes, directors face criminal prosecution for tax evasion and fraud with imprisonment up to 10 years in mainland China and separate criminal exposure in Hong Kong for fraudulent claims made to the Inland Revenue Department, including confiscation of benefits extracted through the sham structure.
