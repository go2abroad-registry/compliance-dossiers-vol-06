Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax Bureau) online tax service platform; for any Hong Kong SAR branch or permanent establishment, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the competent authority, with the eTAX internet filing system.

E-government filing portal

For a People's Republic of China Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP): (1) execute a deed of partnership and register the FIP with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) under the Foreign Investment Law and the Partnership Enterprise Law, with no statutory minimum paid-in capital and with lawful non-resident partners; (2) after the business license is issued, the Unified Social Credit Code assigned by AMR is synchronized to the STA through one-window government registration, and that Unified Social Credit Code serves as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for VAT and income tax purposes; (3) the FIP must complete tax registration through the E-Tax Bureau, confirming entity formation, partnership structure, partners, address, and business scope; (4) foreign non-resident partners are generally not separately registered for a PRC tax identification number unless they have an establishment in China, but the FIP is responsible for filing information returns and effecting withholding where China-source income is distributed; (5) where treaty relief is sought by a foreign partner, a non-resident tax registration and treaty entitlement application must be submitted through the E-Tax Bureau with a certificate of tax residence; (6) if an actual Hong Kong branch or permanent establishment is established, a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate must be obtained from the IRD within one month of commencement, along with any required branch registration under the Companies Ordinance where applicable; (7) the FIP must update the E-Tax Bureau registration promptly upon any change in partners, address, registered capital, or business scope.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None at the PRC national level: there is no check-the-box election for partnerships, and hybrid mismatch elections are not available under PRC domestic law. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is pass-through by statute, with partners taxed on their attributable partnership profits under Enterprise Income Tax or Individual Income Tax as applicable. A VAT status election is available between small-scale VAT taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer status where the relevant turnover criteria are satisfied, but that is an indirect tax status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover de minimis for VAT registration: a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership must obtain a taxpayer identification number and be registered for VAT from establishment. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, and below that threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer; voluntary election as a general VAT taxpayer is permitted to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership may claim the 0% VAT export rate where goods are sold to a non-PRC customer, physically leave the PRC customs territory under a valid customs export declaration, and export proceeds are received through a PRC bank within the statutory forex repatriation period. For self-heating instant food products classified under ISIC 1079, the manufacturing facility must hold valid food production qualifications and be properly recorded for export, and the HS classification of the self-heating pot, exothermic food tray, and food contents must be verified. Goods supplied to Hong Kong must show physical departure from Mainland China through a recognised land, sea, or air exit and, where applicable, Hong Kong inward clearance. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-resident customers and not consumed in China may also be eligible for zero rating; services effectively consumed in China are not eligible.

  • Customs Export Declaration of Goods generated through the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC), bearing the export declaration number and HS code for the self-heating instant food product
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or authorized cross-boundary road-rail manifest evidencing physical departure of the goods from the PRC customs territory to Hong Kong or another foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract issued in Chinese and/or English, identifying the non-resident buyer, Incoterms, commodity description, exothermic component classification, quantity, and export value
  • Bank-issued export proceeds receipt or foreign exchange settlement voucher, including the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System reference and, where requested by the receiving bank, the Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent offshore remittance advice
  • Export food hygiene or inspection release documentation issued by the PRC customs authority for exported food products, where required by the destination customs jurisdiction
  • Electronic export VAT rebate declaration data submitted to the STA through the E-Tax Bureau, linked to the VAT special invoices and the customs export declaration

The FIP must use the STA tax-control digital invoicing system. VAT special invoices, including fully digitalized electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA electronic invoice service platform, with invoice data transmitted to the tax authority in real time at issuance. Anti-fraud controls operate through unique invoice codes, QR codes, digital signatures, encryption, and automatic reconciliation against VAT returns. Export VAT rebate substantiation must be supported by electronic VAT special invoices; handwritten or non-fiscal paper invoices are not acceptable. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; the STA platform is the statutory fiscal clearance layer.

Export VAT zero-rating and refund claims are subject to retrospective audit by the STA, GACC, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Where export declarations are false, shipping documents are forged, goods are diverted back into the domestic market, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, the tax authority will claw back all VAT refunded, charge additional output VAT, impose late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, and may levy administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection and its implementing regulations. Serious cases involving fraudulent export tax rebates, false export customs declarations, or counterfeit VAT special invoices may result in customs penalties, suspension or revocation of export privileges, and criminal liability for the responsible partners and persons under the PRC Criminal Law and relevant judicial interpretations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

In the mainland Chinese regulatory matrix, current-account items are broadly convertible for genuine trade and service transactions, whereas capital-account flows remain subject to a prior-approval or filing discipline. A Foreign-Invested Partnership established in China may hold RMB and foreign-currency operational accounts, and its export-related foreign exchange receipts may be retained or converted at a domestic bank. The Chinese government no longer imposes a sweeping surrender requirement on export proceeds, but the funds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system and reconciled against customs declarations within statutory timelines. The real friction lies in direct investment and profit distribution: inbound capital contributions from Hong Kong must be routed through a capital account, registered in the SAFE system, and verified by a certified public accountant before the cash is fungible for working capital. Dividend or partnership profit remittances require the FIP to demonstrate audited profit, settled tax liabilities, and compliance with current-account payment rules. This hybrid framework means a treasurer must separate every cash flow into current-account and capital-account buckets, because commingling the two is a canonical audit exception and can trigger a bank account freeze, SAFE penalty, or revocation of the cross-border settlement facility. The practical convertibility is therefore high for trade finance but constrained for capital transactions, and all conversions must occur inside licensed banks with a clear economic substance document trail.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion percentage in force; an exporting FIP may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in an Export Proceeds Settlement Account with a domestic bank or convert them into RMB. The binding requirement is repatriation: goods export proceeds must be brought into China and verified against the customs export declaration, and in standard practice the exchange-control matching file must be closed within 180 days from the customs declaration date. If the FIP intends to hold the foreign exchange beyond the approved time or use it for non-trade purposes, a SAFE extension or separate approval is required.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as the primary treasury clearing layer. Foreign-owned digital EMI/neobank brands are not licensed under the PRC banking regulatory framework and are legally barred from opening corporate settlement accounts for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. They cannot access CNAPS for local RMB clearing, cannot perform SAFE transaction reporting, and cannot hold capital-account funds. Some licensed domestic non-bank cross-border payment institutions can support e-commerce collection and settlement as agents, but all funds must be handed over to a regulated domestic commercial bank and such institutions cannot provide trade finance, FX hedging, documentary credit, or capital verification. Therefore, the FIP must maintain its core banking relationship with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner shall originate a SWIFT MT103 or telegraphic transfer from its Hong Kong corporate account to the FIP's SAFE-registered capital escrow account at the appointed mainland Tier-1 bank. The remittance purpose must be coded as Foreign Direct Investment Capital Contribution to a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The receiving bank will require the SAFE FDI registration certificate, the partnership business license, the capital contribution agreement, and the FIP's official seal for the remittance onboarding. Funds will be credited to the foreign currency capital account and may be converted to RMB at spot under the entity's settlement policy. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

PRC EIT: 25% statutory on taxable income of corporate partners in the Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (pass-through entity); Hong Kong Profits Tax: 16.5% (8.25% on first HKD 2 million assessable profit) on the Hong Kong holding/entity level

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

For distributions or profit remittances from a PRC FIP to its HK corporate partner, the statutory non-resident enterprise withholding rate is 10% of the gross amount under EIT Law Article 37. Under the Mainland–Hong Kong DTA, the rate is reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the payer. Hong Kong itself imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by an HK entity.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: PRC contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File / Master File) is statutorily required when annual aggregate related-party controlled transactions reach the thresholds in SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42: generally RMB 200 million for tangible-property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-property transfers, RMB 50 million for financial transactions, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. CbCR notification and filing apply separately to MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of EUR 750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: The PRC FIP must maintain real physical presence in mainland China for manufacturing, including plant, raw-material procurement, production-line decision-making, and quality and hygiene control as Core Income-Generating Activities. The HK partner or entity must pass the OECD and HK administrative substance test for treaty benefits; without substantive functions in Hong Kong, the arrangement may be re-characterized as treaty shopping or a sham. Hong Kong does not have statutory Economic Substance Regulations analogous to BVI/Cayman, but the IRD actively applies economic-substance scrutiny and anti-abuse rules under sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 63 and 64: 50%–500% of the underpaid tax plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%. Hong Kong IRO sections 80(2) and 82A: a fine of up to HK$50,000 and treble the undercharged tax, together with imprisonment of up to 3 years on conviction. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 may impose up to 7 years imprisonment for tax evasion where the underpayment is large and the proportion is high.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the partnership veil if the director or general partner uses the FIP as a facade to evade tax. The director or actual controller may be jointly liable for unpaid CIT, VAT, and WHT; PRC tax authorities can place the individual on the tax non-compliance blacklist, restrict exit from mainland China, and freeze personal assets. In Hong Kong, an officer who wilfully makes a false return is personally liable, may be disqualified under the Companies Ordinance, and may face a travel ban or asset preservation order imposed by the court.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Article 47 GAAR and SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42 authorise denial of treaty benefits, re-characterisation of the transaction, and collection of the 10% statutory WHT plus a 50%–500% fine. Hong Kong IRD may re-characterise under sections 61 and 61A; forged substance records constitute a criminal offence under IRO section 82A.

Director personal liability: Under PRC and HK corporate law, the corporate or partnership veil can be disregarded if the entity is undercapitalised and used for improper purposes. Directors and beneficial owners may be held personally liable for tax arrears after asset transfers; travel restrictions, asset freezing, and cross-border enforcement under mutual legal assistance arrangements between Hong Kong and Mainland China may follow. A director can face disqualification and up to 4 years imprisonment for fraudulent trading under section 275 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 32).
Environmental / Product-Safety / Licensing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PRC Food Safety Law Articles 35, 122, and 124 impose fines of RMB 50,000–500,000 or 5–10 times the value of the unlicensed products, plus suspension and revocation. Dangerous chemicals regulation enforcement can suspend manufacturing and trigger administrative detention; environmental violation penalties under the Environmental Protection Law can be assessed on a per-day-per-continuing-violation basis.

Director personal liability: The director, general partner, or person in charge is personally liable for civil compensation to consumers and for criminal food-safety fraud penalties that may reach 5–10 years or more imprisonment. Customs and health regulators may blacklist the legal representative, suspend export qualifications, and coordinate with HK authorities to restrict the same beneficial owner's access to Hong Kong if the violations involve the same controlled group.
