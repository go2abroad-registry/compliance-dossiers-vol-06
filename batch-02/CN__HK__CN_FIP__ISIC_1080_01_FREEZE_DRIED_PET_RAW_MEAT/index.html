Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-in capital is required for a Foreign-Invested Partnership under PRC law; the capital contribution amounts and contribution schedule are determined solely by the partnership agreement. No blocked escrow account is required. Contributions may be made in cash, in-kind or other lawful assets, and any inbound foreign currency remittance must be routed through a licensed bank in China and recorded for foreign exchange purposes.

Funding & unlock path: Civil Defense and Fire Safety: obtain a building and fire-protection design review and a final fire safety inspection certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department before occupancy; install automatic fire detectors, extinguishers, emergency lighting and an emergency ventilation system in the freeze-dryer hall and ammonia or refrigerant machinery room. Occupational Safety and Structural Clearance: complete a pre-production occupational hazard assessment under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Prevention of Occupational Diseases; obtain special-equipment registration and inspection for vacuum vessels and pressure vessels from the market supervision special-equipment safety authority; verify structural floor loads for freeze-drying chambers and packaging lines; and pass a workplace safety approval for all manufacturing, storage and distribution areas. These clearance certificates must be maintained and renewed at the intervals imposed by the issuing authority.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong, the entity must obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The application is submitted to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department. The BRC serves as the primary tax identification number for the partnership. Additionally, the partnership must obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for each partner (individual or corporate) for tax filing purposes. The process involves submitting Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) and supporting documents such as the partnership agreement, proof of address, and identification of partners. For non-resident partners, a Hong Kong tax representative may be required. The BRC must be renewed annually.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland Chinese regime sharply differentiates current account from capital account. RMB is convertible on current account for goods, services and profit remittances once the underlying transaction is supported by authentic documents and can pass the bank's SAFE-integrated verification. Capital account transactions are controlled: an inbound equity contribution to a Foreign-Invested Partnership requires a SAFE foreign-exchange registration, a dedicated FDI capital account, and, in practice, a CPA capital verification report; if foreign currency is converted to RMB, the use of funds must comply with the SAFE capital-settlement negative list and cannot be diverted to related-party lending, securities, or real estate outside the approved business scope. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory conversion ratio, but they must be collected into a mainland bank account, reconciled with customs records, and either retained in a goods-trade settlement account or settled into RMB at a licensed bank. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is classified as investment income and is executable after the bank verifies audited financial statements, a valid distribution resolution, tax clearance, withholding tax payment, and the absence of adverse KYC findings. The practical convertibility hurdles are usually documentary: opaque UBO chains, mismatched invoice-customs-payment data, missing tax-residency certificates for treaty relief, inconsistent partner

Export proceeds surrender rules

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated statutory authority for all cross-border currency controls affecting the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the offshore Hong Kong SAR clearing node.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and IRD filing practice, a Hong Kong partnership, including an FIP structured as a limited partnership, must submit audited financial statements with the Profits Tax Return when the partnership's total gross income for the basis period exceeds HK$5,000,000 (approx USD 641,025). There is no statutory balance-sheet or headcount audit threshold for partnerships in Hong Kong, but an independent certified CPA audit is also triggered where any partner is a Hong Kong company or other body corporate because that corporate partner must itself file audited accounts, or where a lender, pet food import/export registration authority, customer contract, or manufacturer licence imposes an audited financial statement covenant. For a company-form entity under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), an audit is mandatory irrespective of size, but the FIP is treated as a partnership and the applicable quantified statutory trigger is the HK$5,000,000 gross-income test.

Late filing: administrative fine of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 per return (up to RMB 50,000 in aggravated cases) with reduced export-tax-refund priority; late payment: daily surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax; understatement: 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid amount; deliberate evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due is a criminal referral.
Line 30 (Taxable Income) Taxable income after adjustments, reflecting the corporate partner's allocable share of the FIP's income under Caishui [2008] No. 159. The HK partner's share must be computed strictly under the partnership agreement ratio; special allocations that shift all profit to the HK partner are scrutinised for lack of business purpose. Late filing: administrative fine of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 per return (up to RMB 50,000 in aggravated cases) with reduced export-tax-refund priority; late payment: daily surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax; understatement: 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid amount; deliberate evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due is a criminal referral.
Lines 31 to 34 (Rate and Tax Payable) Applied tax rate and final EIT payable, reduced by withholding tax credits and prepayments. A 25% rate applies unless the partner itself holds an HNT or Small Low-Profit Enterprise qualification; the HK partner cannot claim such incentives merely through the FIP. Late filing: administrative fine of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 per return (up to RMB 50,000 in aggravated cases) with reduced export-tax-refund priority; late payment: daily surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax; understatement: 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid amount; deliberate evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due is a criminal referral.
Schedule G (Related-Party Transaction Annual Report) Full disclosure of controlled transactions: raw frozen meat purchases from the HK affiliate, platform-pay commissions, trademark royalties, intercompany loans, and year-end balances. Mandatory whenever related-party transactions exist regardless of amount; omission even with a nil TP adjustment is itself a compliance violation. Late filing: administrative fine of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 per return (up to RMB 50,000 in aggravated cases) with reduced export-tax-refund priority; late payment: daily surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax; understatement: 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid amount; deliberate evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due is a criminal referral.
