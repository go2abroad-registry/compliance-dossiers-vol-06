Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; statutory electronic filing and registration are performed through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online platform, with customs and export data integrated through the China International Trade Single Window. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department acts as the authority for Hong Kong-resident partners but is not the competening tax authority for the mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP).

E-government filing portal

The FIP must first complete commercial registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The issued business license carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically adopted as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), the VAT registration number, and the enterprise tax code under the integrated multi-license registration system. Within the statutory period after issuance, the FIP must complete tax registration through the e-Tax platform, confirm the competent tax authority, and select VAT taxpayer classification (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer). If the FIP exports, it must obtain an export customs registration code and register in the China International Trade Single Window. For foreign non-resident partners, including Hong Kong SAR partners, the FIP must collect the partner's tax residence certificate and, where applicable, Hong Kong Business Registration Number; no separate Chinese TIN is issued solely for holding a partnership interest, but the FIP must register as a withholding agent for the partner's Chinese-source income allocated through the pass-through partnership.

The issued business license carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically adopted as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), the VAT registration number, and the enterprise tax code under the integrated multi-license registration system. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for income tax classification; the PRC partnership regime mandates pass-through treatment and no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election is available. The only elective classification is the VAT status: an FIP below the general taxpayer turnover threshold may voluntarily register as a VAT general taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; every FIP, irrespective of turnover, must complete basic tax registration and obtain its Unified Social Credit Code as the VAT identification number upon establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim the 0% VAT export rate for electric cargo bicycles manufactured in mainland China when: (a) the FIP is a VAT general taxpayer with an export customs registration code; (b) the goods are physically exported through a customs declaration in the China International Trade Single Window; (c) the goods leave Chinese customs territory and are not subject to reprocessing or unlawful re-import; (d) the sale is made to an overseas buyer under a genuine commercial export contract; (e) the export proceeds are received from the overseas buyer and repatriated into China through the banking channel; and (f) the complete statutory documentation bundle is preserved for tax and customs audit. Export to Hong Kong SAR for onward third-country sale qualifies as an export from China if the goods are not processed or returned in a disqualifying manner.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration generated through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract, and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or equivalent multimodal transport document) evidencing physical export movement
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt or cross-border RMB settlement voucher proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT exemption/refund declaration together with special VAT invoices for purchased inputs and production records supporting the exported electric cargo bicycles

The FIP is subject to the nationwide fully digital electronic invoice system. All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, are issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform and carry a statutory anti-counterfeit electronic signature, unique national invoice number, issuer Unified Social Credit Code, and QR verification code. Invoice dataset is transmitted to the STA in real time through the tax-control invoice management system, making the digital invoice the fiscal clearance instrument. Domestic taxable sales require special VAT invoices for general taxpayer buyers; export sales are supported by export customs declarations rather than a separate VAT clearance stamp. No legacy hardware-based fiscal sealing is required.

The STA and customs authorities jointly audit export zero-rating claims through the China International Trade Single Window and tax risk management systems. If the FIP cannot substantiate actual export, genuine commercial transaction, customs clearance, or foreign-exchange repatriation, the 0% rate is retroactively denied; the FIP must repay all VAT refunds or exemptions obtained, pay late-payment surcharge, and may be subject to administrative penalties of up to five times the VAT amount fraudulently obtained under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. False export declarations can trigger customs penalties, suspension or revocation of export tax-refund eligibility, blacklisting for joint credit punishment, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains an open capital and current account with a freely convertible HKD. The HKMA operates the Linked Exchange Rate System at HKD 7.75-7.85 per US dollar, but there is no restriction on conversion, profit remittance, partnership capital withdrawal, dividend taxation, or repatriation of sales proceeds for entities domiciled in Hong Kong. No prior approval is required from the HKMA for capital flows, although banks may impose enhanced due diligence and commercial substance checks on large cross-border movements. Because the parent sovereign is China, funds traversing the boundary between mainland China and Hong Kong remain subject to SAFE and PBOC regulations. A Foreign-Invested Partnership intending to receive capital from mainland Chinese investors must ensure those funds are structured as approved outbound investment under the official ODI filing and SAFE foreign-exchange registration regime; Hong Kong's free convertibility does not override mainland exchange controls. Within Hong Kong, the HKMA provides robust multi-currency clearing through the Clearing House Automated Transfer System (CHATS) for HKD, USD, EUR and CNH, together with SWIFT connectivity, enabling rapid international liquidity flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil for Hong Kong: there is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender obligation for export proceeds received by a Hong Kong entity. Exporters may legitimately retain foreign currency receipts in any HKD, USD, EUR, CNY/CNH or multi-currency account. If the underlying manufacturing operation in mainland China generates export proceeds, SAFE regulations may require the mainland entity to police trade data verification, repatriation timing and retention of FX in designated settlement accounts according to the prevailing SAFE practice.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) - the de facto central bank, currency board authority and banking regulator of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region under the Currency Ordinance and Basic Law

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Hong Kong does not operate an Electronic Money Institution licence comparable to the UK or EU; non-bank platforms must instead hold an MSO licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance or an SVF licence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. These platforms are not banks and cannot accept customer deposits, provide trade credit, or issue LCs. Many offshore digital EMIs are not legally authorized to open deposit accounts for Hong Kong partnership entities. However, a licensed cross-border fintech with strong nested-bank compliance, such as a reputable MSO or SVF handling trade collections, can function as an auxiliary collection and remittance channel; it must not be relied upon for settlement of capital contributions, LC operations, or regulatory-liquidity-clearing needs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions to the Hong Kong FIP should be cleared through an HKMA-authorized institution using CHATS for HKD, USD, EUR or CNH settlement. For foreign investors outside mainland China, the preferred rail is SWIFT MT103 with a reference containing the FIP unique identifier and the underlying partnership subscription agreement; the receiving bank will conduct a capital verification exercise, and a Hong Kong CPA may issue a contribution certificate where the partnership structure requires it. For capital flowing from mainland China, the payment must first satisfy SAFE outbound investment registration and a mainland bank purpose code for cross-border investment; funds can then settle in CNH through CIPS or CHATS before being credited into the HK FIP capital account. No HKMA licence is required for receipt of capital, but the bank will demand a complete compliance file listing sources of wealth and the economic purpose of the contribution.For supplier settlements and operating expenses, the FIP should use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for high-value payments, and the Faster Payment System (FPS) for small local or urgent Hong Kong domestic payments. Inventory purchases from mainland component suppliers can be paid in CNH via CIPS or CHATS using a declared trade contract; for international buyers of the electric cargo bicycles, the Hong Kong bank can issue commercial customs-backed documentary letters of credit through SWIFT MT700, with settlement via MT202/MT103 upon presentation of compliant transport and customs documents. Documentary collections on DP or DA basis, processed through correspondent bank networks, are permissible for lower-risk or repeat trade counterparties. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the FIP's annual related-party tangible asset transfers, including sales of electric cargo e-bikes and components to or from Hong Kong affiliates, exceed RMB 20 million; intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 10 million; related-party services exceed RMB 40 million; or financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million. Master File is generally required when the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exist. Documentation must be contemporaneous, prepared or updated before the CIT filing deadline, and maintained in Chinese.

Economic substance scope: The PRC tax system does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulation comparable to the UAE ESR, but similar results are achieved through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47, permitting re-characterisation of arrangements lacking reasonable commercial substance; through the beneficial ownership requirement for treaty benefits; and through the attribution of China PE profits using the authorised OECD approach based on functions, assets, and risks. For the Hong Kong parent to claim treaty rates and be recognised as substantive, it must have an office, local employees, board decisions, bank accounts, and profit retention in Hong Kong. The Core Income-Generating Activities for the urban electric cargo e-bike manufacturing FIP are performed in mainland China, including production, inventory, logistics, and quality control; accordingly, the China entity or PE must bear the manufacturing profit, while the Hong Kong affiliate may retain only distribution or branding profit supported by its actual functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax plus a daily surcharge of 0.05%; Article 201 of the Criminal Law punishes tax evasion with imprisonment up to 7 years and criminal fines for significant amounts; invoice counterfeiting under Article 205 can attract imprisonment of up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who direct the false invoicing or sign false returns may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; personal assets may be seized after pierce of the corporate veil; travel bans and blacklisting as serious tax offenders are imposed; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and disqualification from serving on boards of PRC enterprises.
Transfer Pricing Misconduct

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41 and 46 and implementing regulations, the tax authority may recharacterise the arrangement and make transfer-pricing adjustments retroactively within 10 years, levy late-payment interest, and impose a 50%-to-500% fraud penalty if concealment is intentional. Failure to prepare the Local File attracts a fine of up to RMB 50,000 and reverses the burden of proof.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute below-market contracts or authorise sham management fees may be personally liable under piercing provisions for the tax adjustment and penalties; they may face asset freezing, travel bans, and criminal tax evasion charges where the conduct is deliberate and concealed.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and deny treaty benefits, impose the full 10% statutory withholding tax, find a de facto permanent establishment and assess 25% net-basis tax, plus daily surcharges; false declarations may attract a 50%-to-500% penalty; under the MLI Principal Purpose Test, treaty relief is denied even in the absence of explicit fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false residency certificates, beneficial ownership declarations, or board minutes for the shell Hong Kong entity may be civilly liable for the under-withheld tax and penalties; they are subject to travel restrictions and blacklisting as serious tax offenders; in cases of criminal fraud, imprisonment terms of 3 to 7 years may be imposed under PRC Criminal Law and Hong Kong's Theft Ordinance for procuring a tax advantage by deception.
