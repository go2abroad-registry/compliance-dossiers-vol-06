Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent corporate registry

Registrar of Companies, Companies Registry, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, together with the Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department. Electronic filings are accepted through the Companies Registry e-Registry portal.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) of the Laws of Hong Kong, particularly sections 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9; Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), especially section 3 on general partnership liability; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), sections 6 and 7; Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), Part 16, where the general partner is a Hong Kong incorporated company. The foreign-invested partnership is permissible as Hong Kong partnership law imposes no residency or nationality restrictions on partners.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Hong Kong foreign-invested partnership. Contributions may be in cash, moulds, tooling, intellectual property, or inventory as agreed in the Limited Partnership Agreement. No blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Registry. Bank counterparties may, however, require a commercial working capital commitment of not less than HKD 500,000 before opening manufacturing trade accounts.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certification under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572) that the premises are fitted with approved automatic alarm systems, emergency lighting, exit signage, and hose reels; a Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295) licence for storage of flammable paint thinners, bonding agents, and aerosol products limited to designated fire-rated cabinets; structural load inspection by an Authorized Person under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) confirming floor loading capacity for rotocasting machines and injection presses; and occupational safety approval from the Labour Department under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance, including mechanical guard certification, extract ventilation for spray booths, and personal protective equipment programmes.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Hong Kong foreign-invested partnership. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and enquiry portal: eTAX (etax.gov.hk) together with the GovHK Business Registration e-Service for issuance of Business Registration Certificates.

E-government filing portal

For the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) as a limited partnership, the partnership must first be registered with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). Subsequently, the partnership must apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The application is made on the statutory form prescribed by the Business Registration Ordinance, either in paper or through the GovHK Business Registration e-Service; the IRD then issues a Business Registration Certificate which carries the Business Registration Number (BRN), i.e. the partnership's statutory tax identifier. No VAT/GST registration ID exists in Hong Kong because no such tax is levied. Foreign non-resident partners need not separately obtain a Hong Kong TIN for the partnership; each partner's assessable profits are computed and assessed in the partner's own name, using, for individuals, the individual's HKID or passport number, and, for corporations, their own BRN or foreign registration details. The partnership must notify the IRD of any change in partners or business particulars and must keep the BRN current through annual business registration renewal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) does not provide a check-the-box or equivalent entity classification election. Partnerships are fiscally transparent by default; profits are assessed on the partners in their proportionate shares. Individual partners may elect Personal Assessment under the IRO, but this is a separate relief mechanism, not a classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax - the Hong Kong SAR does not impose any general consumption tax; only specific excise duties exist under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no statutory threshold for VAT/GST/sales tax registration because no general indirect tax is imposed. Separately, a business registration obligation arises under the Business Registration Ordinance from the first day of carrying on business in Hong Kong, regardless of turnover, with no de minimis turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept because Hong Kong does not operate VAT/GST. There is thus no 0% export VAT rate to claim, and no VAT refund mechanism is needed. Exports of designer blind box toys from Hong Kong are not subject to any indirect tax at export. For profits tax purposes, export sale profits may qualify as offshore income, and thereby be excluded from Hong Kong profits tax, if the operations essential to the production of those profits are performed by the partnership outside Hong Kong; a purely Hong Kong-based purchasing or delivery activity may expose the profits to territorial assessment under the IRO. Likewise, for qualifying offshore digital services supplied by the partnership to non-resident customers, no Hong Kong indirect tax is chargeable.

Commercial invoices are issued by private contract; only the Business Registration Ordinance and the Inland Revenue Ordinance require records, including invoices, to be kept sufficient to support profits tax, generally for a period of 7 years.

No VAT-related clawback or zero-rating penalty exists because there is no VAT/GST regime. No statutory foreign exchange repatriation deadline for zero-rating is prescribed in Hong Kong; the 180-day figure is applied as a documentary benchmark for export-proceeds substantiation. Incorrect export declarations may be penalised under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), and the Customs and Excise Department can impose fines and seize goods. Where offshore treatment is claimed for profits tax purposes, the IRD may scrutinise the essential operations and, if the claim is unsupported or the underlying documents are fraudulent, raise additional profits tax assessments within the statutory limitation periods and levy penalties under sections 80, 82 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), including treble the tax undercharged for wilful evasion. Records supporting export transactions must be retained for at least 7 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a dual-track convertibility regime. Current account foreign exchange is generally convertible for bona fide trade, services, royalties, and distributable profit remittances, provided the transacting bank completes authenticity and compliance review. Profit remittance from the China Foreign-Invested Partnership to the Hong Kong partner is treated as a current account outward payment and can be executed at a licensed domestic bank after submission of the audited financial statements, tax clearance or withholding certification, partnership profit distribution resolution, and supporting partnership accounts. Capital account convertibility remains restricted. Inbound capital injections from the Hong Kong partner require SAFE foreign-invested partnership foreign exchange registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a licensed bank, and subsequent post-injection use monitoring. The RMB is a managed floating currency, with spot exchange rates bounded by the PBOC daily central parity. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory surrender, but all foreign exchange receipts must flow through licensed banking channels, be linked to customs declarations, and be retained or settled in accordance with SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring rules. Any diversion of capital account funds into prohibited onshore securities, real estate, or non-business purposes is an automatic blocker. Overall, the FIP can operate with a fully convertible current account, but its capital account is strictly regulated and subject to ongoing SAFE reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under the current SAFE trade-in-goods framework. A China Foreign-Invested Partnership may lawfully retain 100% of qualifying export foreign currency receipts in a licensed-bank foreign exchange settlement account or may convert them into RMB at the prevailing bank rate. The key regulatory timeline is the reconciliation requirement: export receipts must be booked and matched against the corresponding customs declaration within 30 days of receipt through the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. If the agreed commercial payment term extends beyond 90 days from customs clearance, the enterprise must file an advance/delayed payment declaration with SAFE or the transacting bank. All receipts above the bank's threshold require full underlying documentation including contract, invoice, customs declaration, and logistics evidence before credit is finalized. Failure to report deferred or prepaid trade items can trigger the bank's trade authenticity downgrade and mandatory reserve requirements on subsequent converting operations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) overseeing the offshore CNH and Hong Kong clearing leg

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary banking rail; Medium only as an ancillary payment gateway. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or similar digital account providers are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot legally hold the FIP's onshore capital account, RMB basic deposit account, or SAFE-registered foreign exchange account. They also cannot perform the statutory SAFE foreign exchange registration, capital verification, or tax withholding functions required for a Chinese FIP. They may, however, be used as cross-border e-commerce collection facilitators for Shopify Payments, Stripe, or foreign marketplace settlement, provided they operate through a licensed Chinese payment institution and maintain all customer funds in a fiduciary trust account at a licensed bank. For treasury-grade liquidity and regulatory compliance, the FIP must maintain its core operating and capital accounts at a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign partner contributions must be remitted by wire transfer from a bank account in the name of the Hong Kong partner, not by a third party or a fintech EMI. The SWIFT MT103 must clearly identify the remitter as the Hong Kong partner, the beneficiary as the China Foreign-Invested Partnership, and must use the SAFE international transaction purpose code applicable to inbound direct investment capital contribution, currently mapped under the SAFE FDI code family for foreign-invested enterprise capital injection. The bank will not release the funds or settle the foreign exchange until the SAFE Foreign-Invested Partnership foreign exchange registration is verified and the funds are credited to the designated capital account. After receipt, the bank conducts authenticity review under the capital account payment-in-advance and payment-for-truth principles, and a licensed PRC accounting firm issues a capital verification report reflecting the actual paid-in contribution. Capital account funds may only be used for the FIP's own operating purposes; any change in use, additional injection, or repatriation must be reported to SAFE through the relevant banks or the Foreign Exchange Administration.For mainland China domestic supplier payments for rotocasting, injection molding, shipping, and airbrush finishing, the FIP should clear RMB directly through CNAPS using its Basic Operational Currency Account. For cross-border trade disbursements to Hong Kong licensors, foreign designer studios, or overseas raw material suppliers, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 bank transfer with an appropriate SAFE trade or service purpose code and matching contract, invoice, and, where applicable, customs declaration. For larger purchases of custom molds, bulk vinyl, and production tooling from overseas vendors, an irrevocable sight commercial letter of credit issued by a Tier-1 Chinese bank is the recommended instrument, because it transfers documentary risk and prevents unauthorized disbursement. Turnover: Ordinary Hong Kong partnerships are not subject to a Companies Ordinance audit mandate; however, if the Foreign-Invested Partnership is constituted as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap 637, audited financial statements are mandatory for every financial year regardless of size. For Inland Revenue Department purposes, a partnership with gross income exceeding HKD 2,000,000 in a year of assessment must submit audited accounts prepared by a certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance. No balance-sheet-total or employee-headcount exemption exists for partnerships: the IRD may also require audited accounts below the HKD 2,000,000 threshold where records are inadequate or an estimated assessment is contested.
