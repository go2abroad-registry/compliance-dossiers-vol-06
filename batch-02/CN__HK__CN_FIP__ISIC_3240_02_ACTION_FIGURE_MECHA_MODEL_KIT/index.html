Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no escrow account, and no blocked capital requirement for limited partnerships. The capital contributions are fixed solely in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be satisfied by cash, property or services. If the partnership is funded by a PRC foreign-invested counterparty, PRC outbound direct investment (ODI) approvals from NDRC, MOFCOM and SAFE may apply, and the investor's bank may voluntarily impose a source-of-funds escrow, but no Companies Registry escrow rule exists.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department approval must be obtained for fire service installations, fire hydrant coverage, emergency escape routes, signage, and emergency lighting under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95). A Fire Safety Plan must be submitted and an annual fire drill log maintained. The Buildings Department must accept the structural engineer's certificate that floor loading and steel-framed mezzanines, if any, are safe for injection-molding machinery and warehousing. The Labour Department must issue an occupational safety clearance covering machine guarding, interlocking devices, manual handling, noise exposure levels, dust extraction, and personal protective equipment. The factory must hold employees' compensation insurance under the Employees' Compensation Ordinance (Cap. 282), and emergency response procedures must be rehearsed with the local civil defense authorities.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no escrow account, and no blocked capital requirement for limited partnerships. The capital contributions are fixed solely in the Limited Partnership Agreement and may be satisfied by cash, property or services. If the partnership is funded by a PRC foreign-invested counterparty, PRC outbound direct investment (ODI) approvals from NDRC, MOFCOM and SAFE may apply, and the investor's bank may voluntarily impose a source-of-funds escrow, but no Companies Registry escrow rule exists.

E-government filing portal

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a managed floating currency regime with full convertibility on current account transactions and material controls on capital account flows. Trade settlements, service fees, and ordinary royalties can be converted through banks with underlying evidence, but cross-border capital injections into a Foreign-Invested Partnership require SAFE foreign investment registration and must enter through a designated foreign exchange capital account. Hong Kong SAR is a separate monetary jurisdiction where HKD and offshore RMB are fully convertible, yet flows between mainland China and Hong Kong are still treated as cross-border transactions for purpose code and SAFE reporting purposes. Profit remittance by the FIP to foreign partners is lawful only after the partnership completes an annual external audit, settles any applicable profits tax on the pass-through partners, and submits tax clearance; the remittance must not exceed cumulative audited distributable profits. Export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated to a licensed bank in China and may be retained or converted; there is no mandatory surrender. Capital account RMB conversion must follow actual-use principles and documentary verification, prohibiting speculative conversion or mismatch with the approved business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods-trade administration, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated through a licensed designating bank within 90 days of the export customs declaration date, unless an extension is filed with SAFE for documented commercial reasons. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; the exporter may retain foreign currency in its export settlement account or voluntarily sell it to the designating bank at the prevailing market rate. Every receipt must be matched electronically to the customs declaration using the bank's trade verification system, and the FIP must maintain trade ledgers for A-class enterprise status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) separately administers the Hong Kong dollar and banking system in Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer are legally barred from acting as the primary treasury or foreign exchange clearing rail for a PRC-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. They do not hold a commercial bank license from PBOC and cannot open a SAFE-supervised foreign exchange capital account or accept export proceeds as a designated foreign exchange bank. Feasibility rating is Low as core banking infrastructure, and no use should be made of an offshore EMI for inbound capital contributions or SAFE-regulated profit remittance. They may have limited utility only as ancillary collection tools for small cross-border e-commerce sales if the funds are ultimately routed through a licensed mainland bank and fully reconciled to customs declarations; any such collection must be supported by a complete audit trail acceptable to the Tier-1 bank's AML committee.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Hong Kong law, a pure registered limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance Cap 37 is not automatically required to appoint a certified public accountant auditor based solely on balance sheet size, turnover or headcount. An independent statutory audit becomes compulsory in the following situations: (i) where the entity is also incorporated as a private company under the Companies Ordinance Cap 622, every non-dormant company must procure audited financial statements regardless of size, so the quantitative threshold is effectively any positive revenue, any positive total assets, or any headcount above zero; (ii) where the partnership holds a licence to conduct regulated activities under the Securities and Futures Ordinance or operates as a trust and company service provider under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance, audited accounts are required by the licensing framework; (iii) where the partnership constitutes a limited partnership fund falling within the Securities and Futures Commission's fund codes, annual accounts must be audited by a Hong Kong certified public accountant; (iv) where financing, insurance or banking covenants impose an audit on a balance sheet above USD 5 million, annual turnover above USD 10 million or average headcount above 20 persons, the partnership must adopt an independent certified audit to maintain contractual compliance. Any statutory audit mandate requires financial statements to be prepared for a true and fair view and audited by a CPA with a practising certificate.
