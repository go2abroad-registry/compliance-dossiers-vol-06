Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Hong Kong Companies Registry requires every registered partnership to maintain a business address. A pure virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address without a physical presence and without staffing will not be accepted if it functions as a mere letterbox because the Inland Revenue Department's contemporaneous memorandum and the Business Registration Office require a true place of business where the partnership's books and records can be produced. However, a serviced office/serviced registered office (a physical suite with lease, letterhead, staffed reception, and access to business records) is permissible. For the FIP business model (foreign-invested partnership holding a PRC WFOE or serving as an OEM contract manufacturer), Hong Kong banks and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority will reject the FIP where the partnership is a shell with only a virtual office and no substantive economic activity in Hong Kong. The Inland Revenue Department applies an economic substance business-presence test: the FIP must have at least one local general partner; a dedicated bank account with Hong Kong banking relationship; local books and records; and governance meetings/decision-making materials physically generated in Hong Kong. Because the underlying commercial operation is a low-risk plush toy manufacturing business, the FIP must demonstrate an actual Hong Kong business function, such as contract negotiation, supply chain payments, or logistics coordination, to avoid sham-entity characterization. A virtual-only address will not be sufficient for the subsequent WFOE investment presentation to the PRC Ministry of Commerce and SAMR foreign investment information reporting regime.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Limited Partnership. Partnership capital is determined solely by the executed limited partnership deed. There is no requirement to place capital in a blocked escrow account; however, upon banking onboarding, the licensed bank may ask the partnership to make an initial deposit and demonstrate source of funds before the account is activated.

Funding & unlock path: The Fire Services Department will issue a Fire Service Installations Certificate only after installation of automatic sprinkler and fire-alarm systems, hose reels, and emergency escape signage are tested and operational. Building Department approval for structural steel racks and mezzanines is required for storage loads. The Labour Department Factories Branch requires compliance with machine guarding, ventilation, and noise control; a pre-start registration inspection and an annual employee safety inspection under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) may also be required. Any storage of flammable cleaning solvents above the exempt quantity requires an FSD Dangerous Goods License. No separate civil defense evacuation plan is needed for small to medium workshops, but all exits must remain unobstructed and a trained fire warden must be appointed.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Limited Partnership. Partnership capital is determined solely by the executed limited partnership deed. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Hong Kong SAR; online electronic portal: GovHK e-Tax (https://www.gov.hk/etax)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity wishing to operate the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong must, within one month of business commencement, obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The responsible partner, or an appointed Hong Kong tax representative, must submit the prescribed application with the partnership agreement and durable identity documentation. The issued Business Registration Number becomes the primary tax identifier for the partnership. Separately, the IRD will open a Profits Tax file and assign a Tax File Number once partnership Profits Tax returns are due; all subsequent filings and correspondence must quote both numbers. Foreign non-resident partners with no physical presence in Hong Kong must appoint a local tax representative for service of IRD notices and signing of tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the FIP is treated as a partnership and is not separately subject to Profits Tax; each partner is assessable on their respective share of Hong Kong-sourced partnership profits, subject to the territorial source principle.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no 'check-the-box' or hybrid-mismatch election regime. Entity classification follows partnership law and the Inland Revenue Ordinance. A taxpayer may, however, seek an advance ruling from the IRD on whether a particular profit item is Hong Kong-sourced or offshore.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST or general sales tax in Hong Kong. Indirect taxation is limited to excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), vehicle first registration tax, and hotel accommodation tax; none of these apply to plush mascot stuffed animal exports.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold. All businesses carrying on trade in Hong Kong are required to obtain a Business Registration Certificate irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong does not operate a VAT/GST system. Export zero-rating is therefore not a tax attribute. For profits tax purposes, cross-border trading income from the sale of plush mascot stuffed animals may be treated as offshore and thereby effectively untaxed if the buyer, seller, negotiations, and contract conclusion occur and perform outside Hong Kong, and no essential profit-generating activity is undertaken in Hong Kong. The goods may be warehoused and shipped through Hong Kong without creating Hong Kong-sourced trading profits.

  • Commercial invoice for the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export shipment from Hong Kong
  • Electronic Export Declaration lodged under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) through the approved electronic trade service provider
  • Proof of payment receipt, bank inward remittance statement, or platform settlement record evidencing full settlement of the export sale in foreign exchange

No mandatory real-time electronic invoice clearance, API fiscal reporting, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists in Hong Kong. The IRD operates the e-Tax and e-Notice portals for filing and communication on a voluntary basis. Export trade declarations must be lodged electronically under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, but that requirement is statistical and customs-related rather than an indirect tax clearance mechanism.

Where an offshore profit claim is made, the effective tax on the export is nil; however, if the IRD determines on audit that the profit was in fact Hong Kong-sourced, or that the supporting documentation is inaccurate, incomplete, or fraudulent, the IRD may issue additional Profits Tax assessments under sections 61 or 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For negligent or fraudulent returns, the assessment window may extend to ten years, with penalties up to 200% of the undercharged tax plus an annual charge of 3%. Criminal prosecution may also be pursued under section 82 for knowingly making false statements or providing misleading information to tax authorities.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China's currency regime combines current account convertibility with a strictly administered capital account. For an FIP engaged in plush toy manufacturing, the current account covering trade in goods and services is freely convertible for bona fide transactions, requiring only declaration and documentation through authorized banks under the Goods Trade Monitoring System. However, the capital account remains restricted. Inbound capital injections into the partnership must be registered with SAFE, recorded in an FDI foreign currency capital account, and can be converted into RMB only for permitted purposes such as purchasing equipment, settling factory leases, and paying employees or domestic suppliers. Profit repatriation requires a cleared tax certificate, statutory audits, and bank review of the underlying operating revenue; dividends or partnership distributions are subject to a 10% withholding tax on foreign partners, unless reduced by treaty. External debt, cross-border guarantees, and disposal of partnership interests require separate approvals. There is no free two-way convertibility, and all conversions occur through the onshore interbank market at rates managed against a trade-weighted basket. Managers must avoid attempting to move capital out of structure without documentary support, as SAFE strictly enforces underlying transaction authenticity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 FX reform, China has abolished the historical mandatory surrender requirement. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign exchange receipts in current-account FX accounts or convert them to RMB at their discretion, provided that all export proceeds are collected and brought back onshore within a standard trade collection period, usually 180 days from the date of customs declaration, with extensions up to 365 days for certain receivables. Every receipt must be reported to the Goods Trade Monitoring System and reconciled with customs data; the bank will verify the payment against the corresponding export declaration. No fixed statutory conversion ratio applies today; genuine trade can be settled freely at the prevailing onshore spot rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the mandate of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex have no legal ability to provide current account, nostro, or foreign exchange settlement services for a mainland-incorporated FIP. Their limited operation in China is confined to cross-border e-commerce payment receiving via licenses with PBOC, but they cannot open corporate deposit accounts, issue CNH, or engage in FX hedging. Consequently, an FIP manufacturing plush toys in mainland China must use a licensed domestic bank for all regulatory filings. For the Hong Kong portion of the structure, digital banks such as ZA Bank or WeLab can provide operational accounts but cannot substitute for the mainland onshore bank relationship required for capital controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injections must be made by wire transfer via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's designated onshore capital account for foreign direct investment. The receiving bank requires the SAFE-issued FDI foreign exchange business registration code and a payment instruction with purpose code 261 for inbound capital contribution or the equivalent. The bank will validate the remittance against the registered paid-in capital, convert the foreign currency at the onshore spot rate, and subsequently require a capital verification report issued by a certified public accountant for registration of the partnership's paid-up capital at the local Administration for Market Regulation. No offsetting, netting, or splitting of payments is permitted.For imports and service payments, the FIP may use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers after submitting import documents and customs declarations; commercial letters of credit with negotiating banks are acceptable for large-scale raw material purchases; documentary collections can be used for recurring consignment trade. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut, and Airwallex have no legal ability to provide current account, nostro, or foreign exchange settlement services for a mainland-incorporated FIP. Their limited operation in China is confined to cross-border e-commerce payment receiving via licenses with PBOC, but they cannot open corporate deposit accounts, issue CNH, or engage in FX hedging. Consequently, an FIP manufacturing plush toys in mainland China must use a licensed domestic bank for all regulatory filings. For the Hong Kong portion of the structure, digital banks such as ZA Bank or WeLab can provide operational accounts but cannot substitute for the mainland onshore bank relationship required for capital controls.

Turnover: Under Hong Kong law, the FIP (registered as a partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance, Cap. 37, or the Partnership Ordinance, Cap. 38) is not itself subject to a compulsory audit, unless it is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637), in which case an annual audit by a certified public accountant is mandatory with no de minimis threshold. Where the entity carries on a trade or business in Hong Kong, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) may require the production of audited accounts under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) if the turnover for the year of assessment exceeds HKD 2,000,000 (approx USD 256,410) or where in the IRD's opinion special circumstances warrant it. For a Hong Kong private company (not applicable here), a statutory audit becomes mandatory if the company fails the 'small company' exemption, i.e., it exceeds any two of the following thresholds: (a) total annual turnover exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approx USD 1,282,051); (b) total assets exceeding HKD 10,000,000; or (c) number of employees exceeding 50. In all cases, the audit must be performed by a certified public accountant registered under the Professional Accountants Ordinance (Cap. 50).
