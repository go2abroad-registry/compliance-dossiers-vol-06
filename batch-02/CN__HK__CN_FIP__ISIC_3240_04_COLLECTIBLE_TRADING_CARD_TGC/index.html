Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A registered office in Hong Kong for a Foreign-Invested Partnership must be a physical address in Hong Kong, and while virtual office and commercial mailbox providers can satisfy the strict letter of the Companies Registry's registered office rule where they operate as licensed service providers with a physical reception and mail-forwarding capability, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department and Hong Kong Monetary Authority-regulated banks will, for an active commercial entity carrying on manufacturing and publishing of collectible trading cards, reject a mere virtual office or commercial mailbox for substantive tax residence, trade characterization, and bank account opening. Because the FIP is expected to pass through income to partners, the Inland Revenue Department will scrutinize whether the partnership has sufficient commercial substance in Hong Kong: physical premises, local managerial staff, books and records kept in Hong Kong, and board/partner meetings held in Hong Kong. A virtual office arrangement without dedicated office space or an independent lease in the FIP's own name carries a high risk of being disregarded for profits tax exemption and may cause the relevant bank to reject the corporate account as non-genuine. The recommended compliance position is a serviced office lease with dedicated tenancy agreement, a visible physical sign, and independently verifiable local management at the address, entirely consistent with the postal address being distinct from the registered address.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. A Hong Kong partnership has no statutory minimum paid-up capital; the capital commitments stated in the partnership deed are private contractual obligations. No blocked escrow account is required under Companies Registry or Business Registration rules, although the licensed bank may require committed capital to be placed in a business savings or designating account for anti-money laundering customer due diligence purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; under the territorial source principle, the FIP is not taxed as an entity, and each partner is assessed individually on their share of profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong. Profits sourced outside Hong Kong are not within the territorial scope of Profits Tax.

Available election forms

None; Hong Kong does not apply check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The only relevant mechanism is the ability to apply to the IRD for an advance ruling on the source of profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, which is a source-ascertainment procedure, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil - Hong Kong does not operate a VAT, GST, or general sales tax regime. Excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance apply only to specified commodities such as alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol, and do not apply to collectible trading cards.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no indirect tax registration exists in Hong Kong. For direct tax purposes, the Business Registration Ordinance requires every business to apply for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement irrespective of turnover, with no economic turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR because no VAT/GST is imposed. Export sales of collectible trading cards are outside any Hong Kong indirect tax net. Where goods are physically exported from mainland China by a mainland affiliate, the mainland entity may qualify for Chinese VAT export zero-rating or export-tax-refund treatment; the Hong Kong FIP itself does not substantiate an indirect tax zero-rating claim to the IRD.

  • Chinese Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for goods leaving mainland China
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing international transportation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation (FIRC) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or purchase order with the overseas customer
  • For mainland export VAT refund: the exporter's VAT refund declaration and corresponding customs electronic manifest data

Not applicable; Hong Kong has no mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, continuous transaction control, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime. The IRD eTAX system is used for direct tax filing obligations only. Export trade declarations to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department serve statistical and licensing purposes, but no indirect tax clearance is required for trading cards.

No VAT clawback exists in Hong Kong because there is no VAT. For mainland China export-tax refunds, the exporter is audited by the State Taxation Administration; if the refund is granted based on fraudulent documentation, fabricated export transactions, or failure to repatriate and verify foreign exchange within the prescribed period, the refund is revoked, the tax authority recovers the refunded amount plus interest, and may impose a penalty of up to five times the unlawfully refunded tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Administration of Tax Collection. In Hong Kong, the IRD may audit Profits Tax returns; if offshore treatment is disproved, profits tax at 16.5% is assessed, and penalties up to 200% of the undercharged tax may be imposed for wilful or intentional understatement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains full current and capital account convertibility for HKD, USD and CNH, with no surrender obligations or compulsory conversion, while the HKD/USD peg under the Linked Exchange Rate System anchors settlement confidence. The Mainland China foreign exchange regime is asymmetric: current account transactions are convertible after banks verify genuine trade and service documents, but capital account transactions, including foreign-invested partnership capital contributions, cross-border loans and profit distributions to foreign partners, remain controlled and require SAFE registration through a designated bank. A Foreign-Invested Partnership receiving capital from Hong Kong must complete SAMR registration, open a SAFE-designated foreign currency capital account, and report the inward remittance under capital account direct investment before voluntary settlement into RMB. Profit remittances to foreign partners are subject to audited financial statements, tax clearance or withholding evidence, and bank review of distributable partnership profits; residual ambiguity in partnership capital account treatment can cause clearing delays. The treasury operating model should therefore separate onshore regulated flows from offshore Hong Kong liquidity, with all cross-border wires carrying correct BOP purpose codes and full underlying documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Mainland China: export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account upon collection; there is no current mandatory conversion ratio and enterprises may voluntarily settle or retain FX. If export payment is not received within 90 days after the customs declaration, a deferred trade-credit report must be filed through the SAFE goods-trade monitoring system, while pre-shipment receipts due within 30 days require reporting if not matched to customs clearance. For Hong Kong SAR: no export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement exists.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the People's Republic of China; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central monetary authority for Hong Kong SAR and administers the Linked Exchange Rate System but imposes no foreign exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore fintech EMIs are generally not licensed banks and are legally barred from providing onshore Mainland China foreign exchange settlement, capital account custody, SAFE capital verification, or acting as the receiving institution for inbound foreign investment capital into a Foreign-Invested Partnership. In Hong Kong, a licensed SVF or EMI may support ancillary collections, card acquiring through rails such as Stripe or Authorize.Net, and small-value cross-border payments, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 licensed bank for SWIFT clearing, letters of credit, statutory FX reporting, or profit remittance. Any EMI integration should be limited to low-value receivable acceptance and must remain subordinate to the primary bank treasury structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Hong Kong investor wires HKD or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the Foreign-Invested Partnership's SAFE-designated foreign currency capital account at Bank of China or ICBC, using the recipient BIC and FIP account number, with the payment narrative stating 'Capital injection into Foreign-Invested Partnership under SAFE FDI registration'. The receiving bank applies the SAFE BOP purpose classification for direct investment capital contribution, performs international receipts reporting, and after credit the FIP may voluntarily settle the funds into RMB for permitted operating expenses. A licensed Mainland CPA then issues a capital verification report and the bank updates the SAFE/FDI registration record. Capital contributions must be sent in a single classified wire rather than split into small payments to avoid trade-credit or structuring triggers.Outbound trade disbursements for raw materials, holographic foils, printing consumables and licensing fees to Hong Kong suppliers are cleared primarily through SWIFT MT103 for invoice-based operating payments, with standard SAFE goods-trade purpose classification. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore fintech EMIs are generally not licensed banks and are legally barred from providing onshore Mainland China foreign exchange settlement, capital account custody, SAFE capital verification, or acting as the receiving institution for inbound foreign investment capital into a Foreign-Invested Partnership. In Hong Kong, a licensed SVF or EMI may support ancillary collections, card acquiring through rails such as Stripe or Authorize.Net, and small-value cross-border payments, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 licensed bank for SWIFT clearing, letters of credit, statutory FX reporting, or profit remittance. Any EMI integration should be limited to low-value receivable acceptance and must remain subordinate to the primary bank treasury structure.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A full independent audit by a Certified Public Accountant ('CPA') is compulsory for a Hong Kong entity unless an explicit statutory audit exemption applies. Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a private company may decline to appoint an auditor for a financial year only if it qualifies as a small private company and all members unanimously pass an audit-opt-out resolution. A small private company must satisfy at least two of the following three quantitative thresholds for two consecutive financial years: (i) annual turnover not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); (ii) total assets not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); and (iii) average number of employees not exceeding 50. A limited partnership is outside the Companies Ordinance audit mandate, but an audit becomes mandatory where (a) any corporate member or the partnership itself is subject to a contractual or tax requirement to produce audited financial statements; (b) the partnership's governing agreement requires an auditor's report; (c) the annual revenue, total assets, or number of partners triggers the audit threshold under an applicable licensed intermediary, bank financing, or investor covenant; or (d) the Inland Revenue Department requests audited accounts in support of the partnership tax return. Dormant entities with no significant accounting transactions may apply for dormant status and exemption from audit, but must still maintain complete books upon recommencing business.
