Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), acting through the local Municipal Administration for Market Regulation of the registered office within Mainland China; the Hong Kong SAR Companies Registry is the issuing authority for the Hong Kong investor's corporate capacity and good standing certificates, not for the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership.

Governing statute

Partnership Law of the People's Republic of China (adopted 1997, amended 2006), Articles 8, 9, 10, 12, 14 and 34; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (2019), Articles 12 and 31; Regulations on the Registration Administration of Foreign-Invested Partnerships (State Council Decree No. 567, effective 1 March 2010); Measures for the Administration of Foreign-Invested Partnership Registration; for the Hong Kong SAR investor, Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) govern corporate capacity and continuing registration evidence.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Foreign-Invested Partnership and no blocked escrow account rule under PRC law. Partners may agree on a contribution schedule in the Partnership Agreement. However, any inbound foreign investment must be settled through a duly opened domestic capital contribution account under SAFE rules, and capital contributions must be genuinely paid and booked before any distribution to partners.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), online through the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; administrative supervision is also exercised through the provincial and municipal local tax bureaus where the Foreign-Invested Partnership is domiciled.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign non-resident partners execute a partnership agreement and register the Foreign-Invested Partnership with the local office of the State Administration for Market Regulation, obtaining a Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code. Step 2: Through the multi-license integration one-stop reform, tax registration is completed simultaneously with commercial registration; within 30 days after license issuance or commencement of operations, the FIP must complete supplementary tax registration on the Electronic Tax Service Platform, file its financial accounting system record, report its bank deposit accounts, and complete real-name authentication for the legal representative, financial officer, and authorized tax agents. Step 4: The entity must apply for invoice allotment and type authorization on the fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform, thereby opening its Digital Tax Account; no separate VAT registration certificate is issued. Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual assessable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 under the STA Order No. 43 General VAT Taxpayer Registration Measures; below that threshold, the entity remains a small-scale taxpayer eligible for the statutory VAT exemption of monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 or quarterly sales up to RMB 300,000, extended by Ministry of Finance / STA Public Notice No. 19 of 2023 through 31 December 2027.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Polyresin statue goods qualify for the 0% VAT export rate when physically exported from the Chinese customs territory, provided: (i) the FIP holds general VAT taxpayer status and files the export refund/exemption mechanism applicable to manufacturing exporters (exemption, credit, and refund method under Cai Shui [2012] No. 39 and the Interim VAT Regulations); (ii) the goods are declared to and cleared by customs, with electronic clearance data successfully matched by the STA; (iii) the sale is made to an overseas customer with consideration payable in foreign currency; (iv) the goods carry lawful input VAT credits evidenced by compliant purchase invoices; and (v) export proceeds are repatriated through bank channels within the statutory 180-day window and the export revenue is declared in the VAT period, with the refund application submitted no later than 30 April of the following year. Cross-border digital supplies and offshore-related services are eligible for zero rating only under the criteria of Cai Shui [2016] No. 36, namely consumption entirely outside China and settlement in foreign currency.

The FIP must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform and its Digital Tax Account; every invoice is transmitted to the tax authority in real time at issuance as structured XML/JSON data bearing a mandatory anti-fraud digital stamp and QR code, without reliance on legacy tax-control disks; high-volume exporters may integrate via the STA enterprise digital API (Le Qi platform) for automated invoice clearance, export confirmation, and reconciliations.

Export zero-rating is continuously monitored through three-way electronic cross-validation among tax, customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange trade-forex data. The STA may defer, suspend, or verify export refunds through correspondence investigations, on-site tax audits, or asset inspections. Where zero-rating is unsubstantiated, or where export proceeds are not collected within 180 days of customs clearance without an approved statutory exception, the transaction is reclassified as an inland taxable sale: the VAT shortfall is clawed back, a late-payment surcharge accrues at 0.05% per day of the overdue tax, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax is imposed under Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law. Fabricated documentation or collusion to claim fraudulent refunds constitutes the criminal offense of fraudulent export tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, punishable by fines and imprisonment escalating to life, together with disqualification from the export refund regime and public listing on the tax dishonesty blacklist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The entity is a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership, so the convertibility regime is that of the People's Republic of China. RMB is fully convertible for current account trade items, but capital account movements remain strictly regulated. Foreign partner capital contributions require FDI registration at SAFE, a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account with a PRC bank, and the funds must be used in accordance with the filed partnership business scope. Profit distributions from accumulated partnership earnings are treated as current account transactions for remittance purposes after tax compliance, but the remitting bank must be provided with audited financial statements, a partner distribution resolution, tax clearance, and the appropriate SAFE payment and filing code. A significant profit remittance hurdle is that distributions to overseas partners require the bank to verify that the partnership has no pending Chinese tax liabilities, and the non-resident partner must provide withholding tax certificates proving that the relevant corporate or individual income tax has been paid or exempted. Surplus capital or capital reductions, by contrast, are capital account items and cannot be freely remitted without SAFE approval. There is no legal parity for automatic outbound conversion of not-yet-earned capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For physical export sales from the PRC FIP, the statutory rule is mandatory repatriation rather than mandatory surrender: all export foreign-exchange receipts must be brought back to a domestic SAFE-designated foreign exchange settlement account no later than the contracted trade payment date or, in the normal goods-trade monitoring cycle, within 90 days after export customs clearance if no different commercial term is declared. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain 100% of the proceeds in foreign currency or sell any amount to an eligible bank at market rate. The receipts must be electronically matched with the customs declaration numbers under the SAFE Goods-Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; non-repatriation, fake declarations, or excessive retention overseas trigger removal from the enterprise trusted list and escalation to physical-inspection status.

Central bank supervision: The operative central bank authority is the People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the linked Hong Kong SAR treasury and clearing node.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability and, for the PRC FIP, legally prohibited as a primary treasury account provider. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex and similar entities do not hold a PBOC/CBIRC commercial banking license in mainland China and therefore cannot open a basic RMB deposit account, receive SAFE-registered FDI capital injections, or remit profits through the SAFE current-account framework. An HK-authorised money service operator could serve an HK corporate treasury node for offshore collections and disbursements, but it cannot replace the onshore Tier-1 bank, and any offshore EMI settlement into China must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed bank under SAFE trade-monitoring rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign partner contribution funds must move via SWIFT MT103 from the HK remitter to the FIP's dedicated SAFE-registered FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a PRC Tier-1 bank. The remittance must carry a clear purpose description such as Foreign investment contribution to Foreign-Invested Partnership, and the receiving bank must report the inward transaction using the SAFE international receipts and payments transaction coding for direct-investment capital injection. After credit, the FIP should instruct the bank to convert to RMB and transfer to the basic operational account only after submitting the capital utilisation declaration. For capital verification, the FIP engages a PRC certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report; the bank's inward credit slip, SAFE foreign exchange registration form, and fund usage vouchers are the supporting dossiers. The FIP must not allow Stripe or PayPal treasury flows to be used as nominal FDI contributions.Outbound supplier and trade settlements should be executed using SWIFT MT103 direct telegraphic transfers for invoice-based goods and services, commercial letters of credit via SWIFT MT700 or MT707 for large purchase orders, and documentary collections on DP or DA terms for lower-risk repeat shipments. Each outward payment must be backed by the underlying purchase or sales contract, invoice, packing list, transport documents and customs declarations where applicable, and must be declared with the correct SAFE trade payment purpose code. For domestic suppliers and raw materials inside mainland China, the FIP should use the domestic CNY clearing rails, specifically the China National Advanced Payment System large-value real-time gross settlement rail, rather than converting foreign currency unnecessarily. For funds from Stripe and PayPal pre-order settlements received offshore, the offshore treasury account should aggregate and repatriate through MT103 to the FIP's onshore bank account with the platform payout reconciliation attached so that each aggregated receipt can be traced to identifiable customers and order events.Delivery-based RMB against foreign-exchange forward contracts with a PRC Tier-1 bank, limited to the amount and tenor of a documented trade or partnership capital exposure.Foreign-exchange swaps combining a spot transaction and a forward transaction to manage short-term liquidity between the RMB operational account and the foreign-currency capital or settlement account.Cross-currency swaps, including fixed-for-floating structures in RMB and other currencies, only when matched to an existing debt, intra-group funding, or multi-year procurement commitment in the same currencies and residual maturity.Offshore RMB non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed only by the HK parent company as an offshore hedge of expected economic exposure; the onshore PRC FIP itself is not permitted to use onshore NDFs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability and, for the PRC FIP, legally prohibited as a primary treasury account provider. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex and similar entities do not hold a PBOC/CBIRC commercial banking license in mainland China and therefore cannot open a basic RMB deposit account, receive SAFE-registered FDI capital injections, or remit profits through the SAFE current-account framework. An HK-authorised money service operator could serve an HK corporate treasury node for offshore collections and disbursements, but it cannot replace the onshore Tier-1 bank, and any offshore EMI settlement into China must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed bank under SAFE trade-monitoring rules.

Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at partnership entity level (Hong Kong FIP is fiscally transparent); partners are taxed on their allocated share at 16.5% for corporate partners and 15% for non-corporate partners, with two-tiered relief of 8.25% / 7.5% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends, profit distributions or partnership distributions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due by the date specified on the issued tax return, normally within one month of the issue date; standard issues on 1 April are due by 2 May. Tax representatives can obtain extensions based on the accounting year-end (e.g. a 31 December year-end extended due date is usually 15 August of the assessment year).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$4,000,000 per controlled transaction category for property and services, HK$4,000,000 for financing transactions, and HK$2,000,000 for intangible-controlled transactions; Master File obligations arise for taxpayers with annual gross income exceeding HK$200,000,000 that also meet the controlled transaction threshold.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) filing like the UAE or BVI, but the IRD applies strong economic-substance requirements through the territorial source doctrine, anti-avoidance rules (IRO ss. 61 and 61A), and DTA benefit limitation principles. A foreign-invested partnership that maintains no office, no staff, no board minutes and no risk-management functions in Hong Kong will fail an offshore profits claim and may be treated as a shell for treaty and AML purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Immediate penalty up to HK$10,000 plus three times the tax undercharged under IRO s.82A; 5%/10% late payment surcharges; criminal prosecution leading to a fine and imprisonment for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally and severally liable for all assessed tax and penalties; where a corporate partner acts as general partner, its directors who consented or connived are personally liable; tax warrants may lead to asset seizure, garnishment, and court-ordered travel restrictions.
Shell company / lack of economic substance

Statutory sanction: IRD will re-source 100% of the profit as Hong Kong-source taxable income; anti-avoidance provisions allow re-characterization of transactions, denial of treaty relief, and an additional GAAR penalty of up to 100% of the tax saved.

Director personal liability: The unlimited general partner is personally liable on any evasion found; directors of corporate general partners may be assessed jointly for unpaid tax; in flagrant cases, the court may issue a proprietary estoppel, freeze assets, and place the responsible individuals on a departure-prevention list.
AML / money laundering through payment rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455), conviction for dealing with tainted property carries a fine of up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 14 years, plus confiscation of the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Any partner or senior officer who knows or has reasonable grounds to believe the funds are proceeds of crime is equally liable; post-charge, defendants may be subject to travel bans, passport surrender, and asset freezing orders.
Transfer pricing non-compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment to arm's-length royalty (normally zero) under IRO s.50A; penalty for failure to keep records of up to HK$100,000; understatement negligence penalty up to three times the tax shortfall; full interest.

Director personal liability: The general partner and any shadow director of a corporate partner can be held jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax; commercial bankruptcy proceedings may be commenced against them personally to enforce the debt.
Non-filing / persistent default

Statutory sanction: IRD issues an estimated assessment using maximum presumable profits; fixed fine of HK$10,000 plus a daily penalty of HK$300; a 5% late-payment surcharge after six months and an additional 10% after one year; judgment can lead to repossession of stock and bank balances.

Director personal liability: The general partner remains personally liable even after dissolution; a tax debt certificate can be filed in court, allowing bailiffs to seize partnership and personal assets; persistent non-compliance may result in an arrest warrant and extradition proceedings if the partner flees the jurisdiction.
