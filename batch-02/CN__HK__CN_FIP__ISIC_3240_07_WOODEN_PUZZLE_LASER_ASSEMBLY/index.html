Hong Kong legislation requires a registered office in Hong Kong capable of receiving service of process and maintaining statutory records. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Limited Partnerships Ordinance imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement. Partners may contribute capital in cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement. No escrow or blocked account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Fire Services Department (FSD) confirming compliance with the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572) and the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95). Structural load inspection certificate from the Buildings Department (BD) under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) to ensure the premises can support the weight of laser cutting machinery and storage racks. Workplace safety approval from the Labour Department under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) covering machine guarding, laser safety interlocks, and emergency evacuation plans.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The Limited Partnerships Ordinance imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement. Partners may contribute capital in cash, property, or services as agreed in the partnership agreement. No escrow or blocked account is mandated.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA). The mandatory online electronic portal is the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform, accessible through the official STA website and administered by provincial and municipal tax authorities; it is used for tax registration, VAT filing, invoice issuance, and export VAT refund applications.

E-government filing portal

For a mainland China-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership (CN_FIP), the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the Administration for Market Regulation in the business license is the statutory taxpayer identification number; no separate VAT ID is issued. Procedure: (1) Complete business registration of the FIP and receive the USCC. (2) Within 30 days, complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting the partnership agreement, articles of partnership, foreign investor identity and legal existence documents, capital contribution schedule, place of effective management and principal place of business details, tax representative/agent appointment, and the expected business scope. (3) If the FIP is a non-resident enterprise with a Chinese permanent establishment, the competent STA branch assigns/confirms a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Identification Number upon filing; provide the overseas registration certificate, board resolution on Chinese PE/business activities, tax residence certificate, and the completed non-resident tax registration application in the Electronic Tax Bureau. (4) The USCC recorded in the tax registration is the single TIN/VAT identifier for all VAT and income tax filings. A pure non-resident seller without PE or registration in China does not obtain a Chinese TIN; the Chinese purchaser or marketplace is required to withhold VAT at the applicable rate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box election or a general hybrid-mismatch election for foreign-invested partnerships. Under Chinese Enterprise Income Tax rules, the partnership itself is not an entity-level income taxpayer; profits are allocated to and taxed at the partner level, while the FIP remains a VAT taxpayer if it makes taxable supplies in China. Treaty-driven permanent establishment outcomes are factual determinations, not elective classifications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales (VAT-exclusive) exceeding RMB 5,000,000 requires registration as a general VAT taxpayer. A mainland China entity is required to complete tax registration and to charge/remit VAT from its first taxable supply in China; there is no monetary threshold that exempts an established Chinese FIP from registration. Small-scale taxpayers with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may use simplified levies; non-resident suppliers without a Chinese establishment do not register but are subject to withholding by the domestic recipient or platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods physically declared to and cleared by China Customs for destinations outside mainland China, including outbound sales to Hong Kong SAR, qualify for zero-rated VAT only when: (a) the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer; (b) the goods are included in the export tax rebate scope and are not on any prohibited-export/rebate list; (c) the export contract, purchase order, customs declaration, transport document, and commercial invoice are consistent; (d) the sales consideration is settled through banks or licensed payment service providers and repatriated to China in accordance with SAFE rules; (e) the export proceeds are received within the statutory period after export; and (f) the FIP retains the complete digital/paper documentation trail for matching by the STA and China Customs. Where the applicable export rebate rate for the HS classification is below the input VAT rate, the zero-rating refund is limited to the published rebate rate.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (the statutory export declaration; export counterpart of a bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or cross-border land carriage cargo manifest evidencing physical departure from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR/overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds receipt confirmation
  • SAFE cross-border receipt/foreign-exchange verification record
  • Export contract/purchase order and Amazon Pay/Stripe settlement reconciliation
  • VAT special invoice or purchase invoice trail supporting input VAT and the export rebate calculation

China has a fully digitalized VAT invoice chain. Taxpayers must issue invoices through the State Taxation Administration's unified electronic invoice platform, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA. General VAT taxpayers must use the national e-invoice system for Special VAT Invoices, and all invoices are subject to the Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud data-matching framework, including digital tax-authority stamping/clearance. The rules require real-time alignment of buyer tax IDs, output VAT rates, price/tax separation, customs codes, and export declaration data; invoices that fail automated fiscal clearance cannot be used to support input VAT credits or export rebates.

The STA, China Customs, and SAFE perform cross-system audits under the Golden Tax Phase IV data-matching framework. If an export zero-rating claim fails documentation or repatriation tests, the tax authority will disallow the zero rate, apply the standard VAT rate retroactively, recover any refund paid, impose daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% on the overdue amount, and assess administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times the evaded or fraudulently refunded tax under the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Repeated or intentional false export/invoice matching failures may also trigger export-tax-rebate disqualification, withholding of future refunds, and referral to the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export tax refund offenses.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible currency regime with no capital account controls on the Hong Kong dollar. The linked exchange rate system ties HKD to USD within a 7.75 to 7.85 band, and the HKMA guarantees full convertibility of the monetary base. Both current account and capital account transactions may be freely settled in HKD, USD, CNY in the offshore CNH pool, EUR, and other major currencies. There are no surrender requirements, no central approval for profit repatriation, and no statutory limitation on dividends or partnership distribution remittances. The practical hurdles are therefore not regulatory FX restrictions but bank-level KYC, source-of-funds proof, beneficial ownership identification, CRS and FATCA reporting, and economic substance verification. For the mainland investor injecting capital into this FIP, SAFE outbound ODI filing, MOFCOM registration, and NDRC filing must be evidenced; in the absence of these onshore approvals, the HK receiving bank will treat the inbound remittance as potentially undeclared capital and may hold it pending compliance clearance. Export proceeds earned by the FIP can be retained freely, converted at negotiated bank rates, or hedged under permitted derivatives. The only meaningful convertibility constraint is upstream in the PRC capital account, not in Hong Kong.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios exist in Hong Kong. Export foreign currency proceeds collected through the FIP's Hong Kong bank accounts may be retained in original currency, converted to HKD, or used for settlement without any HKMA-prescribed timeline. For any mainland affiliate receiving export proceeds onshore under SAFE current account rules, the standard requirement is repatriation of the foreign currency into the domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days of customs declaration; the company may retain the proceeds in foreign currency and no mandatory conversion ratio applies, though voluntary conversion to RMB is permitted.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking authority under the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Government, operating under the Banking Ordinance and the Exchange Fund Ordinance; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China is relevant only for regulating the upstream outbound investment filing of the mainland Chinese investor entering the Hong Kong FIP, not for Hong Kong onshore FX operations.
