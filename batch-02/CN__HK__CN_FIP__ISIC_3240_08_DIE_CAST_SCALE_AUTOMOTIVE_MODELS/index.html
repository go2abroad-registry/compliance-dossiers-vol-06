Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China - Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; for any Hong Kong SAR permanent establishment or branch, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) separately administers Business Registration at https://www.ird.gov.hk

        
E-government filing portal
The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is formed through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code that serves as the entity's tax identification number (TIN). Tax registration is completed automatically through the SAMR-to-STA data exchange; the FIP must then activate access to the STA e-Tax Bureau, complete real-name authentication of the legal representative/principal responsible person and the designated financial/tax officer, and file bank account and payroll details. Foreign non-resident partners are not issued a separate PRC TIN; they are assigned an internal tax file number when they register for China-source income tax on allocable partnership profits or claim treaty/CEPA relief. If the FIP opens a Hong Kong SAR sales or logistics office, it must separately procure a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate from the IRD within one month of commencement of business; this is not a VAT registration because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible. Under PRC income tax law, the FIP is fiscally transparent and is not an enterprise income tax taxpayer; each partner is separately assessable on allocable profits, whether resident enterprise partner, non-resident enterprise partner, or individual partner. For PRC VAT purposes, however, the FIP is a standalone VAT taxable unit and must comply with VAT registration, invoicing, and export zero-rating rules. The Hong Kong SAR dimension is an offshore special territory within China's sovereign framework but with a separate customs and indirect tax system.

        
Available election forms
No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election is available under PRC law; pass-through treatment for partnerships is mandatory. The principal statutory election relevant to indirect tax is the VAT general taxpayer registration election under the PRC VAT Implementing Regulations: a small-scale taxpayer may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status to claim input VAT credits and export VAT refunds, while annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5 million make general taxpayer registration mandatory. No offshore tax-exempt zone election is available to a mainland China FIP.

Cross-border e-commerce export trade modes are eligible if declared under the applicable customs trade supervision code. The FIP must file an export tax refund/rebate declaration through the STA e-Tax Bureau and satisfy the matching of customs declaration data, VAT invoice data, and foreign exchange collection data.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China follows a managed floating RMB exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility and strict capital-account controls. Current-account transactions such as trade receipts and payments must be settled through a bank when the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration and tax documents are presented. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership formed in mainland China with overseas limited partners, all inbound foreign direct investment must first be registered with SAFE through the local branch; the capital must be remitted from an overseas bank directly into the FIP's designated foreign-exchange capital account in the name of the partnership. The receiving bank will match the remittance to the SAFE FDI registration and then allow conversion into RMB through the China Foreign Exchange Trade System central parity. Capital-account convertibility is not automatic: the FIP cannot freely move RMB out for dividend or shareholder-loan purposes without the relevant SAFE filing, audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance and, for foreign exchange purchases, classification in the SAFE business information system. Profit remittances are recognised on a current-account basis once tax and audit proofs are provided, but the overseas partner must not treat the partnership as a pure tax-transparent envelope without substance. Export proceeds from die-cast scale automotive models must be repatriated promptly into China; while China no longer imposes an across-the-board mandatory surrender ratio, all receipts and settlements must be reported, and any failure to bring export proceeds back will trigger trade-credit investigation and possible inclusion in the SAFE attention list. Hedging activities are limited to genuine exposures against the partnership's actual commercial or financing flows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China and credited to the FIP's foreign-exchange settlement account within the trade-contract payment terms, with overdue items beyond 90 days requiring an explanation and supporting evidence to the settlement bank and SAFE. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE will-based settlement regime; the FIP may retain 100 percent of export proceeds in foreign currency and convert only the amounts needed for RMB operating costs, provided each transaction is reported through the SAFE digital system with authentic customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading and receipts.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulates the offshore CNH clearing and settlement leg in Hong Kong SAR.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The overseas partner must first complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration. The remitting bank sends a SWIFT MT103 from the partner's own corporate bank account to the FIP's designated foreign-exchange capital account, referencing the SAFE FDI registration number and the SAFE transaction classification code for inbound direct investment into a foreign-invested partnership. The receiving bank performs name-matching, checks the remitter against the registered partner list, and then settles the incoming currency at the onshore spot RMB rate. After settlement, the RMB must be credited to the capital account settlement sub-account and used only for approved purposes aligned with the partnership's business scope; a Chinese CPA must later issue the capital verification report and the FIP must file the FDI information with the Ministry of Commerce and the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System.Trade settlements should run through the Tier-1 bank's international settlement department. For standard supplier payments, use SWIFT MT103 with full invoicing and contract data. For large upfront purchases of zinc ingots, molten alloy or custom mould steel, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit opened under a trusted advising bank, protecting both importer and exporter. For recurring low-value supplies, documentary collections on a documents-against-payment or documents-against-acceptance basis are acceptable and cheaper than an L/C. All outbound RMB payments into Hong Kong or overseas CNH accounts should be processed through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or the bank's CNH clearing connection to HKMA's Clearing House Automated Transfer System (CHATS), with the underlying validated trade documents preserved for mandatory SAFE trade authenticity verification.Onshore USD/CNY deliverable foreign exchange forwards, permissible only with a proven trade or capital-account exposure, a maximum tenor matching the underlying contract, and no speculative netting.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps used for liquidity alignment between the FIP's RMB settlement account and foreign-currency obligations, subject to SAFE approval and genuine economic purpose.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards available to the Hong Kong parent or treasury centre hedging CNH exposure to the mainland partnership; such NDFs must be executed outside mainland China and may not be booked by the onshore FIP.Currency options may also be purchased from the domestic settlement bank when embedded in hedging programmes approved under the SAFE real-demand rule, but option writing or leveraged structures are prohibited.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is low for capital-account use and only marginal for auxiliary current-account collections. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex and Payoneer do not hold PBOC/NFRA banking licences and are legally barred from operating as the primary depository of a mainland-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership or from receiving SAFE-registered inbound direct investment. They may act solely as invoice-level trade receivable collection facilitators for marketplace sales when partnered with a licensed Chinese commercial bank or a qualified cross-border payment institution; they cannot replace the Tier-1 bank clearing account for capital verification, profit remittance or trade settlement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
16.5% for a corporate partner's share of partnership profits; 15% for an individual partner's share; the Hong Kong SAR FIP itself is fiscally transparent and is not a separate corporation tax subject

0% - Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividend distributions by a Hong Kong company or by a pass-through partnership; the FIP may distribute profits to non-resident partners free of Hong Kong dividend withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Under DIPN 58 and IRO Division 15A, contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation (Local File) is expected where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 10 million in a basis period; a Master File is expected for the MNE group if consolidated group revenue exceeds HKD 6.8 billion (EUR 750 million equivalent); Country-by-Country reporting applies to MNE groups with consolidated revenue above HKD 6.8 billion. The die-cast FIP must maintain TP documentation covering intercompany purchases of zinc-alloy components, brand/royalty payments, management fees, and shareholder debt.

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the territorial-source rules, the foreign-sourced income exemption (FSIE), and DTA anti-abuse doctrine require the HK FIP to have real economic substance in Hong Kong. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the zinc alloy die-casting business include strategic sourcing of raw materials, ownership and custody of moulds, production planning, quality control, pricing to distributors, management of warranty risk and final approval of contracts; those activities must be performed by Hong Kong staff, not by the Mainland toll manufacturer or by an offshore nominee, otherwise the offshore-claim and treaty benefits will be denied.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax evasion via false offshore income claim and sham Hong Kong substance
        
Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(2) and s.82(1): on conviction, a fine of up to HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus an additional tax penalty equal to three times the amount of tax undercharged; the IRD may also issue an estimated assessment with 5%/10% late-payment surcharges and levy default interest.

        
Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil where the partnership is a mere facade; the general partner and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax, penalties and interest; personal civil asset restitution and confiscation orders are available under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455); a departure restriction / travel ban may be imposed; directors face disqualification for up to 15 years and exposure to extradition-related criminal proceedings.

      

      
      

        Transfer pricing manipulation and artificial intercompany financing
        
Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAF empowers the IRD to make transfer-pricing adjustments, disallow inflated interest deductions and recharacterize the controlled transaction; penalties for failure to maintain transfer-pricing documentation can reach 10% of the undercharged tax; if the misstatement is fraudulent or deliberate, IRO s.82 imposes a fine up to HKD 50,000, three times the undercharged tax and imprisonment up to 3 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who sign false or misleading profits tax returns can be held personally liable for the adjusted tax; courts may lift the veil to recover assets transferred to connected persons; directors risk civil restitution, blacklisting and departure prohibition, disqualification, and criminal liability under IRO s.82, with confiscation of the proceeds of non-arm's-length transactions.

      

      
      

        AML / shell-company abuse through SWIFT and PayPal payment rails
        
Statutory sanction: Under Cap. 615 s.5(1), carrying on a money service operation without a licence is punishable by a fine up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25, dealing with property known to represent proceeds of an indictable offence carries a fine up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; the court may also impose confiscation orders and asset freezes.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the FIP is used as a cloak for illicit fund flows; directors, partners and shadow managers face personal confiscation of assets held in Hong Kong and offshore, travel bans and blacklisting by the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit, director disqualification, and a real risk of extradition to Mainland China on cross-border economic crime charges; criminal imprisonment of up to 14 years applies for money laundering.
