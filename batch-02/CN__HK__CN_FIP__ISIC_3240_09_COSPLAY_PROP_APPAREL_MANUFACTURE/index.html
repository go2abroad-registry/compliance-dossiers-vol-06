Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Hong Kong partnership or on a Mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership. No blocked escrow capital account is required by the registry. The executed partnership agreement must state the subscribed capital, contribution schedule, profit-sharing ratio, and partner duties; contributions may be made in cash, machinery, inventory, or intellectual property. Once received, contributions are immediately available for lawful business purposes and must be recorded in the partnership capital ledger. For licensed manufacturing operations, the entity must maintain working capital sufficient to meet workers' compensation, environmental compliance, and Civil Defense obligations, but the registrar does not prescribe any minimum escrowed amount.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department approval is required for means of escape, means of access for fire appliances, fire-resisting construction, emergency lighting, exit signage, and portable extinguishers under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572) and the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95). Civil Defense clearance must be issued before factory occupation, including confirmation of building structural safety under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), emergency power and fire mains, and a Dangerous Goods Licence for any stored solvents or adhesives. The Labour Department must issue the factory registration certificate and verify compliance with the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509), covering risk assessment, machine guarding, chemical hazard communication, personal protective equipment, and the written safety policy.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a Hong Kong partnership or on a Mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership. No blocked escrow capital account is required by the registry. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; e-Tax electronic services portal (eTAX) at https://www.ird.gov.hk; Business Registration Office under IRD (IRDBR portal for business registration)

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), a foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong as a partnership must, within one month of commencing business, lodge a notice of application for registration with the Business Registration Office using the prescribed form together with a copy of the partnership agreement, identity proofs of all partners (including non-resident individuals and entities), the principal business address in Hong Kong, and details of the person who is the business's agent or authorized representative. The Business Registration Office will issue a Business Registration Certificate displaying a unique Business Registration Number (BR number); this BR number serves as the entity's tax identifier for all Hong Kong tax filings. Because a partnership is fiscally transparent, each partner also registers with the IRD under his/her own Personal/HKID or Corporate Taxpayer File Number, where applicable, and the partnership's BR number is used for payroll returns and partnership information reporting. Non-resident partners without a local address must appoint a person resident in Hong Kong to act as their authorised representative for service of notices and tax correspondence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance and the Partnership Ordinance do not provide for any check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The partnership itself is not a taxpayer; profits are assessed directly on the individual partners in proportion to their profit shares. Individual partners may elect Personal Assessment regarding their total income, but this is an individual-level election, not an entity-level pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax. Hong Kong levies duty only on specific dutiable commodities (tobacco, liquors, hydrocarbon oils and methyl alcohol) under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in HKD. There is no turnover threshold triggering registration for any general indirect tax because none exists. Registration/licensing requirements under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance apply only to manufacturers/importers of dutiable commodities and are not based on turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for export in a VAT/GST sense is inapplicable because Hong Kong does not have a VAT/GST. For legal exports under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), goods are generally duty-free; only commodities specified in the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance are dutiable and require licenses. Thus, a cosplay prop/apparel manufacturer exporting manufactured goods from Hong Kong will not be charged indirect tax on export, and no refund mechanism is triggered. For services, a claim that income is offshore (and thus not chargeable to profits tax) must be supported by evidence that the contract is negotiated/executed and the services are rendered wholly outside Hong Kong; this is an income-tax treatment, not a zero-rating refund.

  • Electronic Export Declaration submitted to Hong Kong Customs through the trade electronic submission system (e.g., 'Trade and Customs' or licensed service provider platform) in accordance with the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations
  • Bill of Lading (for sea export) or Air Waybill (for air export) evidencing carriage of goods
  • Commercial Invoice containing full consignor/consignee details, ISIC 3240 description of goods (licensed theatrical costume robes, synthetic wigs, cosplay props/apparel), quantity, unit price, sales terms and total value
  • Packing List with gross/net weights, number of packages, container numbers and seals
  • Sales contract or purchase order and, where applicable, licensing/royalty agreements to evidence the underlying commercial transaction and the business substance

No real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in Hong Kong. There is no compulsory B2B/B2C e-invoicing system and no tax-authority clearance database for ordinary invoices. However, all importers/exporters must submit electronic import/export declarations through the Hong Kong Customs and excise-licensed electronic trade document submission platforms (e.g., Trade and Customs), which serve as customs clearance documentation, not as tax clearance. Businesses are required to retain supporting documents, including invoices, for at least 7 years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Because no export VAT refund is available, the same clawback regime does not exist. However, false or inaccurate export declarations can trigger criminal liability under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), with fines and imprisonment. If a taxpayer claims offshore income exemption for export-related services, the IRD will audit the commercial substance, place of negotiation and performance, and may deny the claim; under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (sections 82, 82A and 80), fraudulent or negligent misstatements are subject to heavy monetary penalties and potential prosecution. Additionally, the Commissioner can issue assessments within the statutory limitation period (e.g., up to 6 years, or longer in fraud cases) to recover any tax properly chargeable.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a currency board system under the Linked Exchange Rate mechanism, with the HKMA maintaining the HKD range of 7.75 to 7.85 per USD. There are no exchange control regulations, no surrender obligations, and no approval requirements for current or capital account transfers. The HKD is freely convertible and profit repatriation, dividend payments and repayment of shareholder loans can be executed through licensed banks without prior regulatory permit. However, because the partnership is Foreign-Invested and capital arises from mainland China, the intermediate compliance layers of SAFE and the People's Bank of China may impose ODI filings, export-import payment verification and pre-settlement currency matching. The bank must verify that the Chinese resident partners have completed the SAFE overseas investment foreign exchange registration and that the remittance purpose code is strictly compatible with the capital nature. For liquidity, HK-based accounts can hold multiple currencies, and no forced repatriation of export proceeds applies to Hong Kong entities. This creates a bifurcated regime where the Hong Kong treasury function is fully liberalized but any upstream injection from mainland China must clear the FX gate of SAFE and the commercial bank's remittance compliance desk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio exists in Hong Kong for export foreign currency receipts. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any currency, held in multicurrency bank accounts, or converted directly through the interbank market. For goods manufactured in mainland China and sold from the Hong Kong trading entity, the mainland manufacturer may be subject to SAFE's 5-day export proceeds repatriation rule and the requirement to settle or retain in an approved foreign currency account; however, that rule does not apply directly to the Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) with regulatory oversight by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China for cross-border renminbi remittances and mainland capital flows

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from mainland China to the Hong Kong FIP must be routed through the SAFE ODI framework. The Chinese partner must obtain a certificate of foreign exchange registration for outbound direct investment, file with NDRC if applicable, and instruct a PRC licensed bank to remit via SWIFT MT103 using the ODI purpose code. The Hong Kong receiving bank will credit the funds to a segregated capital escrow account; immediately after value is confirmed, a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification statement. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong general and limited partnerships are not mandatorily required to file audited financial statements merely because of turnover, total assets, or headcount; the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37) and the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) do not impose an audit obligation for private partnerships. A certified independent CPA audit becomes compulsory when the partnership (i) holds a licence under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571) with annual financial statement filing obligations; (ii) carries on banking, insurance, or money-lending activities requiring audited accounts; (iii) is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund (Cap. 637) whose constituent agreement or the applicable regulator requires an annual audit; or (iv) has financing covenants or a partnership agreement that oblige an audit. For comparison, a Hong Kong private company must be audited unless it is eligible for the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) audit exemption, which requires total annual revenue not exceeding HK$10 million and total assets not exceeding HK$10 million; if those thresholds are exceeded, audit is compulsory.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for unincorporated businesses (Foreign-Invested Partnership); 16.5% for corporations; two-tiered relief: 7.5% or 8.25% on assessable profits up to HK$2,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax, profits tax, or any dividend tax on distributions by Hong Kong companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Per IRD DIPN 59, contemporaneous TP documentation (Local File) is mandatory for controlled transactions above HK$10 million for goods, HK$5 million for services, HK$5 million for intangibles, and HK$10 million for financial transactions; a Master File / CbC notification is required for MNEs with consolidated group revenue at or above HK$6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone ESR statute, but the territorial source rule and DTA principle-purpose test deny treaty benefits to FIPs lacking real business substance; an offshore shell without staff/office in HK will be disregarded, transactions re-characterized, and profits assessed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Wilful Intent)

Statutory sanction: On summary conviction: fine up to HK$50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, plus a shortfall penalty of three times the tax undercharged; on indictment, penalties escalate and daily default penalties may apply.

Director personal liability: Partners or director-equivalent managers who authorize or connive in evasion are personally and jointly liable for penalties; the IRD may obtain court-ordered travel restrictions/departure warrants and imprisonment of controlling persons.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRD may make transfer-pricing adjustments, increase assessable profits, impose additional tax, and apply penalties up to HK$50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged for non-compliance with s.51C/Division 82A obligations.

Director personal liability: Responsible partners/directors who signed false records or deliberately structured non-arm's length prices may be held personally liable for adjusted tax and penalized under s.82A; directors risk disqualification and criminal proceedings.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, re-characterization of the FIP as a conduit, assessment of 16.5% (or 15%) on deemed HK-source profits, and penalties for inaccurate returns; overseas anti-abuse rules may disallow benefits retroactively.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the partnership veil and hold the controlling partners/directors jointly liable for the full tax underpaid plus penalties; sham-conduit findings can trigger criminal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation and cross-border restraint orders.
AML / Record-Keeping Failure

Statutory sanction: Under s.51C IRO, failure to keep records is an offence: fine up to HK$5,000 and a daily penalty of HK$100 for continuing default; coupled with inaccurate returns, it attracts evasion penalties as above.

Director personal liability: Partners/directors responsible for accounting controls are personally liable for fines; the regulator may impose compliance orders, and in serious cases apply disqualification orders and criminal sanctions.
