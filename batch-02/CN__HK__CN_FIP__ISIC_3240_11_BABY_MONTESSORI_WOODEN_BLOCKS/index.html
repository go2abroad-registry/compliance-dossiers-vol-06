Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A bare virtual office comprising only a postal address will fail to satisfy the Companies Registry's registered-office requirement under section 5 of the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), because the partnership must have a physical registered office in Hong Kong where communications and legal notices can be served. More importantly, major Hong Kong banks (HSBC, Standard Chartered, Bank of China (Hong Kong)) systematically reject virtual-office addresses when opening accounts for e-commerce businesses using Amazon Pay and Stripe, as such addresses lack operating substance, cannot receive physical banker's drafts, and are strongly correlated with money-mule activity. The Inland Revenue Department similarly expects a genuine business address for the Business Registration Certificate and will treat a letter-box address as a red flag for a lack of commercial substance, potentially leading to denial of territorial-source profits claims. To maintain the low AML risk tier and secure a bank reference letter, the partnership should lease a physical serviced office with dedicated mailbox, telephone line, and meeting-room access (USD 300-USD 600 per month in core districts), and should separately maintain an operational or warehouse address in the supply-chain territory.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law for a foreign-invested limited partnership. The Partnership may lawfully commence registration with zero minimum paid-up capital. However, each partner must contribute the amount specified in the partnership agreement, and a limited partner may contribute cash, tangible assets, intellectual property, land-use use rights, or other property rights, while only a general partner may contribute services. No blocked escrow account is mandated by the central registration authority, but the partnership agreement may establish a capital call escrow, and the commercial bank opening the basic deposit account may impose internal AML requirements including source-of-funds verification for the contributed capital.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: China Tax Service (Chinese Electronic Tax Bureau) at https://tpass.chinatax.gov.cn/; export and customs interface via the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC) and the China International Trade Single Window at https://www.singlewindow.cn/

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor must first establish the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the local Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR-issued business license carries the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which automatically constitutes the FIP's national Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Within 30 days of business license issuance, the FIP must complete supplementary tax registration with the competent State Taxation Administration bureau through the electronic tax portal, supplying the partnership deed, investor identities, registered address, accounting basis, and bank account details. The tax authority activates the income tax, VAT, and other statutory tax obligations. The FIP should then apply for general VAT taxpayer status, which is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or when export tax refund and zero-rating are intended, and must obtain e-invoice issuing capability through the tax U-Key or digital certificate. Non-resident partners without a PRC tax identity are recorded in the tax file and may receive an STA-assigned non-resident identifier for withholding and pass-through reporting; the FIP itself remains the VAT taxpayer of record.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification; no monetary threshold for basic tax registration because a licensed FIP must register with the tax authority regardless of turnover. For export zero-rating, the FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim the 0% VAT export rate for tangible goods only if it is a general VAT taxpayer and the goods are: (1) actually declared to GACC and leave the PRC customs territory under a lawful export trade mode, including special cross-border e-commerce supervision codes 9610 for retail or 9710/9810 for B2B exports; (2) sold to an overseas buyer for consideration; (3) fully shipped; (4) subject to foreign-exchange receipt repatriation; (5) supported by complete commercial, logistics, and accounting records; and (6) not excluded from the export tax refund catalogue. Qualifying offshore services consumed outside China may also be zero-rated on statutory conditions.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (Customs Declaration of Exported Goods) issued and processed by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China, including the customs declaration number
  • Full set of transport documentation: original Bill of Lading for sea shipments, Air Waybill for air shipments, or customs-accepted courier/consignment note for cross-border e-commerce parcels
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, VAT invoice for exports, and, where applicable, the export inspection or commodity certificate for wooden children's toys
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt record from a Chinese authorized bank, or an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) where applicable, evidencing repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory compliance period

All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be issued through the STA-controlled electronic invoice platform using the taxpayer's Tax U-Key or digital certificate. Invoice metadata is reported in near-real-time to the national Golden Tax system, and each invoice carries a unique invoice code, invoice number, and anti-counterfeit QR code. No physical fiscal printer stamp is required. The FIP's invoice issuance must be reconciled with VAT returns, customs export declarations, and foreign exchange receipts; invoice data not transmitted to the tax authority is not valid input-credit or export-refund evidence.

Tax authorities and customs jointly scrutinise export declarations, logistics documents, VAT invoices, and foreign-exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export or compliant foreign-exchange repatriation, the refund is clawed back, VAT is assessed at the standard rate of 13%, and overdue interest accrues at 0.05% per day. Under the PRC Tax Administration Law, fraudulent VAT refunds or export-tax-refund claims attract fines from 50% to 500% of the refunded or evaded tax, and criminal liability may arise for export-tax-refund fraud. The FIP may also suffer suspension of export VAT refund eligibility, downgraded tax credit, enhanced invoice controls, and customs penalties for false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a free and fully convertible capital account under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with no surrender requirements, no prior approval for current account transactions, and no restriction on profit remittances. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible into US dollars within the 7.75 to 7.85 band, and the Renminbi is eligible for offshore conversion under CNH rules. However, because the underlying manufacturing and trade flows relate to the People's Republic of China, the foreign-invested partnership must treat SAFE as the controlling authority for any capital injection into mainland entities, repatriation of dividends, and settlement of cross-border trade. Under SAFE Circular 16 and the Foreign Investment Negative List, a China FIP is allowed to open a capital account escrow account at a designated domestic bank and to convert registered capital into Renminbi upon capital verification. Profit remittance is permitted after the annual audit, tax clearance, and submission of the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. The practical hurdles include strict beneficial ownership disclosure, economic substance assessment under Hong Kong territorial tax rules, and SAFE's prohibition on fictitious trade financing. The FIP must maintain a complete documentation trail connecting bank inflows, customs export declarations, export proceeds, and downstream receivables from Amazon and Stripe settlement channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 28 and current PRC practice, exporters have no mandatory conversion ratio and no surrender requirement for export receipts; however, all export proceeds must be repatriated through a designated bank into an approved foreign currency account within 90 days after the customs declaration date, unless an extension is explicitly granted. The receipts must be fully reconciled against the trade monitoring system using the matching customs record, invoice, logistics proof, and payment evidence.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to nil. Although Hong Kong does not prohibit licensed digital banks from serving partnerships, the foreign-invested cross-border structure with mainland trade flows demands physical branch banking, trade finance limits, and capital escrow capabilities that digital-only EMIs generally lack. More critically, mainstream fintech e-money institutions such as Airwallex or Currenxie are not licensed as full banks in Hong Kong for capital account services and are legally barred from performing statutory capital verification, SAFE registration, and large-value cross-border letters of credit. Fintech EMIs are not viable for the capital escrow or high-value disbursement rails; they may only support low-value e-commerce auxiliary flows where expressly permitted as a payment service provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions from foreign or Hong Kong partners into the China FIP must be routed through a SAFE-designated bank. The compliant process is: obtain the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate; open a capital account at a domestic SAFE-designated bank; execute an inward remittance via SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate purpose code for conversion to Renminbi or foreign currency retention; allow the bank to submit the transaction to SAFE through the RMB Cross-Border Payment Management Information System (RCPMIS); after funds arrive in the capital escrow account, proceed with capital verification and then convert or deploy the funds strictly in line with the Foreign Investment Negative List. No settlement through personal bank accounts or unlicensed payment intermediaries is permitted.Trade settlements to global buyers and raw material suppliers should use SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for payments denominated in USD, EUR, or HKD; commercial letters of credit for larger Amazon Fulfillment and retail bulk orders; and documentary collections for buyers in emerging markets requiring lower-cost settlement. For cross-border Renminbi flows, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for direct RMB clearing, with Hong Kong banks connecting through CHATS and mainland clearing banks. All outgoing disbursements must reference the corresponding trade purpose code and be supported by invoices, transport documents, and customs declarations to maintain SAFE and bank audit readiness.Onshore CNY/USD forward contracts up to 12 months for covered trade flows and committed capital payments, requiring real transaction documentation.Offshore CNH/USD non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for entities with no onshore hedging capability, within the limits prescribed by HKMA and SAFE for participating banks.Cross-currency swaps between USD and RMB for capital account conversions approved by SAFE, where the underlying exposure is a genuine foreign direct investment or trade obligation.A plain-vanilla currency options overlay is not recommended for initial treasury setup because of the complex MRM rules under SAFE; all derivative transactions must be executed with Tier-1 banks that hold a relevant derivatives licence in Hong Kong and mainland China.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to nil. Although Hong Kong does not prohibit licensed digital banks from serving partnerships, the foreign-invested cross-border structure with mainland trade flows demands physical branch banking, trade finance limits, and capital escrow capabilities that digital-only EMIs generally lack. More critically, mainstream fintech e-money institutions such as Airwallex or Currenxie are not licensed as full banks in Hong Kong for capital account services and are legally barred from performing statutory capital verification, SAFE registration, and large-value cross-border letters of credit. Fintech EMIs are not viable for the capital escrow or high-value disbursement rails; they may only support low-value e-commerce auxiliary flows where expressly permitted as a payment service provider.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder with no permanent establishment in China (PRC CIT Law Article 37). For a Chinese FIP, no entity-level dividend is paid; each partner is taxed on allocable profit. However, if the tax authority recharacterizes the FIP as a corporate vehicle or treats the profit distribution as a deemed dividend, a 10% WHT may apply. Under the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement (DTA), the dividend WHT is reduced to 5% where the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company that directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the PRC payer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Chinese FIP must prepare a Local File if its related-party transactions in any category exceed the following local currency thresholds: tangible asset ownership or use transfer >= RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers >= RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers >= RMB 100 million; provision of services >= RMB 40 million; interest-bearing financing >= RMB 50 million. A Master File is mandatory where the FIP is part of an MNE group with annual cross-border related-party transactions of RMB 1 billion or more, or where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue reaches the statutory threshold. The annual Controlled Transaction Report must be attached to the CIT annual return in all cases involving related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Under PRC CIT Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR), the tax authority will pierce an entity or arrangement lacking commercial substance. The Chinese FIP, as the manufacturer and seller of Montessori blocks, must perform real economic activities in China (procurement, production, inventory, delivery, customer support) to avoid recharacterization as a conduit. For Hong Kong, if the entity is a member of a multinational group, it may also be subject to the Hong Kong Economic Substance Requirement under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2021 for its relevant activity of holding intellectual property or financing; to the extent the HK entity is only a dormant holding company, the Economic Substance Return and notification filings must demonstrate adequate premises and employees in Hong Kong. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a trading or IP royalty business must take place in Hong Kong for treaty benefits to be preserved.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Bookkeeping on Cross-Border E-Commerce Sales

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: order to pay tax and overdue interest, plus a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. PRC Criminal Law Article 201: for tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax due, up to 3 years' imprisonment; if the amount is huge (RMB 2 million or more) and the percentage is large, 3-7 years' imprisonment plus a fine.

Director personal liability: The Chinese general partner's directors and the Hong Kong partner's directors may be personally and severally liable for unpaid taxes if they directed or acquiesced in the evasion. Courts may issue civil asset-freezing orders, exit bans (travel blacklisting), property seizure, and criminal detention. Under PRC Company Law principles and partnership rules, directors can be held liable to contribute personal assets for losses caused by fraudulent conduct; a criminal conviction also results in disqualification from holding director positions.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Hidden Permanent Establishment

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 41 and Implementation Regulation Article 123 empower the tax authority to make a transfer pricing adjustment with a 5-year retroactive period (10 years in serious cases). The underpaid CIT is assessed with a 0.05% daily surcharge and, if no contemporaneous TP documentation exists, a 50%-200% adjustment penalty may be imposed under the SAT transfer pricing rules; for amount adjustments exceeding prescribed thresholds, criminal false tax filing charges may also be stacked.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Chinese FIP and the Hong Kong affiliate may be personally liable for conspiracy to evade tax. The PRC tax authority may apply for a freeze on personal assets and an exit restriction. In Hong Kong, if the Inland Revenue Department finds that a director signed false profits tax returns, the director may be prosecuted for tax fraud under Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 82A, exposing them to a fine of HK$50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged and up to 3 years' imprisonment.
DTA Abuse / Shell Company / Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: China's GAAR (CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2019 No. 35) denies treaty benefits and recharacterizes the arrangement; the FIP or its shareholder is assessed with additional 25% CIT plus interest and penalties. In Hong Kong, failure to meet the Economic Substance Requirement for a relevant entity or the absence of economic substance in the IP holding activity triggers a fine of HK$500,000 for failure to file the ESR notification and a daily default fine; if the failure is done with intent to defraud, the maximum penalty is HK$1,000,000 and 3 years' imprisonment on conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed false ESR reports or Tax Residency Certificate applications may face criminal conviction, personal restitution, and disqualification. Chinese courts may impose a travel ban preventing departure from mainland China for directors failing to settle tax debts; the Hong Kong Companies Registry may void the shell company and strike its directors off the register, exposing personal liability for all outstanding taxes, penalties, and costs; if laundering proceeds through the shell, directors risk additional liability under PRC Criminal Law Articles 191 and 229, with imprisonment up to 10 years in severe money-laundering cases.
