Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: China Electronic Tax Service Platform (National Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform / e-Tax System)

E-government filing portal

Upon the Foreign-Invested Partnership obtaining its business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the tax registration is completed on a consolidated basis: the STA assigns an 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) which serves as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for tax reporting, VAT filing, and export rebate application. The FIP must, within 30 days of establishment, complete tax registration and tax-relevant information confirmation through the e-Tax Service Platform or the administrative licensing hall of the competent local STA office. For categories that include non-resident partners, each non-resident partner must separately register for tax identity purposes as a non-resident taxpayer to obtain a non-resident taxpayer code or must be covered under withholding registration by the partnership/fiscal agent, depending on the nature of the Chinese-source income. The FIP must designate a finance officer, register legal and business addresses, bind a bank account for tax payment, and enroll for electronic invoice issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; for income tax purposes the Foreign-Invested Partnership is treated as a tax-transparent partnership under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax rules, meaning that partnership-level taxable income is not subject to Enterprise Income Tax at the entity level and each Chinese-resident or non-resident partner is taxed on its allocable share. For indirect tax purposes, the FIP is nonetheless an entity-level VAT taxpayer and must issue VAT invoices, charge output VAT on domestic taxable supplies, and separately account for export sales to sustain zero-rating or export-tax-refund treatment.

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not provide a check-the-box election for partnerships or hybrid entity classification. The pass-through treatment of a foreign-invested partnership is mandatory under Chinese partnership income tax rules. No partnership-level hybrid mismatch election is available; however, the General Anti-Avoidance Rules under the Enterprise Income Tax Law may recharacterize arrangements designed to exploit tax-treaty or fiscal-residence mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All taxpayers engaged in taxable commercial activities in China must complete VAT tax registration after obtaining the business license. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales from goods and services reach RMB 5,000,000; below this amount, an entity may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment (with a reduced levy) or voluntarily register as a general taxpayer if eligible. For FPV drone exports through customs, the export sale itself is zero-rated for VAT, subject to export clearance and documentation verification, but the FIP must remain registered and file VAT returns regardless of export turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported FPV racing drones, carbon-fiber airframe assemblies, brushless motors, and related hobby-grade drone components are eligible for the PRC VAT zero rate on exported goods if: (1) the goods are physically exported from China customs territory under a valid customs export declaration; (2) the seller is a registered general VAT taxpayer with a documented export sales contract; (3) ownership has passed to the overseas buyer and risk/delivery terms are commercial exported goods; (4) export proceeds are received through a licensed bank and reported under SAFE foreign-exchange monitoring procedures; and (5) an export VAT refund (restitution of input VAT) claim is duly filed within the statutory period. The supply qualifies as a zero-rated export rather than an exempt domestic sale, thereby enabling refund of attributable input VAT on raw materials, components, energy, tooling, and logistics costs.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Export Clearance Copy) issued by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing physical shipment out of Chinese customs territory
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate / Export Proceeds Collection Receipt or foreign-exchange declaration form confirming receipt of payment from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Export sales contract/order with FOB/CIF or other applicable INCOTERM
  • VAT special invoice or export sales invoice as required by the STA for export VAT refund
  • Export tax refund application declaration files submitted through the STA export rebate electronic system
  • Any inspection or certification documentation if required by the destination market or customs control

China has implemented the full digitalized electronic invoice (Digital Electronic Invoice) regime through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Issuance requires real-time transmission to STA systems, cryptographic sealing/anti-forgery controls, unique electronic invoice numbers, and QR-code verification. Taxpayers must use official VAT invoice control systems and cannot issue invoices outside the STA-authorized platform. Special VAT invoices relevant to input-VAT deduction and export rebate claims are subject to clearance matching, blockchain-style tax-control logging, and post-issuance verification by the purchaser and STA. Any manual invoice or system-generated invoice that has not passed the STA electronic clearance channel is invalid for VAT input credits and export refund substantiation.

The STA and China Customs jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims through cross-checking of customs declared value, export invoice records, logistics data, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is disallowed because of missing or fabricated customs declarations, unsubstantiated freight movement, non-repatriation of export proceeds within the applicable window, or fictitious exports, the STA will claw back any refunded VAT, assess VAT at the standard 13% domestic rate on the export transaction value, impose late-payment surcharges, and may impose a fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax that was unduly refunded or evaded under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Cases involving fraudulent export declarations, forged bills of lading, or circular transnational logistics schemes are referred to criminal authorities under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China and may lead to imprisonment up to life, monetary penalties, confiscation, suspension of export-tax-refund status, and blacklisting of the entity and its beneficial owners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership is subject to China's managed FX regime. For current account transactions, including export proceeds from FPV racing drones and import payments for carbon fiber, brushless motors, and electronic speed controllers, convertibility is in principle full and does not require case-by-case SAFE approval; designated banks perform truthfulness verification using contracts, customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, and logistics records. For capital account transactions, convertibility is restricted. The HK partner's inbound equity contribution must be registered with SAFE under foreign direct investment rules, remitted into a designated foreign-currency capital escrow account, and may be converted into RMB only for lawful business purposes after the bank verifies the partnership deed, capital use plan, and underlying commercial activity. Profit distributions to the foreign partner are remittable abroad after the FIP has paid enterprise income tax, passed an audited financial statement review, and executed a partner-level profit distribution resolution. Practical remittance hurdles arise when the bank suspects that retained profits are speculative or round-tripped, when the FIP has not completed annual foreign investment information reporting, or when cross-border e-commerce proceeds were collected through offshore payment accounts without proper settlement through a PBOC-licensed payment institution. No mandatory surrender ratio exists for current account receipts, but all export proceeds must be repatriated within the statutory timeline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from FPV racing drone sales must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-currency current account within 90 days after the date of export customs declaration, unless a longer contract payment term is registered and accepted in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. The statutory requirement is repatriation of 100% of the declared export value; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero because China abolished compulsory surrender, allowing the FIP to retain USD or HKD in its export proceeds account or convert into RMB through its designated Tier-1 bank. If payment is collected through PayPal or Stripe, those funds must first flow through a PBOC-licensed cross-border e-commerce settlement institution and then be settled into the FIP's domestic bank account with transaction-level export reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the PRC-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong investor's offshore banking leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low direct viability for the PRC FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as PayPal, Stripe, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar non-bank entities are not PBOC-licensed banks in mainland China and therefore cannot maintain the FIP's domestic settlement account, issue RMB clearing instructions, or provide a compliant SAFE capital escrow account. For the Hong Kong incorporated investor, such EMIs may be used as a front-end collection tool, but the proceeds must be consolidated into a properly licensed cross-border settlement channel and then remitted to the FIP's designated Tier-1 bank account as export collection. Legal constraints require any payment institution engaging in cross-border e-commerce settlements for Chinese residents to hold a PBOC Payment Business License and be supervised by SAFE for foreign-exchange conversion; otherwise the route is prohibited.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK partner must instruct a SWIFT MT103 from its HK bank to the FIP's foreign-currency capital escrow account at the designated Tier-1 bank in mainland China. On the wire, the beneficiary must be exactly the FIP's legal name and the purpose clause must state 'FDI equity capital injection under SAFE Direct Investment Registration [registration number]'; the bank will classify it under the SAFE direct-investment capital code rather than a trade or service code. Upon receipt, the bank performs a truthfulness review of the partnership agreement, the HK investor's incorporation certificate, the SAFE FDI registration certificate, and AML screening. A registered PRC CPA must then issue a capital verification report confirming the HKD/USD deposit and any conversion into RMB; the FIP must use the funds only for the approved operating scope and record actual paid-in capital in the commercial registry. Inbound funds cannot be routed via PayPal, Stripe, or third-party EMI accounts.For purchases of raw materials and components, use documentary T/T via SWIFT MT103 with invoice and packing-list matching; for large or prepaid overseas purchases of carbon-fibre rolls or high-grade motors, open an irrevocable sight commercial letter of credit through the Tier-1 bank to protect the FIP and the seller's compliance checks. Documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms are acceptable for stable trade partners only when the underlying bill of lading, commercial invoice, and inspection certificates are endorsed through the clearing bank. All outbound disbursements must be linked to the SAFE trade monitoring system and the bank's trade finance desk, not to e-commerce settlement accounts.Onshore CNY/USD and CNY/HKD forward sale and purchase contracts through a SAFE-approved designated bank with genuine underlying trade or investment invoices and no speculative overlay.Onshore RMB/foreign-currency swaps and cross-currency swaps limited to matching the FIP's foreign-currency debt service or approved profit-remittance exposure, supported by a master agreement with the banking counterparty.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forward contracts are permitted only for the Hong Kong partner or offshore treasury vehicle, not for the onshore FIP, because SAFE prohibits mainland resident entities from conducting unhedged speculative NDF trading; if used offshore, the NDF must be documented as a hedge of the same economic exposure and settled in CNH outside mainland China.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low direct viability for the PRC FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as PayPal, Stripe, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar non-bank entities are not PBOC-licensed banks in mainland China and therefore cannot maintain the FIP's domestic settlement account, issue RMB clearing instructions, or provide a compliant SAFE capital escrow account. For the Hong Kong incorporated investor, such EMIs may be used as a front-end collection tool, but the proceeds must be consolidated into a properly licensed cross-border settlement channel and then remitted to the FIP's designated Tier-1 bank account as export collection. Legal constraints require any payment institution engaging in cross-border e-commerce settlements for Chinese residents to hold a PBOC Payment Business License and be supervised by SAFE for foreign-exchange conversion; otherwise the route is prohibited.

Turnover: Under Hong Kong law, a limited partnership registered under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) is not generally required to file audited financial statements with the Companies Registry unless the partnership deed, a regulatory authority, or a financial institution imposes such a requirement. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Hong Kong profits tax: 16.5% for partner-corporations; 15.0% (standard rate) for partner-individuals; profits tax is assessed at the partner level through a pass-through partnership.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose dividend withholding tax; partnership profit distributions to resident or non-resident partners are not subject to any Hong Kong withholding tax and dividends received by a HK corporate partner are generally exempt from profits tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where annual aggregated covered transactions with associated enterprises equal or exceed HK$400 million; Master File: mandatory where the MNE group's consolidated revenue equals or exceeds HK$680 million. Below those thresholds, DIPN 59 still requires basic records sufficient to demonstrate an arm's length result.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no codified ESR equivalent to BVI/Cayman; however, economic substance is critical for three separate tests: (i) the territorial source doctrine (offshore profits exemption denies relief if the partnership lacks real personnel, premises and decision-making in HK); (ii) the patent box concession requires qualifying R&D functions and strategic decisions to occur in HK; (iii) DTA/PPT entitlement requires a substantive presence to be considered the beneficial owner/IR resident. The IRD may invoke the GAAR (IRO s.61/61A) to recharacterize a substance-less partnership distributing mainland manufacturing profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82: on summary conviction HK$10,000 fine and 6 months' imprisonment; on indictment HK$50,000 and 3 years' imprisonment; the court orders payment of treble the tax undercharged; IRD may also raise estimated assessments and issue notice of demand for the undercharged tax plus 10% additional penalty and interest.

Director personal liability: Partnership liability: the precedent partner and every partner (including non-resident partners) are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid profits tax; the court may order confiscation of assets, Mareva injunctions, travel restriction notices, and imprisonment of the responsible partner. In a limited partnership, the general partners bear unlimited liability; limited partners lose their statutory cap if they take part in management of the tax fraud.
