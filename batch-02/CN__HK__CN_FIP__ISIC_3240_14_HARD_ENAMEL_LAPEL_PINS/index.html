Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong SAR — electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk). The Business Registration Office (under the IRD) issues business registration certificates.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity seeking to establish a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong must first comply with the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) if it is to be constituted as a limited partnership, by filing a registration statement with the Companies Registry to obtain a Certificate of Registration of Limited Partnership. Within one month of commencing business, the partnership must apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) using Form IRBR1 (Partnership application). Required documents include the partnership agreement, a certified true copy of the Certificate of Registration of Limited Partnership (where applicable), each partner's valid passport or identity document and proof of residential address (e.g., a bank statement or utility bill dated within the preceding 3 months), and a local registered business address in Hong Kong. If a partner is a corporation, certified copies of its certificate of incorporation and board resolution appointing a local authorised representative are also required. Upon approval, the IRD issues a BRC containing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the partnership's taxpayer identification number for all IRD correspondence and filing. The BRC must be displayed at the business premises, and the BRN must be quoted on all tax returns and invoices. The partnership must also open a separate profits tax file with the IRD; however, tax is assessed on individual partners rather than on the partnership itself.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or export sales tax, no export zero-rating claim is required. All exported hard enamel lapel pins are automatically subject to 0% indirect tax. The absence of VAT means the ‘zero-rating’ concept is statutorily redundant. Nevertheless, the export must be declared to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) via the Trade Single Window (or previously DTI/E-Cert export declarations), and the goods should be accompanied by a commercial invoice and shipping documents to support free-flowing customs clearance. Hong Kong imposes no foreign-exchange control, so no statutory repatriation deadline applies (reflected as 0 days below).

  • Hong Kong Customs Export Declaration (submitted electronically via the Trade Single Window)
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill

No statutory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance mandate exists in Hong Kong. The IRD eTAX platform supports electronic filing and business registration, but does not require clearance or cryptographic stamping of invoices. Commercial invoices are contractually issued, and taxpayers are required to retain books and records (including invoices) for at least 7 years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

No VAT refund or export zero-rating clawback regime exists in Hong Kong. Therefore, there are no retrospective VAT clawback penalties for unverified exports. However, the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department may audit export declarations under the Import and Export Ordinance; making a false or misleading declaration can result in a fine up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years. In cases involving fraudulent trading documents, the IRD and Customs may also prosecute under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) or common law conspiracy to defraud, with penalties varying according to the value of the fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are fully convertible once the underlying transaction is verified by a designated foreign-exchange bank, but daily flows must be reported through SAFE’s trade monitoring system. Capital account transactions, including foreign-invested partnership (FIP) capital contributions, debt financing, and repatriation of partnership equity, are subject to ex-ante registration with SAFE or a delegated bank, and capital account convertibility is not an automatic right. For the proposed FIP engaged in die-struck brass cloisonné enamel pin production, the principal business involves current account export receipts from Etsy and Stripe, which can be received in RMB or foreign currency and converted at the onshore spot rate, provided the goods are declared through customs and the proceeds are repatriated. Profit remittance to a Hong Kong partner requires a special audit report, tax settlement certificates, and a partnership resolution, and the funds must be debited from the FIP’s RMB account after all distribution formalities. In contrast, Hong Kong has no foreign exchange controls, but RMB/HKD conversion itself is governed by SAFE limits and the HKMA’s clearing arrangements. The key practical hurdle is ensuring every cross-border flow can be traced back to an underlying business contract, customs declaration, and tax filing, because a missing document will cause the bank to reject the remittance and trigger a suspension of the capital flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The PRC does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio as of the current regime; however, all goods-export foreign-exchange proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic bank account within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration. The full amount must be credited to the FIP’s export proceeds foreign-exchange account, and the enterprise must verify the position through SAFE’s Goods Trade Monitoring System. The enterprise may retain the proceeds in foreign currency or sell them on a voluntary basis to the settlement bank at the prevailing onshore spot exchange rate. Any deferred receipt beyond 90 days requires an application to SAFE before the expiry date, with documentary proof of the commercial terms.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the parallel supervisory authority for Hong Kong SAR clearing and settlement facilities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI and neobank accounts (e.g., Payoneer, Airwallex, Currenxie, Stripe Treasury, or HK-licensed virtual banks) cannot act as the primary regulated corporate account for a mainland Chinese FIP. Under SAFE regulations, export proceeds must ultimately be repatriated into the FIP’s domestic bank account; using an offshore EMI to collect Etsy or Stripe payouts and then onward-transmit the funds to the FIP is permitted as a payment collection facilitator, but the EMI must not be used for supplier disbursements, netting, or intercompany treasury purposes without prior SAFE approval. Mainland China does not license foreign EMIs as deposit-taking institutions for domestic entities; therefore the viability rating for EMI as a primary settlement rail is Low, while the viability as an ancillary collection gateway is Medium. In Hong Kong, licensed virtual banks can handle HKD/USD transactions, but they cannot substitute for the mandatory SAFE-registered mainland bank account.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership is not automatically required to file audited accounts solely because it is a partnership. An independent audit by a certified public accountant becomes statutorily required when: (i) the partnership is a body corporate or a company incorporated under Cap 622, in which case audit is mandatory for every financial year irrespective of size; (ii) the partnership's total gross income exceeds HKD 5,000,000 for a basis period and the Inland Revenue Department demands audited financial statements with the BIR52 Partnership Return; (iii) the partnership is a licensed entity under a regulated activity ordinance imposing its own audit requirement; or (iv) the partnership agreement, a limited partner, a bank, or an Etsy or Stripe payment service provider contractually requires a CPA audit as a capital or merchant-risk condition. For smaller FIPs, unaudited financial statements certified by the partnership representative may be accepted by the IRD if the gross income threshold is not exceeded.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return is due within 1 month from the date of the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) filing notice. For an FIP with a 31 March accounting date, the return is issued in April and must be filed by 2 May; for any other accounting date, the deadline is one month from the issue date. E-filing via eTAX may be granted an automatic extension.
