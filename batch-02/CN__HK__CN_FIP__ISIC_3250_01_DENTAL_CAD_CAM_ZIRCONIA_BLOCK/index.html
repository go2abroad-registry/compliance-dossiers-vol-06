Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Partners may contribute cash, in-kind assets, intellectual property, or land-use rights as set out in the Partnership Agreement. No blocked escrow capital account is required at registration; however, foreign exchange remittances made as capital contributions must be recorded through the designated bank under State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification issued by the Fire Rescue Department after fire protection design review and completion acceptance inspection; structural load inspection and certificate for kiln, milling, and warehouse floors; workplace safety pre-evaluation under the Work Safety Law of the People's Republic of China; occupational disease hazard evaluation and regular workplace monitoring under the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases; mandatory personal protective equipment, emergency evacuation plan, and fire drills before production.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), National Electronic Tax Bureau online e-Tax portal; Hong Kong SAR is administered separately by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first register the FIP with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Within 30 calendar days of SAMR registration, the FIP must file the taxpayer registration particulars with the competent State Taxation Administration (STA) tax service office, submitting the SAMR business license, foreign-invested partnership agreement, executive partner appointment and identity documents, foreign partner constitutional documents, capital contribution proof, bank account opening license, registered address documentation, and authorization of the designated tax agent. The STA then opens the electronic tax file, activates the e-Tax account, and enables VAT invoice and export tax refund procedures. A non-resident enterprise with PRC-source income but no registered establishment obtains a non-resident TIN through withholding agent registration at the STA. If the Hong Kong partner carries on business in Hong Kong, it also obtains a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate from the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) but no separate PRC tax ID registration is triggered by that partnership relationship alone.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None available. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box election for a Foreign-Invested Partnership; fiscal transparency is mandatory and each partner is assessed on its allocable share of PRC-source income. Hong Kong similarly provides no partnership check-the-box election; profits tax is territorial and assessed on each partner's share of Hong Kong-source profits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (excluding VAT) is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold; a PRC-established FIP with taxable activities must complete VAT/tax registration from formation with no minimum turnover threshold. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks physically shipped from PRC customs territory to Hong Kong SAR or any foreign destination qualifies for the 0% VAT rate with input VAT refund/rebate. The exporter must be a registered general VAT taxpayer, issue a zero-rated export invoice, evidence a genuine cross-border sale, and prove physical departure from PRC customs territory. Cross-border offshore digital services supplied to overseas recipients are separately eligible under the zero-rating rules where stipulated, but this goods manufacturing export is evaluated under the PRC export goods VAT exemption and refund framework and filed through the China International Trade Single Window.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Customs Export Declaration for Goods, export-tax-refund copy)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / Combined Transport Bill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Bank Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract and Purchase Order
  • VAT Special Invoice for domestic input purchases
  • China International Trade Single Window export clearance data/log record

All PRC VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration national electronic invoice and clearance system (Golden Tax Phase IV and full digital electronic invoice platform). General VAT taxpayers transmitting high-volume export sales must integrate via the STA invoice-issuing API or use the official e-invoice self-service portal; data is cleared in real time and each fully digitalised electronic invoice carries a tamper-proof digital QR code and electronic signature. Hong Kong has no fiscal clearance, API invoicing, or digital stamping mandate.

Customs, foreign exchange, and tax authorities cross-check export declarations, logistics events, and bank remittance records. If zero-rating is not substantiated within 180 days, or the documentation bundle is false or incomplete, STA may reclassify the supply as domestic and assess VAT at 13% plus a daily late-payment surcharge, claw back any input VAT refund, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax under the Law on Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims are additionally subject to criminal liability under the Criminal Law, including fines, imprisonment, loss of export tax-refund filing eligibility, and blacklisting; there is no statute of limitations for fraudulent export refund clawbacks.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a special administrative region of China with no foreign exchange control laws of its own. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible in both current-account and capital-account transactions, and the HKMA maintains a linked exchange rate system between HKD and USD within a band of 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar. There is no surrender requirement for export proceeds, and no prior approval is needed for profit remittances, dividends, loan repayments, or partnership capital distributions. This enables a dental block manufacturing FIP to freely invoice overseas clinics and dental labs in USD or EUR without converting receipts into HKD. The only material constraint arises when funds flow between the mainland Chinese parent and the Hong Kong partnership: cross-border RMB conversion is subject to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules applicable to onshore vs offshore (CNH) pools. If the FIP is treated as a mainland foreign-invested partnership for PRC regulatory purposes, then capital injections and profit repatriation must navigate SAFE foreign-invested partnership registration. In the Hong Kong jurisdiction view, however, no central bank exchange control permission is required beyond standard banking transaction reporting. Overall, the Hong Kong regime scores as a free-convertibility jurisdiction with minimal structural hurdles, provided the underlying trade is supported by proper invoices, packing lists, and delivery evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Hong Kong law and HKMA directives, there is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters and manufacturing partnerships may retain 100% of overseas currency earnings in HKD, USD, CNY, or other permitted currencies, and may deploy those funds for onward settlement without surrendering any proportion to the HKMA or to any commercial bank. Where the counterparty or parent is located in mainland China, the underlying RMB cross-border flow may be captured by SAFE foreign-invested partnership compliance, but this is a PRC-side regime, not a Hong Kong surrender rule.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Hong Kong does not license standalone electronic money institutions (EMIs) with full deposit-taking powers under the Banking Ordinance. Non-bank fintech payment providers can hold a Stored Value Facility (SVF) license or Hong Kong Monetary Authority Money Service Operator (MSO) license, but they are not permitted to open demand deposit accounts, issue capital verification reports, or provide documentary letters of credit. Cross-border digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Neat, and Currenxie can be used as auxiliary rails for collections and mass payouts in some jurisdictions, but for the treasury-grade requirements of the CN__HK__CN_FIP structure, they demonstrate Low primary viability and Medium auxiliary viability at best. Legal constraints, including safeguarding requirements under Hong Kong payment legislation, do not prohibit them from onboarding the entity, but a statutory auditor or receiving bank will not treat an EMI balance as evidence of a cleared bank settlement. Therefore the blueprint mandates a Tier-1 licensed bank as the single source of truth for capital escrow and trade clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions from mainland China or other overseas shareholder/partner entities should be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the partnership's capital escrow account in Hong Kong. For a PRC parent remitting into the HK FIP, the originating bank must file the SAFE foreign investment remittance purpose code (e.g., 621010 for foreign-invested partnership capital contribution or the correct SAFE bpm code) and the receiving HK bank will review the FIP registration certificate, partnership deed, and a capital verification memorandum issued by a Hong Kong certified public accountant. For non-Chinese inbound capital, use a clear payment narrative such as 'Capital Contribution to [FIP name]', the underlying HK business registration number, and reference to the capital verification engagement. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), an independent certified public accountant audit is mandatory for a Hong Kong entity unless it qualifies as a small private company satisfying at least two of the following conditions: annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000, total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000, and average headcount not exceeding 100 employees. Any private entity that breaches any two of these thresholds is legally required to obtain an independent auditor's report. For a pure Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership, the Partnership Ordinance imposes no automatic audit, but the Inland Revenue Department may require certified audited accounts where partnership gross income exceeds HK$5,000,000, where the partnership is involved in intercompany transactions or transfer pricing, or where a partner claims deductions requiring audited substantiation. Licensed financial, insurance, asset management, and dental device manufacturing activities attract mandatory audit or auditor involvement irrespective of size.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200,000,000 for controlled tangible property transactions; RMB 100,000,000 for controlled intangible property or intercompany financing; RMB 40,000,000 for other related-party transactions. A Chinese Local File is mandatory when any threshold is exceeded; a Master File is required if the group's related-party transactions exceed RMB 1,500,000,000 or consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese FIP must retain manufacturing, procurement, quality control, inventory management and shipping decisions as its Core Income-Generating Activities within Mainland China. The Hong Kong affiliate must satisfy the substantial economic substance requirements of the H.K. Foreign Source Income Exemption regime, including a fixed place of business, resident employees, local board meetings and independent commercial decision-making. Failure to maintain such substance exposes the structure to recharacterisation under China EIT Law Article 47 of the General Anti-Avoidance Rules and H.K. Inland Revenue Ordinance GAAR provisions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 49 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 impose a penalty of 50% up to 500% of the underpaid tax, together with daily interest of 0.05%. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes evasion exceeding RMB 100,000 and representing more than 30% of taxable income, with imprisonment of up to seven years and fines of one to five times the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its directors face unlimited personal liability for the FIP tax debts under partnership law. China's tax enforcement can freeze personal bank accounts, impose exit bans (blacklisting), and require civil restitution of diverted assets. Upon criminal conviction, a responsible director can receive 3 to 7 years imprisonment plus personal fines and disgorgement.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Amendment Act 2021, the H.K. entity loses the foreign source income exemption for dividends, interest and IP income, and the profits are deemed Hong Kong sourced and taxed at 16.5%. China's Controlled Foreign Enterprise rules under EIT Law Article 45 may further attribute the H.K. profits to the Chinese partners subject to 25% EIT and back taxes.

Director personal liability: H.K. directors can be personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Ordinance; the court can order personal contribution to company assets and disqualify the director for up to 15 years. Where false statements are filed, the Inland Revenue Ordinance imposes a HKD 50,000 fine and up to three years imprisonment, plus Chinese exit bans and asset restitution through civil enforcement.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement 2016 No.42 and EIT Law Article 44 allow the tax authority to make a deemed profit adjustment and impose a 5% annual interest surcharge. The failure to provide contemporaneous documentation attracts a RMB 10,000 penalty; fraudulent invoice issuance under Criminal Law Article 205 carries criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 15 years when amounts are extremely large.

Director personal liability: The general partner and the H.K. parent company directors who approve the false pricing schedules are exposed to criminal liability for issuing false instruments and tax fraud. Chinese tax authorities may pierce the partnership veil to recover civil assets located in Mainland China, impose exit restrictions, and cooperate with Hong Kong authorities for extradition or asset freezing. Directors face up to 10 years imprisonment in China and enforcement of civil restitution orders against personal wealth.
