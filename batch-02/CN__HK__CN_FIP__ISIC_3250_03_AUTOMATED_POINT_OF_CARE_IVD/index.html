Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Market Regulation Administration of the place of registration, under the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). For a foreign-invested partnership, the registration is processed at the local Municipal or Provincial Administration for Market Regulation with a dedicated foreign investment registration window (e.g., Shenzhen Municipal Administration for Market Regulation, Shanghai Administration for Market Regulation, or the Qianhai/Shekou comprehensive registration center).

Governing statute

Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (adopted 1997, revised 1 June 2007), Articles 1-11, 14-17, 26-31, 33, 63, 85, and 90; Administrative Measures for the Establishment of Partnership Enterprises by Foreign Enterprises and Individuals (Order No. 1 of the Ministry of Commerce and the State Administration for Industry and Commerce, effective 1 March 2010), Articles 1-12; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (effective 1 January 2020), Articles 4-8, 13, 18, and 30; Regulations for Implementing the Foreign Investment Law, Articles 3-5, 17, and 28.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Establishment Registration of a Foreign-Invested Partnership Enterprise (外商投资合伙企业设立登记申请书) filed through the SAMR online service portal or the local Market Regulation Administration's one-stop enterprise registration platform. Supporting statutory instruments include the Partnership Agreement, the Capital Contribution Schedule, the Appointment Notice of Executive Partner, and the Pre-Approved Name Reservation Certificate.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law imposes no minimum paid-up capital or registered capital requirement on a foreign-invested partnership. Partners are free to agree on the amount, form, and payment timing of capital contributions in the partnership agreement; contributions may be made in cash, tangible assets, intellectual property, technology, or other lawful forms. There is no statutory requirement for a blocked escrow account, capital deposit certificate, or bank verification of contribution unless the partnership agreement itself provides for such controls.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portals: eTAX (Electronic Tax Administration System) at https://www.etax.gov.hk and the IRD Business Registration e-Service at https://www.ird.gov.hk. The Registrar of Companies operates the Companies Registry for partnership registration under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 321) and Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the statutory identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Procedure: (1) Register the partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 321) or, where structured as a Limited Partnership Fund, under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637). (2) Lodge the Commissioner-prescribed business registration application with the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business. (3) Receive the Business Registration Certificate and BRN; the BRN is the sole dedicated tax identification number for Hong Kong tax filing purposes. (4) Use the BRN when filing Profits Tax returns, employer returns, and Business Registration renewal submissions; there is no separate VAT ID because Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST. (5) Non-resident partners should obtain a Certificate of Residence from their home tax jurisdiction if treaty benefits are to be claimed under an applicable Comprehensive Double Taxation Agreement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — a Hong Kong limited partnership is fiscally transparent: the partnership has no separate tax personality. Profits are computed and reported by the partnership, but each partner is separately assessable to Hong Kong Profits Tax on their partnership share in accordance with the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), subject to the territorial-source principle.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong provides no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election. Partnership pass-through taxation is mandatory, and entity-level taxation cannot be elected. The Inland Revenue Ordinance applies territorial-source rules automatically to all FIP partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong SAR). The only Hong Kong indirect fiscal impositions are stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117) on certain instruments and customs and excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on dutiable goods; neither operates as a general value-added consumption tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Hong Kong has no VAT/GST economic-turnover registration threshold. The relevant mandatory registration is Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), which applies to all persons carrying on business in Hong Kong irrespective of turnover, unless an express exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST; therefore there is no Hong Kong export zero-rating claim and no Hong Kong VAT refund. If goods are manufactured in Mainland China by a PRC affiliate and exported to Hong Kong or a third country, the PRC VAT export zero-rating rules may apply at the PRC border. To substantiate 0% PRC export VAT, the exporter must prove physical export through PRC customs, collection of export proceeds from the Hong Kong entity or overseas buyer, and compliance with the PRC tax authority's export-exemption and refund documentation requirements. For the Hong Kong FIP, exports are outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax.

  • For any PRC export VAT zero-rating claim by the mainland manufacturing entity: PRC Customs Export Declaration, ocean or air bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, export sales contract, PRC customs-verified export verification/administration record, bank inward foreign-exchange settlement receipt, and the Hong Kong entity's valid Business Registration Certificate.

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not require real-time VAT/GST e-invoicing, electronic fiscal clearance, API telemetry, or digital anti-fraud stamping. A business must keep records sufficient to ascertain liabilities under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); electronic invoices and records are acceptable. For dutiable goods, excise licences, permits and movement documentation under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) may be required.

Since Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, there is no Hong Kong VAT clawback for zero-rated exports. If the mainland exporter fraudulently or inaccurately claims PRC VAT export zero-rating, the PRC tax authority may recover the under-collected output VAT and refunds, impose a late-payment surcharge, and impose administrative penalties ranging from 50 percent to five times the tax involved; criminal liability may arise for fictitious export declarations or false refund claims. In Hong Kong, any person who knowingly makes a false statement, certificate or return for tax purposes commits an offence under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), punishable on conviction by fines and imprisonment. The PRC customs authority may also impose duties, fines, and confiscation of goods and shipping equipment where export declarations are found to be false or misleading.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland People's Republic of China retains a managed-float exchange rate regime and does not offer full capital account convertibility. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in the IVD manufacturing sector, current account items such as trade payments, service fees, royalties, and routine operating expenses are generally convertible after banks perform document-based or digital-pilot authenticity verification under SAFE rules. Capital account transactions, however, remain subject to registration, quota, or approval requirements. Inbound foreign direct investment into the FIP must first be registered by SAFE, after which the partnership opens a foreign-exchange capital settlement account and undertakes onshore RMB settlement against a permitted capital-account payment purpose list. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is lawful only after the annual audit is completed, the corporate income tax is settled, the withholding tax certificate is issued, and the receiving bank is presented with the audited financial statements, the partnership distribution resolution, and the SAFE-motivated payment instruction. There is no absolute capital convertibility and no guarantee of same-day capital mobility; every cross-currency movement must be substantiated by a genuine underlying transaction. Since the 2020 reforms, PBOC and SAFE have further strengthened the real-transaction principle and anti-money laundering supervision, meaning any non-economic cross-border flow is subject to rejection, suspension, or post-event audit.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export sales of automated point-of-care IVD analyzers and microfluidic cartridges, the FIP must repatriate the foreign-currency receipts into its designated Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Receipt Account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration, unless an approved deferred-collection registration is granted. The mandatory surrender ratio has been abolished after the 2012 FX reform; therefore the FIP may retain 100% of the export proceeds in foreign currency indefinitely, convert them into RMB at prevailing market rates, or settle them under the thoroughly verified 'A' classification with its bank. The retained foreign currency must be used for legitimate operational needs or approved capital-account spending. No fixed percentage of forced conversion is now decreed, but SAFE retains the right to audit the authenticity, completeness, and timeline of the export proceeds collection process.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the statutory foreign-exchange administrative authority subordinate to the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Onshore Chinese banking does not permit a mainland-registered FIP to be serviced by a foreign fintech electronic money institution for core treasury operations; entities such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or Hong Kong virtual banks have neither a PRC commercial banking licence nor access to SAFE's capital-account registration system. Such EMIs are legally barred from opening settlement or collection accounts in the name of a mainland Chinese corporate or partnership. The FIP must use a locally licensed Tier-1 commercial bank for any USD/RMB flow; a Hong Kong parent's EMI account may be used only as an auxiliary liquidity pocket outside the PRC and cannot receive the FIP's revenues or disburse its import duty. Therefore the only compliant cross-border digital rail is the licensed bank's proprietary payment gateway or the SWIFT network.

The Hong Kong investor must instruct its bank to make an SWIFT MT103 remittance in USD or CNY to the FIP's FDI capital settlement account at the onshore Tier-1 bank, utilising the foreign exchange purpose code 'FDI-Direct Investment Equity' (and, where applicable, the reinvestment or non-currency contribution supplementary code). The remittance must cross-reference the SAFE foreign-exchange registration number of the FIP; on arrival, the onshore bank credits the capital account and immediately performs a capital account information check under SAFE's 'information first, financial firm second' principle. The FIP then applies for RMB settlement of the foreign exchange through the bank's Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Mobile Console or branch window, submitting the transaction purpose materials such as invoice, contract, customs declaration, lease, or payroll schedule. The bank will verify the capital's intended use against the permitted current or capital account scope, complete the FDI capital verification record, and, if required, issue a Capital Verification Report for customs or administrative formalities. No disbursement may be made until the SAFE registration certificate has been examined and the counterparty's account name matches the verified transaction; cash withdrawal from the capital settlement account is expressly prohibited.The FIP may clear outbound trade payments via (i) direct SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for low-value or repetitive imports of tubes, reagents, sensors and packaging materials; (ii) commercial letters of credit, most commonly issued as irrevocable documentary credits with MT700 advice, for the purchase of high-value automated analyser components and microfluidic cartridge production lines; and (iii) documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for mature, low-risk supplier relationships in Germany, Japan or Singapore. Each disbursement must be matched against the import declaration produced in the China Customs single-window system, an invoice, a transport document, and a purchase order. The bank's 'A' classification allows expedited verification, but the FIP must still submit the SAFE Cross-Border Income and Payment Confirmation Letter where applicable. The payment instruction must state the precise trade-in-goods foreign exchange code (such as 108 or 121) and the importer's name must match the FIP's business licence; any third-party payment must be justified and documented in advance.Onshore RMB/USD forward foreign-exchange contracts with commercial banks, subject to SAFE's real-exposure principle and a 365-day tenor boundaryOnshore RMB/USD FX swaps between the FIP and a licensed PRC bank to manage short-term liquidity mismatches without speculative open positionsCross-currency interest rate swaps for the foreign-currency syndicated loan that finances the IVD manufacturing lineHK offshore NDF or deliverable forward contracts entered into by the parent company, which are outside the mainland onshore derivative perimeter and permissible only for genuine hedging of the FIP's dividend expectation

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Onshore Chinese banking does not permit a mainland-registered FIP to be serviced by a foreign fintech electronic money institution for core treasury operations; entities such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, or Hong Kong virtual banks have neither a PRC commercial banking licence nor access to SAFE's capital-account registration system. Such EMIs are legally barred from opening settlement or collection accounts in the name of a mainland Chinese corporate or partnership. The FIP must use a locally licensed Tier-1 commercial bank for any USD/RMB flow; a Hong Kong parent's EMI account may be used only as an auxiliary liquidity pocket outside the PRC and cannot receive the FIP's revenues or disburse its import duty. Therefore the only compliant cross-border digital rail is the licensed bank's proprietary payment gateway or the SWIFT network.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% HK corporate profits tax standard rate; for an unincorporated partnership FIP the standard rate is 15% (two-tiered 8.25% / 7.5% on first HK$2 million assessable profits, 16.5% / 15% on excess)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no domestic withholding tax on outbound dividend or profit distributions made by a Hong Kong partnership or company. If the upstream entity is a mainland China subsidiary paying dividends to the HK FIP, mainland statutory dividend WHT is 10%, reduced to 5% under the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement only when the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the payor's capital, and 10% in other cases.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File / Country-by-Country Report: consolidated group revenue of at least HK$6.8 billion (approximately EUR 750 million). Local File statutory materiality thresholds: related-party tangible property transactions above HK$200 million per year; related-party transactions involving intangible property and financial transactions above HK$50 million per year, based on IRD DIPN 58 and the transfer pricing provisions in IRO Part 9A.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations filing regime, but the IRD applies territorial source doctrine, the general anti-abuse rule under IRO s61A, and the OECD MLI principal purpose test. For an automated point-of-care IVD manufacturer, Core Income-Generating Activities include R&D, design and validation of microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers, high-risk manufacturing decisions, quality control, procurement, and sales negotiations. These activities must be substantively performed in Hong Kong for the FIP to claim HK-source profits and treaty benefits. A paper-only offshore profit claim without CIGA in Hong Kong is fatal.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Offshore Profit Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: IRO s82(1) (Cap 112): wilful tax evasion with intent is punishable by a HK$50,000 fine, treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for three years. IRO s80(2): knowingly furnishing an incorrect return is punishable by a HK$50,000 fine and treble tax. IRD may also issue increased assessments under IRO s60 and s82A and impose default interest.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the partnership is a mere facade for tax evasion. Directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly sign false returns share joint civil liability for asset restitution and unpaid tax. In criminal cases the court may order Mareva asset freezes, passport surrender, travel restrictions, and blacklisting; individual directors and managing partners face imprisonment terms of up to three years and disqualification as directors.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The IRD may make transfer pricing adjustments under IRO s50AD and re-determine taxable profits using arm's length methods. Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof and exposes the taxpayer to penalties under IRO s80(2) and s82 for incorrect returns. The additional tax is complemented by interest under IRO s60 and may be trebled if fraud or wilful neglect is established.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve fabricated transfer pricing policies, false management accounts, or destructive intercompany agreements cannot hide behind the corporate general partner. Malaysian-style personal pecuniary liability can be imposed where a director causes partnership property to be transferred to related parties; the directors may be required to make civil restitution, may be disqualified, and may face travel bans and imprisonment in aggravated tax fraud cases.
Shell Company / DTA Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief is denied under the principal purpose test in the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement and the OECD MLI. The mainland Chinese tax authority may reassess the source income at the full 10% dividend / 7% interest and royalty rates or apply penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the PRC Tax Collection Law, plus late payment surcharges. In Hong Kong, false treaty claims and incorrect returns attract penalties under IRO s80(2) and s82, including treble tax and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Where the FIP is used as a sham for treaty shopping, courts may lift the corporate veil and hold directors and general partners jointly liable for withheld taxes and civil damages. Directors who authorize false beneficial ownership declarations may be personally blacklisted, subject to exit bans, ordered to repatriate diverted assets, and sentenced to imprisonment for tax fraud and wilful evasion.
