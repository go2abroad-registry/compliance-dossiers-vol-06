Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: eTAX - electronic Tax Filing and Services via GovHK, administered together with the Business Registration Office (BRO) under the IRD.

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) and the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a foreign non-resident entity or foreign-invested partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement of business. The procedure for a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is: (1) submit the Commissioner of Inland Revenue's prescribed application form for partnership business registration, signed by all partners or an authorized representative; (2) attach certified copies of the partnership agreement, identity documents of each partner (passports for individuals or certificates of incorporation for corporate partners), and proof of the Hong Kong place of business; (3) pay the statutory Business Registration fee and levy; (4) receive the Business Registration Certificate, whereon the Business Registration Number (BRN) constitutes the Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number for the partnership and its partners; (5) renew the Business Registration Certificate annually; (6) if employees are engaged, register as an employer with the IRD under Cap. 112 and obtain an employer file number. A non-resident entity that does not carry on a trade or business in Hong Kong but earns Hong-Kong-sourced profits is not automatically required to hold a Business Registration Certificate, but the IRD will allocate a Tax File Number when a Profits Tax Return is issued; there is no separate VAT ID because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under Hong Kong Profits Tax, a partnership is not a separate taxable person; partnership profits are computed in the partnership's name and assessed on each partner in their respective shares. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is therefore pass-through for Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, and territorial source rules apply so that only Hong-Kong-sourced profits are chargeable.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not have a check-the-box entity classification election, and the pass-through treatment of partnerships is compulsory under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). No hybrid mismatch election exists in Hong Kong domestic law. A partnership may seek an advance ruling from the IRD on whether particular profits are Hong-Kong-sourced, but that is an administrative determination mechanism rather than an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales tax in Hong Kong; indirect duties comprise excise duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and stamp duty on specified instruments under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), any person or partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the commencement of business, with no turnover threshold; the annual Business Registration fee and levy apply regardless of revenue volume.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no VAT refund mechanism, and no statutory certificate of export VAT zero-rating. Exports of goods from Hong Kong are generally free of indirect tax or export duty unless the goods are subject to excise duty under Cap. 109 or are strategic/controlled goods requiring an export licence under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60). For the underlying mainland China manufacturing and export leg, the FIP may be eligible for PRC VAT export zero-rating under the State Taxation Administration export tax refund regime if the entity is the registered exporter, provides the required customs and foreign exchange collection documents, and receives export proceeds in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules.

None. Hong Kong does not operate a mandatory electronic invoicing platform, real-time fiscal clearance regime, or anti-fraud digital stamping system for indirect taxes. Taxpayers retain business records in accordance with Cap. 112 record-keeping requirements, and specified returns are filed electronically through the IRD's eTAX portal; no government fiscal invoice authentication is required for Hong Kong indirect tax purposes.

In Hong Kong, the absence of export VAT zero-rating means there is no VAT clawback mechanism; however, false customs declarations or evasion of duty/licensing obligations under Cap. 60 or Cap. 109 are criminal offences subject to fines, seizure of goods and imprisonment. For the mainland China export VAT zero-rating leg, the State Taxation Administration and SAFE may retrospectively disallow the claimed 0% VAT export refund, claw back refunded input VAT plus late payment surcharge, and impose administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law if the underlying customs declaration, foreign exchange repatriation or export document trail is found to be false, incomplete or unverified. In addition, failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory period, including the 180-day goods-trade window, may trigger SAFE verification violations, penalties and loss of export tax refund eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the Hong Kong dollar pegged to the US dollar in a range of approximately 7.75 to 7.85. There are no current account or capital account controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on profit remittances for partnerships lawfully incorporated in the territory. Foreign-invested partnerships enjoy unfettered access to HKD, USD, EUR, RMB in the offshore pool, and are permitted to maintain multi-currency accounts with licensed banks. However, because the sovereign parent is the People's Republic of China, any transaction that touches the mainland is subject to the SAFE regime. For the Hong Kong treasury centre, free convertibility of HKD and the offshore RMB pool will facilitate dividend repatriation to global partners. The only material hurdle arises if the partnership owns a mainland-resident FIP subsidiary or receives PRC-sourced funds; such movements require SAFE registration, capital account approval, and the use of an onshore custodian bank. Profit remittance to mainland China requires tax clearance and an audited financial statement. Overall, liquidity mobility is highest through Hong Kong, while mainland clearing is governed by current FX verification rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong entity, there are no statutory surrender obligations; export proceeds may be freely retained in foreign currency or converted. For mainland China-related operations, under SAFE measures, export proceeds in goods must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days after export customs declaration unless the commercial contract prescribes a longer collection term, and each receipt is subject to trade data verification. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for goods exports in the current SAFE regime; proceeds may be kept in a foreign currency account or converted to RMB at a licensed bank at the enterprise's discretion.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) in coordination with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for cross-border flows involving mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMIs are legally permitted in Hong Kong under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, but their viability for this multinational ventilator manufacturing structure is Low to Medium. Licensed e-money issuers can support payroll, petty cash, domestic retail payments, and limited remittances, but they cannot issue Government Letters of Credit, conduct capital verification, underwrite trade loans, or access SWIFT as full members. Virtual banks with full banking licences, such as ZA Bank and WeLab Bank, may offer corporate accounts, but their cross-border trade finance capabilities are still nascent. For the required LC rails, FX hedging, and high-value cross-border settlement, a Tier-1 commercial bank is legally and operationally indispensable. The HKMA does not bar fintech EMIs, but they cannot replace bank-based clearing, and their use should be limited to ancillary treasury activities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections must be cleared via SWIFT MT103, CIPS for RMB-denominated flows, or CHATS for HKD/USD-denominated flows, directly into the partnership's capital escrow account. Each remittance must reference a purpose code reflecting direct investment equity, typically SAFE BOP code 601 for equity contributions or 602 for quasi-equity shareholder loans. If funds originate from mainland China, the remitter must present outbound FDI approval, SAFE registration, and a certificate of investment to the Chinese bank; the Hong Kong receiving bank will require a matched capital verification certificate before releasing funds to operational accounts. Intermingling escrow funds with operating cash flows is forbidden; disbursement after capital verification requires a board resolution and, when applicable, HKMA notification for large cash deposits.Trade vendor payments for ventilator components will use SWIFT MT103 direct remittances, MT700/MT710 series for commercial letters of credit, and MT400/MT422 for documentary collections. Settlement should be routed via the Hong Kong RTGS (CHATS) for HKD, CIPS for CNH, and correspondent banks for USD and EUR. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs are legally permitted in Hong Kong under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, but their viability for this multinational ventilator manufacturing structure is Low to Medium. Licensed e-money issuers can support payroll, petty cash, domestic retail payments, and limited remittances, but they cannot issue Government Letters of Credit, conduct capital verification, underwrite trade loans, or access SWIFT as full members. Virtual banks with full banking licences, such as ZA Bank and WeLab Bank, may offer corporate accounts, but their cross-border trade finance capabilities are still nascent. For the required LC rails, FX hedging, and high-value cross-border settlement, a Tier-1 commercial bank is legally and operationally indispensable. The HKMA does not bar fintech EMIs, but they cannot replace bank-based clearing, and their use should be limited to ancillary treasury activities.
