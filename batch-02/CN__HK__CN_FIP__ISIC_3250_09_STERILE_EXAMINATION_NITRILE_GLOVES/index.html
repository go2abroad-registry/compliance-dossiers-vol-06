Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the 12366 online tax service platform; for any Hong Kong SAR tax-presence, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and the Business Registration Office via the GovHK e-Tax portal.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_FIP, a PRC foreign-invested limited partnership: (1) execute and file the partnership agreement with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which doubles as the statutory PRC taxpayer identification number; (2) complete tax registration with the local STA office through the SAMR-STA information-sharing system upon establishment; (3) activate the electronic tax account and obtain the STA taxpayer digital certificate; (4) register with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) through the China International Trade Single Window to obtain the customs declarant and export registration code necessary for export VAT zero-rating; (5) complete export tax-rebate taxpayer registration with STA and link the FIP's bank accounts, SAFE foreign-exchange registration, and e-invoice platform account; (6) for each non-resident foreign partner, record the partner's home-jurisdiction tax identification number, country of residence, and permanent address in the FIP's tax files; where a non-resident partner derives China-source income or carries on business through the FIP, register that partner with the competent STA office and obtain the applicable PRC non-resident taxpayer reference number before any withholding settlement; (7) if an actual Hong Kong SAR business presence exists, apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate within one month after commencement of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the CN_FIP is fiscally transparent for PRC Enterprise Income Tax purposes; no entity-level tax is imposed on the partnership itself, and each partner is separately taxable on its allocable profit share as a resident enterprise, resident individual, non-resident enterprise, or non-resident individual. Hong Kong SAR, as a separate offshore special territory, applies a territorial-source profits tax system; a partnership is likewise not taxed at entity level, but the IRD may require a partnership Profits Tax Return based on Hong Kong-source profits.

Available election forms

None - the PRC does not provide check-the-box, Subchapter-S, or hybrid-mismatch elections; partnership fiscal transparency is mandatory. The partnership agreement may allocate profits and losses only if the allocation has economic substance and complies with the arm's-length standard, anti-avoidance rules, and the PRC general anti-abuse rule. For Hong Kong, no formal 'offshore election' exists; a taxpayer may assert through documentation that profits are not Hong Kong-source under the IRD territorial-source principle, but this is a facts-and-documents claim, not a statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; there is no turnover threshold for basic VAT registration - any enterprise or partnership, including the CN_FIP, that undertakes taxable sales in the PRC must complete VAT registration upon establishment. Small-scale VAT taxpayer status may apply below the RMB 5 million threshold, but export zero-rating and input-VAT refunds generally require general-taxpayer registration and export-rebate qualification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT Law, physically exported goods are generally zero-rated, meaning output VAT at 0% is applied and the exporter may claim a refund of creditable input VAT on the exported goods. The CN_FIP must be the principal exporter and customs declarant, the goods, sterile examination nitrile gloves, must physically leave mainland China customs territory, the sale must be made to an overseas buyer, and title and risk must pass outside the PRC. The FIP must also satisfy all applicable statutory licensing for medical-device and PPE export and hold the necessary GACC registration and export tax-rebate status. Qualifying offshore digital services are not within this business scope; the zero-rating analysis is exclusively for export of goods.

The CN_FIP must issue and receive fully digitalized e-invoices through the STA electronic invoice service platform under the Golden Tax System Phase IV framework, with real-time encrypted upload and reconciliation of invoice data with GACC and tax databases. No physical fiscal stamp or legacy pre-print authorization is used; paper invoices, where still permitted, must carry the STA-issued 20-digit invoice number and anti-counterfeit fiscal code. Export invoices must be linked to the customs export declaration data via the China International Trade Single Window, and export-rebate data matching uses the STA export tax system to verify invoice, customs clearance, logistics, and foreign-exchange receipt records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a strict distinction between current-account and capital-account convertibility. The onshore Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is treated as a resident enterprise for foreign-exchange purposes and must be registered with SAFE through the receiving commercial bank under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) framework. RMB is fully convertible for current-account trade and non-trade payments supported by genuine underlying documentation such as export invoices, bills of lading, inspection certificates, and customs declarations. Capital-account inflows, including foreign partner capital contributions, require SAFE admission, a dedicated capital account at the receiving bank, and settlement only after foreign-investment registration and anti-money-laundering checks. The FIP must complete a CPA capital verification report before converting foreign-currency capital contributions into RMB. There is no fixed percentage surrender obligation, but the FIP must repatriate export foreign-exchange receipts to its onshore current account within the statutory declaration cycle. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are permitted after annual audit and tax clearance, but require submission of the partnership tax return, profit-distribution resolution, audited financial statements, and evidence of settlement of all onshore taxes; the settling bank will perform a qualified-payment review and may require proof of tax payment before releasing distribution funds. The Hong Kong SAR maintains full current- and capital-account convertibility for HKD and offshore CNY, but the FIP's onshore exposure remains subject to PRC controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds received in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic current account within the statutory cycle, generally within 90 days from the date of export declaration, and must be reported through the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment system. There is no mandatory percentage surrender ratio: the FIP may retain foreign currency in its current account or settle into RMB at the prevailing bank rate, provided the settlement is matched to the underlying export customs declaration and trade documents. Foreign partner capital contributions are capital-account items and must be converted under the FDI registration framework rather than under export-surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) coordinates the offshore clearing segment for HKD and USD operations.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Low. Airwallex, Currenxie, and similar unlicensed offshore digital EMIs are not licensed commercial banks in the PRC and cannot open onshore RMB settlement accounts or independently clear export proceeds or inbound capital for the FIP. They are legally barred from acting as the FIP's primary treasury provider and may not hold customer funds or provide custodial clearing for PRC entities without a PBOC/SAFE-licensed bank settlement arrangement. The FIP must not route statutory capital or export proceeds through unlicensed offshore EMIs. A PRC-licensed non-bank payment institution may be used only as a supplementary low-value collection tool if it settles through a Tier-1 bank, but the recommended treasury architecture remains wholly bank-based.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (China Enterprise Income Tax, imposed on partners of the tax-transparent Foreign-Invested Partnership; the FIP itself is not subject to entity-level income tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Under Chinese domestic law, dividends paid by a resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise are subject to 10% withholding tax. However, a partnership is not an 'enterprise' for dividend-purposes; profit allocations from the FIP to its partners are not legally dividends and therefore no dividend WHT is triggered on profit distribution. Instead, the non-resident partner is taxed on its allocable share of the FIP's taxable income (25% if a PE exists). If the FIP invests in a Chinese subsidiary and receives dividends from that subsidiary, the dividend distributed onward to the HK partner may be subject to 10% WHT, reduced to 5% under the Mainland-HK Double Tax Arrangement if the beneficial owner is a company holding directly at least 25% of the capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under China's Circular 42 (2016 Announcement 42), a Local File must be prepared and filed contemporaneously for a fiscal year if the aggregate value of all related-party transactions (goods, services, intangible property, interest) exceeds RMB 100 million (approximately USD 14 million) with any related party. A Master File is required if the FIP is part of a multinational group whose consolidated group revenue exceeded RMB 5.5 billion, and the FIP itself has cross-border related-party transactions exceeding RMB 100 million in the preceding year or has paid or received over RMB 100 million in royalties, interest, or service fees. The FIP's intercompany sales of nitrile gloves to a Hong Kong distributor, and any management fees paid to the HK partner, must remain within these thresholds to avoid mandatory documentation penalties; a failure to maintain the Local File can trigger a penalty of RMB 100,000-RMB 500,000 under Circular 42, plus a 25% upward adjustment and interest.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the Economic Substance Regulations of offshore centers, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and its implementation (Circular 698/7) allow the tax authority to recharacterize a transaction that has no reasonable commercial purpose and lacks substance. The CIGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) analysis is used by the Hong Kong IRD for determining whether profits are sourced in Hong Kong; for Chinese purposes, the FIP will be recognized as a PE of the HK partner if the HK partner's core activities (e.g., strategic management, purchasing, quality control) are performed in China through the FIP. To avoid a deemed PE, the HK partner must demonstrate that CIGA are exercised in Hong Kong—such as negotiating contracts, controlling inventory flows, and making pricing decisions at board level in HK—even though the physical manufacturing takes place in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Income Allocation

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Law on Tax Collection and Administration (TCAL) Article 63, the tax authority imposes an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax, plus a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05%. Where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and the evasion rate exceeds 10%, Criminal Law Article 201 applies: imprisonment up to 3 years and a criminal fine; for extremely large amounts (above RMB 500,000), imprisonment of 3-7 years with a fine.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the partnership is a mere conduit of the directors. Managing partners and directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and surcharges. In criminal cases, the court may order confiscation of personal assets, an anti-travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from China, and may impose imprisonment on the responsible director. A prohibition on serving as a director/manager of any enterprise in China for up to 5 years is also authorized.
Abusive Treaty Relief (Beneficial Ownership Manipulation)

Statutory sanction: Under China's Notice 9/2018 on indirect transfers and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47), the tax authority denies the treaty benefit and re-withholds at the statutory 10%, 10%, or 7% rate for dividends, interest, and royalties respectively. A 50%-500% administrative penalty under TCAL is imposed on the withholding agent for failure to withhold, and interest is charged from the payment date. If the amount involved is substantial, the case is referred to the criminal tax fraud unit.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly facilitated the false treaty claim face personal liability for the under-withheld tax, penalties, and interest, and may be blacklisted under China's 'serious tax dishonesty list', resulting in travel bans, restrictions on luxury consumption, and denial of cross-border banking. In criminal cases, the directors may be prosecuted for fraudulent invoicing and tax evasion under Criminal Law Articles 205 and 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation on Related-Party Glove Sales

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41 and Circular 42, the tax authority makes an adjustment to the taxpayer's taxable income, levies an additional tax of 25% on the adjusted amount, and imposes late-payment interest at the benchmark six-month loan rate plus 2%. An administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the resulting tax deficiency is applied under TCAL Article 44 for failure to maintain proper documentation. Where cross-border payments are made to a partner in a tax haven with no economic substance, the authorities may recharacterize the arrangement and treat the payment as a hidden dividend subject to 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the non-arm's-length pricing may be held personally liable in supplemental proceedings for up to the entire underpaid tax and penalties under the 'piercing the veil' principle when the FIP is insolvent or its assets are stripped. Travel bans (blacklisting) and freezing of personal bank accounts are routinely ordered. Criminal exposure may arise if the transfer pricing manipulation crosses the threshold of willful tax evasion, leading to imprisonment of 3-7 years for the responsible directors.
