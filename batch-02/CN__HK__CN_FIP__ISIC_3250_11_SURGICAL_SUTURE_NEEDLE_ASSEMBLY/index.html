None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a foreign-invested partnership under the Partnership Ordinance or the Business Registration Ordinance. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its official online electronic tax service portal, the National Electronic Taxation Bureau/e-Tax platform; for Hong Kong SAR related tax compliance, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTax portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in China, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The procedure is: (1) file the FIP establishment application with the competent SAMR office, submitting the partnership agreement and identifying all foreign and domestic partners; (2) obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code, which serves as the TIN; (3) complete taxpayer registration with the local STA branch through the e-Tax platform within 30 days of the business license issuance, supplying the partnership deed, identification details of partners, business scope, and accounting basis; (4) join the VAT registration by selecting small-scale taxpayer or VAT general taxpayer status; (5) complete Customs registration for export activities through the China Customs single window; and (6) open the required RMB and foreign exchange bank accounts and submit foreign exchange business registration to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for export proceeds repatriation. If any foreign partner is non-resident for tax purposes, the FIP must act as the statutory withholding agent for partner-level taxation. To the extent the FIP has a Hong Kong SAR partner or maintains an office in Hong Kong, the relevant partner or Hong Kong branch must also apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance, obtaining the BR number that serves as the Hong Kong tax reference; no Hong Kong VAT/GST registration applies because Hong Kong has no general indirect tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) in a consecutive 12-month period generally require registration as a VAT general taxpayer in China; turnover below this threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer, or the FIP may voluntarily apply for general taxpayer status where accounting controls satisfy STA requirements.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT is available for goods manufactured in mainland China and physically exported through a customs declaration to a destination outside the PRC customs territory, including the Hong Kong SAR. The exporter must be a VAT-registered taxpayer, the goods must actually leave under customs supervision using the export declaration, the sales price must be arm's length, and the export proceeds must be repatriated through permitted banking channels within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (output of the customs single window export declaration)
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Airway Bill, or Combined Transport Document evidencing actual export
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Export Sales Contract/Proforma Invoice
  • Bank inward remittance certificate or export foreign exchange collection verification confirming the receipt and repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT e-invoice or special VAT invoice matched to the export declaration for refund verification

China operates a fully digitalized electronic invoice system. VAT invoices must be issued through the national electronic invoice service platform using the taxpayer's digital tax-control identity, transmitted in real time to the STA via encrypted APIs or the e-Tax web portal, and each invoice must carry a statutory anti-counterfeit QR code and digital stamp. Paper-only invoices are no longer accepted for standard VAT compliance; export VAT refunds are verified by digitally reconciling the e-invoice with the export declaration data held by China Customs.

The STA and China Customs jointly conduct audit scrutiny of export zero-rating and refund claims. Any unverified, false, or fraudulent export declaration may trigger a reversal of the zero-rated VAT treatment, recovery of unpaid VAT plus a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund can include substantial imprisonment, confiscation of property, and revocation of the enterprise's foreign exchange collection and customs registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating foreign exchange system with evolving convertibility. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) engaged in surgical suture manufacturing, the current account is fully convertible in principle: export proceeds, service revenues and current-account distributions may be converted and remitted after verification of genuine trade background and supporting documents. The capital account remains controlled. Foreign-invested partnership registered capital contributed from Hong Kong partners constitutes FDI, requiring foreign exchange registration at the designated bank; the foreign-currency capital must be credited to a capital-proceeds settlement account and can only be converted to RMB for approved business purposes subject to account-wide negative list verification. There is no mandatory conversion surrender ratio for export proceeds today; exporters may retain foreign exchange in a current account foreign currency settlement account and negotiate conversion with their bank. Profit remittance to foreign partners is treated as a current-account outbound remittance after the FIP has fulfilled its withholding tax obligations, provided audited financial statements, profit-distribution resolution, tax payment certificates and SAFE-level supporting documents are presented. Hong Kong adds a fully convertible HKD channel under the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate system, enabling a lawful RMB-HKD-USD treasury flow with separate regulatory oversight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated and collected into the FIP's domestic foreign currency current account within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration; a written extension may be granted by SAFE in exceptional circumstances. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporter may either maintain the foreign currency in a current account settlement account or sell it against RMB to a designated tier-1 commercial bank at the prevailing spot rate. All export proceeds are subject to total-amount verification and must be supported by valid customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, contracts, and inspection certificates.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the relevant currency and clearing authority for the HKD treasury leg in Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Non-viable as a primary account provider. The People's Bank of China and SAFE do not recognize unlicensed foreign fintech EMI companies as authorized depository institutions for a mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; Airwallex, Nium, and similar HK/Singapore EMI platforms cannot open onshore RMB or foreign currency collection/settlement accounts and cannot issue bank settlement accounts for the FIP without a licensed Chinese commercial bank sponsor. Their lawful use is limited to ancillary cross-border payment aggregation or virtual account management under a licensed partner bank's custody; the FIP must retain its central operational and clearing accounts at a tier-1 PRC bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires the Hong Kong investor to execute a SWIFT MT103 wire transfer to the FIP's designated Capital Proceeds Foreign Exchange Settlement Account at a PRC tier-1 bank, using the SAFE foreign exchange business purpose code for foreign-invested partnership capital injection, typically SAFE code 621 for FDI establishment or 622 for subsequent contribution. The remittance must state the transaction nature as capital contribution and reference the FIP's foreign exchange filing number; the receiving bank will complete foreign exchange settlement verification, mark the funds as capital-proceeds, and will only convert to RMB after checking the negative list and genuine business fund usage. A mainland registered public accountant must then issue a capital verification report in the name of the FIP; surplus contributions beyond the registered capital are not permitted without SAFE approval.For export settlements of surgical suture products, the FIP should use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for open-account terms, irrevocable and where necessary confirmed commercial letters of credit for larger distributor orders, and documentary collections at sight D/P and D/A for mid-market customers. Each disbursement or receipt must be supported by the underlying export contract, pro-forma invoice, customs declaration, original bill of lading, packing list, inspection certificate, and where applicable export cargo insurance policy. PRC banks will require export proceeds to be collected within the 180-day explicit window and will file the trade into the SAFE total-amount verification system; any surcharge or long-tail payment should be identified with the correct trade coding and underlying documentation to avoid false-declaration penalties.Deliverable onshore FX forward contracts in USD/RMB and HKD/RMB executed with a PRC designated bank for genuine current-account trade exposure, tenor up to 12 months, with underlying trade documentation and no leverage beyond real exposure.Deliverable European-style FX options with the same real business requirement for trade settlement; barrier, binary and exotic options are prohibited; option premium must represent genuine hedging rather than speculation.Cross-currency interest rate swap or currency swap is only permissible for registered foreign debt or FDI-related capital amounts with SAFE pre-approval; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited in the onshore Chinese market and may only be considered offshore through an HKMA-authorized bank with clear underlying exposure, never booked by the mainland FIP.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable as a primary account provider. The People's Bank of China and SAFE do not recognize unlicensed foreign fintech EMI companies as authorized depository institutions for a mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; Airwallex, Nium, and similar HK/Singapore EMI platforms cannot open onshore RMB or foreign currency collection/settlement accounts and cannot issue bank settlement accounts for the FIP without a licensed Chinese commercial bank sponsor. Their lawful use is limited to ancillary cross-border payment aggregation or virtual account management under a licensed partner bank's custody; the FIP must retain its central operational and clearing accounts at a tier-1 PRC bank.
