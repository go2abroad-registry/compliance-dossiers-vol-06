Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Hong Kong SAR; online electronic portal: IRD e-Tax at www.etax.ird.gov.hk and GovHK Business Registration e-Service.

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) and Business Registration Regulations (Cap. 310A), a foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong as a partnership must register with the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencement. The Foreign-Invested Partnership must: (1) appoint a Hong Kong tax representative or authorised corporate services provider; (2) complete the prescribed Business Registration application form and attach a certified English copy of the partnership agreement, registration certificates or recognition evidence of each foreign partner, beneficial owner identification, and Hong Kong business address; (3) submit the application electronically through the IRD Business Registration e-Service; (4) pay the applicable Business Registration Fee and Levy; and (5) obtain the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the Hong Kong Tax Identification Number. A separate branch registration is required for each distinct place of business. No VAT/GST registration applies because Hong Kong imposes no value-added tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable - Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT, GST, sales and use tax, or equivalent indirect consumption tax; specific dutiable commodities remain subject to duty under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no Hong Kong VAT/GST registration threshold exists; all persons carrying on business in Hong Kong, regardless of turnover, are required to hold a Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 if they are not otherwise exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT zero-rating claim because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. Dental clear aligner sheets exported from Hong Kong are outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax and no 0% VAT rate can be claimed. Where the underlying manufacturing occurs in mainland China and the export is structured through the Hong Kong FIP, the mainland China export VAT rebate or zero-rating regime may apply to the Chinese manufacturing entity; in that scenario the statutory criteria include verified export customs clearance, collection of export foreign exchange, issuance of a special VAT invoice for exports, and lodgment of the export rebate application under the State Taxation Administration digital system.

  • Hong Kong Export Declaration lodged electronically with the Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) within 14 days of export
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill confirming actual export of the dental clear aligner sheets from Hong Kong
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract identifying the overseas buyer, product description, quantity, and export value
  • Bank credit advice, foreign exchange receipt record, or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) where required by the importing country or by any mainland China export VAT refund process

No real-time fiscal clearance, central e-invoicing API, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in Hong Kong. E-invoicing is voluntary; invoices may be issued electronically, and paper or electronic records must be retained for at least seven years to support profits tax returns and audits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Hong Kong imposes penalties for false or incomplete export declarations under Cap. 60, including fines and imprisonment, and the Inland Revenue Department may reopen assessments and issue additional profits tax penalties for dishonest returns. If a mainland China export VAT rebate is claimed, the State Taxation Administration may deny or claw back the rebate, impose a daily late payment surcharge, apply penalties of up to several times the tax underpaid, and pursue criminal liability where export documentation, foreign exchange repatriation, or customs declarations are fraudulent. All substantiation documents must be retained and made available under audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime in which the Renminbi is convertible for current account items but remains heavily regulated on the capital account. Foreign-invested partnerships established in Hong Kong SAR and operating through mainland China entities must channel cross-border funding through SAFE-regulated routes. Current account transactions such as trade-related payments, service fees, and profit remittances are generally permissible after verification of genuine underlying transactions, but capital account items such as equity injections, loans from foreign shareholders, and repatriation of invested capital require SAFE registration, onshore bank pre-approval, and supporting contracts. Export proceeds derived by the mainland operating entity must be repatriated into China within the statutory period and, depending on the configuration under the Foreign-Investment Partnership (FIP), may require conversion to CNY through designated domestic banks. Profit remittance hurdles include the need for audited financial statements, tax settlement certificates, and SAFE remittance forms; the Hong Kong parent FIP must therefore maintain clean audit trails from the onshore subsidiary to the offshore treasury. For a dental clear aligner manufacturer, intellectual property licensing royalties and equipment import payments are treated as current account items but still require an underlying contract and invoice matching to pass bank scrutiny. Overall, convertibility is workable for genuine trade in goods and services, but treasurers must expect documentation-heavy compliance and bank-level pre-approval for every cross-border flow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated through domestic designated banks; SAFE currently requires repatriation into China within 180 days from the date of export, and although the mandatory surrender ratio has been abolished, enterprises are required to sell or retain forex within SAFE-registered current account limits. Any retention above the enterprise's verified quota must be converted to CNY or held in a designated domestic foreign currency account with a legitimate trade justification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers without a licensed banking presence in Hong Kong SAR or a mainland China branch are generally not viable for this structure. The Foreign-Invested Partnership requires physical SAFE registration, in-person director verification, and onshore clearing through PBOC-licensed correspondent banks. Fintech EMIs that cannot provide direct access to CHAPS, FPS, and CNAPS clearing rails, or that lack a depository relationship with a mainland Chinese bank, will fail the compliance committee review. A restricted pool of licensed Hong Kong stored value facility operators and virtual banks may assist with ancillary payment flows, but the primary inbound capital injection and export proceeds surrender must sit on a full commercial bank license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong FIP to the mainland operating entity must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to a designated domestic RMB or foreign currency capital account at an SAFE-designated bank. The remittance must reference SAFE capital account registration code, the Foreign-Invested Partnership filing reference, and a precise purpose code such as '701110 - Investment in Partnership Capital' for equity injection or '701120' for partner loan. After receipt, the onshore bank requires the original SAFE Foreign-Invested Partnership Registration Certificate, a capital contribution schedule signed by all partners, and audited capital verification report prepared by a Chinese CPA firm. Funds cannot be disbursed for operational purposes until the capital verification report is filed with SAFE, and withdrawals must follow the declared business scope without diversion to securities, real estate, or unauthorized lending.Trade disbursements for the dental aligner sheets should be settled through ordinary SWIFT MT103 letters of credit or documentary collections through Bank of China or HSBC clearing channels. For high-value settlements to overseas distributors, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit adds bank-to-bank confirmation security; for lower-value freight-forwarding and raw material purchases, MT103 payment orders with invoice-level matching are acceptable. The Hong Kong treasury can maintain HKD and USD pools, but all disbursements to mainland vendors must first be converted to CNY with SAFE trade purpose codes and supporting transportation and customs documentation. For exports out of China, the underlying export proceeds must come back through the designated export account within the statutory 180-day window, unless SAFE extends the timeline for genuine construction or project-based trade.Onshore CNY/USD and CNY/HKD deliverable forward contracts with tenure up to twelve months for genuine current account trade exposure.Onshore FX swaps for temporary liquidity conversion between CNY and USD/HKD under SAFE authorization for licensed corporates.Offshore Hong Kong NDFs are available for hedging long-term RMB exposure where onshore deliverable products are maturity-limited, but they must be covered under a documented real operating exposure.Currency options are restricted to limited risk-reversal structures; exotic options are generally prohibited without special SAFE approval.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is imposed on a Hong Kong registered limited partnership merely under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). A mandatory statutory audit applies where the entity is incorporated as a Hong Kong company and does not qualify for the small private company exemption; the exemption requires annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000, total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000, no more than 100 employees in the preceding financial year, the company must not be a member of a group, and a unanimous members' resolution to dispense with the audit must be passed. Banking, insurance, licensed corporations, and entities subject to sectoral regulator audit conditions are always subject to mandatory independent certified audit regardless of turnover, assets, or headcount. For a Hong Kong limited partnership, audited accounts may also become compulsory under financing covenants, the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637), or regulatory licensing conditions imposed by a professional body.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 0 (no statutory monetary threshold for Tier 1 local file, but the IRD requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for connected transactions, including a general TP statement, where the transaction value exceeds HKD 5,000,000 in aggregate, or smaller for specified types; Master File required when the group's consolidated turnover exceeds HKD 1,000,000,000 and the value of connected transactions exceeds HKD 50,000,000, or for certain high-risk transactions).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply the OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as a standalone regime; instead, the IRD applies the territorial source principle and general anti-abuse rules (GAAR) under Section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For a Hong Kong FIP acting as a procurement, distribution, or licensing vehicle for a China parent manufacturer of dental clear aligners, the IRD may challenge the FIP's Hong Kong tax residence and profit attribution of the fact that the FIP lacks adequate commercial substance, office premises, employees, and decision-making functions in Hong Kong. Effective 1 January 2023, the IRD's updated DIPN 60 and the transfer pricing rules require the FIP to demonstrate economic substance (personnel, premises, functions, assets, and risks) to support the arm's-length allocation of profits. The FIP should also comply with the Hong Kong Business Registration Ordinance and maintain actual business operations to avoid being treated as a shell or conduit entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Profits Tax & Salaries Tax)

Statutory sanction: Upon summary conviction: fine of HKD 10,000 plus 3 times the amount of tax undercharged; upon indictment: fine of HKD 50,000 plus 3 times the tax undercharged and imprisonment for up to 3 years. Civil penalties for incorrect returns (without reasonable excuse) may reach 100% of the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners of the FIP may be personally liable for the tax, penalties, and costs if they knowingly signed false returns or directed the evasion; the corporate veil may be pierced where fraud is proven. The IRD may issue a direction to the FIP to pay, and directors may be subject to travel bans, restriction orders, and asset freezing orders. Imprisonment of up to 3 years applies; in egregious cases, disqualification as a company director for up to 15 years may be imposed by the court.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance in Hong Kong

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits (China may impose full 10% withholding tax on dividends/royalties/interest instead of the reduced 5%/7% DTA rates), recharacterization of income as taxable in Hong Kong, and imposition of additional tax, penalties, and interest. Under Section 61A, the IRD may ignore any artificial transaction and assess the tax as if the transaction had not been entered into; penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners may be assessed personally for taxes arising from the artificial arrangement if they participated in the scheme. They may also face personal liability for the FIP's debts if the FIP is deemed a sham, and may be subject to civil restitution claims by creditors, travel bans, and disqualification orders. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) provides for personal liability of directors for business carried on with intent to defraud creditors.
AML / KYC / Foreign Corrupt Practices Act (FCPA) & AML Risk for Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO), failure to conduct customer due diligence or report suspicious transactions carries a fine of up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years per offence. Financial institutions and payment service providers may freeze accounts and report to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit (JFIU). Unlicensed medical device trading in Hong Kong (under the Medical Devices Administrative Control System) may lead to product seizure and prohibition orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners can be criminally liable for AML failures if consent or connivance is shown. Senior management may face personal fines, imprisonment, and travel restrictions where money laundering linked to the FIP's payment flows is proven. Additionally, under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance, public officials involved in certification processes are subject to anti-corruption penalties.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: If the IRD determines that the transfer pricing results in a Hong Kong profit shift, it may assess additional profits tax (up to 16.5% on the adjusted amount) plus a 10% surcharge for late payment and penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax for failure to keep proper records. Interest on underpaid tax at the prevailing rate is also charged.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners who knowingly sign false transfer pricing disclosures or who fail to cause the FIP to maintain adequate documentation may be personally liable for penalties. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the responsible person of a partnership is personally liable for tax returns and penalties. Travel bans may be imposed to compel compliance with tax payment orders.
False Statements to the IRD and Forged Documents

Statutory sanction: Conviction on indictment for making a false statement or entry in a tax return: fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years. Forging, altering, or destroying documents with intent to deceive the IRD: fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fraudulent misrepresentation; the court may order restitution to the IRD and order personal payment of the undercharged tax plus penalties. Persistent offenders face travel bans and compulsory surrender of passports under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance investigation powers.
