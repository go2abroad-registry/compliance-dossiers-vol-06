Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong SAR; electronic filing portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk)

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong partnership, the procedure to obtain a Hong Kong Business Registration (BR) number is: (1) Apply for a Business Registration Certificate using Form IRBR1 (or IRBR2 for branch) within 1 month of commencing business. (2) Provide the business's registered name, principal address, business nature (including ISIC 3250/NAICS 334517), the partnership agreement, and particulars of the partners (including identity documents and addresses). (3) Pay the registration levy and fee per the Business Registration Ordinance. (4) The applicant will receive a Business Registration Certificate, which displays the Business Registration Number (also the taxpayer identification number). This BR number serves as the de facto TIN for tax filing. There is no separate VAT/GST ID. Non-resident partnerships can be represented by a certified public accountant or tax adviser. The application can be made electronically through the eTAX portal or by paper.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible - A Hong Kong partnership is a transparent entity for Profits Tax purposes. The partnership itself is not a separate taxable person; each partner is assessed individually on their share of partnership profits derived from Hong Kong. As an FIP (Foreign-Invested Partnership), it is treated similarly.

        
Available election forms
None available. Hong Kong does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. Partnerships are automatically treated as pass-through. Individual partners may elect for Personal Assessment (v. separate Profits Tax), but that election is personal in nature. No other classification elections exist.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No General VAT/GST/Sales Tax (Hong Kong imposes customs duty only on a limited set of goods)

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable - no VAT/GST regime. Under the Business Registration Ordinance, every business must register within one month of commencement irrespective of turnover; however, certain small entities with monthly average sales not exceeding HK$500,000 may apply for a waiver of the business registration fee (but still must obtain a BR certificate).

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on any goods or services, regardless of export or local supply. Therefore, there is no 'zero-rating' regime. Medical devices (X-ray flat panel detectors) are not subject to customs duty under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance. Exports from Hong Kong are free of indirect tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Hong Kong SAR itself does not exercise restrictive exchange controls, but the mainland-incorporated foreign-invested partnership is subject to PRC foreign exchange jurisdiction. The PRC operates a managed non-fully-convertible currency regime. The current account has been liberalized for genuine trade in goods and services, enabling the FIP to make and receive operational payments through designated banks under authentic transaction documents, but the capital account remains tightly controlled. An FIP must register all inbound direct investment with SAFE through a designated bank before its Hong Kong partners inject capital. The RMB is convertible on the current account, while capital account flows are executable only after SAFE registration, approval, or quota allocation; no automatic convertibility applies to partnership distributions or liquidation proceeds. Profit remittance from the FIP to a Hong Kong partner requires audited financial statements, tax payment vouchers, partnership profit allocation statements, and clearing of all tax obligations. Although the FIP is pass-through eligible for income tax, the bank still treats distribution as a capital-account outbound remittance and will require proof that the beneficiary is the ultimate beneficial owner. Export proceeds must be repatriated into a SAFE-registered settlement account, but the FIP may hold foreign currency or convert it to RMB, subject to SAFE post-event monitoring.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into the PRC within 180 days of customs clearance. There is no mandatory surrender ratio; 0% of the proceeds must be forcibly converted, and an FIP may retain 100% in a SAFE-registered foreign currency settlement account. Any elective conversion to RMB must be executed by a designated bank and reported through the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; deferred receipts beyond 180 days require trade balance notification to SAFE.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the offshore Hong Kong clearing bank leg.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore fintech EMI platforms are not viable for the FIP's statutory treasury. They are legally barred from operating as PRC deposit-taking banks, cannot hold the FIP's Chinese unified social credit code account, cannot access SAFE's cross-border FX monitoring systems, and cannot open a USD/HKD capital account for FDI registration. Such EMIs may at most serve as a supplementary offshore card platform for a Hong Kong foreign partner's own treasury, but they must not be used for capital contributions, export proceeds, or trade settlements involving the PRC FIP.
