Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, operated through the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) national portal of the State Taxation Administration; for the Hong Kong SAR side of the structure, the Inland Revenue Department of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region is the competent authority for profits tax and business registration-related tax matters.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is established under the Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China and the registration rules for partnerships with foreign investment. It must first obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the business license carries the Unified Social Credit Code, which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all Chinese tax registrants. Step 2: Within 30 days after the business license is issued, the FIP must register for tax with the local competent office of the State Taxation Administration (STA) through the e-Tax electronic portal; no separate VAT ID number is issued because the Unified Social Credit Code is used as the VAT and enterprise income tax file number. Step 3: If the FIP undertakes exports of heparin sodium goods, it must also register with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) using the China International Trade Single Window to obtain the customs enterprise registration code, and must complete trade-in-goods foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Step 4: Foreign non-resident partners must submit their home-jurisdiction tax identification number, registration certificate and tax-residence certificate; a Hong Kong partner will obtain a Hong Kong Resident Certificate from the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR to claim treaty or arrangement benefits under the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Double Taxation Arrangement. Non-resident partners are taxed on their attributable China-source income through the partnership withholding and reporting mechanism.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the FIP is, for PRC enterprise income tax purposes, fiscally transparent, and partners are taxed on their distributive shares. For PRC VAT purposes, the FIP is a taxpayer in its own capacity and is separately registered for VAT/general taxpayer obligation.

Available election forms

None: PRC law provides no check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election for foreign-invested partnerships; pass-through treatment is mandatory. The only available election is voluntary registration as a PRC VAT general taxpayer before the RMB 5,000,000 turnover threshold, which affects input VAT credit mechanics rather than entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 RMB of annual taxable sales in a rolling 12-month period is the mandatory PRC VAT general taxpayer registration threshold. There is no threshold for initial tax registration; every newly established FIP must register with the STA from inception and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code as the VAT identifier.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating of export sales of heparin sodium anticoagulant bulk goods is available if the FIP is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory under a valid customs export declaration, the sale is made to an overseas buyer under an export contract, the export shipment is evidenced by a transport document, the FIP owns the goods and (if a trading/foreign-trade enterprise) has obtained lawful input VAT invoices or (if a production enterprise) has actually manufactured the drugs, and the export proceeds are received in foreign exchange within the statutory period and reported to SAFE. The export VAT refund is implemented as exemption and rebate for foreign-trade (trading) enterprises and as exemption, credit and rebate for production enterprises.

  • Customs Export Declaration of the People's Republic of China (Export Goods Declaration Form), including the special copy marked for export tax refund, filed via the China International Trade Single Window
  • Marine Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Special VAT invoice(s) for the taxable inputs and production/purchase cost records supporting the VAT rebate computation
  • Bank receipt certificate for the export proceeds, comprising the PRC authorized foreign exchange bank settlement/credit advice and the SAFE trade-in-goods collection verification record
  • Letter of Credit negotiation advice or SWIFT wire transfer confirmation where settlement is made through L/C or SWIFT

China mandates real-time fiscal clearance through the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system and the Golden Tax Phase IV VAT Invoice Management System. Invoices must be issued through the e-Tax official platform or an authority-approved tax-control software/API, digitally signed with a unique tax-control code, and transmitted to the STA central clearing database immediately at issuance. The National VAT Invoice Checking Platform validates each invoice before a buyer can claim input VAT deduction, and anti-fraud digital stamping is reinforced by automatic cross-matching of invoice data, GACC customs export declarations and SAFE foreign exchange collection records.

Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, export tax refunds are subject to retrospective clawback if the customs declaration, transport documents, invoice data, or foreign exchange receipts are found to be false, unsupported, inconsistent with GACC/SAFE records, or if the goods never actually left China or the sales proceeds were not repatriated within the prescribed deadline. The STA will recapture any zero-rated refund already paid, assess late payment interest, and impose an administrative penalty of 50 percent to five times the fraudulent refund amount. False-export VAT rebate fraud is criminally actionable under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with liability for fines, imprisonment of responsible personnel and forfeiture of illegally obtained refunds; no limitation period applies to recovery of taxes obtained through fraud, evasion or intentional misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a current-account convertible but capital-account regulated regime. Current account transactions, including trade in goods, services, and dividend distributions after prior tax settlement, are generally permitted through designated banks after documentary verification; no individual current-account transaction needs SAFE approval unless it falls under a special control list. By contrast, capital account items, including foreign direct investment contributions, partnership capital withdrawals, cross-border loans, and foreign-currency capital conversions, are subject to mandatory SAFE registration and may only be routed through dedicated capital accounts. RMB is fully convertible for trade settlement within the onshore market, but is not freely convertible for speculative capital transactions; offshore CNH outside Mainland operates under a different clearing system and is unavailable to the onshore FIP for spot conversion. Profit remittance by an FIP is treated as current account distribution only after the audited financial statements, partner resolution, relevant PRC tax certificates, and proof of tax payment are presented; the bank then files a current-account payment declaration and remits via the FIP's domestic settlement account. Practical hurdles include operational approval for distributions, limited ability to carry undistributed profits without explanation, and enhanced compliance checks when a Hong Kong partner routes capital through intermediate holding structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be collected and repatriated through a licensed domestic account within 180 days after the date of customs export declaration. The current statutory mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; the FIP may retain 100% of accepted export proceeds in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert them into RMB at the market rate via a designated foreign-exchange bank. All retained balances are subject to SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring, and each payment must be evidence-matched with customs declarations and invoices. If proceeds remain outstanding beyond 180 days, the FIP must submit the underlying contract, logistics evidence, and collection schedule to SAFE; persistent overdues may trigger downgrade to Category B or C and impose document-intensive daily verification on future FX settlements.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) retains supervision over the Hong Kong treasury booking layer and offshore CNH clearing points.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Hong Kong licensed stored value facilities and neobanks, cannot legally serve as the primary deposit-taking, SAFE-registration, or FX-settlement account provider for a Mainland China FIP. A China FIP must maintain its onshore account and SAFE capital/trade files with a licensed PRC bank to satisfy the SAFE FDI register, customs reconciliation, tax processing, and foreign-exchange controls. Offshore EMIs cannot receive inbound FDI with SAFE registration, cannot collect export proceeds in compliance with the 180-day rule, cannot issue or settle PRC trade LCs, and are not eligible for CIPS. An offshore EMI may act only as a secondary liquidity sweep engine in the Hong Kong treasury vehicle after all compliance obligations have been met onshore; even then, the FIP must ensure no cross-border pooling occurs without an underlying approved trade or investment transaction. Using an EMI as a principal corporate account would constitute unlawful unlicensed payment business under PBOC regulations and invite regulatory rejection.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Hong Kong licensed stored value facilities and neobanks, cannot legally serve as the primary deposit-taking, SAFE-registration, or FX-settlement account provider for a Mainland China FIP. A China FIP must maintain its onshore account and SAFE capital/trade files with a licensed PRC bank to satisfy the SAFE FDI register, customs reconciliation, tax processing, and foreign-exchange controls. Offshore EMIs cannot receive inbound FDI with SAFE registration, cannot collect export proceeds in compliance with the 180-day rule, cannot issue or settle PRC trade LCs, and are not eligible for CIPS. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where any related-party transaction threshold is exceeded: tangible-property transactions in excess of RMB 200 million, intangible-property or financial-asset transfers in excess of RMB 100 million, or other services and intra-group transactions in excess of RMB 40 million. A Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The FIP must also file the annual related-party transaction schedule and maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation for each controlled transaction with the Hong Kong affiliate.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies a robust substance-over-form doctrine under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT GAAR rules. An FIP with Chinese manufacturing activity and Hong Kong profit-taking must prove the offshore partner performs Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in the claimed jurisdiction. For low-molecular-weight heparin sodium fractionation, the CIGA include raw-material procurement, chemical fractionation, pharmacopeia and quality certification, regulatory compliance, logistics, inventory risk, and pricing decisions, all of which must be performed in China by Chinese personnel and assets; holding board meetings in Hong Kong, maintaining a bank account there, or appointing Hong Kong resident directors without operational control is not sufficient.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse (Shell Partnership)

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Law on Tax Administration and Collection Article 63, tax evasion is penalised at 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax; under EIT Law Article 47 the tax authority may recharacterise the arrangement and assess tax plus interest without limitation in fraud cases. Criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may apply where deliberate concealment or false declaration is proven.

Director personal liability: The general partner and managing partner are personally and jointly liable for unpaid partnership taxes and partnership debts under Partnership Enterprise Law Articles 39 and 40. If a corporate director knowingly caused false allocations, the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20 and personal assets may be seized. The tax authority may impose an exit-control travel ban preventing departure from China until all tax is settled; a criminal conviction for tax evasion can result in imprisonment up to three years, or up to seven years for very large amounts, together with fines and disqualification from serving as a director or manager.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41 and 44, the tax authority may make an arm's-length transfer-pricing adjustment, deny deductions for undocumented intercompany fees, and assess interest from the original transaction year. LTAC Article 62 imposes penalties for failure to maintain documentation, and Article 63 imposes a 50% to 500% evasion penalty where the transfer-pricing documentation is materially false.

Director personal liability: The CFO and managing director who certified false transfer-pricing documentation may be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax. Civil asset restitution may be ordered; departure is barred under the tax-exit blacklist while tax remains outstanding; and criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of three to seven years for the responsible officers if the base-erosion plan constitutes deliberate evasion.
Environmental, Pharmaceutical Licensing, and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Environmental Protection Law Article 59, continuing environmental violations attract daily fines; under Criminal Law Article 338, serious pollution is punishable by imprisonment up to three years, or three to seven years where serious consequences result. Unlicensed pharmaceutical manufacturing violates the PRC Drug Administration Law and leads to confiscation, administrative fines, and licence revocation. Failure to use regulated payment rails also triggers anti-money laundering penalties in addition to withholding tax and evasion sanctions under LTAC Articles 62 and 63.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner may be personally liable for environmental remediation costs under the Civil Code and Environmental Protection Law, and the corporate veil is pierced where personal fault is established. Responsible officers face criminal imprisonment for environmental pollution, tax evasion, and money laundering; travel bans and blacklisting apply; and conviction disqualifies the individual from managing a foreign-invested partnership and from holding a pharmaceutical manufacturing licence for active pharmaceutical ingredients in China.
