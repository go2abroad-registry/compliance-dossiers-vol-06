Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong SAR does not impose any minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account requirement for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Capital contributions are determined solely by the Partnership Agreement and may be cash or in-kind.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Fire Services Department under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), including automatic fire detection, alarm systems, sprinklers, portable extinguishers, and emergency exit compliance; structural load inspection and certification from a registered structural engineer or the Buildings Department for heavy equipment and rooftop installations; electrical installation inspection and approval by the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department; and workplace safety approval and a completed risk assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509), including chemical waste producer registration and emergency response protocols.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, via the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform; for Hong Kong SAR-specific matters, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via eTAX, noting that Hong Kong does not levy VAT, GST, or general sales tax.

E-government filing portal

Apply to the local Market Supervision Administration under the Partnership Enterprise Law of the PRC and the Measures for the Administration of Establishment of Foreign-Invested Partnerships for a Foreign-Invested Partnership business license; foreign non-resident partners must submit home-jurisdiction incorporation certificates, tax residence certificates, and/or passport-equivalent identity documents, notarized or apostilled as required. After approval, the Market Supervision Administration automatically transmits the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) to the STA under the multi-license integration reform; the USCC serves as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier, superseding any separate TIN certificate. The FIP must then activate electronic tax registration on the STA National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform, appoint the legal representative or authorized tax agent, and obtain an electronic tax payment account. For VAT operations, the FIP must register its VAT taxpayer profile, elect general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover exceeds or is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or voluntarily apply for general taxpayer status if below the threshold, and obtain the tax UKey for issuing Special Value-Added Tax Invoices. For exports, the FIP must complete foreign trade operator filing with the Ministry of Commerce, register with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) to obtain a customs declaration code, and register foreign exchange receipt/verification arrangements with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through its designated bank. Non-resident partners without a PRC TIN must either provide their home-country TIN or obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number from the STA for withholding and information reporting.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for income tax: the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership is mandatorily transparent under Cai Shui [2008] No. 159, and no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election is available. For indirect tax purposes, a small-scale VAT taxpayer may voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status; for customs/export purposes, the FIP may elect the general trade or processing trade export regime under GACC rules where eligible.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable turnover is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; active FIPs are nonetheless VAT-registered through the Unified Social Credit Code, with turnover below the threshold resulting in small-scale taxpayer treatment unless voluntary general taxpayer registration is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC general-taxpayer FIP may claim 0% VAT on the export of electronic patient monitors where: (i) the goods physically depart from PRC customs territory under an approved GACC customs export declaration; (ii) the FIP holds valid foreign trade operator filing, GACC customs registration, and, for medical devices, the NMPA registration and export sales certificate; (iii) the export is evidenced by commercial invoice, contract, packing list, bill of lading/waybill, and customs declaration; (iv) export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC bank account through a designated foreign-exchange bank within the statutory deadline; and (v) the FIP timely files the export tax refund declaration through the STA electronic tax portal using the manufacturing enterprise exemption-offset-refund method. Small-scale taxpayers may receive export exemption without input VAT refund, so substantive export refunds require general VAT taxpayer status.

  • GACC-approved Customs Export Declaration demonstrating physical export and customs clearance status
  • Duly signed export sales contract, commercial invoice, and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, airway waybill, or other transport document evidencing shipment from a PRC customs port
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt record or Inward Remittance Certificate proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT export tax refund (exemption/offset/refund) declaration submitted via the STA electronic tax platform
  • NMPA medical device registration certificate and export sales certificate for the electronic patient monitor
  • Certificate of Origin or other export licensing/permit documents required by the HS classification
  • Customs-tax data matching record confirming the GACC export declaration has been transmitted to the STA for refund processing

The PRC enforces real-time fiscal clearance under the Golden Tax System IV and the fully digitalized electronic invoice system. All VAT special invoices must be digitally signed with the tax UKey and submitted to the STA e-invoice service platform, where the tax authority automatically validates, stamps, and cross-matches invoice data against sales declarations, input VAT credit claims, and export customs declarations. Fully digitalized e-invoices carry an embedded anti-fraud digital stamp and are cleared in near-real time; invoices that fail tax authority clearance cannot be credited or used to substantiate export zero-rating.

The STA, GACC, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit export zero-rating claims and refund applications. Where export zero-rating is unsupported, fraudulent, or based on false documentation, the STA will claw back the output VAT exemption and/or input VAT refund, disallow carry-forward input credits, and charge late-payment interest at 0.05% per day on the outstanding tax. Under Article 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent procurement of export tax refunds attracts an administrative fine of 100% to 500% of the refund fraudulently obtained; where the amount is material, criminal liability for VAT-rebate fraud may follow under the Criminal Law. For non-fraudulent errors, the assessment/collection period is generally three years, extended to five years in special circumstances; for fraudulent refund claims, recovery is unlimited. SAFE may separately impose penalties for failure to repatriate export proceeds, and the FIP may be suspended from future export tax rebate processing pending enhanced customs and tax audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB remains partially convertible. Current-account transactions, including export proceeds and trade-related working capital, may be settled freely through designated banks after genuine underlying documentation and customs verification. Capital-account transactions are subject to SAFE registration, quota, and purpose discipline. The FIP's cross-border capital contributions, equity injections, profit distributions, liquidation proceeds, and shareholder loans must be structured through a SAFE foreign-exchange registration record; banks verify each payment against that record before converting or remitting. For an export-oriented ECG monitor assembler, receipt of USD/HKD sales proceeds is normally permitted, but any surplus held in foreign currency must be justified by future trade payment schedules or retained under current-account verification. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is lawful after audited financial statements, tax filing, and payment of applicable withholding taxes, but the bank will require evidence of accumulated distributable profits and a dividend remittance tax clearance. The practical effect is that convertibility is not unrestricted: capital flows and profit repatriation are pipeline-based and documentation-heavy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign-exchange current-account and reported in the bank's trade-in-goods monitoring system within 180 calendar days from the date of export declaration unless SAFE approves an exceptional extension for bonded or overseas working capital. There is no mandatory statutory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain up to 100% of verified receipts in foreign currency or sell them to the bank at the spot rate. However, all proceeds must be supported by the customs declaration number, invoice, bill of lading, and the associated ISIC 3250 medical-device export license and registration to avoid write-off discrepancies.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), for the onshore Foreign-Invested Partnership; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong corridor but does not relax PRC capital-account controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as the primary or sole banking relationship. A PRC-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership may not lawfully outsource capital-account receipt, export proceeds verification, or SAFE filing functions to an unlicensed fintech EMI. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business cannot establish onshore settlement accounts or issue SAFE-recognised receipt confirmations for FDI capital injections, export surrender, or dividend repatriation. They may assist the Hong Kong parent or investor in collecting customer payments or pre-funding trade flows outside mainland China, but every cross-border leg into or out of the FIP must clear through a licensed Chinese domestic bank enrolled in the SAFE reporting system. The recommended architecture is a licensed bank at the onshore spine with an optional fintech EMI for treasury visibility in Hong Kong only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong investor must flow from the investor's Hong Kong bank account via SWIFT MT103, or the equivalent CNPAS or RTGS transfer, to the FIP's onshore Capital Settlement Account at the selected Chinese bank. Before the remittance, the FIP must complete SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration and receive the applicable registration or filing code. The SWIFT 70 or 72 tag must cite the SAFE FDI registration number and the approved inward purpose code for direct investment capital contribution. The bank will convert to RMB and credit the FIP's ordinary current account only after SAFE cross-border payment monitoring, capital verification, and where applicable, completion of a qualified accountant's capital contribution verification certificate. The Hong Kong investor must remit from its own licensed bank account and maintain source of funds records ready for the onshore bank's due diligence review.Outbound disbursements for component sourcing, contract manufacturing, and trade settlements should use SWIFT MT103 for high-speed operative payments such as raw materials and technical service fees, irrevocable sight commercial letters of credit for raw-material imports and distributor-backed export transactions, and documentary collections on DP or DA terms for lower-risk open-account trade. Every outgoing remittance must be tagged with the correct SAFE payment purpose code and supported by customs declaration, invoice, contract, and cargo movement evidence where required. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as the primary or sole banking relationship. A PRC-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership may not lawfully outsource capital-account receipt, export proceeds verification, or SAFE filing functions to an unlicensed fintech EMI. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business cannot establish onshore settlement accounts or issue SAFE-recognised receipt confirmations for FDI capital injections, export surrender, or dividend repatriation. They may assist the Hong Kong parent or investor in collecting customer payments or pre-funding trade flows outside mainland China, but every cross-border leg into or out of the FIP must clear through a licensed Chinese domestic bank enrolled in the SAFE reporting system. The recommended architecture is a licensed bank at the onshore spine with an optional fintech EMI for treasury visibility in Hong Kong only.
