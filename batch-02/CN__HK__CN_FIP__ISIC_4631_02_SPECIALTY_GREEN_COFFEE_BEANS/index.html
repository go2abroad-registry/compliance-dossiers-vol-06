Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Market Entity Registration Regulations impose no minimum registered capital, no minimum paid-up capital, and no blocked escrow account for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The partnership agreement must state the type, amount, method, and time of each partner contribution. Contributions may be in cash, in-kind property, intellectual property, land-use rights, services, or other lawful property rights as agreed. A general partner bears unlimited liability for partnership obligations; a limited partner is liable only to the extent of its agreed contribution. The amount and timing of capital calls are contractual matters to be dealt with in the partnership agreement and the first board-equivalent resolution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office (BRO), with electronic filing and account management through the GovHK/eTAX online portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity or foreign-invested partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The application is lodged online via GovHK/eTAX or in paper form at the BRO. Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing an 8-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the statutory Hong Kong taxpayer identifier for business registration and income tax files. Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, no separate VAT identification number is issued. If the foreign partnership has no physical presence in Hong Kong but receives chargeable profits, it should appoint a tax representative and notify the IRD of the Hong Kong business address and responsible persons; the IRD will then issue a Profits Tax file number for tax return purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under Hong Kong Profits Tax, a partnership is fiscally transparent and is not taxed at entity level; assessable profits are computed at partnership level and then assessed upon each partner in accordance with the partnership profit-sharing ratio. The territorial source principle also applies to the underlying profits.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box regime or a hybrid mismatch election. The territorial source principle and partnership pass-through treatment under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) apply mandatorily.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales Tax - Hong Kong is a free port; the only indirect controls are excise duties on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and statutory import/export declaration requirements under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold. Any person or partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), irrespective of gross turnover or profit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept because Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST. Unroasted green coffee beans are not dutiable in Hong Kong, and cross-border movements are subject only to statutory import/export declaration requirements. For Profits Tax purposes, an export sale of goods will not be chargeable if the trading profits are sourced offshore; eligibility depends on evidence that the contracts for sale were negotiated and concluded outside Hong Kong, the goods were delivered outside Hong Kong, and no essential trading operations were performed in Hong Kong.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border movement of the green coffee beans
  • Commercial Invoice and detailed Packing List
  • Purchase contract for the originating green coffee bean supply and the overseas sales contract
  • Correspondence, order confirmations, and negotiation records demonstrating the place of contract formation
  • Bank settlement records, including SWIFT MT103 or Documentary Letter of Credit records confirming receipt of export proceeds

Taxpayers may issue electronic or paper invoices, but all books and records must be retained to support the territorial source assessment; no continuous transaction control system applies to green coffee bean distribution.

No VAT clawback regime applies because no VAT refund is paid. However, false, incomplete, or misleading customs import/export declarations can trigger penalties and prosecution under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), including fines and imprisonment. In a Profits Tax audit, an unsupported claim that profits are offshore will be treated as Hong Kong source income, leading to additional assessments, interest, and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); where incorrect returns are made knowingly or negligently, the penalty may be treble the tax undercharged, plus prosecution for wilful evasion, and the IRD may reopen assessments within the applicable statutory limitation period for fraud or omission cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the RMB is convertible for current account transactions, while capital account transactions remain controlled by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in mainland China by a Hong Kong corporate investor is treated as a Chinese resident enterprise for foreign-exchange purposes. Its registered capital may be injected as foreign currency or RMB under a completed foreign direct investment registration, and the foreign currency can be settled into RMB solely for lawful operating use within the Negative List. Profit distributions to the Hong Kong partner are classified as current account remittances and can be transferred abroad after the FIP provides a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and a registered partner distribution resolution. However, funds cannot be diverted to activities outside the declared business scope, such as intercompany lending, non-business real estate purchases, or securities investment without pre-approval. The practical convertibility obstacle is not administrative prohibition but strict authenticity verification: every conversion and remittance requires granular supporting documentation, including underlying trade contracts, customs declarations, tax proofs, and bank correspondence. The FIP must therefore maintain a complete documentary archive in order to defend any foreign-exchange conversion or outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE goods-trade foreign-exchange administration, the FIP is classified as a Category A trader unless an audit triggers Category B or Category C treatment. For any future export-oriented re-export of green coffee, the export customs declaration proceeds must be repatriated to mainland China within 90 calendar days from the date of customs declaration. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for Category A enterprises; the FIP may retain the full receipt in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert any portion into RMB at the prevailing market rate. Category B and Category C enterprises face temporary suspension of foreign-currency retention privileges, mandatory 100% conversion of receipts, and enhanced verification for every repatriated payment until the bank or SAFE reclassifies them.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acting as regulator only for Hong Kong-leg treasury and parent-company clearing assets.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability: LOW for the onshore treasury function. Foreign digital banks and electronic money institutions such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Currenxie do not hold a PRC corporate deposit-taking licence and cannot provide RMB clearing, foreign-exchange settlement, or capital project accounts for a mainland-established FIP. PBOC and SAFE regulations require corporate current accounts and foreign-exchange accounts to be opened at licensed banks in China; an offshore EMI account would not satisfy capital verification, would not produce acceptable bank evidence for completing the foreign direct investment registration, and would immediately appear suspicious to the Chinese counterparty bank. An EMI can be used only as a data or treasury dashboard, or for non-RMB payment rails outside China, and even then only when the underlying settlement ultimately clears through an onshore licensed bank participant. Therefore, the FIP must open its primary operational and capital accounts with a domestic Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound investment from the Hong Kong partner must first complete the FIP's foreign-exchange registration under SAFE's bank-delegated administration. The wire is sent by SWIFT MT103 using the investment purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contribution and remitted to the FIP's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. On receipt, the bank authenticates the advance receipt against the registered FDI amount, screens counterparty sanctions, and releases the funds for RMB settlement only after the Negative List compliance check. A Chinese certified public accountant then validates the contribution amount and issues a capital verification report, which becomes the legal basis for the FIP's audited capital contribution and future profit repatriation.Outbound disbursements for green coffee purchases must be grounded in genuine trade transactions. After a stable supplier relationship is established, SWIFT MT103 interbank wires can be used for open-account settlement. Documentary collections at sight are suitable for mid-risk jurisdictions. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every entity registered in Hong Kong, including a Foreign-Invested Partnership taxed as a pass-through entity, is required to prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view and to have those financial statements audited by a certified public accountant (practising), regardless of turnover, balance sheet size, or headcount, except where the entity qualifies for the small entity reporting exemption under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance; however, even for small entities, if the partnership's gross revenue exceeds HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,287,000) for a year, an independent audit is compulsory. For larger entities, the compulsory audit threshold is met when any two of the following are present in a financial year: turnover exceeds HKD 100,000,000 (approx. USD 12,870,000); total assets exceed HKD 100,000,000; or average number of employees exceeds 100. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership in the specialty green coffee bean distribution business, the normal operating revenue of containerized single-origin green coffee sales will in most commercially active cases exceed the HKD 10,000,000 threshold, thereby mandating a full independent CPA audit. Further, all partnerships that file a profits tax return with the Inland Revenue Department must attach audited financial statements unless they fall within the narrow de minimis category granted by the IRD, which requires total gross revenue under HKD 2,000,000 (approximately USD 257,000) for the year of assessment; above that, audited statements are statutorily required.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard profits tax rate for unincorporated partnerships; 7.5% concessionary rate on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits under the two-tiered profits tax regime

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Hong Kong imposes no withholding tax on dividends, no dividend distribution tax, and no withholding on partnership profit distributions; repatriation of coffee-trading profits to non-resident partners is tax-free in source and no Form IR1314 relief is required for distributions

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: mandatory where controlled property transfers, provision of services, or intangible property usage exceed HKD 4,000,000 per category and where financing transactions exceed HKD 8,000,000; Master File: mandatory for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue >= HKD 680,000,000 AND (total assets >= HKD 550,000,000 OR average employees >= 100); documentation must be contemporaneous and prepared by the end of the basis period, with the TP report due to IRD within 30 days of request; DIPN 59 provides penalty mitigation for timely voluntary disclosure

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not enact the Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the territorial-source doctrine and DTA entitlement impose de facto substance requirements: an FIP claiming offshore profits must demonstrate that the core income-generating operations (contract negotiation, title passage, risk assumption, freight and insurance arrangements) occur outside Hong Kong; case law (ING Baring, Datatronic) requires real operational integration and rejection of ‘paper-only’ booking; the GAAR (IRO s.61B) and anti-avoidance sections 61/61A allow the IRD to re-attribute profits to the real substance location and impose additional assessment up to 100% of tax avoided

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction under IRO s.82: fine of HKD 50,000 and 3 times the tax undercharged plus imprisonment up to 3 years; indictment: fine of HKD 100,000 and 3 times tax undercharged plus imprisonment up to 5 years; the IRD may issue an estimated assessment and charge interests at official rates up to 8% per annum

Director personal liability: Each partner is jointly and severally liable for the partnership’s unpaid tax; civil restitution extends to a partner’s personal assets; the IRD may obtain a departure prevention direction (travel ban) under IRO s.77 and institute bankruptcy/liquidation proceedings
Offshore-Claim Abuse / Shell Structure

Statutory sanction: GAAR (IRO s.61B) permits the Commissioner to disregard the artificial structure and assess HK profits on the full trading margin; additional assessment up to 100% of avoided tax plus a 100% penalty for deliberate concealment under the MAP, with interest compounding daily

Director personal liability: The IRD can pierce the partnership veil and collect from a former partner who knowingly enjoyed the tax benefit; directors of a corporate partner face disqualification orders and travel bans; criminal liability may follow if the offshore claim is supported by forged LC documents or false board minutes
AML / Structuring / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Failure to notify chargeability under IRO s.51(1): fine of HKD 10,000 plus 3 times tax undercharged; AML violations under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO): fine up to HKD 5 million and imprisonment up to 14 years for money laundering; restraint orders under the OSCO can freeze all partnership and personal accounts

Director personal liability: A partner who knowingly processes the proceeds through personal accounts is personally liable; courts may impose civil asset forfeiture, disqualification and a travel ban pending trial; banks will file STRs and close facilities permanently, causing commercial collapse of the coffee import business
Failure to Maintain Records / TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: First conviction: fine up to HKD 100,000; subsequent conviction: fine up to HKD 200,000; the IRD may reject the accounts and raise an estimated assessment based on gross margin benchmarks; failure to produce TP documentation within 30 days triggers a penalty of HKD 50,000 plus HKD 500 per day

Director personal liability: Partners are jointly and severally liable for the tax arising from the discarded records; a liquidator or bankruptcy trustee may pursue a contribution claim against any partner; the IRD’s published list of tax defaulters exposes partners to reputation damage and banking sanctions
