A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not permissible as the sole registered address for the Mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership because the State Administration for Market Regulation and the municipal tax authority require a physical business premises within the registered administrative district, evidenced by a lease agreement or property ownership certificate, and the tax authority's collection and management system will flag a mailbox address as low-substance for VAT invoice issuance and preferential pass-through treatment. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; for foreign-invested partnership registration, the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department is the parallel authority for business registration, and where the partnership uses a corporate general partner, the Companies Registry remains the registration authority for that corporate vehicle.

Governing statute

Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) of the Laws of Hong Kong SAR, including sections 67-71 (incorporation by registration), section 112 (registered office), section 124 (appointment of directors), sections 373-376 (financial statements), and the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) sections 4-7 for partnership registration; the Foreign-Invested Partnership is structured as a registered limited partnership under Cap. 37, read together with the Companies Ordinance where corporate general partner requirements apply.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership (Form LP1) under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) together with a Statement of Particulars; where a corporate general partner is incorporated, the incorporation instrument is the Incorporation Form (NNC1) and Articles of Association filed under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong SAR imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships or foreign-invested partnerships. The partners may agree on capital contributions by partnership agreement without any statutory escrow or blocked account. For AML and banking counterparty due diligence purposes, the bank may internally require a minimum initial deposit, but this is not a statutory incorporation requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certification is mandatory if the Partnership occupies a warehouse, repacking facility, or any premises where goods are stored in quantities exceeding the thresholds under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295). A Fire Safety Certificate or Fire Services Installation Compliance Certificate must be obtained before operation. The Buildings Department must issue structural load approval for racking and storage, and the Labour Department must be notified under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) for any manual handling or forklift operations, requiring a workplace risk assessment and an approved emergency exit plan. For the current pure digital trade operation with no physical facility in Hong Kong, no civil defense clearance is required, but the registered office must maintain clear fire escape routes and general fire safety compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong SAR imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships or foreign-invested partnerships. The partners may agree on capital contributions by partnership agreement without any statutory escrow or blocked account. For AML and banking counterparty due diligence purposes, the bank may internally require a minimum initial deposit, but this is not a statutory incorporation requirement.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Taxation) online portal; the CN_FIP must register with the STA branch having territorial competence over its registered business address in the PRC. Hong Kong SAR is a separate customs and tax territory; its Inland Revenue Department does not administer VAT/GST, and no Hong Kong VAT/GST registration applies.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete formation at the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which becomes the statutory tax identifier. Step 2: Within 30 days of the business license date, submit the initial tax registration particulars through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau portal or at the competent tax office, including the partnership agreement, SAMR license, legal representative identity documents, registered address proof, and basic bank deposit account certificate. Step 3: Register each foreign non-resident partner for PRC taxpayer identification to the extent a payable or withholding obligation arises; the FIP is a pass-through entity for income tax purposes. Step 4: Receive the electronic tax registration record; the USCC is used as the TIN/VAT number for VAT returns, customs export filings, export tax refunds, e-invoices, and withholding payments. Step 5: Elect or be classified as VAT taxpayer status; the FIP is a small-scale taxpayer until annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, at which time general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory. Step 6: Activate the fully digital e-invoice platform, obtain invoice quota, and receive the anti-fraud digital stamp credentials for real-time invoice clearance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law; the FIP is statutorily transparent and each partner is taxed on his/her/its allocable share. No hybrid mismatch election is available. The only relevant statutory elections are VAT small-scale versus general taxpayer status and, for general taxpayers, the export tax refund method (tax exemption, credit, and refund).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; a small-scale taxpayer with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 qualifies for the statutory VAT exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Organic matcha powder physically exported from the PRC, including exports to the Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, qualifies for the 0% export VAT rate if: (1) the goods are declared to PRC Customs for export; (2) the FIP has general VAT taxpayer status; (3) the export is supported by a valid overseas sales contract and commercial invoice; (4) the VAT return reports the zero-rated export and related input VAT is recoverable; and (5) the export proceeds are received from the non-resident buyer and repatriated into the PRC within the statutory deadline.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document
  • Commercial invoice with zero-rated VAT annotation
  • Packing list
  • Bank Credit Advice and Inward Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate issued under SAFE rules
  • Export tax refund declaration filed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau export tax refund system
  • Foreign exchange receipt verification log showing repatriation within 180 days

Golden Tax Phase IV mandates that all Special VAT Invoices be issued through the STA-approved fully digital e-invoice system and transmitted in real time to the tax authority. Newly registered FIPs must use the digital e-invoice platform, maintain digital invoice quotas, and apply anti-fraud digital stamping; printed-only VAT invoices are ineffective for input VAT credit or export tax refund substantiation.

The STA and PRC Customs may jointly audit all export zero-rating claims. If the goods were not physically exported, the documents are false or missing, or foreign exchange is not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the tax authority will claw back the refunded VAT, charge late-payment interest, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the refunded amount under Article 66 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds may arise when the fraudulently obtained refund exceeds RMB 100,000; customs authorities may also confiscate goods and impose fines for false declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The regulatory matrix splits between mainland China and Hong Kong SAR. Onshore, RMB is fully convertible for current account trade transactions, but capital account flows remain materially restricted and require SAFE registration, approval, or use of a qualified investor channel. A foreign-invested partnership (CN_FIP) receiving non-resident capital contributions will be classified as direct investment; therefore the remitting partner must use an approved bank integrated with SAFE's FDI-PCI system, obtain a foreign exchange registration certificate, and open a dedicated capital account for the escrow of inbound partnership capital. Once funded, those monies are ring-fenced to the FIP's declared business purpose; use for domestic matcha procurement or operating expenses is allowed only after the bank performs its capital project payment compliance review, and any amendment to partner shareholding, early capital return, or dissolution proceeds needs a separate SAFE filing. Dividend or profit distributions to non-resident partners are treated as current account remittances, yet banks will require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and corroboration that the distribution is genuine accumulated profit. In contrast, Hong Kong SAR operates under the HKMA's linked exchange rate system with no exchange controls; the HKD is freely convertible, capital flows in and out without SAFE-style approval, and profit remittance is not subject to a separate foreign exchange permit. The practical constraint in Hong Kong is bank KYC: escalating beneficial ownership documentation, source-of-funds verification, and sanctions screening are mandatory. Any CNY settlement executed through Hong Kong must also comply with the offshore CNH clearing mechanisms administered by HKMA and the designated clearing bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a mainland China-incorporated FIP, export proceeds from matcha powder sales must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign exchange bank and are generally subject to a 100% settlement requirement into RMB unless SAFE has approved a specific foreign exchange account retention limit. The standard general-trade collection window is within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, extendable to 180 days with prior SAFE monitoring registration; the bank reconciles the customs declaration, tax export data, and incoming SWIFT credit. In Hong Kong SAR, there is no statutory export surrender regime; 100% of foreign currency export proceeds may be retained, and those balances can be directly used to settle offshore trade payables without conversion or repatriation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) through its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: CRITICALLY LOW as the primary treasury rail for mainland China operations; MODERATE as an ancillary collection channel for Hong Kong. offshore digital EMIs without a banking or stored value facility license are legally barred from servicing statutory capital account flows, export proceeds surrender, and capital verification for a mainland FIP, because SAFE requires all foreign exchange receipts and payments to flow through a licensed onshore bank with direct SAFE reporting connectivity. In Hong Kong, HKMA-licensed virtual banks are permissible for basic multi-currency current accounts, but they generally do not underwrite complex trade finance, commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or sanctions-heavy matcha procurement portfolios. Therefore, the compliance committee should not select an unlicensed fintech EMI as the sole liquidity clearing provider; tier-1 banks must carry the capital escrow, FX settlement, and LC rails, while a licensed HKMA stored value facility may only be used for small value collections if the counterparty insists on a digital wallet.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10 million per controlled transaction category is the operative Hong Kong threshold for contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation; a Local File and Master File must be prepared where controlled transactions exceed this threshold, and the Master File is additionally expected for MNE groups whose consolidated group revenue exceeds EUR 750 million; documentation must be ready for LRD inspection upon request and failure to maintain it shifts the burden of proof to the taxpayer for arm s length pricing

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but its territorial source rule is effectively an economic substance doctrine: to claim offshore profits on matcha distribution, the FIP must prove that core income-generating activities (CIGA), including procurement decisions, contract negotiation, quality inspection, order acceptance, invoicing, banking, and risk bearing, are carried out by its own staff, premises, and systems in Hong Kong. A letter-box entity without substance will have its offshore-source claim denied and the entire trading profit may be assessed in Hong Kong; treaty benefits are also denied where the partnership is a pure conduit without economic substance

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Material Misstatement of Profits

Statutory sanction: Contravenes Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112) sections 59, 61, 61A, 80(2), 82 and 82A; civil additional tax up to treble the undercharged tax plus a penal fine of up to HK$100,000; on indictment, imprisonment for up to three years and fines up to HK$50,000; the LRD may reopen up to 10 years of assessment and issue an estimated assessment

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the commercial veil where the partnership is an instrument of tax evasion; partners and shadow directors may be personally liable for restitution of evaded tax, interest, and penal costs; the LRD may invoke the Hong Kong blacklisting/travel restriction regime for unpaid tax above HK$5,000, disqualify directors under the Companies Ordinance, and obtain a custody/imprisonment order for up to three years
Shell Company and Treaty Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: The LRD may void the arrangement under section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance and recharacterise income as assessable in Hong Kong; transfer pricing adjustments under sections 50AAF and 50AAI add back non-arm s length profits; statutory penalties reach up to 100% of the additional tax and the DTA claim is denied under the Principal Purpose Test; a sham company may be struck off and its transactions disregarded for tax purposes

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners face civil liability for wrongful trading and fraudulent misrepresentation; asset restitution orders may freeze bank accounts in Hong Kong, China, and treaty states; travel bans may be imposed pending investigation; director disqualification for up to 15 years is available under sections 168E and 168M of the Companies Ordinance; criminal liability for conspiracy to defraud the revenue may result in imprisonment of up to 14 years
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Violates Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap.615), sections 5 and 6, and the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap.455), section 25; corporate penalties may reach HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for individuals up to 14 years; false beneficial ownership declarations attract separate offences under the Companies Ordinance and the AMLO with fines and imprisonment of up to seven years; the PIU may freeze and forfeit all matcha distribution proceeds
