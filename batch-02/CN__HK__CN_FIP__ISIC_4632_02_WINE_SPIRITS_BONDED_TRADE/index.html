Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong does not impose any minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership. Contributions may be in cash or in kind and must be recorded in the partnership agreement. No escrowed or blocked capital account is mandatory; however, for the bonded warehouse and dutiable commodities licence, the Customs and Excise Department requires a customs bond or bank guarantee, which is a functional equivalent of financial security and typically takes the form of a bond equal to the estimated duty and taxes at risk.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department Certificate of Fire Service Installations and Equipment; Buildings Department Consent to Commence Works and Occupation Permit confirming structural loading for heavy palletized storage; Labour Department occupational safety approval covering manual handling of barrels, forklift operation, temperature-controlled storage, emergency lighting and extinguishers; and, as a condition of the Customs bonded warehouse licence, a completed Customs security survey and fire risk assessment.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the in-charge local sub-bureaus of the STA, with electronic filing through the STA Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax; national portal chinatax.gov.cn); bonded and export declarations are submitted through the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC) China International Trade Single Window. For the Hong Kong SAR foreign investor, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD) is the separate competent authority for Hong Kong territorial-source profits tax matters.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investors execute a foreign-invested partnership agreement, appoint an executive/general partner with a place of business or representative office in mainland China, and register the Foreign-Invested Partnership with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) to obtain the Foreign-Invested Partnership Business License. Step 2: Tax registration is automatic and identifier-based; the business license contains the statutory 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which simultaneously functions as the FIP's Tax Identification Number (TIN), VAT registration number and customs declaration identifier. Step 3: Within 30 days of license issuance, the FIP must complete taxpayer registration confirmation on the STA e-Tax platform, including registered address, legal representative, business scope, bank account, accounting policies and foreign partner particulars. Step 4: For bonded wine/spirits trade and export, the FIP must separately register with GACC through the China International Trade Single Window as an import/export consignee/consignor and obtain the Customs Registration Code used for export declarations and bonded warehouse operations. Step 5: Each non-resident foreign partner must supply its foreign TIN and, where treaty relief is expected, an overseas tax-residence certificate; those identifiers are recorded in the e-Tax counterparty and withholding ledgers and reported for Common Reporting Standard purposes where applicable. Step 6: The FIP applies for VAT invoice issuance quota and the electronic invoice digital certificate on the STA platform before issuing any taxable-supply invoice.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None in the PRC domestic-law sense. China does not provide a check-the-box election. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is mandatorily transparent under Ministry of Finance and STA Notice Cai Shui [2008] No. 159, under which enterprise income tax is not payable by the FIP itself; each partner is assessable on its allocated share of partnership profits. No domestic hybrid-mismatch election is available; only tax treaty and general anti-avoidance rules may be invoked to resolve cross-border characterisation issues.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is required from the first taxable activity. The FIP remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer only while cumulative 12-month taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000; once that turnover threshold is crossed, mandatory conversion to general VAT taxpayer status applies. For export zero-rating and input VAT refund claims, general VAT taxpayer status is the substantive operational requirement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim the 0% VAT export rate for aged Scotch and Bordeaux wines only if the goods physically leave the customs territory of mainland China, including lawful release from a bonded warehouse or comprehensive bonded zone for export to Hong Kong SAR or another overseas destination; the FIP is the named exporter on the export customs declaration; the sale is made under a written contract to a non-resident buyer; title and risk in the goods pass outside China; and the export proceeds are received through recognised PRC banking or approved offshore settlement channels within the statutory repatriation period. The export must be invoiced separately as zero-rated, and the associated input VAT may be recovered through the export tax refund/rebate procedure. Goods released from bonded storage into domestic consumption are not eligible for zero-rating and are subject to import VAT and, where applicable, consumption tax/excise duty. Qualifying offshore digital services are not relevant to this wine/spirits bonded-trade scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Goods Declaration filed through the GACC China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading, House Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill proving physical delivery to an overseas destination
  • Bank foreign-exchange collection receipt/inward remittance certificate issued by an authorised PRC bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the non-resident buyer
  • Export sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list and, if requested, certificate of origin
  • Bonded warehouse cargo release/exit receipt and customs transport or transit documents evidencing withdrawal from the bonded supervision zone
  • VAT zero-rated export invoice and the STA-platform export tax refund/rebate application record

All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices and fully digitalised all-digital electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA's national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV system, and every invoice receives a digital anti-counterfeiting tax-control cipher. Bonded warehouse releases are data-matched with GACC customs clearance records through the China International Trade Single Window, requiring strict consistency among the VAT invoice, customs commodity code, wine/spirits quantity, unit price and export consignment details.

The FIP must retain all export and payment documentation under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Using Golden Tax Phase IV data matching against GACC export records and bank foreign-exchange settlement records, the STA may disallow zero-rating if physical export or foreign-exchange repatriation is not proven within the statutory 180-day window. Disallowed zero-rated supplies are reassessed as domestic taxable supplies at the standard 13% VAT rate, with daily late-payment surcharge from the original due date and an administrative understatement penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulent, counterfeit or collusive export declarations are subject to criminal prosecution as export-tax-rebate fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC, carrying substantial fines, forfeiture, imprisonment of responsible persons, and suspension or revocation of customs and tax registrations.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free and open currency regime under the linked exchange rate system managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. The HKD is fully convertible, with no central bank permission required for current account transactions or capital account transfers. The Currency Board mechanism maintains HKD/USD stability within the 7.75-7.85 band, eliminating convertibility risk for trade and project remittances. There are no surrender requirements, no repatriation limitations, and no government screening for legitimate capital flows into or out of HK. The only practical hurdles are banking due diligence, source-of-funds evidence, and regulatory licensing for bonded goods. For a wine and spirits bonded wholesale entity, profits can be remitted freely to overseas partners, provided corporate tax return and profits tax compliance are satisfied and the controlling company does not trigger China's ODI or SAFE filing obligations if the capital originates from China. Thus the assessment is full current and capital account convertibility, with operational friction concentrated on AML/KYC documentary quality and customs-controlled inventory verification rather than exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies for export proceeds in Hong Kong. Exporters may retain earnings in any approved currency in accounts with authorized banks; no export proceeds are required to be surrendered to the HKMA. The only reporting obligation is the standard Banking Ordinance statistical return, which does not impose conversion.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium-to-Low viability as the primary treasury rail. HK-licensed fintech EMIs operating under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 584) are legally permitted to provide multi-currency accounts and basic payment processing, but they are not licensed as full banks and cannot support documentary trade finance products such as commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or bonded warehouse transaction custody. For a medium-risk enhanced wine and spirits bonded trade entity, the use of such EMIs is objectively insufficient for the core clearing architecture. A fintech EMI may be used only for ancillary expenses, payroll, or low-value sweeps, and only if the EMI holds a valid HK SVF licence. All escrow, trade settlement, and foreign exchange execution must flow through a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a dedicated trade finance desk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection to the Hong Kong FIP should be routed through a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank via SWIFT MT103 with payment details clearly marked as Paid-in Capital Contribution and referencing the partnership agreement. No prior HKMA approval is required for capital inflows to Hong Kong. If the funding originates from a mainland China parent or partner, the remitter must first complete the relevant SAFE outbound investment registration and obtain an Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) certificate where applicable; the Chinese remitting bank will use the appropriate SAFE direct investment purpose code and may require a copy of the HK partnership registration, the investment agreement, and the ODI approval documents. Upon arrival, the funds are credited to the Capital Escrow Account and the receiving bank will issue a deposit confirmation. Capital verification is not statutorily mandated in Hong Kong, but the partnership should commission a certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report for investor and Chinese regulatory reporting.For bonded wine and spirits trade disbursements, the preferred clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier wire payments when invoices are standard and the transaction history is established. For high-value or en primeur purchases of Bordeaux and Scotch, use an Irrevocable Confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit to protect both parties and preserve a clear documentary audit trail. Documentary Collections (Documents against Payment or Documents against Acceptance) are acceptable for repeat low-risk suppliers where trust has been built. All disbursements must reference the commercial invoice, bonded warehouse identifier, and relevant excise or customs documentation. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Medium-to-Low viability as the primary treasury rail. HK-licensed fintech EMIs operating under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance (Cap. 584) are legally permitted to provide multi-currency accounts and basic payment processing, but they are not licensed as full banks and cannot support documentary trade finance products such as commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or bonded warehouse transaction custody. For a medium-risk enhanced wine and spirits bonded trade entity, the use of such EMIs is objectively insufficient for the core clearing architecture. A fintech EMI may be used only for ancillary expenses, payroll, or low-value sweeps, and only if the EMI holds a valid HK SVF licence. All escrow, trade settlement, and foreign exchange execution must flow through a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank with a dedicated trade finance desk.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Inland Revenue (Transfer Pricing Documentation and Advance Pricing Agreements) Rules (Cap. 112I) and DIPN 59, a Local File is required unless all connected-transaction categories are below the exemption thresholds: property/goods transactions HKD 50 million, financing transactions HKD 10 million, royalty/intangible transactions HKD 5 million, and other transactions HKD 20 million; a Master File is generally required where the MNE group has consolidated revenue of HKD 6.8 billion or more and the Hong Kong entity has non-resident connected transactions, or where aggregate specified transactions with connected persons exceed HKD 2.5 billion. In bonded trade, a related-party purchase of aged Scotch or Bordeaux wine, storage fees, freight charges, and brand royalty payments must all be measured against these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate the BVI/Cayman-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the territorial source doctrine and treaty anti-abuse rules require real economic substance in Hong Kong: physical bonded warehouse custody, local staff, active management of sales and delivery risk, actual negotiation and execution of wine/spirit purchase contracts, and genuine Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. A Foreign-Invested Partnership that is only a booking vehicle, shifting bonded inventory and profits through shell entities, will be denied offshore treatment, recharacterised as an HK taxable trade, and may be denied treaty benefits for lack of beneficial ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82A (wilful evasion) renders the offender liable on conviction to a fine of HKD 50,000, treble the tax undercharged, and imprisonment for 3 years; IRO s.80(2) also imposes a fine plus treble tax shortfall for incorrect returns without reasonable excuse; IRD may issue default assessments plus late-payment surcharge under s.71A.

Director personal liability: Because the entity is a partnership, the managing/general partners function as de facto directors and are jointly and severally personally liable for the partnership's civil tax debts; the corporate veil does not protect them, and the court may allow seizure of personal bank accounts, real property, and wine inventory, impose a departure prohibition order under IRO s.76/s.77, and sentence the responsible partner to up to 3 years' imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Arm's-Length Violation

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAM empowers IRD to adjust the profits on an arm's-length basis; failure to keep sufficient transfer pricing documentation may result in penalties under IRO s.80(1) and s.80(2) (HKD 10,000 to HKD 50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged), and intentional transfer pricing distortion with concealment can be prosecuted as fraud under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who knowingly approve false intercompany pricing or destroy transfer pricing evidence cannot shelter behind the partnership's separate identity; they may be personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties, have personal assets attached in civil restitution proceedings, be blacklisted and prevented from leaving Hong Kong, and face criminal imprisonment for wilful abetment of tax evasion.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25(1) imposes upon conviction a fine of HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years; the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) subjects a non-compliant DNFBP to disciplinary and criminal sanctions; the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) penalises unlicensed handling of dutiable wine/spirits with fines and forfeiture; the IRD will alternatively pierce the offshore claim, tax the full HK-source profits, and add treble tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate/partnership veil is pierced where the entity is a mere façade: controlling partners are personally liable for AML penalties, asset forfeiture, and restitution; limited partners who participate in management lose limited-liability protection; the court may freeze personal bank accounts, seize real estate, impose travel bans/blacklisting, and impose a custodial sentence of up to 14 years for money-laundering participants.
