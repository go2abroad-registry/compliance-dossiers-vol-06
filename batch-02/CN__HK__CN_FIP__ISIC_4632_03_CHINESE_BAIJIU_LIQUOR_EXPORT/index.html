Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) administered through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform and the China International Trade Single Window for customs-tax information exchange; for any Hong Kong SAR branch or permanent establishment, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTAX portal under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112).

E-government filing portal

For the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership: first register the partnership with the Administration for Market Regulation to obtain the Unified Social Credit Code, which is the PRC taxpayer identification number; then, within 30 days of the business license, complete tax registration with the local STA office using the e-Tax platform, submitting the partnership agreement, articles of partnership, registered office proof, legal representative and all partner particulars including passport or certificate of incorporation for non-resident partners, each partner's foreign tax residence TIN for treaty and withholding purposes, banking account details, accounting system description, and expected VAT status. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: under PRC partnership tax rules, the FIP is transparent for corporate income tax purposes, with each partner taxed on their distributable share under Caishui [2008] No. 159, and no enterprise income tax is imposed at the entity level; however, the FIP is the taxable person for PRC VAT, consumption tax, customs and export rebate purposes. For Hong Kong profits tax, a partnership or any Hong Kong branch of the FIP is likewise taxed on a pass-through basis under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112), with profits tax assessed on the partners at the applicable rates.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; China does not have a check-the-box election. Available indirect-tax elections include choosing general VAT taxpayer status versus small-scale VAT taxpayer status under the PRC VAT rules where annual turnover is at or below RMB 5,000,000, and selecting monthly or quarterly taxable periods. In Hong Kong, the FIP may claim offshore profits treatment in its profits tax return for profits derived outside Hong Kong, but this is a territorial-source claim, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC basic tax registration has no economic turnover threshold and is mandatory within 30 days of business establishment; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, below which the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer; consumption tax and customs export registration have no threshold. For export zero-rating and input VAT refund, the FIP should register as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated for PRC VAT under Article 8 of the Provisional Regulations on VAT. To claim the 0% export rate for Chinese baijiu, the FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer, be registered with China Customs as an export consignor/consignee, hold the required alcohol or food distribution licence, physically export the goods out of the PRC customs territory through a valid customs declaration including export to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, sell under an export contract on commercial terms, and receive the sales proceeds in foreign exchange or in approved cross-border RMB settlement within the statutory period. The export is also exempt from PRC Consumption Tax; input VAT incurred on purchasing the baijiu and related export logistics may be refunded under the export tax refund and exemption mechanism, subject to the STA export rebate schedule for the commodity code and the availability of the supplier's special VAT invoice. The zero rate is unavailable if the goods are sold or consumed within mainland China or if the export documentation package is incomplete.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form in the export tax rebate special copy filed through China Customs
  • Original or certified electronic Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or other transport document proving export from the PRC customs territory
  • Commercial Invoice and export packing list
  • Export contract or sales confirmation showing baijiu product description, quantity, price, Incoterms and destination

  • Bank foreign exchange receipt or International Balance of Payments Income Declaration evidencing repatriation of export proceeds to a PRC bank account

Under the Golden Tax System IV, all VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's unified national Electronic Invoice Platform, with special VAT invoices requiring real-time digital signature, encryption, verification and anti-fraud stamping before issue. Export declarations and export tax rebate claims must be lodged electronically through the China Customs Single Window, which automatically cross-checks invoice data, customs declaration data, transport documentation and foreign exchange collection data with STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

The STA, China Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct automatic and desk-based cross-validation of export declarations, transport records, VAT invoices and foreign exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is claimed without genuine export, on forged documents, or where foreign exchange is not repatriated within the prescribed deadline, the tax authority will claw back refunded VAT and disallow the zero-rated exemption, impose late payment surcharge under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, impose a fine of one to five times the tax defrauded under Article 66 for fraudulent obtaining of export tax refunds, and refer criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export rebates, with penalties including imprisonment and confiscation. The FIP may also be placed on a tax risk blacklist, suspended from streamlined export rebate processing, and subjected to enhanced audit surveillance for subsequent periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is current-account convertible for legitimate merchandise trade; export receipts from baijiu sales may be promptly settled, repatriated and converted through designated foreign exchange banks, provided the underlying customs and documentary evidence is clean. Capital account items, by contrast, remain strictly controlled. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), foreign partner capital contributions are treated as foreign direct investment and become convertible once SAFE/FDI filing or registration is complete, but they are then governed by explicit usage rules: the capital account cannot be used for discretionary securities investment, regulated lending or non-operating purposes. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is a current-account-like outflow that nevertheless passes through a capital registration filter. The FIP must first obtain an audited annual financial statement, complete its corporate income tax settlement, produce a tax clearance certificate, file a partner profit-distribution resolution, and update the SAFE foreign exchange business registration; only then will a bank purchase foreign currency and remit the dividend. Prudent banks also require foreign partners to show historical after-tax retained earnings, verify withholding tax treatment, and confirm that the remittance does not exceed properly computed distributable profits. In practice, the biggest hurdles are delayed annual audits, mismatches between tax vouchers and SAFE codes, and the requirement to file the payment within a designated window; a well-prepared FIP should pre-clear the remittance schedule with the bank compliance officer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 reform, China has no mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may retain export receipts in foreign currency, but they must be repatriated to the FIP's foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days of customs declaration. The exporting FIP must reconcile each inward remittance with the corresponding customs declaration in SAFE's Goods Trade Monitoring System; funds not matched by an actual shipment require explanation. If payment is received more than 90 days after export, the FIP must report the delayed collection to SAFE and provide documentary justification. Foreign currency receipts that are not settled into RMB may remain in the settlement account for current payments or for conversion, but cannot remain indefinitely if unutilized; annual SAFE examination requires balance verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, as delegated by the People's Bank of China (PBOC), the ultimate central banking authority for exchange control in the mainland, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the offshore clearing supervisor for Hong Kong dollar liquidity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility (1/5) as a primary treasury rail. The mainland-domiciled FIP is required by SAFE to hold its capital and export proceeds in a licensed domestic foreign-exchange designated bank. Offshore digital EMIs are not recognized as designated foreign exchange banks and cannot submit the necessary SAFE trade verification reports, anti-money laundering filings, or capital account registration data. Such EMIs can only function as ancillary collection or disbursement wallets of a separately capitalized Hong Kong affiliate after the FIP has lawfully funded that affiliate through approved outward remittance channels; they cannot receive export proceeds directly on behalf of the Chinese FIP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

An Hong Kong-based foreign partner remits capital via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's capital account at a designated domestic bank, using the SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) purpose code for equity contribution. The remitting bank must include the FIP's foreign investment information filing number and the SAFE registration reference. On receipt, the domestic bank verifies the transaction against the Ministry of Commerce Foreign Investment Information Filing record and SAFE's FDI database. Funds are temporarily credited to a restricted capital account, after which a licensed Chinese CPA issues a capital verification certificate. Each partner's remittance must be individually identified; commingled wires are prohibited. The FIP cannot deploy the capital until the bank has completed the foreign exchange settlement of the capital account and updated the SAFE e-registration.Export shipments of baijiu are settled via SWIFT MT103 for open account buyers; through a Commercial Letter of Credit advised or confirmed by Bank of China under UCP600; or via documentary collections (D/P and D/A) through the FIP's bank. Every receipt must carry the correct HS code and SAFE Goods Trade classification, and all shipments must be reconciled with customs declarations in SAFE's monitoring system. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong's transfer pricing regime under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2017 applies to cross-border related-party transactions where the Hong Kong entity or its offshore associated enterprise has annual turnover of HKD 100,000,000 or more and the transaction amount exceeds HKD 10,000,000 for the standard documentation threshold. A Local File is required where the value of the taxpayer's controlled transactions exceeds HKD 10,000,000, and a Master File is required where the total value of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 100,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as applicable in Hong Kong are driven by the Inland Revenue Department's 'Guidance on Transfer Pricing and Economic Substance Requirements' and international base erosion and profit shifting standards. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in global baijiu export, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include sourcing negotiation, inventory and logistics management, contract execution, invoicing, payment settlement, and the performance of distribution functions. The Hong Kong tax authority and the Chinese tax authority will scrutinize whether the Hong Kong FIP has adequate physical office premises, full-time employees with decision-making authority, and actual control over inventory risks. If the Hong Kong entity merely invoices baijiu sales without performing the CIGA, the profits may be recharacterized as attributable to a Mainland China permanent establishment, exposing the non-resident partner to Chinese enterprise income tax at 25% and potential 10% withholding tax on deemed distributions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Offshore Profit Declaration and Shell Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), fraudulent evasion of Profits Tax is punishable by imprisonment for up to 3 years and a fine of treble the amount of tax undercharged. Under Article 6 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, the foreign-invested partnership may be recharacterized as a resident enterprise of China; Article 45 of the PRC EIT Law permits the Chinese tax authority to attribute undistributed profits of a controlled foreign corporation to the Chinese controlling shareholder and impose 25% corporate income tax along with a 10% withholding tax on deemed dividends. The China-Hong Kong Arrangement's anti-abuse provisions, including Article 27 and the principal purpose test, deny treaty benefits to the shell FIP.

Director personal liability: The director or general partner of the Hong Kong FIP is personally liable under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance for fraudulent tax evasion, and upon conviction may be sentenced to 3 years imprisonment and a personal fine of treble the tax undercharged. Under section 82B, the court may order the director to pay penalties and interest out of personal assets. A travel ban may be imposed under the Departure Permit (Prevention of Tax Evasion) Ordinance, and the director may be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Immigration Department. In Mainland China, a foreign-invested partnership nominee director who knowingly participates in paper invoicing may be jointly and severally liable under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion, which carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and criminal fines, as well as personal asset freezing and a prohibition on serving as a director or supervisor of any Chinese enterprise.

Statutory sanction: Under section 50A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), the Inland Revenue Department may adjust the assessable profits of the Hong Kong FIP to reflect the arm's length price, impose additional Profits Tax at 16.5%, and charge interest under section 60A at the prescribed rate.
