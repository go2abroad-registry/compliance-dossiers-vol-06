Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the China Electronic Taxpayer's Hall (e-Tax platform). Hong Kong SAR maintains a separate territorial tax administration under the Inland Revenue Department and levies no VAT/GST; a mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership is not subject to Hong Kong VAT/GST, and a Hong Kong Business Registration number would arise only for a separately constituted Hong Kong branch or entity.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in mainland China, the Administration for Market Regulation issues a Unified Social Credit Code that functions as the taxpayer identification number. The FIP must complete tax filing information through the Electronic Taxpayer's Hall within 30 days of business registration, submitting the business license, partnership agreement, foreign partner identification or passport copies, capital contribution schedule, proof of business premises, bank account opening certificate, and appointment of a responsible tax filing person. Foreign non-resident entities that are FIP partners but have no mainland establishment must obtain a TIN through non-resident enterprise registration if they realize China-source income; otherwise the FIP or withholding agent must report withholdings through the non-resident tax system, and the tax authority assigns a tax registration code for the non-resident partner. Foreign non-resident partnerships making taxable supplies in China without a mainland registration must register as VAT taxpayers under the unified code system before issuing VAT invoices.

Available election forms

None. Under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, partnership income is not taxed at the FIP entity level; each partner is taxed in their separate capacity. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Taxpayers at or below this threshold generally fall under the small-scale VAT taxpayer framework. A Foreign-Invested Partnership making taxable supplies in the PRC must register for VAT regardless of the nationality of its partners.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered VAT general taxpayer may claim 0% VAT zero-rating for physical goods, including keg craft beer, that are exported out of PRC customs territory. The exporter must possess a valid China Customs export declaration, the goods must actually leave the territory, the sales consideration must be collected and repatriated through approved banking channels recorded in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and the exporter must file the export tax refund through the statutory export exemption and refund procedure. Exports by small-scale VAT taxpayers are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, with no input VAT refund.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration issued and electronically cleared by China Customs
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from a PRC port or airport to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and signed export contract for the craft beer consignment
  • Packing list and cold-chain transport temperature records for beer quality compliance
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence, including bank-issued inward remittance certificate or export collection receipt validated in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System
  • VAT special invoice or export invoice carrying the STA tax-control invoice number
  • Alcoholic beverage wholesale license reference and any customs inspection or quarantine release documentation required for exported beer

The STA mandates issuance of tax-control VAT invoices using the Electronic Taxpayer's Hall and the fully digitalized e-invoice platform. General taxpayers issuing VAT special invoices must use the designated tax-control UKey and upload invoice data in real time to the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform for authenticity verification and input credit claiming. Digital invoices carry a single STA fiscal identifier; private or non-cleared invoice platforms are not permitted for VAT-creditable export documentation.

If the export is fictitious, documentation is forged, the goods do not leave China, or foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory monitoring period, the STA may claw back all refunded input VAT, impose late-payment surcharges, and levy administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax wrongfully refunded. Criminal referral for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under the PRC Criminal Law may result in imprisonment, monetary penalties, and asset forfeiture. SAFE may impose separate penalties for false trade settlement or failure to repatriate export proceeds, and China Customs may apply penalties for false export declarations and downgrade the taxpayer's customs credit rating.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a dual-tier foreign exchange framework: the RMB is convertible on the current account for genuine trade and services but the capital account remains subject to registration, use restrictions, and verification by SAFE. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with a Hong Kong investor, every inbound capital contribution must first be registered in the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment system; the remittance is then credited to a designated capital account and cannot be free-converted into RMB for prohibited fields such as securities speculation, entrusted lending, or non-operating real estate investment. The partnership may convert the received USD or HKD into RMB only through a bank with a genuine operational plan, and any unconverted foreign currency must remain inside a permitted domestic foreign-exchange account. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong partner are permissible after the FIP completes annual statutory audits, tax filings, and the bank's profit-repatriation review; this includes a substantial-activity and anti-abuse check before the tax treaty benefits are confirmed. Export proceeds are likewise regulated by the genuine-transaction principle, with the obligation to repatriate commercial foreign-currency income into China and to support each receipt/payment with trade documents. No domestic account or offshore EMI can bypass these verification rails, so the convertibility risk is manageable but strictly process-dependent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE trade-in-foreign-exchange framework, export proceeds must be repatriated into a mainland domestic foreign-currency settlement account or otherwise credited to the FIP's onshore account within 180 days from the customs export declaration date, unless a separately approved overseas account is used. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; China abolished blanket compulsory surrender, so the FIP may retain 100% of its export receipts in a domestic foreign-currency account and convert to RMB at market rates when needed. Delayed receipts beyond the agreed trade terms, or export financing periods exceeding 90 days, must be reported to SAFE through the trade monitoring platform, and every export shipment must be backed by a matching customs declaration, contract, commercial invoice, and transport document.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong clearing and banking nodes used by the Hong Kong investing partner

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for any statutory mainland banking function. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Currenxie are not licensed as PRC commercial banks and cannot legally open RMB settlement accounts, SAFE-registered FDI capital accounts, or export-earnings accounts for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. They may support the Hong Kong partner's ancillary collections or test credits, but using them as the primary treasury rail would create AML and regulatory gaps. The only viable route is a Tier-1 mainland commercial bank as the primary clearing institution, with EMIs used at most as secondary cash-flow visibility tools outside mainland regulatory accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investing partner must route capital as a clean SWIFT MT103 wire from its HK corporate bank account to the FIP's SAFE-registered foreign-currency capital account at a mainland Tier-1 bank. The remittance must carry the appropriate SAFE transaction purpose code for foreign-invested partnership capital contribution and reference the FDI registration certificate number. The receiving bank verifies the funds against the SAFE system, converts them to RMB only after confirming the stated business plan, and requires a CPA capital-verification report (where the partnership agreement or bank policy mandates one). For repeat suppliers, documentary collections at sight (D/P) or by acceptance (D/A) are acceptable and lower bank fees. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for any statutory mainland banking function. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Currenxie are not licensed as PRC commercial banks and cannot legally open RMB settlement accounts, SAFE-registered FDI capital accounts, or export-earnings accounts for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. They may support the Hong Kong partner's ancillary collections or test credits, but using them as the primary treasury rail would create AML and regulatory gaps. The only viable route is a Tier-1 mainland commercial bank as the primary clearing institution, with EMIs used at most as secondary cash-flow visibility tools outside mainland regulatory accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File are mandatory where the FIP or the multinational group records annual turnover above HK$200,000,000 and related-party transactions exceed HK$20,000,000; Country-by-Country (CbC) Report is required where consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds HK$6,800,000,000 (EUR 750,000,000); an 'excluded enterprise' safe harbour applies to businesses with turnover not exceeding HK$200,000,000 and related-party transactions below de-minimis thresholds (HK$44,000,000 for property transfers, HK$22,000,000 for service provision, and HK$44,000,000 for financing transactions).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but an implicit substance doctrine operates through: (i) the territorial-source principle — offshore profit exemption requires demonstrable profit-generating activities and decision-making outside Hong Kong; (ii) the principal purpose test / limitation-on-benefits clauses in Hong Kong's DTAs, which deny treaty relief to shell or conduit structures; and (iii) transfer pricing substance requirements under DIPN 58–60. A craft beer distribution FIP must maintain physical cold-chain/keg storage capacity, local invoicing and bookkeeping functions, local management decisions, hired personnel, and board oversight in Hong Kong; absence of such substance invites full re-characterisation of export profits as Hong Kong-sourced and rejection of concessionary treaty claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Conviction under s.82(1) IRO: fine of up to HK$200,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus an additional tax liability of treble the amount of tax undercharged; prosecution under s.82A(1) IRO for fraud or wilful misrepresentation carries a fine of up to HK$100,000, imprisonment for 3 years, and additional tax of treble the undercharged amount.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner who sign, authorise, or direct the filing of false returns are personally liable on conviction; the IRD may pierce the partnership veil to seek civil restitution, obtain a departure prevention order under s.79 IRO prohibiting the directors from leaving Hong Kong while tax is unpaid, and institute bankruptcy or compulsory winding-up proceedings against the corporate general partner.
Fatal Filing Default / Estimated Assessment

Statutory sanction: Under s.80(1) IRO: first offence fine of HK$10,000, repeat offence HK$20,000; under s.80(2) IRO: the court may additionally impose a fine not exceeding treble the tax undercharged; the IRD may raise an estimated assessment on default, and late payment triggers a 5% surcharge after 6 months and an additional 10% surcharge after 10 months.

Director personal liability: The tax representative and each general partner of the FIP bear joint failure liability; the IRD may impose a travel ban under s.79 IRO against any partner controlling the FIP while tax remains outstanding; directors of a corporate general partner who transferred assets out of Hong Kong to defeat the tax debt can be made personally liable for the full assessed tax, penalties, and collection costs.
Shell Company / Abusive Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: Denial of the offshore claim, full re-characterisation of 100% of the profits as Hong Kong-sourced, additional assessment for up to 6 years (unlimited in cases of fraud), a treble-tax penalty under s.82A IRO, and discretionary transfer pricing adjustment of the inter-company margins.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate the round-tripping structure face imprisonment of up to 3 years for aiding tax evasion; civil restitution orders compel the directors and controlling partners to repay all avoided tax; travel bans, asset freezes, and cross-border information exchange under the Mainland-HK Arrangement expose the structure to criminal referral; the principal purpose test also voids the treaty benefits claimed by the shell partnership.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Inadequate TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under s.58F IRO: any transfer pricing adjustment attracting additional profits gives rise to a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax payable; failure to retain TP documentation attracts a fine of HK$50,000 and a daily default penalty of HK$500; CbC Report non-compliance incurs a HK$50,000 fine per offence.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or implement the mispricing can be assessed personally and prosecuted criminally where the arrangement is found to be a sham; the IRD may impose joint and several secondary liability on the ultimate parent group for the adjusted tax; the FIP's banking relationships and distribution licences are also exposed to revocation where transfer pricing misconduct coincides with AML scrutiny.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Drug Trafficking and Organised Crime Ordinance (Cap. 405) and the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455): maximum fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years upon conviction for money laundering; the Customs & Excise Department, HKMA, and Joint Financial Intelligence Unit may impose regulatory fines, freeze bank accounts, and revoke the FIP's import/export licences.

Director personal liability: Individual general partners and directors of the corporate general partner face criminal prosecution with custodial sentences of up to 14 years; personal assets, overseas bank accounts, and beneficial interests are subject to restraint and confiscation orders under Part III of Cap. 405; upon disclosure of trade-based laundering indicators, the FIP's bank mandates are suspended and the company is flagged in the HKMA AML watchlist, effectively terminating its Swift wire and direct debit payment rails.
