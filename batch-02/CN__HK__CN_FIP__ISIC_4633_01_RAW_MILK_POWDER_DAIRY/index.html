Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessed through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. Supporting customs and foreign exchange authorities are the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) through the China International Trade Single Window and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

E-government filing portal

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete establishment registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation, obtaining the 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code, which is the sole PRC Tax Identification Number and VAT registration identifier. Within 30 days of that business registration, the FIP must complete tax registration activation in the National Electronic Tax Bureau: real-name face authentication of the legal representative and financial officer; identification of competent tax bureau, tax items, and VAT taxpayer type (small-scale VAT taxpayer if expected annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000, general VAT taxpayer if above); registration of accounting system and electronic invoice quota; and filing for payroll withholding taxes if employees are hired. For export trade in whole and skimmed milk powder, the FIP must obtain the GACC customs operating code and e-port registration through the China International Trade Single Window and register with SAFE in the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. A foreign non-resident partner without a PRC permanent establishment does not receive a Uniform Social Credit Code; instead, the FIP as the domestic platform must complete non-resident enterprise withholding registration with the competent tax authority and the tax office will assign a non-resident taxpayer reference number for the HK partner before any PRC-source partnership allocation is paid or credited. The FIP should also maintain the Hong Kong partner’s Hong Kong Business Registration certificate on file to evidence territorial-source claims where relevant under Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance practice.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections. A PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership is statutorily transparent for enterprise income tax and individual income tax; the FIP itself is not an income-tax-paying entity, and each partner is taxed on its allocable share. The FIP remains a separate VAT taxpayer for its dairy commodity trading activity and cannot elect out of VAT registration or general VAT taxpayer classification once the RMB 5,000,000 general taxpayer threshold is exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers initial tax registration because the FIP is automatically tax-registered upon business establishment. The economically relevant threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT-taxable sales: at or below this amount the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer; above this amount the FIP must be registered as a general VAT taxpayer, and general VAT taxpayer status is required to claim export VAT zero-rating with refund.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim the 0% VAT export rate for whole and skimmed milk powder only where the goods have physically left the PRC customs territory to an overseas destination, including Hong Kong SAR; the FIP is a registered general VAT taxpayer; title and risk are transferred to an overseas buyer under the sales contract; the export sale is supported by an export goods customs declaration and international transport documents; the purchase of the exported goods is supported by valid VAT special invoices; export proceeds are collected through legitimate cross-border banking channels in foreign currency or cross-border RMB and reported to SAFE; and the export tax refund application is submitted through the STA system with the full documentation bundle before the statutory deadline. Small-scale VAT taxpayer exports are exempt from output VAT but do not qualify for refund.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (PRC Export Declaration) released by GACC and electronically transmitted to the STA export tax rebate system
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading (B/L) or Air Waybill (AWB) showing the FIP as shipper or exporter and evidencing actual shipment from a PRC port to the overseas destination
  • Bank-issued export collection receipt (modern statutory equivalent of the former Inward Remittance Certificate) or the matching SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System record evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the FIP that match the customs declaration, shipping documents, and sales contract
  • VAT special invoice(s) for domestic purchase of the exported milk powder or related inputs, used to substantiate the input VAT refund computation
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with Incoterms, price, quantity, and payment terms consistent with the declared customs value and remittance records

The FIP must issue invoices through the STA Digitalized Electronic Invoice system or an STA-approved electronic invoice service platform, transmitting invoice data in real time by API to the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Digitalized electronic invoices carry an anti-forgery QR code and use the standard OFD electronic document format. VAT special invoices are subject to automated cross-matching against payment, inventory, purchase input invoices, and export declaration data. Export shipments must be declared through the GACC China International Trade Single Window, and the customs release electronic message is transmitted to STA for export tax rebate matching; no paper fiscal stamp is affixed to the fully digitalized invoices.

STA, GACC, and SAFE jointly audit export declarations, invoice data, logistics documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If the 0% VAT rate or export refund is claimed without the required customs clearance evidence, transport evidence, VAT special invoice evidence, or FX repatriation evidence, STA will disallow the claim, claw back the refunded amount, assess late-payment surcharge, and impose administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the amount evaded or improperly refunded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent or repeated false export zero-rating claims also trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, downgrade of tax credit status, suspension or revocation of export tax rebate qualification, customs administrative penalties, and enhanced SAFE trade-monitoring classification for the FIP.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong is an offshore special territory with fully free capital mobility and no exchange controls: HKD is freely convertible under the HKMA-linked exchange rate system (7.75-7.85 per USD), and there are no surrender, conversion, or profit-remittance licensure requirements. For the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership, RMB is convertible on current account items covering trade proceeds, fees, dividend-equivalent profit shares, and ordinary trade disbursements; capital account convertibility remains selective. Inbound foreign investment into the FIP from a Hong Kong partner is a capital-account transaction requiring SAFE foreign-exchange registration, a foreign currency capital account or RMB capital settlement-waiting account, and compliance with restricted-use rules on capital works. The foreign partner's profit share remitted to Hong Kong is a current-account item, so no SAFE approval is needed if the bank's authenticity review is satisfied; however, the FIP must first evidence the profit via audited financial statements, a partner distribution resolution, and the tax withholding certificate issued by mainland China tax authorities. Export proceeds from dairy commodity sales must be repatriated to a domestic bank and are captured in SAFE's goods-trade monitoring; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, but all RMB conversion must occur through a licensed PRC bank and cannot be executed through unregulated platforms. The principal practical obstacle is not convertibility but documentary precision: SAFE and the bank require matching contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and logistics evidence before any foreign exchange payment or settlement is released.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE goods-trade foreign exchange administration for the PRC FIP, the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: the partnership may retain 100% of export proceeds in its current-account foreign exchange account or voluntarily convert to RMB at the prevailing bank rate. Repatriation is required into the PRC banking system within the contractual payment maturity, and generally no later than 180 calendar days from the date of export customs declaration, unless a deferred-collection filing or offshore-retention registration is approved by SAFE before the expiry date. The Hong Kong jurisdiction imposes no surrender or conversion obligations.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the central banking and currency authority for the Hong Kong SAR clearing jurisdiction

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMIs are not viable as the primary bank for the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership. Mainland China requires all cross-border FX receipts and payments, including export proceeds, import disbursements, and capital injections, to pass through licensed commercial banking channels under SAFE supervision; offshore EMIs cannot open a legal corporate account for a PRC entity, cannot perform SAFE goods-trade verification, and cannot support FDI capital registration or L/C issuance. For the Hong Kong clearing node, licensed EMI and stored-value-facility solutions such as Airwallex or Currenxie may cover niche collection/disbursement needs, but they should be treated as supplementary rails only, not as substitutes for a Tier-1 bank, because SWIFT LC confirmations, trade documents, and FX hedging lines must sit with the banking licensed entities.

15% standard rate for unincorporated businesses in Hong Kong (profits tax); two-tiered rates at 7.5% on the first HKD 2,000,000 and 15% on the excess apply to a partnership where the assessable profits are allocated to a natural person; a corporate partner may be assessed separately at 16.5% on its partnership share.

0% — Hong Kong does not impose any dividend withholding tax on outbound distributions from a Hong Kong partnership or company; therefore profits repatriated to a Mainland China partner are not subject to Hong Kong dividend withholding tax, but the Mainland China partner may still bear Mainland corporate income tax on its taxable share.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 50,000,000 — IRD DIPN 59 expects contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation, including a Local File and, where applicable, a Master File, for controlled transactions exceeding this administrative benchmark; the statutory record-keeping duty under s.51C IRO requires every FIP to retain sufficient records to support the prices, margins, and source of profits, and a Master File is generally expected for MNE groups with consolidated revenue above HKD 2,000,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the post-2023 Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime and the Mainland-HK DTA anti-avoidance framework require real economic substance in Hong Kong for an FIP to claim exemptions on offshore or passive income. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test requires that strategic management, risk assumption, purchase and sales negotiation, logistics, invoicing, treasury, and compliance decisions are genuinely performed by personnel present and empowered in Hong Kong. A shell FIP with no office, no employees, no local board minutes, and no bank ledger substance will fail the CIGA test, causing its full trading profits to be treated as Hong Kong-source and assessable at the applicable profits tax rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Offshore Source Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82(1) – fraudulent evasion of tax: a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years on summary conviction, with the commissioner able to recover an additional tax of up to treble the amount undercharged under s.80(2); the IRD may also reopen assessments for up to 10 years under s.82A and issue default judgments.

Director personal liability: The general partner and its individual directors are treated as the controlling mind; where the corporate veil is pierced, they are jointly and severally liable for the undercharged tax, treble penalty, interest, and costs. The Commissioner may apply under s.77 IRO for a court order prohibiting the director from leaving Hong Kong until the tax is paid or security is furnished, and third-party garnishment notices under s.76 can freeze the director's bank accounts and receivables; criminal confiscation may follow under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455).
Sham Arrangement and Treaty Abuse using a Pass-Through FIP

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.61A (anti-avoidance) empowers the IRD to disregard the sham FIP and recompute its profits as if the transactions had not been entered into; the Mainland General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Article 47 of the Corporate Income Tax Law may recharacterize the structure, impose withholding tax at 10%, and add interest and penalties. Civil penalties include treble tax and a fine of HKD 50,000 under s.82, and the IRD can deny treaty benefits under the Mainland-HK DTA for lack of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly signed false declarations or approved the sham transactions can be subjected to personal tax assessments under the piercing doctrine, a travel ban, a stop-list blacklisting by the IRD, and asset restraint orders. In egregious cases, directors may be prosecuted for conspiracy to defraud the revenue and sentenced to imprisonment for a term of up to 3 years under s.82, with disqualification from managing companies under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).
AML and Financial Crime using Abusive LC / TT Trade-based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes compliance obligations on financial institutions and DNFBPs; trade-based money laundering and false documentation expose the FIP to criminal prosecution for money laundering under Cap. 455 and conspiracy to defraud at common law, carrying fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years on indictment. The IRD may also levy treble tax and criminal penalties under s.82 for returns that conceal the true beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly or recklessly enable the laundering scheme are personally exposed to confiscation orders, pecuniary penalty orders, travel bans, and extradition risk. Civil asset restitution can be ordered against the directors' personal bank accounts, dividends, and properties; where the court pierces the corporate veil, the directors are jointly and severally liable for the full tax-evaded amount plus penalties, and imprisonment of up to 7 years may be imposed for serious money laundering offences.
