Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) electronic filing portal and the Golden Tax Phase IV invoice and data-clearance system. For the Hong Kong SAR leg, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the separate income-tax competent authority; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales-tax registration authority.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with non-resident partners, the SOP is: (1) file the foreign-investment information, partnership agreement, and notarized/certified identity and tax-residence documents of each foreign partner with the Market Regulatory Administration; (2) obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory PRC taxpayer identification number; (3) complete automatic national tax registration through the multi-certificate integration one-window procedure; (4) activate the e-Tax Bureau identity using the legal representative, financial officer, and filing partner credentials; (5) apply for VAT taxpayer classification, electronic invoice quota, and, if exporting, VAT general taxpayer status; (6) complete the export tax refund/exemption filing setup with the STA; and (7) for any Hong Kong SAR branch or representative office, obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD Business Registration Office and use the Hong Kong Business Registration Number as the HK-specific taxpayer identifier.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with non-resident partners, the SOP is: (1) file the foreign-investment information, partnership agreement, and notarized/certified identity and tax-residence documents of each foreign partner with the Market Regulatory Administration; (2) obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory PRC taxpayer identification number; (3) complete automatic national tax registration through the multi-certificate integration one-window procedure; (4) activate the e-Tax Bureau identity using the legal representative, financial officer, and filing partner credentials; (5) apply for VAT taxpayer classification, electronic invoice quota, and, if exporting, VAT general taxpayer status; (6) complete the export tax refund/exemption filing setup with the STA; and (7) for any Hong Kong SAR branch or representative office, obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD Business Registration Office and use the Hong Kong Business Registration Number as the HK-specific taxpayer identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None in the PRC: mainland China does not administer check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections for partnerships. The FIP is pass-through for income tax purposes, with partners taxed on allocated profits. For VAT purposes, the only available election is voluntary registration of a small-scale taxpayer as a VAT general taxpayer, which is mandatory in practice for claiming the 0% export rate and input-VAT credit chain.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover (12-month rolling period) for mandatory VAT general-taxpayer registration; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer treatment applies unless the FIP voluntarily registers as a VAT general taxpayer. An export-oriented FIP is expected to register as a VAT general taxpayer regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The 0% VAT export rate is available to VAT general taxpayers that physically export taxable goods from the PRC customs territory, including shipments destined for Hong Kong SAR, under a customs export declaration; prove actual departure of the goods from mainland China; receive export proceeds in foreign exchange from a non-mainland buyer in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange trade-in-goods monitoring rules; and hold valid purchase VAT special invoices and matched export documentation. Zero-rating is not automatic and is substantiated through the statutory Export Tax Refund (Exemption) declaration process.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form with customs clearance data transmitted by the Customs Administration to the STA Golden Tax system
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document evidencing departure of the pre-packaged hotpot soup base from mainland China customs territory
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange collection/settlement voucher, being the PRC statutory successor to the export proceeds verification mechanism and the functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the Foreign-Invested Partnership
  • Export sales contract or confirmed purchase order
  • Purchase VAT Special Invoice(s) for the taxable goods sold by the FIP in its wholesale distribution capacity

Mainland China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV electronic invoice platform with the fully digitalized Digital Electronic Invoice system. Issuance occurs in real time through the Electronic Tax Bureau or approved API channels; each invoice receives a tax-authority-assigned unique invoice number, an anti-fraud digital stamp, a tax-control seal, and a dynamic QR code. Invoice data including buyer identification, line-level description, tax rate, and OFD/XML file is transmitted synchronously to the STA. Export invoices must be matched with customs export declaration records before zero-rating or refund processing is permitted.

Zero-rated export VAT is recoverable only after documentary and data-matching scrutiny by the STA and Customs under Golden Tax Phase IV. If export proceeds are not received within the statutory exchange-control period, or if the export declaration, transport evidence, or foreign-exchange collection is found false, the tax authority will recover the VAT refund or exemption, assess overdue compensation, and impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underclaimed or refunded tax under the Law on Tax Administration and the Collection of Taxes of the PRC. Fraudulent export tax-refund claims may constitute the criminal offense of defrauding export tax refund, carrying fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts, in addition to Customs and SAFE sanctions including exporter de-registration and tax-payment credit downgrades.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

For a People's Republic of China domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the RMB is not freely convertible under the capital account. Current account items, specifically trade in pre-packaged hotpot soup base, are convertible at domestic banks after a strict authenticity review of invoice, customs declaration, and transportation documents; businesses may purchase or sell foreign exchange freely for eligible current account transactions. Capital account transactions, including the inbound contribution from overseas partners to the FIP, are governed by SAFE registration under the direct investment administration regime. Export proceeds are subject to compulsory repatriation into the mainland banking system, although mandatory sale requirements have been phased out; an FIP may retain 100% of export foreign currency earnings in a designated FX settlement account, but must maintain accurate export verification records. Profit remittance to foreign investors is categorized as a current account remittance once the capital has been lawfully registered and the enterprise has paid corporate income tax and any withholding tax on non-resident partners; the bank will require the audited financial statements, tax payment voucher, and a partnership profit distribution resolution. Practical hurdles include SAFE's cross-checking of trade data, divergent interpretations by local banks regarding the partnership's distribution language, and the need to show economic substance to avoid classification as a shell entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency proceeds generated by an FIP must be repatriated and collected in China no later than 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless an extension is applied for under the SAFE Export Proceeds Verification and Collection Monitoring measures. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio to RMB is zero percent as a general rule; the FIP may elect to retain up to 100% of export proceeds in an Export Foreign Exchange Settlement Account, or convert them to RMB at will, subject to bank verification of the foreign currency receipt and the execution of the trade paperwork. The bank will require a completed Foreign Exchange Receipt/Collection Declaration (Form of Payment and Settlement), the customs export declaration, the invoice, and the transportation documents, and will monitor the account through SAFE's Foreign Exchange Receipt and Payment Institution system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for onshore RMB convertibility and cross-border capital controls; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for offshore HKD/USD clearing and interbank rails.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

7.5% on first HK$2,000,000 assessable profits, 15% thereafter for partnerships/unincorporated businesses (two-tiered profits tax rate); Hong Kong corporations pay 8.25% on first HK$2,000,000 and 16.5% thereafter.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends distributed by a Hong Kong company or on distributions from a Hong Kong partnership.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Within 1 month from the date of issue of the BIR52 by the Inland Revenue Department. Extensions are normally granted where a tax representative files on behalf of a client with an approved extension date, typically linked to the accounting year-end (e.g., 30 April for December year-end, 31 January for March year-end).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 50,000,000 aggregate cross-border controlled transactions (property transfers, services, royalty and interest) triggers the requirement to prepare a Local File. For the Master File, the threshold is a group consolidated revenue of at least HKD 200,000,000 AND cross-border controlled transactions of at least HKD 50,000,000 (or lower domestic thresholds under IRD DIPN 58).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong adopts the territorial source principle and requires substantive economic activity to support claims of offshore (non-HK) income and to obtain treaty benefits under the China-HK DTA. There is no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) law as in the UAE or Barbados; however, the IRD applies anti-abuse doctrines and the Hong Kong-Mainland DTA includes a principle purpose test (PPT) similar to the OECD BEPS Action 6. A Hong Kong FIP acting as a pure conduit, without sufficient employees, office premises and risk-taking business functions, risks being denied treaty benefits, subjected to mainland GAAR and deemed to have derived Hong Kong-source income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: On conviction: a fine of HK$100,000 (or increased under enhanced penalties) plus treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to 3 years (s.82 IRO). The IRD may also issue an assessment to tax the omitted profits without time limitation.

Director personal liability: Partners (including limited partners who act as general managers) are personally jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax of the partnership and for any penalties attributable to their actions. Directors of the partnership's corporate general partner are liable to imprisonment if they knowingly authorised or permitted the false return, and may have travel restrictions imposed under insolvency and court orders.
Shell Entity / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Mainland tax authority may deny treaty relief under the Mainland-HK DTA (making distributions taxable at 10 or 20% locally), impose enterprise income tax at 25% on Chinese-source profits, and apply late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. The IRD may apply the 'economic substance' doctrine to recharacterise offshore claims and assess profits tax, leading to additional tax and penalties of up to HK$50,000 plus treble tax.

Director personal liability: Under Mainland-HK DTA anti-avoidance (PPT) and China's GAAR (s.47 Enterprise Income Tax Law), the general partner or the action director of the corporate general partner may be held personally accountable for taxes where the partnership is used as an empty conduit, with civil liability for restitution and potential travel de-registration by Chinese courts.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may adjust the partnership's profits to reflect an arm's length consideration, assess additional profits tax, and impose a fine of up to HK$50,000 plus additional tax of up to 15% of undercharged tax under s.82A and s.80. The Mainland tax authority may also impose a 5% add-on on underpaid tax for related-party transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who knowingly fail to keep proper TP documentation are liable to penalty assessment of up to triple the undercharged tax; under the Companies Ordinance, reckless or fraudulent trading may lead to civil compensation orders and disqualification from managing companies for up to 15 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Counter-Financing of Terrorism

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Ordinance (AMLO), operating a non-regulated financial business without registration: fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months. For money laundering: on conviction, fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years. Sanctions enforcement may also result in freezing of bank accounts and asset seizure by the Customs and Excise Department.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner may be personally indicted for money laundering where they had actual or constructive knowledge of the fraudulent trade documents; imprisonment is not limited to the 14-year maximum if committed with dishonest intention, and travel bans can be imposed by the court pending trial.
