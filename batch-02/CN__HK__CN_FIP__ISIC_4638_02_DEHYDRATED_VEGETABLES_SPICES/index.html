Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) operated by the provincial STA offices and the national integrated tax platform.

E-government filing portal

A Foreign-Invested Partnership is registered through the integrated business-and-tax one-stop procedure with the State Administration for Market Regulation. The STA assigns the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code as the taxpayer identification number for enterprise income tax and VAT purposes. If the FIP is not auto-registered, the general partner or appointed tax agent must file initial tax registration with the competent local STA office using the partnership registration certificate, foreign investor constitutional and identity documents (notarised and legalised where required), business registration certificate, registered office proof and responsible-person identity documents. The FIP then completes supplementary tax qualification: small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer status under the VAT general taxpayer registration rules, and records the partnership's bank account, accounting standard and legal representative through the e-Tax portal. Non-resident partners obtain tax identity through the partnership's information reporting and the non-resident withholding filing process; they are not required to open a separate mainland Chinese VAT registration merely for holding a partnership interest.

Available election forms

None relevant to entity classification. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All FIPs carrying on taxable activities must register for VAT; annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 or less qualify for small-scale status, while an entity whose annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 is required to register as a general VAT taxpayer. Processed dehydrated vegetables and spices are generally standard-rated in domestic supply, with export sold at zero rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of physically exported goods, in this case bulk dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chilli pepper, qualifies for the 0% VAT export rate and refund of recoverable input VAT under Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Circular No. 39 (2012) and the STA export tax refund administration measures. Eligibility requires that the FIP is the exporter or holds a lawful export delegation agreement; the goods are actually declared and released by Chinese Customs; the export sale is at arm's length; a valid VAT purchase chain, logistics trail and commercial documentation are maintained; and the export proceeds are repatriated through the banking system. Qualifying offshore digital services are not within this specific goods scope.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document (air waybill or multimodal transport document)
  • Export Sales Contract and Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds

The FIP must use the STA unified official VAT invoice system, including the VAT Special Invoice and VAT Ordinary Invoice, through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform integrated with the tax authority's Golden Tax-IV system. General taxpayers are required to issue invoices through the STA-controlled digital e-invoice mechanism, transmit issuance data in real time to the tax authority, complete invoice authentication before claiming input VAT credit, and support ERP-to-tax-authority API or EDI integration where adopted. The digital e-invoice carries an encrypted anti-counterfeiting tax-control code generated by the tax authority; offline issuance is limited to registered exceptional procedures, and all invoices must be reconciled or cancelled through the e-Tax platform without manual alteration.

Tax authorities audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching the Customs declaration, VAT invoice, logistics and foreign-exchange receipts through the Golden Tax-IV and Customs systems. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the required 180-day deadline, or any discrepancy in the supporting documents, will cause denial or reversal of the VAT refund. The competent tax authority will retrospectively claw back the refunded VAT and may impose late payment surcharge and an administrative fine of 100% to 500% of the refunded amount for fraudulent export refund claims under the Tax Administration Law and the Criminal Law; serious cases can attract criminal liability, including imprisonment of the responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions; the Foreign-Invested Partnership's export contract and shipping documents must support every FX receipt and conversion. The effective check is exercised by SAFE through the bank-led trade authenticity and compliance verification rather than by ministerial conversion permits. On the capital side, convertibility is constrained: any injection from the Hong Kong parent into the PRC FIP must be registered as foreign direct investment, credited only to a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and used after funds-use authenticity scrutiny. Profit distribution to nonresident partners is treated as current-account remittance when tax clearance and audited earnings are presented, but liquidation proceeds and partnership capital return are capital-account transactions requiring SAFE deregistration and payment orders. The FIP must therefore maintain separate ledger treatment and not blend trade receipts, remitted profit, or shareholder loans. Remittance hurdles include the need to evidence business substance, satisfy corporate income tax filing on deemed profit, protect against beneficial owner disclosure gaps, and present a certified certificate of payment for current-account remittance. These safeguards materially reduce the risk of disguised capital flight and align with PBOC's anti-money laundering framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds arising from dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper sales must be repatriated into a PRC domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days from the export customs declaration date, unless SAFE has approved an offshore retention facility. There is no mandatory percentage surrender or conversion: the FIP may retain the balance in foreign currency within SAFE-regulated current-account retention limits or sell it to a designated bank at market rate. The statutory compliance obligation is a 100% repatriation requirement for the underlying export invoice value; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Each export receipt must be matched under SAFE's trade verification system before the bank processes the settlement or conversion, and overdue proceeds beyond the prescribed timeline require explanatory documents, possible administrative penalties, and loss of facilitation rights.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability, high legal friction. A PRC-resident foreign-invested partnership may not rely on offshore digital EMIs as primary clearing infrastructure. Fintech EMIs without PBOC banking licenses cannot obtain CNY clearing access, cannot perform current-account FX settlement, cannot open capital-project FX accounts, and cannot file SAFE trade verification returns. They may serve only as an auxiliary multi-currency information channel, with final receipt and disbursement always re-routed through a licensed domestic bank. Using unlicensed EMI rails would trigger AML enhanced due diligence and invite regulatory penalty.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: For a Hong Kong registered limited partnership, an independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit is legally mandated when annual gross income exceeds HK$5,000,000 (approximately USD 641,000), or when the Inland Revenue Department requires audited financial statements under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. There is no small-company audit exemption for limited companies in Hong Kong: every company incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) must be audited annually regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover, or employee headcount. For partnerships, the balance-sheet and headcount thresholds are not statutory audit triggers; however, a lender, investor, or partnership agreement may require a certified audit if total assets exceed HK$10,000,000 or the partnership has more than 50 employees, and such audit becomes a legal contractual obligation.
