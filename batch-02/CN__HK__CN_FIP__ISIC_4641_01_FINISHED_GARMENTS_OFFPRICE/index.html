Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with online compliance and registration filings processed through the China Electronic Tax Administration e-Tax portal; export declarations are handled via the General Administration of Customs China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the local Market Regulation Administration under the PRC Foreign Investment Law and the Measures for the Administration of Foreign-Invested Partnerships; the Business License is issued with a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically recognized as the statutory PRC taxpayer identification number and VAT identification number through the one-license-one-code integration. Step 2: Complete supplementary tax registration information through the provincial e-Tax bureau within 30 days, including legal representative or person in charge, foreign partner identity documents, accounting and bank details, payroll/social insurance indicators, and expected tax types. Step 3: For foreign non-resident partners without a PRC permanent establishment, no separate PRC TIN is required solely for the partnership interest; if such partners become liable for PRC tax on PRC-sourced income, they must obtain a TIN through the e-Tax portal using passport/identity evidence and the FIP formation documents. Step 4: Register the FIP with China Customs as an exporter to obtain the customs exporter code, and enroll in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System for goods trade. Step 5: If export VAT refunds will be claimed, open a designated bank account for tax refunds and complete the export tax refund profile in the e-Tax export tax refund module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for income tax classification. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. A foreign-invested partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent: no Enterprise Income Tax is assessed at the partnership level; corporate and individual partners are taxed on their allocable shares. The only relevant election is the VAT status election between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer, which is a separate indirect tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No tax registration exemption exists after business establishment. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; entities with annual taxable turnover at or below RMB 5,000,000 remain small-scale VAT taxpayers unless they voluntarily elect general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Finished garments exported from PRC customs territory to a non-resident buyer qualify for the 0% VAT rate under the export tax refund/exemption regime if the goods are physically exported, the export sale is evidenced by a China Customs Export Declaration, the FIP holds valid VAT input invoices for the purchased goods, and the export proceeds are received in foreign currency or eligible RMB cross-border settlement and repatriated to China within the statutory period. Shipments from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR are treated as exports for VAT purposes because Hong Kong is a separate customs territory. For a wholesale trading entity, the applicable method is generally exemption-and-refund on the purchase input VAT.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Goods Declaration for Exportation) cleared through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical export movement
  • Signed export sales contract
  • Zero-rated VAT export invoice issued through the STA e-Tax invoice system
  • Bank export proceeds collection confirmation / SAFE-recognized foreign exchange receipt notice
  • Export Tax Refund (Exemption) Declaration filed in the e-Tax export tax refund module

All VAT invoices are issued through the official China Electronic Tax Administration under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV framework. The mandate includes fully digitalized electronic invoices (all-digital e-Fapiao) with automated pre-issuance validation, unique digital invoice codes, anti-counterfeit seals, and near-real-time transmission of special VAT invoice data to the STA. No separate fiscal stamping device is used; however, invoice data clearance is a mandatory condition for valid issuance. Export sales must be evidenced by a zero-rated VAT invoice or export invoice matched to the customs export declaration in the STA data system.

The STA cross-matches VAT invoices, customs export declarations, shipping documents, and bank FX collection records under Golden Tax Phase IV. If the export is unsubstantiated or export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the associated zero-rated refund is clawed back, with daily late-payment interest at 0.05%. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the People's Republic of China and the export tax refund administration rules, false export refund claims can trigger administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the refunded amount, forfeiture of the refund, and criminal prosecution for export tax refund fraud and VAT invoice fraud under the Criminal Law of the PRC in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible for current account items such as goods trade and service trade, subject to genuine commercial documentation and SAFE trade verification, but capital account convertibility remains constrained and operates through a differentiated administrative framework. For the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), inbound foreign direct investment capital contributions are permitted under the Foreign Investment Negative List regime and must be routed through an SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a licensed domestic bank. The FIP must complete FDI information registration prior to receiving the injection, and the inbound capital can be converted into RMB only after a PRC-certified accountant issues a capital verification report and the bank verifies the permitted use. Commodity export proceeds currently do not carry a mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain or sell foreign currency at its discretion through a designated bank, but the receipts must be repatriated to a domestic account rather than kept offshore. Partnership profit distributions and capital redemption payments to the Hong Kong partner are treated as capital or investment-income outflows requiring tax clearance, amended SAFE registrations, audited financial statements, and proper FX payment purpose codes. The Hong Kong SAR itself operates on a free convertibility basis under the HKD/USD linked exchange rate system; however, cross-border RMB flows between the HK treasury vehicle and the mainland FIP remain subject to PBOC/SAFE settlement quotas, CIPS/CHATS clearing rules, and FDI verification requirements, so full convertibility on the Hong Kong side does not remove mainland-side regulatory barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Normal goods-trade export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into the FIP's domestic foreign exchange account within the statutory monitoring period, generally no later than 90 days after the export customs declaration, or within the contractual maturity for documentary trade terms where a bank extension has been registered. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to RMB in the current ordinary compliance environment; the FIP may choose to retain the foreign exchange in a designated account or convert it at the bank voluntarily under SAFE regulations. PBOC/SAFE reserve the right to impose temporary mandatory surrender only under extraordinary balance-of-payments circumstances, and no such ratio is applied in the baseline treasury blueprint.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the monetary authority and central bank for the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange administration authority; the Hong Kong SAR tier falls under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), which does not impose exchange controls but remains a clearing counterparty to the mainland RMB system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Mainland China's PBOC/SAFE regulatory perimeter reserves account-opening, foreign-exchange settlement, and FDI capital verification authority to licensed domestic commercial banks. Foreign fintech electronic money institutions are not licensed as PRC deposit-taking banks and cannot act as the primary regulatory settlement bank for a mainland FIP's capital account, export proceeds, or SAFE-reported trade flows. Such EMIs may be used only as auxiliary payment aggregation or vendor disbursement conduits where the ultimate settlement is completed by a licensed domestic bank; any direct replacement of the Tier-1 banking layer is legally barred for this structure.

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), ss.80, 82 and 82A; maximum financial penalty of HK$50,000 plus treble the tax evaded; civil penalty under s.82A of up to 300% of the tax undercharged; late-payment surcharges and interest apply.
