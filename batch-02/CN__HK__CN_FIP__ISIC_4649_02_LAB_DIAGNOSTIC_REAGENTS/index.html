Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Foreign-Invested Partnership in mainland China. The initial capital is determined solely by the capital commitment schedule negotiated by the partners in the Partnership Agreement, and contributions are made on the agreed instalment dates. No blocked escrow account is mandated, but if the contribution is remitted from outside mainland China, inbound foreign direct investment registration under SAFE and the opening of a foreign-exchange capital account are required before the capital can be settled into RMB, and the capital must be paid into the bank account of the FIP, not into any third-party or offshore account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong has no VAT, so there is no statutory zero-rating mechanism for exports. For profits tax purposes, an offshore claim (effectively a 0% effective tax rate on exported goods) is available if the source of profits is outside Hong Kong. To qualify, the sale must be concluded, negotiated, and delivered outside Hong Kong; goods may transit Hong Kong but the essential contracts and any incidental services must be performed offshore. The IRD applies the 'favourable origin' test under the territorial source principle.

  • Hong Kong Export Declaration (submitted via Trade and Industry Department's electronic data interchange system)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing shipment from Hong Kong to the foreign destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank credit advice issued by a Hong Kong licensed bank confirming receipt of foreign exchange from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial Invoice and fully signed sale contract with the overseas buyer
  • Purchase invoices and supply agreements from the manufacturer (for the Chinese-affiliated supply chain)
  • Correspondence (emails, telegrams, or letters) showing contractual negotiation and execution outside Hong Kong to support the offshore profits claim

No mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance in Hong Kong SAR. The IRD requires only that business records and invoices be retained for 7 years under sections 51C and 51D of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. No anti-fraud digital stamping or API clearance is imposed.

Although Hong Kong has no VAT clawback, false offshore profits claims are audited by the IRD. Misdeclaration may lead to criminal prosecution under sections 80–82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance: a maximum fine of HK$50,000 and additional assessment of up to 3 times the tax undercharged. In civil cases, the IRD may reopen assessments within 6 years (or 10 years for fraud or deliberate evasion). False statements in trade declarations to the Trade and Industry Department can attract separate penalties under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, including fines and imprisonment for egregious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed and non-fully convertible currency regime. Current account transactions, including trade in IVD diagnostic reagents, are generally liberalized, but each payment and receipt must have a genuine and verified commercial underlying. The Foreign-Invested Partnership may receive export proceeds and, if it opts to settle, convert them into RMB after the bank's review of trade documents; however, capital account transactions remain restricted. A mainland FIP receiving capital from its Hong Kong parent must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration through a licensed onshore bank before the inbound remittance can be credited. The funds must be placed in a designated capital foreign exchange account and converted only after capital verification and purpose review. Profit remittance of partnership distributions to offshore partners is treated as a return on investment and is subject to stringent verification, including audited financial statements, tax clearance or withholding tax payment certificates, capital verification reports, prior SAFE registration, and a valid partners' resolution. Remittance is blocked if the FIP has unresolved foreign debt, negative net assets, unreconciled receivables, or non-compliant capital account usage. Export surrender is no longer a fixed percentage; the key discipline is repatriation of export proceeds and matching them to customs declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods trade foreign exchange monitoring regime, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated through a licensed bank and reported against the corresponding customs declaration. There is no mandatory statutory percentage conversion; the entity may hold approved foreign currency or voluntarily settle into RMB. Receipts are expected within the commercial settlement cycle declared on the underlying contract, commonly benchmarked at 90 days after export; overdue receipts must be explained with supporting amendment documents, and prolonged unreconciled outstanding items may result in reclassification to B/C-class status, which then requires 100% document verification and transaction pre-approval by the bank.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is relevant only for the Hong Kong clearing leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and highly constrained. Offshore digital EMI licences obtained in the United Kingdom or Hong Kong do not convey legal authority to open RMB Basic Deposit Accounts or to act as the principal settlement bank for a mainland China FIP. PBOC and the National Financial Regulatory Administration require deposit-taking and RMB clearing services to be provided by licensed commercial banks; payment institutions may only act as ancillary collection channels under bank custody. EMI platforms cannot complete SAFE foreign exchange registration, capital account handling, or export trade verification. Their use is limited to small-value auxiliary collection where the onshore licensed bank remains the compliance owner and settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from Hong Kong to the mainland FIP must follow the FDI route: first, register the FIP with SAMR and obtain the unified social credit code; second, complete SAFE foreign exchange registration at a licensed onshore bank and obtain the FDI registration certificate; third, open a Capital Foreign Exchange Escrow Account in the FIP's name; fourth, receive the HK parent's remittance through SWIFT MT103 with the SAFE-designated purpose code for inbound direct investment capital contribution; fifth, convert into RMB only after submission of the CPA capital verification report and confirmation that the business scope is outside the Foreign Investment Negative List; and sixth, ensure all downstream uses are supported by invoices and trade contracts. Capital account receipts must not be used for securities investment, real estate outside the negative-list exception, or repayment of offshore debts without prior SAFE approval.Outbound trade disbursements for reagent inventory purchases should be executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for routine supplier payments, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value or new relationships, and Documentary Collections for trusted recurring counterparties. Each payment must carry a valid SAFE trade purpose code and be supported by proforma or commercial invoices, purchase orders, customs declarations, bills of lading, and insurance certificates. RMB-denominated cross-border payments should be cleared via CIPS to the Hong Kong affiliate; USD and EUR flows clear through correspondent networks. Turnover: Hong Kong's Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) does not extend statutory audit to limited partnerships, so there is no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold that automatically mandates audit solely for the Foreign-Invested Partnership. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under IRD DIPN 58, a Hong Kong partnership engaged in controlled transactions with associated enterprises must prepare and maintain a Local File if the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds HK$10 million per annum for goods or services, or HK$2 million per annum for financing transactions; a Master File is required where the global group’s consolidated revenue is HK$750 million or more and the Hong Kong entity’s controlled transactions exceed HK$10 million. The partnership must also keep contemporaneous TP documentation for any intercompany sales, management fees, royalty payments, and financing arrangements.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no Economic Substance Regulations equivalent to those of the UAE or Bermuda; however, for the FIP to sustain an offshore profit claim, the operating partner must demonstrate that the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — including order execution, invoicing, inventory risk, logistics, technical compliance for IVD reagents, and after-sales support — are performed in Hong Kong if the profit is claimed onshore, or outside Hong Kong if the profit is claimed offshore. The IRD and the courts will disregard artificial arrangements under sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance if the sole or dominant purpose is tax avoidance. A Hong Kong shell FIP carrying no real substance, with no staff, no office, and no decision-making functions, is a fatal red flag: the IRD will shift the entire profit to the real business location, deny treaty benefits, and issue protective assessments at 16.5%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Offshore Profit Claim for Diagnostic Reagent Distribution

Statutory sanction: Section 82A(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112): on summary conviction, a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years plus three times the amount of tax undercharged; on indictment, imprisonment for 10 years and payment of three times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue estimated assessments and impose additional 10% negligence penalties under section 82A(2).

Director personal liability: A court will pierce the partnership veil where the managing partner or corporate general partner has knowingly orchestrated the fraudulent claim. The individual is personally liable, jointly and severally, for the tax undercharged and the associated penalties; assets that have been transferred out of Hong Kong to foreign bank accounts may be traced and subjected to civil asset restitution. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department may place the individual on a departure-restraint/blacklisting register with the Immigration Department, prohibiting the person from boarding flights leaving Hong Kong until the tax debt and penalties are settled. Criminal conviction carries immediate disqualification from acting as a director and a custodial sentence of up to 10 years.
Anti-Money Laundering / Customs and Trade Sanctions Violations in Cross-Border Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Import/Export Ordinance (Cap.60) and the Trade Descriptions Ordinance (Cap.362): false trade descriptions in goods carry fines up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years. False or fraudulent import/export declarations are prosecutable by the Customs and Excise Department with seizure of the goods, forfeiture of the vessel/vehicle, and an additional penalty of HK$500,000 per schedule item. Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap.455), the court may confiscate the proceeds of the crooked commercial transactions and order compulsory restitution.

Director personal liability: Where a director/partner has directed the use of false declarations or has created a fictitious payment trail, the corporate veil is pierced and the individual’s personal assets, including residential property, bank accounts, and investment portfolios, become subject to confiscation and civil asset restitution. A travel ban and blacklisting registration with the Hong Kong Police and Immigration Department will be imposed. The individual faces imprisonment for up to 5 years on customs charges, and additionally for up to 7 years if the conduct is treated as racketeering or organized crime.
Shell Company / Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance empower the IRD to disregard the artificial royalty and fee arrangement and to assess the partnership on the full commercial profit; the IRD may also disallow the deduction of the intercompany charges. Failure to maintain TP documentation is itself an offence under section 80(2) carrying a fine at Level 3 (HK$10,000) plus three times the tax undercharged. Under the transfer-pricing rules in Part 9A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, adjustments to reflect arm’s length prices will be made and penalty surcharges of up to 100% of the additional tax may be levied. A treaty claim to reduce the outbound royalty withholding will also be denied if the recipient fails the beneficial ownership and substance tests.

Director personal liability: The partners and directors are jointly and severally liable for the additional adjusted profits tax, interest, and penalties if they were aware of or party to the creation of the shell arrangement. The IRD may obtain a charging order over the individual’s assets and ban the individual from leaving Hong Kong pending payment. Upon criminal conviction, the individual may face imprisonment for up to 3 years under IRO s.82A, and the court will generally impose an order to pay the entire undercharged tax plus three times that amount. The director is also disqualified from managing a Hong Kong company for a statutory period and is recorded on the IRD blacklist that is shared with treaty partner tax authorities.
