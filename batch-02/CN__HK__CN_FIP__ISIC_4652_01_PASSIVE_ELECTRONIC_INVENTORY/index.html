Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Hong Kong SAR Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and relevant Business Registration regulations, a registered office must be a physical address in Hong Kong SAR that is not a post office box or a virtual mail-drop. For a foreign-invested partnership engaged in passive distribution of electronic inventory, the Inland Revenue Department and banks will normally reject a pure virtual office because it creates a shell-presumption. However, premium serviced-office space on a floor of an actual commercial building, with a physical suite number, receptionist, and a lease in the entity's name, can meet the registered-office requirement and will generally be acceptable for tax registration and bank account opening, provided the entity maintains board records and business communications at that address. The tax authority will likely require evidence of substance, such as rent receipts, utility bills, and a resident manager.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; registration and ongoing tax compliance are administered through the national Electronic Tax Administration (ETA) portal operated by STA and local tax service offices, with initial establishment registration filed through the local Market Supervision Administration.

E-government filing portal

1) Submit the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registration application to the local Market Supervision Administration, including notarized and/or certified identification of foreign partners, executed partnership agreement, registered address proof, and required resolutions. 2) Obtain the partnership business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which is the PRC statutory tax identification number and VAT registration number. 3) Complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Administration platform, filing the taxpayer profile, accounting and financial record system, applicable tax categories, payroll registration, and withholding registrations. 4) Register as an invoicing entity with the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform for VAT special and ordinary invoices; complete general VAT taxpayer confirmation once the statutory turnover threshold is exceeded or upon eligible election. 5) Complete customs registration with the customs authority for export eligibility. For a foreign non-resident partner not otherwise carrying on business in China, no separate PRC tax identification number is required solely for the FIP; the FIP operates under its own Unified Social Credit Code and applies statutory withholding procedures to taxable distributions or payments to non-resident partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) Submit the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registration application to the local Market Supervision Administration, including notarized and/or certified identification of foreign partners, executed partnership agreement, registered address proof, and required resolutions. 2) Obtain the partnership business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which is the PRC statutory tax identification number and VAT registration number. 3) Complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Administration platform, filing the taxpayer profile, accounting and financial record system, applicable tax categories, payroll registration, and withholding registrations. 4) Register as an invoicing entity with the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform for VAT special and ordinary invoices; complete general VAT taxpayer confirmation once the statutory turnover threshold is exceeded or upon eligible election. 5) Complete customs registration with the customs authority for export eligibility. For a foreign non-resident partner not otherwise carrying on business in China, no separate PRC tax identification number is required solely for the FIP; the FIP operates under its own Unified Social Credit Code and applies statutory withholding procedures to taxable distributions or payments to non-resident partners.

VAT status follows statutory turnover thresholds, except for the available election to register as a general VAT taxpayer when annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000.

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC-established Foreign-Invested Partnerships must complete tax registration and VAT registration upon establishment regardless of turnover. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; general taxpayer status is otherwise available by election.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods exported by the Foreign-Invested Partnership where the goods are actually declared for export, physically leave PRC customs territory, and are sold in a genuine cross-border transaction to a non-resident buyer. The exporter must hold valid customs registration, evidence the export sale and physical transportation, and comply with statutory foreign-exchange collection and repatriation requirements. Input VAT attributable to the export sale is refundable under the export tax refund mechanism, and no VAT is charged on the export invoice. The current business scope is limited to exported electronic components, not offshore digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration form issued by the customs authority stating exporter, consignee, commodity code, value, and quantity)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent air waybill / multimodal transport document evidencing physical export
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract
  • Export proceeds collection verification record (bank/State Administration of Foreign Exchange digital record, equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate, evidencing receipt of export payment from the overseas buyer)

China operates a mandatory nationwide electronic invoicing regime through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued digitally, with invoice data transmitted in real time to the tax authority for anti-fraud validation, QR-code verification, and reconciliation of invoice issuance, tax filing, customs declaration data, and VAT returns. No separate physical fiscal clearance stamp is used; the digital clearance, real-time database matching, and anti-counterfeiting QR code validation constitute the statutory anti-fraud and audit control mechanism.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to statutory verification against customs export data, transport documentation, electronic invoice data, and foreign-exchange collection records. If documentation is deficient, the tax authority may deny zero-rating, assess output VAT at 13% on the sale, disallow the input VAT refund, impose daily late-payment surcharge, and impose administrative penalties. Fraudulent or reckless export tax refund claims may be prosecuted as the crime of fraudulent export tax refund, carrying severe monetary penalties and custodial sentences, in addition to retrospective clawback of all refunded VAT plus applicable interest and surcharges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under the Linked Exchange Rate System with the HKD pegged to the USD within a band of 7.75 to 7.85. The HKMA guarantees full convertibility of the HKD through the currency board mechanism. There are no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of capital or profits for any legal entity registered in Hong Kong, including foreign-invested partnerships. Capital account transactions, loans, and direct investments are freely permitted, and current account transactions such as trade remittances and service payments are unrestricted. The Banking Ordinance and AML/CFT Guidelines impose documentation rather than quantity-based restrictions. The only practical hurdles for a mainland-Chinese-owned partnership involve the cross-border layer: capital flowing from the PRC must satisfy the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) regulations on outbound direct investment (ODI) and foreign-exchange settlement, which require pre-registration, genuine transaction backing, and post-investment reporting. Once funds reach Hong Kong, no additional prior approval is required for ongoing operations, but annual CPA attestation and beneficial owner disclosures are standard bank KYC requirements. Profit distribution to non-resident Chinese partners is free in Hong Kong; however, the PRC partner may be subject to mainland tax and reporting requirements upon inbound remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule exists in Hong Kong. Exporters are free to retain all earnings in any currency, and banks do not impose mandatory conversion. If the Chinese parent is the ultimate recipient, the distribution will be treated as investment income and the Chinese partner must comply with SAFE's inbound flow and tax withholding rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking and monetary regulatory authority of Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is medium-to-high for payment collection and low-value disbursements through licensed virtual banks or stored value facility (SVF) operators in Hong Kong, such as Airwallex or Neat, provided they hold valid licenses under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. The legal constraint is that these EMI providers are generally not licensed to offer commercial lending, import/export documentary credits, or large-scale trade finance; therefore, SWIFT MT103 treasury clearing and LC issuance remain the uncontested domain of tier-1 licensed commercial banks. Physical presence and a long-standing banking relationship with a traditional bank should be maintained for the partnership's core cross-border trade settlement.

The Chinese investing partners must complete SAFE ODI recordation at a Chinese commercial bank before any remittance is made. The inbound wire must be transmitted over the RMB cross-border interbank payment system (CIPS) or SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code reflecting overseas direct investment, and the payment reference must clearly identify the partnership name and the equity contribution tranche. In Hong Kong, the receiving bank will designate a capital escrow sub-account; upon receipt, the bank issues an inward remittance notification, and an independent Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant performs statutory capital verification and issues a certificate confirming that the funds were received in accordance with the Limited Partnerships Ordinance. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Hong Kong registered limited partnership is not subject to a general statutory audit merely because of balance sheet size, turnover, or headcount. A mandatory independent CPA audit is triggered if: (i) the partnership deed, joint venture agreement, finance covenant, or Chinese parent entity's group reporting policy requires an audit; (ii) the partnership is a limited partnership fund under Cap. 637 for which the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance requires an auditor; (iii) the Inland Revenue Department issues a direction under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance requiring audited accounts due to inadequate records or suspected under-declaration; or (iv) the partnership is a member of a Hong Kong corporate group subject to consolidated audit filing with the Companies Registry. By way of comparison, a Hong Kong incorporated private company is statutorily audited unless it qualifies as a small company by meeting at least two of three criteria: annual turnover not exceeding HK$20 million, total assets not exceeding HK$10 million, and not more than 100 employees.
