Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The PRC partnership is transparent for Enterprise Income Tax purposes; profit is taxed at each partner level, not at the partnership entity level. Hong Kong applies territorial profits tax, so offshore trading profits of a Hong Kong-resident partner may remain outside the Hong Kong taxing net if the source of profits is outside Hong Kong.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for partnerships; pass-through treatment is mandatory. Hong Kong does not provide entity classification elections under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

No turnover-based exemption from basic tax registration exists for a licensed PRC partnership. Mainland China leg: the exported used construction excavators must be physically shipped from the PRC customs territory to an overseas destination; the CN_FIP must be the exporter named in the customs export declaration, hold valid PRC customs and foreign trade registration, maintain a genuine commercial sale to an offshore buyer settled by wire transfer or letter of credit, and present completed export customs clearance evidence. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland FIP operates under the PRC foreign exchange control framework, where the RMB is convertible for current-account transactions such as trade in used construction machinery and associated logistics, but capital-account transactions remain controlled and require SAFE registration, bank-level authenticity verification, and tax clearance before remittance. Inbound capital contributions from foreign partners are treated as direct investment and must be injected through a SAFE-registered FDI capital account, with the funds ultimately subject to the FIP's approved business scope. Outbound profit or income distributions are generally viewed as current-account investment income and can be remitted after the corporate income tax filing, audited financial statements, and proof of source and legitimacy are presented, whereas the return of partnership capital or liquidation proceeds requires a capital-account deregistration and a separate SAFE remittance filing. Hong Kong itself has no exchange controls, but because the treasury must bring funds into and out of mainland China, the binding constraint is PBOC/SAFE convertibility. Export proceeds derived from wholesale remarketing of used earthmovers must be repatriated to the mainland FIP's foreign-exchange settlement account; the entity may elect to keep up to 100% of those proceeds in foreign currency and settle into RMB on demand, subject to trade authenticity verification through invoices, customs declarations, and bills of lading. The resulting treasury design should therefore operate a two-layer liquidity matrix: an onshore SAFE-compliant RMB and foreign-exchange settlement layer at a designated Chinese bank, and an offshore HKD/USD treasury and documentary trade layer in Hong Kong governed by HKMA rules but not by PRC convertibility restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE zero-surrendrance framework, the FIP must repatriate export proceeds into its mainland foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days from the customs declaration or the agreed contractual trade settlement date, whichever is later, as supported by the underlying sales contract and customs records. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 0% of export receipts must be converted, and 100% may be retained in foreign currency. Conversion to RMB may be executed on demand through the designated bank's settlement-and-sale system, provided the bank can verify the export invoice, customs declaration, bill of lading, and proof of payment from the overseas buyer.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the offshore monetary authority for Hong Kong-dollar clearing corridors.

Airwallex Fit: High

Low to nil as a primary account. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, Currenxie, and Aspire are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open the mandatory SAFE-monitored foreign-exchange settlement account, process FDI capital registrations, execute RMB settlement-and-sale, or serve as the FIP's primary tax-filing banking relationship. They may be used only as ancillary collection and disbursement channels in Hong Kong for non-mainland transactions, and all funds that enter or leave mainland China must be routed through a licensed mainland commercial bank. Using an offshore EMI as the main treasury account would create a serious SAFE audit defect and a withholding tax and transfer-pricing hazard for the FIP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign partners must remit capital from their HKD or USD bank accounts in Hong Kong to the FIP's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at the designated mainland bank using SWIFT MT103 with the SAFE-approved direct-investment purpose code and the FDI registration certificate number referenced in the remittance narrative. The receiving bank will perform the capital-account confirmation through the SAFE information system, credit the funds, and upon request convert the foreign currency into RMB through the bank's settlement-and-sale platform. The RMB capital settlement balance can then be used strictly for operational expenses within the FIP's approved business scope, such as used excavator purchases, spare parts, freight, insurance, salaries, rent, and trade finance charges. A certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report after the funds are credited to evidence the paid-in partnership capital and support subsequent bank credit facilities or documentary trade applications.For trade settlements, the FIP should use a layered clearing model. Import disbursements for secondhand construction equipment can be made through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for advance-payment or open-account terms, through irrevocable commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 under UCP600 for large or multi-unit purchases, or through documentary collections under URC522 using SWIFT MT400 for seller-drawn drafts against documents. Export collections from overseas buyers will enter the mainland FIP's foreign-exchange settlement account via SWIFT MT103 or via an LC advising/confirming bank and subsequent presentation. The clearing bank must obtain the underlying trade documents before settling, including the commercial invoice, bill of lading, inspection certificate, customs declaration, and the FIP business registration, so that the transaction can be recorded in SAFE's current-account trade monitoring system.Onshore RMB deliverable FX forward contracts (CNY/USD and CNY/HKD) with the account bank under SAFE current-account trade-receivables and trade-payables hedging rules, strictly capped at underlying trade and working-capital exposure.Foreign exchange swaps (CNY/USD or CNY/HKD) for temporary liquidity alignment between the onshore RMB settlement layer and the offshore HKD/USD treasury balances, supported by a documented working-capital or trade-payment cycle.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps (CNY/USD or CNY/HKD) for medium-term HK-funded trade-finance or capital-expenditure hedging, provided the notional matches the FIP/HK funding corridor and no speculative or leveraged overlay is created.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) may be executed by the Hong Kong financing vehicle to hedge RMB exposure, subject to HKMA margin and KYC rules, but the mainland FIP itself may not use onshore NDFs because RMB is deliverable and speculative NDF structures are not permitted under SAFE.Bang-bang or participative structured forwards are permissible only where the structure does not embed a barrier that creates a leverage or option-like payoff beyond the real trade exposure, and only after written approval from the relationship bank's derivatives compliance desk.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to nil as a primary account. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, Currenxie, and Aspire are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open the mandatory SAFE-monitored foreign-exchange settlement account, process FDI capital registrations, execute RMB settlement-and-sale, or serve as the FIP's primary tax-filing banking relationship. They may be used only as ancillary collection and disbursement channels in Hong Kong for non-mainland transactions, and all funds that enter or leave mainland China must be routed through a licensed mainland commercial bank. Using an offshore EMI as the main treasury account would create a serious SAFE audit defect and a withholding tax and transfer-pricing hazard for the FIP.
