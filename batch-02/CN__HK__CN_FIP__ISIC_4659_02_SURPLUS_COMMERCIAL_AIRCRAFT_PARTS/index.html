Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a Foreign-Invested Partnership structured as a limited partnership. The partners are free to set capital contributions and payment dates in the partnership deed; no blocked escrow account is mandated by the Companies Registry. The only escrow element is the commercial aviation payment rail requirement that funds remain in escrow until title and airworthiness documents are delivered. The limited partner contribution must be stated in the Deed of Limited Partnership and the Statement of Particulars, and a limited partner who withdraws capital when the partnership is unable to pay its debts may be liable for repayment under section 11 of Cap. 37.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certificate from the Fire Services Department after inspection of means of escape, fire loads and extinguishing systems; Dangerous Goods Licence from the Fire Services Department under Cap. 295 for storerooms containing dangerous goods; Buildings Department Occupation Permit or annual fire safety inspection certificate; Civil Aviation Department physical security clearance in accordance with the approved Aviation Security Programme; and Labour Department Occupational Safety and Health clearance for manual handling, racking safety and electrical safety certification issued by a registered electrical worker.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity intending to establish a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong must first ensure the partnership is registered under the Business Registration Ordinance via the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department within one month of commencement of business; the IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) and a Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the primary tax identification number for the partnership for profits tax, employer return obligations, and indirect tax administration, noting that Hong Kong does not maintain a separate VAT identification number registration regime because it has no general VAT or GST; the partnership must also appoint a local tax representative if it does not maintain a permanent establishment in Hong Kong, and must file the mandatory notification with the IRD under the Taxation (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 for economic substance and active business requirements; where the partnership is a non-resident person not carrying on business in Hong Kong, no tax registration file will be required unless a Hong Kong-source profits tax exposure arises; the FIP should submit Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) to the Business Registration Office, together with the partnership agreement and evidence of identity of the general and limited partners, and the IRD will allocate the BRN after processing; for withholding tax and dividend distribution reference, the FIP must update the IRD with the partnership’s head office address, principal place of business in Hong Kong, PRC parent information, and the identity of the tax representative in charge of the partnership’s Hong Kong tax affairs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT or GST; Hong Kong relies on a narrow Goods and Services Tax-equivalent regime limited to dutiable commodities (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol) under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, plus hotel accommodation tax (currently suspended during COVID relief), and no sales/use tax is imposed on the sale of goods; the cross-border supply of aircraft parts is therefore outside any indirect tax registration system

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable (no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because, except for licensing of duty-paid warehouses under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, indirect tax registration is not triggered by turnover; the Business Registration Ordinance applies a nominal annual Business Registration Certificate fee of HKD 2,000 per year or HKD 150 for one-year certificates for entities with low profit expectations, but this is not a turnover-based indirect tax threshold; if the FIP deals in dutiable commodities, a Dutiable Commodities Licence is required, but a pure aircraft spares trader has no such obligation)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong has no VAT-style export zero-rating mechanism because exports of goods and services fall outside the domestic indirect tax base entirely; eligibility to claim 0% indirect tax therefore does not require a VAT registration; for customs purposes, the export of civil aircraft rotables and avionics surplus from Hong Kong must comply with the Import and Export Ordinance and utilise the Trade Single Window of Hong Kong Customs for export declarations; the following statutory criteria apply: (i) the goods must legally enter Hong Kong and be exported within the prescribed period, or if in transit, they must be declared through the Ferry Terminal or Air Cargo export declaration system of the Census and Statistics Department; (ii) the FIP as exporter must be entered in the trade declaration system and submit accurate export declarations under seal via the Government Electronic Trading Services (GETS); (iii) the goods must correspond to the Schedule 8 list of Article 2 of the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations; (iv) where the sale is a genuine commercial export to a mainland China or overseas buyer, the FIP must retain evidence of physical export, payment in foreign exchange, and title transfer; no zero-rating refund claim exists because neither input VAT nor output VAT is recorded for exported goods; for the produced PRC-side entity, if the FIP is later integrated into mainland operations, the relevant offshore direct sales export from the mainland entity could qualify for zero-rated VAT under the PRC Export Tax Rebate regime, but that is a separate jurisdiction.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API reporting, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists for general B2B aircraft parts transactions in Hong Kong; the Inland Revenue Department requires only that proper invoices and receipts be retained for profits tax assessment; entities under the eTAX portal must issue invoices containing the supplier's name, BRN, description of goods, date, quantity, and consideration, and must keep records for seven years; there is no government-cleared XML fiscal invoice regime equivalent to the PRC Golden Tax System; for export compliance to mainland China and other jurisdictions, the FIP must, however, comply with electronic invoice requirements of the importing jurisdiction, including but not limited to China's fully digitalized electronic invoice (数电票) system, which the Hong Kong supplier is not itself required to operate.

For Hong Kong profits tax purposes, if the IRD determines that an export sale is not a genuine offshore transaction but the FIP carries on business in Hong Kong and derives the profit there, the full profit is chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax at 16.5% (8.25% for the first HKD 2 million of assessable profits for qualifying entities, but partnerships retain 15% progressive rate for the first HKD 2 million and 16.5% beyond), plus a 10% tax reduction for the 2022-23 assessment was historically granted; no specific indirect-tax clawback applies, but penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance include, upon tax evasion or incorrect returns, a fine up to HKD 50,000 plus additional tax of treble the amount of tax undercharged; for failure to comply with export declaration obligations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, the FIP may be liable to a fine of HKD 50,000 on each declaration and, for knowingly making false declarations, imprisonment of 3 years and a fine of HKD 500,000 under the Customs and Excise Service Ordinance; where a mainland PRC export is involved and the partnership attempts to inject false export documentation into the PRC tax rebate system, the PRC State Taxation Administration will claw back the entire export tax rebate, impose a penalty of one to five times the tax defrauded, and suspend export tax rebate qualification for up to three years; under Hong Kong's foreign exchange control, there is no statutory repatriation deadline, but trade receivables evidenced by IRD must be repatriated to Hong Kong within 180 days for genuine offshore substance under the economic substance rule; failure to maintain proof of currency repatriation within 180 days will be treated by the IRD as a factor indicating the transaction was not an offshore sale, and the profits tax will be reassessed on the full amount with consequential tax penalty for incorrect returns.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a fully convertible currency regime with no surrender requirements, because the Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and the Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS) anchors HKD to the US dollar within a 7.75-7.85 band. Current account items such as trade payments for surplus commercial aircraft parts, commissions, and service fees are freely remittable once the underlying transaction is genuine and properly documented. Capital account items, including inbound equity or loan injections into a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), are also liberalized in Hong Kong, with no prior SAFE-style approval for HK-based entities, but the FIP must maintain proper board resolutions, partnership agreements, and beneficial ownership declarations to satisfy banks' internal compliance. The material profit remittance hurdle in Hong Kong is not exchange control but rather the need to evidence economic substance, corroborating invoices, shipping documents, and aviation industry certifications to rebut any inference of trade-based money laundering. For the mainland China parent or supplier leg, Chinese entities still face SAFE rules on current item verification, and any payment from China to the Hong Kong FIP must be routed with the correct transaction codes (e.g., 121010 for general trade goods) and supported by customs declarations. Overall, the FIP can convert HKD to USD, EUR, or CNY freely for trade settlement, and no central bank approval is required for ordinary aviation parts distribution, but the bank's own internal controls and the underlying substance of the trading entity remain the practical constraints on convertibility and remittance velocity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR does not apply any statutory export proceeds surrender requirement; all foreign currency export receipts may be retained, converted, or disbursed freely in the market. For mainland China-related export legs, SAFE regulations require that export proceeds in foreign currency be repatriated to China and processed through the bank's monitoring system within a prescribed period, typically 180 days from the date of export declaration, and if the proceeds relate to a foreign-invested enterprise, the enterprise may retain them in its capital account settlement after verification; however, the Hong Kong FIP itself is not subject to any surrender ratio.

Central bank supervision: The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking authority for Hong Kong SAR, together with the Hong Kong Association of Banks (HKAB) for clearing house governance; for mainland China cross-border aspects, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) remains the regulatory authority for CN-side capital flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Legal feasibility is low for full-service banking because the core aviation surplus parts distribution involves high-value escrow transactions, letters of credit, and documentary trade cycles requiring a licensed bank's credit intermediation. Hong Kong, however, has a licensing regime under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority for Stored Value Facilities, and a fintech EMI may offer payment/e-money services but cannot act as a primary escrow bank or issue commercial trade finance instruments. The FIP should use a licensed Tier-1 bank as the master account provider, while a fintech EMI may be used only as a sub-account overlay for petty disbursements such as inspection fees or courier payments, not for large aviation aircraft part settlements. The practical reality is that most international EMI providers (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, or similar) operating in Hong Kong can support straight-through SWIFT processing and multi-currency accounts, but their legal construction generally remains as an authorized institution or licensed money service operator with limitations on deposit-taking. Therefore, the recommendation is to structure the primary treasury through a Tier-1 bank and relegate EMIs to an auxiliary rail with strict velocity and counterparty limits; otherwise, transaction turnaround times for aviation-part escrow releases and LC negotiations will be breached.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The typical route for the FIP to receive capital from a mainland China parent or foreign investor is a direct SWIFT wire transfer from the investor's bank to the FIP's HKD/USD capital escrow account in Hong Kong, using SWIFT MT103 with proper beneficiary and purpose codes. For mainland China outward remittances to the Hong Kong FIP as shareholder loan or partnership capital, the remitting Chinese company must file the transaction with SAFE through its designated bank under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) framework, cite the correct SAFE code (e.g., 623011 for capital injection into overseas partnership or 622181 for overseas loan), and complete the capital project verification with the local SAFE branch. The Chinese resident shareholder must present the partnership registration certificate issued by the Hong Kong Companies Registry, the FIP's bank account opening notice, and a written explanation of the commercial purpose. In the absence of a required SAFE registration, the FIP should not receive the capital as a clean wire; instead the investor should route through the Hong Kong branch of a Chinese bank to allow the Chinese bank to complete cross-border capital verification. The capital verification in Hong Kong is not mandatory for the FIP's operating account unless the FIP has to satisfy mainland China's foreign exchange reporting, in which case a Hong Kong certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report confirming the receipt of funds into the FIP's escrow account and the corresponding partnership capital allocation.For outbound trade disbursements from the Hong Kong FIP to suppliers of surplus commercial aircraft parts, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers when the supplier requires immediate settlement; however, for high-value and documentation-heavy aviation transactions, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) issued at the FIP's account bank and advised through a correspondent bank is the recommended rail to protect the buyer's prepayment exposure. A documentary collection (D/P or D/A) is permissible for repeated suppliers with established performance, but it does not provide the same security as an L/C and may require the FIP to accept the risk of the supplier's failure to present conforming documents. The participation of the aviation escrow agent in the payment flow is common: the buyer instructs the bank to release funds to the escrow agent, the escrow agent verifies the airworthiness certificate, and then executes the wire to the supplier. For USD-clearing, the FIP's bank will require that the beneficiary bank is not on OFAC or HKMA sanction lists; for non-USD currencies, the FIP may use the Clearing House Automated Transfer System (CHATS) in HKD or TARGET2 in EUR. All outbound trade disbursements should be supported by the underlying sales contract, pro-forma invoice, commercial invoice, airway bill, and inspection certificate; otherwise the bank's transactional monitoring system will flag the payment.Vanilla deliverable forward contracts in USD/HKD, EUR/HKD, and CNY/HKD for firm trade settlement commitments up to 12 months from order to settlement date, provided the FIP has a documented underlying aviation trade exposure and the bank's ISDA or master FX agreement is in place.Plain vanilla currency swaps between HKD and USD for the purpose of matching the FIP's asset and liability currency mix on capital escrow balances, but only for hedging actual capital flows and not for speculative market-making.FX options (call and put) with straightforward expiry and strike are permissible for protecting the FIP's bid/ask spread on surplus aircraft parts tenders, but only with bank-approved credit limits and full margin coverage.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing offshore CNY (CNH) may be permissible for the mainland China-linked legs when a deliverable CNY contract is not accessible to the Hong Kong FIP; however, the bank must validate the underlying trade invoices and the notional must be strictly invoice-backed.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility is low for full-service banking because the core aviation surplus parts distribution involves high-value escrow transactions, letters of credit, and documentary trade cycles requiring a licensed bank's credit intermediation. Hong Kong, however, has a licensing regime under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority for Stored Value Facilities, and a fintech EMI may offer payment/e-money services but cannot act as a primary escrow bank or issue commercial trade finance instruments. The FIP should use a licensed Tier-1 bank as the master account provider, while a fintech EMI may be used only as a sub-account overlay for petty disbursements such as inspection fees or courier payments, not for large aviation aircraft part settlements. The practical reality is that most international EMI providers (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, or similar) operating in Hong Kong can support straight-through SWIFT processing and multi-currency accounts, but their legal construction generally remains as an authorized institution or licensed money service operator with limitations on deposit-taking. Therefore, the recommendation is to structure the primary treasury through a Tier-1 bank and relegate EMIs to an auxiliary rail with strict velocity and counterparty limits; otherwise, transaction turnaround times for aviation-part escrow releases and LC negotiations will be breached.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 7,500,000 – a Hong Kong FIP that enters into cross-border controlled transactions in excess of this annual aggregate amount, whether for inventory, services, royalties, or financing, is required to prepare and maintain a contemporaneous Local File in connection with its BIR52 filing; a Master File is required when the worldwide group turnover exceeds HKD 680,000,000; the FIP must also submit a transfer-pricing disclosure statement through the electronic filing platform, identifying the selected arm’s-length methodology and benchmarking study for each controlled transaction

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no formal Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Caribbean regimes, but the economic-substance concept is firmly embedded in three doctrines: the offshore profits source rule, the Mainland-HK DTA beneficial-ownership requirement, and the transfer-pricing Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis; a HK FIP trading certified civil aircraft rotables must demonstrate that it performs economic functions, has real employees, contracts negotiations, inventory risk, procurement decisions, and credit/default risk; if the partnership merely invoices through Hong Kong while the relevant CIGA occur in Mainland China or an offshore territory, the IRD will tax the profit in Hong Kong, reject the offshore claim, deny treaty relief, and potentially disregard the FIP as a sham.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner’s directors are personally exposed: the court will pierce the partnership veil if the partnership is used as a facade to cover the individual partner's wilful default; directors may be jointly and severally liable to pay the undercharged tax and penalties out of personal assets, subject to civil asset-freezing injunctions, travel restrictions pending prosecution, and disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance; a criminal custodial sentence of up to 3 years applies to the responsible officer who signed the false declaration
Shell Company / Abusive Transfer Pricing and DTA Relief Abuse

Statutory sanction: Violates Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61, 61A, 61B (artificial transaction and tax avoidance), section 50AA (transfer-pricing prohibition), and section 82A; the IRD may recharacterize the transaction, assess the transfer-pricing adjustment on the commercial value of the aircraft parts, deny the offshore claim, impose treble penalties, and revoke any DTA certificate of residence; on the Mainland side, the State Taxation Administration can re-characterize the transaction under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and reclassify the dividend as a taxable distributor profit

Director personal liability: The directors of the Hong Kong corporate general partner will be held personally liable for breach of fiduciary duties under the Companies Ordinance and for knowingly making false arrangements; they may be ordered to repay distributions to the partnership so that the partnership can settle the adjusted tax, and may be disqualified as directors for up to 15 years; the regulatory authorities may place them on a tax non-compliance blacklist, prevent them from leaving Hong Kong for travel to treaty jurisdictions, and the case may be escalated to criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the revenue
AML/CFT and International Sanctions Violation in Aviation Payments

Statutory sanction: Contravenes the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615), particularly the customer due diligence and record-keeping requirements of Schedule 2 and sections 20-23; penalties on conviction may include a fine of up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for a non-compliance or money-laundering offence; where commercial aircraft parts are treated as dual-use goods, the investigator may refer the matter to the United Nations Sanctions (Amendment) Ordinance and international financial sanctions regimes for additional asset-freeze and confiscation orders

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or recklessly permit the laundering of escrow proceeds are personally subject to criminal prosecution, civil forfeiture of assets traceable to the wire transfers, and blacklisting by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit; the court may also issue a travel ban while the assets are located, and the foreign-bond procedure may prevent the defendant from leaving Hong Kong; directors of the corporate general partner may be sentenced to up to 7 years’ imprisonment and disqualified from managing any Hong Kong company for ten years or more.
