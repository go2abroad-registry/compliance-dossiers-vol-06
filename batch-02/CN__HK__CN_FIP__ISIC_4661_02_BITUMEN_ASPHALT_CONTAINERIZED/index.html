Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the STA Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the China Tax Service mobile application; the one-stop enterprise formation registration is synchronized with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR).

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registered in mainland China: (1) the FIP is formed by filing a partnership registration with SAMR and obtains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax administration; (2) tax registration is completed automatically through the SAMR-STA data synchronization mechanism, activating the FIP's e-Tax Platform account; (3) the FIP must then complete supplementary tax registration data on the e-Tax Platform, including legal representative, financial officer, accounting system, expected taxable activities, and bank account information; (4) the FIP files its VAT taxpayer classification (small-scale or general taxpayer) with the competent local STA office; (5) a foreign non-resident partner of the FIP is deemed to carry on a trade or business in China through the partnership and must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number from STA for reporting and withholding of Chinese-source income allocated by the FIP.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible: the FIP is fiscally transparent for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) purposes, so the partnership itself is not a CIT payer and each partner is taxed on its allocable share of China-source and China-related profits; however, for VAT, consumption tax, and other turnover taxes the FIP is an entity-level taxpayer and must invoice, file, and pay in its own name (Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident partners taxed only on China-source income). — For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registered in mainland China: (1) the FIP is formed by filing a partnership registration with SAMR and obtains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax administration; (2) tax registration is completed automatically through the SAMR-STA data synchronization mechanism, activating the FIP's e-Tax Platform account; (3) the FIP must then complete supplementary tax registration data on the e-Tax Platform, including legal representative, financial officer, accounting system, expected taxable activities, and bank account information; (4) the FIP files its VAT taxpayer classification (small-scale or general taxpayer) with the competent local STA office; (5) a foreign non-resident partner of the FIP is deemed to carry on a trade or business in China through the partnership and must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number from STA for reporting and withholding of Chinese-source income allocated by the FIP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the FIP is fiscally transparent for Corporate Income Tax (CIT) purposes, so the partnership itself is not a CIT payer and each partner is taxed on its allocable share of China-source and China-related profits; however, for VAT, consumption tax, and other turnover taxes the FIP is an entity-level taxpayer and must invoice, file, and pay in its own name (Territorial_Scope applies to non-resident partners taxed only on China-source income).

Available election forms

None for US-style check-the-box; PRC law does not allow entity classification elections for partnerships. Available statutory elections include: (1) voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer for an FIP with annual taxable sales below the mandatory threshold; (2) election to remain a small-scale taxpayer and use the simplified levy method (currently 3%, with temporary reductions to 1% during relief periods) with an annual sales ceiling of RMB 5,000,000; (3) election of the small-scale taxpayer VAT exemption where monthly taxable sales do not exceed RMB 100,000; (4) allocation of partnership profits in accordance with the partnership deed for CIT pass-through reporting. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available; China applies its own anti-avoidance and anti-hybrid rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory for any FIP engaging in taxable sales of goods in mainland China from establishment. Mandatory general taxpayer registration applies when the standard annual taxable turnover for goods (including containerized bitumen/asphalt) exceeds RMB 5,000,000; an FIP below this threshold is classified as a small-scale taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayers whose monthly sales do not exceed RMB 100,000 may claim the prevailing exemption; exports are zero-rated and also count within the registration framework for refund entitlement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero rate (0% VAT with entitlement to input credit/refund) applies where: (1) the FIP sells containerized drummed or bagged penetration-grade bitumen to an overseas buyer and the goods are physically exported from the customs territory of mainland China; (2) the export is evidenced by a GACC-approved customs export declaration and the goods are shipped under a bill of lading; (3) the commercial invoice, sales contract, and export documents are consistent; (4) the export proceeds are received from the overseas buyer in foreign currency and repatriated to the FIP's bank account with the settlement bank; and (5) the FIP is a general VAT taxpayer and has filed the export sales for exemption/refund within the statutory declaration period. Qualifying offshore services and digital services supplied by an FIP to non-resident recipients and consumed wholly outside China may also be zero-rated where the statutory supporting evidence is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Goods Customs Declaration Form)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) evidencing actual export shipment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank-issued Export Proceeds Collection Voucher and SAFE cross-border receipt declaration
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract matching the customs declaration
  • SWIFT letter of credit or TT wire settlement records where applicable

China mandates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT general taxpayers must issue FDEIs or controlled special VAT invoices in real time through the e-Tax Platform; invoices are cleared and stored on the national tax data platform without a separate fiscal stamping device. API-based electronic invoicing is accepted for ERP/backend integration, and anti-fraud monitoring is enforced by real-time matching of invoice issuance, customs export declarations, and SAFE foreign-exchange settlement records.

The STA and GACC jointly audit export zero-rating claims through the Golden Tax Phase IV data platform, cross-matching export declarations, VAT invoices, bills of lading, and SAFE/foreign-exchange receipt data. Where export zero-rating is unsubstantiated, fraudulent, or foreign exchange is not repatriated within the statutory period: (1) the claimed VAT refund or exemption is clawed back with late-payment interest at 0.05% per day; (2) an administrative penalty from 50% up to 5 times the tax involved is imposed under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law; (3) the FIP's export tax rebate classification is downgraded and export rebates may be suspended for up to 36 months; and (4) criminal liability for export tax fraud may be pursued against the FIP and its responsible individuals, with fines and imprisonment available at law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible currency under the linked exchange rate system. The Hong Kong dollar operates within a strong-side and weak-side convertibility zone against the US dollar, and there are no surrender, approval, or repatriation requirements for current account or capital account movements. Non-resident partners may freely convert HKD, offshore CNY (CNH), USD, EUR and other major currencies at licensed banks. Profit distributions and capital withdrawals from a Foreign-Invested Partnership established in Hong Kong are not subject to currency conversion limits. The only practical frictions arise when the funding source is Mainland China: those remittances are then governed by SAFE onshore rules, including outbound investment filing, annual quota registration, and authentic end-use documentation. Capital account convertibility in HK is therefore unrestricted, while current account convertibility is the default regime. The HKMA does not impose exchange control rules on the receipt, retention, or payment of export proceeds. Instead of exchange control approval, banks perform commercial reasonableness checks, customer due diligence, and anti-money-laundering verification to ensure the underlying bitumen trade is genuine and the capital flows align with the FIP partnership agreement and corporate treasury mandate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain proceeds in any eligible currency, convert freely, or apply them directly to trade settlement. If the same Foreign-Invested Partnership or a related mainland exporting entity realizes export receipts from the PRC, SAFE requires current account proceeds to be repatriated through onshore designated banks and supporting trade documents must be presented; non-RMB export revenues may generally be retained in a foreign exchange account, but the corresponding SAFE verification and bank filing obligations must be complied with. In HK, the liquidity discipline is documentary genuineness rather than a fixed percentage surrender, so the treasury function should maintain clean underlying invoices, bills of lading, customs filings and payment contracts.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China governs any onshore CNY remittance routed from or through Mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is high for auxiliary payment processing but low for capital escrow and documentary trade. Fintech EMIs and stored value facility licensees under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance cannot issue letters of credit, cannot provide capital verification, cannot act as a regulated escrow agent for equity injections, and generally lack the correspondent banking depth needed for containerized bitumen settlement. Some Hong Kong virtual banks may open corporate accounts for payroll or expense disbursement, but for a low-risk wholesale bitumen trading partnership the primary clearing rail must pass through a full-service Tier-1 licensed commercial bank. Fintech EMIs can be used only as a secondary channel for small expatriate or domestic payments, subject to the bank account being in the FIP's name and all transactions being fully tracked.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: For a Hong Kong registered Foreign-Invested Partnership, the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) does not impose a statutory audit threshold; however, under IRD administrative requirements for Form BIR52, where the partnership's gross income for the year exceeds HK$5,000,000 the accounts submitted with the return must be audited or certified by a practising certified public accountant. If the entity were instead structured as a Hong Kong private limited company, the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) requires a statutory audit unless the company qualifies for the small private company audit exemption: annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000, balance sheet total not exceeding HK$10,000,000, and 100 employees or fewer, with the additional condition that members' unanimous consent is obtained; exceeding any of those thresholds triggers a mandatory independent CPA audit.
