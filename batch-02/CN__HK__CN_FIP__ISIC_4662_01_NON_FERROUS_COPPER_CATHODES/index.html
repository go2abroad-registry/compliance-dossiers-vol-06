Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Ordinance and Limited Partnership Ordinance, a registered office must be a genuine physical address in Hong Kong and a Post Office box number is prohibited. A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not per se prohibited for registry purposes where a physical suite and mail scanning service is used, but for the present wholesale copper cathode business the Inland Revenue Department, Hong Kong Customs and the relevant bank will reject pure virtual offices because the entity must demonstrate actual place of business, substance for profit-tax residence and origin, safe custody of inventory documentation, and credible invoice and warrant handling. A staffed serviced office with a dedicated desk, nameplate, lease, utilities and local management may be accepted; a non-staffed mail-forwarding virtual office is commercially unacceptable and will defeat bank account opening, LME warrant transfer and documentary LC issuance.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) and the China Digital Electronic Invoice Platform for mainland China; and the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, administered through the eTAX electronic services portal for Hong Kong SAR tax registration and filings.

E-government filing portal

For a mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP): (1) register the FIP with the local Administration for Market Regulation to obtain the partnership business licence and the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the Chinese tax identification number (TIN); (2) within 30 days of obtaining the licence, complete the STA tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) or in-person at the competent tax office, submitting the partnership agreement, business licence, details of foreign investing partners, and non-resident partner identification documents including foreign TINs; (3) complete VAT taxpayer registration; if projected annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, the FIP must register as a general VAT taxpayer, otherwise it may maintain small-scale taxpayer status unless an election to be a general taxpayer is made; (4) file a Non-Resident Enterprise tax registration file for any foreign partner that is deemed to maintain a taxable establishment in China and ensure that the FIP obtains a separate VAT record for export declarations. For a Hong Kong entity or branch: (1) apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) within 30 days of commencing business; (2) use the BRC number and branch code as the Hong Kong tax identification identifiers; (3) separately obtain a Profits Tax file with the IRD for the entity or partnership; and (4) complete the IRD taxpayer registration through eTAX with details of the controlling entity, business nature (ISIC 4662), and address.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the PRC, a Foreign-Invested Partnership is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer; each partner is separately assessed on their allocated share of the partnership's profits. Hong Kong partnership law similarly treats partnerships as transparent for Profits Tax, with each partner being subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax only on profits of Hong Kong source.

Available election forms

None. Neither mainland China nor Hong Kong SAR provides a check-the-box election for Foreign-Invested Partnerships; the pass-through classification is statutory. No hybrid mismatch election is available in either jurisdiction, although OECD/G20 hybrid mismatch outcomes will be analysed under the relevant China and Hong Kong anti-avoidance provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) annual taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT registration in mainland China; lower turnover FIPs must still perform tax registration and may be registered as small-scale VAT taxpayers. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT or GST registration threshold because no such indirect tax exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating under mainland China VAT law applies to physical export sales of goods, including Grade A refined copper cathodes, where the FIP is the legal exporter, the goods are actually shipped out of the PRC customs territory, the export declaration is filed through the China International Trade Single Window, the export sale is separately accounted for, and the proceeds are repatriated to China through approved foreign exchange channels within the statutory deadline. For Hong Kong SAR, no VAT zero-rating is relevant; a Hong Kong trader will instead rely on the territorial source principle under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, under which profits derived from goods exported out of Hong Kong and sold outside Hong Kong are not subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax where the operations and source of profit are outside Hong Kong.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Entry) filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal B/L, together with LME warrant transfer records where applicable) evidencing physical export of copper cathodes
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Foreign Exchange Receipt Verification Certificate issued by a bank authorised for cross-border foreign exchange
  • Export commercial invoice and itemised packing list
  • Electronic or paper special VAT invoice for the purchase of the copper cathodes, evidencing recoverable input VAT
  • Contract, letter of credit (LC) documents, and title transfer records sufficient to prove an actual offshore sale
  • Customs release / clearance notice and any customs inspection or survey report obtained during export

Mainland China: mandatory use of fully digitalised electronic invoices (e-invoices) through the State Taxation Administration Digital Electronic Invoice Platform; invoice data must be submitted in real time via the electronic tax bureau application programming interfaces (APIs) and is subject to Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud clearance, digital interception, and an anti-counterfeit QR code / invoice code. Hong Kong SAR: no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-based invoice validation, and no digital stamping mandate; business records and invoices are maintained only to satisfy the IRD's record-retention requirements.

Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying of the PRC and STA regulations governing export VAT refunds, any export zero-rating claim is subject to documentary desk audit, risk classification, field inspection, and retrospective clawback. If the customs documentation or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is missing, false, or irregular, or if the goods did not in fact leave the PRC customs territory, the export sale will be recharacterised as a domestic taxable sale, the zero rating or refund will be clawed back in full, and the FIP will be assessed for VAT at the standard rate plus a daily overdue tax surcharge of 0.05%. Where fraud is established, an administrative penalty of between 50% and 5 times the tax amount in question may be imposed, and the FIP may be suspended from export-tax-refund status. Serious cases may also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the PRC for fraudulent export tax refund or VAT invoice fraud. In Hong Kong, any false or misleading declaration made to the IRD in connection with profits tax or business registration is subject to penalties, fines, and imprisonment under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), although there is no separate VAT clawback regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully liberalized capital and current account with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no prior approval mechanism for foreign currency conversion. The Hong Kong dollar operates under a currency board architecture linked to the US dollar within a band of 7.75 to 7.85 HKD per USD, giving the jurisdiction a stable and highly convertible monetary base. The Hong Kong FIP may freely receive, hold, convert, and remit funds in any major currency, including USD, EUR, RMB (as offshore CNH), and HKD, without mandatory repatriation or conversion of export proceeds. Profit repatriation to partners, including non-resident principals, is unrestricted under the partnership deed and requires no central bank license or exchange control permit. The only practical hurdles are documentary in nature: the receiving Tier-1 bank will require source-of-wealth evidence, a clear economic purpose, and consistent trade documentation for every inward or outward remittance. For capital originating from mainland China, the remitter must separately comply with the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange outward direct investment rules, but once funds land in Hong Kong they are purely market-convertible. Cross-border RMB clearing is efficiently supported by the CNH offshore market, while the HKMA's triangular clearing systems and Continuous Linked Settlement provide liquidity for commercial bank payment settlements. Overall, the jurisdiction poses no sovereign-level convertibility hurdle; compliance risk sits at the bank's AML/KYC underwriting stage rather than at the FX regulatory stage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rules apply in Hong Kong SAR. Exporters and trading entities, including the Hong Kong FIP, may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts indefinitely, without mandatory conversion into HKD or any prescribed timeline for sale or repatriation.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), operating through the Linked Exchange Rate System as the de facto central bank and foreign exchange manager for Hong Kong SAR; no separate foreign exchange administration exists and the HKMA delegates day-to-day convertibility operations to authorized institutions under the Banking Ordinance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to Medium for the specific copper cathode wholesale arbitrage model. Offshore or Hong Kong licensed digital EMI platforms are not authorized institutions capable of providing full documentary trade credit, LME warrant settlement, or large-value capital escrow services. A fintech EMI may lawfully operate in Hong Kong as a Money Service Operator under the AMLO or as a Stored Value Facility licensee, but such providers cannot underwrite commercial letters of credit, issue guarantees, or hold partnership capital in a manner acceptable to LME clearing members and trade counterparties. For this entity, offshore digital EMIs should be restricted to minor vendor payments or receivables collections and cannot replace a Tier-1 commercial bank for core treasury operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital to the Hong Kong FIP should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the designated Tier-1 bank account. For US dollar injections use purpose code 'Capital Contribution – Partnership Equity Injection'; for RMB injections use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) with a purpose narrative referencing the partnership capital account. If the remitter is a mainland Chinese entity, the remittance must be backed by a SAFE outward direct investment filing and the mainland bank's foreign exchange purchase receipt. The Hong Kong receiving bank will treat the credit as paid-in capital upon receipt of the partnership deed, the inward remittance advice, and an auditor's confirmation letter if capital verification is required by the partnership agreement.
