Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Companies Registry will accept a virtual office address as the statutory registered office of a limited partnership, provided that the address is a physical location in Hong Kong capable of receiving service of process and mail, and is not a PO Box or a non-existent address. However, for the purposes of this medium-risk cross-border procurement entity in battery-grade lithium carbonate, the Inland Revenue Department and Hong Kong banks will almost certainly reject a pure voice mailbox or non-staffed virtual-office arrangement. Banking institutions, when conducting customer due diligence under the AMLO, require a demonstrable commercial place of business, a physical desk or meeting facility, a local telephone listing, and a statement that the partnership intends to maintain a meaningful business presence. A serviced office with dedicated meeting rooms and a known corporate service provider is acceptable if the office is shared with multiple companies, yet the banks will evaluate the actual substance, not just the address. Accordingly, virtual offices are deemed permissible only for registry purposes, but are assessed as incompatible with the expected banking and tax needs of this partnership unless reinforced with a professional registered agent and a serviced office contract of at least 12 months.

None under Cap. 637. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no minimum capital contribution window, and no blocked escrow account requirement imposed by the Companies Registry. For bank-account opening and SWIFT letter-of-credit facilities, the chosen licensed bank may, at its AML/credit discretion, require an initial operating deposit or margin, but this is a bank policy and not a Companies Registry statutory condition. Foreign investors may subscribe as limited partners without any mandatory local capital locking in Hong Kong.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China for mainland China VAT and corporate income tax compliance, with local provincial electronic tax bureau portals; Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region for Hong Kong profits tax/business registration via the eTAX electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership: (1) obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Administration for Market Regulation; the USCC is also the taxpayer identification number under the one-license-one-code reform; (2) within 30 days of business license issuance, complete tax-registration information confirmation at the competent local tax service office of the STA using the electronic tax bureau; (3) register for VAT as a small-scale taxpayer or apply for VAT general taxpayer status depending on annual taxable sales and projected export operations; (4) apply to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for foreign-exchange registration if export receipts are to be collected in foreign currency. For the Hong Kong SAR element: a foreign non-resident entity or partner carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) for a Business Registration Certificate and obtain the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the only taxpayer identifier in Hong Kong; there is no separate VAT ID or TIN. The BRN application must be lodged within one month of commencement of business, with the partnership deed, identity or incorporation documents of the partners, and a Hong Kong registered address.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the RMB is fully convertible for most current-account transactions but remains subject to prudential controls on capital-account items. For the CN_FIP, the onshore operating entity resides in mainland China, so all foreign-exchange activities are regulated by PBOC/SAFE rather than by Hong Kong rules. Capital injections from Hong Kong partners must be registered with SAFE through a designated domestic bank, and the proceeds must flow into a restricted capital settlement account; the supervision authority cross-checks actual investment, use of funds and documentation. Current-account payments, including procurement of lithium carbonate, are permitted on a real-transaction basis, but each payment must align with the contract, invoices, customs data and the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. Profit remittance from the FIP to foreign partners is permissible only after annual audited financial statements, Chinese tax payments, a valid distribution resolution and a SAFE remittance application; the bank verifies that no retained or excess funds are being diverted. The FIP cannot freely borrow cross-border without a Foreign Debt registration, and any conversion between CNH and CNY occurs across distinct clearing pools. Therefore, the FIP requires disciplined treasury planning to avoid delays in customs reconciliation, tax clearing and capital verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The CN_FIP must repatriate export proceeds into a domestic foreign-exchange account opened with a licensed Tier-1 bank within 90 days after the customs export declaration, and must submit the associated data to the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. No uniform compulsory ratio of conversion is applied under current regulations, but the A/B/C classification controls the retention and settlement framework: A-class enterprises may retain foreign currency and settle to RMB flexibly; B/C-class enterprises must present full trade documents for each receipt and may be forced to settle specific proceeds into RMB through the bank. For battery-grade lithium carbonate arbitrage, the FIP should maintain separate foreign-exchange accounts for goods-trade collection and capital settlement to enable clean reconciliation and AML/TBML surveillance.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the monetary authority and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange regulator; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is recognized as the offshore clearing and CNH market counterparty for the Hong Kong treasury shell, but all onshore FX and capital-account controls for the CN_FIP are administered by PBOC and SAFE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as the primary onshore treasury or capital-account rail. Mainland China does not license independent fintech EMI entities to open or hold SAFE capital settlement accounts, process FDI inflows, perform capital verification, or conduct foreign debt registration on behalf of a foreign-invested partnership. Offshore EMIs licensed in Hong Kong, Singapore or other jurisdictions may be used for the Hong Kong treasury shell's internal expense payments and ancillary working capital only, but they cannot settle the CN_FIP's trade-related customs declarations or capital injection. Any use of an EMI for the mainland China entity would violate PRC payment and FX regulation and expose the FIP to administrative penalties, blocked remittances and unreconciled tax/FX positions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity and debt capital must first complete SAFE foreign investment registration and arrive through the designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed Tier-1 bank. The Hong Kong remitter must send a SWIFT MT103 or bank-to-bank MT202 with the FIP name and a SAFE capital-account purpose code for inward foreign direct investment; the payment cannot be switched to trade purpose. The receiving bank will validate the foreign exchange business registration, partner identity and source of funds; after release, the FIP must obtain a capital verification report from a PRC certified public accountant within the statutory period. Settlement to RMB must be executed by the same bank, and the funds must be applied solely to approved battery-grade lithium carbonate procurement, with no pass-through, no onward investment, no entrusted loans, and no repayment of shareholder loans unless separately registered.All disbursements to lithium-carbonate suppliers should use auditable clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 direct wire transfers for low-value or spot purchases; irrevocable documentary letters of credit for medium-to-large purchase commitments; and documentary collections for established supplier relationships with post-shipment payment terms. Every outward remittance must cite the corresponding import contract, commercial invoice, packing list, customs declaration number and SAFE goods-trade monitoring reference. If paying in RMB to overseas suppliers, the FIP should use CIPS/SWIFT interoperability and maintain separate RCPMIS reporting. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (standard Profits Tax rate for corporations in Hong Kong; for partnerships, profits flow through to partners and are taxed at each partner's applicable rate — 8.25% on first HKD 2 million for corporate partners, 7.5%/15% progressive/standard for individuals)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% — Hong Kong does not impose any dividend withholding tax or distribution tax on the repatriation of profits by a Hong Kong partnership or corporation. Partnership profit distributions to non-resident partners are tax-free in Hong Kong.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Hong Kong entity must prepare a Local File and Master File when it meets all of the following for a year: (a) annual turnover exceeds HKD 100 million; and (b) the aggregate amount of specified intercompany transactions exceeds: (i) HKD 5 million for transfers or use of intellectual property; (ii) HKD 10 million for financial transactions (e.g., loan interest, guarantees); (iii) HKD 30 million for all other controlled transactions (e.g., purchase and sale of goods). A Master File is required if turnover exceeds HKD 200 million (or group revenue exceeds HKD 2 billion) and any of the specialised threshold is crossed. Country-by-Country reporting is triggered for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least HKD 6.8 billion. A partnership engaged in cross-border lithium carbonate procurement must test its intercompany purchase price from its Chinese parent against these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted a distinct Economic Substance Regulation similar to the offshore regime of the Cayman Islands or BVI. Nevertheless, the IRD applies a territorial source principle together with general anti-avoidance rules (IRO s.61, s.61A) to deny tax benefits where an entity lacks genuine economic substance. For a lithium carbonate procurement partnership, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) — such as concluding purchase contracts, negotiating terms with suppliers, holding stock and risk, and providing management oversight — must actually take place in Hong Kong. If the partnership merely passes title on paper while the substantive activities occur in mainland China, the IRD will reattribute the profit to the mainland entity and impose profits tax plus penalties. To mitigate this risk, the FIP should maintain a local office with substantive staff, hold board meetings in Hong Kong at which commercial decisions are made, ensure local directors have actual decision-making authority, and avoid being a pure order-taking or invoicing agent. The presence of trade licences, bank accounts, and a proper accounting function in Hong Kong is persuasive evidence of economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Deliberate Understatement or Offshore Profit Mischaracterisation

Statutory sanction: Section 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) states that a person who wilfully with intent to evade tax makes untrue statements in a return shall be liable to a fine of HKD 50,000 and to imprisonment for 3 years, and to a further fine equal to three times the amount of tax undercharged. Section 80(1) imposes a fine of HKD 10,000 plus treble tax for failure to file a correct return without reasonable excuse. In addition, the IRD may exercise its powers of search, seizure, and audit, and may issue an extended assessment for past seven years (or six years in non-fraud cases) with interest.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under IRO s.82(5): where an offence is committed by a body corporate or partnership, any director, partner, chief executive, or other officer who consented to, connived in, or facilitated the offence is guilty of the same offence and is personally liable for fines and imprisonment. The IRD may also obtain a civil judgment against the directors personally, freeze personal bank accounts, and order restitution of the tax loss from personal assets. In serious cases, the High Court may issue a travel ban preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong until the outstanding tax and penalties are settled. Directors may further be disqualified from holding office under the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Shell Company Substantiation

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.50C, failure to keep or provide transfer pricing documentation upon request is an offence punishable by a fine of up to HKD 100,000 (and a further daily fine of HKD 2,000 for continuing default). Under s.61A, the IRD may adjust the considered tax to an arm’s length amount and impose an additional penalty equal to 100% of the resulting tax deficiency (or up to treble tax in aggravated cases). For shell companies, the IRD can disregard the Hong Kong entity and assess the profits on the actual beneficial owner, applying punitive interest at 12% per annum.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve transfer pricing policies without obtaining suitable benchmarking or appointing qualified advisors may be held personally liable for the tax deficiency if they acted in bad faith or with gross negligence. Under the IRO, any person who knowingly assists in the preparation of a false return is jointly and severally liable for penalties. The IRD may assess the directors for the additional tax as ‘controlling officers’ under s.82(5) and can attach personal assets. Civilly, other partners may bring a derivative action against the directors for breaching fiduciary duties and causing the partnership to suffer penalties. Directors may be restricted from leaving Hong Kong if the unpaid tax exceeds HKD 500,000 and the IRD applies to the District Court for a travel prohibition order.
AML/CFT and Dual-Use Goods Non-Compliance in the Lithium Carbonate Trade

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) constitutes an offence, with a maximum penalty of a fine of HKD 5 million and imprisonment for 14 years. Under the Import and Export (Strategic Commodities) Ordinance (Cap. 60), unauthorised exportation or importation of controlled goods is liable on conviction to a fine of HKD 500,000 and 2 years’ imprisonment; if the goods are intended for hostile weapon use, the penalty may rise to 7 years for violating the United Nations Sanctions Ordinance (Cap. 537). Further, the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department may seize the shipment, forfeit the goods, and freeze all related bank accounts under the Proceeds of Crime Ordinance (Cap. 405).

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are personal offenders if they had actual knowledge or were reckless in shutting their eyes to money laundering. Upon conviction, the individuals face imprisonment and a criminal record; the authorities can also make a restraint or confiscation order against all personal assets, including properties held in trust or through nominees. The court can make a disqualification order under the Companies Ordinance, prohibiting the director from being a director of any Hong Kong company for up to 15 years. The Immigration Department can issue a travel ban and revoke passports.
