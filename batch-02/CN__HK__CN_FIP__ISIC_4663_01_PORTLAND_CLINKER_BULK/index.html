E-government filing portal

Step 1. Step 2. Submit the application through the IRD eTax portal or in person to the Business Registration Office, providing the partnership name, principal place of business in Hong Kong, particulars of all partners, and the Hong Kong registered address. Step 3. Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box regime and has no hybrid mismatch elections. A partnership is transparent by statute under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); partners are separately assessed on their shares of partnership profits and no entity-level taxation election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; only excise duties on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST. A Business Registration Certificate is mandatory from the commencement of business regardless of turnover, and import/export declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) are required for the goods shipment irrespective of value.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax and therefore has no 0% export VAT rate, no export VAT refund scheme, and no export zero-rating claim to substantiate. For Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, the export of Portland clinker may only avoid Hong Kong tax if the profits are in fact derived outside Hong Kong; the source-of-profits test under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), not the export label, determines chargeability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland Chinese renminbi is not fully convertible on the capital account. Current-account transactions, including import/export settlement, are generally convertible once the underlying trade is verified by the SAFE-designated bank (customs declaration, invoice, contract, bill of lading). Capital-account items such as foreign-partner capital contributions, cross-border loans, or repatriation of a partnership liquidation surplus require separate SAFE/PBOC registration or approval. For an FIP registered on the Mainland, the foreign partner's equity injection must enter through a capital escrow account under the FDI code, and downstream use is restricted by the negative list; capital cannot be freely repurposed for domestic securities, unlicensed lending, or real estate speculation. Profit remittance from the FIP to Hong Kong partners is a current-account-like distribution, but it is subject to proof of audited financial statements, annual tax clearance, and the SAFE/CBIRC payment-factoring verification; in practice banks enforce strict documentary checks before releasing a profit or liquidation distribution. The Hong Kong SAR, by contrast, imposes no exchange control, no surrender requirement, and full HKD/USD convertibility within the linked exchange-rate band.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-in-goods administration, a Mainland FIP must repatriate export proceeds to a domestic foreign-exchange current-account bank within 90 days after the date of the customs export declaration (except for trade-financed receivables properly registered with SAFE). Once repatriated, the FIP may either retain the foreign currency or convert it to CNY at the onshore rate; there is no statutory mandatory conversion percentage. The Hong Kong-side treasury channel is exempt from surrender obligations: no conversion ratio, no collection deadline, and no forced repatriation into the Mainland.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the Hong Kong SAR central banking authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for primary treasury use; MEDIUM only as a supplementary HK-side sub-account. A Mainland-regulated FIP cannot legally use an offshore EMI as its SAFE-reportable banking channel. PBOC and SAFE require cross-border trade proceeds and capital injections to flow through a licensed domestic designated foreign exchange bank. Fintech EMIs are not licensed Chinese banks and therefore cannot issue capital verification, apply foreign-exchange purpose codes, participate in CIPS, or support trade-declaration reconciliation. In Hong Kong, a licensed money-service operator can facilitate remittance but cannot offer commercial LC, documentary collection, or full onshore CNY clearing. Accordingly, Tier-1 licensed banks must remain the backbone for all regulated flows; an EMI may only handle unregulated operational funds outside the Mainland compliance perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 with the remitter named as the true foreign partner and the beneficiary as the FIP's capital escrow account at the Tier-1 bank. The wire must state the SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code for FDI capital contribution / partnership capital injection, and include the FIP's Unified Social Credit Code in field 70 or the beneficiary reference. The receiving bank will verify against the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration System, record the inbound capital in the capital account information system, and require the FIP to complete the capital payment declaration. A Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report after receipt and, if the bank requests it, the FIP must demonstrate that the funds will be deployed only for the permitted wholesale distribution business scope. The capital may be retained in foreign currency or settled into CNY through the eligible bank, subject to the negative list restrictions.For operating disbursements to clinker suppliers and freight providers, route USD, EUR, or HKD settlements via SWIFT MT103/202 under a full documentary audit trail. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Partnership Ordinance (Cap 38) and the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), a partnership is not subject to statutory audit under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622); therefore no quantitative turnover, balance sheet, or headcount threshold directly mandates a certified CPA audit for the entity. However, an independent audit becomes compulsory if the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) falls within a regulated activity, namely: (i) it is a licensed firm under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap 571) with client assets or regulated activities triggering annual audit by an incorporated auditor registered with the HKICPA; (ii) it is a money service operator under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615), where annual audited accounts must be submitted irrespective of volume; (iii) it is an insurance or banking institution under the Insurance Ordinance (Cap 41) or Banking Ordinance (Cap 155), in which case asset thresholds and regulatory capital triggers require financial statements audited by an approved auditor. For a purely wholesale commercial FIP selling Portland cement clinker in bulk, none of these mandates are triggered solely by revenue, assets, or employee count. The only quantitative audit references in Hong Kong are the audit exemption thresholds under section 746 of the Companies Ordinance, which apply exclusively to private companies: a private company is no longer exempt from audit if its annual revenue exceeds HK$10,000,000, total assets exceed HK$10,000,000, or its number of employees exceeds 100. Those thresholds are cited solely as the relevant statutory benchmark; a partnership cannot be compelled to audit merely by reaching those figures. A partnership may voluntarily adopt an audited presentation in accordance with its partnership agreement or a financing covenant, in which case the audit must be performed in accordance with the Companies Ordinance audit standards for partnerships, using Hong Kong Standards on Auditing (HKSA) issued by the HKICPA.
