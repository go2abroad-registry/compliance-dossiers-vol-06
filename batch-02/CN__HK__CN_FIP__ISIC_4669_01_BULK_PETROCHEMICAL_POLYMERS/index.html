Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office address or commercial mailbox will generally be accepted by the Hong Kong Companies Registry as a formal registered office address only if a licensed trust or corporate service provider maintains the premises and accepts legal notices. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Market Regulation Administration (SAMR), local municipal or provincial registration service hall, acting through the National Enterprise Registration and Credit Information Disclosure System and the nationwide enterprise registration online platform.

Governing statute

Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (2006 Revised, effective 1 June 2007), Articles 2, 14, 17, 60-64, and 84; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (effective 1 January 2020), Articles 2, 4, 13, 28-31, and 33-35; Measures for the Administration of the Establishment of Partnership Enterprises in China by Foreign Enterprises or Individuals (State Council Order No. 567); and the applicable Special Administrative Measures for Foreign Investment Access (Negative List). The Hong Kong jurisdiction is the source jurisdiction of the foreign investing partner; the Foreign-Invested Partnership itself is formed in the People's Republic of China as a PRC statutory entity.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foreign-Invested Partnership Establishment Registration Application Form submitted together with the executed Partnership Agreement and SAMR's unified registration documents; registration is integrated with the Foreign Investment Information Report.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Partnership Enterprise Law and the Foreign Investment Law impose no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Foreign-Invested Partnership and no blocked escrow account is required. Contributions are governed by the Partnership Agreement; cash contributions from the Hong Kong partner must be paid through a SAFE-designated bank and recorded on the capital contribution schedule; a limited partner's liability is limited to its subscribed but unpaid contribution; in-kind contributions must be valued in accordance with the Partnership Agreement and PRC valuation practice.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for a pure wholesale trading office without warehousing or processing. If the FIP operates a warehouse for polymer pellets, it must obtain a fire safety inspection and fire clearance certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department, pass the structural load review for high-bay racking, file the workplace under the Work Safety Law, provide portable fire extinguishers and fire alarm systems, implement dust explosion prevention measures for polyethylene and polypropylene fines, and maintain an emergency evacuation plan. For offices only, the FIP must comply with ordinary fire safety standards for the leased premises and maintain a valid building fire acceptance record provided by the landlord.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX – Electronic Tax System (https://www.etax.gov.hk). The Business Registration Office of the IRD administers business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate TIN or VAT ID. For a foreign-invested partnership (FIP) established as an ordinary or limited partnership under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) or the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), the statutory taxpayer identity is the Business Registration Number (BRN) obtained from the Business Registration Office. Procedure: (1) Determine whether the partnership carries on business in Hong Kong; a partnership with a Hong Kong place of business is registrable. (2) Submit an application through the eTAX Business Registration e-service or in writing to the Business Registration Office within one month of commencement of business, using the prescribed form for business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance. (3) Attach the partnership agreement, proof of legal existence of each corporate partner in its home jurisdiction, identity documents for individual partners, a Hong Kong registered business address, and details of the person in charge of the partnership. (4) Pay the statutory business registration fee and levy, or apply for a fee/levy exemption only if the business qualifies under the low-turnover exemption in the Business Registration Ordinance. (5) Receive the Business Registration Certificate bearing the BRN; register each additional place of business with a Branch Registration Certificate. (6) The BRN is thereafter used by the IRD for assessment and correspondence. The IRD will issue the partnership annual Profits Tax Returns (Form BIR52) showing assessable partnership profits; non-resident partners are assessed in their own names on their shares of Hong Kong-sourced partnership profits and may be issued Form BIR54 where applicable, but no separate TIN is created for the partnership beyond the BRN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the FIP is fiscally transparent under Hong Kong inland revenue law. The partnership files a return but is not chargeable to profits tax at entity level; each partner, including non-resident partners, is assessed on their share of partnership profits. The territorial-source principle applies, so only Hong Kong-source profits are chargeable under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no transparency election, and no hybrid mismatch election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Partnership pass-through treatment is mandatory. The only structurally relevant option is the low-turnover exemption from business registration fees/levy under the Business Registration Ordinance, which does not alter tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Value-Added Tax (VAT), Goods and Services Tax (GST), or general sales tax in Hong Kong. Narrow indirect duties exist on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and air passenger departure tax under the Air Passenger Departure Tax Ordinance (Cap. 140). Petrochemical polymers in wholesale form are not dutiable commodities.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable: Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST and therefore has no VAT/GST registration threshold. A separate Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is required for a business carrying on trade in Hong Kong, but this is not an indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, exports of petrochemical polymers are outside the scope of indirect taxation. There is no 0% export VAT zero-rating mechanism, no input VAT credit system, and no export VAT refund. Cross-border sales of bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granules therefore do not require any VAT zero-rating substantiation. Trade declarations may still be required for Hong Kong trade statistics.

  • Export/Trade Declaration lodged through the Trade and Industry Department's DTI e-Declaration System under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations made under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical cross-border movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Sales contract or purchase order demonstrating the export transaction and buyer/seller relationship
  • Shipping instruction / freight booking confirmation, where applicable
  • Certificate of Origin only when requested by the buyer or used to claim preferential tariff access; this is optional and not a Hong Kong tax condition

None. Hong Kong does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API-based e-invoicing reporting, or anti-fraud digital stamping for VAT/GST purposes. Business tax returns are filed through eTAX, and export/re-export trade declarations are lodged electronically through the Trade and Industry Department's DTI e-Declaration System under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations made under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60).

There is no VAT zero-rating or VAT clawback because Hong Kong levies no VAT/GST. If export trade declarations are falsified or incomplete, penalties and prosecution may arise under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations and the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60). The IRD may audit the underlying profits tax position; any false or misleading profits tax return, failure to disclose Hong Kong-source partnership profits, or fraudulent documentation can lead to additional tax assessments, financial penalties, and prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). No statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline applies in Hong Kong.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR maintains full current and capital account convertibility for HKD and USD and is free from any exchange surrender requirement, while Mainland China applies the classic current-account-convertible capital-account-controlled framework. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is subject to SAFE supervision for each inbound capital contribution from its Hong Kong partners, with funds routed into a designated foreign-currency capital account and registered or verified after receipt. Current-account trade payments for polymer purchases can be made through licensed banks once authenticity review is satisfied, using invoices, customs declarations, and transport evidence. Capital-account conversion of foreign currency into RMB requires an approved capital account settlement declaration and immediate linkage to an actual business expenditure. Profit and distribution remittances from the FIP to its Hong Kong partners are permissible after audited financial statements, tax returns, and distribution resolutions are filed with the remitting bank, but the bank must also confirm that non-resident partner withholding obligations have been settled. The one-way distinction between onshore CNY and offshore CNH creates a structural basis risk, and treasury must therefore separate Mainland-only capital flows from Hong Kong clearing liquidity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Mainland China legs of the FIP, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-currency account or settled into RMB through an eligible bank. The trade foreign-exchange monitoring system requires collection within 180 days of the export customs declaration unless SAFE has approved overseas retention or a centralised treasury arrangement. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts may be held in foreign currency within permitted account limits or converted voluntarily, provided every settlement is matched to the relevant customs declaration, invoice, cargo documents, and collection records.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for Mainland China foreign-exchange controls, and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and banking supervisor for the Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as the primary banking rail for the Mainland China FIP. Independent digital EMI service providers are not qualified by SAFE to receive foreign-invested partnership capital contributions, to hold the SAFE-registered capital account, or to act as the issuing or confirming bank for irrevocable LCs under the partnership trade book. The Hong Kong parent may use a licensed virtual bank for operational sweep accounts in Hong Kong, but all Mainland China RMB and FDI flows must clear through a domestic Tier-1 bank. Onshore account-opening and FX settlement cannot lawfully be outsourced to an EMI.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is mandated for limited partnerships registered under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) of Hong Kong, irrespective of turnover, total assets, or headcount. However, a compulsory independent audit will apply if: (i) the partnership is a registered institution under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571), authorised under the Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41), or licensed under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155); (ii) the partnership is a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637 (not applicable here); or (iii) the partnership agreement or a binding third-party financing agreement (e.g. bank facility) contractually requires an audit. For reference, a Hong Kong private company is exempt from statutory audit if it meets any two of the following: annual revenue ≤ HK$20,000,000, total assets ≤ HK$10,000,000, and employees ≤ 50, per section 141D of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% corporate profits tax on assessable profits; 15% standard rate for unincorporated business and partnership profits (individual non-corporate partners are taxed at progressive rates, while corporate partners are taxed at 16.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no dividend withholding tax and no tax on dividends distributed by a Hong Kong FIP; distributions to Chinese or other foreign partners are tax-free at source in Hong Kong

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10,000,000 aggregate specified controlled transactions (per taxpayer per year); separate Hong Kong CbC Reporting obligation applies to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750,000,000; Local File and Master File must be prepared within contemporaneous time and updated annually

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a Caribbean-type Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the IRD and the PRC-China tax authorities apply an analogous 'substance over form' doctrine, especially for treaty benefits and offshore profit claims. A Hong Kong FIP trading petrochemical polymers must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: contracts signed and negotiated by local staff, strategic procurement and logistics decisions made in Hong Kong, senior management meetings held in Hong Kong, financial and hedging risks borne in Hong Kong, and a leased physical office. Where CIGA is found to be in Mainland China, the IRD and China SAT may re-source profits to China and deny treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – pure shell offshore profit claim

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61 gives the Inland Revenue Department power to disregard artificial transactions and reassess profit at 16.5%; s.61A further implies a 16.5% tax on the entire arrangement; s.80(1) imposes a fine of HKD 10,000; s.82A permits addition of tax up to 100% of the undercharged tax for wilful evasion; where China SAT asserts jurisdiction under PRC Entity Establishment rules, unpaid CIT plus surcharges may reach 50%-500% of unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced on fraud grounds; general partners and Chinese-appointed directors face joint and several civil liability for the partnership's unpaid supply-chain debts and tax arrears. Directors may be placed on the IRD's enforcement blacklist, imposing travel bans and no re-entry permits, and face imprisonment up to 3 years under IRO s.82 with another 3-year term under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 (tax evasion), which can be executed through mutual legal assistance.
Transfer Pricing – non-arm's length polymer margins

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.50AAA the Commissioner adjusts the taxable profits to arm's length; failure to keep contemporaneous documents exposes the taxpayer to default assessments and penalties under IRO s.80(2); China-side counterparty may be adjusted under PRC Special Tax Investigation Regulations, with overdue tax interest at LIBOR+5% and penalties capped at 2 times.

Director personal liability: Directors or managing partners who sign and certify TP declarations are jointly and severally liable to restore diverted profits to the partnership under their fiduciary duties. A court may order asset freezing orders and publish the director on the Hong Kong and PRC joint tax non-cooperative list, which prevents flight and commercial re-entry; on indictment, directors face imprisonment for up to 3 years for fraudulent misrepresentation and up to 10 years for conspiracy to defraud revenue authorities where customs or LC fraud is involved.
AML/DTA Abuse – beneficial ownership sham

Statutory sanction: China tax authority denies the 5% beneficial ownership relief and reassesses the full 10% statutory dividend WHT plus late-payment surcharges (0.05% per day) and administrative fines of 50%-500% of unpaid tax under PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63; HK IRD may apply IRO s.61A to recharacterize dividend flow as cross-border service income.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shammed HK partner face personal liability for the unpaid Chinese tax, restitution of treaty benefits with compound interest, and blacklisting on China's tax integrity 'clearly dishonest taxpayer' ledger, which imposes travel bans and credit downgrades; on criminal referral, offshore directors can be charged with tax evasion conspiracy under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years, as well as HK independent money laundering offences under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance with asset confiscation and up to 14 years' incarceration.
