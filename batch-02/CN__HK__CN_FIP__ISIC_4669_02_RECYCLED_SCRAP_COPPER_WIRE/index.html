Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement on a foreign-invested limited partnership. Partners are free to agree the amount, timing, and currency of capital subscriptions in the partnership agreement; no blocked or escrow capital account is mandated by law.

Funding & unlock path: The Partnership must obtain, before operations, a Fire Services Certificate (FSD) attesting compliance with the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance, required for any storage premises. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (SAT) via the New Electronic Tax Bureau portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, together with the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the eTax portal at www.ird.gov.hk, because the foreign-invested partnership is formed under PRC law and uses Hong Kong as the offshore transshipment and documentary collection jurisdiction.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Apply to the local Administration for Market Regulation for a Foreign-Invested Partnership business licence under the Foreign Investment Law; the issued 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code is automatically the Chinese Taxpayer Identification Number. Step 2: Complete tax registration through the integrated one-stop registration window; the SAT branch automatically creates the taxpayer profile, VAT registration status, and tax filing obligations. Step 3: Apply for general VAT taxpayer status if expected annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000; otherwise the partnership may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Step 4: Register with the General Administration of Customs to obtain the ten-digit Customs Registration Code and enable customs export declarations. Step 5: Open a basic deposit account and a foreign exchange settlement account in a designated bank, and complete the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-in-goods registration for export proceeds collection. Step 6: If the partnership also carries on business in Hong Kong, apply to the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement under the Business Registration Ordinance; no separate VAT ID is required in Hong Kong because Hong Kong does not levy VAT. Step 7: Preserve the Hong Kong IRD partnership file number and Business Registration Number for tax returns, offshore-profit claims, and documentary LC or Swift TT payment processing.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer at the entity level; each partner is taxed on the partnership's allocable profits. For Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, the partnership is assessed in the partnership name under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but partners are charged on their respective shares; only Hong Kong-source profits are chargeable. Cross-border scrap trading profits may be treated as wholly offshore and outside the Hong Kong tax net where negotiating, contracting, purchase, resale, and execution activities are not performed in Hong Kong, subject to IRD scrutiny and offshore-claim evidence.

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a check-the-box election; the pass-through classification of a partnership is mandatory under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and Enterprise Income Tax rules. Hong Kong also has no check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election. Partners may only apply for treaty or double-taxation arrangement benefits through the SAT non-resident tax treatment procedure, with no unilateral fiscal-transparency election available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In Mainland China, VAT registration is mandatory from the date of business licence issuance with no turnover exemption; the threshold for compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration is CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales. The Foreign-Invested Partnership may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer below that threshold. Hong Kong SAR has no indirect tax registration threshold and imposes no VAT or GST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for 0% VAT only where: (1) the recycled scrap copper wire is physically exported from Mainland China customs territory to Hong Kong SAR or another destination; (2) the Foreign-Invested Partnership has obtained a China Customs Export Goods Declaration in its own name; (3) the sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and customs declaration match in commodity description, quantity, and value; (4) the sale is a genuine cross-border trade with an overseas buyer; (5) export proceeds are received through the designated foreign exchange account and reported to SAFE; and (6) the goods and exporter are not listed as prohibited or restricted for export tax refund by SAT and General Administration of Customs joint measures. The exporter must apply for the export tax rebate under the Chinese export exemption and refund procedures; the statutory licence required for waste metal export and any environmental or trade controls must also be held.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (customs copy and export-tax-rebate copy)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Sea/Air Waybill issued to the Foreign-Invested Partnership as shipper
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract matching the customs declared value and HS classification
  • Special VAT invoice for the purchased scrap copper used to substantiate the input VAT claimed in the export refund calculation
  • Bank-issued Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt or bank foreign exchange income declaration evidencing actual receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer, being the Chinese statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate
  • SAFE trade-in-goods foreign exchange verification record or export proceeds monitoring report confirming timely repatriation

Mainland China operates the VAT Invoice Management System under Golden Tax Phase IV. VAT invoices must be issued from tax-controlled equipment, uploaded to the SAT portal in near real time, and bear the statutory digital verification code and electronic signature of the taxpayer. Fully digitized e-invoices are being phased in nationally and are accepted as fiscal evidence for VAT and export refund purposes. The SAT's centralized clearance system validates invoices electronically without third-party physical stamping. Hong Kong SAR has no mandatory electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime.

Under the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, claiming or retaining export zero-rating without actual export, with false customs declarations, or without the required customs and foreign exchange documentation triggers SAT and Customs audit review, retrospective clawback of the VAT rebate plus interest, and penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded or one to five times the amount of fraudulently obtained export tax refund; criminal liability may also be pursued for export-refund fraud. The exporter may be downgraded under the export tax refund classified management system, resulting in mandatory document scrutiny for every future shipment, temporary suspension of export refund rights, and multi-year retrospective examination of prior export declarations. In Hong Kong, an incorrect offshore-profit claim or non-disclosure may lead to additional assessments, fines, and penalties up to treble the tax evaded under the Inland Revenue Ordnance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

For a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership trading recycled scrap copper wire, the onshore regime remains partially convertible. Current account items are operationally convertible: trade receipts, service payments, wages and profit remittances may be processed through designated banks with genuine supporting vouchers, without individual SAFE approval. The capital account remains controlled; foreign-direct-investment capital contributions require SAFE registration, a dedicated capital settlement account, and use of funds within the negative list. Export proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days after customs declaration; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, but the business must surrender the export verification data and may keep the foreign exchange in a current-account FCY account. Profit remittance to the HK partner is conditioned on audited financial statements, paid or settled tax, and a SAFE reconciliation; improperly documented distributions are classified as capital-account outflows and may be blocked. Treasury planning must therefore separate incoming equity capital, trade cash flow, and distributable profit, and maintain complete vouchers for every remittance. The FIP should treat SAFE registration and voucher archiving as continuous operational controls rather than one-time compliance events.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Within 180 days after each export customs declaration, 100% of export proceeds must be repatriated to a SAFE-designated trade collection account in China. No mandatory conversion ratio currently applies (0% compulsory sale); the FIP may retain the foreign currency or voluntarily settle to RMB at the designated bank's spot rate, and must submit export verification/surrender data to SAFE through the bank.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign exchange authority, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) governs the HK clearing bridge

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating: Low / legally barred as primary clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs hold no PBOC banking license and no SAFE foreign-exchange licence; they cannot open a SAFE-registered capital account, cannot process export verification, cannot issue commercial letters of credit, and cannot lawfully handle China-source trade proceeds for the onshore FIP. EMIs may only serve ancillary operational cash needs of the HK parent, never the PRC entity's regulated treasury flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent remits through SWIFT MT103 to the PRC FIP's FDI Capital Settlement Account at a designated mainland tier-1 bank. The payment must include the SAFE foreign-investment registration file number, the purpose code category for direct investment paid-in capital, the underlying partnership agreement reference, and the FIP's full legal name. Currency can be USD or CNY; USD receipts are converted to RMB only after the bank checks the SAFE negative list and records the capital settlement. The bank completes receipt verification and, where required, a Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report; the FIP then updates SAFE registration within the statutory reporting window. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: For a Hong Kong registered partnership, no mandatory audit is imposed by the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). A mandatory independent CPA audit arises under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) where the partnership has a corporate general partner that is a Hong Kong company, and that company ceases to qualify for audit dispensation under section 395. The statutory exemption threshold is a private company satisfying at least two of the three tests: (1) total annual revenue does not exceed HK$10,000,000; (2) total assets do not exceed HK$10,000,000; and (3) average number of employees does not exceed 50. If fewer than two of those tests are satisfied, an independent certified audit is legally mandatory. In addition, the Inland Revenue Department by practice requires audited financial statements to be filed with the BIR52 partnership return where gross annual trading income exceeds HK$5,000,000, where a financial institution requires certified accounts under a loan or documentary credit facility, or where cross-border tax positions or an investor's home jurisdiction mandate an audit.
