Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37, Laws of Hong Kong), sections 4 (registration and registry), 5 (certificate of registration), 6 (registered office), 7 (name and general restrictions), 8 (limited partners and capital contributions), 9 (liability of limited partners), 10 (general partners), 11 (variation in partnership) and 12 (register of limited partnerships); Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) for default partnership rules; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), section 5(1) and Business Registration Regulations; where the general partner is a body corporate, Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a limited partnership. The partners must state the agreed capital contribution of each limited partner in the statement of contributions filed with Form LP1. A limited partner who withdraws or receives back any part of their contribution while the partnership cannot pay its debts may become liable for partnership obligations under Cap. 37 sections 8 and 9.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department clearance in the form of a Fire Service Installations Certificate and, where applicable, a Dangerous Goods Licence under Cap. 295; fire hydrant, extinguisher, emergency exit and building access approvals; Buildings Department or authorized structural engineer clearance for floor loading and racking stability; Labour Department approval through Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) compliance, including a written risk assessment, first-aid facilities, personal protective equipment, chemical safety data sheets, training, and accident reporting procedures.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a limited partnership. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the E-Tax Office portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. The Hong Kong SAR has a separate tax administration under the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), which remains relevant only for the separate territorial tax position of the Hong Kong partner and does not administer the PRC FIP's VAT or export tax refunds.

E-government filing portal

For a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the statutory taxpayer identifier is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCI) contained in the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The FIP must: (1) complete SAMR formation in the intended mainland China location; (2) rely on the automatic multi-license tax registration exchange with the STA to open the enterprise tax file without a separate VAT registration certificate; (3) when the FIP has non-resident partners, the foreign non-resident entity does not obtain a separate TIN solely as a partner, but the FIP as withholding agent must execute withholding registration with the competent STA office on the first distribution or payment of PRC-source income, at which time the foreign partner is assigned a non-resident tax reference number; (4) if the FIP intends to export, register in the customs registration system through the China International Trade Single Window and complete bank foreign-exchange receipt/settlement account registration using the USCI; and (5) complete VAT taxpayer status determination (general or small-scale) and connect to the STA-approved VAT invoice issuing system.

The FIP must: (1) complete SAMR formation in the intended mainland China location; (2) rely on the automatic multi-license tax registration exchange with the STA to open the enterprise tax file without a separate VAT registration certificate; (3) when the FIP has non-resident partners, the foreign non-resident entity does not obtain a separate TIN solely as a partner, but the FIP as withholding agent must execute withholding registration with the competent STA office on the first distribution or payment of PRC-source income, at which time the foreign partner is assigned a non-resident tax reference number; (4) if the FIP intends to export, register in the customs registration system through the China International Trade Single Window and complete bank foreign-exchange receipt/settlement account registration using the USCI; and (5) complete VAT taxpayer status determination (general or small-scale) and connect to the STA-approved VAT invoice issuing system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Under PRC partnership and income tax law, the partnership is fiscally transparent for corporate income tax and individual income tax purposes on a mandatory basis. No U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available for a PRC FIP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for VAT general taxpayer status; all PRC business entities, including FIPs, must complete tax registration from inception regardless of turnover, and below the threshold the entity may remain as a small-scale VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC general VAT taxpayer exporting physical goods, including prilled urea and potassium chloride, is eligible for the 0% VAT export rate only when the sale is made to an overseas purchaser, the goods are declared to PRC Customs and physically depart the PRC customs territory, the commercial terms are genuine and on arm's length pricing, the export proceeds are received from the overseas buyer and settled through a PRC designated bank, and the FIP files the export declaration and export tax rebate/zero-rating records with the STA within the statutory deadline. Qualifying offshore digital services are zero-rated only where supplied to non-resident customers and consumed entirely outside mainland China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export goods declaration) issued through the PRC International Trade Single Window with final customs release status
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, multimodal bill of lading, or air waybill evidencing physical export from PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and purchase/sale contract for the exported goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt/settlement confirmation showing collection of export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Export tax rebate declaration data set and all electronic transmission logs submitted to the State Taxation Administration

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved invoice control and electronic invoicing systems. In the fully digitalised e-invoice regime, the FIP must issue all-electronic VAT invoices through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform; each invoice is checked against the taxpayer identity, product or service tax classification code, VAT rate, and zero-rating/export marker and is cleared with a digital fiscal mark in near real time. Enterprise resource planning and third-party billing interfaces must meet the STA interface/API specifications for data transmission, invoice number allocation, and anti-fraud validation. The local STA automatically risk-screens repeated issuance, threshold breaches, and mismatches between invoices and customs export declarations.

If the supporting documentation is missing, unverifiable, inconsistent with customs or foreign-exchange data, or otherwise insufficient, the STA will disallow the 0% export rate, reclassify the transaction as a domestic taxable supply, assess VAT at the applicable domestic rate, impose late-payment surcharge, and claw back any previously refunded input VAT. Where false documentation is intentionally used, the penalty under the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection ranges from 50 percent to five times the tax underpaid or refunded, and criminal liability under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law can extend to life imprisonment plus confiscation of property for fraudulent export tax refunds. The recovery period for fraudulent or wilful evasion is unlimited, and the STA may also revoke the FIP's export tax refund qualification.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime under the authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Foreign-invested partnerships (FIPs) may open RMB and foreign currency accounts, but must comply with current account convertibility rules that require underlying trade documentation for cross-border payments. Capital account transactions, including injections of equity or shareholder loans, remain subject to registration, approval, and authenticity checks. Profit remittances for FIPs generally require a tax clearance certificate (e.g., Certificate of Taxpayer Registration with tax payment proof), audited financial statements, and a board resolution on dividend distribution. The PBOC and SAFE closely monitor large-value transactions and suspicious flows. While Capital Account Convertibility has been progressively liberalized, full unrestricted convertibility is not yet available. In practice, FIPs must annually perform cross-border declarations via the Foreign Exchange Administrative Information System (ASOne) and obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate. For export proceeds, current rules mandate that exporters repatriate foreign exchange to China within a statutory timeframe (typically 180 days from the date of goods export) and may either retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert them at negotiated rates. There is no mandatory surrender ratio, but all export earnings must be recorded and reconciled with customs declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 13 (2019) and relevant decrees, all export receipts from goods must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs clearance unless otherwise approved. There is no mandatory conversion quota; exporters may hold the foreign currency in a domestic foreign exchange account or sell it to designated banks at market rates. However, the exporter must file the export collection declaration (涉外收入申报) and reconcile with customs and logistics records. Failure to repatriate may result in penalties and administrative sanctions.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or specialist payment aggregators have no legal standing to provide onboarding or FX services directly to an onshore Chinese foreign-invested partnership. The People's Bank of China and SAFE require any organization handling cross-border RMB or foreign currency payments for domestic entities to hold a national payment business license (支付业务许可证) and a cross-border foreign exchange payment license. None of the major offshore EMIs hold such licenses. Hence, the FIP must transact exclusively through licensed domestic commercial banks or their designated onshore branches. Offshore EMIs can be used only in the off-shore Hong Kong vehicle if a separate HK entity is established, but even then the onshore FIP cannot route funds through such EMIs without non-compliant direct participation.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Beneficial ownership opacity – FIP structure may involve multiple layers of partners or silent investors, creating difficulty in identifying ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs).

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity – FIP structure may involve multiple layers of partners or silent investors, creating difficulty in identifying ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs).

Defense evidence: Submit a certified organizational chart illustrating all partners with share or profit ratios exceeding 10%, passport copies of every partner, an UBO declaration per PRC AMLA regulations, audited financial statements of the partnership, source-of-funds evidence such as bank statements and tax returns, and a comprehensive corporate registry extract from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR).
Trade-based money laundering risk – Commodity trading in urea and potash can be exploited through invoice manipulation, over/under pricing, or phantom shipping.

Root cause: Trade-based money laundering risk – Commodity trading in urea and potash can be exploited through invoice manipulation, over/under pricing, or phantom shipping.

Defense evidence: Provide signed purchase and sale contracts, proforma invoices, original bills of lading, customs declarations (进出口货物报关单), independent SGS or equivalent surveyor certificates, warehouse storage receipts, port weight certificates, and documented payment flows aligned with each shipment. A real-time reconciliation of trade documents against shipping timelines and freight charges should also be included.
Sanctions and restrictive measures exposure – Fertilizer raw materials, particularly potash and urea, may originate from or transit through jurisdictions subject to international sanctions (e.g., Russia, Belarus, Iran).

Root cause: Sanctions and restrictive measures exposure – Fertilizer raw materials, particularly potash and urea, may originate from or transit through jurisdictions subject to international sanctions (e.g., Russia, Belarus, Iran).

Defense evidence: Deliver enhanced due diligence (EDD) reports, sanctions screening outputs from recognized databases (OFAC, UN, EU, HM Treasury, and China's own sanctions list), end-user certificates and end-use declarations confirming the products are solely for agricultural application, and contract clauses stating absence of sanctioned parties in the supply chain. A legal opinion from a reputable PRC or HK law firm on sanctions compliance should accompany the dossier.
Unverified source of capital injection – The initial or incremental capital contribution to the FIP may derive from offshore entities lacking economic substance, raising money laundering concerns.

Root cause: Unverified source of capital injection – The initial or incremental capital contribution to the FIP may derive from offshore entities lacking economic substance, raising money laundering concerns.

Defense evidence: Supply offshore partnership formation documents, an equity resolution or capital injection circular, audited financial statements of the investing entity, source-of-funds tracing via SWIFT MT103/MT202 records, tax residence certificates from the offshore jurisdiction, and an anti-money laundering certification issued by the home regulator. The bank may also require the investment vehicle's bank statements for the prior six months to prove no layered transaction chains.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions into the FIP must be routed through the national Foreign Exchange Administrative Information System (ASOne) after obtaining the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate from SAFE. Incoming cross-border wires must use the standard purpose code 622 (Investment in/by Enterprise) or 605 (Foreign Direct Investment Contributions). Inbound remittances should be denominated in RMB, USD, EUR, or HKD and credited to the designated capital escrow account (资本金账户). Upon receipt, the FIP must hire a licensed PRC accounting firm to perform capital verification (验资) and issue a verification report, which the bank uses to convert the funds into operational working capital or to apply for foreign exchange settlement under the actual-use principle (实需原则). All such settlements must be subject to the Positive List for Capital Account receipts and cannot be used for speculative investments.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Wise, Revolut, or specialist payment aggregators have no legal standing to provide onboarding or FX services directly to an onshore Chinese foreign-invested partnership. None of the major offshore EMIs hold such licenses. Hence, the FIP must transact exclusively through licensed domestic commercial banks or their designated onshore branches. Offshore EMIs can be used only in the off-shore Hong Kong vehicle if a separate HK entity is established, but even then the onshore FIP cannot route funds through such EMIs without non-compliant direct participation.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% for corporate partners; 15% for individual partners (Hong Kong profits tax on unincorporated partnerships is charged at partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions to non-residents. Accordingly, distribution of the FIP’s after-tax profits to a mainland Chinese parent or partner is free of Hong Kong withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong does not prescribe a statutory de minimis threshold in the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (DIPN 59 and 60) require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions. In practice, a safe harbour applies where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with associated enterprises does not exceed HK$10 million in a year. An MNE group with consolidated group revenue of at least HK$6.8 billion is required to prepare and maintain a Master File and a Local File.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a general Economic Substance Regulation similar to BVI/Cayman, but the territorial source principle and the 'main purpose' anti-abuse rule under the DTA effectively require substantive economic activities. The partnership must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for fertiliser trades - such as negotiating and executing sale and purchase contracts, shipping logistics, and risk management - are performed in and from Hong Kong. If the FIP merely books invoices while the real operational decisions are made in mainland China or elsewhere, the IRD may recharacterise the structure under IRO s.61 / s.61A and deem the profits taxable in Hong Kong or deny treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Contravention of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82 (criminal tax evasion) and s.80 (failure to provide returns). On summary conviction, a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus a further penalty of treble (3 times) the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD also has power to impose higher penalties for multiple counts and to publicly name the offender.

Director personal liability: Under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) and the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), the general partner (and any corporate partner’s directors) who knowingly participates in falsifying the return is personally liable for the tax undercharged plus penalties. Civil asset restitution orders may freeze the personal bank accounts of the partner/director; travel bans (blacklisting) are issued by the IRD for outstanding tax liabilities; criminal conviction for tax fraud can result in imprisonment and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.61 and s.61A, the IRD can ignore the artificial arrangement and assess the true economic profits in Hong Kong, with penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided. Additionally, the Limited Partnerships Ordinance requires accurate registration and continuous filing of changes; providing false particulars may result in deregistration and a fine of up to HK$100,000. The IRD may also refuse the partnership’s claim for Hong Kong tax residence for DTA relief.

Director personal liability: The courts may pierce the partnership veil to hold the controlling director of the corporate general partner personally responsible for the tax liabilities and penalties. If the shell arrangement defrauds creditors or tax authorities, the director may face civil claims for fraudulent trading, disqualification as a director, and a travel restriction order prohibiting departure from Hong Kong. Criminal liability arises if the shell is used to enable tax evasion by third parties, leading to imprisonment of up to 3 years under IRO s.82.
AML / Trade-Based Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Under the Organised and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25, dealing with property known or reasonably believed to represent proceeds of an indictable offence is punishable by a fine of HK$5 million and imprisonment for 14 years. Failure to comply with AMLO (Cap. 615) customer due diligence and record-keeping requirements is a separate offence punishable by a fine of HK$1 million and imprisonment for 2 years on conviction. The Financial Services and Treasury Bureau may also impose asset freezing orders and revoke any relevant licences.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, and compliance officers who consent to or connive in money laundering are criminally liable in their personal capacity. They may face up to 14 years in prison and an unlimited fine. The court can issue a travel bar denying exit from Hong Kong pending trial, freeze personal assets derived from the illegal activity, and order the confiscation of all proceeds under the Confiscation of Proceeds of Crime Ordinance. Extradition proceedings may also be initiated by foreign authorities, and a criminal record results in automatic disqualification from corporate directorship.
