None. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and enquiry portal: eTax (etax.gov.hk) and GovHK (www.gov.hk); business registration is also integrated with the Companies Registry for corporate entities, but the IRD is the competent tax authority for profits tax, salaries tax, and property tax.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Appoint a Hong Kong registered office or local tax representative if the foreign non-resident entity has a place of business in Hong Kong. Step 2: Apply for business registration with the IRD using the approved Business Registration application form (IRBR 1) for partnerships; this must be done within one month of commencement of business. Step 3: Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) containing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the statutory tax identifier for the partnership. Step 4: For tax filing purposes, the partnership itself is not separately assessed; the partners are assessed individually on their share of partnership profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Each non-resident partner with assessable profits must file a Profits Tax Return and may need to appoint an authorized representative under section 79 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Step 5: For bank account opening and trade finance transactions, present the certified BRC, partnership agreement, and identification documents of the partners. No separate VAT ID is issued because Hong Kong imposes no value-added tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax is levied in Hong Kong SAR. Limited indirect duties apply to alcoholic beverages, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109); no such impositions apply to sulfur mineral ore trading.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST; no indirect tax registration threshold exists. For Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), any person carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencing business, irrespective of turnover or profit.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST export zero-rating regime. For dutiable goods, a zero-rate of duty may apply on export only if the goods are exported under customs supervision and in compliance with the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109). For non-dutiable commodities such as sulfur mineral ore, no export VAT or duty is levied, and no VAT refund mechanism exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed electronically via the Trade Single Window / DTI
  • Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent ocean transport document
  • Dutiable Commodities Export License or permit (only if the goods are within the scope of Cap. 109; not applicable for sulfur ore)
  • Letter of Credit / SWIFT MT103 confirmation for trade finance and bank repatriation audit trail

No real-time electronic fiscal clearance, API reporting, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies. The IRD operates eTAX for electronic submission of tax returns and correspondence. For customs trade declarations, the Customs and Excise Department requires electronic submission through the Trade Single Window / DTI (Digital Trade and Transportation Information System) for export declarations. Paper invoicing remains permissible for commercial transactions, but full audit trail documentation must be maintained.

No VAT zero-rating clawback mechanism exists in Hong Kong. For customs matters, false or misleading trade declarations are subject to statutory enforcement by the Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) and the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), including financial penalties, forfeiture of goods, and criminal prosecution. Profits tax non-compliance remains subject to IRD penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including potential additional tax up to 100% or 200% of under-assessed tax, depending on the nature of the wilful default, plus fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under an open capital account and full convertibility for HKD, USD, CNH, and other major currencies. There is no statutory differentiation between current-account and capital-account convertibility in Hong Kong, no surrender requirement for export proceeds, and no restriction on profit remittance or capital outflow at the HK level. However, the sovereign China dimension introduces a second control layer: the PRC maintains current-account convertibility for genuine trade and services payments but requires banks to verify contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax documents. Capital-account items, including inbound capital injections into a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership or outbound equity investment from China, require SAFE registration, filing, or approval before remittance. Profit remittance from a PRC operating entity to the Hong Kong FIP is treated as a current-account dividend/profit outflow and will require audited PRC financial statements, a profit appropriation resolution, tax payment certificates, and a single tax settlement certificate. Export proceeds from PRC exporters must still be repatriated into domestic designated foreign-exchange accounts, but SAFE no longer imposes a mandatory conversion ratio. The result is a dual-regime treasury structure: Hong Kong provides immediate offshore liquidity agility and free repatriation, while any crossing of the mainland border is permissioned, documented, and audit-trailed through a licensed bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong statutory framework: nil. Export proceeds earned by the Hong Kong FIP from sulfur ore brokerage or freight forwarding are fully retainable, convertible, and remittable without surrender. For PRC-affiliated exporters, SAFE requires export proceeds to be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign-exchange account within the prescribed period, generally 90 days from the export customs declaration date or the contractual payment due date, with deferred receipt registration available for approved longer tenors. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero under the current no-surrender regime, but any voluntary conversion to RMB must be supported by valid customs declarations, commercial invoices, contracts, and balance-of-payments transaction codes.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for PRC-linked foreign exchange and capital controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury bank; conditionally acceptable as an ancillary collection rail only. Under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance, only an authorized institution licensed by the HKMA can take deposits, operate corporate current accounts, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide capital verification custody. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank fintech payment firms, even when registered as Money Service Operators, are legally barred from offering deposit-taking, trade finance, or segregated client escrow account services in the manner required for dry bulk sulfur ore settlements. A licensed Hong Kong virtual bank could serve as a secondary operating account, but it is not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank when the transaction requires SWIFT MT103, commercial LC, documentary collections, CIPS connectivity, and a stable capital escrow account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

PRC partner injections into the Hong Kong FIP must be routed through a licensed PRC bank after all required SAFE foreign-exchange registration and, if applicable, NDRC/MOFCOM overseas investment approval. The remittance should identify the correct SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code, e.g., 621101 for outward direct investment equity/partnership capital injection or 101010 for trade prepayment if the capital is pre-funding goods trade. The HK receiving bank should receive SWIFT MT103 with field 70 referencing the SAFE registration number and field 72 stating the purpose code; the funds should be segregated in a capital escrow account and held until a HK certified public accountant issues a capital verification report and the partnership register is updated. For RMB-denominated injections, the transfer should use the RMB CHATS/CIPS clearing rails with a licensed CNH clearing bank, and the receiving account should be a properly designated CNH settlement account to preserve the offshore RMB classification.Outbound payments for sulfur ore cargo, marine transport, freight, demurrage, and brokerage settlement should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for direct documented payments and MT202 for interbank reimbursements. For documentary trade, the FIP should use irrevocable commercial letters of credit under UCP600, either sight or usance, with the full set of shipping documents including commercial invoice, clean on-board ocean bill of lading, insurance certificate, weight certificate, and inspection certificate. Documentary collections under URC522 (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for established counterparties with stable payment history. HKD and USD payments should clear through CHATS; CNH payments through the HKMA RMB RTGS system and CIPS; EUR payments through HK Euro RTGS. Every outward transaction must carry the related contract/LC reference, the underlying SAFE purpose code where visible, and a clear audit trail reconciling cargo documents to payment instructions.Deliverable FX forwards (USD/CNH, USD/HKD, EUR/USD, and USD/CNY where onshore conversion is permitted) limited to hedging documented trade receivables or payables, with tenors aligned to the underlying contract and no naked speculative positions.CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) executed with HKMA-licensed authorized institutions, with notional capped at the projected net export/import exposure of the FIP and requiring refreshed trade documentation before rollover or extension.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between CNY and USD/HKD limited to the tenor and notional of an underlying commercial LC, partner capital injection, or trade finance facility; onshore participation requires a SAFE-approved designated bank and one-time registration/filing, and the swap must be fully subordinated to a real treasury risk event.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury bank; conditionally acceptable as an ancillary collection rail only. Under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance, only an authorized institution licensed by the HKMA can take deposits, operate corporate current accounts, issue documentary letters of credit, or provide capital verification custody. Offshore digital EMIs and non-bank fintech payment firms, even when registered as Money Service Operators, are legally barred from offering deposit-taking, trade finance, or segregated client escrow account services in the manner required for dry bulk sulfur ore settlements. A licensed Hong Kong virtual bank could serve as a secondary operating account, but it is not a substitute for a Tier-1 bank when the transaction requires SWIFT MT103, commercial LC, documentary collections, CIPS connectivity, and a stable capital escrow account.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for unincorporated businesses (partnerships); 16.5% for corporate partners. Under the Two-Tiered Profits Tax Rate regime, each partner's first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits is taxed at 7.5% (unincorporated) or 8.25% (corporation); the excess is taxed at 15% or 16.5% respectively. The FIP itself is pass-through and is not a separate taxpayer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Hong Kong imposes no withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions. Distributions by the FIP to its non-resident partners are tax-free in Hong Kong and no notification to the IRD is required.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD. A Master File and Local File are mandatory where the HK taxpayer's annual turnover exceeds HKD 200,000,000 and controlled transactions with related parties exceed: HKD 44,000,000 (transfers of goods, including sulfur/mineral ore commodity purchases); HKD 22,000,000 (transfers of intangibles or financial assets); HKD 11,000,000 (provision of services, financing, guarantees and other transactions). Country-by-Country Report threshold: group consolidated revenue of at least HKD 6,800,000,000. Documentation must be prepared contemporaneously and furnished within 21 days of IRD request (IRO s.50AOA, DIPN 59/60).

Economic substance scope: Applicable in layered form: (1) Territorial source doctrine — offshore trading profits are exempt; the IRD applies the 'Lion & Globe' source test (negotiation, conclusion and performance of contracts outside HK); the FIP must evidence offshore decision-making for each voyage-lot transaction. (2) FSIE economic substance requirements (Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2022, effective 1 January 2023) — passive income (interest, dividends, disposal gains, IP royalties) received in HK is deemed taxable absent adequate economic substance in HK (sufficient employees, premises and local expenditure for core income-generating activities) or qualifying participant exemptions. The sulfur trading profit is active business income, but any incidental interest or fee income creates exposure. (3) No separate codified ESR law exists in HK (unlike BVI/UAE); however, IRD anti-avoidance provisions (IRO s.61 and s.61A) and the six-year/ten-year assessment windows operate to challenge shell FIPs lacking substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — False Returns and Willful Omission

Statutory sanction: Fine up to HKD 50,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged and imprisonment up to 3 years (IRO s.82); summary conviction: fine up to HKD 10,000/level 2 plus treble tax; the IRD also discounts penalties for voluntary disclosure, but fraud cases are referred for prosecution.

Director personal liability: The precedent partner who signs the return is criminally liable; all general partners of the FIP hold unlimited personal liability under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38); corporate directors of a corporate partner may be jointly liable under the aiding-and-abetting provisions; a court may issue a departure prohibition order via IRD recovery actions to prevent absconding during prosecution.
Shell Arrangement / Abusive Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: China tax authority denies treaty benefits and applies the full 10% WHT plus late payment surcharge; HK IRD may reopen assessments within 10 years of fraud or 6 years of neglect, re-source the profits to HK, assess additional tax up to 100% of the tax undercharged, and impose treble penalties (IRO s.61A, s.82); the FSIE regime additionally deems the income taxable unless substance is proven.

Director personal liability: The IRD may pierce the FIP structure and assess the general partner or the controlling PRC parent as the de facto taxpayer; officers of the corporate general partner face civil restitution proceedings; personal asset tracing and bankruptcy/winding-up petitions may follow; principals may face China-side exit bans (出境限制) under PRC tax collection law if the PRC FIP is assessed on the same profits.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's Length Commodity Pricing

Statutory sanction: Penalty of up to 10% of the amount of under-assessed tax for failure to prepare adequate contemporaneous documentation (IRO s.50AOA); transfer pricing adjustments with interest; treble tax penalty if the IRD establishes willful or fraudulent underpricing; denial of the corresponding adjustment and foreign tax credit.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate partner and the general partner are jointly and severally liable for the FIP's tax debts up to the amount of distributions received; officers who executed non-arm's length pricing agreements may face personal assessments under the 'responsible person' provisions (IRO s.57(3)); cross-border egregious cases can trigger PRC Special Tax Adjustment investigations against the Chinese counterparty.
