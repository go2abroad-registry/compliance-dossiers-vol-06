Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong partnership law imposes no minimum paid-up capital, no capital verification certificate, and no blocked escrow account requirement. Partners are free to agree capital contributions, profit shares and loss shares in the partnership agreement, and funds may be held in ordinary operating accounts once the partnership is registered.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with electronic filing via the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; Hong Kong SAR activities are separately supervised by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through its Business Registration Office and eTax portal at https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/ese/.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC foreign-invested partnership (FIP), the process begins with registration under the Foreign Investment Law and the Partnership Enterprise Law with the local authority for market regulation (for example, the Municipal Administration for Market Regulation in the relevant pilot-free-trade zone). Upon issuance of the business license, the FIP automatically receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which constitutes its national tax identification number (TIN) for VAT, corporate income tax withholding, customs, and export tax rebate purposes. The USCC is synchronised to the STA through the multi-license integration system, so no separate TIN application is required. The FIP must then complete VAT taxpayer registration with the competent state tax bureau, selecting either small-scale taxpayer status when annual taxable turnover does not exceed CNY 5,000,000, or general VAT taxpayer status to issue VAT special invoices and claim export refunds. For export activity, the FIP must obtain a customs registration code from the General Administration of Customs via the China International Trade Single Window and complete the export tax rebate filing registration with the STA. Non-resident partners that do not hold a PRC TIN must separately register for a non-resident taxpayer identification number through the STA e-Tax platform before the partnership income tax distribution return is filed. For any Hong Kong presence or parallel Hong Kong partnership, a Business Registration Certificate must be obtained from the IRD Business Registration Office, and the registration number serves as the Hong Kong taxpayer reference.

None under PRC law, because the FIP is automatically treated as fiscally transparent under the Partnership Enterprise Law and Caishui [2008] No. 159; each partner is taxed on the distributive share of partnership income. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT Law and implementing regulations, exports of physical goods are zero-rated and eligible for the VAT export refund (exemption, credit, and refund method) for general VAT taxpayers if: the goods are declared and physically exported through a PRC customs checkpoint; the FIP has completed export tax rebate filing before the export; the export transaction is real, the commercial documents are authentic, and the foreign exchange payment is received and repatriated to a PRC bank account within the statutory period. Re-invoicing of goods that never enter PRC customs territory (offshore trade or direct delivery from a foreign supplier) does not qualify for PRC export zero-rating and may fall outside PRC VAT altogether; any such claim would be treated as fraudulent and clawed back. In the Hong Kong SAR context, offshore trading profits may be exempt from profits tax, but that is a direct-tax relief separate from VAT zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (outbound goods declaration) electronically recorded by the General Administration of Customs via the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or multimodal transport document issued by the independent carrier
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate (inward remittance certificate) issued by a PRC-designated bank, demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds in convertible currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying sale/purchase contracts
  • VAT special invoice received from the upstream PRC supplier to substantiate the input VAT credit used in the refund calculation
  • Export tax refund application filing receipt and any customs verification printout generated by the e-Tax or Single Window system

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled Unified VAT Invoice System (tax-control equipment and the fully digital electronic invoice platform). Invoices are transmitted in real time to the STA with digital encryption and online verification, and are matched through the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform. The Golden Tax Phase IV system performs continuous anti-fraud and risk analytics. Export declarations must be transmitted through the China International Trade Single Window, which links customs, tax, foreign exchange, and bank data. The FIP must reconcile each export with the corresponding electronic invoice, customs declaration, and receipt of foreign exchange before claiming a zero-rating or refund benefit.

Under the PRC Tax Administration Law (Article 66) and Criminal Law (Article 204), fraudulent or baseless export zero-rating claims trigger immediate clawback of the refund, daily late-payment interest of 0.05% of the clawed-back amount, and an administrative penalty of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained. The STA and General Administration of Customs cross-check export declarations, bills of lading, VAT invoices, and foreign-exchange receipts through Golden Tax Phase IV and the Trade Single Window; any false document, circular transaction, unverified transport, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the prescribed deadline leads to retroactive cancellation of zero-rating, recovery of refunds with penalties, suspension or revocation of export refund eligibility, and referral to criminal authorities. In severe cases, individual participants may be sentenced to fixed-term imprisonment, up to life imprisonment. Hong Kong IRD separately applies anti-avoidance and penalty provisions of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to false offshore-profits claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR operates as a separate monetary jurisdiction with a fully convertible currency, the Hong Kong dollar, linked to the United States dollar through the Linked Exchange Rate System. The HKMA imposes no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of profits, capital, dividends, or partnership distributions for legally formed entities operating in Hong Kong. However, the owning shareholders or partners that are resident in mainland China remain subject to mainland China's Foreign Exchange Administration Rules and SAFE supervision when injecting capital into or receiving distributions from the Hong Kong FIP. Current account items such as trade settlements, service fees, and reasonable surrogate payments are generally permissible without SAFE prior approval, while capital account items including direct investment, acquisition of equity or partnership interests, and cross-border funding require SAFE registration, ODI filing, or other ad hoc approvals. The practical challenge for the FIP is not the Hong Kong convertibility but the upstream Chinese outbound investment compliance; the FIP must maintain full documentation of the Chinese parent's capital remittance route, including the SAFE ODI certificate, the capital verification report issued by a PRC certified public accountant, and the source of funds declaration for each partner. Profit repatriation from Hong Kong to China can be executed freely by the Hong Kong FIP, but the Chinese recipient must ensure that the inward remittance falls within an approved quota and that any required PRC enterprise income tax holding or tax filing is satisfied. For a re-invoicing business, funds frequently flow into Hong Kong from overseas buyers or from mainland Chinese procurement entities. While the Hong Kong FIP itself enjoys full convertibility, the bank and the AML compliance team will expect a clear economic substance trail, including contractual obligations, trade documentation, freight forwarding records, and payment timing logic, to avoid the characterization of illegal cross-border arbitrage or round-tripping. Overall, the regime is open and flexible for the Hong Kong entity, but the governing constraint is the heightened scrutiny of trade-based money laundering risks inherent in a general re-invoicing hub model.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no export proceeds surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no statutory timeline for retaining foreign currency receipts. The Hong Kong FIP may maintain USD, HKD, EUR, or CNY accounts and schedule outbound payments freely. However, where the underlying sales are booked by a mainland Chinese affiliate, SAFE requires the Chinese exporter to repatriate export proceeds through the Chinese banking system within the prescribed time frame and to observe any applicable forced conversion ratio if the proceeds are held in a restricted category. This blueprint is written specifically for the Hong Kong FIP, and therefore no surrender obligation attaches to the Hong Kong treasury activity.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the Hong Kong SAR central banking authority; People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland Chinese cross-border capital flows

Turnover: A Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership is not automatically required to appoint a certified public accountant auditor merely by reason of its partnership status. However, the Inland Revenue Department requires certified audited financial statements, prepared by a certified public accountant (practising), where the partnership's gross income in the relevant basis period is HK$5,000,000 (approximately USD 640,000) or more, where an offshore profits exclusion is claimed, or where the IRD expressly issues a notice requiring audited accounts. If the FIP has a corporate general partner that is a Hong Kong-incorporated company, that corporate partner is subject to the Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 audit mandate unless it qualifies as a small private company. A small private company is one satisfying at least two of the following three quantitative tests: total annual revenue not exceeding HK$10,000,000; total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000; and 50 or fewer employees. If the corporate partner fails the small-company test, it must be audited in accordance with HKFRS and the applicable Companies Ordinance provisions. In addition, an independent CPA audit is contractually required by most licensed banks supporting Swift_LC, TT_Wire, and XT_Transfer rails for any re-invoicing partnership with annual gross throughput in excess of HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000), given the medium-risk enhanced AML profile.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (PRC standard CIT rate; the FIP itself is a pass-through partnership, so the headline rate applies at the level of the corporate/non-resident partners)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC withholding tax on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise. For a pass-through FIP, profit remittances to partners are not classic dividends; the partner is taxed on its distributive share of China-source business profits. Under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA, dividend-type payments from a PRC resident to a Hong Kong beneficial-owner company are reduced to 5% where that company directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the paying entity.
