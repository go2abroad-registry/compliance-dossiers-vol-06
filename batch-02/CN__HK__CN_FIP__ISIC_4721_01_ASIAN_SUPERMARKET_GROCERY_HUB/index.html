Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for limited partnerships, no share premium requirement and no blocked escrow account mandate. Capital contributions are fixed solely by the Partnership Deed Schedule A; the foreign-invested partners must transfer their agreed cash subscriptions to the partnership account within 30 days of registration for banking and operational continuity.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: GovHK e-Tax (eTax) online services at www.ird.gov.hk

E-government filing portal

For non-resident entities forming a Hong Kong partnership, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) number issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The foreign entity must submit Form IRBR 201 (Application for Registration of Business) either online via the GovHK business e-registration service or in person at the Business Registration Office, providing the partnership name, activities, principal and business addresses, and full details of all general and limited partners. Upon approval, the BRC is issued with a unique BR number used as the tax ID. No minimum capital is required and no separate TIN certificate exists; the BRC is the sole tax identification instrument for the FIP.

For cross-border transactions, zero-rating eligibility is determined by the destination jurisdiction (e.g., mainland China Export VAT Refund rules). In Hong Kong, goods that are directly exported are outside the scope of Hong Kong profits tax provided the trade operations are conducted and controlled outside Hong Kong, and no indirect tax arises on the export.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a hybrid convertibility regime. The current account is generally convertible for bona fide trade-related payments, subject to bank authenticity review, including invoices, contracts, shipping documents and customs records. Capital account transactions remain controlled; the FIP must obtain FDI registration and open a dedicated capital account before receiving inbound equity or partnership capital from Hong Kong. Inward capital can be converted to RMB only for approved business purposes, such as inventory procurement, rent, payroll, shelf infrastructure and permitted working capital; conversion and payment must occur in a single bank transaction to prevent diversion. Profit remittance to HK partners is permissible after annual audited financial statements, tax clearance, partnership distribution resolution and a valid SAFE registration are presented to the designated bank. Hurdles include stale or inconsistent UBO documentation, mismatched invoice and customs entries, unfiled tax returns, and failure to update the FIP registration after partner changes. Export receipts must be repatriated but there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio; enterprises may retain FX or convert voluntarily.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign-currency account within 180 calendar days of the customs declaration date. No statutory mandatory conversion ratio applies to the export receipts of the FIP; the enterprise may retain up to 100% of the foreign currency in its settlement account or voluntarily sell such proceeds to a licensed onshore commercial bank on the interbank FX market. If SAFE imposes temporary counter-cyclical measures or the FIP is placed under special balance-of-payments administration, SAFE may direct the settling bank to require full or partial immediate conversion into RMB. The FIP must submit the export customs declaration, invoice, transportation document and contract to the receiving bank for trade authenticity verification before settlement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with local branch supervision over the FIP's registered address

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the FIP. Under PRC banking and FX regulations, the only legal channels for opening foreign-currency settlement and capital accounts for a foreign-invested partnership are licensed domestic commercial banks. Offshore fintech EMIs cannot hold principal RMB balances, cannot act as a clearing participant in CIPS, and are not permitted to serve as the first beneficiary for cross-border capital account inflows. The FIP must therefore rely exclusively on the branch banking network of a SAFE-approved bank.

The receiving bank will perform capital verification through a certified auditor where required, confirm the credited amount against the approved paid-in capital limit, and settle the RMB into a restricted capital settlement account. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% flat standard rate for unincorporated businesses; two-tiered concession of 7.5% on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits per partner (Hong Kong Profits Tax, Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap.112).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Hong Kong does not impose any withholding or dividend tax on distributions from a partnership or company to non-resident partners/shareholders.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for a large taxpayer (annual turnover ≥ HKD 500 million) with connected transactions exceeding HKD 50 million (goods), HKD 10 million (intangibles/financials/other services). Master File required when group consolidated turnover ≥ HKD 6.8 billion. A smaller enterprise (annual turnover ≥ HKD 10 million) must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation if claiming treaty benefits.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no formal ESR, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance requires that profits be derived from substantive Hong Kong operations. For treaty relief, the OECD Principal Purpose Test precludes benefits where the partnership lacks genuine CIGA (inventory management, procurement and sales generation) in Hong Kong. The PRC investor must avoid a shell structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Profits Tax fraud)

Statutory sanction: Breach of Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82(1) (fraudulent evasion) and s.82A; additional tax of up to 300% of the undercharged tax; a fine on conviction of up to HK$50,000 plus imprisonment for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil applies to a sham partnership; general partners liable jointly and severally for the recovered tax, interest and penalties. Directors/partners may be blacklisted by the IRD and barred from leaving Hong Kong under s.76 departure prohibition, with private assets exposed to civil execution and primary offenders subjected to a 3-year custodial sentence.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance for Non-Resident Payments

Statutory sanction: Under s.79(1) of the IRO, the payer is personally liable to pay the amount of tax not withheld; conviction triggers a fine of HK$10,000 and daily default fines; IRD may disallow the debit from the partnership's tax computation.

Director personal liability: Every partner/director who knowingly permits the unreported remittance is jointly and severally liable. Courts can pierce the veil if funds are moved overseas to avoid liability; criminal prosecution may lead to imprisonment (up to 3 years), and individuals may be placed on a travel ban (blacklist) pending payment.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing and Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Commissioner's power under s.50AAF to adjust profits to an arm's-length level; penalty under s.50AD for lack of documentation; s.82A additional tax up to 300% of the undercharge. The DTA may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns and artificial transaction documents may face criminal liability for knowingly assisting tax evasion; personal civil liability extends to restitution of the corporate veil is pierced for an integrated instrumentality. Travel blacklisting, disqualification orders, and imprisonment penalties up to 3 years apply on conviction.
