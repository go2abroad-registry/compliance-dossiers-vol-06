Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Registration of Limited Partnerships Section, 14/F, One Exchange Square, Central, Hong Kong

Governing statute

The Foreign-Invested Partnership is established as a limited partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 321) of Hong Kong, read together with the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), and must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Applicable sections are sections 4 (registration), 5 (statement of limited partnership), 6 (registered office) and 7 (certificate of registration) of Cap. 321, and section 5(1) of Cap. 310.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form LP1 - Statement of Limited Partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 321), together with the original Limited Partnership Deed and the consent of the designated representative

None. There is no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a Hong Kong limited partnership under Cap. 321; no statutory blocked escrow account is required. Capital contributions are left entirely to the partnership agreement.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region — electronic portal: eTAX (Electronic Tax Services); and, for mainland China supply-chain/export VAT purposes, the State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China and the General Administration of Customs (GACC) of the People's Republic of China — electronic portals: Electronic Tax Bureau and China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

If the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is carrying on business in Hong Kong, it must, within one month after commencement, file the business registration application with the Business Registration Office (BRO) of the IRD through eTAX, attaching the partnership agreement, identity documents of all general and limited partners, the principal place of business address in Hong Kong, and proof of the Hong Kong registered/representative office address; the BRO issues the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) and the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the Hong Kong statutory tax reference for all IRD filings, and any branch location must be separately registered. If the FIP is or additionally acts as a mainland China exporter, the tax and market-regulation departments complete registration concurrently at establishment and the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) is the legal taxpayer identification number for State Taxation Administration purposes; the FIP must then apply to the GACC for import/export registration and to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through its bank for membership in the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange system, and should apply for general VAT taxpayer status as needed for export VAT refund.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — the FIP is a fiscally transparent partnership: no entity-level profits tax in Hong Kong and no entity-level enterprise income tax in mainland China; income/loss is allocated to each partner and assessed in the partners' hands, subject to the Hong Kong territorial-source principle for profits tax and PRC pass-through treatment for partnership income.

Available election forms

None — Hong Kong has no check-the-box election; the pass-through treatment of partnership profits is mandatory under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). In mainland China, a partnership may not elect entity-level taxation, and the pass-through rule under Caishui [2008] No. 159 applies. Availability of advance rulings and anti-hybrid/transfer-pricing rules under the IRD/STA may affect cross-border characterisation, but no statutory entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Hong Kong: no general VAT/GST/sales tax (only narrow duties on selected dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109); mainland China: Value Added Tax (VAT) for the mainland supply-chain and export leg.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST/sales tax is imposed; every business carried on in Hong Kong must obtain Business Registration from the first day. For mainland China VAT, registration is mandatory upon establishment; an enterprise must be a general VAT taxpayer once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, with voluntary general VAT taxpayer application available only below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical goods exported from mainland China to a destination outside the customs territory of the People's Republic of China are eligible for VAT zero-rating/export refund where the exporter is the FIP (or its mainland exporting entity), the goods are actually shipped and discharged abroad, the export sale is evidenced by a completed China Customs export declaration, and the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated and recorded within the statutory period. For Shopify DTC cross-border retail sales, eligibility can be claimed under cross-border e-commerce retail export supervision code 9610 in the China International Trade Single Window, provided the four-stream match (order stream, payment stream, logistics stream, and customs declaration stream) is reconciled. Qualifying offshore digital services may be zero-rated only where supplied to an overseas customer and consumed wholly outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), Bill of Lading, and Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); for cross-border e-commerce retail exports, also the China Customs export clearance list under supervision code 9610 submitted through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Full set of clean ocean Bills of Lading or airway/express courier waybills (DHL, FedEx, UPS, or equivalent) evidencing physical export from the Chinese port and delivery to the overseas consumer country
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export sales contract/order, and the Shopify platform order report showing the overseas consumer, item descriptions, and destination addresses
  • Payment and FX documentation: Stripe/PayPal/Shopify Payments settlement reports and the bank inward remittance certificate/FIRC or SAFE-compliant export proceeds collection record
  • Four-stream reconciliation worksheet matching order, payment, logistics, and customs declaration data, maintained for tax audit and refund claims

Hong Kong has no real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing stamping, or anti-fraud digital stamping requirement. For mainland China operations, the FIP must use the STA Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (Full Digital Invoice) platform, with real-time reporting/validation of VAT invoices; export goods are cleared through GACC via the China International Trade Single Window, where e-commerce export orders, payment data, logistics data and customs declarations must align to obtain VAT zero-rating.

The tax authority will disallow and claw back zero-rating/export VAT refunds if the physical export, foreign-exchange repatriation, or four-stream match is not substantiated within the required period; the exported goods are then treated as domestic taxable supplies and output VAT is assessed with late-payment surcharge. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, evasion through false export declarations is punishable by administrative fines of up to five times the tax involved and, for fraud, criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China; false customs declarations also expose the exporter to penalties under the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Current account transactions are broadly convertible for genuine trade and services, with export proceeds subject to repatriation but no mandatory surrender. Under the PRC's decentralised current account administration, a Chinese bank may process payments after reviewing the underlying trade documentation and a valid SAFE purpose code. The capital account, by contrast, remains a prudential licensing regime: a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) cannot freely receive foreign capital until the SAFE or its delegated bank completes foreign investment registration, opens a dedicated capital account, and records each inbound remittance. After foreign exchange settlement of capital funds, the resulting Renminbi must be used within the positive-list scope of the partnership's actual operations; investment in wealth-management products, onshore equity, entrusted loans, or offshore lending is prohibited. Profit remittance to foreign partners is permitted only after audited financial statements, annual income tax filing, application of the deemed profit method or actual partnership profit allocation, and withholding tax payment (10% for non-resident enterprises or 20% for individuals unless a lower treaty rate applies, with Hong Kong treaty benefits subject to substantial business and beneficial ownership tests). The practical friction is high: every remittance must be reconciled against registered capital contributions, actual revenue, and tax evidence, and the bank maintains a rolling compliance file.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE trade foreign-exchange monitoring system, goods-trade export proceeds from the DTC cross-border e-commerce sales must be repatriated to the PRC within 90 calendar days from the date of the export customs declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the current statutory surrender requirement is 0% of export proceeds, meaning the FIP may retain the funds in a designated foreign exchange settlement account. Any voluntary conversion to Renminbi must be executed at a licensed domestic bank and supported by the underlying export documentation. For e-commerce retail exports, the FIP should maintain consolidated platform settlement reports, order manifests, and customs e-commerce declarations to evidence the maturity and validity of each repatriation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) of the People's Republic of China

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions must route via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's own offshore bank into the FIP's SAFE-registered Capital Account held with a PRC Tier-1 commercial bank. The SWIFT beneficiary field must state the exact partnership name, account number and the SAFE 'Foreign Exchange Capital Contribution' purpose code. After funds are credited, the FIP must present the payment notification, the partnership capital subscription agreement and evidence of lawful source; the bank then processes the 'Foreign Investment / Capital Item Business Registration' transaction code and completes foreign exchange capital settlement under the positive-list rules. A licensed PRC CPA must subsequently issue the capital verification report. Inbound capital funds cannot enter a personal account, cannot be loaned onshore, and cannot be diverted to securities, wealth-management, entrusted loans or offshore investments.For trade settlements, the FIP may instruct its domestic bank to send SWIFT MT103 messages for direct supplier invoices; use Commercial Letters of Credit through the bank's trade finance desk for high-value staggered shipments; or use Documentary Collections, including documents against payment and documents against acceptance, for open-account transactions with trusted counterparties. Every disbursement must be supported by underlying trade documents such as a purchase contract, pro-forma invoice, bill of lading or airway bill, and customs declaration, and must carry a valid SAFE purpose code. Payments to related Hong Kong parties require arm's-length transfer-pricing documentation, and the Trade and Foreign Exchange Monitoring System will flag any material deviation from the declared goods-trade background.Onshore CNY forward contracts (USD/CNY or HKD/CNY) executed with a PRC-licensed bank under genuine current-account trade exposure or approved foreign debt flows, subject to SAFE real-demand verification and supporting trade documents.Onshore cross-currency swaps (USD versus CNY or HKD versus CNY) for permitted foreign debt or capital-account repayment hedging, provided the underlying obligation was approved by SAFE and the swap does not create synthetic leverage.Onshore FX option strategies, including bought options and risk-reversal structures, used strictly to hedge identifiable trade or capital-account currency exposure; naked short option positions are prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible in the onshore PRC interbank market; a PRC FIP may not execute NDFs with an onshore bank, and any offshore NDF usage must remain solely with the Hong Kong parent or foreign holding entity outside the PRC banking perimeter.

They cannot lawfully accept Renminbi deposits or execute domestic settlement in the PRC. Shopify Payments, PayPal and Stripe are acceptable only as sales-collection rails that sweep proceeds onward to a licensed domestic bank account through a licensed payment institution; they cannot serve as the treasury or liquidity-clearing account. All offshore funds, including HKD or USD balances held by foreign PSPs, must be repatriated into the FIP's PRC bank and reported under SAFE trade monitoring.
