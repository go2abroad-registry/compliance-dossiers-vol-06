Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region

Governing statute

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) will be constituted as a Hong Kong limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), Laws of Hong Kong, with residual partnership-law rules supplied by the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), and business registration governed by the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The applicable provisions include those on formation, registration, names, registered office, general and limited partner liability, and post-registration amendments.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership (Form LP1) under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), followed by Business Registration Application (Form IRBR 1) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310)

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership and no blocked-escrow or capital-lockup requirement. Capital contributions, profit-sharing ratios, and withdrawal rights are governed solely by the Limited Partnership Deed and the general residual provisions of Cap. 37 and Cap. 38.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the China Electronic Tax Administration portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with supporting records on the STA public portal at chinatax.gov.cn; for the Hong Kong SAR element, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region through the eTAX electronic services portal at www.ird.gov.hk, including the Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the foreign-invested partnership (FIP) with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code, which functions as the TIN/VAT taxpayer identification number for all China tax purposes. Step 2: Complete tax registration through the STA electronic tax platform within 30 days of business licence issuance by linking the Unified Social Credit Code to the FIP tax profile and submitting the partnership deed, foreign-invested enterprise filing, managing partner identity documents and non-resident partner ownership statements. Step 3: Register for VAT status under the same TIN; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales have exceeded or are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, while a small-scale VAT taxpayer profile may be elected below that threshold. Step 4: File tax registrations for each foreign corporate or individual partner with the competent STA authority, including the partner's foreign tax identification number and, where treaty relief is claimed, a certificate of tax residence. Step 5: Complete China Customs registration to obtain the customs declaration code needed for export zero-rating, and link the FIP VAT invoice profile, customs registration and bank FX settlement accounts. Step 6: Apply for digital invoice (fapiao) issuance under the fully digital electronic invoice system and receive STA-authorised API credentials for system integration. Step 7: Report designated foreign-currency bank accounts to SAFE through the settlement bank and prepare for export foreign-exchange receipt verification. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for entity classification: the PRC does not provide check-the-box elections, entity classification elections or hybrid mismatch elections in the US or OECD sense; partnership pass-through is mandatory for income tax. Available elections are limited to VAT status and collection-basis choices, namely the small-scale taxpayer election for annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000, the election to use simplified VAT collection rates when eligible, and the general taxpayer registration obligation once the threshold is exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory economic turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; basic TIN registration is required for every FIP within 30 days of incorporation regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers below the threshold may use simplified VAT collection rates, and qualifying small-scale monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 may be exempt under current STA relief policy. Reduced headline rates of 9 percent for specified goods and 6 percent for services apply where relevant, while exports of goods and qualifying offshore services are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT (export exemption and refund for general VAT taxpayers; exemption without refund for small-scale taxpayers) applies to goods physically exported from mainland China after formal customs clearance, including cross-border e-commerce retail exports under customs supervision code 9610, B2B e-commerce exports under 9710/9810, and bonded retail exports under 1210 where applicable. Eligibility requires sale through a licensed cross-border e-commerce platform, physical exit of the goods from the customs territory, payment in foreign currency through SAFE-approved banking channels, repatriation of export proceeds within 180 days of export declaration, and complete order, logistics, customs and payment documentation. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated if expressly listed within the VAT export exemption and refund scope.

  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or cross-border logistics manifest evidencing export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank/SAFE export collection verification evidencing foreign-exchange repatriation
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • TikTok Shop platform transaction/order records and settlement statements
  • Original VAT fapiao or export-invoice documentation where required
  • Customs export goods declaration with HS commodity code, quantity and export value

China operates a real-time fiscal invoice clearance regime under the Golden Tax-IV system. All VAT invoices (fapiao) must be issued through STA-authorised systems; fully digital electronic invoices carry a unified national anti-fraud digital stamp and are transmitted to STA in real time at issuance. Enterprises must integrate accounting, ERP and e-commerce settlement systems with the STA electronic invoice platform through approved API interfaces. No invoice may be issued offline or outside the authorised digital clearance channel. For TikTok Shop retail, each VAT invoice and export invoice must be reconciled with the platform order dataset, customs declaration and payment settlement record.

STA, China Customs and SAFE cross-match export declarations, logistics events, platform transaction data, invoice data and FX receipts in real time. If zero-rating is claimed without the mandatory documentation bundle or with false documents, the refund is denied and any VAT refunded is retrospectively clawed back, plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05 percent, a penalty of 50 percent to 500 percent of the tax evaded, suspension of export tax-refund qualification, downgrade of the taxpayer credit rating, and customs penalties for false export declaration. Fraudulent export refund claims, forged documentation or knowing misrepresentation constitute criminal tax fraud under PRC law, and responsible partners and management may be subject to personal criminal and civil liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under the HKMA currency board system with the Hong Kong dollar fully convertible across both current and capital accounts. There are no surrender requirements, no residual exchange-control regulations, and no legal barrier to remitting partnership profits or investment liquidation proceeds to CNY, USD, GBP, or other major currencies. Mainland China, by contrast, maintains current-account convertibility conditioned on genuine trade or service verification, while capital-account flows are permissible only through regulated channels such as FDI, ODI, or QDLP/QFII quotas. Profit remittances from a mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership to non-resident partners are subject to withholding income tax and require bank verification of audited financial statements, tax paid certification, and a valid foreign-exchange registration or settlement form. The composite CN-HK structure should therefore maintain the HK treasury ledger as a free cross-border settlement rail while isolating China-side proceeds in a SAFE-compliant ledger to avoid unlawful capital movement. HK does not differentiate retained versus surrendered export proceeds, whereas a mainland seller or exporter deemed to be the PRC beneficiary must repatriate goods-trade proceeds promptly and retain complete customs and logistics evidence for SAFE authenticity review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts; HKD may be held, converted, or remitted freely. For Mainland China, SAFE goods-trade monitoring requires all export foreign currency receipts arising from goods trade to be repatriated to a licensed bank in the PRC; there is no compulsory conversion ratio, so proceeds may either remain in a designated foreign-currency account or be settled into RMB according to the enterprise's declared settlement intention. The safe collection benchmark is generally no longer than 90 days from customs declaration or the contracted settlement term; overdue items are flagged by the SAFE monitoring system and must be reconciled with customs declarations, commercial invoices, and logistics evidence. Any capital-account receipts or exceptional deferrals require documented SAFE approval before disposal.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for Mainland China cross-border capital controls; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and bank regulator for Hong Kong SAR, operating the currency board for the freely convertible HKD

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise B2B is legally permitted to serve Hong Kong businesses only as a licensed money service operator for remittance, not as a deposit-taking bank. Feasibility rating is High for ancillary vendor and influencer payouts through its API, but Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury or capital escrow provider because HKMA licensing requires any deposit-taking entity to hold a full banking licence, and Wise does not provide the regulatory protection, hedging, or capital-verification infrastructure required for corporate treasury. The FIP should therefore deploy Wise B2B and TikTok Shop payout rails as sub-ledgers while keeping master cash positions in a Tier-1 licensed Hong Kong bank such as HSBC or BOCHK.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A PRC-based partner or investor should first complete SAFE and NDRC ODI registration or the applicable overseas investment filing before injecting equity into the Hong Kong FIP. The remittance should be sent as a SWIFT MT103 with a reference such as FIP capital contribution under the executed Limited Partnership Agreement, and the PRC remitting bank must apply the correct SAFE direct-investment purpose code on the outbound instruction. On the Hong Kong receiving side, the Tier-1 bank will credit the Capital Escrow sub-ledger and may require a legal opinion confirming due formation of the FIP, a board or partner resolution authorizing the account signatories, and a Certified Public Accountant capital-verification report if the bank’s internal policy requires it. If funds flow in reverse into Mainland China, the onshore FIP must complete SAFE FDI capital account registration and use bank capital-account settlement procedures; HKD settlement should use CHATS, USD should use HK Clearing House settlement rails, and CNH should be cleared through the BOCHK CNY clearing platform.For supplier and vendor payments, use SWIFT MT103 for routine disbursements, and use irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit or Documentary Collections (Documents against Payment / Documents against Acceptance) for high-value bulk inventory purchases. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is required if the partnership’s turnover exceeds HKD 100,000,000 and its related-party non-financial transactions exceed HKD 20,000,000 (or financial transactions exceed HKD 10,000,000). Master File is required if the ultimate group’s consolidated turnover exceeds HKD 600,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to Caribbean offshore centres. However, the territorial source principle, combined with anti-avoidance provisions in IRO Sections 61, 61A, and 61B, requires the FIP to demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — such as finalising sales contracts, managing inventory, negotiating with the TikTok platform, and making strategic commercial decisions — are performed in Hong Kong to support offshore income exemption or treaty resident status. The IRD will scrutinise shell-type FIPs where all substantive activities take place in mainland China while profits are booked in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Sections 82 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO). On summary conviction: fine up to HKD 10,000 and three times the tax undercharged, imprisonment up to 3 years. On indictment: fine up to HKD 50,000, imprisonment up to 10 years, and three times the amount of tax undercharged. The court may also impose daily continuing penalties for ongoing failure.

Director personal liability: For an FIP, the managing partners and directors who sign, counsel, or connive in false returns are personally jointly and severally liable. Civil remedies include asset restitution, garnishment of personal bank accounts, freezing orders, and departure prohibition orders (travel bans) issued by the Commissioner. Criminal imprisonment is mandatory for fraudulent evasion, and the court may disqualify directors for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRO Section 50AA authorises the IRD to adjust the partnership’s assessable profits to reflect arm’s length pricing. Failure to comply with TP documentation rules may attract penalties under IRO Section 80 (up to HKD 100,000) plus additional tax undercharged, interest under Section 71, and penalties of three times the amount of tax evaded. The IRD may also issue advance pricing agreements and revise them retroactively in cases of non-disclosure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm’s length transfer prices and ignore TP documentation obligations are jointly liable for the tax undercharged. Under IRO Sections 85 and 86, the IRD may recover unpaid tax from directors personally. If the arrangement is a sham designed to shift profits to tax havens, courts may pierce the partnership veil, hold directors liable in civil damages, impose bans on leaving Hong Kong, and refer the case to the Police for criminal prosecution that could lead to imprisonment.
Economic Substance Deficit / Shell Entity / Abusive DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: IRO Sections 61A and 61B (Hong Kong GAAR) allow the IRD to disregard the avoidance arrangement and re-assess profits as if the substance existed. Additional penalties under Section 82 apply where the arrangement involves fraudulent evasion. The Commissioner may also impose a departure prohibition order to prevent flight of assets and seek police investigation. Failure to maintain proper records and economic substance may trigger penalties under Section 51C and Section 80.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil means that partners and directors can be held personally liable for the FIP’s unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties. Civil asset restitution may reach assets in mainland China and Hong Kong, including personal residences and bank accounts. Directors may be blacklisted by the IRD, prohibited from leaving Hong Kong, disqualified from holding directorships, and, where the shell arrangement is implicated in money laundering or fraudulent tax evasion, imprisoned for up to 10 years under the Criminal Procedure Ordinance.
