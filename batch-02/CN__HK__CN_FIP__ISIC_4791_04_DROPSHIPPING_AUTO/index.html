Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, Hong Kong SAR; and Companies Registry (Limited Partnership Section), Hong Kong SAR

Governing statute

Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), sections 3, 4, 7 and 24A; Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37), sections 4, 5, 6 and 7; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), sections 5 and 6; Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), Part 2 and Schedule 2. Hong Kong does not maintain a separate statutory vehicle titled 'Foreign-Invested Partnership'; the term is a factual descriptor of a Hong Kong partnership in which one or more partners are non-Hong Kong residents, and the formation is carried out under the above partnership and business registration legislation.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form IRBR1 - Business Registration Application under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); and Form LP1 - Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37)

None under Hong Kong law. The Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37) and Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) prescribe no minimum paid-up capital. Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online taxpayer services are delivered through the National Electronic Tax Bureau platform and the regional Electronic Tax Administration systems maintained by the competent local STA office for the Foreign-Invested Partnership's registered address.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) organized in China, the statutory taxpayer identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon formation. The FIP must first complete enterprise name pre-approval and partnership registration with SAMR under the Foreign Investment Law and the applicable measures on foreign-invested partnerships. After receiving the SAMR registration certificate containing the USCC, the FIP must complete supplementary tax registration with the competent STA within the statutory time period, file its accounting and bookkeeping policies, register a tax-filing controller, bind the relevant payment rails and bank accounts, and obtain an electronic invoice quota. The USCC becomes the FIP's tax identification number for VAT, income tax withholding, individual income tax reporting, and other transactional taxes. A non-resident partner of the FIP is not automatically required to obtain a separate Chinese TIN unless that partner has a permanent establishment or otherwise derives China-source income requiring direct registration; in that case, the non-resident partner registers with the competent STA for a separate taxpayer identification number or fulfills obligations through the FIP as withholding agent. The FIP must also complete e-commerce operator recordal, customs registration as an exporter if it engages in cross-border dropshipping, and foreign-exchange registration with the relevant designated bank and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For PRC enterprise income tax purposes, a Foreign-Invested Partnership is not a taxpayer; each partner is taxed at the partner level as either an individual income taxpayer or an enterprise income taxpayer depending on partner status. For VAT and other indirect taxes, the FIP is the entity-level taxpayer and must register, collect, invoice, and remit VAT in its own name where the taxable supply occurs within China.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch recharacterization election exists for PRC partnerships; pass-through treatment is mandatory under the partnership taxation rules. The FIP may elect to be registered as a VAT general taxpayer even if its annual taxable turnover is below the statutory threshold, provided it has sound accounting. An export-oriented FIP may select the applicable export tax refund method, generally the exemption-and-refund method for trading-type FIPs, or the exemption-credit-refund method if it qualifies as a production enterprise. Treaty or arrangement benefits, including those available under the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Double Tax Arrangement, are asserted through separate resident certification and beneficial ownership claims and do not alter the partnership's default pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for registration as a VAT general taxpayer; below this threshold the FIP generally remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer, subject to a temporary monthly sales exemption of CNY 100,000 for small-scale taxpayers. There is no turnover threshold for basic tax registration: all active FIPs must complete tax registration with the STA upon establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A zero VAT rate applies to goods physically exported from China where the FIP is the exporter, the goods are declared to and released by China Customs, the export is genuine and supported by transport documentation, and title and risk in the goods pass to an overseas customer. For automated dropshipping, the goods must leave China through normal customs procedures, typically using cross-border e-commerce retail export or B2B export supervision models, and the export objects must be sold into the overseas market rather than into domestic circulation. Qualifying offshore services supplied by the FIP to non-resident persons may also be zero rated if the service is rendered entirely outside China and supported by a contract, payment from outside China, and evidence of performance outside China; for goods-based dropshipping, the zero-rating analysis is driven by customs export clearance and receipt of foreign exchange, not merely by the use of Stripe or PayPal settlement.

  • Customs Export Declaration record with electronic customs release mark showing the FIP as exporter or as the principal to the export customs procedure
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing that the goods physically left China and were delivered to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or a bank-issued foreign exchange collection record from a PRC designated bank evidencing receipt and settlement of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the export declaration, shipment, and sales order
  • Export sales contract or purchase order for the dropshipping sale and the corresponding automated e-commerce transaction log
  • Export VAT invoice or Special VAT Invoice used in connection with the export sale and tax refund application
  • Cross-border e-commerce export transaction list or related customs declaration schedule where a customs supervised e-commerce model such as 9610, 9710, or 9810 is used
  • Payment platform settlement evidence from Stripe, PayPal, or similar rails linking the overseas buyer's payment to the goods shipped and the eventual foreign exchange repatriation

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV digital tax control system and mandates the use of All-Digital Electronic Invoices (known as Shudian Piao) for most active VAT taxpayers, including e-commerce operators. Fully digitized electronic invoices are issued through the national e-invoice service platform, carry a unique 20-digit invoice number, use cryptographic tax-control encoding, are digitally signed and uploaded transactionally to the STA in real time, and require no physical fis- cal device. The STA performs real-time one-by-one invoice verification and risk screening; e-commerce and automated dropshipping businesses must connect applicable billing systems through approved APIs or platforms to enable automated invoice issuance, red-invoice management, and rejection of untraceable duplicate invoices. Traditional Special VAT Invoices remain subject to the same tax-control stamping and encryption requirements. Failure to comply with e-invoicing and clearance obligations may result in invoice quota suspension, blocking of exemption and refund claims, and tax administrative penalties.

The STA and customs jointly subject export zero-rating and refund claims to risk classification, document verification, and post-clearance audit. If documentation is incomplete, non-conforming, or inconsistent across the customs declaration, bill of lading, sales contract, payment trail, and foreign exchange record, the zero-rated treatment may be reversed, the VAT refund clawed back with overdue tax interest, and the export transaction recharacterized as domestic supply at 13% VAT. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims may trigger fines ranging from 50% to five times the tax involved, suspension of export tax refund qualification, inclusion in the tax dishonesty blacklist, enhanced AML review of the FIP and its representative, and criminal liability for export tax refund fraud under the PRC Criminal Law, potentially including severe imprisonment penalties. Where foreign exchange is not repatriated within the 180-day statutory window without acceptable justification, the undertaking may be required to account for VAT on the unrepatriated export receipts and may be subject to SAFE penalties for non-repatriation of trade proceeds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is an onshore PRC partnership and therefore operates under China's current-account convertibility and capital-account control framework. Current-account foreign-exchange receipts and payments related to genuine dropshipping trade, platform fees, logistics, and service debits are generally permitted through domestic designated foreign-exchange banks on a transaction-authenticity review basis; banks verify contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and payment-processor settlement reports. RMB is freely usable within the current account for legitimate trade transactions, but full free convertibility is not granted. Capital-account inflows from Hong Kong partners in the form of partnership capital contributions require prior SAFE remote-supervision registration through the receiving bank and must be remitted into a specially designated FDI capital account; once settled into RMB, the funds must be used inside the approved business scope and cannot be deployed for securities investment or non-operating lending. Profit and distribution remittances to non-resident partners are treated as current-account outbound remittances after enterprise income tax withholding and issuance of a valid tax payment certificate to the bank, but the authorities may require proof that distributions are computed from realized audited profits and that the partnership has completed annual tax filing. Surrender of export proceeds no longer follows a fixed percentage rule; rather, trade proceeds must be repatriated to China and either retained in a current-account foreign-exchange account or sold to a designated bank, with goods-export collection generally required within 90 days from shipment or customs declaration unless registered with SAFE as a deferred collection. HKD and USD may be converted into CNY for genuine current-account settlements after submission of the underlying trade and payment documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's trade-in-foreign-exchange administration framework, export foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated to China and reported through the bank's trade-monitoring system; the general collection deadline for goods exports is 90 days from the date of customs declaration, extendable to 180 days with an offshore-receipt period registration. There is no current statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain export proceeds in a current-account foreign-exchange account or convert them into RMB at the prevailing market rate. For automated dropshipping exports delivered through cross-border e-commerce platforms, the enterprise must match platform settlement records with customs manifests and use a registered bank or licensed cross-border payment institution channel; overdue receipts and mismatched monetary amounts are subject to the bank's enterprise trade-category controls and SAFE verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) applicable to HKD clearing and banking supervision in Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Global fintech EMIs and digital banks are not licensed to maintain local onshore current accounts, foreign-exchange capital accounts, or RMB basic settlement accounts for a PRC-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for FDI capital injection, profit remittance, and SAFE reporting. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable only as customer card-acquiring platforms for the dropshipping marketplace, not as banking counterparties; any settlement into China must be channelled through a licensed cross-border payment institution or a SAFE-designated commercial bank under current-account trade-authenticity rules. Capital-account inflows and outflows cannot legally be routed through offshore EMIs.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner initiates remittance through HKMA CHATS/RTGS or SWIFT MT103/MT202 to the FIP's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a SAFE-designated PRC bank, quoting the remittance purpose as direct investment in a Foreign-Invested Partnership and using the bank-mandated inward FDI capital purpose code. The receiving bank must complete or confirm the SAFE FDI registration before crediting, verify the remitter matches the registered foreign partner, prevent co-mingling with operating receipts, and release settled RMB only for approved business expenditures. After the capital call is received, the FIP must obtain an independent CPA capital verification statement and submit the annual foreign investment information report. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury rail. Global fintech EMIs and digital banks are not licensed to maintain local onshore current accounts, foreign-exchange capital accounts, or RMB basic settlement accounts for a PRC-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; they cannot replace a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for FDI capital injection, profit remittance, and SAFE reporting. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable only as customer card-acquiring platforms for the dropshipping marketplace, not as banking counterparties; any settlement into China must be channelled through a licensed cross-border payment institution or a SAFE-designated commercial bank under current-account trade-authenticity rules. Capital-account inflows and outflows cannot legally be routed through offshore EMIs.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: prepare when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible/financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related transactions (services, royalties, interest) exceed RMB 40 million. Master File: prepare when total annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion. Special documentation for cost sharing arrangements applies under SAT Bulletin 2016 No.42.

Economic substance scope: The FIP, its Chinese parent, and the HK booking entity must each demonstrate economic substance. PRC applies CIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and SAT Bulletin 2017 No.6, while HK applies its offshore-profits source doctrine and the passive income economic substance requirement: core income-generating activities (CIGA) — contract execution, inventory/risk management, customer invoicing, payment settlement, and strategic decisions — must be performed by the jurisdiction claiming the income. If the Chinese FIP or Chinese employees perform CIGA, the profits sourced there are China-source, the HK foreign-source claim collapses, and China will tax the enterprise as a PE at 25% or by withholding at 10%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / underlying sales concealment in automated dropshipping

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily interest of 0.05%; the tax authority may use Article 35 deemed-assessment powers. Criminal Law Article 201 triggers criminal liability if the evaded tax exceeds the statutory threshold (unpaid tax over RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax due), punishable by up to seven years' imprisonment, fines, and forfeiture.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors/general partners who signed the false allocation returns are jointly and severally liable to restore diverted assets; courts may order personal bank account freezing, exit-entry travel bans/blacklisting under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, and imprisonment under Article 201 for fraud; personal assets can be executed where corporate assets are commingled.
Transfer pricing and contemporaneous documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Article 41 and Article 114, the tax authority may adjust taxable income by arm's length pricing and levy penalty interest up to five percentage points above the benchmark rate; failure to provide documentation under SAT Bulletin 2016 No.42 and Tax Administration Law Article 44 gives a fine from RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 (RMB 50,000 serious) and the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed intercompany agreements at non-arm's length prices breach their fiduciary duty under Company Law Article 20/21; they can be held personally liable for the resulting CIT, penalties and interest, subjected to asset preservation, travel restriction/blacklisting, and, where deliberate fraud is proven, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201.
Treaty abuse / shell entity and beneficial ownership failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and SAT Bulletin 2015 No.7 deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the arrangement, and may impose the full 10% (or 25% if PE) Chinese tax with late-payment interest and 50%-500% fraud penalties; Tax Administration Law Article 63 and Criminal Law Article 201 apply if the treaty abuse is found to be intentional tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and HK shell will face piercing of the corporate veil: restoration of treaty benefits, joint and several liability over the underpaid WHT, personal asset seizure, blacklisting/travel bans, and imprisonment up to seven years for fraudulent treaty shopping; PRC courts will disregard the HK entity where it is a mere conduit.
