Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Taxation) national electronic tax administration platform and the relevant provincial or municipal electronic tax service portal.

E-government filing portal

For the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the entity must first be incorporated with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR). Under the integrated multi-license One-Run-Done procedure, the AMR automatically transmits the registration record to the STA, and the partnership receives an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID) for all PRC tax purposes. The FIP must then activate its electronic tax profile in the STA e-Taxation portal, complete VAT taxpayer classification (general VAT taxpayer or small-scale VAT taxpayer), designate finance and legal representatives, and register its bank account and accounting standards. A foreign non-resident partner without a PRC TIN must be registered in the STA non-resident taxpayer registration module through the FIP acting as the withholding agent; the partner must provide its Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and, where treaty relief is sought, a Hong Kong Certificate of Resident issued by the Inland Revenue Department. If the FIP intends to make zero-rated exports, it must separately obtain export tax refund (zero-rating) qualification through the STA export tax rebate system and register as a Customs exporter with the General Administration of Customs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may apply the 0% VAT rate to physical subscription-box goods sold to non-PRC customers and physically exported from the PRC customs territory, including exports to Hong Kong SAR, which is outside the PRC customs territory for VAT purposes. Eligibility requires the FIP to be the exporter of record, to possess a verified Customs export declaration, to issue a zero-rated export invoice, to receive payment through the banking system, and to repatriate the export proceeds within the statutory deadline. Goods must not be consumed, sold, or re-imported into the PRC before the zero-rating is substantiated; qualifying offshore digital services are not the operative category for this physical subscription-box merchandise.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (electronic export declaration issued by the General Administration of Customs with a unique customs registration number)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical dispatch out of PRC customs territory
  • Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation (bank-generated remittance receipt recorded in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; the PRC statutory counterpart of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Zero-rated export invoice issued through the official electronic invoice platform together with the export sales contract and logistics records

The PRC mandates the STA All-Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform for e-invoice issuance. Invoices must follow the official electronic invoice schema, carry a unique electronic invoice number and an anti-counterfeiting two-dimensional digital stamp, and be transmitted to the tax authority in near real time. General VAT taxpayers may issue special VAT invoices only through STA-authorised invoicing systems or accredited API-connected e-invoice service providers; uncontrolled self-printed or blank paper invoices are prohibited. For zero-rated exports, the electronic invoice must clearly mark the zero-rated sale and be reconciled with the Customs export declaration in the export tax rebate system.

The export tax refund system automatically cross-matches Customs export declarations, electronic invoices, logistics documents, and bank foreign-exchange receipt/repatriation records before any refund is released. If the zero-rating is not substantiated, the tax authority will revoke the 0% rate, assess output VAT retroactively plus late-payment surcharges, claw back all refunded input VAT, and may impose a monetary penalty of one to five times the amount of tax obtained by fraudulent or false export declarations under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. The taxpayer's export tax rebate qualification may be suspended or revoked, the taxpayer may be placed on the tax integrity blacklist, and criminal liability for fraudulent export refunds may be pursued under the Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland Chinese renminbi is fully convertible only on current account for the purpose of trade settlement and service payments, consistent with the IMF Article VIII obligations, whereas capital account items such as foreign partner capital contributions, partnership profit repatriation, liquidation proceeds and cross-border hedging remain subject to strict SAFE licensing, bank registration and use-of-funds monitoring. A Foreign-Invested Partnership operating a recurring subscription box service must expect the relevant SAFE-designated bank to verify each conversion from foreign currency to RMB against a lawful business background, a truthful declaration and an underlying commercial document set. E-commerce trade proceeds from cross-border subscription commerce may be converted at will after entering the current account of a SAFE-registered bank, but the same account cannot be used for capital injection conversions without the capital account FDI registration and CPA verification. The Hong Kong SAR leg is fully convertible across HKD, USD, EUR and CNH, with no surrender requirement, no foreign exchange approval hurdle and no profit remittance retention tax at the exchange-control layer. The principal profit remittance hurdles from a Chinese mainland FIP are the obligation to prepare audited financial statements, settle all applicable tax liabilities and obtain a tax clearance certification before transferring funds abroad, as the receiving bank must preserve documentary proof for each distribution to satisfy SAFE and anti-money-laundering oversight. Overall convertibility is best characterised as broad current-account freedom, tight capital-account approval, and HK as the flexible liquidity bridge for route foreign currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework for the mainland FIP, export receipts must be repatriated into the entity's designated foreign-exchange current account within 90 days of the customs export declaration date, unless the underlying trade contract expressly provides a shorter payment term; there is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio in force, meaning the FIP may retain the foreign currency in a SAFE-approved deposit account or convert the proceeds into RMB at the bank's prevailing spot foreign-exchange rate. For the Hong Kong SAR leg, there is no statutory surrender requirement, no prescribed repatriation timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio, as Hong Kong operates a free-floating foreign-exchange regime under the HKMA.

Central bank supervision: For the Chinese mainland exchange-control leg the authority is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); for the Hong Kong SAR treasury leg the central banking and monetary authority is the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), which administers a freely convertible currency regime without exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is low to nil for the onshore SAFE-regulated treasury because foreign e-money institutions and card-like platforms such as Wise, Revolut or Currencycloud are not licensed as PRC commercial banks or SAFE payment institutions with lawful foreign-exchange settlement authority; they therefore cannot clear export proceeds, capital injections or statutory RMB conversions attributable to the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. On the Hong Kong side, global e-commerce acquiring rails such as Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net can be used for card acceptance and low-value consumer refunds into a licensed Hong Kong EMI or bank account, but the dominant operating cash balances and all mainland CNY exposure must remain in Tier-1 SAFE-designated banks. Any attempt to route mainland trade proceeds through an unlicensed offshore EMI would constitute illegal cross-border foreign-exchange business and expose the entity to regulatory criminal liability, so the offshore EMI utility must be strictly limited to ancillary Hong Kong acquiring and non-mainland foreign-currency receivables.

All foreign capital contributions from the Hong Kong investor must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's SAFE-registered capital account maintained at a Tier-1 mainland commercial bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI viability is low to nil for the onshore SAFE-regulated treasury because foreign e-money institutions and card-like platforms such as Wise, Revolut or Currencycloud are not licensed as PRC commercial banks or SAFE payment institutions with lawful foreign-exchange settlement authority; they therefore cannot clear export proceeds, capital injections or statutory RMB conversions attributable to the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. On the Hong Kong side, global e-commerce acquiring rails such as Stripe Billing and Authorize.Net can be used for card acceptance and low-value consumer refunds into a licensed Hong Kong EMI or bank account, but the dominant operating cash balances and all mainland CNY exposure must remain in Tier-1 SAFE-designated banks. Any attempt to route mainland trade proceeds through an unlicensed offshore EMI would constitute illegal cross-border foreign-exchange business and expose the entity to regulatory criminal liability, so the offshore EMI utility must be strictly limited to ancillary Hong Kong acquiring and non-mainland foreign-currency receivables.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% general CIT rate for Chinese resident corporate partners; 0% CIT at the FIP pass-through entity level; 10% statutory withholding tax on non-resident partner distributions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends and profit distributions paid to a non-resident corporate investor; 5% reduced rate under the Mainland-HK Double Tax Arrangement if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident enterprise directly holding at least 25% of the capital and satisfies the substantive business test.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, financial or intangible-property transactions exceed RMB 100 million, other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is required when aggregate cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The China-HK DTA benefit, PRC CIT Law Art. 47 GAAR, and the Beneficial Ownership rules require the HK partner to carry on real Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including strategic procurement decisions, inventory risk management, contract negotiation, financing, and management of brand intangibles; if those functions are actually performed by the Chinese FIP, the HK entity is a conduit, treaty relief is denied, profits are reattributed to a Chinese PE, and the structure becomes taxable in China at 25% with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse Through a Sham Hong Kong Shell

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 47 and Implementation Rules Art. 120 allow recharacterization of the sham structure; Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 63 imposes penalties of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax plus daily interest of 0.05%; the Hong Kong entity loses treaty protection and the Chinese PE profit is assessed at 25%.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who approved the sham shell can be personally liable through veil-piercing or joint and several liability; they may face civil asset restitution, public dishonesty blacklisting, exit/travel bans, and criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 with imprisonment of up to 7 years for significant evasion, plus fines and confiscation of assets.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 41 and Art. 44 compel an arm's length reallocation of income; the underpayment is subject to interest at 0.05% per day and penalties of 50% to 500% under Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 63; failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation can attract fines up to RMB 50,000, and the transfer pricing adjustment cannot be offset by the HK affiliate's resulting losses.

Director personal liability: Directors, financial controllers, and general partners who signed false intercompany contracts can be ordered to restore diverted assets; they face civil liability for the unpaid tax, travel bans and tax blacklisting; the Chinese tax authority may refer the case for criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Art. 201 for tax evasion, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and personal asset seizure for fraudulently shifted profits.
Failure to Withhold Cross-Border WHT on Distributions, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 37 and Art. 38 impose withholding on the payer; Tax Collection Administration Law Art. 69 imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax; the FIP remains liable for the unpaid tax and the 0.05% daily late-payment interest; if the foreign recipient cannot be reached, the FIP and its responsible officers are required to satisfy the tax arrears from company and personal assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly caused the withholding failure can be held personally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax, blacklisted for tax dishonesty, subjected to exit and travel bans, and prosecuted criminally for intentional tax evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201; imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines may be imposed where the diverted amounts are substantial.
