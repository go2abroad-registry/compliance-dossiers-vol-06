Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Hong Kong law, a limited partnership or LPF must maintain a registered office in Hong Kong that is a physical street-address location capable of receiving service of process. The Companies Registry accepts a serviced-office or virtual-office address because it provides a physical delivery point. However, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) adopts a substance-over-form analysis; a virtual office or commercial mailbox is permissive for filing purposes but is highly likely to be rejected by IRD when assessing whether profits are attributable to Hong Kong and by banks for account opening. For an online consignment and secondhand luxury goods e-commerce platform using Stripe and Escrow_Pay, the payment processors require a verifiable physical business address and a mature local bank relationship. A virtual-only office increases FATCA/CRS compliance risk, invites an IRD audit, and may cause Stripe and escrow providers to suspend the merchant account; therefore a physical leased office or a high-quality serviced office with dedicated meeting rooms and resident staff is strongly recommended.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the National Electronic Tax Administration Service Platform (e-Tax) and local tax service offices for Mainland China. The Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong SAR is the separate competent authority for Hong Kong-sourced profits tax and business registration; Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Execute a partnership agreement and complete foreign investment information reporting; obtain the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) Business License from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Step 2: The Unified Social Credit Code on the business license constitutes the entity's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax purposes; no separate TIN application is required. Step 3: Log into the STA e-Tax portal, confirm the tax registration data, submit the supplementary taxpayer profile (legal representative, finance contact, bank account information, accounting system details) and activate electronic filing credentials. Step 4: Register for VAT by confirming taxpayer status in the e-Tax system; if annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, the enterprise must register as a VAT general taxpayer; below that threshold it is a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general status. Step 5: If the partnership will export goods, register as a foreign-trade operator with the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) and obtain the customs registration number from the General Administration of Customs of China; complete the exporters' VAT refund declaration registration in the STA export tax rebate system. Step 6: For any non-resident partner without a mainland establishment, the FIP as withholding agent must verify or obtain a non-resident Tax Identification Number at the competent tax office and, if treaty relief is sought, secure the partner's Certificate of Tax Residence from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department. Step 7: If a Hong Kong branch or permanent establishment is separately maintained, apply for a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate with the Business Registration Office; this is not a VAT/GST registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is fiscally transparent for Chinese enterprise income tax purposes; the partnership itself is not an EIT taxpayer, and each partner is taxed on its distributable share of the partnership income. For indirect tax, the FIP is an independent VAT taxpayer on taxable supplies and may be a zero-rated exporter for goods physically exported.

Available election forms

None for income tax entity classification; China does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election, and partnership transparency is statutory. The relevant statutory election is the VAT status election: taxpayers below the RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover threshold may elect to register as VAT general taxpayers if they meet the prescribed accounting and fixed-place-of-business requirements, and general taxpayers may elect the simplified levy method at a 3% levy rate reduced to 2% for qualifying sales of second-hand/old goods under the applicable transitional rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Tax registration is mandatory for every business entity from the date of business license issuance regardless of turnover. The VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (excluding tax); below that, a taxpayer is generally classified as a small-scale taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 may qualify for a temporary VAT exemption under current STA policy. Export-trade registration with MOFCOM and Customs is required separately.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To obtain the 0% VAT export rate for physical goods, the FIP must satisfy all of the following: (1) the sale is made to an overseas customer under an export contract or cross-border e-commerce sales order; (2) the goods physically leave Mainland China through a customs-supervised port, postal or express channel using a recognized export customs clearance procedure; (3) the FIP issues an export-oriented VAT invoice and reports the export sale in its VAT return; (4) the customs export declaration data matches the invoice and logistics records; and (5) export proceeds are received by the FIP and repatriated into a bank account in China within the statutory 180-day window unless an approved extension applies. For qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating or exemption applies only to cross-border services that fall within the STA's published list of zero-rated/exempt cross-border services and are supplied to overseas recipients with written offshore contracts and offshore payment; that is secondary in this vintage-luxury goods resale mandate.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (customs release/exit confirmation via the General Administration of Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) or through-rail cargo waybill
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract and online-sale order/consignment documentation
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt and settlement/transfer record evidencing inward remittance and repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export VAT refund declaration data and electronic export-rebate submission filed through the STA export tax rebate system
  • Logistics delivery evidence such as courier shipment tracking and delivery confirmation for cross-border e-commerce exports

China operates a national VAT invoice ('fapiao') system under the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV. Taxpayers must issue invoices through the STA-approved electronic invoice service platform; fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) are transmitted in real time with unique invoice codes and cryptographic anti-fraud digital fingerprints. There is no independent fiscal stamp; centralized e-invoice clearing is performed by the STA platform, and large taxpayers must integrate certified ERP systems through approved APIs/interfaces. Export invoices and red-letter (credit-note) invoices are subject to automatic cross-matching with customs export declaration data and foreign-exchange settlement records.

Post-export audits are conducted by the STA in coordination with the General Administration of Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, using automated cross-matching of customs export declarations, VAT invoice data, logistics manifests and foreign-exchange settlement results. If zero-rating or export refund is disallowed or unsubstantiated, the refund is clawed back retroactively, and the taxpayer must repay the refunded tax plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the due date. Under Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection, false export declarations and tax-fraud penalties range from 50% to 500% of the tax or refund amount; serious cases are referred for criminal prosecution as smuggling or VAT export fraud, with possible confiscation of goods, fines and imprisonment. Clawback and assessment periods under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection are generally three years, extended to five years in special circumstances, with no statute of limitation for fraudulent evasion or wilful refusal to pay.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a separate customs and monetary territory within the PRC framework. The HKMA administers the Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS) and does not impose exchange controls, mandatory surrender, or prior approval requirements for cross-border current account or capital account transactions. The HKD is freely convertible within the regulatory boundaries of LERS (7.75-7.85 per USD), and commercial banks execute conversions at market rates. The PRC SAFE convertibility regime applies solely to onshore China flows and is not applicable to a Hong Kong domiciled FIP. Accordingly, export earnings from vintage luxury resale may be retained in any currency, used for offshore sourcing, or settled through correspondent banks without central bank approval. Profit remittances to partners are unrestricted and are not subject to Hong Kong withholding tax on dividends. Despite this, bank-led AML/KYC screening requires documentation of the underlying transaction chain, especially for high-value pre-owned luxury goods, to ensure that free convertibility is not abused for money laundering, terrorist financing, or sanctions evasion. The commercial substance of the FIP, the beneficial ownership chain, and the provenance of traded goods will therefore be the true regulatory gateways to liquidity access.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to Hong Kong SAR. There is no statutory timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no obligation to surrender export foreign currency receipts. A Hong Kong entity may keep 100% of its export proceeds in the original currency (HKD, USD, EUR, GBP, CNH, etc.) for any duration. If the same physical goods pass through onshore China customs or are export-invoiced from a sister PRC entity, then SAFE rules on repatriation and settlement of current-account receipts will attach to the Chinese legal entity; in that separate leg, export receipts must be repatriated into China and either settled in CNY or retained in designated foreign exchange accounts in accordance with SAFE current-account administration, normally within the prescribed period and under matching trade documentation.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) - Currency Board and Banking Supervisor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not legally barred in Hong Kong if they hold the relevant HKMA Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence or a Customs and Excise Department Money Service Operator (MSO) licence; unlicensed EMIs cannot service a Hong Kong entity. For a Medium-Risk_Enhanced luxury resale FIP, the fintech EMI viability is Medium. These platforms are acceptable as front-end collection gateways (Stripe, Escrow_Pay, and licensed EMIs such as Airwallex or Neat) but must not operate as the sole treasury ledger or escrow custodian. The primary clearing relationship must sit with a Tier-1 commercial bank because the bank provides capital escrow segregation, documentary LC issuance, FX derivative confirmations, and KYC defensibility that the bank compliance committee requires. A key legal constraint is that payment aggregators generally hold customer funds under their own licences and may classify a luxury resale entity as high risk, causing rolling reserves or account holds; this must be documented in the projected cash-flow model.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the Hong Kong FIP, capital injections should be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the partners' bank accounts to the Tier-1 bank escrow account. For non-mainland partners, no HKMA approval is required; the bank will record the transaction under its statistical reporting and AMLO/KYC file. The payment reference must clearly state: FIP capital contribution, the partner name, and the partnership registration number. If a mainland Chinese entity is contributing capital, the remittance must first be supported by a SAFE Outward Direct Investment filing/accreditation, and the Chinese remitting bank will use the appropriate SAFE BOP purpose code for overseas direct investment capital contribution (commonly 621011 as updated by SAFE). The bank will also require a Chinese-side capital verification or investment registration report before releasing funds for conversion into HKD/USD. For the Hong Kong entity itself there is no statutory capital verification process; instead, the receiving bank requires a full source-of-funds dossier including bank statements, source-of-wealth evidence, the partnership agreement, CRS declarations, and a satisfactory AML system match.Outbound trade disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier purchases, consignment payouts, and commission payments. Each MT103 must reference the underlying invoice number and describe the transaction as vintage luxury goods sourcing or consignment payout. For larger auction-house or dealer acquisitions above the bank-defined threshold, a Commercial Letter of Credit (sight or term) is recommended because it bundles authentication evidence with shipping documents before payment release. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are viable for repeat suppliers with stable trade history. Customer refunds should be routed through the original receiving rail (Stripe or Escrow_Pay) where possible, and cross-border wire refunds should use MT103 with the original transaction reference. Payments to mainland Chinese suppliers should be settled in CNY using the offshore CNH clearing channel through Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited, retaining the invoice, import customs declaration, and goods description for audit. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not legally barred in Hong Kong if they hold the relevant HKMA Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence or a Customs and Excise Department Money Service Operator (MSO) licence; unlicensed EMIs cannot service a Hong Kong entity. For a Medium-Risk_Enhanced luxury resale FIP, the fintech EMI viability is Medium. These platforms are acceptable as front-end collection gateways (Stripe, Escrow_Pay, and licensed EMIs such as Airwallex or Neat) but must not operate as the sole treasury ledger or escrow custodian. The primary clearing relationship must sit with a Tier-1 commercial bank because the bank provides capital escrow segregation, documentary LC issuance, FX derivative confirmations, and KYC defensibility that the bank compliance committee requires. A key legal constraint is that payment aggregators generally hold customer funds under their own licences and may classify a luxury resale entity as high risk, causing rolling reserves or account holds; this must be documented in the projected cash-flow model.
