Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal eTAX (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/etax/index.htm). The Business Registration Office, a unit within the IRD, issues the statutory Business Registration Certificate that serves as the Hong Kong tax identification instrument.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) constituted as a Hong Kong limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), the statutory tax identifier is the Business Registration Number. Procedure: (1) register the limited partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance; (2) after commencement of business, submit the prescribed application for business registration to the Business Registration Office of the IRD, using the eTAX portal or the applicable paper channel, within one month under the Business Registration Ordinance; (3) upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the 8-digit Business Registration Number that is the TIN for all profits tax filings and IRD correspondence; (4) non-resident partners do not receive a separate Hong Kong TIN merely by holding a partnership interest, unless they themselves carry on business in Hong Kong; if any foreign corporate partner establishes a place of business in Hong Kong, that partner must also register as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance and obtain its own Business Registration Certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO), a partnership is not a separate taxpayer. The FIP computes income at partnership level but profits are assessed on the partners in accordance with their profit-sharing ratios. The limited partnership form remains transparent for Hong Kong profits tax, and there is no entity-level corporate income tax.

Available election forms

None - Hong Kong has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. The relevant statutory mechanisms are: (a) the territorial source principle, under which partners may claim that profits sourced outside Hong Kong are not chargeable to profits tax; (b) Personal Assessment for individual partners under Part VII of the IRO; and (c) the automatic two-tiered profits tax rates for unincorporated businesses where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Hong Kong imposes no broad-based VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The only indirect taxes are selective duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance and certain statutory levies; they do not apply to pet supplies e-commerce.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST/sales-tax registration threshold because no general indirect tax exists. A person carrying on business in Hong Kong must still complete business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance irrespective of turnover; the applicable fee is paid to the IRD.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong. There is no VAT/GST and therefore no 0% export VAT rate, no zero-rating claim, and no export VAT refund mechanism. Cross-border sales of pet supplies, including remote sales through Stripe and PayPal, are outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax. For Hong Kong profits tax purposes, the exporter should document the commercial chain and support any assertion that profits are sourced outside Hong Kong under the territorial source principle.

  • Hong Kong Import/Export Declaration filed electronically with the Census and Statistics Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) for the physical movement of goods
  • Commercial invoice and sales order documentation
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, air waybill, or courier consignment note
  • Shipping instructions and insurance certificates, plus origin export customs documents from mainland China when pet products are sourced from China
  • Payment records from Stripe, PayPal, bank transfer advices, or other payment rails evidencing settlement

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoice clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or API reporting mandate exists for VAT/GST purposes. General invoice and record-keeping obligations arise under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, requiring business records to be retained for at least 7 years. Import and export trade declarations are required for physical goods movements through the Government Electronic Trading Services but are not indirect-tax clearance procedures.

No VAT clawback is possible in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST is imposed. However, false or unverified trade declarations and failure to lodge required export declarations are offences under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60), punishable by fines and imprisonment. For profits tax, unsupported claims that profits are non-Hong-Kong sourced, or failure to keep proper records, may be adjusted by the IRD on audit; penalties may include additional tax of up to three times the undercharged tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, with criminal prosecution for fraudulent evasion under section 82.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed but partially convertible currency regime. Current account items, including cross-border e-commerce sales proceeds and payments for goods, are generally freely convertible once the bank has verified the underlying trade transaction through the SAFE trade-monitoring system. Capital account items remain tightly controlled. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership, an inbound foreign equity injection must first be registered with SAFE and then received in a dedicated FDI capital settlement account at a licensed domestic bank; the foreign currency may be settled into RMB only for agreed business purposes such as rent, payroll, inventory purchases, licenses, and routine operating expenses. Profit distributions to non-resident partners are current-account remittances, but only after the FIP has produced audited annual financial statements, paid applicable enterprise/individual taxes, and submitted a partnership resolution approving the distribution. Profit remittance also requires the absence of accumulated undistributed deficits, a clean tax clearance statement, and documentary evidence of the partner's residency status. In practice, the most material hurdles are mismatches between bank records and customs declarations, delays in tax clearance, and SAFE's restrictions on the use of unsettled capital, which can postpone settlement and force additional compliance submissions. Export proceeds are no longer subject to a fixed surrender ratio for ordinary current-account receipts; instead, all export proceeds must be repatriated to China and may be retained in a foreign-currency current account or settled to RMB at the exporter's discretion, subject to bank verification of the transaction background.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current trade and payment rules, export proceeds do not carry a mandatory conversion ratio; the full amount may be retained in a foreign-currency current account or settled into RMB voluntarily. For ordinary goods exports, the statutory benchmark requires the export proceeds to be repatriated and received or settled through a licensed bank within 90 days from the date of customs declaration, or within the payment terms shown in the contract, whichever is later, and any delay requires prior SAFE approval. Cross-border e-commerce export proceeds under ISIC 4791 can be received by licensed banks or qualified cross-border payment institutions using order, logistics, and clearing data; the same repatriation rule applies, and no compulsory surrender percentage is imposed.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA e-money institutions, EU EMIs, or Hong Kong stored value facilities) do not hold banking licences in mainland China and are legally barred from opening domestic corporate settlement accounts for this FIP. PBOC and SAFE permit only designated foreign-exchange banks and licensed cross-border payment institutions to process foreign exchange settlement. The practical position is a low feasibility rating for offshore EMI use as a treasury account provider. Stripe and PayPal can be used as merchant acquiring rails only if the settlement of funds flows directly into the FIP's own bank account in China through a licensed payment channel or into a properly structured Hong Kong treasury vehicle, with no attempt to establish an offshore EMI account in the name of the Chinese FIP. Any offshore EMI must be ring-fenced to treasury-purposed jurisdictions outside mainland China; otherwise the FIP will face SAFE penalties. A better architecture is using a licensed mainland cross-border payment institution or the bank's cross-border e-commerce settlement module.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant route: the non-resident partner instructs its offshore bank to send an SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's FDI Capital Settlement Account at a designated Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must use the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code for direct investment equity capital inflow and clearly state that the payment is a contribution under the Foreign-Invested Partnership Agreement, quoting the FIP's commercial registration number and SAFE registration number. The receiving bank will verify the capital against the partnership agreement and SAFE registration file, perform authenticity checks, and credit the funds only after confirmations are received. Following crediting, the FIP must engage a PRC certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report, update the SAMR system if required, and ensure the capital is reported in SAFE's cross-border balance of payments declaration. Conversion of foreign currency into RMB must occur through the bank's capital settlement account service, with every disbursement restricted to permitted business purposes and validated against invoices and contracts. Any deviation from the declared purpose, including short-term lending to partners, is prohibited and will trigger foreign exchange penalties.For paying overseas suppliers and service providers, the FIP may use SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers with proper purpose codes and supporting documents, Commercial Letters of Credit for bulk procurement of pet products and supplies, and Documentary Collections where suppliers accept open account or documentary terms. For mainland Chinese suppliers, use RMB settlement via CIPS and the FIP's basic deposit account. Each outbound foreign exchange payment must be matched to customs declarations, contracts, invoices, and proof of delivery for goods exports to satisfy SAFE's authenticity verification framework and to avoid false trade classifications. E-commerce refunds may be aggregated through licensed cross-border payment institutions, but they must be linked to the original order and cannot be structured as capital outflows or disguised repatriation.Deliverable CNY foreign exchange forward contracts with a licensed bank for genuine trade receivables and payables denominated in USD, EUR, HKD, and CNY, limited to trade-backed exposure.At-market cross-currency swaps for converting foreign-currency borrowings into CNY under the FDI capital account and for cash-flow matching of approved trade receipts, subject to SAFE registration and bank credit limits.Onshore FX options as premium-based hedging instruments for export proceeds, provided the underlying exposure is verified and the transaction is executed through a bank holding SAFE market-maker qualification.Offshore non-deliverable forwards for CNY are not directly accessible to the mainland FIP; any such hedge must be executed through a separate Hong Kong treasury vehicle and comply with HKMA and SFC regulatory requirements.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK FCA e-money institutions, EU EMIs, or Hong Kong stored value facilities) do not hold banking licences in mainland China and are legally barred from opening domestic corporate settlement accounts for this FIP. PBOC and SAFE permit only designated foreign-exchange banks and licensed cross-border payment institutions to process foreign exchange settlement. The practical position is a low feasibility rating for offshore EMI use as a treasury account provider. Stripe and PayPal can be used as merchant acquiring rails only if the settlement of funds flows directly into the FIP's own bank account in China through a licensed payment channel or into a properly structured Hong Kong treasury vehicle, with no attempt to establish an offshore EMI account in the name of the Chinese FIP. Any offshore EMI must be ring-fenced to treasury-purposed jurisdictions outside mainland China; otherwise the FIP will face SAFE penalties. A better architecture is using a licensed mainland cross-border payment institution or the bank's cross-border e-commerce settlement module.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10 million (for Local File) and HKD 100 million (for Master File) in aggregate covered transactions. A CbC report is required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds HKD 6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like the UAE, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.61A and s.61B allow the IRD to disregard artificial transactions that lack commercial substance. The territorial source principle requires that profits be derived from Hong Kong to be taxable. For a FIP engaged in e-commerce, the IRD will scrutinize whether the partnership has sufficient substance (e.g., office, employees, decision-making) in Hong Kong to justify its profits. If the partnership is a mere shell with no real activity, the IRD may re-characterize the profits as sourced elsewhere or impose penalties for tax evasion. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be performed in Hong Kong to claim territorial source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Underreporting Income

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82A, willful tax evasion is punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus a further fine of up to 3 times the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue a tax assessment based on estimated profits and impose a 10% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors (or partners in a FIP) who knowingly participate in tax evasion may be personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The IRD can pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.337 if the entity is used for fraudulent purposes. Directors may face civil asset restitution, a travel ban (blacklisting) preventing departure from Hong Kong, and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years. In severe cases, the Department of Justice may prosecute under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) for fraud.
Failure to Maintain Proper Records

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.51C, failure to keep adequate records is an offence punishable by a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months. The IRD may also estimate profits and issue a tax assessment, which the taxpayer must disprove.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for ensuring the entity maintains proper records. If the failure leads to an understatement of tax, the director may be held personally liable for the tax shortfall and penalties. The court may order the director to pay restitution and impose a travel ban. In cases of deliberate destruction of records, the director may face criminal charges under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) for perverting the course of justice.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO s.61A (artificial or fictitious transactions) to disregard the arrangement and re-attribute profits to the actual economic owner. Penalties include additional tax of up to 100% of the tax undercharged, plus a fine of up to HKD 100,000. The IRD may also impose a penalty for failure to notify chargeability to tax under s.80(2).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use a shell structure to avoid tax may be personally liable for the tax and penalties. The court may pierce the corporate veil under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.337 if the entity is a sham. Directors may face civil asset seizure, a travel ban, and criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the Inland Revenue, leading to imprisonment of up to 7 years under the common law offence of conspiracy to defraud.
