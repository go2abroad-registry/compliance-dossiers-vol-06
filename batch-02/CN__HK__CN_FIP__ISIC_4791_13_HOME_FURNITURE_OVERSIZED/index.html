Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital, no blocked escrow account, and no capital contribution deadline are prescribed under Hong Kong law for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Partners are free to agree on capital timing and amount; however, limited partners' liability cap under s. 4 of Cap. 37 is displaced if they take part in management. For banking and payment-processor onboarding, funds deposited into the partnership account will be treated as initial working capital and subjected to normal KYC/AML review; no government escrow account is required.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is mandatory for any physical warehouse or office used by the FIP under the Fire Services Department annual inspection regime. The FIP must install and maintain fire alarms, hydrants, extinguishers and emergency lighting, and obtain a valid Fire Service Installations and Equipment certificate. If the premises are located in a composite building, a Fire Safety Direction under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572) may be issued. The Buildings Department must certify structural load compliance for oversized-furniture storage racks, and the Labour Department requires a written manual-handling and workplace safety policy under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509). For a pure digital storefront with no physical warehouse or storage, enter 'Not Applicable' in the compliance register and retain the basis for that conclusion.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local tax bureau at the CN_FIP place of registration; all registrations, filings and invoice clearances are performed online via the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax Bureau).

E-government filing portal

The CN_FIP obtains its 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) upon issuance of the business license. The USCC is the entity's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Within 30 days of the business license date, the CN_FIP must complete taxpayer confirmation/registration with the local STA office through the e-Tax Bureau, complete financial and accounting system filings, bind the basic bank deposit account (tripartite tax-agency agreement), elect VAT taxpayer status, and apply for invoice issuance authorization. A non-resident partner (including a Hong Kong corporate or individual investor) must provide a duly authenticated/certified certificate of incorporation, business registration certificate or passport, registered address proof, and Chinese translation for the SAMR and tax registration files; if the non-resident partner has no mainland China TIN, the FIP must record the partner's home-jurisdiction tax identification number for Enterprise Income Tax withholding. To engage in export zero-rating, the FIP must also complete export enterprise registration with customs and complete the foreign exchange trade payment filing under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. China applies mandatory pass-through treatment to partnerships under Cai Shui [2008] No. 159: the FIP is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer and each partner is taxed on its distributive share. No check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available at the FIP level for income tax; the only entity-level election is the VAT general taxpayer versus small-scale taxpayer status, which is an indirect tax registration choice, not a pass-through election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (sales excluding VAT) over any continuous 12-month period, above which mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer applies; for a cross-border e-commerce FIP, registration as a general VAT taxpayer is necessary from inception to issue statutory VAT invoices and to access export zero-rating and input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The CN_FIP may claim the 0% VAT export rate for sales of oversized furniture where the goods are physically exported from mainland China to an overseas destination (including Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan), the sale is concluded with an overseas buyer, the export is declared under a valid customs declaration, and the export proceeds in foreign currency are repatriated to the FIP within the prescribed timeframe. Cross-border e-commerce retail and B2B export may be declared under customs supervision modes 9610, 9710 or 9810 depending on the fulfilment model. The 0% rate is a zero-rating with refund of input VAT; if the FIP cannot evidence actual export and foreign exchange receipt, the supply reverts to 13% output VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) (Export Goods Declaration Form) showing the furniture HS classification and export trade mode
  • Bill of Lading (ocean, air or multimodal) or international express shipment record proving physical exit from mainland China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Proceeds Bank Collection Voucher matched to the customs declaration value
  • Commercial invoice and packing list corresponding to the declared goods and buyer
  • Sales contract or verified online order from the non-resident customer
  • Cross-border e-commerce transaction record and end-to-end logistics tracking data

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved digital electronic invoice system under Golden Tax Phase IV using the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform. Special VAT invoices and ordinary VAT invoices must be cleared in real time; system-to-system API integration is required for high-volume issuers. Each cleared invoice receives a unique 20-digit invoice code, encrypted QR code, and anti-fraud digital stamp; the tax authority validates the invoice before the counterparty can use it for tax deduction. Voiding or red-flush invoices must be likewise filed in real time. There is no offline manual invoice clearance for a general VAT taxpayer under the fully digitalized e-invoice regime.

The STA, customs and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If documentation is missing, ambiguous or inconsistent, the tax authority may deny the 0% rate, assess output VAT at 13% plus late-payment interest, and impose late-payment penalties. Where fraud or false declaration is found, the refund will be clawed back retroactively and the FIP may be subject to administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the evaded tax; repeated or large-scale fraud is referred for criminal investigation under the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates and can lead to imprisonment up to life and confiscation of property. Moreover, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days may trigger SAFE verification, loss of future zero-rating treatment, and additional administrative sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong does not maintain any statutory foreign exchange control. The HKMA operates a Linked Exchange Rate System with the HKD/USD band at 7.75-7.85. Current-account payments such as e-commerce revenues, freight forwarding, insurance, and trade settlement are freely convertible without case-by-case approval. Capital-account operations including capital contributions to a Foreign-Invested Partnership, shareholder loans, and distributions to foreign partners are equally unrestricted in Hong Kong, provided the remitting bank has completed AML and source-of-funds checks. In practice, the only material convertibility bottleneck arises where a mainland Chinese partner subscribes capital into the HK FIP: that side will be governed by SAFE's ODI/QDLP framework, requiring a registered investment purpose and a compliant outbound remittance; non-registered transfers into HK from PRC are not permissible. Once funds are in HK, no surrender rule, no conversion quota, and no Central Bank permission applies to conversion between HKD, USD, CNY (CNH), EUR, and the major commodity currencies. Profit remittance out of Hong Kong is tax-clean, with no dividend withholding tax and no repayment approval from HKMA; the FIP need only satisfy CRS self-certification and latent-tax/qualified-corporate documentation. For an e-commerce seller of oversized furniture, sales receipts from Stripe and Affirm can be swept to the bank account in USD or settled via HKD; conversion timing is at treasury discretion, not mandated by the regulator.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no export-proceeds surrender or conversion requirement; all HK export proceeds may be retained in the original currency. If the goods are physically exported from mainland China by a PRC group manufacturing entity, SAFE requires those proceeds to be received back into a PRC-designated foreign-exchange account within the statutory verification period (generally 90 days from the customs declaration date for goods exports), and the exporter must present the customs declaration and payment evidence to the settling bank under the current-account verification system; conversion to CNY is not mandatory, but the funds must be repatriated into China unless retained under an approved offshore account arrangement.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and manager of the Linked Exchange Rate System; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China is the relevant authority only for capital transfers initiated from mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

High as a secondary collection rail, but not viable as the sole treasury mandate. Hong Kong-licensed Money Service Operators, Stored Value Facilities, and digital banks can provide multi-currency accounts and open-market FX conversion; well-known providers such as Airwallex, Statrys, Neat, or Currenxie are permissible for HK entity collection. However, such EMIs are legally constrained from issuing documentary letters of credit, standby guarantees, and structured trade finance, which are essential for oversized furniture procurement and freight settlements. The core operating and capital escrow accounts must be held with a Tier-1 licensed bank; EMIs can be used for low-value Stripe/Affirm sweeps, not for LC-backed disbursements or partnership capital verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound injections from Hong Kong or overseas partners are routed by SWIFT MT103/ISO 20022 to the FIP's Tier-1 bank account using remittance narrative 'Capital contribution - Foreign-Invested Partnership'. Hong Kong does not impose exchange-control permission or a specific foreign exchange purpose code for the receiving bank, but a capital contribution certificate signed by the partners and, where required, an auditor's capital verification report should be lodged to prove the entry is equity, not loan proceed. For capital injected by mainland Chinese partners, the PRC remitter must complete SAFE ODI registration or use an approved QDLP quota before wiring; the PRC bank will record the remittance under its outbound direct investment purpose and the receiving Hong Kong bank will require the SAFE registration certificate, partnership agreement, board/partner resolution, and source-of-funds documentation to complete KYC.Use SWIFT MT103 for open-account disbursements to suppliers and freight forwarders, attaching commercial invoices and transport references. For large or new procurement relationships, issue an Irrevocable Documentary Letter of Credit by MT700 with the Hong Kong bank as the issuing bank, protecting the FIP against mismatch between documents and terms. For established recurring suppliers, utilise Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) via the same Tier-1 bank. Local HKD payments settle through CHATS or FPS; cross-border CNH payments to PRC factories settle through the Hong Kong Offshore RMB Clearing House operated under Bank of China (Hong Kong). Card and BNPL rails from Stripe and Affirm must never be used for supplier disbursements; they are solely for consumer receipt collection.Outright deliverable FX forwards between HKD, USD, and CNH for committed trade and freight cash flows up to twelve monthsNon-deliverable forwards referencing the onshore USD/CNY fixing for exposure to PRC-related settlement where CNH deliverability is limitedCross-currency swaps between HKD, USD, and CNH for partnership capital and inventory financing up to five yearsPlain-vanilla FX call and put options for contingency hedging of oversized-furniture seasonal peaks

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: High as a secondary collection rail, but not viable as the sole treasury mandate. Hong Kong-licensed Money Service Operators, Stored Value Facilities, and digital banks can provide multi-currency accounts and open-market FX conversion; well-known providers such as Airwallex, Statrys, Neat, or Currenxie are permissible for HK entity collection. However, such EMIs are legally constrained from issuing documentary letters of credit, standby guarantees, and structured trade finance, which are essential for oversized furniture procurement and freight settlements. The core operating and capital escrow accounts must be held with a Tier-1 licensed bank; EMIs can be used for low-value Stripe/Affirm sweeps, not for LC-backed disbursements or partnership capital verification.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for a Hong Kong unincorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership; two-tier concession rates apply (7.5% on first HKD 2,000,000 assessable profits, 15% on the remainder); 16.5% standard corporate rate would apply if the IRD treats the FIP as a corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose a dividend withholding tax. A Foreign-Invested Partnership distribution is not a dividend; assessable profits are allocated to partners and taxed at their respective Hong Kong profits tax rates.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is expected under IRD DIPN 58 when the aggregate annual value of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 10,000,000; Master File preparation is expected for a Hong Kong entity within a multinational group with consolidated group turnover exceeding HKD 400,000,000; Country-by-Country Report is required for groups with consolidated revenue exceeding HKD 6,800,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations, but its territorial source doctrine and treaty anti-abuse rules require the FIP to perform real Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong. For an oversized furniture e-commerce business, CIGA include pricing and contract negotiation, inventory and logistics control, customer management, settlement risk, key staff decisions and use of the Hong Kong bank accounts. If these are controlled from mainland China and the Hong Kong entity is only an invoicing shell, the IRD can reassess Hong Kong-source profits, the mainland tax authority can assert a PE in China, and the China-HK treaty benefits can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Deliberate Misdeclaration of Hong Kong-Source Income

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82A: on conviction, fine up to HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to three years, plus an additional assessment of three times the tax undercharged. s.80(2) also imposes a HKD 10,000 fine and HKD 100 daily default penalty for failure to file the return.

Director personal liability: A director or managing partner who signs and files the false return is personally liable for the treble tax and unpaid profits tax; the IRD may obtain a certificate for departure prohibition and the court can order asset freezing, restitution, disqualification and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Sham Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.61 and s.61A authorise the Commissioner to disregard artificial and fictitious transactions, reattribute profits as Hong Kong-source and impose additional tax plus default penalties of up to three times the tax undercharged. If the sham is maintained to deceive the IRD, criminal liability under s.82A also applies.

Director personal liability: Because a partnership is not a separate juridical person, general partners and the directors of corporate partners are personally exposed to the full reconstructed tax liability; courts may pierce the corporate veil, order personal restitution of diverted profits, disqualify directors and impose custodial sentences for fraudulent trading.
Money Laundering and Structuring of Partnership Receipts

Statutory sanction: Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance Cap. 455 s.25: dealing with property known or reasonably suspected to represent crime proceeds is an offence carrying a fine up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years. Additionally, the IRD may impose tax evasion penalties and confiscation orders under the proceeds-of-crime framework.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners and beneficial owners face personal criminal conviction, confiscation of personal and corporate assets, travel restrictions, disqualification from managing Hong Kong entities and civil restitution claims; the corporate veil will be pierced where the FIP is used as a facade for the laundering of tax evasion proceeds.
