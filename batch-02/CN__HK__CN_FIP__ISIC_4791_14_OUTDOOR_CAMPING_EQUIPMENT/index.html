Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a hybrid convertibility regime under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, provided authentic documentation is presented to a designated bank. However, capital account transactions remain heavily controlled and require SAFE registration or approval. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) receiving equity contributions from Hong Kong must complete FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) information registration with SAFE before any inbound remittance; the funds enter a capital account (currency account) that can only be settled into RMB after bank verification of the intended use. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds since the 2012 deregulation, but exporters are required to repatriate foreign currency receipts into China within 180 days of export clearance and to monitor via the Trade Monitoring and Clearing System. Profit remittance to foreign partners is treated as a current account transaction once annual audited financial statements and tax clearance have been obtained, but banks will apply a higher review threshold for partnerships with HK elements to prevent disguised capital flight. In practice, dividend or partnership profit remittances are allowed for FIPs only after completing annual tax reporting for the foreign partner and submitting evidence of withholding tax payment (where applicable). The central bank and SAFE maintain an anti-money laundering (AML) and counter-financing of terrorism (CFT) framework focused on traceability of each cross-border wire and the ultimate beneficial ownership (UBO) of the entity. This creates a demanding but manageable compliance environment for a low-risk retail e-commerce FIP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export FX proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration and entered into an FX settlement account. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain 100% in foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB via a designated bank. A written application to SAFE is required if the enterprise fails to repatriate within the 180-day window due to exceptional commercial reasons.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Low for primary treasury. Fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Payoneer) are not licensed banks in China and are not authorized to hold capital-account foreign exchange or to execute the required SAFE registrations. For an inbound FIP, the initial capital contribution must be wired to a domestic tier-1 bank account in the FIP's legal name; an offshore EMI cannot serve as the custodian for that capital. EMIs may be used as ancillary collection channels for export sales from overseas e-commerce platforms, but the funds must be swept into the FIP's onshore account and reported as current account receipts. Since the foreign EMI is not regulated by the PBOC or SAFE, relying on it for profit repatriation or corporate operational disbursements would create a severe AML compliance gap and is not recommended.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Complex UBO transparency at the Hong Kong/China cross-border level; the bank may suspect opaque ownership layers

Root cause: Complex UBO transparency at the Hong Kong/China cross-border level; the bank may suspect opaque ownership layers

Defense evidence: A complete corporate organizational chart showing all shareholders to UBO level for both Hong Kong and China entities; passport and address proof for every UBO holding 10% or more; trust deeds for any trust layer; source of wealth declaration backed by personal bank statements, tax returns, and employment or business records.
Inbound capital injection for a partnership without a company seal or board resolution; banks flag non-corporate borrower ownership of proceeds

Root cause: Inbound capital injection for a partnership without a company seal or board resolution; banks flag non-corporate borrower ownership of proceeds

Defense evidence: Signed and notarized partnership agreement (Hong Kong/English versions), SAFE FDI registration form with QR code, capital contribution schedule, and a board resolution or partnership meeting minutes approving the capital injection; plus a certified copy of the Hong Kong investor's bank account statement showing the origin of funds.
High-volume e-commerce trade receipts involving Stripe/PayPal may appear as aggregated settlements with missing trade evidence

Root cause: High-volume e-commerce trade receipts involving Stripe/PayPal may appear as aggregated settlements with missing trade evidence

Defense evidence: Six months of platform settlement reports (Stripe and PayPal), transactional-level logs, export customs declarations, bills of lading, and logistics tracking records that match the clearing deposits; plus a letter from the payment processor confirming that the FIP is the verified merchant.
Profit distribution or partner loan repayments to a Hong Kong investor could trigger concerns of disguised capital flight

Root cause: Profit distribution or partner loan repayments to a Hong Kong investor could trigger concerns of disguised capital flight

Defense evidence: Annual audited financial statements, a Chinese tax filing proving the partnership profit allocation, corporate income tax clearance certificate for the FIP, withholding tax payment receipts for the Hong Kong partner, and a formal partnership resolution on profit distribution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor must transfer funds via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's designated capital account at a Chinese tier-1 bank. The wire must include the SAFE FDI registration number in field 72 (remittance information) and a purpose code matching 'Foreign-invested partnership capital contribution' under the BOP classification. Upon receipt, the bank will perform a transaction review, match the amount to the submitted capital contribution schedule, and process the capital account settlement. A certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report, which is filed in SAFE's reinvestment and use system. The FIP may thereafter transfer settled RMB to its basic operating account for trade and business expenses.For supplier payments, the FIP can use SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers of trade settlements, or commercial letters of credit (LC) issued by its tier-1 bank when dealing with strong counterparties. Documentary collections are acceptable for low-value shipments or when the supplier requires a lower-cost instrument. All disbursements must be matched to import or purchase documentation (contracts, invoices, customs records) and reported with the applicable current account purpose code.
