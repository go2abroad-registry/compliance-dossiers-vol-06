Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portals: GovHK (www.gov.hk) and IRD eTAX (www.etax.gov.hk); Customs and Excise Department (C&ED) for import/export controls; Trade and Industry Department (TID) for textile trader registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested limited partnership, first ensure the limited partnership is formed and registered with the Hong Kong Companies Registry under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37), or under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637) if constituted as a limited partnership fund. The partnership must then apply to the Business Registration Office (BRO) of the IRD within one month of commencement of business using the statutory Business Registration application (Form IRBR1). The BRO issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC); the Business Registration Number shown on the BRC is the partnership's Hong Kong tax identification number. Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT/TIN or equivalent indirect tax identifier. The partnership is fiscally transparent: partnership taxable profits are computed in the name of the partnership under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and the resulting profit share is assessed on each partner, including non-resident partners, at the applicable Profits Tax rate. There is no minimum capital requirement, non-resident ownership is permitted, and the BRC must be renewed annually; any change in partnership particulars must be reported to the BRO within one month.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box entity classification election and no hybrid mismatch election regime. A partnership is mandatorily transparent for Profits Tax; the source of profits is considered at partnership level, while the liability to tax falls on the individual partners.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Hong Kong VAT/GST or state sales and use tax; selective excise duties apply only to dutiable commodities such as tobacco, liquor, motor fuels and biodiesel under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109); cross-border goods are subject to import/export declaration controls under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — Hong Kong has no VAT/GST or sales tax registration threshold and no indirect tax registration regime. A Business Registration Certificate is mandatory within one month of carrying on business in Hong Kong under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), irrespective of turnover; this is a business and Profits Tax registration requirement, not an indirect tax threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim, no export VAT refund mechanism, and no export zero-rating substantiation requirement. Exports of fast-fashion apparel and footwear from Hong Kong are simply outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax. The relevant cross-border compliance obligation is the electronic customs export declaration and textile trader registration, not VAT. For Profits Tax, export profits may be regarded as offshore if the contracts are made, negotiated and performed outside Hong Kong, so the partnership should retain purchase contracts, sales contracts, logistics documents and banking records to substantiate any offshore profit treatment.

  • Textiles Trader Registration Certificate issued by the Trade and Industry Department (required for textile/apparel exports from Hong Kong under the Import and Export (General) Regulations)
  • Electronic Export Declaration lodged with the Customs and Excise Department through an approved Dedicated Electronic Service Provider before shipment, specifying the HS/apparel commodity classification and export particulars
  • Commercial invoice showing seller, buyer, description, quantity, unit price, total value and delivery terms
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export from Hong Kong
  • Purchase, sales, warehousing and logistics documentation supporting the offshore sourcing and fulfilment model for Profits Tax purposes
  • Bank receipts, SWIFT messages or remittance evidence demonstrating foreign exchange repatriation to the Hong Kong partnership account (evidentiary, not required for any VAT refund in Hong Kong)

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing, digital stamping, or tax-clearance-before-invoice mandate exists in Hong Kong. The Electronic Transactions Ordinance (Cap. 553) gives legal validity to electronic invoices and records. For Profits Tax purposes, records must be kept under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and electronic records are accepted. For customs purposes, import/export trade declarations must be lodged electronically through approved Dedicated Electronic Service Providers (DESPs), and apparel/textile exporters must hold a Textiles Trader Registration Certificate issued by the Trade and Industry Department; these are customs and trade control procedures, not fiscal anti-fraud clearance mechanisms.

Because Hong Kong has no VAT, there are no VAT clawbacks, export zero-rating penalties or VAT refund recovery risks. False or incorrect customs export declarations are criminal offences under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (General) Regulations, punishable by fines and imprisonment. For Profits Tax, if a foreign-invested partnership or its partners file incorrect returns or fail to keep adequate records, the IRD may raise additional assessments, impose fines, and charge additional tax up to treble the undercharged tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); in cases of fraud or wilful evasion, the IRD may extend the ordinary six-year assessment limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency-board regime in which the Hong Kong dollar is freely exchangeable against the US dollar within the 7.75-7.85 band, and the jurisdiction imposes no current-account or capital-account approvals, no surrender obligations, and no profit remittance barriers. The mainland China side, however, subjects the Foreign-Invested Partnership to a managed RMB regime: current-account items such as the settlement of export goods, cross-border e-commerce proceeds, and ordinary service fees are convertible once the bank has verified the supporting trade documentation under SAFE rules; capital-account items, including the initial foreign-capital injection, additional partnership contributions, external debt, equity divestments, and profit remittances by non-resident partners, require SAFE registration, dedicated capital accounts in a licensed bank, and bank-level diligence on the underlying economic substance. In practice, this FIP must keep the Hong Kong treasury hub separately funded in HKD or USD while ensuring every remittance from the PRC side to the Hong Kong hub is underpinned by a genuine trade, service, or investment purpose; otherwise the capital-account scrutiny will delay or block the transfer. Profit distributions from the PRC FIP to non-resident partners further require an audited financial report, a partnership distribution resolution, a tax payment certificate, and a SAFE outbound-remittance purpose code; the Hong Kong leg has no equivalent restriction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, export and cross-border e-commerce foreign-currency proceeds are subject to the SAFE Trade Monitoring System: the FIP must repatriate eligible receipts to a licensed PRC bank and match them to customs, logistics, and bill-of-lading records; there is no fixed government-mandated conversion percentage for ordinary exporters after the 2012 voluntary-settlement reform, but all receipts must be collected through an approved account and any deferred collection exceeding 90 days from the expected payment date must be reported to SAFE as an adjustment, with the FIP allowed to either retain the foreign currency in a designated account or convert it into RMB. Hong Kong SAR imposes absolutely no export surrender requirement, no conversion quota, and no foreign-exchange receipt verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the PRC foreign-exchange regulator, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the Hong Kong SAR clearing and currency-board leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Very Low as a primary banking solution, and practical only as an ancillary collection layer. Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar fintech EMIs are not licensed as deposit-taking banks in mainland China and are legally barred from maintaining a SAFE-registered capital account, a PRC basic deposit account, or a CNY operational account for this FIP; they cannot perform the statutory SAFE registration, FX purpose-code validation, or capital verification that a PRC Tier-1 commercial bank performs. Such EMIs may only process secondary payment flows, for example receiving Shopify Payments or Klarna payouts from selected marketplaces, if settlement is ultimately cleared into the FIP's licensed bank account and every fund movement is reconcilable. In Hong Kong SAR, money service operators and stored-value facility licensees are not full banking counterparts; the corporate treasury should not rely on them for credit, same-day HKD clearing, or regulatory capital protection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very Low as a primary banking solution, and practical only as an ancillary collection layer. Such EMIs may only process secondary payment flows, for example receiving Shopify Payments or Klarna payouts from selected marketplaces, if settlement is ultimately cleared into the FIP's licensed bank account and every fund movement is reconcilable. In Hong Kong SAR, money service operators and stored-value facility licensees are not full banking counterparts; the corporate treasury should not rely on them for credit, same-day HKD clearing, or regulatory capital protection.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax on resident corporate partners; the Foreign-Invested Partnership itself is a pass-through entity and is not a separate enterprise income taxpayer under PRC law.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC withholding rate on dividend-equivalent outbound distributions to non-resident enterprises; for the pass-through FIP, non-resident corporate partner profit allocations are treated as PRC-source enterprise income and subject to 10% withholding under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 37.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice 42/2016, a Chinese Local File is mandatory when annual aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million; a Master File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 10 billion. In addition, annual related-party transaction reporting is mandatorily filed whenever the FIP has controlled transactions with foreign related parties above the statutory threshold.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply offshore Economic Substance Regulations to a Chinese FIP, but economic substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and through the permanent establishment analysis in the China-HK DTA. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the Hong Kong partner contract negotiation, risk bearing, inventory ownership and strategic management must be demonstrated in Hong Kong. A Hong Kong pass-through without office, employees and substantive decision-making will be denied treaty benefits and may be treated as having a permanent establishment in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and Artificial Profit Shifting through Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Article 41 and Article 47, the tax authority may reallocate profit to the FIP and impose additional corporate income tax plus a special interest adjustment. Failure to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation can trigger fines under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, and a presumption that profits were shifted to overseas related parties.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Chinese general partner and the Hong Kong investing partner may be subject to piercing of the corporate veil where the absence of substance is used to evade tax. Consequences include civil restitution of diverted profits, inclusion on the PRC tax blacklist triggering travel bans, and criminal imprisonment of up to seven years for the responsible officers under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for serious tax evasion.
Tax Evasion by Under-Reporting E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, this constitutes tax evasion and is sanctioned by an order to repay the tax underpaid, plus a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. A daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% accrues on unpaid tax. Criminal liability arises where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax due is evaded, punishable by imprisonment of up to seven years and criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, chief financial officers and managers who design or approve false declarations are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax and interest. The tax authority may blacklist responsible individuals, preventing them from leaving China until all tax liabilities are settled. Criminal conviction can additionally impose imprisonment and a prohibition on serving as a director or manager.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion and Illegal Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Article 37 and Article 39, the FIP as withholding agent is liable to pay the unwithheld amount, with a penalty under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 69 of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax. The non-resident corporate partner remains primarily liable for the unpaid tax, and the FIP may be ordered to freeze distributions pending tax clearance.

Director personal liability: The managing partner and finance directors may be held personally liable for the unwithheld tax as responsible withholding officers, with civil asset restitution enforced from personal estates. Exit bans and blacklisting by the tax authority prevent the responsible directors from travelling abroad. Where repatriation is structured to conceal tax evasion, criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 may result in imprisonment of up to seven years, asset confiscation and director disqualification.
