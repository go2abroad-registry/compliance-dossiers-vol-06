Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a foreign-invested partnership and no blocked escrow account requirement. Partner contributions are contractual and must be paid as set out in the partnership deed; all subscription monies must be credited to the partnership dedicated bank account before trading and evidenced by bank confirmation for anti-money-laundering purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the national e-Tax electronic tax administration portal and the China International Trade Single Window for export/customs data integration

E-government filing portal

1. File the foreign-invested partnership formation dossier with the local Administration for Market Regulation under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and Foreign Investment Law. 2. Obtain the partnership business license, which contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax purposes. 3. The USCC registration is automatically shared with the STA, completing the baseline national tax registration; within 30 days of license issuance, the partnership must file supplementary tax registration with the competent in-charge tax authority, including bank account details, accounting rules, legal representative information, and VAT taxpayer status. 4. If annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, or if the partnership elects general taxpayer status voluntarily, register as a general VAT taxpayer and obtain the digital tax control UKey for issuance of special VAT invoices. 5. Non-resident partners must disclose their home-jurisdiction tax identification numbers to the partnership; where the partnership makes China-source allocations subject to withholding, the partnership must register as a withholding agent, and the non-resident partners must obtain a Chinese taxpayer identification number if required for treaty relief or compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election; partnership pass-through taxation is mandatory for Enterprise Income Tax purposes. The only available operational elections are VAT status elections: remaining a small-scale taxpayer below the RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover threshold, or voluntarily registering as a general VAT taxpayer; and, for export transactions, selecting the exemption-and-refund method versus the exemption-without-refund method for input VAT on exported goods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; every entity with a Unified Social Credit Code is automatically placed on the basic VAT tax registration, and the simplified small-scale taxpayer method is available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The sale of physical goods from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR is treated as an export for PRC VAT purposes and qualifies for 0% VAT zero-rating, provided the exporter is a VAT-registered taxpayer (normally a general VAT taxpayer), the goods actually depart the mainland customs territory, the export declaration is filed through the China International Trade Single Window (including cross-border e-commerce retail export supervision code 9610 or B2B export code 9710, as applicable), consideration is received from the Hong Kong buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated into China within the prescribed period. Hong Kong SAR itself levies no VAT, GST, or sales tax on the importation of goods, so no separate Hong Kong indirect tax registration or reverse-charge filing is required for the buyer side.

  • Customs export declaration (electronic bill of entry/export clearance data) issued by the Customs of the People's Republic of China through the International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or logistics carrier receipt evidencing physical departure of the goods from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange collection receipt or inward remittance certificate (bank export proceeds receipt) evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds to China
  • Sales order and payment settlement records from the e-commerce payment rails (Stripe/PayPal) for the 9610/9710 export models
  • Export VAT digital e-invoice matched to the customs export declaration data

All VAT invoices are issued through the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice system (digital e-invoice platform). Invoice records are transmitted to the tax authority in real time; special VAT invoices must be verified through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform; and export invoices are automatically cross-matched with customs export declarations, foreign exchange collection data, and export tax rebate files. There is no physical fiscal stamp, but encryption, tax UKeys, digital e-invoice codes, and real-time e-Tax reporting operate as the mandatory anti-fraud clearance mechanism.

The STA, customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct joint data matching under the Golden Tax system, cross-validating VAT invoices, customs export declarations, logistics records, and foreign exchange receipts. Any unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating claim results in immediate refund clawback, late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and an administrative fine equal to 50% to 500% of the tax involved, or one to five times the fraudulently obtained refund under Article 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund and false VAT invoice issuance may result in imprisonment and additional fines under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a controlled current-account regime and a regulated capital-account regime. The CN_FIP, once established onshore, is subject to SAFE monitoring for capital contributions, foreign debt, trade receipts, and outward profit remittances. Current-account receipts and payments must be supported by genuine trade or service contracts; capital-account transactions, including foreign partner capital injections into the partnership and any subsequent liquidation distributions, require registration and bank-level purpose-code verification. There is no general free convertibility for speculative balance-sheet flows. By contrast, Hong Kong operates as a fully convertible financial jurisdiction with no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no statutory restriction on capital or profit repatriation for account holders. The Hong Kong dollar is freely exchangeable against major reserve currencies through the Linked Exchange Rate System administered by the HKMA. Current-account payments and capital-account flows in HKD, USD, EUR, GBP, and offshore CNH do not require pre-approval within the Hong Kong banking system. The material friction arises only when funds cross into or out of the onshore Mainland Chinese regulatory perimeter. For the CN_FIP, registered capital contributions must be routed into a SAFE-monitored capital settlement account using an approved FX purpose code, while profit remittances require audited financial statements, tax settlement certification, and a bank payment order. Principal and dividends may be freely repatriated from Hong Kong, but onshore RMB conversion remains subject to trade or capital-account genuineness review. This asymmetry means the Hong Kong layer is the flexible treasury hub and the Mainland China layer is the controlled settlement periphery.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign-currency receipts; all export proceeds may be retained in HKD or foreign currency, freely converted, or used for offshore trade payments. For the onshore Mainland China segment of the CN_FIP, SAFE requires export receipts to be repatriated into a designated foreign-exchange account or settled through a domestic bank using the Trade Administration Information System; no fixed percentage must be surrendered to the central bank, but receipts must be reconciled with customs declarations within the prescribed timeframe, typically 180 days for immediate proceeds and extendable to approximately 210 days with a SAFE-registered deferral, and any abnormal mismatch generates a SAFE investigation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the central FX regulator for the CN_FIP's onshore Mainland China operations; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for the offshore Hong Kong treasury layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Medium for this CN_FIP structure. Hong Kong-licensed SVF and Money Service Operator fintech platforms can handle low-value operational collections, including Stripe/PayPal settlements and small recurring payables, but they are not authorized banks and cannot open a SAFE-registered onshore capital account for the CN_FIP, cannot issue documentary letters of credit, and may not provide the trade verification or capital-source audit trail required by Mainland China regulators. For offshore digital EMIs, the legal constraint is material: they typically lack a full banking license and cannot participate in CIPS or the HKMA settlement systems, and onshore Chinese authorities do not recognize them as regulated FX banks. Their use should be limited to secondary operational collection wallets, with a daily sweep to a licensed tier-1 bank; the principal cross-border clearing and FX rails must remain with licensed banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital contributions for the CN_FIP must first satisfy SAFE foreign-invested partnership registration and obtain a domestic bank account notice. The compliant route is a customer-originated SWIFT MT103 remittance from the Hong Kong treasury account to the onshore SAFE-monitored capital settlement account, using the FX purpose code designated for non-resident paid-in capital to a foreign-invested partnership. The receiving bank verifies the cash-in, matches the remitter and beneficiary legal names, and records the SAFE registration reference before crediting. The onshore partner then engages a certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report for the partnership. RMB injections may be routed via offshore CNH conversion and cross-border RMB clearing through CIPS; foreign-currency injections are converted onshore at the prevailing spot rate through the receiving bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 680 million group consolidated revenue and HKD 50 million in annual controlled transactions trigger Local/Master File; CbC report threshold is HKD 6.8 billion consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not impose ESR as in offshore centres. Instead, the source principle serves a similar function: profits are taxable only if they arise in or are derived from Hong Kong. For an online maternity/baby e-commerce business, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include: procurement, order acceptance, payment processing, and delivery. If these CIGA are located in mainland China, the profits are non-HK sourced and exempt; if executed onshore in Hong Kong, they are taxable. Transfer pricing must align profit allocation with actual economic substance and functional risks.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Section 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Cap. 112: upon summary conviction, a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years plus treble the amount of tax undercharged. Section 80: late or incorrect filing, fine up to HK$10,000 plus a further 10% of tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: The general partners or directors who signed the return are personally and jointly liable for any false statement; the corporate veil will be pierced to confiscate personal assets to satisfy the tax and penalties. The Tax Court can issue a travel ban / departure prohibition order under the IRO for tax evasion; civil recovery actions may extend to the director's personal bank accounts and property.
Transfer Pricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under IRO ss. 50AAK and 58C and the TP documentation rules, the IRD may impose penalties up to HK$50,000 and a daily default penalty of HK$500 for continued non-compliance; in addition, the tax shortfall may be increased by 50% if the IRD considers that the failure was deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve transactions without fair compensation or who destroy TP records may be held personally accountable for the tax adjustment, fines and interest. Courts may issue a disqualification order and a personal asset freezing order for active concealment; personal criminal liability for false records arises under Section 82D of the IRO.
Anti-Money Laundering / Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), dealing with criminal property carries a maximum fine of HK$5,000,000 and 14 years' imprisonment. The Companies Registry may strike off the partnership and disqualify directors for up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors/partners who control the shell entity face personal criminal liability, restitution orders to victims, and confiscation of proceeds under the confiscation regime. The authorities can apply for travel restrictions (blacklisting) to prevent departure, and courts may pierce the veil to enforce personal liability against the directors' assets for the debts of the shell.
