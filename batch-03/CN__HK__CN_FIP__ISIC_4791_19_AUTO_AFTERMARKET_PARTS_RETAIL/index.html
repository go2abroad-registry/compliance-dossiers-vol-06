Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China, with the Business Registration Office under the IRD; electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (https://www.gov.hk/en/residents/taxes/etax/). The IRD is the sole competent tax authority for Hong Kong Profits Tax, Property Tax, Salaries Tax, and Business Registration. The State Taxation Administration of Mainland China has no direct jurisdiction over the Hong Kong FIP unless the FIP creates a permanent establishment or taxable presence in Mainland China.

E-government filing portal

For the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) formed as a limited partnership in Hong Kong, the statutory tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN), not a TIN or VAT ID. The registration procedure is as follows: (1) register the limited partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or the applicable partnership statute, supplying the partnership name, nature of business, registered office in Hong Kong, address of the general partner, and the statement of limited partners; (2) within one month of commencement of business in Hong Kong, apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for business registration using the statutory application form for registration of a business or partnership and pay the prescribed registration fee and levy; (3) for every foreign non-resident partner, provide certified passport or overseas certificate of incorporation, proof of residential or registered address, and, where the partner is a non-resident corporation, a certificate of good standing or equivalent corporate existence document, together with a power of attorney appointing the Hong Kong-based general partner or authorised representative; (4) upon approval, the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate and the Certificate of Registration of Business and assigns an 8-digit BRN to be shown on invoices, import/export documents, and business correspondence; (5) because the partnership is transparent, the BRN is used for the FIP's administrative tax file while each partner separately reports their share of the partnership's assessable profits in their own Profits Tax filings. If the FIP employs staff, the partnership must also register as an employer for payroll and Mandatory Provident Fund purposes.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box election or any hybrid-mismatch entity election. The FIP is mandatorily transparent for Hong Kong Profits Tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the partnership itself is not chargeable to tax, and each partner is assessed on their distributive share of the partnership's Hong Kong-sourced profits. None - Hong Kong SAR has no VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. Indirect tax exposure is limited to stamp duty and duty on a small class of dutiable commodities (liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol), none of which applies to online retail sales of automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR because the jurisdiction does not impose VAT or GST on exports or on supplies of automotive parts; there is no zero-rating claim to make and no refund of irrecoverable VAT. If the fulfilment logistics involve goods exported from Mainland China by a PRC exporting entity, the PRC VAT zero-rating and export-rebate regime may apply to that PRC exporter under the Provisional Regulations on Value-Added Tax and the export goods and services zero-rate and refund catalogue. The Hong Kong FIP itself cannot claim PRC export zero-rating because it is not a PRC VAT taxpayer. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Chinese legal framework the RMB is fully convertible only for genuine current-account items; trade in goods and services supported by real contracts, invoices and customs declarations can routinely cross the border through SAFE-designated banks, while capital-account transactions remain subject to registration, FDI filing, quota supervision and in some cases SAFE approval. For the subject Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), foreign partner contributions are admitted through the SAFE FDI registration process into a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account; the subsequent conversion into RMB must follow the approved business purpose and is monitored on the Electronic Foreign Exchange Administration Platform. Profit remittance back to the Hong Kong SAR partner is treated as a current-account investment income remittance and requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificate from the state tax authority, a valid distribution resolution among the partners and the underlying capital contribution record, but there is no mandatory surrender ratio; SAFE abolished compulsory export surrender for goods trade, allowing 100 percent foreign-currency retention, but all export receipts must be repatriated into a Chinese bank account and verified. Within the Hong Kong SAR, the HKMA guarantees full free convertibility for HKD under the currency-board discipline, with no surrender or remittance restrictions, making the Hong Kong treasury layer a clean offshore liquidity nexus for settling cross-border e-commerce cash flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Notice No. 5 for Goods Trade administration, all export proceeds from the online retail of automotive aftermarket parts must be collected into the FIP's designated foreign-exchange current account or a licensed cross-border payment institution's clearing account within 90 calendar days from the date of customs declaration for the physical goods cycle. The mandatory conversion ratio is nil, meaning 100 percent may be retained in foreign currency, but the bank must verify each receipt against the SAFE electronic goods-trade monitoring system using commercial invoices, customs export data, delivery tracking and a transaction-level settlement ledger. Where PayPal, Stripe or an equivalent aggregator is used, the licensed Chinese payment intermediary must provide a break-down detail file so the onshore bank can identify the underlying order; any collection that remains outstanding beyond 90 days requires a formal deferred-collection filing with proofs of credit terms and counterparty aging.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the supreme monetary and prudential authority, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated regulator for foreign-exchange and cross-border capital flows, and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the currency-board supervisor of the offshore HKD/USD banking leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Direct account opening at an offshore digital EMI such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex or Payoneer for the mainland-incorporated FIP is legally impracticable. PBOC and SAFE require domestic-entity settlement to be routed through a licensed Chinese commercial bank or a licensed bank-backed cross-border payment institution, and foreign EMIs are not recognised as deposit-taking or clearing institutions in China; their IBAN or sort-code rails cannot satisfy SAFE verification, customs reconciliation or capital-account controls. PayPal may be used only through a licensed Chinese payment intermediary that sweeps funds into the onshore FIP bank account with a full transaction-level report, while Stripe does not formally support settlement to mainland Chinese entity accounts. Overall viability is Low for direct onshore FIP use and Medium for an affiliated Hong Kong SAR treasury vehicle that then injects capital on a fully documented basis; in all cases the funds must be consolidated through the FIP's licensed bank channel before treasury deployment.

PayPal may be used only through a licensed Chinese payment intermediary that sweeps funds into the onshore FIP bank account with a full transaction-level report, while Stripe does not formally support settlement to mainland Chinese entity accounts. Overall viability is Low for direct onshore FIP use and Medium for an affiliated Hong Kong SAR treasury vehicle that then injects capital on a fully documented basis; in all cases the funds must be consolidated through the FIP's licensed bank channel before treasury deployment.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard corporate income tax rate (China); the FIP itself is fiscally transparent and not subject to CIT at the partnership level. Non-resident corporate partners are taxed on their distributable share either at 25% if the partnership constitutes a PE, or 10% withholding on certain passive income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (statutory under CIT Law, Article 37, for non-resident enterprise without PE deriving dividend income from Chinese resident enterprises; note that partnership profit distributions to partner are not dividends if the partner has a PE – such distributions are PE profits already taxed at 25%).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40,000,000 (minimum local file threshold for 'other related-party transactions' under SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42; tangible asset transfers above RMB 200,000,000; intangible and financial asset transfers above RMB 100,000,000 each; master file required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 2,000,000,000).

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulation akin to offshore jurisdictions. However, the GAAR in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law empowers tax authorities to disregard entities lacking reasonable commercial purpose and substance. For the FIP to be respected, it must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, including: physical office or e-commerce operational facilities in the mainland, staff managing procurement, logistics and customer service, and strategic decision-making functions. If the FIP acts merely as a conduit to shift profits to Hong Kong without substance, the Chinese authorities will recharacterise the structure and deny treaty benefits. In addition, the Hong Kong partner seeking DTA relief must satisfy Hong Kong's economic substance requirements (as per the Inland Revenue Ordinance's territorial source concept).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Reporting of E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC (tax evasion): if the amount evaded is relatively large (≥ RMB 50,000) and exceeds 10% of the tax due, imprisonment up to 3 years; if huge (≥ RMB 500,000) and exceeds 30%, imprisonment 3-7 years, plus fines. Administrative: 50% to 5 times the tax evaded and daily surcharge of 0.05% on unpaid tax (Tax Collection Administration Law, Articles 63-64).

Director personal liability: Directors/officers who knowingly participate are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution (unpaid tax, interest, penalties). Criminal prosecution may target the responsible individuals; they face confiscation of personal assets, travel bans, and blacklisting (失信联合惩戒).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Insufficient Documentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Article 48: tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment and impose interest on the underpaid tax at the base lending rate plus 5 percentage points (Special Tax Adjustment measures, SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42). Failure to provide documentation triggers penalty of RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000 (General Rules of Tax Collection). In serious cases, additional 25% penalty on underpaid tax under Tax Collection Law Article 69.

Director personal liability: Directors can be held personally liable for negligence leading to understated tax where the entity becomes insolvent. In extreme cases, fraudulent transfer pricing can be prosecuted as tax evasion, subjecting directors to criminal liability and personal monetary restitution. They may also be banned from leaving China until the tax is settled.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 (general anti-abuse rule) and Article 10 of SAT Bulletin 2018 No. 1 (denial of treaty benefits): the tax authority can recharacterise the structure, reallocate income to the true economic substance, impose full 25% tax or 25% plus interest, and deny all tax treaty benefits. Under the Multilateral Convention (MLI), the Principal Purpose Test may disallow treaty relief. Penalty: 50%-100% of the tax shortfall if classified as a false commercial arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors of the FIP may face personal liability if they knowingly signed false declarations of substance. Under the joint punishment mechanism for tax-related dishonesty, directors may be subject to travel bans (including denial of exit visa), entry bans to China, and disqualification from directorship. If the abuse leads to fraudulent evasion, criminal liability for tax evasion may attach, with imprisonment up to 7 years and personal civil liability for restitution.
