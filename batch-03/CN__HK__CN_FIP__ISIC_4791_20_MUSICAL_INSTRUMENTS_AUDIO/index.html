Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform at etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

After market registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation, the FIP completes tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the STA serves as the VAT taxpayer identification number. A foreign non-resident partner does not receive a separate Chinese TIN solely because of its partnership interest; the FIP must file non-resident contractor/withholding registration in the STA non-resident taxpayer module using the partner's foreign tax identification number, legal name, address, and certified proof of Hong Kong SAR residence, enabling the STA to issue a non-resident taxpayer filing number for China-source withholding obligations. The FIP must also register for VAT, payroll withholding, and export tax refund (exemption) eligibility through the same electronic portal.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. China does not maintain a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. The partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent for enterprise income tax; partners are taxed on their allocated profits. The FIP may elect general VAT taxpayer status, but that is an indirect tax accounting election and does not alter pass-through income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis VAT registration threshold; every entity carrying on taxable supplies in China must obtain tax registration. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below this level the FIP is eligible for small-scale taxpayer treatment with applicable reduced rates and related exemptions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported tangible goods sold to overseas customers and physically removed from China through official customs procedures are eligible for the 0% export VAT rate, with entitlement to refund of attributable input VAT. The FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer, hold a valid export customs declaration, demonstrate actual shipment and delivery offshore, collect the export proceeds from a non-mainland counterparty within the statutory repatriation period, and file an export tax refund (exemption) application with STA. Goods sold or consumed in China remain taxable at 13% or the applicable reduced rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Customs export goods declaration/bill of entry record) issued through the General Administration of Customs single window
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or other original/electronic transport document evidencing export shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the musical instruments and studio audio equipment
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, and proof of online platform transaction
  • VAT

The FIP must issue VAT invoices through the STA Full-Digital Electronic Invoice (fully digitalized e-invoice) platform, which transmits invoice data in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV system; the STA platform performs anti-fraud digital authentication, invoice quota control, and centralized invoice verification. Legacy paper VAT invoices are allowed only where specifically authorized, and all invoice data is subject to electronic clearance and matching against VAT returns and export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates a free currency convertibility regime with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and unrestricted current and capital account flows under the Linked Exchange Rate System. However, the mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is subject to the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, where current account items such as trade receipts and service payments enjoy de facto convertibility after legitimate underlying transaction verification, while capital account items remain tightly controlled. The FIP's foreign investment capital contribution must enter through a SAFE-monitored capital account, be registered under FDI rules, and be verified by the receiving domestic bank before conversion into CNY. RMB profits generated by the FIP can be remitted offshore as investment income only after annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and documentary proof of partnership profits; Chengdu, Beijing, Shanghai and other regional SAFE branches may apply reinforced substance checks for pass-through partnerships. Export proceeds from cross-border e-commerce sales of musical instruments and audio gear must be repatriated into China through designated bank channels within the statutory timeframe, though voluntary settlement is permitted; the proceeds cannot be silently parked offshore for unrelated purposes. Dividend-like profit distributions to foreign partners face an additional practical hurdle: the bank will require the FIP's tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, a resolution of the general partner, and a SAFE-verified remittance application. Capital account conversion for debt, equity, or intra-group financing also requires SAFE registration or approval, and funds converted into CNY must be used strictly in accordance with the registered business purpose. Thus, while Hong Kong is fully convertible, the FIP's treasury operations are anchored by SAFE controls and treaty-based withholding tax considerations, making the overall regime stringent only on the mainland side of the structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, export-related foreign currency receipts from cross-border e-commerce and physical goods sales must be repatriated into a designated domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days of shipment or collection; since 2012 the mandatory surrender requirement was replaced by voluntary settlement, so the FIP may retain the foreign currency in its current account or convert to CNY at prevailing rates, but full repatriation and SAFE trade verification via the bank is mandatory. Hong Kong SAR imposes no surrender or repatriation rules on export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-cautionary for any EMI attempting to replace local licensed banking. Stripe and PayPal can be used as offshore collection aggregators, but they cannot legally provide onshore CNY clearing or SAFE-compliant capital account services for a mainland FIP. Fintech EMIs such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer may facilitate cross-border receivable collection from marketplace sales in foreign currency, yet the FIP will still need a licensed domestic bank account in China for statutory capital verification, tax payment, VAT refunds, payroll, and supplier settlements. Non-bank payment institutions operating in mainland China require a license from the PBOC, and those without a banking license are prohibited from engaging in foreign exchange settlement for import/export trade or maintaining resident RMB clearing accounts. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should use EMIs only as a supplementary collection rail for overseas consumer payments, with all compliant funds swept into the licensed domestic bank account through the EMI's licensed money-transfer partner within one to two business days.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner or parent will remit foreign currency via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's opening bank in mainland China, with the payment narrative identifying the FIP name, partnership registration number, the word 'Capital Contribution', and the applicable SAFE BOP transaction purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection. The receiving bank will credit the funds to the FIP's capital escrow account, notify SAFE through the Foreign Exchange Account Management System, and require submission of the FIP business license, FDI registration certificate, capital contribution schedule, and partner identity documents before converting to CNY. After conversion, the CNY must be placed in the designated capital account settlement reserve and used only for legitimate business expenses such as inventory purchases, logistics, marketing, and operational payments; cash withdrawal from the capital account is prohibited.Export collections from Stripe and PayPal should be swept through a licensed payment intermediary to the FIP's domestic foreign-exchange settlement account under the cross-border e-commerce export trade code, with each remittance matched to a customs declaration or e-commerce export list. Supplier payments inside China will use CNY clearing via the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) with electronic bank drafts or online corporate banking transfers. For international supplier payments, the FIP will use SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers with the underlying justified trade documentation; letters of credit and documentary collections are available for high-value audio equipment purchases, using the HSBC or Bank of China cross-border trade desk. All outbound remittances must pass the bank's anti-money laundering transaction monitoring, and any payment exceeding the bank's threshold will trigger a supporting document review under SAFE's current-account verification rules.

Turnover: For a Hong Kong-incorporated company, an independent certified public accountant audit is statutorily mandatory when the company does not qualify as a small private company under the Companies Ordinance, Cap. 622. The exemption thresholds require satisfaction of any two of the following three conditions for the financial year: annual total revenue not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000); total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000); and average number of employees during the year not exceeding 50. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong law does not prescribe a fixed statutory threshold in the Inland Revenue Ordinance; however, the IRD Transfer Pricing Practice Note requires contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 10 million per transaction category; CbC reporting is required for multinational enterprises with consolidated group revenue of at least HKD 6.8 billion

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies a territorial source doctrine rather than a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute; nevertheless, the IRD may invoke s.61A (general anti-abuse provision) and s.50AAP (transfer pricing) to disregard or recharacterise arrangements lacking genuine economic presence. For e-commerce activities, the taxpayer must prove that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as online store management, inventory risk, payment settlement, marketing, and customer contracts are actually performed by staff and premises in Hong Kong, not merely by the Chinese manufacturing parent; otherwise the HK partnership may be treated as a tax shell and profits reallocated to China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(2) and s.82(1): automatic fine of HKD 10,000 plus daily default fines; where willful fraud is proven, fine up to HKD 50,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, and a tax penalty of 3 times the undercharged tax

Director personal liability: A director or general partner who executes an incorrect return without reasonable excuse is personally liable; courts can pierce the corporate veil to enforce civil restitution of the unpaid tax plus interest; IRD may obtain travel restriction orders and blacklist the individual under the departure prevention regime; criminal conviction may lead to disqualification from being a director under the Companies Ordinance and imprisonment
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAP and s.61A empower the IRD to make primary and secondary transfer pricing adjustments, reallocate profits from HK to the real economic substance in China, and deny the partnership's offshore profit claims; penalties for failure to keep contemporaneous records can reach HKD 50,000 plus daily default fines; undercharged tax can be recovered with up to 3 times additional penalty

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced if the HK FIP is a mere facade for the Chinese principal; directors and general partners become personally jointly and severally liable for the group's tax debt; civil asset restitution may be ordered through Mareva injunctions and asset preservation orders; travel bans (blacklisting) are available where unpaid tax exceeds a prescribed threshold; criminal liability for conspiracy to defraud the revenue carries imprisonment of up to 7 years
AML and Beneficial Ownership Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), failure to comply with customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations is punishable by fines up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2018, failure to file CbC notification or tax returns can trigger a maximum fine of HKD 50,000 for a defaulting officer

Director personal liability: Hong Kong courts will lift the corporate veil for sham arrangements and impose personal liability on directors and partners for tax debts, unpaid penalties, and the costs of revenue investigation; civil remedies include assets freezing orders, civil restitution, and bankruptcy against the individual; the Hong Kong Immigration Department may impose a travel prohibition/blacklist; criminal sanctions include forfeiture of assets and imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering offences under Cap. 615
