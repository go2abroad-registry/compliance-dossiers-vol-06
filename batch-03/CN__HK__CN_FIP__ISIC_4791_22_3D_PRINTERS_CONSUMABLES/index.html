Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law does not prescribe a statutory minimum paid-up capital, no share capital is required for a limited partnership, and no blocked escrow account is mandated. Capital contributions, drawdown schedules and capital account mechanics are governed by the executed Partnership Agreement. If the PRC investor is a corporate partner, the PRC foreign-investment information filing must be completed under the Foreign Investment Law, but no escrow or paid-up capital condition is imposed by Hong Kong.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for pure online retail, because no fire safety permit is issued for e-commerce office activities. For the current scope, no civil defense fire permit or structural load inspection is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Taxation) via etax.chinatax.gov.cn. For the Hong Kong SAR component, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the distinct competent tax authority and operates the eTax portal; however, no Hong Kong VAT/GST/sales tax is imposed on the FIP for indirect tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC foreign-invested partnership (CN_FIP), the foreign and PRC investors must first register the foreign-invested partnership with the local office of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtain a Business License and an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days of obtaining the Business License, the FIP must complete integrated tax registration with the STA through the China Electronic Tax Administration, activating its PRC taxpayer identity, VAT taxpayer profile and invoice registration; the USCC serves as the sole PRC tax identification number (TIN/VAT ID) and is used for VAT, enterprise income tax, individual income tax withholding and export tax rebate filings. Foreign non-resident partners are separately registered with the STA as non-resident withholding taxpayers where applicable. If a separate Hong Kong entity or branch is established, apply to the Hong Kong Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate bearing the IRD Business Registration Number; Hong Kong does not issue a VAT ID because it imposes no value-added tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; the PRC foreign-invested partnership is tax-transparent for PRC enterprise income tax and individual income tax, meaning the partnership itself is not an enterprise income tax taxpayer and partners are taxed on their allocated taxable income at the partner level. For VAT, the FIP is itself a VAT taxpayer and must invoice, charge and remit VAT in its own name. The Hong Kong territorial-source profits tax classification applies separately if the FIP carries on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong; a Hong Kong sourced income position does not alter pass-through eligibility.

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or any hybrid-mismatch entity classification election to treat the FIP as a corporation for PRC tax purposes; profit allocation is based on the partnership agreement and economic substance, not an entity tax election. For VAT only, the taxpayer may elect general VAT taxpayer status if below the mandatory threshold or may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, but this is not a partnership classification election and does not alter the pass-through income tax treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

General VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory once annual taxable turnover for sales of goods exceeds RMB 5,000,000; a newly established FIP with projected taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer when completing SAMR and STA tax registration. Entities with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 are generally small-scale VAT taxpayers unless they elect to become general VAT taxpayers. Any taxpayer making taxable supplies, including export sales qualifying for zero-rating, must complete VAT registration with the STA; there is no separate VAT threshold below which export sales can remain unregistered.

All PRC VAT invoices must be issued through STA-controlled fiscal systems; the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice environment requires real-time filing of invoice data to the tax authority and use of official e-invoice API integrations for business-to-business sales. Special VAT invoices are transmitted through the National VAT Invoice Management and Verification system and carry an official digital stamp; paper invoices, where still permitted, require UKey issuance and real-time reporting. Customs export clearance is handled through the China International Trade Single Window with full data exchange among customs, the STA, banks and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange; export VAT refund and exemption applications are filed electronically in the STA Export Tax Refund module and are cross-checked against customs declarations, transportation documents, purchase invoices and foreign-exchange receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, pegging the Hong Kong dollar to the US dollar via a band. There are no foreign exchange surrender requirements, no prior approval on current or capital account transactions, and profit remittances are free of FX control. However, this entity is a China-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), and its onshore operations are therefore subject to mainland China's Foreign Exchange Administration. The renminbi is not fully convertible on the capital account. Capital injections from foreign partners into the FIP must be registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and may only be converted into local currency upon presentation of authentic commercial documents. Current account payments such as royalties, service fees and supplier payments are permitted with relevant contracts and invoices. Dividend/profit remittance to non-resident partners requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the PRC tax authority, and a bank verification of the partnership's capital contribution. Online retail export proceeds, when routed through Chinese payment gateways, must be repatriated and settled in a domestic designated account; although full retention in foreign currency is allowed, each transaction must bear supporting order, logistics and customs declarations to satisfy the bank's compliance review. This creates a practical operational hurdle for frequent, low-value e-commerce sales, necessitating robust reconciliation systems.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a Hong Kong-incorporated entity, there is no surrender obligation; export earnings can be retained in any currency without conversion quota. For the underlying Chinese FIP, SAFE Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration under Trade in Goods provide a general 90-day repatriation requirement from the date of export customs declaration. The enterprise may either keep the proceeds in a foreign exchange account or convert into RMB at prevailing rates. Mandatory conversion was abolished in 2012; the conversion ratio is zero unless the enterprise itself opts to sell. The FIP must file monthly export receipts with SAFE and ensure that the amounts match the customs data; any discrepancy of more than 5% triggers an inquiry.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to Very Low. Although Hong Kong has developed fintech infrastructure, digital-only banks (virtual banks) are licensed under the Banking Ordinance, and they generally target retail/SME customers rather than cross-border partnership structures. Entities such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer are electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating under scattered licenses; they are not authorized by the HKMA to offer full corporate banking services to a Chinese FIP. The HKMA's Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence covers payment tools, not corporate deposit accounts. Moreover, the underlying business uses Stripe and Shopify Payments as payment processors; these rails settle into merchant accounts at traditional banks. For a China-linked entity with regulatory capital and trade-verification needs, opening an account with an offshore EMI is legally impractical because (a) most EMIs refuse Chinese FIPs due to FATF and FX concerns; (b) they cannot perform the required SAFE verification for capital injections; and (c) trade finance and documentary credit facilities are unavailable. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely on licensed domestically incorporated banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection for the FIP should follow this protocol: (1) SAFE registration of the partnership under the FDI rules in China, yielding a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate (FERC); (2) open a capital account at a designated Tier-1 commercial bank (e.g., Bank of China Shanghai Branch) under the FERC; (3) foreign partners transfer funds via SWIFT MT103 from their Hong Kong corporate account using purpose code 126011 (foreign investment injection) or 110011 (working capital); (4) the receiving bank verifies the amount against the FERC and issues a Payment Instruction Notice — for RMB conversion, the bank will check the underlying business purpose; (5) after conversion, funds are booked into the Renminbi basic account. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to Very Low. Although Hong Kong has developed fintech infrastructure, digital-only banks (virtual banks) are licensed under the Banking Ordinance, and they generally target retail/SME customers rather than cross-border partnership structures. Entities such as Wise, Revolut, or Payoneer are electronic money institutions (EMIs) operating under scattered licenses; they are not authorized by the HKMA to offer full corporate banking services to a Chinese FIP. The HKMA's Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence covers payment tools, not corporate deposit accounts. Moreover, the underlying business uses Stripe and Shopify Payments as payment processors; these rails settle into merchant accounts at traditional banks. For a China-linked entity with regulatory capital and trade-verification needs, opening an account with an offshore EMI is legally impractical because (a) most EMIs refuse Chinese FIPs due to FATF and FX concerns; (b) they cannot perform the required SAFE verification for capital injections; and (c) trade finance and documentary credit facilities are unavailable. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely on licensed domestically incorporated banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No statutory threshold is codified in the IRO; however, the IRD expects all connected-party transactions to be supported by contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation. A prudent taxpayer producing local/master files should do so where aggregate annual cross-border connected transactions exceed HK$10,000,000 (or where the connected party is a tax haven entity, above HK$2,000,000).

Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong does not maintain separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like the UAE, the Inland Revenue Ordinance s.61A (GAAR) and the source principle require that a partnership's profit be attributable to real income-generating activities carried on in Hong Kong. A shell FIP with no local office, no employees, and whose management decisions are made in mainland China may be re-characterized as having a PE in China, thereby losing HK offshore exemption and risking treaty denial under the beneficial ownership rule.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Offshore Profit Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82A: additional tax of up to 3 times the undercharged tax plus a fine of up to HK$50,000. For willful evasion with intent to defraud, s.82 provides a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years. The IRD may also raise a deemed assessment based on estimated net profit from the mainland PE and charge interest at 8% per annum.

Director personal liability: The senior general partner (and any director of the corporate partner) may be personally assessed for the shortfall as a nominee of the partnership. A travel ban and asset freeze may be imposed under the Tax Recovery (Joint & Several Liability) Ordinance if the debt exceeds HK$10 million. Criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment of the individual signatory for up to 3 years, plus disqualification from serving as a director for 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50A + s.50F (Transfer Pricing Rules): the IRD may re-write the transaction to an arm's-length price, disallow the excessive deduction, and impose additional tax computed at 16.5% on the re-adjusted profits. Failure to maintain suitable transfer pricing documentation is an offence under s.51C(6), punishable by a fine of HK$100,000 and a daily default fine of HK$2,000. For material misdeclaration, the penalty may be up to 200% of the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable for civil penalties if they knowingly signed a false return (IRO s.82). The IRD may seek an order for the director to repay the avoided tax plus interest. A criminal conviction under s.82A carries up to 3 years imprisonment. In egregious cases, the Department of Justice may invoke the Theft Ordinance (s.1) for obtaining services by deception, carrying up to 14 years imprisonment.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company / AML

Statutory sanction: Under the China-HK Arrangement, the mainland tax authority may deny 'beneficial owner' status and apply domestic WHT at 10% (dividends) or 7% (interest), retroactively demanding the tax differential plus late interest. Under Chinese Criminal Law Art. 201, tax evasion of RMB 100,000 or more is subject to a fine of 1-5 times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to 7 years. In Hong Kong, the IRD may use the GAAR (s.61A) to disregard the HK treaty residence, and also invoke the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) for dealing with property known to be proceeds of tax evasion, punishable by 14 years' imprisonment and unlimited fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the sham HK structure may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid China WHT, plus Chinese tax surcharges and penalties. They face a cross-border travel ban and an ICE database blacklisting. On criminal conviction, a director may receive up to 14 years imprisonment for money laundering under HK law, and up to 7 years in mainland China for tax evasion. Civil recovery may extend to personal bank accounts and residential property under Proceeds of Crime legislation.
