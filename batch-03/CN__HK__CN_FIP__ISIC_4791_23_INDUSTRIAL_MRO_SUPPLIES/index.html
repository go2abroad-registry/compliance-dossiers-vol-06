Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual-office or commercial-mailbox address is rejected as the sole registered office for a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership and for bank onboarding. Chinese municipal tax bureaus require a verifiable physical operating address consistent with the business license, especially for e-commerce and B2B MRO catalogue sales with inventory and logistics operations. SAMR will require the registered address to match physically available premises. In Hong Kong, the registered office must be physical and cannot be a post office box; banks require substantive local business operations or a legitimate explainable substance, and a virtual mailbox alone triggers enhanced scrutiny and likely account refusal.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online via the e-Tax Portal (https://www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing or participating in a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership must apply for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) with the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department. The application must be submitted by or on behalf of the partnership, providing the registered partnership name, principal place of business in Hong Kong, nature of business activity, and identification details of all partners, including non-resident partners, together with the partnership formation document or foreign corporate parent evidence as applicable. Upon acceptance, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate displaying the Business Registration Number, which serves as the sole statutory tax reference number for the partnership for Profits Tax filing and all administrative purposes. No separate VAT/GST identification is issued because Hong Kong does not operate a VAT/GST system. For non-resident partners without a Hong Kong identity number, the IRD accepts the Business Registration Number of the partnership as the reference for offshore profits tax determinations; there is no separate TIN procurement step in Hong Kong.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency regime for both current and capital account transactions. The HKD is pegged to the USD within a 7.75-7.85 band under the linked exchange rate system operated by the HKMA. There are no surrender requirements on export receipts, no approvals required for inward or outward equity injections, and no dividend or profit remittance restrictions imposed on non-resident partners. The exception to unconstrained convertibility is the mainland China leg: when capital is injected by a mainland partner into the Hong Kong FIP, SAFE registration and outbound direct investment filing requirements apply before the remittance is released. For treasury purposes, the FIP may hold USD, HKD, EUR, and offshore CNH balances with no statutory conversion requirement. Profit remittance can be executed freely by MT103 or local RTGS, subject only to ordinary CDD verification, tax reserve considerations, and bank risk appetite. Therefore, the treasury constraint is not regulatory convertibility but the documentation burden needed to satisfy HKMA-licensed banks that the transaction is legitimate and the source and use of funds are transparent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory surrender rules apply in Hong Kong SAR. Exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency receipts, hold them in non-HKD accounts, and may remit or convert at any time with no mandatory conversion ratio or statutory timeline. If the underlying export proceeds flow through the mainland trade system, SAFE verification of trade documentation and reporting under mainland foreign exchange rules may be required at the onshore bank.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China as the competent authority for any mainland-side cross-border remittance or settlement

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury account. Under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance, only licensed banks, restricted licence banks, and deposit-taking companies may take deposits or provide corporate current accounts; the HKMA-regulated stored value facility (SVF) licence does not permit offshore EMI-style deposit banking. Offshore digital EMIs such as international payment startups are therefore legally barred from acting as the main bank for this FIP unless they obtain full HKMA banking/SVF authorization. HKMA-licensed virtual banks exist (Mox Bank, ZA Bank, WeLab Bank), but they generally lack the trade finance, commercial L/C, documentary collections, complex FX hedging, and multi-currency clearing capabilities required for industrial MRO cross-border disbursements. The suitable approach is to keep the liquidity core in a Tier-1 bank and use EMIs only for small-value e-commerce payout automation if they hold a valid MSO/SVF licence.

Mainland-connected source of funds / unexplained cross-border capital injections

Root cause: Mainland-connected source of funds / unexplained cross-border capital injections

Defense evidence: Twelve months of bank statements for every contributing partner; audited financial statements of the FIP or a certified capital contribution report from a Hong Kong CPA; source-of-wealth letters; mainland income tax returns and residence certificates; if the injection originates in the mainland, a completed SAFE/MOFCOM/NDRC outbound direct investment registration or its HK-equivalent recognition; treaty reliance and FX purpose code confirmation.

Root cause: E-commerce MRO catalogue crossing sanctions or dual-use export control thresholds

Defense evidence: Product-level HS/ECCN classification and non-dual-use declaration; Hong Kong Trade and Industry Department export licence (if applicable); supplier and end-user KYC files; restricted-party screening results from official UN, EU, HK and mainland sanctions lists; invoice/commercial documents for the relevant transaction; a written compliance policy confirming MRO hardware items are for general industrial use and not nuclear, chemical, or biological weapons programs.

Root cause: FATCA/CRS classification mismatch or pass-through partner opacity

Turnover: Hong Kong does not provide a size-based audit exemption: every Hong Kong private company, including a corporate general partner of the FIP, must be independently audited by a certified public accountant (practising) for every financial year, regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount, unless it is dormant within the meaning of section 359B of the Companies Ordinance.
