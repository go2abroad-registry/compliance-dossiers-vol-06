Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filings and VAT administration are conducted through the STA's New Electronic Tax Bureau and the fully digitalised electronic invoice platform. For a Hong Kong SAR branch or Hong Kong-sourced profits, the separate competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) Hong Kong, which administers profits tax through its e-Tax system; Hong Kong imposes no VAT, GST or sales tax.

E-government filing portal

The China Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first be incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The issued business licence contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which simultaneously constitutes the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number of the domestic entity. Within 30 days of licence issuance, the FIP must complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau, declare banking and accounting details, and select the applicable VAT taxpayer status. For a foreign non-resident partner without an establishment or place of business in the PRC, no separate VAT ID is required solely because of its partnership interest; China-sourced income is generally subjected to withholding at the source, and the partner may rely on its home-jurisdiction tax reference number and the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement for treaty relief. To access export zero-rating and input VAT refund, the FIP should voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer before the first export shipment. If the FIP later creates a Hong Kong branch, that branch must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the Hong Kong IRD for profits tax purposes, but no indirect tax registration will arise in Hong Kong.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. China does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election for a foreign-invested partnership; the FIP is statutorily pass-through for Enterprise Income Tax purposes, and each partner is taxed on its distributive share. No hybrid mismatch election is currently available under PRC domestic law, and the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement applies to determine tax residence and permanent establishment exposure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

An FIP with annual VAT-taxable sales of goods (including portable battery generators and solar panels) exceeding RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period is mandatorily required to register as a general VAT taxpayer. Below this threshold, the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to simplified collection at the statutory 3% rate, with small-scale monthly sales of RMB 100,000 or less generally exempt from VAT. However, for export zero-rating and input VAT refund eligibility, general VAT taxpayer registration is the operative statutory route.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim the 0% VAT rate on export sales of solar portable power equipment when the goods physically leave PRC customs territory under the PRC Customs export supervision modes, including cross-border e-commerce retail export under customs supervision code 9610, B2B export under 9710, and overseas-warehouse export under 9810 where applicable. The FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer, issue a compliant commercial export invoice, report the export sales in its VAT return, match the export declaration data with logistics and foreign-exchange receipts, and retain full title and transport documentation proving delivery to the overseas buyer. Qualifying offshore digital services are not within the product scope; embedded software is not separately zero-rated as a digital service.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs bill) issued by PRC Customs (GACC)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or international courier transit document evidencing export and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the FIP
  • Export sales contract, online purchase order, or platform transaction record identifying the overseas buyer
  • Input VAT special invoice issued by the upstream domestic supplier for the purchased portable power and solar panel goods
  • Bank export-proceeds collection voucher or SAFE trade-monitoring foreign-exchange receipt record demonstrating inward remittance from the overseas buyer

Invoicing must be performed through the STA's fully digitalised electronic invoice platform, using the all-electronic invoice system integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV database. VAT invoice data are transmitted in near real time to the competent tax authority; each fully digitalised invoice carries a system-generated invoice number, QR anti-counterfeit code, electronic signature and tax-control code, fulfilling the anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. No physical fiscal stamping is required. Enterprises may integrate their ERP systems with the STA platform through the official API or through authorised third-party electronic invoice service providers. Domestic buyers and tax officers verify invoices on the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform.

The STA, PRC Customs (GACC) and SAFE perform triangulated audit checks between customs export declarations, logistics tracking data, VAT invoice flows and foreign-exchange receipts. If export zero-rating is claimed without actual physical export, or with false documentation, unrepatriated proceeds, or inflated invoices, the STA will claw back the refunded VAT and input tax credit, impose statutory late-payment surcharges from the due date, and assess a monetary penalty of 50% to five times the tax amount under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law Article 63. Fraudulent export VAT refunds may also trigger criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 204, which penalises tax-rebate fraud with imprisonment and fines of one to five times the fraudulently obtained refund. Golden Tax Phase IV risk scores may additionally suspend or annul the FIP's export-refund eligibility retroactively, and recovery may proceed against the partnership and, to the extent permitted by partnership law and liability structure, the responsible partners.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully open capital account with no foreign exchange controls. The HKD is freely convertible under a well-regulated currency board system linked to the US Dollar within a guaranteed conversion zone. Current account transactions (e.g., trade in goods and services, remittances, interest and dividend payments) and capital account transactions (including foreign direct investment, portfolio flows, and loan repayments) are unrestricted. There are no surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no profit remittance restrictions for entities incorporated in Hong Kong. A foreign-invested partnership can receive equity injections, service fees, or intercompany loans from its mainland Chinese parent without prior approval from any exchange authority, provided the banking declarations and underlying trade/investment documentation are in order. The HKMA does not discriminate between residents and non-residents for FX purposes. However, payments that involve the mainland Chinese partner may be subject to the People's Bank of China and SAFE on the China side, not to Hong Kong's regulations. For this specific e-commerce business, all sales proceeds from global customers can be retained in any convertible currency, freely moved to settlement accounts, and used for operational or investment expenses. The absence of FX controls makes Hong Kong an ideal jurisdiction for cross-border treasury centralisation, with the only compliance burden being standard AML record-keeping and beneficial owner disclosure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory export proceeds surrender requirements and no mandatory conversion ratios. Exporters and trading companies may retain all foreign currency receipts, hold them indefinitely in any bank account, and deploy them freely without consent from the HKMA. All receipts can be converted to HKD, USD, RMB, or any freely traded currency at market rates through authorized institutions. The same applies to re-invoices and third-party trade settlements.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) - the de facto central bank and foreign exchange authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Medium under strict HKMA licensing boundaries. Virtual banks (e.g., Mox, ZA Bank, Livi, and WeLab) and licensed stored-value operators (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, and Lianlian Global) can hold corporate accounts, facilitate multi-currency payments, and connect to the Faster Payment System (FPS). However, for a foreign-invested partnership with significant cross-border flows from China and a requirement for documentary trade services, these EMIs are typically not licensed to issue letters of credit or provide capital escrow custody. Some EMIs have limited interoperability with bank guarantees and cannot support complex treasury operations like forward FX execution or same-day netting through a bank treasury desk. Moreover, the HKMA's regulatory perimeter prevents EMIs from offering deposit-taking facilities; funds must be routed onward to a licensed bank or held as safeguarded stored value under conditions. Given the low-risk retail nature of the business, using an EMI as a payment gateway for Stripe/Affirm receivables is feasible, but the primary treasury relationship must reside with a Tier-1 licensed bank for operational resilience, statutory capital verification, and maintaining audit-trail integrity for the Chinese FIP's cross-border capital account filings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the mainland Chinese FIP or its foreign investors must be executed via SWIFT MT103 using the proper ISO 20022 transaction purpose code for equity participation (e.g., 'TREA' or the HKMA-specific 'Capital Contribution'). The remitting bank on the China side must comply with SAFE's Outbound Investment framework; the funds should be credited to the Hong Kong partnership's Capital Escrow Account. Upon receipt, the Hong Kong bank will perform a sanctions screening and source-of-funds validation. A capital verification certificate must be prepared by an independent CPA and filed with the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department within one month of injection. The HKMA does not require a separate licensing step, but the entity must ensure that the total aggregate capital does not breach any prior approval threshold under Hong Kong's Partnership Ordinance. Turnover: There is no statutory audit requirement for a Hong Kong unincorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) and no balance-sheet, turnover or headcount threshold triggers a mandatory CPA audit. A certified audit becomes legally or commercially mandatory only if: (a) the partnership agreement, investor rights agreement, bank facility, payment-processor agreement (e.g., Stripe or Affirm BNPL), or an IRD enquiry requires audited financial statements; (b) the general partner is a corporate body whose company-level financial statements require audit under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); or (c) the partnership is licensed under the Securities and Futures Ordinance. For comparison, all Hong Kong private companies are required to appoint a CPA auditor under s.405 of the Companies Ordinance regardless of turnover, assets or headcount; only dormant companies may dispense with an audit. Therefore no quantitative audit exemption is available for a company, and none applies to the FIP.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) rate for resident enterprises; 10% final withholding rate for non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment in China; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) itself is pass-through and not an EIT taxpayer under Caishui [2008] No. 159

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on China-source dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise without a PE. For FIP profit distributions, the allocable profit share is treated as China-source income subject to 10% withholding under Caishui [2008] No. 159, unless the non-resident partner has a PE in China and pays tax on a net basis.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation) is mandatory when annual related-party transactions reach: RMB 200,000,000 for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for financial asset transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for provision of services. Master File preparation is triggered when the total amount of related-party transactions in a fiscal year reaches RMB 10,000,000,000. The annual related-party transaction report must be filed with the EIT annual return whenever any related-party transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: China is not an ESR jurisdiction in the UAE sense, but the FIP and its non-resident partners are subject to substantive economic substance review via the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47), the Beneficial Ownership rules under STA Announcement [2018] No. 9, and the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA's anti-abuse protections. A Hong Kong partner must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong—including strategic decision-making, treasury management, risk assumption, and beneficial ownership of contracts. The Chinese FIP must have real operational substance for online retail: employees, inventory, warehousing, e-commerce platform sellers, payment processing, and after-sales support. A shell arrangement without personnel, premises, or business functions will be disregarded and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Under-Declaration of FIP Profit Allocations to Non-Resident Partners

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 37 and 38; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax; Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% of the un-withheld amount; a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge accrues. If the tax evaded is substantial and fraudulent, Criminal Law Article 201 applies with fines and imprisonment up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years if the amount is huge.

Director personal liability: The general partner (whether an individual or a corporate general partner) is personally and jointly liable for the partnership's unpaid taxes under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law Article 39. Courts may pierce the corporate veil of a corporate general partner under Company Law Article 20 if the partnership was used to evade tax. Directors and the managing partner can be placed on the dishonest judgment debtor blacklist, subject to travel bans, asset freezes, and seizure of personal property, and may face criminal imprisonment for tax fraud.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Failure to File Related-Party Transaction Documentation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 and 45; State Taxation Administration Announcement [2016] No. 42; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44. The tax authority may re-adjust income to the arm's length result, impose special tax adjustment interest (the RMB benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points), and levy a penalty of 50% to 100% of the additional tax for absence of contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who knowingly engineered non-arm's length pricing can be held jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax and penalties. If the pricing behavior is found to be intentional concealment, criminal tax evasion liability may attach under Criminal Law Article 201. The responsible individuals may face asset freezing, travel bans, personal bankruptcy exposure through the unlimited liability of the general partner, and imprisonment for offenders.
Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Failure in Hong Kong DTA Claims

Statutory sanction: STA Announcement [2018] No. 9 and EIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule). Treaty benefits will be denied, and the Chinese tax authority will apply the statutory 10% withholding rate, impose late-payment interest, and add a penalty of 50% to 100% of the tax understated. The taxpayer may also be subject to special tax investigation with a 10-year look-back period.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and the HK partner who signed false beneficial ownership declarations are personally liable for resulting tax liabilities under the corporate-veil piercing doctrine where abuse of the legal form is proven. Travel bans and exit restrictions under the SPC blacklist mechanism apply to legal representatives and actual controllers. Criminal liability for fraud and forgery of official documents may result in up to 7 years' imprisonment under Chinese criminal statutes, and the Hong Kong IRD may separately prosecute perjury or tax evasion for false TRC applications.
