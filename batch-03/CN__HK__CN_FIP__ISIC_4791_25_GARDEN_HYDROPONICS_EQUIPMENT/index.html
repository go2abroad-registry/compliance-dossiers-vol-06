Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk). The IRD also houses the Business Registration Office responsible for Business Registration Certificates under the Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310.

E-government filing portal

1. Determine whether the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is carrying on business in Hong Kong within the meaning of the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), which triggers the obligation to obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) within one month of commencement. 2. If the FIP is structured as a limited partnership, register the partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) and obtain the Certificate of Registration of Limited Partnership. 3. Submit the prescribed business registration application to the Business Registration Office of the IRD, supplying the partnership agreement, identity documentation of all foreign and resident partners, registered address in Hong Kong if maintained, and confirmation of the person responsible for the partnership's financial affairs. 4. Upon approval, the IRD issues the BRC with a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the de facto tax identification number for the partnership. 5. Because Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT or GST registration number, and no general indirect tax exists, the BRN is also used for customs trade declarations where the partnership is registered as an importer/exporter with the Trade and Industry Department under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60). 6. If the FIP is a foreign non-resident entity not carrying on business in Hong Kong but still deriving Hong Kong-source profits, it may be required to appoint a Hong Kong-resident tax representative to receive notices and correspond with the IRD under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); each non-resident partner will be assessed directly on its allocated share of partnership profits.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch classification elections. A partnership is transparent for Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes: the partnership is not chargeable to tax as an entity, and each partner is chargeable on that partner's share of partnership profits. The two-tiered Profits Tax rate schedule applicable to unincorporated businesses applies automatically, not by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales Tax (Hong Kong SAR; selective excise duties may apply only under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109, and stamp duty only under the Stamp Duty Ordinance, Cap. 117)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not operate a general indirect tax registration threshold. Registration as an importer/exporter with the Trade and Industry Department under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) is required for persons importing or exporting goods, but this is a trade registration obligation, not an indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT zero-rating mechanism. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, so there is no statutory VAT export zero-rating or VAT export refund. For Hong Kong Profits Tax, an export trading FIP may be eligible for an 'offshore claim' if the profits neither arise in nor are derived from Hong Kong under the territorial source principle of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the relevant indicators include where contracts are negotiated and concluded, where purchase and sale activities are performed, and where economic operations take place, rather than merely the physical destination of the goods.

  • Hong Kong Trade and Industry Department (TID) Importer/Exporter Registration Certificate obtained under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60)
  • Electronic Export Trade Declaration lodged through an approved digital platform with the Hong Kong Trade and Industry Department (e.g., DTTN/Tradelink) where applicable
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Road Cargo Manifest evidencing physical export movement from Hong Kong
  • Commercial invoice, detailed packing list, and signed export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bank inward payment evidence such as SWIFT MT103/MT202 confirmation or remittance advice, retained for source-of-profits substantiation
  • Buyer-side Certificate of Origin or Form A if required by the importing customs administration, though this is not a Hong Kong tax document

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoice clearance, API tax filing, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies to any VAT, GST, or sales tax because no such tax is levied in Hong Kong. For Profits Tax purposes, records including invoices, vouchers, bank statements, contracts, and electronic trade documents must be kept sufficient to enable the partner's assessable profits to be readily ascertained, generally for at least seven years under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Electronic trade declarations for exports are lodged through approved digital platforms such as the Digital Trade and Transportation Network (DTTN), but this is a customs/trade-statistics requirement, not a tax clearance mandate.

There is no VAT export zero-rating or VAT refund to claw back in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST is chargeable. However, the IRD will scrutinize any offshore profits claim for export trading income by reviewing the place of contract formation, the place where the officers/partners performed substantive sales and procurement functions, the movement of goods, and the bank flow of sale proceeds. If an offshore claim is rejected, the IRD may issue additional Profits Tax assessments within the statutory assessment period, generally six years after the end of the relevant year of assessment for non-fraud cases, and without time limit in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. Penalties may include penal tax of up to treble the amount of tax undercharged under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and fines or imprisonment for knowingly furnishing incorrect returns under section 80. Failure to hold a Business Registration Certificate while carrying on business in Hong Kong is separately enforceable under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility for trade and services but strict capital-account controls. The RMB is freely convertible for bona fide cross-border trade payments and service settlements; however, every transaction flowing through a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must be supported by a valid underlying trade or investment document and must be reported to SAFE through the designated bank. Capital-account transactions, such as non-resident partners subscribing partnership capital in Chinese yuan or foreign currency, shareholder loans, capital reduction, withdrawal of equity, and profit remittance, are subject to onshore registration, prior verification, and post-event monitoring. Export proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic banking system except where an offshore retention structure is approved under SAFE's special economic-zone or FTZ policy. Profit remittance by a foreign partner is legally possible only after the FIP has completed annual audited financial statements, filed corporate income tax in compliance with deemed profit rules, and proved the reserve for distributable profits is attributable to tax-paid, audited earnings; the remittance must be accompanied by the SAFE FDI registration notice, tax payment/reduction certificates, the partnership resolution, and audited balance-sheet statement. In practice, convertibility is effective for genuine commercial use but is de facto staged: banks apply a documentary test to every inbound and outbound flow and will reject any remittance that does not match the declared business scope of the hydroponics e-commerce enterprise.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods-trade verification framework, export foreign currency receipts from the online retail and physical export of garden hydroponics equipment must be repatriated to the mainland through a domestic bank within 90 days of the relevant customs export declaration, unless the company holds an approved FTZ offshore account or SAFE special exception allowing retention. A one-time extension of up to 180 days may be granted for large capital equipment contracts or confirmed letters of credit. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio in the current negative-list environment; the exporting FIP may retain forex in an export revenue account or convert to RMB at the interbank central parity rate. However, the bank must match every export receipt with customs electronic data under SAFE's trade and settlement verification system; any receipt that remains unreported for 180 days or that lacks matching export declaration will trigger an automatic AML/CFT monitoring alert.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) serves as the territorial banking regulator for the Hong Kong treasury lane.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. In mainland China, Stripe and PayPal act only as third-party online payment gateways; they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide onshore CNY corporate accounts or hold client funds as treasury balances. An offshore fintech EMI such as Airwallex or Payoneer cannot lawfully serve as the FIP's domestic Chinese bank account provider; it can only function as a payment gateway that settles through a PBOC-licensed onshore partner. Any use of an offshore EMI to maintain, sweep, or pledge client funds for the FIP is legally prohibited because the EMI is not supervised by SAFE/PBOC for onshore deposit balances. The FIP may use these rails for consumer collection, but all treasury liquidity must ultimately rest in a domestic tier-1 commercial bank account subject to SAFE cross-border reporting and transaction declaration requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital for the Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership must be remitted by the non-resident investing partner from its own bank account through a SWIFT payment into the FIP's onshore capital-escrow account opened with the designated domestic bank. The bank must first complete SAFE Foreign Exchange registration for the FIP and issue the Foreign-Invested Enterprise or Partnership Capital Account registration certificate. The remittance instructions must carry the SAFE-approved capital-transaction purpose code, the overseas investor's legal name and SAFE file number, and the partnership's unified social credit code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Hong Kong's transfer pricing rules (sections 50AAJ-50AAT of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, effective 13 July 2018), the documentation threshold for a Local File is HKD 4 million for controlled transactions with related parties, and HKD 8 million for controlled transactions with one or more related parties plus an aggregate amount of HKD 4 million for transactions other than financial transactions. A Contemporaneous Master File is required where the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 680 million and the controlled transactions exceed HKD 40 million in aggregate. For the China-inbound FIP, the same thresholds apply. In China, a Master File must be filed if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion, and Local File documentation is required if related-party transactions exceed RMB 20 million (excluding financial transactions) or RMB 40 million (including financial transactions). To avoid penalties, maintain documentation in both Hong Kong and China to the respective thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate Economically Substantive Regulations framework analogous to the UAE ESR, but it requires real economic substance under the common law 'resident' and 'source' principles. For DTA benefits and Hong Kong profits tax territoriality, a Foreign-Invested Partnership must demonstrate substantial business activities in Hong Kong: a physical office, local employees, local decision-making, and actual execution of core functions such as order processing, logistics coordination, payment settlement, and customer support. For China's controlled foreign company rules, a Hong Kong enterprise may be treated as a tax haven pass-through vehicle unless the China partner proves the Hong Kong entity has substantive business activity. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in the digital gardening equipment sector include: supplier sourcing and contract negotiation with China-based manufacturers; IP ownership and royalty management; website / platform operations and data hosting; warehousing and fulfilment decisions; and financial management including treasury and invoice settlement. If these activities are performed from China mainland while Hong Kong merely title-holds assets, the risk of recharacterization by the PRC Tax Administration and the Hong Kong IRD is extreme.

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: The IRD may raise additional assessments under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance equal to 10% of the undercharged tax. Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the Hong Kong partner / general partner or the China parent director knowingly directed the false offshore claim.
