None. The Hong Kong Limited Partnerships Ordinance does not prescribe a minimum paid-up capital, and no statutory escrow or blocked capital account is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the PRC/Foreign-Invested Partnership leg: State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, exercised through the provincial and municipal tax service bureaus and the STA Electronic Tax Bureau online portal. For the Hong Kong SAR offshore/fulfillment nexus: Inland Revenue Department, including the Business Registration Office and the eTAX electronic filing portal. The Chinese FIP is registered for PRC tax with the STA; Hong Kong filing and Business Registration are separately required if the partnership derives Hong Kong-source profits or carries on business in Hong Kong.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-invested partnership obtains legal existence by registration with the competent local Market Regulation Administration, which issues the Unified Social Credit Code that is the statutory taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes. Within 30 calendar days of that registration, the partnership must complete tax registration with the competent State Taxation Administration bureau through the electronic tax bureau, producing the partnership registration certificate, the partnership agreement, foreign-investor identity documents, business-scope description, accounting/financial statement particulars, and relevant licenses; the tax bureau then activates the income-tax and VAT taxpayer profiles, determines taxpayer type, and registers the expected withholding-agent obligations for partner distributions and cross-border payments. Non-resident partners without a permanent establishment in China are separately registered/identified through the PRC withholding system and must supply their foreign tax residence information and tax identification numbers to the partnership for treaty and compliance reporting. For the Hong Kong nexus, if the Hong Kong operation is a branch or separate contracting/facility activity, the FIP or its designated partner must apply for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance, obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD Business Registration Office, and then file the partnership profits tax return; the partnership itself is not taxed at entity level, but each partner is assessable on Hong Kong-source profits attributable to the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for income-tax entity classification: the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance both treat partnership profits/losses as passing through to partners, and no check-the-box election is available to convert a foreign-invested partnership to an entity-level taxpayer. The partnership may make administrative indirect-tax elections in mainland China, such as small-scale versus general VAT taxpayer status where the statutory turnover conditions are satisfied, and may elect export-tax-refund aggregation methods; no hybrid mismatch entity election is available to this CN/HK cross-border fulfillment structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales, which is the mandatory PRC turnover threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold a small-scale VAT taxpayer status may apply, with voluntary general registration available in certain cases. For Hong Kong, there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST is imposed; the Hong Kong threshold is instead the business-registration duty under the Business Registration Ordinance, with profits-tax liability only on Hong Kong-source profits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero percent export VAT is available under the Value-Added Tax Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing rules only for goods that are physically exported from mainland China, are the subject of a valid electronic customs export declaration, are invoiced to the overseas buyer, and are supported by actual foreign-currency repatriation in the exporter's bank account. For cross-border e-commerce overseas-warehouse fulfillment, the cargo must leave China under an export customs declaration using the applicable cross-border e-commerce export customs regime; the exporter, the consignor on the bill of lading, the invoicing entity, and the bank-account receiving party must be consistent, and the goods must not be consumed or sold within China. Qualifying cross-border services, including certain logistics or warehousing services provided to an overseas recipient and wholly consumed outside mainland China, may be zero-rated or exempt under the PRC cross-border service rules; the Hong Kong SAR has no VAT, so no Hong Kong export zero-rating or refund claim arises.

  • Customs Export Declaration accepted electronically through China International Trade Single Window or the customs authority export-clearance system
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, multimodal transport document, or Air Waybill showing export from a Chinese customs port and delivery to an overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract, and HS-code-level product declaration matching the customs export data
  • Foreign exchange receipt advice or Inward Remittance Certificate issued by a SAFE-authorized Chinese bank in the exporter's name demonstrating repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export VAT rebate claim file containing the customs declaration reference, digital VAT invoice data, QR/electronic invoice code, and logistics movement records
  • Overseas-warehouse or third-party logistics delivery records proving that goods entered the overseas fulfillment facility only after export

For PRC VAT purposes, the FIP must issue all VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration electronic invoice platform, using fully digitalized electronic invoices with the statutory anti-fraud digital stamp and near-real-time fiscal clearance; invoice data are matched by the STA against customs export declarations and foreign-exchange receipts for export claims. Paper VAT invoices, where authorized, must comply with the national electronic invoice management controls and monitoring-code rules. Hong Kong has no electronic VAT invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate; its eTAX system is used for profits tax and business registration filings, not indirect-tax invoice clearance.

The State Taxation Administration and China Customs will audit the consistency of the export declaration, bill of lading, invoice, overseas warehouse records, and foreign-exchange receipt; if documentation is missing, contradicted, or manufactured, the claimed zero rate is reversed, the output VAT is assessed at the applicable PRC rate, late-payment surcharges are imposed, the refund is clawed back, and the FIP may be disqualified from export-tax-refund treatment and placed on the tax-untrustworthiness list. Fraudulent declarations, forged bills of lading, overstated export values, or fictitious FIRCs can attract administrative penalties, customs smuggling penalties, and criminal liability for false export tax-rebate fraud under PRC criminal law. Hong Kong has no VAT zero-rating refund, but an FIP claiming offshore profits without economic substance in Hong Kong may be subject to additional assessments under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, plus penalty tax for incorrect returns and potential prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a managed floating exchange rate system with strict, registration-based foreign exchange controls. Under SAFE's trade foreign exchange monitoring framework, RMB is fully convertible for bona fide current account transactions such as goods trade, logistics fees, warehousing charges and service fees, provided the settlement bank has verified the customs declaration, contract, invoice and payment purpose code. Capital account convertibility is conditional: an FIP receiving capital from Hong Kong must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration, open a dedicated capital account at an authorized bank, and obtain SAFE confirmation; funds can then be drawn down in tranches. Profit distribution to foreign partners is lawful after annual audited accounts and Chinese tax settlement, but the remittance must be matched to actual distributable partnership income and cannot exceed the CPA-certified amount. Practical hurdles include strict attribution to specific trade invoices, the high likelihood of requests for supplementary evidence when invoice and customs data mismatch, temporary freeze of FX sale or purchase when SAFE macro-prudential indicators exceed thresholds, and a statutory requirement that capital injection proceeds must be deployed within six months. The overall control chain means treasury managers must plan each inbound and outbound remittance as a documentary event rather than a simple cash transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Administrative Measures on Verification and Monitoring of Trade Foreign Exchange, export receipts must be repatriated to the onshore bank within 90 days of the export date; each remittance is paired against the associated customs declaration and the bank writes a trade item code on the transaction confirmation. There is no standing mandatory conversion ratio under normal economic conditions: the exporter may retain 100% of eligible export proceeds in a foreign exchange current account or convert them to RMB at market rate. However, SAFE may impose extraordinary macro-prudential surrender requirements, for example up to 50% of daily incremental export foreign exchange sold to designated banks, during balance-of-payment equilibrium actions. The FIP must record all receipts in the SAFE online monitoring system and produce customs data for any receipt exceeding USD 30,000.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), the Central Bank of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Revolut and Wise are not licensed deposit-taking institutions or foreign exchange settlement agents in mainland China. While Airwallex holds money service operator licenses in Hong Kong and payment licenses in certain common-law jurisdictions, it cannot operate an onshore CNY clearing account for a mainland-incorporated FIP and cannot act as a banking partner for SAFE-regulated capital account events. Under the SAFE 'Payment Institution Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Business' regime, only licensed PRC payment institutions such as Lianlian International or PayPal China partner A) may process cross-border FX collection or disbursement for an onshore entity; offshore EMIs are legally barred from that rail. The FIP may use Airwallex only as a supplementary overseas collection drawer for marketplace receivables, subject to file-level approval from the onshore settlement bank and immediate funding into the BOC account via MT103. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injection from the Hong Kong investor into the mainland FIP, the remittance must be a SWIFT MT103 in CNY or USD to the dedicated FDI capital account opened after SAFE registration. The remittance narrative must state 'FDI capital injection to FIP [legal name]' and the receiving bank will assign the statutory FX purpose code for direct investment under the capital account. The bank will block the payment unless the remittance matches the SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration certificate and the bank can issue a capital account fall-in confirmation. After receipt, a licensed PRC CPA must issue a capital verification report and the bank and SAFE must receive it within fifteen working days. Funds can be transferred from the capital account to the operating current account only against actual 3PL infrastructure capex evidenced by a board resolution and a full invoice package; no free movement between capital and operating balances is permitted.Outbound trade disbursements for the cross-border e-commerce fulfillment hub should use SWIFT MT103 for vendor payments to overseas warehouse landlords, courier companies and last-mile logistics providers, supported by original invoices, settlement letters and the FIP's SAFE trade status. For high-value equipment procurement for temporary storage nodes, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by ICBC is the preferred clearing rail after the bank files the import settlement ledger. Documentary collections are acceptable only for periodic handling fee settlements below USD 100,000 and only if the presentation documents exactly match the approved trade invoice. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Revolut and Wise are not licensed deposit-taking institutions or foreign exchange settlement agents in mainland China. While Airwallex holds money service operator licenses in Hong Kong and payment licenses in certain common-law jurisdictions, it cannot operate an onshore CNY clearing account for a mainland-incorporated FIP and cannot act as a banking partner for SAFE-regulated capital account events. Under the SAFE 'Payment Institution Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Business' regime, only licensed PRC payment institutions such as Lianlian International or PayPal China partner A) may process cross-border FX collection or disbursement for an onshore entity; offshore EMIs are legally barred from that rail. The FIP may use Airwallex only as a supplementary overseas collection drawer for marketplace receivables, subject to file-level approval from the onshore settlement bank and immediate funding into the BOC account via MT103. Overall viability rating: Low; the EMI is not a replacement for tier-1 domestic clearing infrastructure.
