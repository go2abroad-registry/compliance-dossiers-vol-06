For a bonded warehouse operator in a customs free trade zone, virtual office and commercial mailbox arrangements are not permissible. The Inland Revenue Department and the Customs and Excise Department require the entity to maintain a physical registered office and a separate operational bonded depot inside the free trade zone, with dedicated staff, visible signage, original books and records, and customs-licensed storage. Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessible through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) framework via the official STA portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn, with local municipal STA sub-offices exercising supervisory jurisdiction over the free trade zone bonded depot.

        
E-government filing portal
For a foreign-invested partnership (FIP) established in the People's Republic of China, the statutory tax identity acquisition procedure is as follows: (1) Submit an online enterprise registration application with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) using the National Enterprise Credit Information Registration Platform, furnishing the partnership agreement, foreign partner constitutional documents (including, where applicable, a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and Certificate of Incorporation), and capital contribution evidence; upon approval, SAMR issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which becomes the entity's Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). (2) Within 30 calendar days of the SAMR registration, the FIP must complete a tax registration filing through the Electronic Tax Bureau with the competent local STA office, providing the legal representative identity, tax practitioner engagement, banking account details, and expected tax types; the taxpayer electronic account is activated immediately and the USCC is the official TIN. (3) The entity's VAT registration is automatically activated concurrently; if the FIP's expected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, VAT general taxpayer status must be registered through the same e-Tax portal. (4) For a foreign non-resident enterprise that does not establish a Chinese entity but derives taxable income or carries out taxable supplies in China, the statutory route is the Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration (Withholding) procedure, filed with the local STA through a qualified withholding agent or by direct electronic registration, supported by the foreign enterprise's certificate of registration, a tax residency certificate from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (for DTA relief), and an appointed tax representative's power of attorney; the non-resident TIN is issued as a separate taxpayer number distinct from the USCC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
None. The PRC tax system does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. A partnership, including a foreign-invested partnership, is automatically treated as a pass-through entity for enterprise income tax and individual income tax purposes, with partners taxed on their distributive shares. No entity-level tax election is legally available under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law; foreign partners may, however, seek treaty benefits under the applicable Double Taxation Arrangement through a separate tax residence certificate application.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
6%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
There is no economic turnover threshold for basic tax registration in China; all legal entities, including FIPs, must complete tax and VAT registration upon establishment. The mandatory turnover threshold affecting VAT status is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000, above which the taxpayer is required to register as a VAT general taxpayer. Foreign non-resident enterprises supplying taxable services in China must register for VAT through a withholding agent regardless of turnover.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under PRC VAT law, goods physically exported from the customs territory are zero-rated. In a bonded free trade zone, goods moved from a domestic enterprise into a bonded depot are deemed exported at the time of entry into the zone and become eligible for zero-rating and input VAT refund when the goods are subsequently shipped to an overseas destination. Qualifying bonded warehouse and storage services supplied by the depot directly to a non-resident enterprise for goods entering or leaving the customs territory may also be eligible for zero-rating under the cross-border services zero-rating measure, provided the service recipient is outside China, the contract and payment are in convertible foreign currency, and the service is actually rendered in connection with the export. The zero-rated claim must be supported by evidence that the goods left the customs territory, the foreign exchange was received within the statutory period, and the customs and banking documents are genuine and unaltered.

China mandates electronic invoice issuance through the State Taxation Administration's full digital electronic invoice system, operating under the Golden Tax IV framework. VAT invoices must be issued via the authorised Electronic Tax Bureau infrastructure, with real-time transmission to the national e-invoice database and immediate production of a unique electronic invoice verification code. Large-volume taxpayers are required to connect their enterprise resource planning systems to the e-invoice platform through the approved application programming interface (API). There is no physical fiscal stamp; instead, every invoice carries a digital identity that is authenticated on the national tax portal. For bonded free trade zone transactions, the customs export declaration number and the associated VAT invoice must be electronically cross-referenced in the e-tax system and the China International Trade Single Window before an export zero-rating claim may be filed.

      
The tax authorities retrospectively audit export declarations, foreign exchange collection records, logistics chains, and invoice trails. If zero-rating is applied without actual physical export or with forged or incomplete documentation, the STA will claw back the input VAT credit, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid amount, and levy an administrative fine equal to 50% to 500% of the unpaid or falsely-refunded tax, pursuant to the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and its implementing regulations. Customs authorities may additionally impose fines up to ten times the duty evaded and revoke the bonded warehouse registration. Where the fraudulent export refund amount exceeds RMB 50,000, the matter constitutes the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with criminal penalties ranging from imprisonment to life imprisonment, depending on gravity.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
SAFE maintains a dual system: current-account transactions are largely convertible once supported by genuine trade documents, while capital-account transactions require genuine underlying approvals and registration. The CN FIP, as a Foreign-Invested Partnership in a customs free-trade zone bonded depot, must treat capital contributions from the HK partner as inbound foreign direct investment; these funds can enter only through a registered foreign-exchange capital account. RMB profits and liquidation proceeds are generally remittable to HK after tax settlement, but the bank will require audited financial statements, tax clearance, proof that the partnership is actually operating, and a capital-account disbursement application. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under current SAFE rules; export FX must be repatriated to a designated bank account and logged in the SAFE trade monitoring system, and conversion to RMB or retention is at the enterprise's choice. The FTZ/free-trade-account regime can speed cross-border RMB flows, but it does not remove capital-account supervision.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export receipts must be repatriated within the SAFE-monitored collection term, generally 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, or by a later estimated collection date registered with the bank if the underlying commercial contract grants longer credit terms. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may either retain the foreign currency in a designated current-account foreign-exchange account or convert it to RMB through a designated bank after validation of contract, invoice, customs declaration, and bill-of-lading data.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), for the onshore PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central-bank authority for the offshore Hong Kong leg and does not impose exchange controls

An offshore EMI may service the HK partner's treasury side but cannot receive the FIP's capital injection or act as the principal clearing bank; attempting to route capital through an EMI will trigger immediate KYC/AML rejection.

        FIP is a pass-through entity with multiple overseas limited partners and a corporate Hong Kong general partner; beneficial ownership is not visible on a single share register.

Defense evidence: Signed partnership agreement and amendments; Hong Kong corporate registry current extract and certificate of good standing for the corporate GP; Certificate of Incumbency; directors and shareholders registers; UBO declaration on the bank's standard form naming every 5% or greater natural person; notarized ID/passport copies and source-of-funds documents for each UBO; corporate financial statements for the GP.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The Hong Kong partner executes a SWIFT MT103 wire to the FIP's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at a designated Chinese Tier-1 bank, using the official SAFE purpose code for foreign-direct-investment capital contribution to a foreign-invested enterprise. The receiving bank performs the capital information registration and verifies the FIP's FDI form before crediting statutory capital; RMB conversion is permitted only for genuine business expenditures under the payment-before-settlement rule, with supporting invoices, contracts, and a CPA capital-verification schedule. FTZ free-trade accounts may be used as an eligible channel for cross-border RMB from HK, but capital-flow classification and SAFE reporting remain mandatory.Clearing rails include direct SWIFT MT103 remittance through the Chinese bank's correspondent network for bonded inventory and logistics payments; commercial letters of credit opened in favor of HK or offshore suppliers and settled through the presenting bank; and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-value repetitive trade flows. RMB payments to Hong Kong can be routed through CIPS for same-day value, while HKD flows use the Hong Kong CHATS system and USD flows use SWIFT/CHIPS via correspondent accounts. Every disbursement must be supported by customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and warehouse receipt to satisfy SAFE current-account trade matching.Onshore deliverable FX forwards for trade-related receivables and payables, executed under a bilateral ISDA/CSA derivative framework with a licensed Chinese bankCross-currency swaps for long-term partnership capital, bonded warehousing capex, and committed repayment obligationsBought FX options and simple option combinations that hedge existing or committed trade exposuresOffshore non-deliverable forwards are generally not permissible for the onshore FIP; any NDF-style hedge would require explicit SAFE authorization and is not available for this mainland-regulated entity
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - legally infeasible for the onshore FIP account stack. Mainland China does not recognize an offshore digital EMI as a bank, and only a licensed domestic bank can hold the SAFE-registered capital-account funds, perform FX registration, or provide a RMB basic deposit account. An offshore EMI may service the HK partner's treasury side but cannot receive the FIP's capital injection or act as the principal clearing bank; attempting to route capital through an EMI will trigger immediate KYC/AML rejection.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
16.5% standard profits tax rate for corporations; 8.25% on assessable profits up to HKD 2,000,000 under the two-tiered profits tax regime, with the remainder at 16.5%

0% – Hong Kong does not impose dividend withholding tax, whether under the Income Tax Ordinance or as a final tax; dividends paid by a Hong Kong partnership to non-resident partners are also not subject to any form of dividend withholding tax because the partnership return is not itself taxed

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10,000,000 (aggregate amount of controlled transactions per annum) – this is the recommended threshold below which the IRD will not generally expect contemporaneous documentation on a mandatory basis, but the obligation to apply the arm’s length principle under IRO s.50AA is absolute. For a bonded warehouse operator in a free trade zone, standard transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) should be maintained if the partner has associated enterprises involved in logistics, storage, cross-border freight, and insurance. The statutory documentation obligation is not expressed as a fixed amount in the IRO, but IRD DIPN 58 and DIPN 59 clarify that taxpayers must keep sufficient records to prove arm’s length pricing; in practice, a transaction threshold of HKD 10 million for controlled transactions is used as a de facto documentation trigger by tax advisers and by the IRD in audit settings.

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime similar to the UAE or BVI. However, economic substance is a critical factor in: (1) establishing eligibility for treaty benefits (the Limitation on Benefits clause requires real business activity in Hong Kong); (2) defending an offshore income exclusion claim under s.14 IRO – the IRD will scrutinise whether the profit-generating activities (core income-generating activities, CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong; (3) satisfying the beneficial-ownership test for reduced withholding tax rates under the DTA. A bonded warehouse entity must demonstrate that its staff, warehouse management, import/export procedures, contract negotiation, billing, and risk management are actually carried on in Hong Kong or in the free trade zone, as appropriate, to avoid being deemed a shell company. If the entity only holds bare legal title and outsources all operations to a related party outside Hong Kong, the IRD may treat that as a sham and re-classify the income as chargeable to profits tax at full rates.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion – Understatement of Offshore Income
        
Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82 – willful tax evasion: fine of HKD 50,000 plus three times the amount of tax undercharged; imprisonment for up to 3 years on first conviction; for subsequent convictions, fine of HKD 100,000 and 6 months’ imprisonment. In addition, the IRD may impose a non-criminal penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged under s.82A for negligent or intentional understatement.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership’s unpaid tax (Partnership Ordinance Cap. 38, s.14). A partner who signed or caused the filing of a false return can be personally sued by the IRD for the full tax shortfall if the partnership assets are insufficient. Moreover, the IRD will seek a travel restriction order (Hong Kong Civil Procedure Order 44) preventing the delinquent director from leaving Hong Kong. Criminal imprisonment sentences are served personally; there is no corporate veil to protect the partner if the court finds that the partner acted with intent to evade tax.

      

      
      

        Shell Company – Lack of Economic Substance / Sham Arrangement
        
Statutory sanction: Although Hong Kong has no anti-shell company statute, the IRD will invoke the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under s.61A IRO (transactions with sole or dominant purpose of obtaining a tax benefit) and may disregard the artificial steps, re-characterising the income as arising in Hong Kong. The tax assessed will be doubled under s.61A if the arrangement was artificial; additionally, the IRD may impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided under the Taxation (Trading and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance. For treaty abuse, the DTA’s LOB clause will deny treaty benefits, leading to full 16.5% tax on profits in China/HK, with interest on underpaid tax at 10% per annum.

        
Director personal liability: The veil will be pierced under common law fraud or sham doctrines. Partners and directors are personally liable for the entire tax deficiency, penalties, and interest if they knowingly used the partnership as a sham to misrepresent its substance. Courts may order restitution of assets transferred out of the partnership under insolvency laws. Travel bans are issued under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance s.76(2) to recover tax; a director may be arrested on leaving Hong Kong for failing to pay the assessed liabilities. There is also exposure under the Organised and Serious Crimes Ordinance if the sham is connected to money laundering.

      

      
      

        AML / Anti-Money Laundering – Breach of Record-Keeping and Sheltering Beneficial Ownership
        
Statutory sanction: AMLO Cap. 615, s.9(1) – failure to keep records: fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for 6 months; failure to perform CDD: fine of HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for 2 years. The Financial Services and Treasury Bureau may also impose civil penalties on the partnership and its compliance officer. For money laundering (Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing offences under Cap. 455, s.25), a conviction can lead to a fine of HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years.

        
Director personal liability: Directors or partners who knowingly allow the partnership to conceal beneficial ownership are treated as co-principals and can be personally indicted. The corporate veil offers no shield – courts will apply a 'controlling mind' test. In addition to criminal penalties, the court can order forfeiture of the warehouse assets and the proceeds of the goods. Directors face personal bankruptcy if restitution is ordered, and travel bans are routinely imposed pending trial. A personal asset freeze under the United Nations Anti-Terrorism Ordinance is possible. Permanent disqualification from serving as a director in Hong Kong is mandatory under the Company Ordinance for a conviction involving fraud.
