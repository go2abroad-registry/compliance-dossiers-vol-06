Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portal: eTAX (https://www.ird.gov.hk/etax) operated jointly with the Business Registration Office for commercial registration

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate tax identification number; the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) serves as the tax identifier for all entities including partnerships. For a foreign non-resident investing in a Hong Kong partnership: (1) File an application for business registration using Form IRBR1 (Business Registration Application) with the Business Registration Office, either in person, by post, or online via the eTAX portal under the Business Registration e-service; (2) Provide the partnership deed (as a Chinese or English translation), the foreign investor's identity and due-diligence documents (certified passport/permanent resident document, and corporate resolution if the partner is a company); (3) Pay the statutory Business Registration levy and registration fee (composite fee currently HKD 2,150 per year, subject to annual budgetary adjustments); (4) Receive the Business Registration Certificate with the BRN (standard format begins with the digits 1-3 plus 6 digits, not the 0 prefix used for some government departments or the tax file number of IRD); (5) Where a partnership is expected to derive any profits assessable to profits tax, the person in charge (usually the precedent partner) must also notify the IRD under section 51 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and the IRD will open an employer or partnership tax file and issue a separate revenue file number (tax number) for correspondence and returns; (6) For non-resident partners, no personal TIN is issued unless such partner also carries on a trade in Hong Kong and becomes independently chargeable; any foreign partner should nevertheless quote the partnership's BRN on all tax correspondence, profits tax returns (BIR51), and offshore claims. There is no minimum capital requirement for registering a Hong Kong partnership, and 100% foreign ownership is allowed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Hong Kong partnership is tax-transparent; profits tax is assessed on each partner separately for their respective share of partnership profits, subject to territorial source principle)

Available election forms

None (Hong Kong does not offer check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; the tax treatment of a partnership is legally prescribed under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and is based on territoriality, not on entity classifications available in other jurisdictions)

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General VAT/GST/Sales Tax (Hong Kong levies no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or broad-based sales and use tax; only selective excise duties on alcoholic beverages, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol, plus hotel accommodation tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable under indirect tax law (no VAT/GST registration threshold exists); however, every person carrying on a business in Hong Kong, including a partnership, must obtain a Business Registration Certificate within 1 month of commencement, irrespective of turnover, under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for indirect tax purposes because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST and consequently no zero-rating or export refund mechanism. For profits tax purposes, profits arising in the ordinary course of an offshore trade (where contracts are negotiated and concluded outside Hong Kong, including many fulfilment/kitting/assembly arrangements for foreign principals) are chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax only on a territorial source basis, and accordingly an offshore claim can be made to leave the profit beyond the scope of profits tax. The statutory test is case law (e.g., Hang Seng Bank Ltd v CIR), and the IRD's departmental interpretation and practice notes apply.

  • Hong Kong Export Declaration (submitted via the DTI electronic system through eTradelink) for any goods subject to statutory import/export declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, where the goods are physically moved through Hong Kong
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / cargo consignment note evidencing physical export and transit route out of Hong Kong
  • Sales contracts, purchase orders, and offer-and-acceptance correspondence evidencing that the substance of the transaction, including contract formation, occurred outside Hong Kong
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or equivalent bank credit advice (SWIFT MT103) evidencing the receipt of the sales proceeds from overseas customers
  • Packing lists, warehouse incident records, and kitting/assembly work orders demonstrating that the Hong Kong activity is limited to routine storage and value-added postponement with no key profit-generating activity in Hong Kong
  • Form IR1313A (Offshore Profits Claim Questionnaire) and supporting schedules as requested by the IRD when making an offshore claim

Not applicable (Hong Kong has no real-time e-invoicing clearance, API fiscalisation, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate; taxpayers may freely issue paper or electronic invoices, and the IRD accepts commercial electronic records as supporting evidence subject to general document retention rules under the Inland Revenue Ordinance)

No VAT clawback applicable because no VAT zero-rating exists. However, any offshore claim on profits tax is subject to strict IRD audit. If the IRD rejects the offshore claim or finds that the facts were misrepresented, the full amount becomes assessable to profits tax at 16.5% (for the year of assessment 2025/26, first HKD 2,000,000 at 8.25% through two-tiered rates), plus potential additional assessments for up to 6 years (or 10 years for fraudulent or negligent statements). Penalties under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) may rise to 200% of the tax undercharged, and criminal prosecution with imprisonment may apply where wilful evasion is shown.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible currency system under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with HKD traded freely against USD, RMB and other major currencies. The HKMA operates as a currency board, with banknote issuance backed by US dollars in the 7.75-7.85 per USD band; there are no exchange control restrictions, no approval requirements for capital account transactions, and no surrender obligations for trade or capital receipts. Profits, dividends, capital contributions, trade proceeds and loan repayments may be freely converted and remitted abroad in any currency. This full convertibility extends to the Hong Kong FIP as a legal entity. The practical liquidity constraint arises on the mainland side: RMB flows between the Hong Kong FIP and PRC entities are subject to SAFE registration, bank verification and, for capital account movements, prior approval under separately regulated cross-border RMB and foreign exchange regimes. Thus, while Hong Kong provides unrestricted convertibility, the treasury blueprint must include proper Chinese ODI approvals, trade documentation and payment purpose declarations for any capital injection or settlement that crosses the border, and must segregate mainland-source receipts caused by PRC exporter reconciliation rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong. There is no statutory surrender timeline, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no requirement to repatriate foreign currency export proceeds into HKD. The HK FIP may retain export receipts in USD, HKD, RMB or other currencies indefinitely. If underlying goods originate from mainland China, PRC current-account export verification rules apply to the mainland exporter and require reconciliation of customs declarations with foreign exchange receipts; however, the HK FIP itself is not subject to any export proceeds surrender regime.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for mainland-side cross-border fund movements, State Administration of Foreign Exchange of the People's Republic of China (SAFE) also applies

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Conditional and non-core. Hong Kong does not have a full EMI banking licence class; digital payment providers such as Airwallex operate under money service operator or stored value facility authorisations and cannot legally accept demand deposits or provide regulated trade finance. They are viable for small-value FX conversion, supplier refunds, merchant payouts and multi-currency wallet convenience, but the FIP should not route capital injections, tax-relevant settlements, or documentary trade transactions through these rails. Core liquidity and clearing must sit with HKMA-authorised banks to preserve audit integrity and avoid scope-of-licence risk.

Turnover: Hong Kong general partnerships and limited partnerships do not have statutory audit thresholds under the Companies Ordinance Cap. 622. A mandatory independent certified CPA audit is not triggered by revenue, balance sheet size, or headcount in the partnership itself. The only quantitative audit benchmarks in Hong Kong apply to private companies: a private company or a private company group loses audit exemption if it crosses at least two of the following three thresholds in a financial year: annual total revenue exceeding HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,560,000); total assets exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000); and 50 or more employees. A Hong Kong partnership may nevertheless become obliged to produce certified or audited accounts if the Inland Revenue Department issues a notice under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance demanding validated accounts, if the partnership agreement mandates an audit, if a bank or financial covenant requires an audit, or if any corporate partner must obtain audited financial statements for its own statutory filing in Hong Kong or abroad.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard People's Republic of China (PRC) Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate for an eligible corporate partner; 0% at the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) level because the FIP is fiscally transparent under Caishui (2008) No.159.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident company to a non-resident Hong Kong corporate shareholder under CIT Law; however, an FIP profit allocation is not technically a dividend because the partnership is transparent; the Hong Kong partner is taxed directly on its allocable business profits (25% net CIT if a PE is found, or a 10% Chinese-source withholding under the deemed profit method where no PE and the income is treaty-covered).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual CIT return due within five months after the fiscal year-end (Hong Kong/China fiscal year normally 31 December; due 31 May). Quarterly prepayments due within 15 days of each quarter-end. Non-resident withholding returns due within seven days following the payment to the Hong Kong partner; refund claims for treaty over-withholding may be filed within three years.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No.42 (2016), contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory when a taxpayer's annual related-party transaction in any category (tangible property, intangible property, finance, or services) reaches RMB 20 million or more; a group-wide Master File is mandatory when the consolidated annual revenue of the ultimate parent's group is RMB 5 billion or above. The HS codes / ISIC 5210 fulfillment kitting arrangement with the Hong Kong partner will almost always meet this threshold due to inventory transfers and service recharges.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the CIT Law's substance-over-form principle, Article 47 general anti-abuse rule, and the Mainland/HK DTA beneficial ownership requirement operate as an ESR-CIGA screen. The Core Income-Generating Activities of the fulfillment, kitting and assembly operation (receipt of inventory, handling, storage, assembly, order fulfilment, invoicing and logistics) occur in mainland China; therefore the Hong Kong partner must demonstrate its own staff, premises, decision-making, bank accounts and board activities in Hong Kong for any profit booked or treaty claim made. If the Hong Kong partner is only an invoice mill, the Chinese tax authority will treat the activity as a PE, allocate 100% of the profit to China and deny DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent income misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection Article 63: monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax plus daily 0.05% late-payment interest; serious cases are prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 with fines and confiscation; the tax authority may freeze bank accounts and distrain assets.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the Hong Kong partner is a bare shell or the directors signed false accounts; directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall, penalties and surcharges; the SAT may impose exit bans/travel restrictions and list the individuals in the blacklist; criminal conviction for tax fraud carries imprisonment of up to seven years for large sums.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and artificial profit stripping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and SAT Announcement 2016 No.42 permit the tax authority to perform transfer pricing adjustment, assess the underpaid tax, add benchmark interest and a penalty interest premium, and impose documentation fines; if the deliberate mispricing converts to evasion, Article 63 penalties of up to 5 times the adjusted tax apply; tax authorities may look back ten years for serious historical tax evasion cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing declarations can be held personally liable for the restored tax and interest; they may be subject to seizure of personal property, exit bans, loss of tax creditworthiness, and debarment from senior positions; if the non-arm's-length arrangement is found to have fraudulent intention, the responsible manager faces up to seven years imprisonment and criminal fines.
Shell entity / DTA abuse and AML non-compliance

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and the PRC tax treaty anti-abuse doctrine allow the denial of treaty benefits; the full 25% CIT or 10% statutory WHT is restored plus daily surcharges and penalties; AML law violations for false declarations or concealed beneficial ownership can lead to fines of up to 10 times the suspicious transaction amount and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Articles 191 and 312; tax authorities may also issue a public blacklist order preventing the entity from participating in cross-border transactions.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the corporate veil because the Hong Kong shell lacks independent economic substance; directors and shadow principals owe the full tax liabilities from their own assets; the people's courts impose personal travel bans (exit restrictions) and can compel repatriation of offshore assets; criminal money-laundering convictions subject individuals to up to ten years' imprisonment, dissolution of assets, and disqualification from serving as directors; the Hong Kong general partner of an LPF or the FIP manager may also face personal liability under partnership law for wrongful distributions.
