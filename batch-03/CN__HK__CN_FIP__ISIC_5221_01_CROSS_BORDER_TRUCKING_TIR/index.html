Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through provincial/municipal tax service bureaus and the official E-Tax Bureau online platform.

E-government filing portal

Within 30 days after obtaining the Foreign-Invested Partnership business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation, file tax registration with the competent local tax service bureau via the E-Tax Bureau online platform or in person. Submit the business license, partnership agreement, identification and tax-residence certificates of the foreign and Hong Kong partners, registered address proof, bank account information, and authorized-person identity documents. The tax authority assigns the Unified Social Credit Code as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), registers the partnership as a VAT taxpayer (small-scale or general), enrolls it for individual income tax withholding for individual partners and corporate income tax registration for corporate partners, issues a digital invoice quota, and opens the taxpayer electronic account. Changes to partnership structure, capital or business scope must be reported to the tax authority within 30 days.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None as a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. China treats partnerships as statutorily pass-through for income tax; the only relevant election is the VAT general-taxpayer election for an FIP whose annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5 million, which is subject to STA approval and the regular VAT invoice and clearance regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply in China with no turnover exemption. Mandatory general taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Domestic road transport is generally taxed at 9%, while qualifying export transport is zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer FIP that provides international transportation services by road from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR/Macau SAR, or through TIR transit to a foreign destination, may apply the 0% VAT rate. The service must be genuinely cross-border, evidenced by TIR Carnet, CMR waybill, customs export declaration for the goods carried, and a direct contractual and banking receipt relationship with a counterparty located outside mainland China. Input VAT related to the zero-rated journey (fuel, tolls, repairs, equipment) is creditable or refundable under the ordinary VAT refund and export-service zero-rating rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form) for the goods carried
  • TIR Carnet with customs office stamps and seals at departure, exit and arrival
  • CMR consignment note under the Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road
  • TIR vehicle approval certificate and customs registration documentation
  • Commercial invoice and signed cross-border transport services contract
  • Bank foreign-exchange collection receipt / service trade revenue voucher issued by an authorized bank in China

All invoices must be issued through the STA's full digital electronic invoice system on the E-Tax Bureau portal; issuance and transmission to the tax authority occur in real time, with automated anti-fraud risk screening and no physical stamping clearance. The zero-rated export invoice must be a digital VAT invoice referencing the transport contract and the TIR/customs documentation, and is cross-checked against customs and banking declarations.

The STA and customs conduct risk-based audits of zero-rated transport claims by cross-referencing TIR Carnet stamps, customs export declarations, CMR waybills, invoices and bank FX receipts. If the documentation is incomplete, the 180-day FX collection deadline is missed without valid commercial justification, or the export nature is found false, the zero rate is clawed back and VAT is assessed at the applicable rate (currently 9% for road transport) with overdue interest. Administrative penalties may be 50% to 500% of the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent zero-rating or export-refund claims constitute export-tax-refund fraud and can trigger customs penalties, SAFE sanctions, tax-priority blacklisting and criminal liability under PRC law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC foreign exchange administration framework, the RMB is convertible for genuine current account trade and transport services, but the capital account remains managed. For this CN/HK cross-border trucking FIP, the partnership must complete SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration before receiving HK partner capital; the capital receipt must be channeled through a designated onshore bank in the SAFE capital project information system. Current account receipts from TIR freight services must be repatriated to a PRC foreign exchange account, after which the entity may either retain 100% in foreign currency or convert to RMB at the bank's onshore rate; blanket mandatory surrender is no longer imposed. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is lawful only after annual tax settlement, payment of all applicable partner-level income taxes, issuance of a tax settlement certificate, audit of the partnership financial statements, and SAFE verification of the underlying freight income; remittance can be blocked if the bank cannot match TIR Carnet exit/entry stamps with invoices. The Hong Kong SAR leg has no exchange controls and HKD is freely convertible, creating an asymmetric corridor that SAFE monitors for round-tripping. Cross-border offsets, same-day matching flows, and artificial freight invoicing are prohibited; the treasury must demonstrate bona fide transportation economics.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export-type services of the FIP, trade-related foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated to a PRC-designated bank account no later than 180 days from the customs exit date or service completion date shown on the TIR Carnet. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain 100% in a foreign exchange settlement account or sell any portion to the bank. However, if the proceeds are retained, the bank must still verify them under SAFE's electronic trade monitoring system, and any conversion to RMB for operational use requires a valid commercial invoice. If the 180-day repatriation deadline is breached without an approved delayed-collection filing, the bank will downgrade the entity's trade facilitation rating and SAFE may impose administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, which exercises foreign exchange control under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the Hong Kong SAR clearing leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as primary clearing partner for the Mainland leg. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Currenxie do not hold PRC banking licences and cannot lawfully receive FDI capital, settle TIR cargo revenue, or issue tax-compliant China bank statements; PBOC/SAFE regulations treat deposit-taking and RMB settlement as bank-only activities. EMIs may act only as supplemental payment integrators for overseas fuel card reloads if they partner with a licensed sender and do not hold the entity's treasury balances. For the Hong Kong leg, HKMA-licensed virtual banks are permissible for HK current accounts and FX but still cannot service onshore SAFE reporting.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from HK partners must be routed to the FIP's SAFE-designated bank account in the PRC. The receiving bank must obtain a SWIFT MT103 from the remitting Hong Kong bank, complete SAFE FDI registration in the capital project information system, and screen the payment narrative for capital-contribution purpose. Use the bank's SAFE capital account code and include in field 70 the FDI registration reference and the /INV/CAP/FIP/ marker. The bank must implement payment-based settlement: foreign exchange is converted to RMB only after the FIP submits verified business contracts, TIR-related invoices, and planned payment use. A licensed PRC CPA must issue a capital verification confirmation once the contribution is booked. Repatriating HK funds in RMB through designated clearing channels is permissible but still requires the same SAFE validation to avoid disguised loan risk.Operational outbound payments for cargo insurance, repairs, tolls, fuel, and cross-border logistics services should be settled by SWIFT MT103 for direct suppliers. For large fleet purchases and fuel supply contracts, use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (MT700/MT707) with presentation of transport and survey documents; documentary collections (DP/DA) may be used where both counterparties accept bank-added control. Fuel card clearing must go through a licensed bank or card acquirer that supports actual card-holder purchase data, not unmapped cash withdrawals. TIR toll and bond guarantee payments should be drawn from an RMB operating prepaid account or bank card, with each payment supported by invoice and vehicle registration/GPS data. All outward remittances must use genuine trade purpose codes and be released only after SAFE system reconciliation.Onshore RMB/foreign currency forward contracts with a licensed PRC bank, restricted to genuine underlying FX exposure from confirmed TIR freight invoices, with no speculative rollover beyond 12 months.Non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts written by the Hong Kong treasury vehicle for offshore CNH/HKD exposure, not executable onshore; onshore NDF remains prohibited for PRC-resident entities without SAFE case-by-case approval.Cross-currency swap (CCS) between RMB and HKD/USD with SAFE-approved hedging purpose for the FIP capital injection and long-term equipment loans, requiring a documented cash flow schedule and annual effectiveness review; pure FX speculation is forbidden.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong is statutorily required to have its financial statements audited by a certified public accountant (practising) if: (a) annual turnover in Hong Kong exceeds the small entity threshold of HK$5,000,000 (approximately USD 640,000); or (b) total assets exceed HK$3,000,000 (approximately USD 385,000); or (c) headcount exceeds 20 employees; or (d) the partnership has any cross-border or offshore income claim requiring territorial tax treatment, which requires an audited computation; or (e) the partnership is required by its parent or funding institution to prepare consolidated financial statements. For the described ISIC 5221 cross-border TIR trucking entity, annual revenues will almost certainly exceed the HK$5,000,000 threshold given the high-value nature of bonded logistics, thus compulsory statutory audit applies.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual EIT return is due within 5 months after the fiscal year-end, i.e. by 31 May of the following year for a 31 December year-end. Quarterly provisional EIT prepayment returns are due within 15 days after the end of each quarter.
