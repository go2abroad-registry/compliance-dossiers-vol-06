Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Hong Kong SAR (for limited partnership registration) and Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department (for business registration certificate).

Governing statute

Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37) of the Laws of Hong Kong, particularly sections 4 (Registration), 5 (Effect of Registration), and 7 (Particulars to be Registered); Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) sections 6 and 9; Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) Part 11 (Non-Hong Kong Companies) as applicable to foreign-invested partnerships; and the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) for general partnership principles.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form LP1 (Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership) and Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration)

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 20 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships. Capital contributions are determined by the partnership agreement and may be in cash, property, or services. No escrow or blocked account is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification is required from the Fire Services Department (FSD) for any onshore facilities, including offices and warehouses. For vessels, the Marine Department issues a Certificate of Survey and a Safety Management Certificate under the International Safety Management (ISM) Code. Occupational safety on vessels is governed by the Merchant Shipping (Safety) Ordinance (Cap. 369) and the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509). The vessel must undergo a structural load inspection and obtain a Load Line Certificate. Additionally, a Port Facility Security Plan must be approved by the Marine Department under the International Ship and Port Facility Security (ISPS) Code.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships. Capital contributions are determined by the partnership agreement and may be in cash, property, or services. No escrow or blocked account is mandated.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online registration and compliance portal: National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax platform) accessible at the provincial STA electronic tax bureau websites under the unified China Tax electronic portal; tax affairs handled through the Electronic Tax Bureau (Electronic Tax Administration) system with the Unified Social Credit Code as the master data key.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established by Hong Kong and other non-resident partners, the mandatory sequence is: (1) Complete SAMR (State Administration for Market Regulation) enterprise name approval and business license registration; the business license contains the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which by operation of law constitutes the FIP's Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number; (2) Foreign partners must supply notarized, apostilled/legalized identity or incorporation documents with certified Chinese translation - for Hong Kong partners, a Hong Kong Certificate of Incorporation and current Business Registration Certificate; (3) Within 30 calendar days of receiving the business license, complete the initial tax registration (including income tax, VAT, withholding, city maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge) at the competent local STA branch of the FIP's registered address using the e-Tax portal or in-person counter; (4) Complete the FIP's tax registration information report, including partnership agreement allocation ratios, partners' identities and residential status; (5) Enroll for the fully digitalized electronic invoice system and tax control device authorization; (6) If the FIP will engage in vessel chartering subject to water transport licensing by the Ministry of Transport, the tax registration file must attach the corresponding shipping business license (Ship Operating Permit or Non-Vessel Operating Common Carrier endorsement as applicable).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - The FIP is fiscally transparent for China Enterprise Income Tax: the partnership itself is not a taxpayer; profits are allocated to partners pursuant to the partnership agreement and taxed in the hands of the partners; non-resident partners are taxed only on China-source income under the territorial scope principle, and HK resident partners may claim offshore credit relief under the China-Hong Kong comprehensive double tax arrangement.

Available election forms

None - China has no check-the-box entity classification election or hybrid mismatch election analogous to the U.S. or OECD regimes; the pass-through transparency of an FIP is mandatory under the Measures for the Administration of Foreign-Invested Partnerships. Available elections limited to: (a) VAT status election between small-scale taxpayer simplified method (3% levy rate, no input credit) and general taxpayer method (standard rate with input VAT credit) where annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000; (b) partnership profit/loss allocation ratios as stipulated in the partnership agreement, subject to arm's length substance requirements; (c) election to apply deemed expense methodology for non-resident partner withholding in certain cross-border scenarios as prescribed by STA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is compulsory for every entity obtaining a business license regardless of turnover; the Unified Social Credit Code is the VAT registration number. Mandatory general taxpayer status applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the FIP may elect small-scale taxpayer status at the 3% simplified levy rate, subject to current preferential reductions and the monthly small-scale exemption policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and the STA export VAT measures, the zero rate (0% VAT with full input credit and refund) applies to: (a) international water transport services engaging vessels to carry passengers or cargo from a port in China customs territory to a port outside China customs territory; and (b) cross-border services supplied to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China, including the chartering and operation of offshore platform support vessels hired by non-resident oil and gas contractors for voyages and station-keeping exclusively outside China customs territory. If the chartered vessel performs any part of the contract within Chinese territorial waters or is used by a domestic operating company in China's continental shelf operations, the service is taxable at the standard (9%) rate and zero-rating is unavailable. The FIP must establish a complete paper trail demonstrating the offshore destination of the service, the non-resident party contracting the charter, and the receipt of foreign currency consideration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs export bill / export customs clearance document) evidencing the physical departure or deemed export of the vessel or vessel-related supplies from China customs territory, where goods or spares are involved
  • Bill of Lading (or sea waybill / charter party receipt) showing carriage from a China port to a foreign destination or offshore installation outside China customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a designated Chinese foreign exchange bank evidencing the receipt and conversion of foreign currency charter hire from the non-resident charterer
  • Cross-border vessel charter agreement (English/Chinese bilingual) specifying the charter party, offshore service area, and non-resident counterparty
  • Commercial invoice and packing list (bilingual) together with the fully digitalized VAT invoice annotated as zero-rated export
  • Vessel delivery / redelivery certificates, voyage reports and satellite AIS logs proving the service was performed outside China customs territory
  • Service Trade Foreign Exchange Verification Notice and registration records from the SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) digital monitoring system confirming the foreign exchange receipt is matched to the declared export service
  • Ship Operation License or approved Vessel Operation Permit evidencing the legal capacity for international offshore marine charter during the VAT refund filing period

China has mandated the fully digitalized electronic invoice (Digital e-Invoice) system nationwide, replacing paper VAT invoices; every invoice must be issued through the National Electronic Tax Administration platform with real-time upload to the Golden Tax System Phase IV (anti-fraud tax control infrastructure); invoices carry a unique 20-digit electronic invoice number and encrypted QR code / digital anti-fraud stamp; no fiscal clearance stamping by a clearance authority is required for issuance, but all invoice data is subject to near-real-time risk screening by the STA; tax control devices remain required for taxpayers that still issue paper special VAT invoices.

The STA, China Customs and SAFE maintain a joint export audit framework; claimed zero-rated refunds are examined against customs declarations, FX repatriation records and service trade verification. Where zero-rating is found improper: the refund and input VAT credits are clawed back with late-payment interest; a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount is imposed under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law for evasion by false export declarations; suppliers committing fraudulent export VAT refund are barred from export tax rebate qualification for a period, blacklisted in the Untrustworthy Taxpayers list and referred for criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for defrauding export tax refunds, punishable by up to life imprisonment and confiscation in aggravated cases; the retrospective clawback period is 5 years from the end of the taxable year, extended to 10 years where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed foreign exchange regime with full current-account convertibility for lawful trade and service transactions, but a strictly controlled capital account. RMB is fully convertible under the current account for remitting freight, charter hire, insurance premiums, and other genuine maritime operational expenses. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity or partnership capital contributions from Hong Kong into a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), require SAFE registration, a foreign exchange capital account, and post-receipt capital verification by a qualified Chinese accounting firm. Profit and dividend remittances from the FIP to its Hong Kong partners are permitted only after annual audits, corporate income tax settlement by the FIP in the Chinese mainland (or through its partners as pass-through if so classified), and the presentation of a completed 'Service Trade & Profit/Remittance Certificate' from the local SAFE branch or via the bank's digital compliance portal. No mandatory surrender or forced conversion ratio currently applies to export or service receipts, but all foreign currency earnings from offshore vessel charters and maritime support services must be repatriated into China within 180 days after the relevant customs declaration or service completion date. Repatriated dollars may be retained in a designated foreign exchange settlement account or converted to RMB at the market exchange rate. The FIP must also lodge a 'Payment/Delivery Verification' record with SAFE within 30 days of any remittance and maintain underlying charter party contracts, invoices, and operational logs as audit evidence. In essence, convertibility is available for genuine working capital and service flows, while equity, liquidity, and leverage-related capital movements are individually screened by SAFE and the reporting licensed commercial bank.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, export and service proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the export declaration or service completion date. There is no statutorily mandated conversion ratio; the FIP may hold the foreign currency in an approved foreign exchange settlement account or convert all or part of it into RMB at prevailing rate, but each transaction must be supported by the underlying offshore vessel chartering agreement, commercial invoices, and a valid marine logistics service contract.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from opening domestic RMB settlement accounts or serving as a formal depository bank for a Chinese-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. The PBOC and SAFE only license approved payment institutions for cross-border intermediary settlement, but such institutions do not carry the legal status of banks and cannot provide capital verification, FX conversion certificates, or SAFE-registered fiduciary accounts for a China FIP. The FIP must maintain its primary treasury relationship with a licensed domestic commercial bank, while a fintech EMI may be used at best as a niche overlay for small incidental payments, subject to full PBOC scrutiny and without replacing the bank account structure.

Turnover: Under Hong Kong law, an unincorporated limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37) has no statutory audit obligation solely by reason of size, turnover, or headcount. A mandatory certified CPA audit is legally triggered only when the partnership holds an SFC-regulated activity licence under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, is a licensed trust or company service provider under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance, is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap 637 whose fund documents require audited accounts, or is required under a vessel finance facility or charterer agreement. For Hong Kong private companies, by analogy, a mandatory statutory audit is triggered when the company is not a small private company, meaning it fails to satisfy at least two of three criteria: annual total revenue not exceeding HK$100 million, total assets not exceeding HK$100 million, and headcount not exceeding 100 employees. For this offshore platform support vessel chartering partnership, no compulsory size-based audit arises under Cap 622 unless the partnership is separately constituted as a company or a Hong Kong-registered vessel operating entity is subject to Marine Department licensing conditions requiring audited financial statements.
