Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department; and Limited Partnerships Section, Companies Registry, Hong Kong SAR

Governing statute

Not applicable as to Companies Act; the primary governing legislation is the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), particularly sections 3 (definition of partnership), 7 (continuation of partnership), 12 (rule that partnership property is held by partners) and 39 (joint and several liability), read together with the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) s.5, the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) ss.4-9, and the Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) Part 2 for AML/CTF duties of a Foreign-Invested Partnership carrying on a designated non-financial business in Hong Kong.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership. The partnership agreement may prescribe a subscribed capital schedule and drawdown mechanism; contributions become due only upon a call by the general partner. Where the entity is registered as a limited partnership, the amount contributed or agreed to be contributed by each limited partner must be stated in Form LP1, but no proof of deposit or escrow is required by law.

No Civil Defense fire permit is required for an office-only tenant.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax) of the STA. For the Hong Kong SAR partner/investor, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the separate competent authority for Hong Kong profits tax, but the mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) itself has no Hong Kong tax registration and is registered with the PRC tax authorities.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) under the Partnership Enterprise Law and the Foreign Investment Law; the AMR issues a Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT identification number for the FIP. Step 2: Within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, complete tax registration through the STA e-Tax platform at the competent tax office, submitting the partnership agreement, partner register, foreign investor identification documents, legal representative identity, PRC bank account details, and the crew recruitment/crew management business scope. Step 3: The competent STA office enters the FIP into the VAT taxpayer database and records whether the FIP is a small-scale taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer; general VAT taxpayer status is compulsory once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, or may be elected voluntarily if the FIP maintains proper accounting. Step 4: Apply for the all-digital electronic invoice (e-invoice) quota and link the USCC to the national e-invoice service platform; all subsequent VAT invoices, customs declarations, and foreign-exchange receipts will be automatically reconciled through the e-Tax data environment. Step 5: For each non-resident partner, the FIP must report the partner's home-jurisdiction tax reference number to the STA; if the non-resident partner has no PRC TIN, the tax authority will create an internal non-resident filing record for withholding on China-source partnership allocations. Step 6: A foreign non-resident entity supplying crew agency services into mainland China without forming a mainland FIP must separately register for VAT under its own name with the STA at the place of performance, using its foreign tax reference, the offshore service contract, and the non-resident's local contact details; the STA will assign a non-resident taxpayer record for VAT filing and invoice issuance.

Each partner, whether corporate or individual, resident or non-resident, is taxed on its distributable share of partnership income under the EIT Law or Individual Income Tax Law, as applicable. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the PRC-registered FIP is fiscally transparent for Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) purposes; the partnership itself is not an EIT taxpayer. Each partner, whether corporate or individual, resident or non-resident, is taxed on its distributable share of partnership income under the EIT Law or Individual Income Tax Law, as applicable. For VAT purposes, by contrast, the FIP is itself a taxable person and must account for output VAT, input VAT, and export zero-rating.

Available election forms

None in the US check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch sense. PRC tax law applies mandatory partnership transparency; no entity-level corporate income tax election exists for a partnership. The only available election is the voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer, which is an indirect-tax status election, not an income-tax election. The PRC-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement may be invoked by a qualifying HK partner to reduce withholding rates, but that is a treaty relief mechanism, not a domestic entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the statutory threshold requiring compulsory registration as a general VAT taxpayer. All newly formed FIPs must complete tax/VAT registration within 30 days of the Business License regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers are those with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000; non-resident suppliers with no mainland establishment must register from the first taxable supply performed in mainland China, with no de minimis turnover exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (i) physical goods exported from mainland China - such as ship stores, uniforms, and crew safety equipment sold to a non-resident shipowner - only when the goods are covered by a PRC Customs export declaration and the export proceeds are collected from the overseas buyer; and (ii) cross-border taxable services supplied to a non-resident recipient and consumed wholly outside mainland China. Maritime crew recruitment and crew management services supplied to a non-resident shipowner/operator for a vessel engaged in an international voyage can qualify for 0% VAT treatment where the service is consumed offshore and the consideration is paid in foreign currency by the non-resident principal. Services performed or consumed in mainland China, including recruitment of Chinese seafarers, domestic payroll processing, training in China, or crew agency margins sourced from China-resident payers, remain subject to VAT at 6%. The FIP must provide a complete audit file demonstrating the offshore nature of the service and the corresponding receipt of foreign exchange.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for each physical export shipment
  • Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill showing the overseas consignee/port of discharge
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-currency receipt/collection advice evidencing foreign-exchange repatriation
  • Signed offshore service agreement or crew management contract naming the non-resident shipowner/operator and specifying the international voyage
  • Commercial invoice and e-invoice statement expressly recording the 0% VAT treatment
  • Port clearance or vessel itinerary/IMO documentation proving the vessel's call at a PRC port and subsequent international voyage
  • Crew lists and seafarer employment contracts demonstrating that the crew were supplied to the non-resident operator

VAT invoices are cleared through the STA Golden Tax Phase IV environment. The FIP must issue all-digital electronic invoices (e-invoices) through the STA national e-invoice service platform or approved tax-control equipment, transmitting invoice data to the STA in near real-time via API/XML interfaces. Each invoice is authenticated by the STA and receives a unique tax-control code and anti-fraud QR/digital stamp before finalization; the STA automatically reconciles e-invoice data with VAT returns, customs declarations, and foreign-exchange receipts. Paper or manually completed invoices are not accepted for export zero-rating or input VAT credit.

The STA Golden Tax IV system cross-matches export declarations, bills of lading, e-invoices, VAT returns, contracts, and foreign-exchange receipts. If export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, or the supporting documents cannot be produced, the 0% rate is revoked and output VAT is assessed at the applicable rate, with late-payment interest of 0.05% per day computed from the original filing date. Fraudulent, seriously incorrect, or unsupported zero-rating claims are treated as tax evasion under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China; the STA retrospectively claws back the entire VAT refund/zero-rated input credit, imposes an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax, and may refer the case for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law for export-tax-refund or VAT-invoice fraud. Recovery periods may extend to 20 years for concealed fraudulent claims, and the FIP can lose general VAT taxpayer status and future eligibility for zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a Special Administrative Region with complete foreign exchange freedom. There is no central bank in the traditional sense; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) implements the Linked Exchange Rate System through a currency board, maintaining a USD/HKD band of 7.75–7.85. Both current account transactions and capital account transactions are freely convertible for residents and non-residents alike. The HKD is issued by three note-issuing banks under 100% backing in the Currency Board Account. No surrender requirements, exchange controls, prior approval mechanisms, or profit repatriation restrictions exist. Non-resident investors may freely remit dividends, royalties, and capital gains abroad. Cross-border flows between mainland China and Hong Kong remain subject to the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) in the mainland, but once funds reach Hong Kong they face no convertibility or remittance hurdles. The currency is fully convertible and there is no administrative distinction between current and capital account treatments in foreign currency pricing. Profit remittance hurdles are absent, and there is no statutory requirement to convert any export earnings into HKD or any other domestic currency. This makes Hong Kong a neutral liquidity hub and a treasury gateway for a Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in ship crew recruitment, facilitating multi-currency payroll clearing through global correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. The Hong Kong SAR imposes no export proceeds surrender rules. All export foreign exchange proceeds may be retained in HKD, USD, or any other freely convertible currency. There are no statutory timelines for conversion and no mandatory ratios.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI services providers operating without an HKMA-authorized banking license are not legally permitted to maintain deposit-taking or remittance accounts for Hong Kong corporate entities under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155). A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must therefore rely on domestic licensed banks. Licensed virtual banks (e.g., ZA Bank Limited, Airstar Bank Limited) are viable for low-risk operations but may present limitations in multi-currency payroll clearing and compliance acceptance for partnership structures. The optimal viability rating for the target crewing agency is Low to Medium for fintech/EMI-only solutions and High for the Tier-1 banks listed above, because these banks possess global correspondent clearing lines necessary for seafarer payroll disbursements and cross-border trade settlements.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% for corporations / 15% for unincorporated partnerships; two-tiered profits tax rate applies to the first HK$2 million of assessable profits at 8.25% for corporations and 7.5% for unincorporated businesses

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose any statutory dividend withholding tax. Distributions or profit shares remitted by the Foreign-Invested Partnership to partners, whether resident or non-resident, are not subject to a separate dividend withholding tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be completed and filed within one month from the date of issue of BIR52. For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the return is ordinarily issued in April and due by early May; for a 30 June fiscal year-end, the return is ordinarily issued in November and due by mid-December. A tax representative may apply to the IRD for an extension.
