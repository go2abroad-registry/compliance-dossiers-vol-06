Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Foreign-Invested Partnership under Hong Kong law. Each partner shall contribute capital to a designated escrow bank account prior to issuance of the Business Registration Certificate; capital may be in cash, property, or services, but must be fully recorded in the partnership capital account ledger. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Hong Kong partnership is fiscally transparent; no entity-level profits tax; partners are assessed individually on their shares, and only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are taxable under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap. 112).

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no partnership classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election. The pass-through treatment of partnerships is mandatory under Hong Kong tax law. Offshore profits exclusion is claimed factually and not by election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No national VAT, GST, or sales and use tax in Hong Kong; the indirect tax landscape consists of narrow excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and non-tariff import/export declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

HKD 0 / not applicable for VAT or GST. Every person carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain Business Registration under Cap. 310 regardless of turnover. For hydrocarbon oil used in marine bunkering, a Dutiable Commodities Licence under Cap. 109 and the applicable excise payment or duty suspension conditions may apply; this is an excise control, not a VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT or GST to zero-rate. For profits tax, cross-border marine bunkering income may be treated as offshore and not chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax only if the profits have a source outside Hong Kong. The taxpayer bears the burden of proving that negotiation, execution and performance took place outside Hong Kong; physical delivery of fuel to a vessel within Hong Kong waters will generally indicate a Hong Kong source. There is no VAT refund mechanism.

  • Bunker delivery note and marine fuel sale confirmation identifying vessel name, delivery location, fuel grade and quantity
  • Outward ship or cargo manifest and electronic export declaration acknowledgment lodged under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations where the supply is recorded as exported ship's stores
  • Commercial contract and purchase/sale confirmations showing negotiation and execution place
  • Port clearance or certificate of port of call and vessel departure record evidencing the international voyage
  • Bank settlement and foreign exchange records, including inward remittance advice issued by an Authorized Institution under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155), in place of or equivalent to an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Bill of lading or voyage confirmation where cargo movement is separately documented

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing clearance mandate, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies to Hong Kong tax invoices. IRD accepts electronic business records if the entity satisfies the record-keeping obligations in the Inland Revenue Ordinance and Business Registration Ordinance. Marine bunkering documentation (bunker delivery note, deposit records, vessel manifests, Trade Single Window declarations, and bank payment records) must be retained, but there is no government pre-clearance of invoices.

The Inland Revenue Department scrutinises offshore profits claims on a full-fact basis. If a taxpayer fails to substantiate the offshore source of bunkering income, IRD will assess the profits to Hong Kong profits tax, compute underpaid tax, and may impose statutory fines and additional tax. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap. 112, incorrect returns and failure to notify chargeability can result in penalties up to treble the tax undercharged plus statutory fines, and criminal liability for wilful evasion carries higher fines and imprisonment. Assessments may be raised or reopened within six years; the period extends to ten years in cases involving fraud or wilful evasion. There is no VAT clawback because no VAT is levied, but any excise duty privileges or duty suspension granted for hydrocarbon oil under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance are subject to audit by the Customs and Excise Department, recovery of unpaid duty, and applicable penalty provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully open capital account under the Basic Law of the Hong Kong SAR and operates the Linked Exchange Rate System. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible at the HKMA's 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar conversion band. No surrender requirements apply, and profit remittance can be executed through any authorised bank without central approval. The absence of exchange controls does not, however, eliminate regulatory friction when funds move between mainland China and Hong Kong. The People's Republic of China's State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) retains current-account convertibility under a genuine transaction test and capital-account convertibility under specific licences and registrations. A Chinese foreign-invested partnership counterparty must evidence the underlying bunkering trade contract, invoice, shipping documents, and SAFE registration when subscribing capital to a Hong Kong partner or receiving distributions. Bunkering for ocean-going vessels is generally classified as offshore trade, making USD settlement eligible for current-account treatment if the goods are physically delivered outside mainland customs territory. For Hong Kong treasury purposes, the practical constraints are therefore documentary rather than conversion-based: every cross-border remittance must have a clean audit trail, a valid international transaction code, and no mismatch in beneficiary name, vessel name, or purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR statutory framework imposes no export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion percentage; marine fuel export receipts are freely retained in USD, HKD, or any fully convertible currency. Where the operating parent is a mainland China foreign-invested partnership, current-account export proceeds must be repatriated through designated banks within 180 days from customs declaration or payment settlement date when the export is declared on the mainland; SAFE does not prescribe a fixed surrender ratio, and conversion may be executed at free-market rates.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the jurisdiction's de facto central bank, with coordinated control exercised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China for any capital or current account remittance touching mainland Chinese partners or parent entities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs have low viability as the principal treasury rail for this Hong Kong-based marine bunkering partnership. Hong Kong does not issue an 'EMI' licence; regulated non-bank institutions fall under the HKMA Stored Value Facility or virtual banking regimes. While HKMA-licensed virtual banks may open accounts remotely, none currently offers the full trade financing, capital verification escrow, and bilateral trade LC infrastructure required for bunkering settlements. Mainland SAFE regulations also disallow a Chinese foreign-invested partnership from clearing contractual trade proceeds through an unlicensed offshore EMI without a bank drawee rail. Therefore, fintech EMIs are permissible only as a sub-limit operational float for office expenses, never for the core capital escrow or vessel disbursement account.

While HKMA-licensed virtual banks may open accounts remotely, none currently offers the full trade financing, capital verification escrow, and bilateral trade LC infrastructure required for bunkering settlements. Mainland SAFE regulations also disallow a Chinese foreign-invested partnership from clearing contractual trade proceeds through an unlicensed offshore EMI without a bank drawee rail. Therefore, fintech EMIs are permissible only as a sub-limit operational float for office expenses, never for the core capital escrow or vessel disbursement account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds: HKD 10,000,000 for controlled goods transactions and HKD 5,000,000 for controlled services, intangible and financing transactions; Master File is required when the consolidated group turnover exceeds HKD 200,000,000, in line with IRD DIPN 60.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies an economic substance doctrine through the FSIE regime; a foreign-invested partnership claiming offshore exemption for ship bunkering must prove a Hong Kong fixed place of business, adequate employees, and local decision-making for Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as fuel sourcing, price negotiation, contract execution, delivery logistics, invoicing and treasury management. The classic ESR and CIGA principles are mirrored in IRD DIPN 58 and OECD permanent establishment and beneficial ownership jurisprudence; substance failure converts offshore income into taxable Hong Kong-source income and may also block treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: IRO sections 80(2) and 82A: criminal fine up to HKD 50,000, imprisonment up to 3 years and additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged; IRD may also issue estimated assessments and enforce civil recovery of the unpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil of the corporate general partner; directors who approve the false return are personally liable for civil restitution of underpaid tax and penalties, may be blacklisted with travel restriction orders where tax debt exceeds HKD 10,000, may be disqualified under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622, and may face criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: IRD may adjust assessable profits under IRO section 50AAF and impose additional tax under section 82A up to 100% of the undercharged tax; failing to provide transfer pricing documentation can attract a HKD 10,000 fine and three times the tax undercharged, and the penalty may be imposed in addition to the TP adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who sign incorrect returns or TP documentation are personally exposed to civil assessments and asset restitution, disqualification orders, travel bans under IRD collection powers, and criminal prosecution for false statements where knowledge and intentionality are established.
Shell Company, AML and Bunkering Licence Abuse

Statutory sanction: Wrongful dealing with proceeds of crime is indictable under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance and Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance Cap. 615, with penalties on indictment of up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years; unlicensed bunkering attracts Shipping and Port Control Ordinance sanctions and forfeiture of fuel or vessels.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the partnership is a mere façade; directors may be personally liable for civil restitution of all laundered funds, property restraint orders may freeze personal assets, immigration blacklisting and travel bans may be imposed, disqualification under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 is available, and extradition or cross-border criminal proceedings may follow for serious money laundering and fraud offences.
