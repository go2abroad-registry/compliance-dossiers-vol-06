Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637), Part 3 registration provisions and section 11; Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 352), sections 3 to 7; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), sections 5 and 6. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong. Capital contributions are contractual and may be cash, assets, or services agreed in the partnership agreement. No blocked escrow account is required at incorporation, and no capital verification certificate is required, although stamp duty may arise on an agreement transferring immovable property or shares if used as a capital contribution.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office (BRO); online service channels are the IRD eTax portal and GovHK business e-services.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business. The partnership must submit the prescribed business registration application form for partnerships, together with: (i) the executed partnership agreement; (ii) a certificate of registration from the Companies Registry if the entity is a limited partnership registered under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37); (iii) identity or incorporation documents for every general and limited partner, including non-resident partners; (iv) a certified copy of the partnership's overseas registration certificate, if any, and the registered or principal business address of each foreign partner; and (v) proof of the Hong Kong business address and particulars of the person responsible for management in Hong Kong. The application may be delivered to the BRO in person or submitted electronically through the IRD's official e-services. Upon approval, the BRO issues a Business Registration Certificate and a Business Registration Number, which serves as the partnership's Hong Kong tax identification reference. The IRD subsequently opens profits tax files and assigns Tax Reference Numbers to the partnership and to each partner for assessment of the partner's respective share of partnership profits. Because a partnership is transparent for Hong Kong profits tax, there is no separate corporate tax identification number for the partnership; each partner uses the partner's own IRD Tax Reference Number. Non-resident partners who do not maintain a place of business in Hong Kong may be required by the IRD to provide particulars and, where necessary, to appoint an authorized Hong Kong resident representative or person in charge under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) for service of documents and performance of partnership tax obligations. Changes in partners, business address, or cessation of business must be reported to the BRO within the statutory period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the partnership is transparent; profits tax is chargeable on each partner's share of profits, not on the FIP itself)

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not offer US-style check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch elections. Individual resident partners may, however, elect personal assessment under Part VII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) to aggregate assessable income and apply personal allowances, where beneficial.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax. Hong Kong imposes only selective excise duties on certain dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold. Business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is mandatory for every person carrying on a business in Hong Kong within one month of commencement, without any turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on supplies, goods exported from Hong Kong or services supplied for offshore use are outside the scope of any Hong Kong consumption tax; there is therefore no zero-rating claim or refund mechanism to substantiate. For profits tax purposes only, income from offshore towage and salvage services may be treated as sourced outside Hong Kong under the territorial source principle, but this is a profits tax analysis governed by section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), not a VAT/GST zero-rating rule.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The FIP's functional treasury and FX environment is grounded in mainland China, not Hong Kong, so convertibility is governed by PBOC and SAFE. China maintains current-account convertibility with documentary verification and selective capital-account convertibility through the foreign-investment registration system. Current-account receipts from offshore towage and salvage services may be credited to a domestic foreign-currency account and either retained or voluntarily converted into CNY after verification of the underlying service contract, invoice, and tax filings. Capital-account transactions, including foreign-partner capital contributions, partnership capital reductions, liquidation, and outbound profit distributions, require SAFE registration, the opening of a designated Foreign-Exchange Capital Account, and a CPA capital verification or equivalent evidence. Profit remittance is permissible after annual audit, tax clearance, and partnership resolution, provided the amount is consistent with disclosed earnings and the foreign investor's equity/partnership share. For a China-registered FIP with Hong Kong treasury pass-through, additional scrutiny applies to beneficial ownership, source-of-funds, sanctions exposure, and the economic substance of the HK intermediary. Full CNY free float or unrestricted capital-account convertibility is not available.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory conversion percentage or forced surrender of export or service-trade foreign-currency receipts under current SAFE rules. Since the 2012 reform, the fixed surrender obligation was abolished; a Chinese FIP receiving foreign-currency consideration for offshore towage and salvage services must repatriate such current-account receipts into China through a designated bank in a timely manner and report them under the service-trade and/or goods-trade foreign-exchange monitoring system. Goods-trade export proceeds are generally expected to be collected within the statutory monitoring window, commonly referenced as 180 days from the customs declaration date. Service-trade proceeds, including Lloyd's salvage awards, P&I insurance recoveries, and emergency towing fees, are subject to reasonableness review based on the contract, invoice, and delivery records, and must not be held offshore for non-business reasons. The FIP may retain receipts in a current-account foreign-currency account or freely settle them into CNY without prior approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-nil as a primary treasury rail for a China-registered FIP. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual banking platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, or similar are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open or maintain a compliant local capital account, basic RMB settlement account, or foreign-exchange capital account for a Chinese FIP. They cannot provide the required SAFE registration links, capital verification reporting, outbound-service-trade tax certification support, or CNY clearing in China. Furthermore, capital verification and profit remittance require a regulated domestic commercial bank. An EMI may be used only as an ancillary collection account for small third-party receivables outside China, and only after the FIP has validated the EMI's anti-money laundering controls, sanctions compliance, and bankruptcy remoteness. Any attempt to treat such an EMI as the main treasury account would breach PBOC's RMB bank account measures and SAFE's foreign exchange administration rules.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

A China-registered FIP using a Hong Kong pass-through treasury or escrow entity, which may present as an artificial shell arrangement obscuring the true economic location and final use of funds.

Root cause: A China-registered FIP using a Hong Kong pass-through treasury or escrow entity, which may present as an artificial shell arrangement obscuring the true economic location and final use of funds.

Defense evidence: Provide a full ownership chart tracing every partner to the ultimate natural-person beneficial owner; the Hong Kong entity's Business Registration Certificate, Company Register extract, lease agreement, staff list, and substantive-presence records; the notarised Chinese and English FIP partnership agreement; declarations from P&I clubs and vessel charterers; audited financial statements of the HK pass-through entity; and a no-shell certification from the Hong Kong registered agent.

Large one-off influxes from Lloyd's escrow, insurance/ P&I settlements, or emergency salvage awards exceed normal transaction monitoring thresholds and may appear inconsistent with the FIP's registered business scope.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity/partnership capital must be routed from the foreign partner's bank directly into the FIP's SAFE-regulated Foreign-Exchange Capital Account at a qualified onshore commercial bank. The SWIFT MT103 instruction must clearly identify the FIP name and reference the purpose as FDI capital contribution to a Chinese foreign-invested partnership. The remitter must be the registered foreign partner, not a third party, or the bank will reject the inflow. The receiving bank will verify the FIP's Foreign Investment Information Report, the Foreign Investment Negative List status, the notarised partnership agreement, and the SAFE capital identification code. After credit, the FIP may either retain the foreign currency or apply for capital-account settlement into CNY through the bank's capital project information system. No early pre-conversion is permitted; the settlement must respond to an identified business need. A CPA capital verification report should be prepared for the contribution, and the partnership must promptly update SAMR/SAFE reporting records.Outbound trade and service-trade payments from the FIP should be executed via SWIFT MT103 from the onshore operating account into the beneficiary's bank, using the correct Chinese foreign-exchange purpose code and referencing the underlying towage/salvage contract and invoice. For physical supply contracts for bunkers, tow gear, or equipment, a Commercial Letter of Credit or standby LC may be used if requested by the supplier; for typical maritime salvage disbursements, clean telegraphic transfer is preferred. All service-trade outbound payments above applicable thresholds must be accompanied by an outbound-payment tax certification issued by the Chinese tax authority and supported by the contract, invoice, proof of service performance, and sanctions screening records. For Lloyd's Standard Form escrow settlements, the escrow release instructions should be matched to an MT103 wire with a full remittance narrative, and HKD flows in Hong Kong can clear through HKCHATS while USD flows clear through CHIPS/Fedwire and cross-border RMB flows through CIPS.Onshore CNY forward exchange contracts with a SAFE-qualified bank, permitted only under the real-demand principle to hedge documented current-account cash flows such as towage receivables, salvage fees, and disbursement obligations.Cross-currency swaps linked to approved foreign-currency debt or capital-account exposures, restricted to tenor-matched hedging and subject to SAFE registration and underlying transaction verification.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) and CNH forwards may be used at the Hong Kong treasury or offshore parent level to hedge CNH/USD exposure, but cannot be booked inside the mainland FIP and are not permissible substitutes for onshore SAFE-regulated hedging.

Turnover: For Hong Kong corporate and registered non-Hong Kong entities, a mandatory independent CPA audit is required unless the audit exemption under section 359D of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) is available. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for partnership profits under the unincorporated business rate of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s5(1); a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is pass-through for profits tax, and each corporate partner is further chargeable at 16.5% on its allocated assessable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong law imposes no withholding tax on distributions, profit shares or dividend-equivalent payments made by the FIP to non-resident partners. No Hong Kong source tax is deducted at the time of repatriation.
