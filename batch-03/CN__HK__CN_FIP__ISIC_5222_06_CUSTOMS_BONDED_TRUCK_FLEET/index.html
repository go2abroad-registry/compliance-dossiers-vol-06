Virtual offices and commercial mailbox addresses are rejected for a Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in customs-bonded road feeder trucking. The local SAMR office, tax bureau, and banking partners will require a physical leased business premise in the registered administrative district, a dedicated office with signage, an original lease contract, and a person-in-charge present during normal business hours. Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No minimum paid-up capital applies to a Foreign-Invested Partnership under PRC partnership law. Partners are free to agree on cash or in-kind capital contributions in the partnership agreement, and no escrow or blocked-account requirement is imposed. Inbound foreign exchange contributions from non-resident partners must be routed to a partnership bank account and may require SAFE registration and foreign currency account opening procedures.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety inspection certificate from the municipal Fire and Rescue Department for the parking, dispatch and maintenance depot; civil defence structural load inspection and fire acceptance for any enclosed maintenance building; workplace safety registration under the Work Safety Law with occupational hazard monitoring; fire extinguisher inspections for all vehicles; and annual road vehicle safety inspections for each bonded truck.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with the National Electronic Tax Administration platform (e-Taxation portal) used for taxpayer registration, VAT filing, digital invoice issuance, and export tax administration; for the Hong Kong SAR jurisdictional leg, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTAX portal govern any Hong Kong profits tax and Business Registration Ordinance obligations, but the primary foreign-invested partnership tax identification is established with STA.

E-government filing portal

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (CN_FIP) is first registered with the Administration for Market Regulation to obtain a Business License bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the authority data-sharing channel and constitutes the partnership's China Tax Identification Number. The partnership then completes Tax Registration Information Confirmation through the National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Taxation) portal, where it declares its principal business as ISIC 5222 bonded road feeder trucking, confirms the responsible tax officer, financial and accounting systems, shareholder/partner details, and any foreign non-resident partner information. Foreign non-resident partners that are not already registered in China must provide their incorporation/passport evidence and a designated Chinese tax representative; the STA will issue a Non-resident Taxpayer Identification Number where needed for withholding, while the partnership itself uses the USCC as its VAT and income tax identifier. No additional TIN application is required for the partnership because the USCC is the statutory tax registration number for domestic tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China; each partner is separately taxed on its distributive share of the partnership's profits. For indirect tax purposes, the partnership is itself a VAT taxpayer for the Chinese bonded road feeder transport services, while the Hong Kong SAR profits tax treatment of the partners follows Hong Kong's territorial source principle if a Hong Kong branch or carrying on business test is met.

Available election forms

None. China does not have a check-the-box entity classification election. The CN_FIP is mandatorily pass-through for income tax purposes, and no hybrid mismatch election is available at entity level. The VAT general taxpayer election for turnover below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold is an indirect tax status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers initial tax registration; every FIP with a Business License must complete STA tax registration. For VAT status, registered VAT taxpayers with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale taxpayer status with a simplified VAT rate, while annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 make VAT general taxpayer registration mandatory. A CN_FIP engaged in bonded cross-border trucking is expected to become a general taxpayer in order to issue 9% VAT invoices and to sustain any 0% export zero-rating claim.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and the STA cross-border taxable services zero-rating measures only to a VAT general taxpayer that supplies genuinely cross-border transportation services. The bonded road feeder service must be provided to a non-resident recipient, the transport leg must cross or terminate outside mainland China (including to/from Hong Kong SAR, Macau SAR, and other foreign customs territories for VAT boundary purposes), the consideration must be received from overseas in a foreign currency, and the service must be part of the international carriage of goods under customs supervision. A purely domestic bonded feeder movement between two mainland bonded zones or airports is not automatically zero-rated and will remain taxable at 9% unless it is contractually supplied as an international transport service to an offshore principal.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, so ordinary bonded trucking service fees, airport feeder proceeds, fuel reimbursements, and logistics disbursements can be settled through designated foreign exchange banks after authenticity verification. The capital account remains heavily regulated: foreign-invested partnership capital injections from Hong Kong, shareholder or subordinated loans, equity transfers, liquidation recoveries, and any onward outbound investment require SAFE registration, filing, or approval. Surplus RMB cannot be converted into foreign exchange for overseas deployment unless a legal underlying capital event is proved. Profit remittance to non-resident partners faces practical hurdles: the bank will require a CPA-audited annual financial statement, proof that corporate income tax and any withholding tax on the partner's distributive share have been paid, the completed SAFE direct investment report, and the underlying service invoices or distribution resolution. Foreign-currency receipts from cross-border bonded transport may be retained in an FX collection account or converted into RMB, but every conversion must match the actual underlying transport order and customs movement. Hong Kong's free convertibility does not provide onshore access, so all mainland-side capital and settlement movements run through SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The PRC no longer applies a fixed mandatory surrender ratio to export or service trade receipts. Under SAFE's trade-related FX monitoring framework, foreign exchange proceeds from bonded trucking services must be repatriated to a designated FX settlement account within the period recognized by the goods and services trade monitoring system, normally within 90 days of the service completion or the relevant customs release event; any item outstanding beyond the threshold must be reported to SAFE and may be subject to an authenticity re-review. Once repatriated, the FIP may retain up to 100% of foreign currency receipts in its current account FX deposit account or convert them into RMB at the bank's real-time market rate. The bank will assign an A/B/C trade classification; Category B entities face closer vetting, and Category C entities generally need to obtain SAFE approval for each conversion. No mandatory conversion percentage is currently imposed beyond the repatriation and reporting duty.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank for Hong Kong dollar clearing and offshore renminbi liquidity within the Hong Kong leg of the treasury architecture.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as commercial banks in the PRC and cannot hold domestic RMB corporate accounts, process SAFE foreign exchange capital registrations, or issue import/export settlement reports. A Hong Kong-licensed EMI such as a Stored Value Facility operator may be used as an ancillary collection account for HKD or CNH receipts generated by Hong Kong-based sales agents, but the FIP's statutory capital, SAFE-monitored current account receipts, and outbound trade wires must reside with a tier-1 SAFE-designated bank. Onboarding an EMI instead of a bank would fail the capital verification and tax filing trail. Feasibility is low as a primary treasury rail and medium only as a Hong Kong-leg collection wallet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital contributions from Hong Kong must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, not a general operating account. The remitter must include the exact partnership name and SAFE FDI filing code in the beneficiary field. Upon receipt, the onshore bank suspends the funds to capital verification, checks the bank receipt against SAFE's capital project information, and applies the relevant payment-in-lieu rules before releasing RMB-converted funds for permitted expenses. The CPA then issues a capital verification report confirming subscription, valuation, and timing; only after that report can distributions or further capital repatriation be requested.Domestic RMB disbursements for fuel, maintenance, landing fees, and staff payroll use CNAPS and ACH_Direct bank-wire rails on the basic operational account. Cross-border payments for truck or trailer purchases, Hong Kong sub-contractor haulage, airport landing charges, and freight-forwarder settlement use SWIFT MT103, with Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value equipment purchases and documentary collections on a D/P or D/A basis for medium-value consignments. Every outward service payment above the tax declaration threshold must be accompanied by a tax-filing certificate, the transport contract, invoice, and customs transport manifests. Purpose codes must match the actual service, and all wires should be traceable to the GPS-sealed trip file.Onshore deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts in RMB/USD and RMB/HKD for the FIP's real current-account exposure, executed with a tier-1 SAFE-authorized bank.Onshore FX swaps and cross-currency interest-rate swaps used solely to hedge the cash-flow mismatch between CNY revenue and HKD or USD costs, or to hedge SAFE-registered fleet financing.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted as a direct onshore hedging book for the FIP; only a separately capitalized Hong Kong affiliate may transact NDFs, and gains or losses cannot flow through the FIP's operating account.Plain-vanilla FX options may be added only with documentary proof of underlying trade orders; exotic digital, leveraged, or speculative products are not permitted.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed as commercial banks in the PRC and cannot hold domestic RMB corporate accounts, process SAFE foreign exchange capital registrations, or issue import/export settlement reports. A Hong Kong-licensed EMI such as a Stored Value Facility operator may be used as an ancillary collection account for HKD or CNH receipts generated by Hong Kong-based sales agents, but the FIP's statutory capital, SAFE-monitored current account receipts, and outbound trade wires must reside with a tier-1 SAFE-designated bank. Onboarding an EMI instead of a bank would fail the capital verification and tax filing trail. Feasibility is low as a primary treasury rail and medium only as a Hong Kong-leg collection wallet.
