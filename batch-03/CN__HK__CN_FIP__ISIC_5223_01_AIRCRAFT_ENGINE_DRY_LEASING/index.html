Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a Hong Kong SAR limited partnership or a Hong Kong company acting as the structural vehicle, the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) requires a registered office address in Hong Kong that is a physical location where documents may be served, but a virtual office or a licensed trust or company service provider's address is acceptable only if the provider offers a physical reception of service of process, mail handling, and a registered street address that is not a P.O. Box; however, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD), the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), and most Hong Kong licensed banks will, for a medium-risk aircraft-engine dry-leasing entity, reject a pure virtual office or commercial mailbox because the entity must demonstrate real commercial substance through the location of its principal place of business, the presence of at least one full-time resident manager or employee, the maintenance of accounting records and bank signature authority in Hong Kong, and the ability to produce original contracts, insurance certificates, and maintenance records on demand. For a Mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership, the Partnership Law and SAMR rules require a fixed, registered business premise in Mainland China for the FIP itself; a virtual office is generally impermissible for the FIP's own registration, although the FIP may lease a serviced office from an accredited business center as long as it is a true physical office with a lease agreement in the FIP's name, not a mailbox-only arrangement. If the operating strategy is a Hong Kong partnership holding a Mainland FIP, the Hong Kong partnership may use a professional firm's physical registered office while the Mainland FIP must have its own physical, exclusive-use or shared physical office with clearly demarcated partitioned space that would pass SAMR on-site verification. Given that the industry is aviation asset leasing with medium-risk AML classification, the combined expectation of banks, the Economic and Trade Office, and the tax authorities is that the registered office must be a genuine office or a serviced office with a lease term of at least one year; a pure mail-forwarding service is operationally unacceptable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box election, a partnership tax classification election, or a hybrid mismatch election. Partnerships are mandatorily tax-transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the partnership itself is not chargeable to profits tax and each partner is separately assessable. Taxpayers may apply to the IRD for an advance ruling on the source of profits or treatment under the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, but this is an administrative ruling procedure, not an elective tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax; Hong Kong imposes only narrow duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and a hotel accommodation tax on prescribed hotel services, neither of which applies to aircraft engine dry leasing.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST regime. The Business Registration Ordinance requires every business carried on in Hong Kong, including a partnership, to obtain a Business Registration Certificate from day one with no minimum turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT/GST terms: Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST and therefore has no 0% export VAT rating, no export VAT refund, and no input VAT credit mechanism. The equivalent relief under Hong Kong's territorial profits tax is the offshore-source exclusion: income from cross-border dry leasing of turbofan aircraft engines is not chargeable to profits tax if the profits do not arise in or are not derived from Hong Kong, as determined by the 'operations and source' test under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. The lessor must be able to demonstrate that negotiation, execution, and performance of the lease occurred outside Hong Kong, that the engine was delivered and used outside Hong Kong, and that rental proceeds were received outside Hong Kong. There is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in Hong Kong.

  • Electronic Import/Export Declaration lodged with the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) where the engine physically moves through Hong Kong customs territory.
  • Air Waybill (AWB) or Master/Supplementary Bill of Lading evidencing cross-border movement of the turbofan engine from the lessor's location to the lessee's location outside Hong Kong.
  • Certified copy of the dry lease agreement, engine serial number (ESN), airworthiness/delivery certificates, and redelivery reports evidencing operation wholly outside Hong Kong.
  • Escrow settlement statements and SWIFT MT103/MT202 credit advices evidencing collection of lease rentals outside Hong Kong; no Inward Remittance Certificate is required in Hong Kong.
  • Tax residence certificates or partnership registration documents of the non-resident lessor and partners supporting the offshore-source claim and any applicable treaty protection.

No statutory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API integration, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate is imposed in Hong Kong. Taxpayers may issue paper or electronic invoices; however, under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), all business records sufficient to ascertain profits must be retained for at least seven years.

No VAT/GST clawback is available in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST is levied. For profits tax, if the IRD rejects an offshore-source claim and determines that the aircraft engine leasing profits were derived from Hong Kong, the profits become assessable to profits tax at the two-tiered rate of 8.25% on the first HK$2 million of assessable profits and 16.5% on the remainder. Incorrect or false returns may trigger additional tax of up to three times the undercharged tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and fines/imprisonment under section 80 for wilful evasion. Separate penalties apply under the Business Registration Ordinance for failure to register, update, or produce the Business Registration Certificate.

Current-account convertibility

China maintains controlled convertibility: RMB is freely convertible for current account transactions such as lease rentals, maintenance and ordinary operating expenses, but capital account items including foreign-partner capital contributions, equity/partner equity transfers, external debt and distributions to non-resident partners remain subject to SAFE registration and designated-account routing. Practically, an FIP cannot remit capital in or out without an FDI Foreign Exchange Business Registration certificate issued via SAFE or a delegated bank. Profit remittance requires a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, partnership agreement clauses, and a bank payment application under the current-account item, and the bank verifies that funds correspond to after-tax distributable profits. The Hong Kong side is unconstrained: HKD and USD are fully convertible under the currency board with no surrender requirements. Operational friction arises on the China side: capital-account receipts must enter a designated capital settlement account, and any foreign currency exposure is trimmed by voluntary settlement with the designated bank. Foreign partners must also ensure the FIP's business scope does not fall on the foreign investment negative list; aircraft engine dry leasing is permitted, but each inward remittance is separately verified against the underlying contract.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer mandates compulsory surrender of export or service trade receipts; all foreign currency receipts from aircraft engine dry leasing must be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange account maintained with a licensed bank after invoice maturity, normally within 90 days, unless SAFE has expressly approved offshore retention. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may voluntarily settle the proceeds for RMB or retain the foreign currency balance. The bank requires the signed dry lease agreement, payment schedule, engine identification and airworthiness records, commercial invoices, and the entity's tax identification number to apply the correct SAFE transaction code.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China FIP; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as de facto central bank under the Hong Kong SAR currency board

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) rate applicable to resident enterprises in China; Foreign-Invested Partnerships (FIPs) are tax-transparent, so partners bear tax at their own applicable rates: 25% for corporate partners, or 10% final withholding for non-resident corporate partners without a permanent establishment in China, subject to treaty relief)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder. Since an FIP is a partnership, profit distributions to partners are not legally 'dividends' but are taxed as business profits; a non-resident corporate partner with no permanent establishment in China will be subject to a final 10% withholding on its allocable share of China-source income (Article 3.2, EIT Law; pursuant to STA public notice 2021 No. 17).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, local transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is required if the total annual related-party transaction amount reaches RMB 200 million (¥200,000,000), or if any single related-party tangible property transaction reaches RMB 40 million (¥40,000,000), or any financial asset transaction reaches RMB 100 million (¥100,000,000), or other transactions reach RMB 20 million. The Master File must be prepared if the group's combined annual revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion. For this FIP, given the high contract value of aircraft engine dry-leasing, prepare contemporaneous Local File and Master File (if group-wide threshold met) and submit the annual related-party transaction report (Form A105000) with the CIT return in all cases where the transactions exceed the Chinese disclosure threshold (which is lower: all related-party transactions must be reported when the annual total is above RMB 20 million, even if local file is not required).

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a separate 'Economic Substance Regulation' regime like offshore jurisdictions, but substantive economic activity is embedded in: (1) the Beneficial Ownership Notices (Pearl River/Hong Kong DTA interpretation), requiring the HK company to show real business substance (employees, offices, decisions); (2) General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 47 EIT Law) may re-characterize a profit- shifting structure lacking commercial purpose; (3) the permanent establishment (PE) doctrine – if the HK partner manages the leasing business from China or the FIP operates through a fixed place in China, it constitutes a PE, and profits are subject to normal 25% EIT instead of 10% WHT; (4) Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test for qualifying as a Hong Kong resident under the DTA; the lessor must demonstrate that strategic decisions on engine acquisition, financing, risk management, and contract negotiation occur in Hong Kong. Merely holding legal title in Hong Kong with all operations outsourced to the mainland FIP will fail any substance challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fictitious Expense Invoicing / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (TCAL): the taxpayer is ordered to pay the underpaid tax plus a daily 0.05% surcharge, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the unpaid tax. If fictitious VAT invoices are involved, Article 205 of the Criminal Law imposes a fine and imprisonment up to life sentence, while Article 201 (tax evasion) permits imprisonment of up to 7 years where the amount evaded is RMB 50,000+ and exceeds 30% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where partners/directors acted with intent to evade tax. Directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax under Article 20 of the Company Law and TCAL implementing rules; personal assets may be seized for restitution. Criminal prosecution can result in travel bans (blacklisting by the National Immigration Administration), exit control orders, and imprisonment of the responsible directors or partners for up to 7 years in addition to fines.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Withholding Tax on Cross-Border Rentals/Royalties

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of TCAL: the withholding agent (the FIP) is fined 50% to 3 times the amount of the undeducted tax; the tax itself remains collectible from the payee. Additionally, Article 37 imposes interest and penalties on the taxpayer (HK recipient) for non-payment. Criminal liability arises if the amount is substantial and the act is intentional, charged as tax evasion with penalty of imprisonment up to 3 years or higher.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners of the FIP are personally responsible for ensuring withholding obligations are fulfilled. Where they knowingly instruct or omit to withhold, they become jointly liable for the monetary penalty and may be subject to criminal imprisonment under Article 201 if engaged in a conspiracy. Civil liability includes compensation for the company's loss. The directors may be placed on the blacklist for exit control (limited entry to Hong Kong and mainland China), and personal bank accounts may be frozen as part of recovery.
Shell Company/BO and AML Violations (Beneficial Ownership Frauds and Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of EIT Law permits the STA to recharacterize the structure and levy a 25% penalty surcharge plus interest. The AML Law of the PRC (rev. 2024) imposes fines of up to RMB 2 million on the entity and RMB 100,000 to 100,800 on responsible individuals; criminal money laundering under Article 191 of the Criminal Law (amended 2020 – Money Laundering Law) is punishable by imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and confiscation of property. Article 154 (illegal taking abroad of exchange) can lead to imprisonment up to 15 years for illegal outward capital remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors or partners engaging in the sham entity design are personally liable for the restitution of all unpaid taxes and are barred from holding corporate directorships. Upon proof of fraudulent incorporation or asset transfers, courts may pierce the veil under Article 20(3) of the Company Law, making shareholders/directors personally accountable for all corporate debts. Criminal exposure includes criminal detention up to 3 years for AML violations, and for large-scale money laundering, up to 10 years' imprisonment plus fines. Travel bans are imposed automatically for criminal cases, and the director's personal assets are subject to criminal asset freeze and confiscation.
