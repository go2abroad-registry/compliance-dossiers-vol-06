Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Hong Kong law permits a registered office to be a service provider's address for business registration and filing under the Companies Ordinance and the LPF Ordinance. However, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and the Hong Kong banking sector generally reject a pure virtual mailbox for an ocean freight forwarding NVOCC entity because of lack of operational substance. The IRD may disallow foreign-sourced income claims and require actual staff, a lease agreement, utility bills, a telephone line, and physical records. Under AMLO, a virtual office that only forwards correspondence creates an enhanced risk indicator; banks and the Marine Department will likely decline account opening or license endorsements without a physical office. For an NVOCC operation, we advise a serviced office or a dedicated leased office with at least a 12-month term and a meeting room able to satisfy customer due diligence inspections.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships. However, the partnership agreement must specify the capital contributions of each partner, and such contributions must be fully paid in cash or in kind as agreed. No escrow account is mandated by statute.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - No physical premises beyond a standard office. The Fire Services Department may inspect the office premises and issue a Fire Safety Certificate if the office is in a commercial building. However, no special civil defense clearance is required for a non-industrial business.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for limited partnerships. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box election; partnership pass-through treatment for enterprise income tax is mandatory. The FIP may elect simplified VAT treatment as a small-scale VAT taxpayer until annual taxable sales exceed the statutory general taxpayer threshold, and may voluntarily register as a VAT general taxpayer. Hong Kong IRD likewise applies its statutory territorial-source partnership rules without a corporate check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers PRC taxpayer registration; registration is mandatory once the FIP obtains its business license. A VAT registration-status threshold applies for general VAT taxpayer status at annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000, after which small-scale simplified VAT treatment ends and the FIP must issue standard VAT invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The PRC FIP may claim a 0% VAT export rate only if the NVOCC ocean freight forwarding service is supplied to a non-resident entity or qualifying overseas purchaser, the service is fully consumed outside the PRC in accordance with the cross-border service consumption rules, the consideration is collected from a non-resident in foreign exchange or through an approved RMB cross-border settlement channel, and the service is not ancillary to a domestic onshore transaction. The FIP must demonstrate that the freight forwarding relates to goods exported from the PRC or to an international ocean leg and must preserve the complete zero-rating evidence file for the statutory retention period. Where the service qualifies only as VAT-exempt under the applicable STA rules, input VAT credit on related purchases may not be claimed; for qualifying zero-rated services, input VAT credit and export refund procedures may apply.

  • PRC Customs Export Declaration, including the electronic customs declaration number, for the underlying export cargo
  • Master Bill of Lading and House Bill of Lading issued by the ocean carrier or NVOCC evidencing the international ocean shipment
  • Signed NVOCC service contract and commercial invoice describing the ocean freight forwarding supply and the overseas recipient
  • Freight forwarder booking confirmation, container number, seal number, or equivalent cargo movement evidence
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt or foreign exchange settlement certificate, or SAFE cross-border payment verification record evidencing receipt of the freight and forwarder charges
  • Export-service zero-rated VAT invoice issued on the STA e-invoice platform and the associated VAT export or refund declaration package

All VAT invoices, including the export-service zero-rated invoice, must be issued through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau / e-invoice service platform. The platform performs real-time fiscal clearance through the Golden Tax System, embeds a unique national invoice number and tax-control electronic stamp with digital encryption and QR authentication on every invoice, and requires API or tax-control device reporting of invoice metadata to the STA. Invoices issued outside the platform are invalid. Hong Kong imposes no equivalent e-invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate; records must be retained for IRD inspection.

Tax authorities may revoke the 0% rate and assess output VAT, late payment surcharge, and penalties if the FIP fails the documentation, consumption-outside-China, or foreign currency repatriation tests within the prescribed period. Under Article 63 of the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, false export declarations or fraudulent zero-rating claims constitute tax evasion; the FIP must repay the underpaid tax and may be subject to a penalty of 50% to five times the tax amount. Where fraud reaches criminal thresholds, referral to the Public Security authorities under the PRC Criminal Law export tax refund fraud provisions is possible, with the FIP and responsible officers subject to criminal liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a partially convertible currency under a managed floating foreign-exchange system. The RMB is fully convertible upon current-account receipts and payments, including freight forwarding, shipping agency fees, and related trade-in-service proceeds, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by tax vouchers, signed service contracts, and SAFE-compliant transaction declarations. By contrast, capital-account transactions remain restricted. An inbound capital contribution to a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership must first be registered under the Foreign-Invested Partnership regime, then remitted into a designated foreign-exchange capital account at a licensed bank, after which it may be settled into RMB only after bank-side SAFE checks and, where required, a capital verification certificate. Profit distribution to offshore partners is permitted only when the partnership has booked distributable profits, settled all Chinese taxes, and obtained the tax authority's online foreign-exchange remittance certificate. Export and service proceeds must be brought onshore into a designated account and reported through SAFE's trade monitoring systems; the beneficiary may retain the foreign currency or request conversion at a licensed bank, but cannot keep proceeds offshore beyond the regulated repatriation timeline. These restrictions create a mandatory treasury architecture in which a single Tier-1 Chinese bank acts as FX settlement bank, custody bank, and clearing correspondent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's total-amount verification model, export and service-export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within the statutory declaration and receipt window, generally within 90 days of invoice issuance or the contractual payment milestone for ocean freight forwarding services. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; the FIP may retain 100% of its foreign-currency receipts in the designated account, but it may not leave the proceeds outside the PRC banking system beyond the regulated period. Any conversion to RMB must be executed at a designated bank using the prevailing spot middle price and reported under current-account verification codes. The bank will require a matching service contract, tax clearance certificate, booking note, and bill-of-lading evidence.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally non-viable as a primary account-holding rail. Offshore digital EMIs holding an electronic money licence in Singapore, Hong Kong, or the United Kingdom are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC to maintain current-account or capital-account deposits for a PRC-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership. PBOC payment-institution rules reserve the custody of corporate funds, FX settlement, and capital-account administration for licensed commercial banks and designated FX banks. An offshore EMI may only act as a non-custodial technology overlay or API connection to the licensed Chinese bank, and even that requires a domestic regulatory filing or a bank integration agreement. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must use direct banking rails at a Tier-1 state-owned commercial bank for all onshore account infrastructure, while the EMI may be used solely as a visa-card or invoicing widget outside China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The offshore investor must wire USD or HKD to the FIP's designated foreign-exchange capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank using SWIFT MT103. The remittance message must include the partnership's full legal name, the registered FIP filing code, and the SAFE purpose code allocated for registered-capital contribution to a foreign-invested partnership. Before the transfer, the FIP must complete the administrative registration with the municipal Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the bank-issued SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Notice. The beneficiary bank will screen the MT103 against the approved capital schedule, apply FX/AML checks, and post the funds to the capital account. The capital contribution must then be verified by a Chinese certified public accountant, after which the funds may be settled into RMB and released to the partnership's basic operating account. The RMB proceeds cannot be used for prohibited items such as securities trading, speculative property purchases, or out-of-scope investments. Documentary collections are acceptable for controlled title release under URC522, particularly where the NVOCC acts as intermediary between the overseas customer and the shipping line. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally non-viable as a primary account-holding rail. Offshore digital EMIs holding an electronic money licence in Singapore, Hong Kong, or the United Kingdom are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC to maintain current-account or capital-account deposits for a PRC-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership. PBOC payment-institution rules reserve the custody of corporate funds, FX settlement, and capital-account administration for licensed commercial banks and designated FX banks. An offshore EMI may only act as a non-custodial technology overlay or API connection to the licensed Chinese bank, and even that requires a domestic regulatory filing or a bank integration agreement. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must use direct banking rails at a Tier-1 state-owned commercial bank for all onshore account infrastructure, while the EMI may be used solely as a visa-card or invoicing widget outside China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File preparation is required where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000 for tangible goods, or RMB 100,000,000 for intangible assets, services, or financial transactions; a Master File is additionally required where the multinational enterprise group's related-party transaction turnover in China exceeds RMB 2,000,000,000 or the group meets the OECD CbCR threshold of RMB 5,500,000,000 consolidated revenue in the preceding year.

Economic substance scope: The FIP must demonstrate genuine economic substance in China through its NVOCC license, actual staff responsible for booking, route planning, cargo consolidation, container loading supervision, invoicing, and risk management, and substantive control over cash flows. The HK partner claiming treaty benefits must prove that it performs core income-generating activities in Hong Kong, including treasury, contract negotiation, and operational oversight, rather than acting as a shell. Chinese tax authorities will apply the Economic Substance Doctrine through the general anti-avoidance rule (EIT Law Article 47), the special tax treatment framework, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test; failure to meet these standards results in treaty override and reallocation of profits to the Chinese FIP permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, underreporting income or falsifying costs yields a penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax, plus daily interest of 0.05%; if criminal tax evasion is established under Criminal Law Article 201, the responsible entity is fined and the persons directly in charge may be sentenced to up to seven years' imprisonment plus fines.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where directors knowingly caused the FIP to submit false statements: directors are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax, are placed on the State Taxation Administration non-payment blacklist (which triggers travel bans, exit-control measures, credit restrictions, and disqualification from new enterprises), and may face criminal imprisonment of up to seven years and personal asset confiscation.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Tax and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 37 and the Withholding Administration Measures, failure to withhold triggers a penalty of 50% to 300% of the uncollected tax and daily late-payment interest; fraudulent treaty claims are rejected under the principal-purpose test, and the full 10% tax becomes immediately payable, with further fines under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners bear personal liability for the tax debt if assets of the FIP are dissipated before tax collection; they are jointly liable for civil restitution, face a travel ban through court or tax-related exit-restriction orders, and may be criminally prosecuted for obstructing tax collection under Criminal Law Article 202 if they deliberately suppressed the withholding obligation, leading to imprisonment from three to seven years.
Unlicensed NVOCC Operations and AML Contamination

Statutory sanction: Under the Regulations on International Ocean Shipping (Order No. 20, 2019), unlicensed NVOCC operations attract fines of up to RMB 200,000, confiscation of illegal gains, and suspension of the business license; the Anti-Money Laundering Law (2022 revision) imposes fines from RMB 500,000 to RMB 5,000,000 for serious violation, and Criminal Law Article 191 imposes asset forfeiture and imprisonment of five to ten years for organised money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the resulting unpaid taxes and for the costs of dissolution; the legal-entity veil is pierced where the partnership was used as an instrument of criminal money-laundering, giving creditors and the state direct claims on personal assets. The directors suffer automatic travel bans, bank-account freezes, and disqualification from serving as directors of any PRC or HK company for up to ten years, together with criminal imprisonment of up to ten years and fines for each money-laundering transaction.
