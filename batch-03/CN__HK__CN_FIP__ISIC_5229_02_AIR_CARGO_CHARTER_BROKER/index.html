Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), acting through the local Municipal or Provincial Market Supervision and Administration Bureau, Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration Office, at the FIP's principal place of business.

Governing statute

Foreign-Invested Partnership is governed by the Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (2006 Revision), Articles 14, 15, 16, 62 and 63, together with the Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (2020), Articles 2, 30 and 31. Because the entity is a partnership rather than a company, the primary Companies Act is not applicable; the operative implementing instrument is the Administrative Regulations on the Registration of Foreign-Invested Partnership Enterprises (SAMR Order, as revised in 2015), Articles 2 through 10.

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Foreign-Invested Partnership Enterprise, filed on the SAMR National Enterprise Registration Portal or in person at the authorized Market Supervision and Administration Bureau window.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 65 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under PRC partnership law. The partners are free to set contribution amounts in the partnership agreement. There is no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Contributions may be made in cash, in kind, or by assignment of rights as agreed; foreign exchange contributions must be remitted through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital contribution account after foreign direct investment (FDI) registration, and actual contributions must be recorded in a capital contribution schedule filed with SAMR.

Funding & unlock path: Office-based FIPs must file fire safety completion acceptance with the local Fire and Rescue Department and maintain occupational safety compliance under the Work Safety Law. If the FIP operates a cargo warehouse or ground handling facility, it must obtain a Civil Defense Fire Permit or fire acceptance opinion, complete a structural load inspection for the storage area, and obtain work-safety pre-employment registrations before commencing operations.

Paid-in capital rule: None under PRC partnership law. The partners are free to set contribution amounts in the partnership agreement. There is no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Contributions may be made in cash, in kind, or by assignment of rights as agreed; foreign exchange contributions must be remitted through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital contribution account after foreign direct investment (FDI) registration, and actual contributions must be recorded in a capital contribution schedule filed with SAMR.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Administration / National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn); for Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD), electronic portal: eTAX (www.ird.gov.hk).

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) formed in the People's Republic of China: first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number/TIN and VAT taxpayer ID for mainland China. Within 30 days of SAMR registration, file enterprise tax registration with the competent STA tax office through the integrated one-form/one-window procedure, supplying the partnership agreement, foreign investor identity and residency documents, registered address certificate, and declared business scope including air cargo charter brokerage. Through the same process, obtain VAT registration and the taxpayer status identification as a general VAT taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer. If a foreign non-resident partner has no permanent establishment or business presence in China, that foreign partner must be separately registered with the STA for non-resident withholding tax purposes and will receive a non-resident withholding taxpayer file number; this is a payer-side registration rather than an entity-level election. For any Hong Kong SAR nexus, a foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance within one month of commencement; the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the Hong Kong tax identifier, and profits tax follows the territorial source principle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. PRC partnerships, including FIPs, are mandatorily transparent for enterprise income tax under Cai Shui [2008] No. 159; income is allocated to partners under the distribution-after-determination model, and the partnership itself is not an enterprise income tax taxpayer. The PRC and Hong Kong SAR tax systems do not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election for this FIP.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover exemption from basic VAT registration: any taxable supply activity in mainland China requires VAT registration. The small-scale taxpayer monthly exemption threshold is CNY 100,000 (quarterly CNY 300,000), and mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered by annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000. Hong Kong SAR has no indirect tax registration threshold because no VAT/GST/sales tax is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim PRC export VAT zero-rating only if the taxable service is an international air cargo charter brokerage service supplied to a customer outside mainland China, the service is received and consumed entirely outside China, and the transaction is supported by contracts, actual outbound movement of cargo or aircraft, foreign-currency settlement through approved banks, and a compliant VAT/customs declaration. PRC VAT requires digital e-invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV system: VAT invoices are issued through tax-authorized software or the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform, transmitted to the STA in real time, and stamped with an anti-fraud digital verification code; fully digitalized e-invoices require API-based integration with the STA and real-time fiscal clearance before issuance for high-volume taxpayers. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a managed current-account convertibility system. Foreign-invested partnerships are permitted to remit operating revenues and partnership profit distributions offshore, but every transaction must be documented and booked through a designated foreign-exchange bank. Capital-account items such as foreign partner capital injections and external debt remain tightly controlled and require SAFE registration before the bank can debit or credit the relevant receipt/settlement accounts. There is no de facto convertibility for purely speculative flows. For an air-cargo charter broker, export freight service fees are current-account receipts: they may be retained in a foreign-currency settlement account or converted to RMB at negotiated rates, but the underlying invoices, charter-party agreements, load sheets, and airway bills must be archived and linked to the remittance. Profit distribution to the HK partner is permissible after audited accounts, tax clearance, and SAFE verification, but the receiving bank will conduct a spend-down and source-of-funds trace. In Hong Kong, the HKD is freely convertible under the HKMA Linked Exchange Rate regime; however, the mainland FIP remains the regulated treasury hub.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No fixed mandatory surrender ratio applies since the 2012 SAFE liberalization; export and service-trade foreign-currency receipts may be retained in a settling bank foreign-currency account or sold at negotiated rates. The statutory duty is repatriation into the PRC banking system: the FIP must credit the designated trade foreign-currency account, provide invoice, airway bill/charter-party evidence, and ensure each export receivable is collected within the commercial settlement term, for air cargo brokerage typically 30 to 90 days from invoice or uplift; overdue statuses beyond SAFE's monitoring threshold must be reported and explained.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign-exchange administration to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) retains oversight of the Hong Kong dollar and HKD clearing systems.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally barred as a primary treasury rail for the mainland FIP. Foreign digital EMIs, including Airwallex, Wise, and Revolut, cannot maintain licensed RMB deposit or clearing accounts for a mainland foreign-invested partnership, and PBOC/SAFE rules prohibit enterprise cash management moving entirely outside designated foreign-exchange banks. Such EMIs may be used only as narrow bilateral receivables/payables tools in Hong Kong, not as the compliance ledger for the mainland entity, and all cross-border capital flows must still go through a licensed mainland bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK partner must first complete MOFCOM/local market-supervision foreign-invested partnership establishment filing and obtain the SAFE foreign-exchange registration notice for the FIP before wiring. The remittance must be sent by SWIFT MT103 from the HK partner's corporate account to the FIP's capital escrow/settlement account in the designated mainland bank, using the SAFE-approved foreign-exchange purpose code for foreign-invested partnership capital contribution, not a service or goods code. After receipt, the bank debits/credits the capital-account settlement book, and if the funds are to be converted to RMB, settlement is completed under the bank's capital-item settlement authenticity review. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% corporate profits tax (for corporate partners) / 15% standard rate for unincorporated individuals; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is treated as tax-transparent for Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, with each partner being separately assessed.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Hong Kong does not impose dividend withholding tax on outbound distributions by a partnership or corporation; dividends are also not taxable in the hands of Hong Kong-resident recipients.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Filing is due within 1 month from the date of issue of the return (the "due date"). If the FIP appoints a Hong Kong tax representative, an automatic extension may apply; for accounting periods ending 31 December, the extended due date is usually 16 August of the following year; for periods ending 31 March, the due date is usually 15 November. A further extension may be granted on application to the Commissioner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Master File and Local File must be prepared by the Hong Kong FIP if it belongs to an MNE group whose consolidated group revenue in the prior accounting period exceeds HK$750 million (approximately US$96 million), and the FIP's aggregate related-party transactions exceed the following de minimis thresholds: goods – HK$88 million; financial transactions – HK$11 million; intangible property – HK$28 million; services – HK$22 million; other – HK$11 million. A Country-by-Country Report is additionally required if the group revenue exceeds HK$6.8 billion (or the equivalent of EUR 750 million). The FIP should also benchmark all charter-brokerage commission transactions with the PRC affiliate under the arm's length standard.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the territorial source principle imports a substance test: profits derived from air-cargo charter brokerage are taxable only if the core income-generating activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong. The FIP must show that the negotiation, execution, performance, and invoicing functions are exercised in Hong Kong, or that the non-HK source position is supported by full documentation. The IRD may challenge a "paper FIP" by re-allocating profits under the transfer-pricing rules and the traditional anti-avoidance provisions (sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance). For purposes of the China-HK arrangement, central management and control (CMC) must be demonstrated to avoid double non-taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Offshore Declaration

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.80(2) – upon conviction on indictment, a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years; under IRO s.82A, additional tax of up to 300% of the undercharged tax may be assessed. The IRD may also impose a daily penalty from the due date for non-filing.

Director personal liability: For a limited partnership, the general partner and any managing partner who signed or approved the false return are personally and jointly liable. The court may order civil asset restitution and a travel ban (blacklisting) under the IRO enforcement powers; directors of a corporate general partner may face disqualification, contempt of court, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years for wilful tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAF – the IRD may adjust the assessable profits to an arm's length amount; s.51C – failure to keep sufficient records is a criminal offence, fine up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months; s.82A / s.80 – additional tax up to 300% of undercharged tax and penalties for intentional or negligent filing.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate general partner is a shell, the court may pierce the partnership veil and hold its directors and shareholders personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalties. Directors may be jointly and severally liable under the tort of conspiracy, ordered to disgorge improper benefits, and subjected to a Hong Kong immigration restriction (travel ban) pursuant to a court order.
AML / Shell-Company / Failure to Maintain Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) – failure to carry out customer due diligence or keep records: on conviction, a fine up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years. Under IRO s.61 and s.61A, the arrangement may be void against the Commissioner; in China, the GAAR may be applied to recharacterise the transaction and impose penalty interest. The business may also be struck off and the air-cargo brokerage licence (if applicable) revoked.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who knowingly participate in a sham or use the FIP as a façade for fraud face civil liability for restitution, a worldwide asset freeze, travel restrictions (blacklisting by the IRD or courts), disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years, and criminal liability for money laundering or fraud involving imprisonment up to 14 years (under the Theft Ordinance and Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance).
