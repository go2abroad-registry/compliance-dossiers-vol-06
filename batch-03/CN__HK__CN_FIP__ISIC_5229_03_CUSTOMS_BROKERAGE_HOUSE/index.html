Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership is domiciled in mainland China and therefore operates under a strict but administratively simplified foreign exchange control regime. RMB is convertible on the current account for trade, services, and remittances that are supported by genuine underlying documentation; SAFE-regulated designated banks verify contracts, invoices, customs declarations and tax certificates. The capital account is not freely convertible. Foreign partner capital contributions into the FIP, shareholder loans, profit distributions, and liquidation proceeds require a SAFE foreign exchange registration (FDI registration) and a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account or settlement account before funds can be paid or converted. For profit remittance, the FIP must present an audited financial statement, tax payment/tax-clearance certificates, a partnership resolution on profit distribution, and proof that the RMB funds were lawfully obtained. There is no fixed surrender threshold for export receipts, but foreign currency must be repatriated into domestic designated accounts within prescribed periods. Cross-border RMB flows to Hong Kong are expedited through the CNH market but remain subject to genuine-trade and capital-account documentary verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE imposes no mandatory surrender/conversion ratio, but export or service-trade foreign currency receipts must be credited to a domestic designated foreign-exchange account under the FIP's current FX account within 180 days from the date of the relevant customs export declaration or the specified payment-date window in the underlying contract, whichever governs; the bank monitors receipt through the trade counter system. Receipts may be retained in FX or voluntarily converted to RMB at the spot rate, and delayed repatriation requires a written SAFE justification plus shipping, customs, invoice and collection documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the dual FX-control authorities for the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership; the Hong Kong SAR offshore clearing hub is supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for CNH settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not feasible for the PRC entity. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from servicing a mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership as principal current or capital account providers because they do not hold PBOC banking licences or SAFE FX licences, cannot perform foreign-investment registration, cannot open FDI capital or RMB settlement accounts, cannot access China's trade-counter verification system, and cannot make Chinese customs electronic duty payments. They may only support the Hong Kong connected treasury vehicle for liquidity concentration, but the mainland FIP must use licensed domestic banks for all RMB/CNY and FX clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK-based investor remits the FIP contribution through SWIFT MT103, or through CIPS for RMB, to the FIP's SAFE-registered FDI Capital Account at the designated Chinese bank, quoting the SAFE FDI business registration reference as payment remark and using the FX purpose code mapped by the receiving bank for direct investment into a foreign-invested partnership. The receiving bank performs FX authenticity verification and, after credit, can settle the funds into RMB into the FIP's capital settlement account. The FIP must then obtain a capital verification report issued by a PRC CPA evidencing paid-in capital; the report must tie the bank in-payment to the SAFE registration and partnership agreement. No part of the capital settlement shall be used for RMB-denominated entrusted loans, repayment of domestic loans, purchase of real estate outside the business scope, or non-current activities unless separately approved.For customs brokerage fees and imported/exported freight services, the FIP may disburse through SWIFT MT103 with relevant service-trade purpose codes. For merchandise settlements under broker-managed cross-border trade, commercial documentary letters of credit issued by Chinese banks or confirmed by Hong Kong banks, documentary collections, and telegraphic transfer remittances are standard. All outbound payments must be matched to underlying customs declarations, sales and purchase contracts, invoices, and tax clearance certificates; Chinese banks verify through the Cross-Border Payments and Receivables System and the electronic customs tax payment channel. The FIP may also use the national Customs Integrated Tax Payment Portal for duty and tax payments by bank debit, while the Hong Kong clearing hub can use its own commercial banking rails for group liquidity pooling.Onshore RMB/Foreign Currency Forward Contracts with genuine trade or capital-item underlying and bank-confirmed documentation, with no net speculative positions.FX Currency Swaps (RMB/Foreign Currency cross-currency swaps) for conversion of arm's-length trade debt or cash-flow requirements, subject to SAFE hedging registration.FX Options and option combinations on RMB against eligible foreign currencies, limited to hedging of probable but uncertain cash flows arising from customs brokerage receivables and payables.Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for the PRC FIP; any NDF hedging must be executed in the Hong Kong CNH market by the offshore treasury affiliate and must not be booked in China.
