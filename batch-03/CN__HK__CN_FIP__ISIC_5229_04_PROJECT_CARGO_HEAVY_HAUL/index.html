Hong Kong law allows an LPF to maintain a registered office at a lawful street address in Hong Kong that is not solely a post-office box. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may satisfy the Companies Registry's minimum letter-of-the-law requirement, but the Inland Revenue Department and banks carrying out HKMA-supervised account opening and business-verification procedures will generally reject a pure virtual office as insufficient commercial substance for the OOG heavy industrial project-cargo industry. A physical office with local staff, operating records, and bank evidence is strongly recommended; virtual office usage limited to supplementary mail handling may be tolerated when a real operation exists elsewhere. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk), with partnership registration forms accessed through the Companies Registry Integrated Companies Information System (ICRIS).

E-government filing portal

For the CN_FIP vehicle, the Hong Kong limited partnership must first be registered with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). On registration, the Companies Registry transmits the partnership particulars to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The Business Registration Office issues a Business Registration Certificate and assigns a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory business tax identification number in Hong Kong. No separate VAT identification number exists because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST. A foreign non-resident partner is not separately pre-cleared for tax registration; the partnership bears the registration duty. If the partnership derives Hong Kong-source profits, it must file the relevant profits tax return even when the partners are non-resident, and an authorised tax representative in Hong Kong should be appointed to receive IRD correspondence. The obligation to register business arises within one month after commencement of business in Hong Kong, and there is no minimum capital requirement for the partnership.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the partnership is fiscally transparent; Hong Kong territorial-source profits tax applies at the partner level, and offshore income is outside the scope unless linked to a Hong Kong trade, profession, or business).

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no Check-the-Box entity classification and no hybrid mismatch election. A partnership may, through its profits tax return, advance an offshore-source claim for income derived from OOG heavy-haul project cargo services performed outside Hong Kong; the foreign-sourced income exemption (FSIE) regime may additionally exempt qualifying foreign-sourced passive income from profits tax, subject to the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No Value-Added Tax, Goods and Services Tax, or general Sales and Use Tax applies in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is limited to Stamp Duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117) and duty on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold. The Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) instead requires every person or partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong to obtain a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong as a VAT zero-rating concept because Hong Kong has no VAT. Export cargoes and offshore logistics revenues are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax. For profits tax purposes, the OOG project cargo partnership may treat revenue as offshore if negotiation, execution, and performance of the contracts occur outside Hong Kong and no Hong Kong-source trading nexus arises; documentary evidence is required at audit. If the underlying goods are exported from mainland China, China VAT export zero-rating or export refund may apply, but that is a separate mainland State Taxation Administration regime outside the Hong Kong indirect tax system.

  • Cargo manifest submitted to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) for the exporting vessel or aircraft; this is the Hong Kong functional equivalent of a customs export declaration.
  • Negotiable ocean Bill of Lading issued by the carrier or carrier's agent confirming the out-of-gauge goods uplifted from the Hong Kong port and naming the consignee outside Hong Kong.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and OOG cargo movement papers, including dimensions, axle-load / weight certificates, and vessel stowage / lashing survey report, where applicable.
  • Foreign-exchange repatriation evidence: SWIFT MT103 / telegraphic transfer advice from the paying bank or a receipted bank credit advice showing the foreign currency settlement; Hong Kong does not use an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC).

If a Hong Kong taxpayer claims an unsupported offshore-source or FSIE exemption, the Inland Revenue Department may raise extended assessments, impose additional tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance for incorrect returns, and in fraud or wilful evasion cases pursue criminal penalties. For mainland China export VAT claims, fraudulent export documentation triggers clawback of the refund, late-payment interest, ad valorem administrative penalties, and revocation of export tax refund eligibility under the Law on Administrative Penalties for Tax Violations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a free and open capital account, supported by the Linked Exchange Rate system that pegs the Hong Kong dollar (HKD) to the US dollar (USD) within the band of 7.75 to 7.85. Capital account items, including equity injection, intra-group loans, and profit repatriation, are fully convertible and require no prior approval from HKMA. Current account transactions, such as trade-related freight payments and project cargo disbursements, are equally unrestricted. There are no surrender requirements or compulsory conversion ratios for export proceeds generated by Hong Kong entities; a logistics partnership may hold operating receipts in USD, CNH, EUR, or any other freely tradable currency. The principal compliance hurdles arise when funds move between Hong Kong and mainland China: Chinese corporate partners investing into the Hong Kong partnership must complete SAFE outbound FDI registration, and any dividends or partnership distributions returned to China are treated as offshore investment income subject to Chinese enterprise income tax and foreign exchange settlement formalities. While Hong Kong itself imposes no exchange controls, the Chinese wall creates practical settlement friction, especially for large project cargo advances that require purpose codes and supporting trade documentation. Overall, convertibility in Hong Kong is unrestricted for both residents and non-residents, but cross-border RMB flows are disciplined by the CNH/CNY windows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Hong Kong domiciled partnerships, there is no statutory export proceeds surrender timeline nor mandatory conversion ratio under the HKMA/JETCO framework. Exporters may freely retain, credit, or convert proceeds. If the entity were a mainland China FIP, SAFE would require repatriation of current-account export proceeds within the goods trade window (typically 180 days from customs declaration) and permit retention in a licensed onshore foreign currency account, with conversion to CNY at the entity's discretion. This Hong Kong vehicle, however, is entirely outside such surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), acting as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for Hong Kong SAR; cross-border investment from mainland China also falls under the regulatory purview of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium for ancillary payments but Low as the primary treasury rail for heavy project cargo. Hong Kong-licensed fintech EMIs and virtual banks (e.g., Airwallex, Statrys, Mox, WeLab) can provide multi-currency wallets and cheap FX, but they are not licensed trade-finance banks and cannot issue commercial LCs, trust receipts, or handle capital-verification escrow on behalf of Chinese ODI investors. Additionally, several EMIs restrict onboarding of partnership structures with opaque non-resident beneficial owners, and the logistics counterparties require standard SWIFT MT103/MT700 rails. Therefore, a hybrid model is viable: use an EMI for low-value operating expenses and sub-agent payments, while maintaining Tier-1 bank accounts for capital injection, LC issuance, and milestone settlements.

There is no automatic grace period, and operating after expiry without renewal is a continuing contravention. $274 USD Late or non-renewal is an offence under Cap. 310, with a maximum fine of HK$5,000 and imprisonment for 6 months, plus daily fines. The IRD may suspend the business registration certificate and recover the unpaid annual fee and levy as a civil debt; persistent non-renewal can support deregistration or strike-off action where the entity is corporate or carries on business in a trade name.
Profits Tax Return for Partnership (Form BIR52, including financial statements, tax computation and partnership allocation schedule) Within 1 calendar month of the date of issue of the annual tax return. The IRD normally issues BIR52 annually according to the partnership's basis period; e-filing through eTAX generally obtains an automatic 1-month extension, but the official due date is calculated from the demand notice. $0 USD Failure to file without reasonable excuse is an offence under Inland Revenue Ordinance s.80(1), with a fine of up to HK$10,000. The IRD may raise an estimated assessment and impose additional tax of 5% of the unpaid amount if the default exceeds 6 months, plus a further 5% after an additional 6 months, with statutory interest accruing on unpaid profits tax. Continued default may lead to prosecution and enforcement including distraint against business assets.
Employer's Return for the Year of Assessment (Form BIR56A and IR56B statements for each employee, including nil return) Annually within 1 month after the issue date of the prescribed return. The statutory issue season is April, following the 31 March year of assessment; e-filing may attract a further one-month extension as notified by the IRD. A nil return must still be submitted when the FIP has no employees. $0 USD An employer that fails, without reasonable excuse, to file the return commits an offence and is liable to a fine of HK$10,000 under Cap. 112. Material omission of a chargeable employee may attract an additional penalty equal to 10% of the tax undercharged; deliberate false returns may result in prosecution and further court-imposed penalties. The default does not by itself trigger strike-off but can expose the general partner to personal enforcement of tax debts.
Annual Statutory Registers and UBO/SCR Compliance Review (internal register audit; no filing) On or before the annual accounting reference date, or within 15 days of any partner or UBO change; annual re-confirmation must be completed no later than 6 months after the accounting reference date to support audit and tax filing planning. $0 USD Non-maintenance of statutory registers is an offence under Cap. 622 for corporate vehicles, with a fine of HK$10,000 and daily default fines. For a limited partnership, failure to keep the partners' register, charges register or minute book can cause the general partner to lose limitation of liability protection, become personally liable for partnership debts, and receive an auditor's or reporting accountant's qualified opinion on the annual accounts.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) rate; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) itself is fiscally transparent under PRC tax law, so no EIT is levied at the partnership level. Corporate partners are taxed at 25% on their distributable share; individual partners are taxed at 5%-35% progressive Individual Income Tax rates on business income.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding under EIT Law Article 37

The annual EIT return is due within five months after the fiscal year-end, i.e. no later than 31 May for a calendar-year taxpayer. The related-party transaction report must be filed at the same time as the annual EIT return.
