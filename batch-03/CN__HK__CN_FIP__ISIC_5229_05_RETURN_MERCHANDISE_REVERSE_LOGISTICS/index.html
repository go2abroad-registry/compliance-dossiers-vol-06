Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Hong Kong foreign-invested limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37). The partners may agree capital commitments in the partnership agreement, contributions may be in cash or in kind, and no blocked escrow account is required by Hong Kong law.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department letter of compliance under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap 95) for any storage premises with an aggregate floor area exceeding 230 square metres; Buildings Department structural load approval for racking and mezzanine floors; Labour Department registration under the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap 59) if powered cargo handling machinery is used, and a general occupational safety plan under the Occupational Safety and Occupational Health Ordinance (Cap 509) for workplace operations.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, China; Business Registration Office; online portals: GovHK Business Registration service and IRD eTax portal

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not maintain a separate statutory 'Foreign-Invested Partnership' category; the FIP is therefore established as a general partnership under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) or, where limited liability coverage is desired, a limited partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37). Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), the partnership must apply to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong. The application is made on the prescribed business registration application form and must be accompanied by identity documents of all general and limited partners; for non-resident corporate partners, certified certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, and registered office address; for non-resident individual partners, valid passport or other government-issued identification; and evidence of the Hong Kong business address. If a non-resident corporate partner is itself carrying on business in Hong Kong, it must also be registered with the Companies Registry as a non-Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). On approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the de facto Hong Kong tax identification number for the partnership and is used for all IRD correspondence. There is no separate TIN or VAT number issuance for partnerships. The BRC must be displayed at the place of business and renewed annually or on the triennial cycle, and changes in partners or partnership particulars must be reported to the Business Registration Office within the statutory period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box entity classification election and no specific hybrid-mismatch election. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a partnership is transparent for profits tax purposes: the assessable profits are computed at the partnership level, but the tax is charged on and recoverable from each partner in proportion to their profit share under IRO s.22. Individual resident partners may elect Personal Assessment under IRO s.41 where it reduces total tax payable, but that election does not change the partnership's pass-through classification. Hong Kong's territorial-source principle under IRO s.14 also applies; only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are chargeable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, sales tax, or similar consumption tax in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is limited to specific duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), hotel accommodation tax, and stamp duty

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax and therefore has no turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. However, any person, including a partnership, carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover, unless a statutory exemption applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST/sales tax, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no zero-rating claim, and no output tax to release. Goods and services leaving Hong Kong are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax. The relevant direct tax question is governed by the territorial-source rule in the Inland Revenue Ordinance: profits from offshore trading operations may be outside Hong Kong profits tax if the source of profits is outside Hong Kong, but this is not a VAT zero-rating mechanism.

  • No VAT zero-rating documentation bundle is required in Hong Kong because no VAT zero-rating claim exists.
  • For physical shipments, the Customs and Excise Department may require an Import/Export Declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, lodged through the Trade Single Window or DCTIS.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list.
  • Transport document such as Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or road manifest, as applicable.
  • Certificate of Origin, if required by the destination jurisdiction or requested by the importer.
  • The Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is not a Hong Kong statutory document; payment receipt is evidenced by bank statements and reconciliation records.

None. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, government-approved e-invoicing, or anti-fraud digital stamping for indirect tax purposes. Ordinary commercial invoices are accepted. The IRD eTax portal is used for tax filing and payment, and the Trade Single Window and Dutiable Commodities and Trade Information System are used for import/export declarations, but neither operates as a fiscal invoice clearance mechanism. Taxpayers must retain sufficient business records and source documents under IRO s.51C.

There is no VAT or export VAT rebate regime in Hong Kong, so there is no VAT clawback or zero-rating penalty. If a partnership asserts that export trading profits are outside Hong Kong profits tax, the IRD will scrutinize the place of contracting, place of performance, substantive commercial activities, and fund flows under IRO ss.14 and 14A. Incorrect returns or insufficient records may trigger penalties under IRO s.82A, including a maximum fine of HK$50,000 and additional tax of up to treble the tax undercharged. False trade declarations to the Customs and Excise Department are separately punishable under the Import and Export Ordinance and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed, current-account convertible but capital-account restricted foreign exchange regime for domestic entities, including foreign-invested partnerships. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is a Chinese legal person establishment; therefore its RMB and foreign currency movements are subject to SAFE oversight. Current account transactions, including reverse logistics service fees, returned goods resale proceeds, insurance refunds, and after-tax profit distributions to foreign partners, are permitted to be settled through designated foreign exchange banks without SAFE pre-approval, provided that genuine transaction documents are presented and the underlying economic reality is verified. Capital account transactions, such as the initial capital injection by the Hong Kong partner, additional investment from offshore investors, or cross-border loans, require prior SAFE registration through the relevant designated bank or directly with SAFE. Profit remittance hurdles include the completion of an annual statutory audit, periodic tax filing, enterprise income tax or individual income tax withholding for partners, presentation of a partner resolution authorising the distribution, and submission of the tax payment certificate. All settlement and sale operations must be supported by the real trade or investment background; synthetic or shell transactions will be rejected. These rules impose a practical requirement for the FIP to maintain meticulous documentation for each capital flow and to engage a licensed bank with strong cross-border compliance infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange regulations on trade foreign exchange settlement, the FIP must repatriate the full value of any foreign currency export proceeds (including returned merchandise salvage sales and disposal proceeds) to its domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory timeframe, generally 90 days from the date of export customs declaration or within 90 days from the due date for receipt under the underlying settlement terms. The repatriated proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or voluntarily converted to RMB at the market spot rate on presentation of the export declaration, commercial invoice, and the bank's verification system match. There is no mandatory conversion ratio or forced sale to the central bank; the FIP retains full discretion over currency retention subject to genuine transaction verification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)
