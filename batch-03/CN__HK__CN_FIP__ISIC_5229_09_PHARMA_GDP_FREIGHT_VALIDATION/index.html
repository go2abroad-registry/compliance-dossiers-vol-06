Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) - the Chinese central tax authority with competency over the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) through its provincial, municipal, and county tax service offices; online electronic filing and registration are handled through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (the e-Tax portal) available on the China Tax Services online platform. For the Hong Kong SAR overlay contained in the composite jurisdiction, the competent authority is the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD), administered through the IRD e-Tax portal.

        
E-government filing portal
The FIP obtains its statutory TIN through the integrated market-administration and tax-registration process administered by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR): (1) complete the foreign-invested partnership establishment registration under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Measures for the Administration of Establishment of Foreign-Invested Partnerships; (2) upon issuance of the business license, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically generated and is treated as the FIP's TIN for all PRC tax filings; (3) the SAMR synchronizes the establishment record to the STA under the multi-license integration system, and the FIP must complete the supplementary tax registration information report through the STA e-Tax portal, including the registered address, bank account, principal investor details, responsible person, and planned invoicing method; (4) if the FIP is a VAT general taxpayer or intends to claim export zero-rating, it must apply for general taxpayer status and electronic invoice issuance capacity through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau; (5) each foreign non-resident partner that is not otherwise a PRC taxpayer and has no permanent establishment in China must be registered as a non-resident enterprise taxpayer with the competent STA office of the withholding agent or place of payment, using the non-resident taxpayer registration facility in the e-Tax system and submitting the partnership agreement, foreign certificate of incorporation or business registration, tax residence certificate, and the power of attorney of the appointed withholding agent; (6) for Hong Kong SAR nexus, if the foreign partner also holds a Hong Kong Business Registration Number and carries on business in Hong Kong through the partnership, the IRD Profits Tax registration must be maintained through the IRD's Business Registration Office and e-Tax portal; otherwise no Hong Kong entity tax file is required solely for the Chinese-formation FIP.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
None - PRC law does not provide a check-the-box election for partnerships. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is mandatorily tax-transparent for Corporate Income Tax: the partnership files no CIT; each partner is assessed on its distributive share in accordance with the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and the partnership allocation schedule. For VAT, the FIP is treated as a separate VAT taxpayer and cannot elect out of VAT registration. Foreign partners may seek tax-treaty relief (including the PRC-Hong Kong SAR arrangement) through the competent authority procedure, but this is not a classification election. Hong Kong also has no check-the-box mechanism; the IRD applies territorial-source taxation and assesses Profits Tax at the partner level only on Hong Kong-source profits.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales - the statutory threshold above which VAT general taxpayer registration is compulsory. Every entity engaged in taxable sales must complete VAT registration even below this threshold; below CNY 5,000,000, the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer using the simplified collection rate until the threshold is exceeded or a voluntary general taxpayer application is approved.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
For goods: 0% VAT/export rebate eligibility arises only when the FIP sells goods to an overseas customer, the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory under customs supervision, the export customs declaration is completed in the name of the FIP, and the export sale is supported by the documentary chain required by the STA and the General Administration of Customs. For services: 0% VAT is available only for qualifying cross-border services enumerated in the STA/MOF cross-border service catalogue, including international transportation and certain logistics services consumed wholly outside Mainland China; the FIP must be a VAT general taxpayer, the service must be supplied to an overseas party, the place of supply must be outside China, and the consideration must be received in foreign currency from outside China. Freight-forwarding and logistics-auxiliary services that do not meet the zero-rating conditions are taxable at 6% (or, if supplied as actual transportation, at 9%) and must instead rely on the cross-border service exemption if applicable.

The FIP must use the STA Electronic Tax Bureau's invoicing control environment. Special VAT invoices are generated through a tax control device or, for full digital electronic invoices (FDEI), through the STA's electronic invoice service platform with real-time issuance, secure encryption, and digital anti-counterfeit authentication. All output special VAT invoices must be reported in the monthly VAT filing and invoice database reconciliation; FDEI does not require a separate physical anti-counterfeit stamp. Input invoices must pass verification through the VAT invoice verification platform before crediting input VAT. No separate VAT ID is issued; the USCC is endorsed as the TIN/VAT identifier.

      
The zero-rated VAT refund is provisional until the complete export documentary bundle and foreign exchange repatriation trail is verified. If the STA finds the goods did not leave the PRC customs territory, the service was not consumed outside China, the purported export services were fictitious, the invoices were falsely issued, or the foreign currency proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day deadline without a valid SAFE/STA deferral, the refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest and output VAT is re-assessed at the standard rate or applicable reduced rate. Fraudulent export VAT-refund claims are punishable under the PRC Law on Tax Administration and Collection and the Criminal Law by fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the refunded or sought amount, suspension of export-tax-rebate qualifications, blacklisting in export verification systems, and criminal liability up to life imprisonment for the most aggravated forms of VAT-refund fraud.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Mainland China imposes strict capital account controls while maintaining current account convertibility under the SAFE registration system. The FIP may freely convert and remit foreign exchange for trade-related current account transactions, including freight, insurance, and logistics fees, provided verifiable underlying contracts and invoices exist. Capital account flows, however, are heavily regulated: inward foreign capital contributions into the FIP must be remitted to a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, reported through the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system, and converted in accordance with the negative list and business scope restrictions. Profit distributions, interest, and liquidation remittances to foreign partners are treated as capital account outflows requiring documented audited financials, tax clearance certificates, and SAFE approval or confirmation, reducing convertibility for dividend remittance to a discretionary, evidence-driven process. For the Hong Kong leg, the HKD is fully convertible and linked to the USD within the narrow band managed by the HKMA, but CNY outward transfers from the mainland entity remain subject to PBOC/SAFE oversight. Thus the treasury must treat RMB as partially convertible, maintain full documentation, and route all transfers through approved onshore banking channels, avoiding unlicensed fintech EMI bypasses.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under current PBOC/SAFE practice, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds, but the FIP must repatriate 100% of its export receivables to a designated domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB at the corporate settlement rate at an authorized bank, subject to electronic verification and matching against customs and logistics documentation. Any overdue or unrepatriated balance must be supported by a prior SAFE-approved deferred collection arrangement.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the FX authority for the China-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central banking authority for the Hong Kong SAR clearing leg.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Airwallex, or TransferWise cannot maintain RMB clearing accounts or foreign exchange capital accounts for a mainland China-resident FIP because PBOC and SAFE require all capital and current account flows to pass through licensed domestic banks. They may provide ancillary multi-currency wallet services to the Hong Kong parent, but cannot serve as the primary clearing rail or substitute for the SAFE-registered Chinese banking relationship. Viability: not feasible for the China entity; limited supplementary use for HK-side treasury only.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Beneficial ownership opacity created by layering a Hong Kong limited partnership structure over the China-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership, making ultimate beneficial owners non-obvious.
        
Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity created by layering a Hong Kong limited partnership structure over the China-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership, making ultimate beneficial owners non-obvious.

        
Defense evidence: Full organizational chart; signed partnership deed; general and limited partner identification documentation including passports and proof of address; UBO declaration; HK Companies Registry current and historical registration extracts; audited financial statements for all intermediate entities; bank reference letters from all connected corporate partners.

      

      
      

        Cross-border HK/CN payment flows trigger enhanced sanctions and PEP screening, particularly if any partner surname matches a globally designated political exposure or if logistics counterparties operate across multiple customs districts.
        
Root cause: Cross-border HK/CN payment flows trigger enhanced sanctions and PEP screening, particularly if any partner surname matches a globally designated political exposure or if logistics counterparties operate across multiple customs districts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound foreign capital from the Hong Kong investor must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's designated foreign exchange capital account at the chosen Tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance must reference the FIP's Foreign Investment Information Report receipt, use the approved SAFE purpose and transaction code for foreign investment capital injection, and be accompanied by the partnership agreement, capital contribution schedule, and bank FX confirmation. After receipt, the bank will perform SWIFT or CIPS settlement and capital verification under the SAFE registration, converting funds into RMB at the spot rate if needed. The funds cannot be used for purposes outside the registered GDP freight business scope unless a separate SAFE approval is obtained.The FIP should use SWIFT MT103 for international freight, insurance, and logistics payments to carriers and overseas suppliers, while domestic RMB payments should be routed through CIPS or the local bulk interbank payment system. For pharmaceutical GDP freight transactions, Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections are appropriate for high-value shipments where title documents such as airway bills, bills of lading, and customs declarations must be exchanged under bank intermediation. Direct ACH is not a permissible cross-border rail for the mainland FIP; ACH may only be considered for intra-HK HKD clearing through the Faster Payment System or CHATS if an HK treasury vehicle is used. Every outbound disbursement must be matched with the underlying GDP logistics contracts, customs declarations, carrier invoices, and insurance certificates to satisfy SAFE current account verification and AML monitoring.

Turnover: A Hong Kong incorporated or registered entity, including a foreign-invested partnership operating as a non-Hong Kong company or an eligible partnership with a corporate general partner, is legally required to appoint a certified public accountant (practising) to audit its financial statements if: (a) the entity is incorporated in Hong Kong and is not eligible for the exemption under Section 5 of the Companies (Exemption of Companies and Interested Bodies from Auditing Requirements) Regulation, i.e., small private companies with revenue not exceeding HKD 20,000,000 and total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 and employees not exceeding 50 during the financial year may still be exempt only if all members agree and the company is a private company or an eligible body; (b) the entity is a foreign company registered under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and must deliver to the Registrar audited financial statements that would be required if it were a company incorporated in Hong Kong; (c) the partnership is a limited partnership whose general partner is a Hong Kong company or a non-resident body corporate that itself must file audited accounts; (d) any shareholder, partner, lender, or regulatory body (e.g., the Drug Office for GDP licence holders) requires an independent audit as a condition for licensing, financing, or investment. In addition, a Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in pharmaceutical GDP freight that handles government-related contracts, clinical trial supply chains, or cross-border MNC principal relationships is practically required to undergo a full IFRS audit to satisfy counterparty due diligence. Quantitative statutory thresholds under the Companies Ordinance and the Companies (Auditing) Rules: any company or non-resident company not eligible for the audit exemption must have its financial statements audited by a practising auditor; the exemptions apply only to private companies and eligible bodies meeting two of the following three criteria: revenue not exceeding HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,560,000), total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000), and number of employees not exceeding 50 (headcount threshold). There is no balance-sheet-only audit threshold; the audit requirement is universal for all companies registered under the Companies Ordinance unless the small private company exemption applies. For a partnership that is not required to audit under the Companies Ordinance, the IRD may nonetheless request audited accounts when the partnership has a permanent establishment in Hong Kong and the IRD is not satisfied with the completeness of the accounts; in practice, any cross-border GDP logistics partnership with invoicing from Hong Kong will require audit certification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: For the standardized schema, the Local File threshold is HK$10,000,000 per controlled transaction category; the Master File threshold is an MNE group consolidated global revenue of HK$6,800,000,000 under IRD DIPN 58. Where the Foreign-Invested Partnership has intercompany freight, GDP pharma validation, cold-chain logistics, or management service transactions with connected non-resident enterprises exceeding these thresholds, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained at the Hong Kong tax representative's office.

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not impose a generic Economic Substance Regulation on trading partnerships, but the general anti-avoidance rule under IRO s.61A disregards artificial arrangements lacking commercial substance. For treaty benefits, the non-resident partner and the Hong Kong FIP must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong: contract negotiation, risk management, qualified staff supervision, GDP-compliant quality assurance, and pharmaceutical freight decision-making. A booking entity with no premises, no local employees, and no commercial activity will be denied a Tax Residency Certificate and the arrangement may be recharacterized on the Mainland as fully taxable Mainland-source income.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion - False Offshore Claim
        
Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82(1), wilful tax evasion with intent to defraud is punishable by a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years. The IRD may also impose additional tax of up to three times the amount undercharged under IRO s.82A, issue best-of-judgment assessments under IRO s.59(4), and disregard artificial arrangements under IRO s.61 and s.61A. The Hong Kong Companies Registry may deregister the entity as an inactive shell.

        
Director personal liability: The court will pierce the corporate veil where the partnership is used as a facade for fraud. Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) if they acted as a partner or knowingly permitted evasion. Directors can be separately prosecuted for tax evasion, face a travel ban and departure prohibition order under IRO s.77(2A), be disqualified as directors, and receive criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years. Civil asset restitution and anti-money-laundering forfeiture actions are available.

      

      
      

        Pharmaceutical GDP License Violation and Customs Fraud
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Pharmacy and Poisons Ordinance (Cap. 138), supply or possession of unregistered pharmaceutical products is punishable by a fine of HK$100,000 and up to 2 years' imprisonment per count. Under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) s.18A, false customs declarations may attract a fine of HK$500,000 and 2 years' imprisonment. Violation of GDP licensure conditions exposes the entity to immediate suspension or revocation of the GDP certificate and forfeiture of pharmaceutical cargo.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and designated officers who consent or connive in the unlawful supply or false declaration are personally liable. They may face separate criminal prosecution, imprisonment up to 2 years for each count, asset forfeiture of tainted freight, blacklisting from public procurement and freight forwarding licenses, and a travel restriction while criminal proceedings are pending.

      

      
      

        AML and Beneficial Ownership Failure
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) s.5, non-compliance with customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations is punishable on conviction by a fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for 2 years on a continuing basis, while failures in prescribed disclosures may trigger separate enforcement by the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department and the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

        
Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil if the partnership is a mere asset protection vehicle. Directors face civil asset restitution orders, personal disqualification, travel bans, blacklisting on the regulatory enforcement list, and imprisonment up to 2 years. The IRD may also treat the structure as a sham under IRO s.61A and hold directors personally liable for resulting taxes and penalties.
