Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office; online electronic portal: eTAX (www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), every person carrying on a business in Hong Kong, including a non-resident foreign investor or a foreign-invested partnership, must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for business registration within one month of commencing business. For a partnership, the responsible or authorized representative must submit the prescribed business registration application through eTAX or in person, accompanied by certified true copies of the partnership deed or equivalent constitutive document, evidence of registration or recognition from the foreign jurisdiction for each non-resident partner, identity documents of the general and limited partners, proof of the Hong Kong place of business, and a description of the business activities. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) and a Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the statutory tax identifier for the partnership; no separate TIN or VAT identifier is issued. The partnership remains an assessed partnership for profits tax, while tax liability passes through to the partners under section 22 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). The BRC must be displayed at the place of business and renewed annually.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a free convertibility regime for current and capital account transactions. The HKMA's currency board architecture pegs the Hong Kong dollar to the US dollar within a convertible band, and the law does not require surrender of export proceeds or prior approval for foreign exchange conversion. A foreign-invested partnership in Hong Kong may hold HKD, USD, CNH, RMB, EUR, and other currencies in segregated accounts, remit dividends or profit shares to overseas partners, and use derivative hedging instruments as long as underlying commercial exposure is demonstrable. From the mainland China perspective, the foreign-invested partner must complete SAFE outbound investment registration and prove the capital account purpose through the banks; current account remittances such as service fees, logistics payments, and profit returns should be supported by contracts and customs documentation. The practical hurdle is not Hong Kong's exchange control but the mainland's capital account verification process; to remit funds into Hong Kong as equity capital, the mainland enterprise must obtain the ODI certificate and FX filing number, and to receive profit distributions from Hong Kong the parent must report it as an investment gain, pay applicable withholding or corporate income tax, and supply audited accounts. The FIP's HK bank will treat all non-trade-related currency movements as higher risk and require a written treasury policy demonstrating traceability from underlying parcel consolidation contracts to settlement instructions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong entity, there is no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; export foreign currency receipts may be retained in any currency or settled at the entity's discretion. For mainland Chinese export counterparties, SAFE currently requires trade-related receipts to be repatriated and netted against customs declarations under the genuine transactions principle; enterprises may keep the proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert into RMB through a designated bank, and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% under the existing retention regime. The applicable repatriation timeline is governed by the SAFE banking administration and is generally tied to the maturity of the underlying trade receivable, with cross-border e-commerce export proceeds subject to reconciliation against logistics and payment data.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) - de facto central bank and currency board operator for Hong Kong SAR; for all cross-border RMB/CNY flows and mainland capital movement, the People's Bank of China (PBC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the controlling regulatory authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Medium viability as a complement; Airwallex and similar licensed cross-border digital EMIs can handle low-value merchant payouts and API-driven reconciliation in Hong Kong, but they are not authorized to act as the primary treasury rail for capital escrow, SAFE-coupled inbound remittances, or trade finance. Under the Hong Kong licensing framework, most digital EMIs hold either a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence, a Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence, or an authorised institution authorisation; unless the EMI has obtained a full banking licence from the HKMA, these authorisations do not confer the full balance-sheet and clearing-guarantee capacity of a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank. The HKMA supervisory expectation is that the FIP maintains its substantive clearing and source-of-funds repository in a commercial bank; the EMI may be used only for low-latency parcel settlements and payout automation, and it cannot accept fiduciary deposits, issue commercial letters of credit, or provide discretionary overdraft facilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Mainland-driven inbound capital route into the Hong Kong FIP: (1) The mainland foreign-invested partner must first obtain the SAFE Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) filing and the applicable outbound foreign exchange registration for the FIP; the bank instruction must identify the transaction as direct equity investment in a Hong Kong entity, with a purpose code consistent with outbound direct investment and the SAFE filing reference in the SWIFT field 70 if an MT103 is used. (2) The funds are then credited into the FIP's designated escrow or operating account at a Tier-1 HK bank; the receiving banker will request the FIP to provide the LPA, the partnership capital schedule, and a receipt letter confirming whether the funds are for working capital or capital contribution. (3) For capital verification, the FIP should instruct a Hong Kong certified public accountant to issue a capital contribution certificate and an auditor's report mapping the inbound wire to the partnership's books. No HKMA minimum capital is required for a partnership, but the bank's internal credit committee will expect the clearing account to be reconciled within 10 business days to the source of funds, and any subsequent onward transfer to a mainland supplier must be implemented only through the approved trade settlement cluster.Outbound settlements for freight, linehaul, and merchant payouts should be routed through the HK banking system using the following rails: SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier and logistics payments; commercial letters of credit (LC at sight) where the supplier insists on bank payment undertaking and the transaction value exceeds the agreed threshold; documentary collections (DP and DA) for moderate-value transactions where the FIP needs to tie the release of shipping documents to payment; and local clearing through CHATS for HKD, USD, or CNH payments to Hong Kong freight forwarders and counterparties. Business Registration Certificate renewal and Business Registration levy (Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310) Annually on or before the expiry date shown on the current Business Registration Certificate, ordinarily one year after commencement or the prior renewal; the IRD issues a demand note and payment must be received by the due date, and if no demand is received the business must apply and pay at least one month before expiry. $301.28 USD Late payment attracts an additional penalty fee of HK$300 if paid within one month after expiry, escalating to a further additional penalty of HK$1,000 if still unpaid after one year; operating with an expired certificate renders the business registration invalid, subjects each general partner to prosecution under Cap. 310 with a maximum fine of HK$5,000 and imprisonment for one year, and the business cannot lawfully continue trading.
Profits Tax Return for Partnerships (Form BIR52) Within one month from the date of issue of Form BIR52; the IRD normally issues the return in April for accounting periods ending 31 December of the preceding year, and a tax representative may apply for the standard block extension scheme, commonly extending filing to mid-August or late November depending on the accounting year-end. $0 USD Failure to file without reasonable excuse is an offence: maximum fine of HK$10,000 plus additional tax of up to three times the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue an estimated assessment on default; unpaid tax will attract a 5% additional tax surcharge after six months and a further 5% surcharge after twelve months from the date the assessment becomes final.
Employer's Annual Return package (Forms BIR56A and BIR56B) - applicable if the partnership employs any person in Hong Kong Within one month from the date of issue; Employer's Returns are generally issued in early April for the tax year ending 31 March, with the ordinary deadline on or before 1 May, and approved e-filing extensions typically allow filing by early June. $0 USD Non-filing without reasonable excuse carries a maximum fine of HK$10,000 and, where the default reduces taxable income, additional tax of up to three times the amount undercharged; the IRD may issue estimated assessments and pursue prosecution; continued default does not directly strike off the partnership but exposes the general partner and responsible persons to penalty claims.
Limited Partnership Fund annual return (Form LF3) - required only where the Foreign-Invested Partnership is constituted as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637; not applicable to an ordinary Cap. 37 Limited Partnership Within 42 days after the anniversary date of registration of the Limited Partnership Fund; the return confirms the authorised representative, registered address, principal place of business and general partner particulars. $13.46 USD Late delivery is an offence; each general partner and each responsible person is liable to a fine of up to HK$50,000 and, for a continuing offence, to a daily default fine of HK$1,000 per day. Persistent default may cause the Registrar to strike the Limited Partnership Fund off the register, with consequent loss of limited liability statutory protections.
Annual statutory registers verification, partners' register update, and UBO interest-transfer notification record Within 30 days after any transfer, assignment, or change in a limited or general partner's interest, contribution, or liability; an annual update no later than the partnership anniversary is required to keep the statutory partners' register, minute book and UBO records accurate. No separate public filing is required unless the LPF annual return or Business Registration renewal has not already verified the information. $0 USD Failure to maintain statutory registers or to notify the Companies Registry of prescribed changes within 30 days is an offence: a fine of up to HK$50,000 and daily default fines of up to HK$1,000 may apply; knowingly making a false or misleading statement in the registers can result in imprisonment and fines of up to HK$100,000.
