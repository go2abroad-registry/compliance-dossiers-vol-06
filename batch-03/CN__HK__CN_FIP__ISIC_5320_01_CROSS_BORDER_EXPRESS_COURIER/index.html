Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Hong Kong Companies Registry, Limited Partnership Section, 8/F, OneMain North Tower, Central, Hong Kong, in coordination with the Inland Revenue Department, Business Registration Office, Revenue Tower, 5 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), sections 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9 and 11; Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) as the residual default; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), section 5; Air Transport (Licensing and Provision of Services) Regulations under Cap. 448 where aircraft or air cargo is involved; Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) for partner due diligence and record keeping.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed for a Hong Kong partnership, and no blocked escrow account is mandated by the Limited Partnerships Ordinance. Capital contribution is determined contractually in the partnership deed. Nevertheless, under the Commercial AML and formation compliance policy, the partners must deposit all initial capital contributions into an escrow account at a licensed Hong Kong bank and maintain such funds blocked until the Business Registration Certificate and Limited Partnership Certificate have been issued.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department approval under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295) and Fire Service (Installations and Equipment) Regulations, covering fire alarm systems, emergency lighting, means of escape, and extinguishing equipment; Buildings Department structural safety and occupant-load certification for mezzanines, conveyor systems, and vehicle ramps; Labour Department occupational safety approval under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509), including a risk assessment, safety supervisor appointment, fire drill plan, and licensed machinery inspection; Civil Defense emergency planning for facilities with large workforce concentrations or storage of hazardous materials.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the national China Tax Service platform; Hong Kong SAR tax administration is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), accessible through the GovHK eTAX portal, with Business Registration administered by the Companies Registry.

E-government filing portal

An FIP established under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law is first registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or the local market regulator to obtain a business license carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is accepted as the statutory Tax Identification Number for PRC tax purposes, and no separate CIT or VAT certificate number is issued. Tax registration is completed through the multi-license integration mechanism: after market registration, the SAMR automatically transfers the enterprise registration information and USCC to the STA; the FIP must then log into the Electronic Tax Bureau portal within 30 days, complete supplementary taxpayer registration, set a taxpayer identity, designate a tax responsible person, confirm tax law obligations (including VAT, CIT, stamp duty and withholding), and apply for VAT general taxpayer status if annual taxable sales exceed or are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000. If the FIP also carries on a separate permanent establishment or registry activity in Hong Kong SAR, it must separately apply to the Hong Kong Companies Registry within one month of commencing business and to the Hong Kong IRD for a Business Registration Certificate, using the Business Registration Number as the Hong Kong tax reference.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law contains no check-the-box election, no fiscally transparent entity election, and no hybrid mismatch election for a Foreign-Invested Partnership; pass-through treatment is mandatory. Hong Kong profits tax likewise does not provide an entity-level check-the-box election; an exemption for non-HK-source profits is available as a factual territorial-sourcing claim, not as an elective regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers base VAT registration; the FIP must register with the STA upon issuance of its business license. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when cumulative taxable sales in any rolling 12-month period exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer and may voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status, subject to statutory conditions, in order to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui [2016] No.36 and the current export VAT rebate rules, cross-border express courier services may be zero-rated where the supply is made to an overseas recipient, the payment is received in foreign exchange and repatriated into China, the service is wholly consumed outside the PRC or relates to goods physically exported from China Customs territory, and the FIP is a VAT general taxpayer retaining full export documentation. For courier shipments, the zero-rating substantiation link is the proof that each parcel or shipment left the PRC through Customs and that the courier fee was received from the overseas client in hard currency. If the service does not meet the wholly-outside-China consumption test, VAT applies at the standard 6% rate.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration form filed through the China International Trade Single Window, with Customs electronic release notation
  • Outbound carrier document, being the master or house Air Waybill or Bill of Lading, evidencing physical departure from the PRC and delivery to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and the express courier service agreement or customer purchase order
  • Bank foreign exchange collection receipt or export proceeds settlement certificate issued through SAFE reporting channels, supported by SWIFT MT103 credit advice where available
  • VAT zero-rated export sales ledger and tax-control e-invoice records matching the Customs declaration numbers and shipment dates
  • Internal parcel scan records from the China departure gateway and overseas proof of delivery records

Invoice issuance is controlled by the STA through the Tax Control Invoice System and the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform under Golden Tax Phase IV. VAT invoices must be generated from tax-controlled equipment or through the official National Electronic Invoice Service Platform; invoice data are uploaded to the STA in real time and each valid invoice carries an anti-counterfeiting QR code and a tax-control signature or digital stamp. API integration is permitted only through STA-approved official interfaces; the FIP cannot use a private billing system to issue fiscal invoices unless it integrates with the STA's electronic invoice service platform and submits the required real-time reporting and clearance data.

Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection and Levying, if export zero-rating is claimed without verified Customs exit documentation, without actual foreign exchange repatriation within 180 days, or where any document is found false, the STA will reverse the zero rate, require the FIP to pay output VAT at the standard 6% rate, claw back any refunded input VAT, and assess late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date. Administrative fines range from 50% to five times the underpaid tax; material falsification may constitute tax evasion or export rebate fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, exposing responsible officers to fines and imprisonment. The Customs Law of the People's Republic of China separately authorizes penalties for false or incomplete export declarations, and the STA may additionally suspend or disqualify the FIP from future export tax rebate treatment and impose enhanced audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated foreign exchange regime bifurcating current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions related to international express courier services, including freight charges, logistics service fees, customs clearance disbursements, and cross-border transit payments, are generally freely convertible after submission of genuine transaction evidence and are subject to bank verification and SAFE electronic monitoring. Capital account transactions, however, remain heavily controlled. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in mainland China with a Hong Kong partner is subject to foreign exchange rules applicable to foreign direct investment: registered capital contributions must enter through an onshore licensed bank account dedicated to foreign investment and must be registered under the SAFE direct investment database. The RMB is convertible for trade purposes but not freely convertible on the onshore market; an offshore CNH market exists in Hong Kong but onshore RMB cannot circulate offshore unless cleared through designated RMB clearing banks. Profit remittance by the Hong Kong partner is permitted after annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and a partnership resolution approving distribution have been presented; the remittance is subject to withholding tax at the applicable rate if the FIP is treated as an enterprise under Chinese rules. Practical inconvenience arises from the need to match every payment to a purpose code, submit documentary proof, and wait for bank pre-review, especially for capital account outflows. Thus, full current account convertibility coexists with strict procedural hurdles, and capital account transactions remain selectively convertible on a real-demand, case-by-case basis.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless SAFE has approved a deferred payment. No mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; enterprises may retain 100% of export receipts in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert all or part into RMB at the prevailing market rate at a licensed bank. Receipts must be attributed to specific customs declarations within 90 days for trade verification purposes. Proceeds received 90 days or more after the export date must be reported through the banks under SAFE surveillance; direct cross-border remittance by overseas customers must include the exporter's invoicing details and correct transaction codes.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) serving as the corresponding HKD clearing and settlement oversight authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic foreign exchange settlement accounts for mainland Chinese entities. An FIP cannot receive capital injection through such EMI rails because FDI proceeds must be remitted to a licensed onshore bank with a SAFE registration and a specific foreign investment purpose code. These EMIs also cannot satisfy China's corporate account opening, KYC, and foreign exchange verification requirements, and their use for export proceeds surrender or capital account flows is expressly non-compliant. The only lawful cross-border treasury architecture is a dual-banking structure: an onshore licensed bank for RMB and official FX settlement, and a fully licensed bank in Hong Kong for CNH and multi-currency clearing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner must subscribe capital using SWIFT MT103 remittances to the FIP's capital escrow account held at a licensed mainland Chinese bank. Before receipt, the FIP must complete the SAFE foreign direct investment registration and obtain a Foreign-Invested Partnership establishment certificate from the Market Regulation Administration. The payment narrative must specify the purpose code for foreign direct investment and reference the FIP's registered name and FDI registration number. If the injection is in RMB, the funds must be cleared through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or equivalent RMB clearing channel using reference to the Hong Kong RMB clearing bank. The receiving bank will perform capital verification by reconciling the SWIFT MT103 with the SAFE registration, the signed partnership agreement, and the capital contribution certificate. Once credited, the capital account funds may be converted to RMB for approved operational expenses only after the bank has verified the purpose and the corresponding tax deductions, and must not be disbursed earlier than legally allowed.The FIP shall execute trade settlement through the onshore banking channel. For operational payments to overseas logistics agents, customs brokers, airway suppliers, and last-mile courier partners, the bank will issue SWIFT MT103 international transfers with correct transaction purpose codes, linking each payment to invoices and contracts. For higher-value, time-sensitive purchases of courier equipment or consolidated prepaid logistics capacity, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) is recommended to provide payment security and document flow. Documentary collections, including documents against payment (D/P) and documents against acceptance (D/A), are permissible for stable counterparties after the banking relationship has matured. All outbound payments are subject to current-account verification against customs declarations and trade documents; RMB settlement to Hong Kong counterparties can be effected through CIPS or RMB payment systems, while non-RMB currencies settle through SWIFT.Onshore RMB FX forwards obtainable from licensed Chinese banks, permitted only where there is a genuine current or capital account transaction, with maturity aligned to the underlying payable/receivable and stringent real-demand verification.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD or HKD to hedge medium- to long-term balance-sheet exposure, subject to SAFE approval and supported by audited contract or loan documentation.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) available to the Hong Kong parent as a separate hedging vehicle, but not directly tradable by the mainland FIP because onshore NDF trading for RMB is not permitted.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic foreign exchange settlement accounts for mainland Chinese entities. An FIP cannot receive capital injection through such EMI rails because FDI proceeds must be remitted to a licensed onshore bank with a SAFE registration and a specific foreign investment purpose code. These EMIs also cannot satisfy China's corporate account opening, KYC, and foreign exchange verification requirements, and their use for export proceeds surrender or capital account flows is expressly non-compliant. The only lawful cross-border treasury architecture is a dual-banking structure: an onshore licensed bank for RMB and official FX settlement, and a fully licensed bank in Hong Kong for CNH and multi-currency clearing.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% (unincorporated partnership two-tier headline rate: first HKD 2,000,000 at 7.5%; excess at 15%; corporate partners assessed on their shares at 8.25%/16.5% two-tier rate)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no statutory withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions. A Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong is pass-through, so profit distributions to foreign partners are not subject to any Hong Kong dividend withholding tax; the tax is collected at partner level.
