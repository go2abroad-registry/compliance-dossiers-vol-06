Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the PRC sovereign territory: State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, accessible through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the national electronic invoice service platform. For the Hong Kong SAR sub-jurisdiction: Inland Revenue Department (IRD), including the Business Registration Office, accessible through GovHK and the IRD e-Tax electronic filing portal.

E-government filing portal

PRC leg: The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) first obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The USCC constitutes the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for PRC tax administration. The FIP must then complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days after issuance of the business license, selecting the competent tax authority office, activating the VAT and income tax filing modules, and establishing electronic invoice rights. Hong Kong leg: The FIP must apply for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance with the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, resulting in the issuance of a Business Registration Certificate and Business Registration Number (BRN). The BRN serves as the Hong Kong tax identification reference. Non-resident partners are not required to obtain separate PRC TINs unless a PE or withholding obligation arises; for Hong Kong Profits Tax, non-resident partners carrying on business in Hong Kong are assessable in their individual capacities on their partnership shares, with the partnership filing obligations administered through the IRD.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC VAT: there is no turnover threshold for initial VAT/tax registration; the mandatory general VAT taxpayer threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales, above which the FIP must register as a general VAT taxpayer and charge output VAT at applicable rates (13% for goods, 6% for services, 9% for certain categories). Hong Kong SAR: no indirect-tax registration threshold exists because no VAT is imposed; business registration is compulsory upon commencement of business irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC: exported goods, including bubble-tea syrups, concentrates, and physical franchise supply-chain goods sold to overseas franchisees, qualify for the 0% export VAT rate and eligible input-VAT rebate when the goods physically depart mainland China, export customs procedures are completed, title passes to a non-resident buyer, and the foreign-exchange proceeds are repatriated through a designated PRC bank. Cross-border franchise licensing and related services supplied to a recipient located outside mainland China and wholly consumed outside mainland China may also be zero-rated or treated as outside the charging scope of PRC VAT, subject to the statutory documentation set. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST exists, so export zero-rating is not a Hong Kong requirement.

  • Commercial invoice and digital e-invoice or special VAT invoice issued to the overseas franchisee
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical export movement from mainland China
  • Packing list, export contract, certificate of origin, and product specification documentation

PRC: The fully digitalised electronic invoice system is mandatory on the STA national electronic invoice service platform; special VAT invoices and digital e-invoices must be transmitted in real time to the tax authority for automatic verification and anti-fraud digital encryption, with API connectivity required under the Golden Tax Phase IV environment. Hong Kong SAR: no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory API-based e-invoicing system, and no digital stamping requirement; invoices are issued voluntarily without pre-clearance.

PRC: Tax authorities, PRC Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit zero-rating and export-rebate claims. Any missing customs, transport, invoice, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence invalidates the 0% rate and re-classifies the supply as taxable at the applicable VAT rate; claimed input-credit rebates are recovered together with late-payment interest. Deliberate export-tax-rebate fraud triggers recovery of the entire rebate, a monetary penalty of one to five times the defrauded tax amount, suspension or revocation of export-rebate qualification, and criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 204. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT zero-rating regime exists, so no VAT clawback penalty arises; however false or incorrect profits tax declarations are subject to IRD additional assessments, penalties up to three times the tax undercharged, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating RMB regime with current-account convertibility for genuine trade and services, but all capital-account transactions remain stringently controlled by PBOC/SAFE. For an onshore China Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), RMB is freely usable for operating receipts and payments that are supported by genuine underlying contracts, including franchise royalty receivables, syrup export income, rent, payroll, supplier invoices and tax. Cross-border capital receipts such as partnership capital contributions, shareholder loans, and foreign-exchange conversion of equity are not freely convertible: the FIP must complete SAFE FDI-capital registration with the designated bank, use an approved capital-account facility, and observe positive-list disbursement rules. Profit repatriation and dividend/liquidation distributions are current-account remittances only after audited financials, capital verification, tax clearance/withholding evidence and the underlying partnership profit distribution resolution are lodged; PRC withholding tax generally at 10% for non-residents, subject to tax treaty reduction, must be paid and evidenced. Significant structural hurdles remain for early redemption, premature liquidation, assignment of partnership interests, and round-tripped investments, which require prior SAFE approval, fair-value certification, and special interbank payment codes. Thus, the currency is operationally convertible but strategically reserved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-forex administration, an FIP exporting boba syrup and related ingredients must repatriate export foreign-currency receipts to its PRC designated bank accounts in accordance with the underlying contract; where the expected receipt date is later than 90 days after customs declaration, the FIP must register a deferred collection with the Trade Forex Monitoring System before the original maturity. The default statutory collection cap is 90 days from customs declaration for goods exports, unless extended and justified by commercial terms. There is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio or mandatory conversion percentage: the FIP may choose between retaining the foreign currency in its export collection account or settling to RMB at the onshore spot rate. The bank will perform truthfulness verification through electronic trade records, customs data, contracts and invoices before allowing conversion or payment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the competent PRC foreign-exchange control authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability is Low and materially restricted for the onshore PRC FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, N26 and similar are not licensed by SAFE/PBOC to maintain foreign-exchange or RMB settlement accounts for PRC domestic or foreign-invested entities. They cannot receive approved FDI capital injections nor execute SAFE trade verification and payment declaration. An onshore FIP may only use a domestic designated bank or a domestic joint-stock bank with an FX derivatives license for trade and capital flows. The HK parent, if separately established, may use HK-licensed virtual banks or EMIs for its own offshore liquidity, but those rails must not be commingled with the PRC entity's export proceeds, capital account or royalty settlement. A pragmatic hybrid is to use onshore Tier-1 bank accounts for all PRC FIP flows and a separate HK treasury account for the investor, with intercompany transfers documented as arm's length payments.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

After SAFE has registered the FIP as a Foreign-Invested Partnership and the investor has completed the state-assigned annual reporting via the market supervisory authority, the foreign partner must open an onshore Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated bank. The remitting bank in Hong Kong must instruct a SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary account naming the FIP's capital account, beneficiary bank SWIFT/BIC, purpose code corresponding to Direct Investment Capital Contribution in the SAFE International Balance of Payments transaction classification, and the SAFE-FDI registration reference number in the beneficiary-to-info field. The PRC receiving bank credits the FX capital account, performs a verifiable receipt declaration and then, upon presentation of the FIP's business license, partnership agreement and investor resolution, allows conversion to RMB and transfer to the RMB basic account strictly for positive-list permitted purposes including wages, raw materials, rent, taxes and operating expenses. The external auditor should issue a capital verification report only when required by the partnership agreement; there is no statutory minimum capital, but the actual contribution must be remitted from the overseas investor's own account and cannot be advanced by an unrelated third party.All outbound settlement must be routed through the FIP's onshore FX settlement account using domestic bank payment rails, not offshore EMIs. For standard high-value supplies and franchise inventory purchases from third-party suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code Trade Payment or Goods Import and supporting invoice, contract, proforma invoice, bill of lading and customs record. If the FIP is importing tea, milk powder or packaging from a Hong Kong or offshore supplier, an irrevocable confirmed at-sight Letter of Credit is recommended: the FIP applies through its Tier-1 bank, the L/C is advised and negotiated through the beneficiary's bank, and the corresponding HKD or USD settlement is executed on presentation of compliant documents. For recurring low-ticket shipments of syrups and franchise supplies from mainland to related franchisee stores in Hong Kong, a Documentary Collection at sight or with tenor is permitted and provides control over shipping documents while avoiding open-account risk to the franchisee. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, N26 and similar are not licensed by SAFE/PBOC to maintain foreign-exchange or RMB settlement accounts for PRC domestic or foreign-invested entities. They cannot receive approved FDI capital injections nor execute SAFE trade verification and payment declaration. An onshore FIP may only use a domestic designated bank or a domestic joint-stock bank with an FX derivatives license for trade and capital flows. The HK parent, if separately established, may use HK-licensed virtual banks or EMIs for its own offshore liquidity, but those rails must not be commingled with the PRC entity's export proceeds, capital account or royalty settlement. A pragmatic hybrid is to use onshore Tier-1 bank accounts for all PRC FIP flows and a separate HK treasury account for the investor, with intercompany transfers documented as arm's length payments.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 100,000,000 aggregate related-party transactions in a tax year for mandatory Local File preparation; RMB 1,000,000,000 aggregate cross-border related-party transactions for mandatory Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: The Mainland-Hong Kong DTA benefit requires the Hong Kong partner to have real economic substance in Hong Kong, including substantive board decisions, local management, employees, premises, and the performance of core income-generating activities in Hong Kong. The PRC tax authorities may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the PRC CIT Law and the principal purpose test to deny treaty benefits where an FIP or Hong Kong affiliate is a conduit or shell. For Hong Kong profits tax purposes, offshore sourcing and any preferential claims require evidence that the franchise licensing functions, trademark management, pricing decisions, and key business risks are located in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and DTA Fraud

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny the DTA benefit and impose the full 10% statutory withholding rate. For under-declaration, Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%. If the case is criminalized under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, the responsible individuals face fines and imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers who control the Hong Kong conduit can have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law principles: they become jointly and severally liable for the underpaid PRC withholding tax, civil restitution of the fraudulent tax benefit, and any late surcharge. They also face tax blacklisting, exit restrictions, travel bans, and potential criminal imprisonment of 3 to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing and Royalty Stripping

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment under Article 41 of the CIT Law, disallow the excessive royalty deduction, reallocate taxable profit to the PRC entity, and impose interest and late-payment surcharges. Failure to prepare the Local File may attract penalties under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law of RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000. If the adjustment is treated as tax evasion, the penalty escalates to 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements, control the royalty pricing, and cause the underpayment of tax are at risk of personal joint and several liability for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties. Asset restitution orders, freezing of personal bank accounts, travel bans, and director disqualification can be imposed. Criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law can result in up to 7 years imprisonment.
Money Laundering and VAT/Invoice Fraud

Statutory sanction: The conduct violates the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and Article 191 of the Criminal Law, which imposes asset confiscation and imprisonment of up to 10 years for money laundering. Issuing false VAT invoices violates Article 205 of the Criminal Law, carrying fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment in severe cases. The Tax Collection Law imposes additional penalties of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and daily late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners cannot hide behind the pass-through FIP structure. The general partner bears unlimited joint and several liability for the partnership's unpaid taxes and civil restitution. Personal assets may be frozen and confiscated. Directors face criminal prosecution for money laundering and tax evasion with combined imprisonment exposure of up to 10 years, plus travel bans and blacklisting from the financial system.
