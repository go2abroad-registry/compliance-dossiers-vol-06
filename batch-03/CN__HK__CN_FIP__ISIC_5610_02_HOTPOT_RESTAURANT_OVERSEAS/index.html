Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong partnerships are not subject to minimum paid-up capital or escrow-blocked account requirements. Capitalization is determined by the Partnership Agreement; contributions may be cash, property, or services, and must be recorded in the partnership books. Each partner's capital contribution and distribution entitlement must be stated in the agreement. The only mandatory payment is the annual business registration fee and any levy under Cap. 310.

Funding & unlock path: A valid Fire Service Installations and Equipment Certificate under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), structural load and means-of-escape inspection under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), and a workplace safety assessment under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) are mandatory. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk); the Business Registration Office of the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong limited partnership, apply to the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. File the prescribed statutory business registration application form together with a certified copy of the partnership agreement, the foreign investor's certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration certificate, and proof of identity of the local authorised representative. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) with a seven-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the Hong Kong tax identifier. A non-Hong Kong resident partner is not issued a separate VAT or GST number because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. If the partnership has no Hong Kong-sourced assessable profits, a nil profits tax return is filed through eTAX.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not maintain a check-the-box election or a general hybrid mismatch election. The partnership is statutorily transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112): profits are computed at the partnership level but assessed on the partners in their respective shares. Foreign tax classification of the partnership may differ, and the transfer pricing and anti-hybrid rules apply automatically where a mismatch arises; no elective regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, sales or use tax is levied in Hong Kong. The relevant indirect taxes are only stamp duty and excise duties on specified commodities; neither applies to hotpot restaurant dining or condiment supply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. Business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) commences on the first day of business rather than on any turnover amount.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong: not applicable because there is no VAT/GST export zero-rating system. The export of goods from Hong Kong is outside the scope of Hong Kong profits tax only if the profit source is outside Hong Kong. Mainland China: the Chinese exporter of hotpot condiments is eligible for VAT export zero-rating where the goods are physically exported from mainland China under customs supervision, the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, the sale is to a non-mainland buyer, and the exporter collects foreign currency as required by State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules. Such zero-rating is effected as export exemption and refund (or exemption without refund) at an effective VAT rate of zero. Qualifying offshore digital services are not relevant to this hotpot restaurant scope.

For mainland China export VAT zero-rating, the tax authority may audit the customs declaration, logistic evidence, and foreign exchange receipt. If the export is not verified or is fraudulently documented, the entire refunded VAT is recovered with interest, a fine of one to five times the fraudulent refund is imposed, and criminal liability may be pursued. In Hong Kong, because no VAT/GST or export zero-rating clawback exists, the corresponding risk is limited to profits tax penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, where false or misleading profits tax declarations can lead to additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax and criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current account items, including export proceeds from condiment sales shipped to the overseas hotpot restaurants and cross-border service fees for franchise or technical support, are freely convertible and may be settled into RMB or retained in foreign currency subject to genuine transaction verification by the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account items, however, remain strictly licensed. Inbound investment by the foreign partners into the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must complete a SAFE foreign direct investment registration and open a capital escrow account before funds can be converted. Onward outbound routing from the FIP to the Hong Kong holding vehicle requires prior approval or filing under the Overseas Direct Investment framework administered by the National Development and Reform Commission and the Ministry of Commerce, followed by a SAFE ODI foreign exchange registration that fixes the remittance quota. The Hong Kong SAR leg is fully convertible, with the HKD freely traded under the Linked Exchange Rate System and no surrender obligations whatsoever. Profit and dividend remittance hurdles arise on the onshore side: the FIP must evidence true distributable earnings through statutory audited financial statements, discharge any PRC enterprise income tax liability, and obtain a SAFE profit remittance verification each time a cross-border capital distribution is executed. No mandatory export surrender applies to current account receipts today, but all export proceeds must be repatriated and verified through the trade credit reporting system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Guidelines (2020 Edition), the mandatory export surrender ratio has been abolished to zero percent, so the FIP may freely elect to keep export proceeds in foreign currency or settle them into RMB. Nevertheless, all export proceeds derived from condiment and equipment shipments to the overseas hotpot restaurants must be repatriated into the onshore banking system and verified through the enterprise trade credit reporting framework; the statutory repatriation window is generally five years from the date of the export customs declaration, and any receivable aged beyond the deadline requires a documented justification submitted to SAFE through the designated bank.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the sovereign People's Republic of China leg; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank for the Hong Kong SAR treasury leg

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions have partial viability only on the Hong Kong leg. HKMA-licensed stored value facilities and money service operators may lawfully handle HKD settlement, local merchant acquiring, and international disbursements for the overseas hotpot restaurants. However, they are legally barred from the PRC onshore leg: the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations mandate that all cross-border foreign exchange receipts and payments must be routed through designated foreign exchange banks, and the PBOC does not grant EMI banking licenses that can bypass a commercial bank. Therefore, an EMI cannot carry the FIP's inbound FDI, outbound ODI, or export proceeds repatriation, and recommending one as the primary onshore treasury rail would constitute a compliance violation. The optimal design restricts EMIs to the restaurant point-of-sale and acquiring layer, with all cross-border flows passing through licensed Tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant routing protocol is a five-step sequence. Step one: the foreign partners transmit the FDI capital by SWIFT MT103 into the FIP capital escrow account at Bank of China Limited, quoting the SAFE investment purpose code with a remittance reference of Foreign Direct Investment in Foreign-Invested Partnership. Step two: the designated bank verifies the incoming payment against the SAFE FDI registration and executes the foreign exchange settlement into RMB. Step three: the FIP completes the NDRC and MOFCOM ODI filings and secures the SAFE ODI foreign exchange registration that fixes the authorised remittance quota for the Hong Kong holding vehicle. Step four: the FIP instructs the bank to convert and wire the outbound investment from the capital escrow account to the Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited treasury account, quoting the ODI purpose code; the wire is cleared through the China National Advanced Payment System and then via the Hong Kong Dollar Clearing House Automated Transfer System. Step five: the Hong Kong receiving bank confirms book-entry credit to the operational currency account, and the FIP files the capital verification report with SAFE via the designated bank to close the compliance loop.Four clearing rails are deployed for trade settlements. Rail one: SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for high-frequency low-value payments such as fresh ingredient procurement, staff payroll, and landlord rent, quoting the appropriate current account purpose code. Rail two: commercial letters of credit issued at sight by Bank of China Limited and advised through Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited for bulk condiment shipments, protecting the exporter against payment default. Rail three: documentary collections, both documents against payment and documents against acceptance, for established suppliers granted open-account terms. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when the Hong Kong entity's annual turnover or gross income exceeds HKD 20,000,000 and the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 5,000,000; Master File is required for a multinational group with consolidated group revenue of at least HKD 680,000,000; Country-by-Country Report threshold is HKD 6,800,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The Hong Kong FIP must demonstrate real commercial substance in Hong Kong: local office, employees with actual functions, board and partner meetings held in Hong Kong, local bank accounts used for operational payments, and substantive decision-making records. For the Mainland China hotpot operations, the Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in the jurisdiction where the restaurants operate, including purchasing of ingredients, restaurant management, customer service, and condiment production or procurement. The China-Hong Kong structure will be closely scrutinised under anti-abuse and beneficial-ownership rules; a shell company without substance is not entitled to treaty benefits, and income may be reallocated to where the significant people functions are performed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), section 82A: on conviction, a fine of HKD 100,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for 3 years; the IRD may also issue reassessments and demand full supporting evidence for all sales and procurement records.

Director personal liability: The managing partners and any directors of corporate general partners who knowingly signed or authorised the false return can be personally prosecuted. The corporate veil is pierced where fraud is established: personal liability for all undercharged tax and penalties, asset restraint, travel blacklisting, prohibition on acting as a director or manager, and serious risk of custodial sentence.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement and Hong Kong transfer pricing rules; full Chinese withholding taxes become payable at 10% on dividends and 10% on interest and royalties, plus late payment surcharge; the IRD may disregard the Hong Kong entity under substance over form principles and assess profits in the hands of the actual economic owner.

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners may be jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties. Courts may impose a travel ban, asset freezing order, disqualification from managing any legal entity, and, where the arrangement is connected to laundering the diverted profits, criminal imprisonment for money laundering.
Anti-Money Laundering and Beneficial Ownership Compliance

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) and Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455): failure to perform customer due diligence and keep proper beneficial ownership records can lead to fines of up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years on indictment; dealing with property known or reasonably believed to represent proceeds of an indictable offence is punishable by imprisonment for up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are personally exposed to criminal prosecution if they knowingly permit money laundering through the FIP. The court may issue restraint and confiscation orders over personal and corporate assets worldwide, impose travel bans, and order disqualification as a company director or partner. Civil personal liability for the full value of laundered funds may also attach where the director did not act in good faith and with reasonable care.
