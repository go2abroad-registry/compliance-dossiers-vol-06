Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital rule applies to a Foreign-Invested Partnership in the People's Republic of China. No blocked escrow account is required. The partners must, in the Partnership Agreement, set out each partner's capital commitment and the payment schedule. A Hong Kong partner's inward capital contribution must be remitted through a licensed bank after the foreign direct investment registration is completed with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange or a delegated commercial bank. A limited partner's liability is limited to its agreed capital contribution provided it does not participate in management. The bakery chain business is an open permitted category under the Foreign Investment Negative List, so no additional centralized capital pre-approval is required.

Funding & unlock path: The bakery stores and central kitchen must obtain fire protection design review, fire completion acceptance or filing, and a Public Gathering Venue Fire Safety Inspection Opinion before operation. Fire suppression systems, automatic fire alarm systems, emergency lighting, exit signage, and evacuation plans must be installed and tested according to building class. Civil Defense structural load inspections are required for central kitchen equipment, walk-in freezers, dough mixers, and storage racks; basements and conversion units must receive civil defense approval. Occupational clearance includes workplace ventilation, heat stress management, food handler health checks, safeguarding of ovens and mixers, hazardous material safety data sheets, emergency response drills, and a documented occupational health and safety management system approved by the competent labor safety authority.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR, including the Business Registration Office (BRO); electronic portal: eTAX (Electronic Tax System) operated by the IRD.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor establishing a Hong Kong partnership, the statutory tax identification number (TIN) is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Procedure: (1) execute the partnership deed and, where applicable, register the limited partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37) or Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637); (2) within one month after commencement of business, submit the prescribed business registration application to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department, attaching the partnership registration certificate, executed partnership deed, partner register, identity and incorporation documents of each foreign corporate or individual partner, and proof of registered office address in Hong Kong; (3) the Business Registration Office issues the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the BRN; (4) display the BRC at the Hong Kong place of business and use the BRN in all tax correspondence; (5) use eTAX to update partnership particulars, file tax returns, and pay renewal fees. A VAT ID is not applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT. Non-resident partners who are assessable on allocated partnership profits may need to file Profits Tax returns through the partnership or an authorized tax representative.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A partnership is treated as a transparent conduit, and each partner is charged to Profits Tax separately on the partnership profits allocated to that partner.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax in Hong Kong SAR; indirect impositions are limited to stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117) and customs/excise duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold. All businesses, including partnerships, must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST on exports or domestic supplies, so there is no export zero-rating claim, no input VAT refund mechanism, and no export zero-rating certificate. For Profits Tax purposes, an export trading profit derived by a Hong Kong partnership may be treated as offshore income if the contracts are negotiated, executed, and fulfilled outside Hong Kong; the source rule is territorial, not an indirect tax zero-rating relief.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods from Hong Kong
  • Commercial invoices, packing lists, sales contracts, and purchase orders for the export transaction
  • Bank credit advices or inward remittance records from the Hong Kong bank evidencing receipt of export proceeds

None. Hong Kong does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoicing approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime for indirect taxes. The IRD eTAX portal is used for electronic filing of tax returns and business registration administrative matters, not for transactional e-invoicing clearance.

No VAT zero-rating clawback exists because Hong Kong has no VAT. However, if a partnership claims offshore Profits Tax treatment for export income and the Inland Revenue Department disallows that claim, the tax undercharged is recoverable, and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) may apply, including additional tax of up to treble the undercharged tax for fraudulent or negligent returns, criminal fines, and imprisonment. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with the renminbi (CNY) conditionally convertible. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are freely convertible under the IMF Article VIII obligations, subject to SAFE verification and documentation requirements. However, capital account transactions remain strictly controlled and require prior approval, registration, and dedicated accounts. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), inbound capital contributions from the Hong Kong partner must be registered with SAFE via the Foreign Investment Information Reporting System, and funds must be drawn into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a domestic designated bank. Profit remittances to the overseas partner are permitted only upon annual audit, tax clearance from the State Taxation Administration, and SAFE-recommended post-verification; no conversion limitations are applied to profits attributable to paid-in capital, but deferred remittances may be scrutinized for money-laundering or tax leakage risks. The surrender requirement for export receipts has been largely liberalized, yet all foreign currency receipts from export sales must be repatriated to the onshore banking system and recorded against customs declarations before any use or remittance. Non-wholly-owned partnerships engaging in royalty payments require explicit registration of the stream as a 'service trade' related transaction, with a signed contract and invoice submitted to the bank for direct reporting to SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE Circular on goods trade foreign exchange administration, all export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days of the customs export declaration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain 100% of proceeds in foreign exchange or voluntarily settle into CNY at the prevailing interbank rate, subject to SAFE's trade verification and anti-money laundering monitoring. Any deviation from the 90-day limit or unexplained non-repatriation triggers a qualified foreign enterprise investigation and potential inclusion in the 'suspect enterprise list'.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for any onshore regulatory purpose. China's foreign exchange administration does not license offshore fintech EMI institutions (e.g., Revolut, Wise, Airwallex) to hold CNY settlement accounts or to facilitate SAFE-regulated trade and capital transactions for domestic entities. Such EMIs are legally barred from servicing the FIP in a manner that would satisfy corporate banking compliance; the FIP must use a designated Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all CNY and foreign exchange clearing, including capital account settlements, trade payments, and royalty remittances. Fintech EMIs may only be considered for auxiliary cross-border cash management outside mainland China, never as the primary treasury clearing rail.

Such EMIs are legally barred from servicing the FIP in a manner that would satisfy corporate banking compliance; the FIP must use a designated Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for all CNY and foreign exchange clearing, including capital account settlements, trade payments, and royalty remittances. Fintech EMIs may only be considered for auxiliary cross-border cash management outside mainland China, never as the primary treasury clearing rail.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax on the corporate partner of the Foreign-Invested Partnership; the FIP itself is a look-through partnership and is not a separate CIT payer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on PRC-source dividends paid to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the China-HK tax arrangement if the beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the paying PRC company. Ordinary FIP profit distributions allocated to the corporate partner and already subject to tax as business profits generally are not subject to a second dividend WHT, but any recharacterized distribution is exposed to 10% WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory if annual related-party tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 20,000,000, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000. Master File obligations may arise for large multinational groups under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 depending on group revenue and transaction volume; the threshold must be tested annually.

Economic substance scope: The FIP must maintain substantive Core Income-Generating Activities in China: central kitchen employees, inventory risk, production decisions, franchise quality control, and active management. Although China does not maintain a UAE-style ESR statute, the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule and the Hong Kong DTA treaty benefit rules require substance-over-form. A HK partner that is a mere mailbox company with no management or operations in Hong Kong will fail the beneficial ownership and Principal Purpose Test, resulting in denial of treaty relief and restoration of the 10% statutory WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company profit misattribution and tax evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 authorizes recharacterization under GAAR. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax and late interest of 0.05% per day. Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion over the statutory threshold with up to 7 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The general partner of the FIP and the HK directorial shareholders are jointly and severally liable for unpaid partnership taxes. PRC tax authorities may freeze personal bank accounts, seize offshore-linked assets, impose travel bans/blacklisting under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and pierce the corporate veil where the shell was used to evade tax.
Transfer pricing non-compliance and absence of Local File

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 permits the tax authority to adjust taxable income according to the arm's length principle; late interest and a 5 percentage point surcharge apply. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 additionally exposes the entity to 50%-5x penalties for fabricated books and false records.

Director personal liability: Directors and the general partner who signed or approved the false related-party invoices are jointly liable for the adjusted tax. Personal assets may be attached, exit bans may be imposed under the tax blacklisting system, and willful false documentation triggers criminal liability for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Failure to withhold on royalties and abusive DTA claim

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes on the withholding agent a penalty of 50% to 3 times the tax that should have been withheld. Tax underpayment interest of 0.05% per day continues to accrue, and the non-resident taxpayer remains liable for the unpaid tax unless the withholding agent satisfies it.

Director personal liability: The responsible director or general partner who ordered the unwithheld payments is personally and severally liable to reimburse the FIP and the State. The PRC court may freeze personal assets, impose exit-control blacklisting, and treat the offshore wire transfers as fraudulent concealment. Criminal liability for tax evasion applies if the scheme is proved willful, leading to imprisonment up to 7 years.
