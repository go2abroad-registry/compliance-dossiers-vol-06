Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a Hong Kong limited partnership. The partners are free to fix capital contributions by deed. There is no blocked escrow account rule under the Limited Partnership Ordinance; however, in practice capital subscriptions may be held in an escrow account pending drawdown call notices.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Service Installations and Equipment Certificate under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), building occupancy certificate/structural compliance under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), and Occupational Safety and Health compliance under Cap. 509, including a hazard assessment of the self-serve broth stations with anti-scalding, slip-resistance, and emergency evacuation approvals.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including its Business Registration Office (BRO), with the electronic tax filing portal 'eTAX' accessible via the HKSAR Government website. The BRO is located at Revenue Tower, 5 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong.

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), every person or entity carrying on business in Hong Kong, including a foreign-invested partnership (FIP), must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) within one month of commencing business. The procedure for a foreign non-resident partnership is: (1) Execute and provide the partnership agreement (or signed certified extract); (2) prepare and sign Form IRBR1 - Application for Business Registration (for sole proprietorships and partnerships); (3) attach certified true copies of identification documents for all partners (passport or national identity card, and for corporate partners, certificate of incorporation or equivalent and certificate of good standing); (4) provide proof of the Hong Kong principal place of business (e.g., tenancy agreement, rate demand, or utility bill) and the partners' residential/business addresses; (5) pay the prescribed Business Registration Fee and Protection of Wages on Insolvency Levy; (6) submit the application and documents to the Business Registration Office of the IRD, in person or by post. Upon approval, the IRD issues a BRC bearing a Business Registration Number which serves as the entity's tax payer identification number (TIN) for all Hong Kong tax filings and correspondence with the IRD. There is no separate VAT identification number because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate any check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election mechanisms. A partnership is automatically fiscally transparent; each partner is severally assessed to profits tax on its own share of partnership profits. Individual partners may separately elect for Personal Assessment under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but that is a personal election of the partner, not an entity-level election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax - Hong Kong levies no general consumption tax; the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) applies only to specified alcoholic drinks, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST registration threshold. Every business operating in Hong Kong must hold a Business Registration Certificate as a condition of legal operation; turnover governs only the amount of the Business Registration fee and levy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT or GST and therefore no zero-rating of exports. Exports of goods or services from Hong Kong are outside the charge to any Hong Kong consumption tax. Accordingly, there is no export VAT refund claim to substantiate. For profits tax, export income may be taxable if the source of the income is in Hong Kong, but the relevant determination is made under the territorial source principle in sections 14 and 15 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, not through a VAT rate. While Hong Kong law imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, the 180-day benchmark in this dossier reflects the PRC State Administration of Foreign Exchange export-proceeds repatriation rule applicable where proceeds are settled through a mainland Chinese bank.

Commercial invoices may be issued in any format, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) requires that business records, including invoices and receipts, be retained for not less than seven years.

There is no VAT clawback mechanism in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST is imposed. Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese foreign exchange regime operates under a strict capital account control framework administered by SAFE while current account transactions are generally freely convertible for genuine economic activities. For the CN_HK cross-border FIP, the partnership's domestic fast-casual franchise revenue is RMB-denominated current account income and can be freely used for local operating expenses, supply chain purchases, payroll and tax payments. However, the partnership is subject to a genuine transaction standard requiring complete underlying documentation for all cross-border payments and receipts. Outbound royalty payments to the Hong Kong licensor are classified as current account services and are permissible once the FIP has completed its SAFE foreign exchange registration, provided the payment reflects an arm's-length license agreement, a withholding tax certificate, and a customs/SAFE trade in services declaration. Profit distributions and capital liquidation proceeds are capital account items requiring audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates and either prior SAFE approval or a post-event filing depending on whether the partnership allocates profits to a resident or non-resident partner. The FIP must not net receivables and payables offshore, and all foreign currency flows must be routed through a designated domestic account at a licensed bank. The use of offshore digital EMIs as the primary settlement rail would violate the statutory requirement that cross-border flows be transacted through licensed domestic financial institutions and reported to SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 2012 No. 1 and related update notices, all export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days from the date of customs declaration for goods exports, or within 90 days from the due date of an export service contract for service exports such as franchising and master licensing fees. There is no mandatory conversion ratio currently in force; the FIP may retain the foreign exchange in its settlement account during the statutory retention period, but must verify all receipts within the SAFE online system on a monthly cycle. All retained foreign exchange must be used for legitimate current account transactions, and any conversion to RMB must be executed at a licensed bank at the prevailing counter rate. For the FIP's royalty income received from overseas sub-franchisees, the funds must be credited to a designated current account and settled or retained only after submission of the intellectual property license agreement, tax invoices and supporting revenue reconciliation to the bank.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

The partnership must file the return and pay the tax, but partners may be jointly and severally liable. Late filing penalties: HK$1,200 for late filing, plus an additional HK$10,000 if the return is filed more than 6 months late. For willful evasion, penalties up to 200% of tax undercharged and imprisonment up to 3 years. Failure to file may also result in estimated assessments and additional penalties.
