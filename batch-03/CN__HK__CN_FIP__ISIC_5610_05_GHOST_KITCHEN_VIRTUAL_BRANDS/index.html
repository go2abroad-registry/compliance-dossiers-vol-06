Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the China Electronic Tax Administration portal; enterprise formation is registered through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) integrated one-stop system. Because the payload identifies Hong Kong SAR as the constituent jurisdiction, the competent authority for the Hong Kong partner is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong, administered through the IRD eTax portal and the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_FIP, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by SAMR upon formation of the Foreign-Invested Partnership. The full SOP is: (1) submit formation documents for the FIP to the local SAMR office, including the partnership agreement, foreign-investor identification, and food-service license prerequisites, and obtain the USCC; this USCC functions as the TIN and VAT registration number; (2) complete tax registration within thirty days through the STA e-Tax one-stop mechanism so that the partnership gets a taxpayer profile, payroll withholding registration, VAT registration slot, and stamp tax and social insurance flags; (3) evaluate VAT status and register either as a small-scale taxpayer below the RMB 5,000,000 general-taxpayer threshold or as a general VAT taxpayer if annual taxable sales exceed that threshold or input VAT credit recovery is needed; (4) apply for the Tax UKey and/or the fully digitalized electronic invoice issuance capability in the local tax bureau's electronic invoice platform; (5) enter all Hong Kong and other non-resident partners in the tax file and obtain from the IRD a Certificate of Hong Kong Tax Residence for any treaty-rate, Double Tax Arrangement, or reduced-withholding claim; (6) complete foreign-exchange registration, if applicable, with a Chinese bank under SAFE rules so that cross-border delivery-platform payouts and export proceeds can be tracked and substantiated; (7) where a Hong Kong entity is also created, register it with the Hong Kong Business Registration Office and keep the Business Registration certificate and Profits Tax file current with the IRD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible. Under PRC law, a Foreign-Invested Partnership is fiscally transparent: it does not pay Corporate Income Tax at the partnership level, and each partner is taxable on its distributive share of the partnership's profits. Nevertheless, the CN_FIP is a separate taxpayer for VAT, stamp tax, and other indirect taxes. The Hong Kong partner remains subject to Hong Kong's territorial Profits Tax only if profits arise in or are derived from Hong Kong; China-source profits are not automatically taxable in Hong Kong, and the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement would be applied consistently with the facts.

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax system has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election for FIPs; pass-through treatment is mandatory. The only administrative elections are VAT-related: election to remain a small-scale taxpayer below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, election to become a general VAT taxpayer to recover input VAT, and the election to apply 0% VAT export zero-rating by filing for an export rebate in the Chinese export tax-refund procedure. Hong Kong profits tax is imposed by territorial-source principles, so no entity-classification election exists; a Hong Kong partner may substantiate offshore income status through fact and source evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls. The Renminbi (RMB) is freely convertible for current account items such as cross-border trade settlement, service payments, and dividend remittance once bona fide documentation is filed with SAFE. However, capital account transactions, including inbound equity investment, partnership capital contributions, and external debt, remain subject to a registration-based administrative permission system. Export proceeds from the ghost kitchen's trade operations must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted into RMB; there is no mandatory surrender percentage, but all receipts must be reported under the Foreign Exchange Receipt and Payment Verification System. Profit remittance by the Foreign-Invested Partnership to its Hong Kong partners faces strict documentary hurdles: it must be funded from verified after-tax distributable profits, supported by a CPA capital verification report, an auditor-approved distribution resolution, tax clearance certificates, and a SAFE payment registration. Foreign exchange purchases for profit remittance require underlying profit and tax documents, and the offshore receiving account must comply with China's outbound remittance codes. The integration of HK and CN structures creates arbitrage risk, requiring banks to monitor round-tripping and shell-entity exposures. Overall, RMB current-account convertibility is robust, but capital-account movements require significant compliance architecture.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China via designated banks within 180 days of the export customs declaration date; no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed, but 100% of foreign currency receipts must be registered in the SAFE monitoring system, and any overseas retention above approved thresholds requires a separate SAFE licence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (Wise, Airwallex, etc.) cannot lawfully provide settlement, capital escrow, or corporate banking services to a Chinese domestic FIP for RMB or SAFE-regulated foreign exchange transactions. They are not licensed by SAFE to operate as designated foreign exchange banks in mainland China and cannot open domestic current accounts or process capital account remittances. Their utility is strictly limited to ancillary offshore activities of the HK parent. Feasibility rating for the FIP is low; legal constraint is absolute under SAFE and PBoC rules.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard Hong Kong Profits Tax rate for an unincorporated partnership (Foreign-Invested Partnership); corporate partners are separately chargeable at 16.5% on their attributable share of assessable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no statutory dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Hong Kong company, and a Hong Kong partnership’s profit allocation to a non-resident partner is likewise not subject to a separate dividend withholding tax; tax is instead assessed on the partner’s Hong-Kong-sourced assessable profits.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The BIR52 return must be filed within one month from the date of issue stated on the return. Extensions are generally granted for accounting periods ending 31 March or 31 December; for 31 December year-ends the extended filing date is typically 15 August of the following year, and for 31 March year-ends it is typically 15 February of the subsequent year, subject to IRD practice.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Gross income or turnover from all business activities, including delivery-only restaurant sales, virtual-brand food sales, delivery platform reimbursements, and ancillary income. Declare gross receipts in Hong Kong dollars. Non-resident partners must ensure the entity separately identifies any income sourced outside Hong Kong. If the ghost kitchen takes orders through UberEats or DoorDash apps, the foreign-sourced portion of the income must be supported by a territory-source statement and documentary evidence of where the underwriting, order acceptance, and delivery logistics decisions occur. Failure to file a Profits Tax Return on time may lead to an estimated assessment under IRO section 59(3), a late-filing penalty of up to HKD 10,000, and additional tax of up to 100% of the undercharged tax where neglect or wilful default exists. Fraudulent evasion attracts an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to three years. The IRD may also reopen prior years where false declarations are discovered.
Box 2 Total allowable deductions, comprising food ingredient costs, kitchen rent, utilities, labour and delivery courier charges, platform commission fees, packaging, insurance, amortization, licensing, and other ordinary and necessary operating expenses. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Inland Revenue (Transfer Pricing Documentation) Rules require a mandatory Local File and, where relevant, a Master File, when the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds HKD 10,000,000 in a year; additional group-level thresholds apply for Master File preparation. Controlled transactions include the payment of delivery-platform fees to a related party, royalties for virtual brand licences, intercompany management fees, and cross-border capital advances from the mainland China parent or HK intermediate holding entity.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the IRD applies the substance-over-form doctrine through the territorial-source principle, anti-abuse rules under IRO section 61A, and the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis. For the ghost-kitchen virtual-brand FIP, the CIGA includes brand development, packaging, kitchen production, order acceptance, delivery co-ordination, payment processing, and the exercise of management over delivery contracts. If those activities occur in mainland China while Hong Kong merely holds the partnership name or books profits, the IRD will re-source the profits to Hong Kong or the mainland tax authority, and treaty relief will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Offshore Claim and Under-reporting of Ghost-Kitchen Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) section 82, evading tax by false return or omission is an offence punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000 plus 300% of the tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to three years. The IRD may raise estimated assessments under section 59(3) and issue additional assessments for the prior six years.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the partnership veil where the FIP is a mere cloak for the director’s personal trading. The director or de facto controller becomes personally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, penalties, and IRD costs. Personal tax liability is directly assessed on the partner, and the individual may be blacklisted, denied travel across mainland China-Hong Kong border points, served with restraint orders over personal assets, and imprisoned for up to three years.
Sham Transfer Pricing and Absence of Economic Substance in the Virtual Brand Structure

Statutory sanction: The IRD applies IRO section 50A and DIPN 58 to disregard the sham pricing and adjust taxable profits upward. A transfer pricing adjustment may carry additional tax of up to 100% of the tax undercharged. Failure to keep required documentation can also expose the person to a specific penalty of HKD 50,000 under section 50D and potential criminal liability for abetting a false tax declaration.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or endorse sham intra-group agreements can be held personally liable under IRO section 80 for abetting the preparation of a false return. Where the transfer pricing scheme strips profits out of Hong Kong, the court may treat the partnership as the alter ego of its controller and order the director to disgorge the diverted amounts, restore misappropriated assets, and pay confiscation amounts; criminal imprisonment of up to three years applies if fraud or wilful default is proven.
AML and Beneficial Ownership Failure in Delivery Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) requires licensed businesses to keep records and carry out customer due diligence; failure to do so is a criminal offence punishable by a fine of up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to seven years.
