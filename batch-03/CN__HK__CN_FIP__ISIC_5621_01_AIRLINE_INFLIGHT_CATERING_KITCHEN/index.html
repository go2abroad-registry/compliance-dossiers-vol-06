Competent corporate registry

Formation / constitutional filings

Application for Registration of a Limited Partnership (Form LPA1) together with the Business Registration Application (Form IRBR1), executed Partnership Deed, Statement of Particulars, and consent of the general partner.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Hong Kong limited partnership regime imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. The Partnership Deed may stipulate cash or in-kind capital contributions at agreed valuation; tax classification is pass-through eligible unless the partnership elects otherwise.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certificate of fire service installations and annual fire safety inspection; Buildings Department structural certification and Occupation Permit confirming floor loading; Labour Department occupational safety and health registration and workplace safety approval under Cap. 509, including personal protective equipment and confined-space protocols; and Civil Defense-compliant emergency evacuation plans and airport security access control measures acceptable to the Airport Authority Hong Kong.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, operating the Business Registration Office and the e-Tax portal (GovHK, Section: Business Registration).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a branch or partnership in Hong Kong: 1) Obtain a Business Registration Certificate by completing Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) and lodging it with the Business Registration Office of the IRD, along with a copy of the partnership deed, identity documents of all partners, proof of local registered address, and where applicable, the certificate of incorporation from the foreign jurisdiction. 2) A local resident manager or authorised representative must be appointed to receive notices from the IRD. 3) The IRD issues a Business Registration Number (BRN) in the format '11111111' on the Business Registration Certificate; this number is the de facto tax identification number for the entity. 4) For partnerships, no separate TIN is issued to the partnership; each partner's Taxpayer Identification Number (HKID or Certificate of Incorporation number for corporate partners) is used for profits tax purposes. 5) Registration must be completed within one month of commencement of business; failure attracts a fine of up to HKD 5,000.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong partnerships are automatically treated as pass-through vehicles: the partnership itself is not an assessable entity; each partner is separately assessed on its share of partnership profits. There is no check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election available under Hong Kong law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General VAT/GST/Sales Tax (Nil Regime)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no general indirect tax is imposed; therefore no turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. (Specific duties may apply to excisable goods such as alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oil, but these are outside the scope of a VAT/GST system.)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - because Hong Kong levies no VAT/GST, there is no zero-rating mechanism for exports. Goods exported from Hong Kong are 'no-duty' (subject to the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, which impose a registration fee rather than a VAT/GST). No indirect export tax refund claim exists.

No mandatory electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance regime. Hong Kong's IRD does not require e-invoicing for tax purposes; businesses may issue ordinary commercial invoices. The IRD's e-Tax platform is used solely for tax returns and payments, not for invoice clearance.

Not applicable for VAT/GST purposes, as no such tax is in force. However, transactions involving export documentation (e.g., Customs declaration forms, Bills of Lading) are still subject to IRD's anti-abuse scrutiny for profits tax purposes, including transfer pricing audits. False declarations to the Department could attract penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including a fine and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC umbrella, the Renminbi is convertible for current account transactions following the principle of current account convertibility, but the capital account remains tightly regulated by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). The Hong Kong SAR treasury node operates under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Linked Exchange Rate System, where the Hong Kong dollar, US dollar, RMB and other currencies circulate freely subject only to standard international anti-money-laundering vigilance. For the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership, inbound capital arrives through a SAFE-registered capital account and must be converted to RMB strictly within the permitted capital settlement scope; every onward disbursement must carry a genuine underlying business purpose. Profit remittances and partner distributions are permitted only after the FIP demonstrates audited financial statements, completed corporate income tax filings, a partnership distribution resolution, and a SAFE verification that the payment is not disguised capital flight. There is no mandatory export surrender ratio in China today; export proceeds may be retained entirely in an onshore foreign exchange account. The material hurdle is not bank counter convertibility but the documentary burden: tax clearance, partnership resolutions, proof of operation substance, and matching transaction purpose codes to the airline inflight catering contracts. For the Hong Kong treasury node, there is no surrender or remittance restriction, but any funding downstream into mainland must be clearly tagged as either capital account or current account in accordance with the foreign investment negative list and FDI registration rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the mainland FIP, SAFE applies goods and service trade collection verification rather than a fixed mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds must be repatriated to an onshore foreign exchange account and verified by a designated bank against contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and for catering services the bonded airport delivery records. The FIP may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a licensed foreign exchange settlement account or convert it to RMB at market rate; retention outside mainland without SAFE approval is prohibited. For the Hong Kong treasury node, no surrender or conversion requirement applies because Hong Kong SAR imposes no foreign exchange controls.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR treasury node.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. Offshore fintech EMI platforms do not hold a branch license from the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Authority or SAFE, cannot maintain the statutory basic deposit account, and cannot receive an inbound capital injection for FDI verification. Such platforms are not viable for the FIP's capital or treasury operations. For the Hong Kong SAR treasury node, HKMA-licensed virtual banks are technically viable for operational cash management, but unlicensed offshore EMIs should be used only for limited pre-paid expense rails and never as the primary account structure for statutory cross-border clearing. Overall viability rating: Low for the mainland FIP; acceptable but with caution for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is mandatory where annual controlled transactions exceed HK$5,000,000; Master File documentation is required for a Hong Kong ultimate parent entity with consolidated group revenue exceeding HK$6,800,000,000. The Foreign-Invested Partnership must maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for intercompany airline catering procurement, royalty or management fee arrangements, and related-party financing.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to BVI or Cayman; however, the IRD applies substantive anti-abuse and substance-over-form doctrines. DTA relief requires central management and control in Hong Kong and genuine business substance. An operational airport inflight catering kitchen with physical premises, refrigeration assets, permanent employees, and airline contracts readily satisfies this standard, but shell structures or offshore paper arrangements will be denied treaty benefits and may be re-characterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing

Statutory sanction: Summary conviction: fine up to HK$10,000 plus additional tax equal to three times the undercharged assessment; Indictment: fine up to HK$50,000, imprisonment for up to 3 years, and restitution of three times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: The responsible partner or any person in actual management is personally liable. Courts may impose travel bans, asset freezing, disqualification from managing enterprises, and civil recovery orders against personal assets.
Artificial Profit Shifting / Shell Company Substance Deficit

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the artificial structure, reassess the entire Hong Kong-source profit, and add a 100% tax-avoidance penalty. The partnership may lose its treaty benefits, and the Business Registration Certificate may be cancelled.

Director personal liability: Partners exercising control are jointly and severally liable for reassessed tax and penalties; a manager or shadow principal may be subject to disqualification, an overseas travel prohibition, and personal restitution to the Inland Revenue.
Money Laundering / AML Deficiency in Aviation Contracts

Statutory sanction: Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, money laundering carries a maximum fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment of 14 years. Breach of AML record-keeping and due-diligence obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance attracts fines of up to HK$1,000,000 and six months' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Individuals knowingly involved may be imprisoned, disqualified from directorship and partnership management, and subjected to travel restrictions. The court may impose civil forfeiture, freezing of personal bank accounts, and bar orders preventing property disposal.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The IRD may adjust the transfer price, reassess taxable profits, and impose a surcharge of up to 100% of the undercharged tax. Failure to comply with a TP document request triggers fixed penalties and daily default penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Managing partners may be jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties; aggravated non-compliance can result in criminal prosecution for negligent filing, personal liability for the corporate groups' unpaid PE profits, and travel restriction orders pending settlement.
Habitual Late Filing and Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The IRD issues best-of-judgment assessments plus an automatic 5% late-payment surcharge, escalating to 10% after six months. Prosecution may result in fines of up to HK$10,000 plus treble undercharged tax, together with an award of prosecution costs.

Director personal liability: The responsible partner remains personally accountable; a court may order disqualification from managing the partnership, prohibit departure from Hong Kong, and require the individual to personally satisfy outstanding taxes as a condition of continuing business operations.
