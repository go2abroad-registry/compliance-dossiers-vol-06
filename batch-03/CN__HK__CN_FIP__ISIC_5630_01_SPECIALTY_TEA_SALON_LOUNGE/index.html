Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership in Hong Kong. The Partnership Deed alone determines the amount, timing and valuation of subscriptions. No escrow or blocked account is mandated; however, banks may require the capital to be reported as source of funds for anti-money-laundering purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; online statutory filing is conducted through the GovHK eTAX portal and the Business Registration Office (BRO) of the IRD. The State Taxation Administration of Mainland China has no jurisdiction over HKSAR-sourced tax matters; Hong Kong constitutes a separate customs and tax territory.

E-government filing portal

Because the HKSAR has no separate legal vehicle called a 'Foreign-Invested Partnership', the entity is mapped for Hong Kong tax purposes to an ordinary partnership or, where the partners so choose, a limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). Step 1: Form the partnership under Hong Kong law and then apply to the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencement of business. Step 2: File the official Business Registration application form issued by the Business Registration Office (the IRBR1 series), attaching the partnership agreement, certified identity documents of each partner, proof of the Hong Kong business address, and, where a non-Hong Kong corporate partner is a party, evidence of its Part 16 registration under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) so that it may regularly carry on business in Hong Kong. Step 3: Receive from the BRO the Business Registration Certificate and the Business Registration Number (BRN); the BRN is the de facto tax identification number of the partnership and must be displayed at the registered business premises and quoted on invoices, receipts, and correspondence. Step 4: Ensure that each non-resident partner has a Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) or is identified for IRD purposes by the partner's Hong Kong identity document or foreign company registration number; the IRD will use the BRN for the partnership return and will assess each partner separately on their share of partnership profits. Step 5: Appoint a Hong Kong resident tax representative or authorized representative to accept Profits Tax returns, notices, assessments, and correspondence. Step 6: Open a Hong Kong bank account in the partnership name and retain all bank statements, accounting records, invoices, and contract files because the IRD requires records sufficient to determine taxable profits under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no partnership tax classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is transparent by default: Profits Tax is not charged on the partnership itself but on each partner separately in respect of the partnership profits. A claim that a particular export profit is offshore and therefore not taxable in Hong Kong is an evidentiary claim based on the territorial source principle and not a statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable: Hong Kong imposes no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. The only residual indirect duties are excise duties on dutiable commodities (alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol) under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Hong Kong. Every person carrying on a business in Hong Kong, including a partnership, must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD Business Registration Office from the first day of business, regardless of turnover. Importers, exporters, manufacturers, and warehouses dealing in dutiable commodities must separately hold licences and obtain permits under the Dutiable Commodities Regulations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

There is no VAT zero-rating regime in Hong Kong because no VAT exists. Export transactions are outside the scope of indirect taxation; tea and ceramic products are not dutiable commodities, so exports are not subject to excise duty. For direct tax, a Hong Kong partnership claiming that export profits are non-taxable offshore profits must establish, on the facts, that the profits arose from contracts concluded and performed outside Hong Kong; the IRD will apply the historical source tests in such cases. Export zero-rating substantiation is therefore replaced by source-of-profit substantiation.

  • Hong Kong Import/Export Declaration (shipping note) lodged via the Customs Electronic Declaration System (CED) within the statutory declaration window;
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Hong Kong entity to the offshore buyer;
  • Packing list;
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement from Hong Kong to the export destination;
  • Road Cargo System (RCS) cargo declaration and vehicle manifest where goods are moved by road into Mainland China or other connected destinations;
  • Import/export licences or permits under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) for any controlled commodities, together with applicable food-safety or phytosanitary certificates for tea products;
  • Bank SWIFT MT103, bank credit advice, or bank statement evidencing receipt of export sale proceeds from the non-resident customer; Hong Kong imposes no foreign exchange controls and no Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is issued or required.

No real-time electronic invoicing clearance, no tax authority pre-approval API, and no anti-fraud digital stamping is imposed by the IRD or the Customs and Excise Department. Business records and supporting vouchers, including POS receipts and credit-card settlement reports for the specialty tea salon, must be retained for at least seven years. Cross-boundary commercial road cargo movements must be declared through the Customs and Excise Department's Road Cargo System (RCS), and dutiable commodity movements are controlled through the Dutiable Commodities Information System (DCIS); export declarations for sea and air shipments are lodged electronically through the Customs Electronic Declaration System.

Although there is no VAT to claw back, the IRD may reopen partnership returns and assessments if export profits were later found to be Hong Kong-sourced. Inland Revenue Ordinance section 82A authorises additional tax up to treble the amount undercharged for fraudulent or wilfully incorrect returns, and criminal fines and imprisonment may be imposed on conviction. Misdeclarations in customs export declarations are separately penalised under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), with possible forfeiture and seizure. The 180-day foreign exchange benchmark referenced in this dossier is an internal group-control convention; HKSAR law does not impose a statutory FX repatriation deadline.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the Hong Kong dollar freely convertible into United States dollars within the prescribed band of HK$7.75 to HK$7.85 per USD. There are no restrictions on current account transactions such as trade settlements, service payments, dividend or profit remittances, or capital account movements including foreign direct investment, loan repayments, and portfolio transfers. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority does not impose surrender requirements on export proceeds, nor does it mandate any statutory conversion ratio for foreign currency receipts. The sole statutory mechanism is the licensing and supervision of authorized institutions for anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing purposes under the AMLO. In practice, the Foreign-Invested Partnership should expect no exchange control barriers to repatriating distributable profits from its Hong Kong Special Tea Salon Lounge operations, provided that the underlying funds are sourced from legitimate commercial activity and are accompanied by clear audit trail documentation, proper invoicing, and tax compliance filings. The dominant treasury practice is to maintain multi-currency accounts to reduce conversion costs, use spot FX when necessary, and deploy forward contracts with authorized banks for hedging operational exposures. Because the Hong Kong dollar is USD-pegged, businesses face minimal currency risk in USD-denominated transactions, but CNY, EUR, JPY, and GBP exposures must be managed actively. The city's banking system is characterized by deep interbank liquidity, efficient CHATS real-time gross settlement for HKD, USD, and CNY, and a robust SWIFT-driven clearing environment that supports high-volume cross-border activity. Accordingly, the Foreign-Invested Partnership should treat Hong Kong as an effectively open FX jurisdiction, with compliance attention concentrated on KYC documentary substantiation, source of wealth and funds verification, and sanctions screening rather than on exchange controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules or mandatory conversion ratios in Hong Kong. Exporters may retain all foreign currency receipts in designated accounts and are free to convert, hedge, or remit them at any time. The only requirements are AML obligations to maintain transaction records for at least five years and to report large or suspicious transactions under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance and the AML/CTF Ordinance.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) together with the Hong Kong Association of Banks (HKAB) operating under the Hong Kong dollar Linked Exchange Rate System

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Hong Kong's regulatory perimeter requires any entity engaged in the taking of deposits, issuance of stored value facilities, or provision of money service / money changing operations to obtain authorization from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority, the Customs and Excise Department, or a combination of both. Offshore digital EMIs based in Singapore, Lithuania, or the UK are not automatically permitted to service Hong Kong entities or hold Hong Kong business operational accounts. They are generally treated as unregulated service providers for deposit-taking and therefore cannot be relied upon as primary treasury rails. They may be used only as auxiliary payment corridors for pure pay-out scenarios where the EMI possesses a valid money service operator license under Hong Kong law, but their ability to support payroll, FX hedging, and corporate credit is legally constrained. The clear recommendation is to anchor treasury architecture in a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank in Hong Kong with full deposit-taking, trade finance, and merchant acquiring authority; offshore EMIs can be considered for e-commerce digital wallet settlement support after a detailed legal review of the specific licensed activities and cross-border fund flow permissions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections to the Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership should be routed via SWIFT MT103 or CHATS for HKD-denominated transfers, with clear payment purpose codes aligned to the local regulatory framework. For capital injections from mainland China, funds must comply with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) regulations on outward ODI remittances, including proper filing under the overseas investment framework or use of the Hong Kong entity's offshore financing facilities if permissible. Hong Kong does not operate a capital verification regime analogous to China's mandatory foreign exchange registration, but banks typically request a capital contribution schedule, board or partner resolution, source of funds documentation, and a statement of intended use. Any remittance from the China parent into the Hong Kong FIP should be tagged with the relevant bank purpose description such as 'General Business Funding' or 'Equity Contribution' and must not mention unsupported personal transfers. For cross-border receipts in excess of HK$120,000, the bank will require underlying trade documentation or investment verification under the HKMA's transaction monitoring framework. The recommended rail is CHATS for same-day HKD and USD settlement, with SWIFT for CNY, GBP, EUR, or JPY transfers. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

0% at the Foreign-Invested Partnership level because the FIP is a tax-transparent entity under Caishui 2008 No. 159; resident corporate partners face the 25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate; non-resident corporate partners face 10% PRC withholding tax unless a 5% or 7% China-Hong Kong DTA rate is approved.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory PRC dividend and profit distribution withholding tax rate is 10% for a non-resident corporate enterprise. Under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, the rate is reduced to 5% if the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company directly or indirectly holding at least 25% of the capital or profits interest and satisfies economic substance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200,000,000 for related-party tangible asset transfers such as tea leaf and ceramic purchases; RMB 100,000,000 for financial asset or intangible asset transfers including trademark licensing; RMB 40,000,000 for intercompany services such as management, marketing, and technical support, aggregated under SAT Public Notice No. 42 of 2016. If any threshold is exceeded, a contemporaneous Local File and supporting benchmarking must be prepared; a Master File is required for large multinational groups with consolidated revenue exceeding the SAT threshold and cross-border related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: The FIP is not subject to UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations because it is a PRC partnership. However, the economic substance doctrine is enforced under PRC EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement No. 60 of 2015 when treaty benefits are claimed. The Hong Kong corporate partner must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities, including approval of contracts, procurement decisions, management oversight, and pricing of the tea salon brand license, occur in Hong Kong. Substance warning: if the management team, procurement center, and strategic decision-making are in mainland China while profits are booked to a Hong Kong shell, the tax authority can recharacterize the arrangement, deny DTA relief, impose the full 10% withholding tax, and apply statutory penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse Through a Fictitious Hong Kong Partner Structure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority denies treaty benefits under SAT Announcement No. 60 of 2015 and PRC EIT Law Article 47, recharacterizing the allocation as dividend distribution taxable at 10% statutory WHT. Under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, the underpaid tax is collected with a daily late surcharge of 0.05% and a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax. The FIP as withholding agent is also penalized under Article 69 at 50%-300% of the unwithheld tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate general partner and its directors may be jointly and severally liable for the denied tax through asset restitution; the legal representative and finance director face exit-control and blacklisting under the SAT major tax violation disclosure system; deliberate treaty abuse is prosecuted under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment of three to seven years for large evasion amounts.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Failure to Maintain a Local File

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Articles 41 and 48, the tax authority makes a deemed arm's length adjustment with a five-year lookback. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, the resulting underpayment attracts a 0.05% daily late surcharge and a 50%-500% penalty for false filing. The SAT may publicly disclose the entity in the major tax violation announcement and impose additional interest for controlled transactions.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or directed the non-arm's length invoices and related-party contracts can have the corporate veil pierced under the PRC Civil Code and the General Partner unlimited liability rule, making their personal assets subject to restitution. The responsible directors are blacklisted and prevented from leaving mainland China without tax clearance; if false documentation is shown to be intentional, they face criminal liability for tax evasion with imprisonment up to seven years.
Anti-Money Laundering and False Invoicing Through Unreported Cash and Shell Suppliers

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, the unreported cash and deducted false invoices trigger recovery of unpaid tax, a 0.05% daily surcharge, and a penalty of 50%-500% of the tax evaded. Under PRC Invoice Management Measures, false invoicing attracts a fine of up to RMB 500,000 and cancellation of the entity's invoice privileges. Criminal liability arises under PRC Criminal Law Article 205 for fabricating false invoices, with confiscation of illegal gains and imprisonment of up to seven years for serious ordinary false invoice crimes.

Director personal liability: The directors, financial controller, and actual controller are personally exposed if they ordered or knowingly accepted false invoices and unreported cash. Piercing the corporate veil attaches personal civil liability for the unpaid tax, assets may be frozen or seized, and the SAT will issue an exit ban and blacklisting order preventing offshore travel. In a criminal prosecution, responsible individuals may be sentenced to imprisonment, and the legal representative can be held personally liable for restitution of all evaded tax and forged invoice gains.
