Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A mail-forwarding virtual office or commercial mailbox-only address is generally not acceptable for a PRC foreign-invested partnership. Partnership Enterprise Law and SAMR registration practice require a fixed premises that the FIP actually occupies and controls, evidenced by a lease agreement, the lessor's property ownership certificate, and a matching tax registration address. Banks refuse mailbox-only addresses for opening corporate accounts and merchant settlement such as Stripe and AdSense payouts, and tax authorities may reject such an address for invoice issuance. A serviced-office sublease may be accepted only if the FIP is the named tenant, has dedicated working space and signage, and receives service invoices in the FIP's name. We recommend a physical operating office in the same municipal district as registration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership. Each partner's contribution is contractually set in the Partnership Deed and must be credited to the partnership's escrow or client account. There is no statutory requirement for a blocked escrow account, but banking practice and Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Ordinance (Cap. 615) diligence may require funds to remain in the name of the partnership before drawdown.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic filing portal: eTax (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/etax/etax_login.htm)

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident partners establishing a Hong Kong partnership must complete business registration with the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department. Step-by-step: (1) Deliver Form IRBR1 - Application for Business Registration (partnership variant) and the relevant partnership deed / limited partnership agreement to the Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business. (2) Submit the prescribed particulars: proposed business name, principal place of business in Hong Kong, nature of business, partnership commencement date, profit-sharing ratio, and identity documents for all general and limited partners. (3) Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC), which serves as the statutory tax identity document, and a Business Registration Number (BRN) of the format Hxxxxxxx. The BRN is the functional equivalent of a TIN for all Inland Revenue filings. (4) The partnership is then registered for Profits Tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and the IRD assigns a separate Profits Tax file number if the partnership is a self-contained accounting unit. (5) Non-resident partners that do not have a Hong Kong address must appoint a Hong Kong tax representative under section 57A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance for service of notices and for compliance obligations. (6) The BRC must be renewed annually; there is no separate VAT/GST registration because Hong Kong has no consumption tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong operates a compulsory flow-through regime for partnerships; no 'check-the-box' election exists to treat a partnership as a taxable entity, nor is there a hybrid-mismatch entity election. Under sections 22 and 22A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the assessable profits of a partnership are determined as for a separate body, but tax is assessed and charged on each partner individually in proportion to his share. Partners cannot elect corporate taxation; they may, however, make certain operational elections for capital allowances and loss relief, but no entity-classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Hong Kong has no Value-Added Tax (VAT), no Goods and Services Tax (GST), and no general sales tax. The only indirect taxes are narrow excise duties on tobacco, alcohol, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol, plus stamp duty on specified instruments and Motor Vehicle First Registration Tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold activates indirect-tax registration because no VAT/GST/sales tax exists in Hong Kong. Any person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance irrespective of turnover. For narrow duties, registration arises on importation or supply of dutiable goods, not by turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Since Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, there is no statutory 0% export VAT rate, no zero-rating provision, and no VAT refund for exported goods or offshore digital services. For Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, a partnership supplying digital magazine services to non-resident customers may be eligible for an offshore-sourced profits exclusion if the services are performed wholly outside Hong Kong. The eligibility criteria are: (a) the contract for the services is negotiated, executed, and effective outside Hong Kong; (b) the services, including digital content creation, hosting, and advertising placement, are physically rendered and delivered outside Hong Kong; (c) the customer receives and consumes the services outside Hong Kong; and (d) no Hong Kong resident employee participates in the revenue-generating operational activities. The burden is on the taxpayer to prove the offshore source of profits on the balance of probabilities.

None. Hong Kong does not mandate electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or SAF-T filings. Tax invoices are not legally prescribed; however, for Profits Tax purposes, every person carrying on a business must keep sufficient records including invoices, receipts, contracts, and other supporting documents to enable profits to be ascertained, and must retain them for at least seven years. Electronic filing of tax returns is available through the IRD's eTax platform on a voluntary basis.

No indirect-tax clawback exists because there is no VAT/GST to claw back. For Profits Tax, an offshore-profit claim is audited by the IRD under the territorial-source principle. If the claim is rejected, the full profits are charged to Profits Tax at the applicable rate, and the IRD may impose a penalty under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance: additional tax up to 300% of the tax undercharged, plus a fine of up to HK$50,000, or upon summary conviction a fine of HK$10,000 plus three times the undercharged tax. Retrospective assessments can be made within six years of the end of the year of assessment, or ten years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. There is also a statutory offence for knowingly making false or misleading statements in any document furnished to the IRD.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). The current account, including trade in goods, services, and profit remittances arising from bona fide commercial transactions, is generally convertible upon submission of underlying transaction documentation and completion of bank verification. The capital account, however, remains heavily regulated: inbound direct investment into a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) requires a Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate issued by SAFE, and the corresponding capital contributions must be remitted into a Renminbi or foreign currency Capital Account opened with a domestic bank that has SAFE-authorized foreign exchange business qualifications. Once the capital injection is verified by a Chinese certified public accountant and the SAFE registration is updated, funds may be used only for the approved business purpose within the partnership's business scope. Foreign partners may repatriate dividends or distributed profits only after the annual audit, tax clearance from the State Taxation Administration, and submission of the profit remittance application, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, FIP registration documents, and the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. In practice, banks apply a look-through approach to ultimate beneficial owners, and remittances of royalties or digital advertising service fees are scrutinized against the service trade tax payment vouchers. Additional hurdles include currency surrender requirements for export proceeds, exchange rate volatility between USD and CNY, and SAFE's sensitivity to round-tripping where a Hong Kong SAR company owned by Chinese residents attempts to inject capital back into mainland China. The Hong Kong SAR itself maintains a separate free-floating currency, the Hong Kong dollar, with no foreign exchange controls and full current and capital account convertibility under the Linked Exchange Rate system managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. The practical treasury challenge is to bridge the two regimes: the mainland FIP requires domestic corporate banking rails for operational costs in RMB, while the Hong Kong intermediary entity can efficiently accumulate global subscription and advertising proceeds in USD or HKD and disburse distributions to international partners without PBOC approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 28 and current practice, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into China via domestic designated foreign exchange accounts or surrendered to a domestic bank for conversion into RMB. The statutory timeline is generally within 30 days after the date of receipt of the foreign exchange settlement proceeds, though SAFE can grant extensions for genuine commercial reasons. Since the introduction of the 2020 SAFE reform measures, all export foreign currency receipts are subject to mandatory repatriation, but there is no fixed forced conversion ratio for current account receipts; the exporter may retain balances in a foreign exchange account or convert them at market rates. However, for practical settlement under the FIP's capital verification and annual SAFE reporting, export-related foreign currency proceeds collected by an associated Hong Kong sales entity may be considered offshore and therefore outside mainland surrender obligations, unless the mainland FIP is the named beneficiary or exporter. If the FIP directly receives export proceeds for digital magazine subscriptions or sponsored content, the full amount must be repatriated to China within 30 days and reported via the bank's foreign exchange control system with the relevant goods or service trade declaration code.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI service providers and virtual banking platforms not licensed by the CBIRC or HKMA are largely barred from opening domestic RMB accounts for mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnerships. Foreign digital banks such as Mercury, Wise Business, Stripe Treasury, or Payoneer cannot provide the required SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration, capital verification, or domestic tax withholding infrastructure required for the mainland FIP. In Hong Kong, licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, Mox Bank, and Ant Bank are permitted to serve Hong Kong incorporated companies under HKMA licensing, but for the mainland FIP treasury structure they cannot serve as the sole clearing or compliance rail. The practical viability is as follows: Low for mainland FIP core banking; Medium for ancillary merchant settlement in Hong Kong or cross-border contractor payout; High for the Hong Kong entity's inbound subscription payments via Stripe and AdSense integrations, provided funds are periodically swept to a licensed tier-one bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong shareholder to the mainland FIP must comply with the Foreign Investment Law and SAFE regulations. The Hong Kong parent should designate one of the tier-one commercial banks in mainland China as the receiving bank, obtain a pre-approved Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate for the FIP, and remit funds via SWIFT MT103 using China's inward remittance purpose codes, typically code 621101 for 'Direct Investment Capital Injection' or code 622101 for 'Investment in Partnership Equity'. The remittance should be denominated in a convertible currency such as USD or HKD, although settlement can be in RMB via a cross-border RMB letter of intent or through the RMB clearing mechanism after conversion. The funds must land in the FIP's Capital Escrow Account and remain frozen until a Chinese certified public accountant issues a capital verification report confirming that the contribution matches the amount registered with the Administration for Market Regulation. Within 15 working days following the remittance, the FIP is required to update the SAFE registration and complete the Foreign Exchange Business Direct Reporting system. No disbursement for operating expenses should occur before the capital verification is complete. In the digital magazine and sponsored media business, the FIP may simultaneously retain offshore revenue in its Hong Kong entity; however, if the mainland FIP is the direct service provider to advertisers, any trade consideration invoiced by the FIP must be routed back to mainland China under service trade code 222019 or the relevant advertising and media purchase code.For outbound disbursements relating to trade and service payments, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct correspondent bank credits, suitable for payments to advertisers, journals, authors, and international content contributors. The FIP must issue a contract or invoice, complete the SAFE tax withholding process if the beneficiary is a non-resident, and submit the payment application form, the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, and documentary evidence such as pro-forma invoices or signed licensing agreements to the domestic bank. For larger or recurring media and print vendor settlements, commercial letters of credit (L/C) can be used, though digital media transactions rarely require the documentary L/C complexity. Documentary collections, which are lower-cost but slower, are only advisable for physical magazine or promotional merchandise exports from China; they require clean or documents-against-payment instructions and the underlying bill of lading or forwarder's cargo receipt. For service contracts, particularly digital advertising placements on the portal, banks will require the China Digital Payability service trade declaration, the tax payment receipt for withholding on cross-border service payments, and an explanation of the corresponding SAFE service trade code, before permitting the MT103 to be released. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI service providers and virtual banking platforms not licensed by the CBIRC or HKMA are largely barred from opening domestic RMB accounts for mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnerships. In Hong Kong, licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, Mox Bank, and Ant Bank are permitted to serve Hong Kong incorporated companies under HKMA licensing, but for the mainland FIP treasury structure they cannot serve as the sole clearing or compliance rail. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: There are no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover or headcount thresholds that automatically require an unincorporated Hong Kong partnership to appoint a certified public accountant auditor. Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap.622) s.405, only companies are required to deliver audited financial statements, and the audit obligation is size-independent; a company must be audited unless it is dormant or, in the case of a private company, all members agree in writing to dispense with the auditor. The HK FIP therefore has no statutory audit trigger based on assets, revenue or employees. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for unincorporated businesses including the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP); 16.5% for corporate taxpayers; the FIP is therefore taxed at the partnership rate of 15%.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Hong Kong imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong company and no withholding tax on partnership distributions made to resident or non-resident partners. A distribution from the FIP is a payment of the partner's share of profits, not a dividend, and is not subject to further withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong law does not impose a general registration threshold, but contemporaneous Master File and Local File documentation should be prepared when the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds HKD 100 million in a basis period. A lower threshold of HKD 50 million may be applied by the IRD for intra-group financing and financial service transactions. A Country-by-Country Report is required only for MNE groups with consolidated annual revenue of approximately HK$6.8 billion (EUR 750 million) or more.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations statute in the style of the BVI or Cayman Islands, but the territorial source doctrine, transfer pricing rules and the requirement of beneficial ownership for treaty relief perform an equivalent function. The FIP must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities are carried out in Hong Kong. For the digital magazine portal, relevant CIGA include editorial decision-making, content curation, advertising contract negotiation, sales-force management, server and platform operations, payment processing, customer and advertiser credit risk, and foreign currency treasury management. A partnership that merely receives revenue through a Hong Kong booking entity while all strategic and operational functions are performed in Mainland China will be treated as lacking economic substance and may have its offshore-source claim or treaty claim rejected.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent offshore-source declaration

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 80(2), 82(1) and 82A. On conviction, the partnership and the responsible partners are liable to a fine of up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for three years, plus additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also raise an estimated assessment under section 59(3) and recover the tax as a civil debt.

Director personal liability: The managing partner or partners are personally and jointly and severally liable for the tax, penalties and costs. Under section 22, a resident partner may be assessed in place of a non-resident partner and must bear personal civil liability. The court may order asset restitution and, in serious cases, impose a departure prevention order prohibiting the individual from leaving Hong Kong until tax obligations are settled. Imprisonment is available for willful evasion.
Transfer pricing manipulation and fictitious intercompany royalties

Statutory sanction: The Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2018, sections 50AAK and related anti-avoidance provisions under sections 61 and 61A. The IRD will adjust the profits to the arm's-length result, disallow the deductions, impose additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged under section 82A, and may prosecute for false accounting. Directors and partners who knowingly sign false accounts may face prosecution under the Crimes Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners who execute or sign the fictitious service agreements are personally liable for the additional tax, and the corporate veil or limited partnership shield will be pierced where the arrangement is a sham. The court may order the restoration of diverted profits, and individuals may be subject to civil asset seizure, travel restriction and disqualification from holding a directorship or partnership management role. Criminal imprisonment of up to three years, or longer for fraud under the Crimes Ordinance, may be imposed.
AML, shell-company abuse and false DTA beneficial ownership claim

Statutory sanction: Contraventions of the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Ordinance, Cap 615, section 9 (money laundering) are punishable on indictment by a fine of HKD 5 million and imprisonment for 14 years. False declarations to the IRD for treaty relief are punishable under Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 80 and 82. The IRD will deny treaty benefits, impose additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged and refer the matter to the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit.

Director personal liability: A limited partner who takes part in the management of the partnership loses the limited liability shield under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance, Cap 37, and becomes liable for all partnership debts and taxes. Directors and partners who control the scheme are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and may face civil asset recovery proceedings, arrest and departure prohibition. Conviction for money laundering carries a serious imprisonment term, and the court may order forfeiture of assets and permanent blacklisting from Hong Kong financial and corporate administration systems.
