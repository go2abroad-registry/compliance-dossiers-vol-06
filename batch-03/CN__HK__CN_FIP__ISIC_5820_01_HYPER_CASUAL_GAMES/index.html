Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Limited Partnership Ordinance imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked capital account requirement. The partners may agree cash, property, or services as capital contributions in the Limited Partnership Agreement. A licensed bank may impose internal minimum balances for account opening, but the state does not require any prescribed minimum capital for a Hong Kong limited partnership.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable – no Civil Defense fire permit, Dangerous Goods Licence, or structural load certificate is required for virtual digital publishing operations. If the partnership leases a physical office in Hong Kong, the landlord or building owner must provide fire service installation certification under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap 95) and compliance under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap 123), and the partnership should obtain an occupational safety risk assessment for its office premises.

Paid-in capital rule: None. The Limited Partnership Ordinance imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked capital account requirement. The partners may agree cash, property, or services as capital contributions in the Limited Partnership Agreement. A licensed bank may impose internal minimum balances for account opening, but the state does not require any prescribed minimum capital for a Hong Kong limited partnership.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: China Electronic Taxpayer Hall (e-Taxation) on the official STA website at www.chinatax.gov.cn. For Hong Kong SAR application, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTax portal at www.ird.gov.hk constitute the separate competent authority for Hong Kong profits tax and business registration, but Hong Kong has no VAT/GST authority function.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing or joining a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete SAMR establishment registration, which automatically issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax purposes. Within 30 days of issuance of the business license, the FIP must complete tax registration with the STA through the Electronic Taxpayer Hall, at which point the USCC is activated for VAT, corporate income tax, withholding, and invoice issuance. Foreign non-resident partners that themselves have no fixed place of business in China and derive PRC-source income are subject to source withholding registration administered by the FIP as withholding agent, and no separate VAT identification number is required unless the non-resident partner directly supplies taxable services in China. If the foreign entity is a Hong Kong entity, it must separately obtain a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate from the IRD and, if taxable, a Profits Tax file reference under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but no VAT/GST number is issued in Hong Kong. VAT registration for the FIP is entity-level and does not flow through to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible – the PRC FIP is tax transparent for corporate income tax; partnership profits, gains, losses, and credits are allocated to the partners under the Partnership Enterprise Law and PRC partnership taxation rules, and each partner is taxed on its allocated share at its applicable resident or non-resident rate.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate a check-the-box election for partnerships; a Chinese FIP is mandatorily treated as a pass-through entity. PRC hybrid mismatch rules under the corporate income tax special tax adjustment framework apply to deny inconsistent tax outcomes where a foreign partner jurisdiction treats the FIP as an opaque entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland China: no VAT registration turnover threshold exists; all FIPs must complete tax registration within 30 days of establishment. The compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable turnover above CNY 5,000,000 on a rolling 12-month basis. Hong Kong SAR: no threshold applies because no VAT/GST regime exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under China's VAT export regime, 0% VAT zero-rating applies to (i) physical goods exported out of PRC customs territory, provided the customs export declaration names the FIP as exporter; (ii) qualifying exported services consumed entirely offshore, including ad-monetized hyper-casual game publishing revenue derived from overseas end-users and paid by non-resident ad networks such as AdMob and AppLovin, where the service recipient is outside China and the services are not physically rendered in mainland China; and (iii) software-related cross-border taxable services within the statutory zero-rating/exemption list. The FIP must prove commercial reality through contracts, offshore consumption, foreign exchange receipt, and service delivery evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill as equivalent transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); in mainland China, the Cross-Border Receipts and Payments Declaration issued by the receiving bank is the statutory equivalent
  • Export sales contract, commercial invoice, packing list, and for digital services: signed offshore service agreement, AdMob/AppLovin payout statements, user distribution metrics, and evidence that the game services were consumed outside PRC customs territory

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice regime for VAT taxpayers: all VAT invoices must be issued through the STA national electronic invoice platform, with real-time data transmission to the Golden Tax Phase IV supervision system. The platform validates each invoice by a unique invoice code and digital anti-fraud stamping; taxpayers integrate through the Tax Control U-Key or full-digital e-invoice API. E-invoices must be electronically archived and are subject to instant clearance surveillance. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT and therefore no fiscal clearance, e-invoicing mandate, or tax authority invoice stamping; Hong Kong commercial invoices are ordinary trading documents without clearance.

The STA may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating and claw back VAT refunds where the FIP fails to substantiate physical export, foreign exchange repatriation within the 180-day statutory window, or offshore consumption of digital services. Under the Law of the PRC on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent export refund claims attract recovery of the full unpaid or refunded VAT plus late-payment interest and surcharge, administrative penalties of between 50% and 500% of the evaded tax, and, under the Criminal Law, export tax fraud liability including fines and imprisonment up to life. Taxpayers are also placed on enhanced scrutiny profiles, suspended from export refund facilitation for extended periods, and subjected to retroactive audits covering prior filing years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region operates a separate monetary and financial system within the People's Republic of China. There is no foreign exchange control regime administered by the People's Bank of China inside Hong Kong. The HKMA maintains the Linked Exchange Rate System, allowing currency convertibility for both current-account and capital-account items without prior approval. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible; foreign exchange receipts and capital inflows may be retained, repatriated, or invested without surrender requirements. Profit and dividend remittance from a Hong Kong treasury booking centre or partnership to non-resident partners is unrestricted, subject only to tax compliance and AML/KYC checks. However, if the ultimate financial owner is a mainland Chinese foreign-invested partnership or a Chinese investor injecting funds from the mainland, cross-border RMB/USD flows are subject to SAFE direct investment registration, foreign debt registration, capital-account supervision, and bank receipt confirmation. Non-resident partners in Hong Kong can freely subscribe capital in HKD, USD, or offshore CNH, but any corresponding capital injection from mainland China into the international structure requires ODI/FDI registration, tax declaration, and a compliant payment trail before profits are repatriated. In short, convertibility is complete at the Hong Kong treasury layer, while mainland controls continue to apply as upstream regulatory gates.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies in Hong Kong. Export proceeds from the hyper-casual games business, including AdMob and AppLovin payouts, may be credited directly to a multi-currency operating account and held in USD, HKD, or any freely convertible currency. For mainland-connected export or service income, SAFE requires the enterprise to declare authenticity, settle collected foreign exchange through a designated bank, and submit export/transaction documents under the current-account verification system; surplus proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted at the enterprise's discretion within the verified quota.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China as the upstream controlling authority for mainland Chinese cross-border flows.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility for fintech EMIs is High only as an ancillary collection or payout rail for the AdMob/AppLovin revenue stream, but Low as the sole infrastructure for capital escrow and treasury clearing. A legally viable EMI must hold a Hong Kong Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO). Such EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide the same credit, documentary trade, or capital-verification functions as Tier-1 licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs without a Hong Kong licence are legally barred from soliciting or maintaining Hong Kong jurisdictional treasury accounts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility for fintech EMIs is High only as an ancillary collection or payout rail for the AdMob/AppLovin revenue stream, but Low as the sole infrastructure for capital escrow and treasury clearing. A legally viable EMI must hold a Hong Kong Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO). Such EMIs are not deposit-taking institutions and cannot provide the same credit, documentary trade, or capital-verification functions as Tier-1 licensed banks. Offshore digital EMIs without a Hong Kong licence are legally barred from soliciting or maintaining Hong Kong jurisdictional treasury accounts.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false pass-through allocation

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Art. 63: fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge; if the fraudulent return is criminalized under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201, imprisonment up to 7 years and fines for amounts considered large.

Director personal liability: The managing partner or director who signed the false return is jointly and severally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax and penalties; courts may pierce the FIP/HK veil upon finding the partnership was a mere instrumentality; personal travel bans, asset freezes, property seizures and Social Credit blacklisting apply.
Shell company / DTA relief abuse

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 denies treaty benefits for non-beneficial-owner conduits; EIT Law Art. 47 GAAR recharacterizes the scheme and imposes statutory 10% WHT plus surcharges and 50%-500% evasion penalties; serious abuse can trigger criminal fraud prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201/205.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the sham HK holding company bear personal civil restitution exposure; the corporate protection is lost where the entity is found to be a facade; directors can be denied exit from China, have assets frozen, be blacklisted on the integrated social credit system, and be personally jailed for deliberate treaty-shopping fraud.
AML / cross-border payment rail failure

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines up to RMB 5 million on the institution and up to RMB 500,000 on responsible persons for serious AML violations; Hong Kong Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes fines up to HK$1 million and imprisonment up to 7 years; PRC Criminal Law Art. 191 money-laundering penalties reach 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and the managing partner are personally liable for AML compliance failures; regulators may pierce the partnership to recover laundered funds, impose civil penalties, issue travel bans (exit denial), blacklist the directors, and convict them for money laundering where the payment routing was knowingly designed to evade tax or sanctions.
