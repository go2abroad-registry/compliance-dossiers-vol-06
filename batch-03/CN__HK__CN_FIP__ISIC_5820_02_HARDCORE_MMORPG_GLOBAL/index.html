Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTax (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/etax.htm)

E-government filing portal

For a partnership, the entity itself does not obtain a separate Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN); instead, each partner must have a TIN (for individuals: Hong Kong Identity Card number or passport number; for corporations: Business Registration Number). The partnership must register for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The application is made via Form IRBR 1 (Business Registration Application) to the Business Registration Office of the IRD. For non-resident partners, they must apply for a TIN from the IRD by submitting a completed Form IR 1121 (Application for Taxpayer Identification Number) along with supporting documents such as passport copy and proof of address. The partnership must provide details of all partners and their profit-sharing ratios. Upon approval, the partnership receives a Business Registration Certificate with a Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the primary tax identifier for the partnership. Each partner's TIN is then used for individual tax assessment under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, the RMB is fully convertible for current account items, including cross-border trade in services and software royalties, subject to genuine transaction verification. Capital account transactions remain strictly controlled; foreign direct investment (FDI) into a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) requires SAFE registration and a dedicated capital account. Profit remittance by the FIP to overseas partners is permissible only after submission of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a SAFE-designated bank verification. Additionally, the 'surrender and settlement' mechanism has been replaced by a 'foreign currency retention' system, allowing companies to retain export proceeds in foreign currency or convert at their discretion, provided the underlying transaction is bona fide. Special attention is required for cross-border digital game revenue derived from App Store/Google Play payouts, which must be classified under current account service trade codes (e.g., 228020) and substantiated with platform payout statements. The offshore RMB (CNH) market in Hong Kong remains segregated from the onshore CNY market, and any capital flow between them is subject to macro-prudential limits and cross-border financing rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC rules do not require mandatory conversion of export proceeds into CNY; the previous surrender requirements were abolished effective 2001. Nevertheless, all cross-border FX receipts must be repatriated to a designated domestic bank account within 6 months of the transaction date and registered with the SAFE-in-licensed bank no later than 5 business days after receipt. The conversion ratio is at the enterprise's discretion, but any conversion exceeding USD 50,000 equivalent requires underlying contract and tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with delegated enforcement authority to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI solutions such as Revolut Business, Wise, or Airwallex are legally prohibited from providing multi-currency business accounts or payment services to onshore PRC entities. Only licensed commercial banks and qualified payment institutions approved by PBOC and SAFE can operate FX settlement and clearing. Offshore EMIs may, however, serve the Hong Kong SAR treasury function if a parallel HK entity is established, but not for the CN-FIP itself. Therefore, the viability of fintech EMI for the onshore FIP is rated 'Nil' for treasury accounts; they can only be used for employee expense cards or procurement in limited jurisdictions with no FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For injecting foreign direct investment into the CN FIP, the following compliant route applies: (1) Register FDI with SAFE through the designated bank (FDI Foreign Exchange Registration); (2) Open a Capital Account (FDI) to receive inbound wire remittances from the overseas partner, with the remittance purpose coded as 'Equity Injection for Establishment of FIP' under current SAFE codes (e.g., 601); (3) Upon each inward remittance, the bank verifies the registration certificate, the amount being within the total inscribed capital, and requires a 'Payment Use Letter' for any within-CNY re-conversion; (4) After settlement into CNY, the funds must be credited to a separate RMB basic settlement account and used strictly for the partnership's business operations; capital verification by a local CPA must be completed within 30 days. Inbound shareholder loans also require a 'Foreign Debt' quota registration and may be converted into CNY under the national macro-prudential policy.Outbound disbursements from the FIP for trade in services (such as software royalties, server hosting, marketing, and administrative fees) are processed through the current account. The company must submit an 'Application for Settlement and Sale of Foreign Exchange' with supporting documents: commercial contract, invoice, tax certificate evidence for royalties, and a non-trade outbound payment tax filing (for remittances > USD 50,000). The preferred clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers; (b) Standby Letters of Credit for contractual royalties; (c) documentary collections for peer-to-peer transactions. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An ordinary Hong Kong partnership is not automatically subject to a statutory audit under the Partnership Ordinance or the Companies Ordinance; however, an independent audit becomes compulsory if (i) the partnership is required by the terms of its financial institution lending, private equity or group reporting covenants to produce independently audited financial statements; (ii) the partnership is elected or structured as a Hong Kong private company or limited liability vehicle under the Companies Ordinance and fails the 'small private company' exemption; or (iii) the partnership is a Licensed Corporation or a financial institution regulated by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority or Securities and Futures Commission. 0% statutory dividend withholding tax in Hong Kong; however, where a Chinese-source distribution is made from a mainland enterprise or partnership to a Hong Kong partner, the PRC dividend withholding tax is 10% under domestic law, reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement if the Hong Kong beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the enterprise for 12 months before the distribution.
