Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk).

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong, the partnership must apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business. The application can be lodged online through eTAX or in person at the IRD's Business Registration Office. The IRD will issue a Business Registration Number which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number for the partnership. Non-resident partners are not required to obtain a separate Hong Kong tax file number for the partnership, but each partner (including any non-resident corporate partner) is responsible for reporting its allocable share of partnership profits on its own Hong Kong Profits Tax return; the partnership must also, upon receiving notices from IRD, submit partnership returns setting out each partner's share. Where the partnership has no physical presence in Hong Kong and the non-resident partners have no or limited clearly defined offshore activities, a professional tax representative may be appointed to liaise with the IRD and to file the relevant returns, safe custody of records, and correspondence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

For the Hong Kong SAR entity, the Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible under the Linked Exchange Rate System, and there are absolutely no restrictions on current or capital account transactions. USD, EUR and CNH (offshore renminbi) can be freely bought, sold, hedged and remitted without surrender requirements or approval. However, because the Foreign-Invested Partnership is ultimately owned and may have onshore Chinese partners, the regulatory overlay of the People's Bank of China and SAFE becomes relevant when funds cross the mainland border. A subscription of capital from mainland partners into the HK FIP is classified as outward direct investment (ODI) by Chinese residents and requires SAFE registration with a commercial bank, a certificate of overseas investment filing, and a business plan. Conversely, if the HK FIP injects capital into a China-based subsidiary, it must register under the FDI regime at the local SAFE bureau, open a RMB capital account, and use the funds only for genuine business expenditures under the Negative List. Profits distributed from the PRC subsidiary to HK must be verified by an audit, tax payment certificates, and a board resolution. No mandatory currency conversion applies for current account export proceeds in China; they may be retained in a foreign exchange account or settled at a bank, but repatriation to the mainland is required within the timeframe of the bank's foreign exchange receipts monitoring system (typically 180 days for service trade).

Export proceeds surrender rules

Because Hong Kong has no foreign exchange control, there is no statutory surrender timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for funds received by the HK FIP. For any repatriation of service trade proceeds (game publishing royalties) to mainland China, SAFE's 2012 Notice on Further Simplifying and Improving Foreign Exchange Administration Policies for Current Account Transactions requires the recipient to bring the foreign exchange receipts onshore and settle or retain them in a domestic foreign-exchange account; the receipt must be matched against the underlying contract and digital store settlement statement within the commercial bank's electronic verification window, normally not exceeding 180 days from the due receipt date. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the entity may choose to settle 0% or 100%, but 100% of receipts must be repatriated unless a SAFE exemption is granted.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China for cross-border capital flows with mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Medium - Complementary use only. HKMA-regulated digital banks and licensed Money Service Operators (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, Currenxie) are legally allowed to open accounts for Hong Kong corporate entities and are not barred from servicing the FIP. They can accelerate small-value payouts and provide low-cost foreign exchange for advertising spend. However, they are not licensed to act as designated banks for SAFE capital registration, cannot issue Commercial Letters of Credit, and do not provide the credit rating and custody strength required for a primary treasury account. Therefore, the entity's primary settlement rail must be at a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank; fintech EMIs should be used only for operational float and as an ancillary liquidity node.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Steam and PlayStation payouts should be received in USD into the FIP's designated USD account at HSBC or Standard Chartered Hong Kong. The platform's payment agent will send a SWIFT MT103 with ISO 20022 purpose code SERV (services) or ROYL (royalties), referencing the partner agreement and settlement period. On receipt, the treasury operation should mark the credit against the platform settlement statement, update ERP, and maintain a payout ledger. If the FIP needs to inject capital into a PRC subsidiary or fund an onshore entity, the HK treasurer must first complete SAFE FDI registration for the Chinese entity, open a Capital Account at a domestic SAFE-designated bank (e.g., Bank of China Shanghai branch), and then remit USD via MT103 with purpose code CAPT (capital transfer). The onshore bank will verify the SAFE registration number, amount ceiling, and negative list before converting to RMB at the spot rate; the funds are then released into the RMB capital account and restricted to genuine production and marketing expenditures. All inbound remittances must be accompanied by payment notifications, partner settlement reports, and the relevant CA/FDI filings.For outbound trade and operational disbursements (game localization costs, motion capture contractors, marketing agencies), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 driven through the bank's electronic banking portal, with purpose codes such as SERV (service fee), ROYL (royalty), or INTL (intercompany transfer). For construction contracts, hardware procurement, or post-production deliveries involving purchase orders and invoices, the entity may use Commercial Letters of Credit (LC) issued sight or deferred, especially for physical media manufacturing or promotional materials. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are less suitable for digital services but can be used for high-value physical shipments. All outbound payments should be recorded in the customs/SAFE monitoring systems if related to goods, or in the service trade payment system if services. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong statutory audit responsibilities apply to all tax-resident persons, including foreign-invested partnerships, which are required to prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view and to attach an auditor's report to the Profits Tax Return. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the Partnership Ordinance, a certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance must certify the statement of profits or losses when the partnership's gross income exceeds HKD 2,000,000 per annum; for a Hong Kong incorporated limited partnership or foreign-invested partnership with gross income not exceeding HKD 2,000,000, an auditor's report is still required if the Commissioner of Inland Revenue demands it, unless the partnership elects to file the simplified Form BIR52 for small businesses and the IRD accepts the unaudited figures. The compulsory audit threshold is therefore HKD 2,000,000 gross turnover, notwithstanding that any partnership taxed under the global pass-through regime with cross-border Steam and PlayStation Store revenue should expect a demand for audit. The Companies Ordinance analog threshold of HKD 10,000,000 is not the direct trigger, but a partnership with total assets exceeding HKD 7,500,000, turnover exceeding HKD 50,000,000, or 50 or more headcount would be classified as a 'large' enterprise under the SME Financial Reporting Framework and will fail to qualify for any small-entity exemption, making statutory audit mandatory; in practice, a foreign-invested partnership operating under the Hong Kong offshore sales regime must obtain an independent CPA audit if its gross receipts exceed HKD 2,000,000, if the Commissioner requests, or if it is otherwise not qualified for the small-business waiver. Annual audit of consolidated cross-border digital publishing revenue is also advisable where the partnership is a licensed Steam publisher because payment processor level accounts may require an audited financial statement, but that requirement is contractual rather than a statutory threshold.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% standard corporate profits tax rate; 8.25% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits for corporations. For this Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the unincorporated business rates apply: 7.5% on the first HK$2,000,000 and 15% on the excess above HK$2,000,000.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions made by a partnership to any partner, whether resident or non-resident. A distribution by the FIP to a Mainland China investor or any offshore investor is not subject to dividend withholding tax in Hong Kong.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$100,000,000 in aggregate controlled transactions for mandatory Master File and Local File contemporaneous documentation under IRD DIPN 59; the Country-by-Country Report threshold is HK$6,800,000,000 for qualifying MNE groups. Even below HK$100,000,000, the FIP should maintain transfer pricing documentation because the IRD may require documentation for any intercompany transaction where royalties, management fees, platform service fees, or profit allocations to non-resident partners are involved.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the IRD enforces economic substance through the territorial source principle and the general anti-avoidance rules under IRO ss.61, 61A, and 20AB. The FIP must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) actually take place in Hong Kong: contract negotiation with Steam/Valve and Sony PlayStation, pricing decisions, game-list curation, localisation, revenue acceptance, and risk management. If the FIP is a shell used solely to route royalties through the Mainland/Hong Kong DTA, the IRD can disregard the structure and reattribute profits to the ultimate beneficial owner in Mainland China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mismatch and False Intercompany Royalty Deductions

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO s.50AB and s.61A to adjust the partnership's profits to an arm's-length amount, disallow the excessive deductions, and issue additional tax assessments. Penalties include an additional tax of up to 100% of the undercharged tax; if the misstatement is deliberate, the penalty may be increased to 200% and criminal fines of up to HK$50,000 may apply.

Director personal liability: General partners and directors of a corporate general partner who knowingly approve non-arm's-length pricing can be personally assessed for the tax shortfall under the partnership liability rules. Piercing the corporate veil is available where the corporate partner is a facade; directors may face restitution of diverted profits, disqualification from managing companies, and imprisonment of up to three years for fraudulent tax evasion.
False Declaration of Offshore Steam Revenue and Source Misreporting

Statutory sanction: Wilful failure to disclose Hong Kong-sourced assessable profits is an offence under IRO s.80 and s.82. The court may impose a fine of HK$50,000 and additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged; on conviction by indictment, the maximum custodial penalty is three years imprisonment. The IRD also has the power to make estimated assessments with a 10% surcharge where records are inadequate.

Director personal liability: Any director, general partner, or tax return signatory who participates in signing a false return is jointly and severally liable for the civil penalty. The Hong Kong court may order a travel ban or departure prohibition for a person leaving Hong Kong with unpaid tax liabilities under s.77 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Director disqualification of up to 15 years is possible if the conduct shows unfitness to manage a company, and personal bankruptcy proceedings can follow for unpaid tax.
Shell Company / Treaty Shopping and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO s.61A to cancel the tax advantage, recharacterise the arrangement, and assess profits to the counterparty or beneficial owner. A claim for treaty benefits may be denied under the Principal Purpose Test and anti-abuse provisions of the DTA. Civil penalties of 100% to 200% of the tax avoided may be imposed, and the partners who signed false statements expose themselves to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under s.82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is permitted where the FIP is used as a sham or a mere facade concealing the real activities of Mainland Chinese investors. Directors of a corporate general partner may be held personally liable for trading while insolvent and for fraudulent trading under section 903 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Liability includes contribution to the partnership's debts, return of misappropriated assets, a statutory disqualification order, and, where cross-border criminal conduct is proven, extradition risk and imprisonment of up to fourteen years under Hong Kong fraud and tax conspiracy legislation.
