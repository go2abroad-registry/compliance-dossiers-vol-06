Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX (Electronic Tax Services) at https://www.etax.gov.hk, complemented by GovHK for business registration services.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate Tax Identification Number (TIN) or VAT ID. The statutory tax identity for all entities, including a Foreign-Invested Partnership, is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Step 1: Determine whether the partnership is carrying on business in Hong Kong; any partnership carrying on business must apply for business registration within one month of commencement. Step 2: Complete and submit the Application for Business Registration (Form IRBR1) to the Business Registration Office of the IRD; for a limited partnership, also file the statement of limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) with the Companies Registry if the partnership is constituted as a limited partnership. Step 3: Provide particulars of all partners, including full names, addresses, passport/identity numbers, and for non-resident corporate partners a certified copy of the overseas incorporation certificate and registered office address. Step 4: Pay the prescribed Business Registration fee and levy; no minimum capital is required. Step 5: The IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate displaying the eight-digit BRN, which is the entity's Hong Kong TIN-equivalent for all tax filings, profits tax correspondence, and business registration renewal. For profits tax purposes, the IRD will issue Profits Tax Return Form BIR52 (for partnerships) to the registered business address; a foreign non-resident partner is assessed only on its share of profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong. There is no statutory requirement to appoint a tax representative, but the partnership must maintain a Hong Kong business address for IRD communications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for entity classification. The partnership is always tax-transparent: the IRD assesses partnership profits on an aggregate basis and each partner is separately chargeable to profits tax on its agreed profit share under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Individual partners may separately elect Personal Assessment, but this election does not change the partnership-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General Indirect Tax (Nil VAT/GST/Sales Tax); Hong Kong imposes only selective excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on liquors, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol, plus stamp duty and motor vehicle first registration tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Every business carrying on business in Hong Kong, including a Foreign-Invested Partnership, must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) from day one regardless of turnover. Excise duty licences for dutiable goods are activity-specific and not turnover-based.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong does not impose VAT or GST on any supply, there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim, no export zero-rating substantiation procedure, and no export VAT refund mechanism. Digital services such as esports tournament platform subscriptions supplied from Hong Kong to customers abroad are simply outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax. The only relevant tax treatment is the territorial source rule for profits tax: services performed and profits sourced outside Hong Kong may be treated as offshore profits and thus not chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax.

  • No Hong Kong VAT-specific documentation is required because no VAT/GST applies.
  • For physical goods exported from Hong Kong, the standard statutory trade documentation is an electronic Export/Trade Declaration filed under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, supported by a Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and, if the goods are dutiable commodities, a permit under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).
  • For profits tax offshore-source substantiation, the IRD will accept partnership financial statements, contracts with customers and platform users, bank statements showing settlement via Stripe and PayPal, and evidence of where services are performed and managed. No Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) concept exists in Hong Kong.
  • The documentation bundle may also include an explanation of the esports platform's hosting, matchmaking, tournament administration, and customer support functions to demonstrate that the profits are derived from activities performed outside Hong Kong.

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API filing, or anti-fraud digital stamping applies in Hong Kong. The IRD uses eTAX for voluntary electronic tax filing; businesses may issue commercial invoices in paper or electronic form. For imports and exports, statutory electronic trade declarations are required under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations for consignments that are not specifically exempt, but no VAT clearance mechanism exists.

There is no VAT export clawback because no VAT/GST is imposed. However, the IRD will scrutinize any claimed offshore-source profits from cross-border esports platform operations. If the partnership or any partner makes a false or incorrect claim regarding the source of profits, fails to file Profits Tax Return Form BIR52, or evades tax through deliberate misstatements, the IRD may raise additional assessments, impose fines, and charge additional tax of up to three times the amount of profits tax under-assessed under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). In serious cases, criminal prosecution is possible. All customs and trade declaration records must also be accurate under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, as false declarations can attract separate penalties and seizure actions by the Customs and Excise Department.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account transactions. The Renminbi is fully convertible on current account items such as trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, provided the transactions are supported by genuine documentation and are verified by designated foreign exchange banks. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, cross-border loans, and equity transfers, remain subject to SAFE registration, approval, or post-event filing. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must obtain a foreign exchange registration certificate from SAFE before accepting capital injections from its Hong Kong partner. The partnership's foreign currency capital pool is held in a designated foreign exchange capital account and can be converted into RMB only for bona fide business purposes within the approved business scope. Surrender requirements for current account receipts have been abolished, but export proceeds must be repatriated within statutory deadlines and either retained in a foreign exchange account or converted to RMB through a bank. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permissible after tax settlement, an annual audit, and submission of a board resolution, provided the partnership has sufficient audited retained earnings and has fulfilled all Chinese tax obligations. Significant hurdles remain for capital account outflows, including the need for SAFE approval for cross-border guarantees, and the prohibition on free conversion of RMB for overseas investment without prior registration. Overall, the currency is convertible for genuine trade and investment, but with strict ex ante and ex post supervision.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE 'Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Trade in Goods' (2012), export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain the full 100% of foreign currency in a settlement account or convert it to RMB at its discretion, subject to the bank's verification of the underlying transaction. For service trade proceeds, such as esports platform fees and tournament sponsorship, there is no fixed statutory timeline, but proceeds must be credited through a licensed bank and matched with the supporting service contract and invoice. Capital account foreign exchange receipts, including capital contributions from the Hong Kong partner, must be remitted directly into the FIP's foreign exchange capital account and are subject to the SAFE capital account payment verification system; conversion to RMB is permitted on a payment-based basis only, meaning the RMB equivalent is released to the operating account after the bank verifies the intended use.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut, Wise, and Payoneer) have no legal ability to open local settlement accounts for a China-incorporated FIP, because the banking and foreign exchange business in mainland China is restricted to licensed banking institutions. Stripe and PayPal can be used only as third-party payment aggregators that collect cross-border transaction receipts on behalf of the platform; they cannot perform foreign exchange conversion, hold RMB on behalf of the entity, or support capital account flows. The viability of fintech EMI rails is strictly Low for treasury management and zero for capital injection, and any attempt to use such channels for investment or settlement could trigger SAFE violations and bank account freezes. The entity must use Tier-1 domestic banks for all regulatory reporting, clearing, and FX conversion.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner must remit the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 directly to the FIP's foreign exchange capital account held at a designated Tier-1 bank in mainland China. The remittance instruction must reference the FDI registration code obtained from SAFE, and the 'payment purposes' field must clearly indicate the capital contribution under the category 'investment in a foreign-invested partnership'. Upon receipt, the bank will verify the capital contribution against the registered total investment amount and issue a foreign exchange capital verification report. The FIP may then convert the foreign currency into RMB into the basic deposit account on a payment-based conversion basis, meaning each conversion requires an underlying expense document (e.g., rent, salary, equipment purchase). The converted RMB cannot be used for residential real estate, equities, entrusted loans, or reinvestment in securities.
