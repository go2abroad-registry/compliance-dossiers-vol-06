Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the national e-Tax electronic portal; the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is the separate competent authority for any Hong Kong SAR-resident partner or offshore distribution branch.

E-government filing portal

The China Foreign-Invested Partnership (CN_FIP) must first complete business registration with the Administration for Market Regulation to obtain a Unified Social Credit Code, which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax matters. Within 30 days of the business license issuance, the partnership must complete tax registration on the STA e-Tax portal, declaring its pass-through partnership classification and VAT taxpayer status. Foreign non-resident partners that do not have a permanent establishment in China must, where applicable, complete non-resident tax registration with the competent tax authority, submit treaty relief documentation, and obtain or reference the partner's home-jurisdiction TIN; the Chinese tax authority will then activate the CN_FIP's taxpayer record, including VAT obligations, through the electronic tax filing system.

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; the CN_FIP is nonetheless required to complete VAT registration upon commencing any taxable activity in China, including cross-border digital content distribution.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods and qualifying cross-border services, including offshore streaming and licensing of vertical micro-dramas, may be zero-rated under PRC VAT when the customer is a non-resident, the service or digital content is fully consumed outside mainland China, consideration is received in foreign currency or other legally permitted cross-border settlement currency, and the exporter maintains evidence of customs exit or offshore digital delivery, including contracts, platform settlement statements, and payment receipts. For physical goods, zero-rating is substantiated by customs export clearance; for digital services, zero-rating is substantiated by non-resident customer agreements, IP license records, Apple/Google/Stripe disbursement reports, and bank collection evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration certified by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC
  • Bill of Lading or Sea/Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract or Customer Purchase Order
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt or bank-issued inward payment/remittance verification from a designated PRC bank
  • Cross-border platform settlement statements from Apple App Store, Google Play, and Stripe evidencing receipt of offshore digital content revenues

The CN_FIP must issue VAT invoices through the STA electronic invoice service platform using fully digitalized electronic invoices. The platform enforces real-time API validation, digital tax-control stamping, anti-fraud encryption, and automatic data transmission to the Golden Tax Phase IV information system; invoices cannot be lawfully issued outside the authorized national platform, and invoice data must reconcile with VAT returns.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If required customs declarations, transport documents, invoices, cross-border payment evidence, or foreign exchange repatriation records are missing, inconsistent, or unverifiable, the zero-rating will be retrospectively reversed and VAT assessed at the applicable rate with statutory late-payment interest. Fraudulent export tax refund claims are subject to administrative penalties from 50% to 500% of the refunded tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China and may be prosecuted as criminal fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland China foreign-invested partnership is fully subject to China's foreign exchange control framework. Current account transactions, such as service trade fees, app-store royalties, and operating expenses, are generally convertible if genuine supporting contracts, invoices, and payment records are presented to a licensed domestic bank, which applies a real-demand authenticity check under SAFE rules. Capital account transactions are strictly controlled. The Hong Kong parent's equity contribution to the FIP must be registered with SAFE before remittance, then paid into a dedicated foreign-currency capital account at a domestic bank. Conversion of that capital into CNY is permitted through the willing-conversion policy, but the resulting CNY must be held in a capital-conversion pending-payment account and used only for the declared business purpose. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permitted only after annual financial statements, audit or CPA review, tax clearance, and SAFE registration; the bank must verify the partnership's pass-through tax treatment and the absence of unrepatriated distributable losses. The HKD leg is unrestricted because Hong Kong has no exchange controls and the HKD is linked to the USD under the HKMA framework. The core treasury challenge is reconciling Hong Kong-side Apple/Google app-revenue receipts with the onshore FIP capital regime: mobile platform proceeds should be classified as service-trade income and properly settled through the designated bank before onshore disbursement. In practice, foreign investors should not commingle cross-border shareholder loans, capital injections, or royalty income without SAFE-approved accounts and documented contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE regime, there is no mandatory surrender ratio or fixed statutory conversion deadline for export proceeds in goods trade. Export receipts must be repatriated into the licensed banking system and reported through the trade monitoring system. A-listed enterprises may retain eligible foreign currency in a current account or sell it voluntarily; B/C-listed enterprises must settle within the verification window prescribed by SAFE, generally aligned with the customs declaration and payment-cycle review. For this FIP, vertical micro-drama revenue is service trade and digital platform revenue, not goods export, so no export surrender obligation applies; Apple/Google payouts received offshore may be repatriated as current-account receipts upon presentation of contracts, platform settlement statements, and tax documentation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the HKD Linked Exchange Rate System but imposes no foreign exchange controls in Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open onshore capital accounts, are legally barred from clearing CNY via CNAPS, and cannot obtain SAFE FDI foreign-exchange registration for the FIP. An offshore EMI may be used only as a secondary collection pocket for Hong Kong-sourced App Store revenue, provided the funds are ultimately swept into a licensed mainland commercial bank treasury account. The statutory banking relationship must remain with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank for capital injections, current-account settlement, and profit remittance.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% on the corporate partner's allocated share of China-source income of the Foreign-Invested Partnership (China EIT Law Art. 4); the Hong Kong corporate partner may also be subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax at 16.5% on territorial profits, with 8.25% applying to the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends or China-source profit distributions remitted to a non-resident corporate partner; the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement can reduce the rate to 5% if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the payer.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual EIT return must be filed and settled on or before 31 May of the following year; quarterly prepayment declarations are due within 15 days after each quarter-end; non-resident WHT is generally due on the date of payment or remittance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory under SAT Announcement 2016 No.42 when any of the following thresholds is exceeded in a fiscal year: related-party tangible-property transfers above RMB 200 million; related-party intangible-property transfers above RMB 100 million; related-party financial-asset transfers above RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions above RMB 400 million; the Master File is additionally required if the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese tax administration applies the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 as the functional equivalent of economic-substance screening; the Hong Kong partner claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities with respect to the vertical micro-drama intellectual property, financing, and distribution are actually performed in Hong Kong through adequate premises, qualified staff, and genuine board decision-making; outsourced activities located in mainland China will not support a treaty claim and may trigger recharacterization, denial of treaty benefits, and withholding-tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Abuse in China-HK DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: SAT will deny DTA benefits under EIT Law Article 47 and the beneficial-ownership rule, reassess the income as China-source, add WHT at 10%, impose a daily late surcharge of 0.05%, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law; criminal referral follows if false declarations are intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who sign false treaty declarations or knowingly allow sham Hong Kong structures may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20; they can be jointly and severally liable for the restitution of the unpaid tax, subjected to exit controls and tax blacklisting, and prosecuted under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion with penalties including property confiscation and imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer-Pricing Profit Shifting of App-Store Revenues

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41 and SAT Announcement 2016 No.42, SAT may apply TNMM or profit split and adjust the taxable income to an arm's-length result; the adjusted tax is collectible with late surcharge, and where contemporaneous documentation is absent or false, an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be imposed, in addition to fines under Article 62 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law for failure to provide filing material.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who knowingly approve below-arm's-length transfer prices or destroy/withhold TP documentation can be held personally liable for the resulting CIT shortfall; they may face travel bans, freezing of personal assets, blacklisting in the tax credit penalty register, and criminal liability for aiding tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law with imprisonment from three to seven years where the amount is large.
Withholding Tax Evasion and AML Red-Flag Structuring

Statutory sanction: A withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the uncollected tax under Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, plus daily late surcharge of 0.05%; intentional concealment through alternative payment rails triggers the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, with fines up to RMB 5,000,000 for an institution, and criminal asset-confiscation measures for unlawful cross-border transfers.

Director personal liability: The directors, general partners, and financial controllers who orchestrate the non-withholding scheme will be jointly and severally liable for the unremitted tax and penalties; they can be placed on the exit-ban and tax-violation blacklist; the corporate veil will be pierced to reach personal bank accounts; conviction for tax evasion or money laundering carries maximum imprisonment of up to seven years, permanent travel restrictions, and confiscation of personal assets used or derived from the unlawful scheme.
