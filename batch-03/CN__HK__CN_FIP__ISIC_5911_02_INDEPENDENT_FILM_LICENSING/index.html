Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The Partnership Deed must, however, state the agreed capital contributions, and the partners must credit their contributions to the partnership account before any drawdown or distribution. For bank onboarding, the bank may require an accountant's capital confirmation or an escrow arrangement as a contractual matter, but no statutory escrow is mandated.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable. No civil defense fire permit, structural load inspection, or workplace safety approval is required for an intellectual property licensing business. General Fire Safety Ordinance Cap. 95 requirements apply only to physical office premises, under which the landlord must supply a valid fire services certificate and the office must maintain emergency exit and extinguisher compliance, but no separate industrial civil defense clearance is granted or required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with the electronic filing and account interface accessed through the GovHK e-Tax (eTAX) portal; for mainland PRC tax obligations arising from a fixed place of business, the State Taxation Administration (STA) and its Electronic Tax Bureau are the competent authorities.

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-registered foreign-invested partnership (FIP), the partnership must first register under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) or, if limited liability is intended, the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). Within one month of commencement of business, the partnership must file an application with the Business Registration Office of the IRD and obtain a Business Registration Certificate; the Business Registration Number (BRN) printed on the certificate is the partnership's statutory tax file reference. The IRD will then open a partnership tax file and issue the Profits Tax Return (Partnership); the return must be completed on a pass-through basis, and each partner's share of profits is assessable to profits tax in the partner's name. Foreign non-resident partners without a Hong Kong address must appoint a tax representative resident in Hong Kong and notify the IRD of the agent's particulars. If the partnership creates a mainland PRC permanent establishment, the non-resident entity must apply to the STA Electronic Tax Bureau for Unified Social Credit Code registration within 30 days of commencement of activity, and file any required VAT, corporate income tax, and withholding returns.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales tax in Hong Kong SAR; PRC Value-Added Tax (VAT) applies to supplies with a mainland China tax nexus

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST is imposed. For a mainland PRC VAT nexus, mandatory VAT general-taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; small-scale VAT taxpayer registration applies from the first taxable supply unless an exemption is available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong: exports of physical media and cross-border film-licensing royalties are outside the scope of any Hong Kong VAT/GST because no such tax is levied; no zero-rating claim is required. PRC mainland: under the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Notice [2016] No. 36 (VAT pilot notice), a PRC VAT taxpayer may apply zero-rating or tax exemption for cross-border sales of services and intangible assets, including qualifying copyright and film-licensing rights, only where the services or intangibles are supplied to an overseas customer, are wholly consumed outside the PRC, foreign exchange proceeds are received, and the approved export schedules and supporting documents are filed with the VAT return.

  • China International Trade Single Window Customs Export Declaration (for physical carrier media export)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical film prints or master carriers)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-designated bank cross-border income declaration voucher
  • Signed cross-border film licensing or intellectual property licensing agreement
  • Commercial invoice matched to the cross-border agreement
  • Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) issued from the STA e-invoice platform
  • Tax residence certificate or overseas business registration of the licensee
  • Self-declaration that the licensed rights are wholly consumed outside the PRC
  • Copyright registration or ownership record for the licensed film

Hong Kong imposes no government-mandated real-time invoice clearance and no anti-fraud digital stamping for ordinary electronic invoices; invoicing is governed by commercial practice and records-keeping obligations under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Where a mainland PRC VAT filing obligation exists, the STA's Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime is mandatory for registered taxpayers: invoices are issued through the national electronic invoice service platform, encrypted in OFD format, carry a QR code and digital signature, and are transmitted in real time to the STA tax control system.

PRC tax authorities may deny the zero-rating or exemption, claw back credited input VAT, and impose late-payment interest and penalties under the PRC Law on Tax Administration, generally ranging from 50 percent to five times the amounts involved; fraudulent export-tax behavior may also attract criminal liability. In Hong Kong, the IRD may raise additional assessments and, under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), impose additional tax of up to three times the amount of tax undercharged for incorrect returns, evasion, or wilful default, together with daily fines for non-filing.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account transactions under IMF Article VIII, which covers cross-border royalties, film licensing fees, trade-related service payments, and profit remittances, subject to strict documentary genuineness verification by the designated settlement bank and submission of the SAFE/BOP filing forms. Capital account items remain under progressive liberalisation: a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete FDI registration with the local SAFE office, open a designated FDI capital settlement account at a domestic Tier-1 bank, and receive foreign capital through the SWIFT system; the funds may be converted into RMB for genuine business purposes within the registered scope but cannot be deployed outside that scope or used for equity investments without further approval. Since the 2012 SAFE reform, there is no mandatory surrender or fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds, yet 100% of foreign currency business receipts must be repatriated onshore through regulated banking channels within the statutory collection period (generally 90 days, extendable to 180 days with SAFE filing) and may be retained in a current account FX position only up to the SAFE-approved cap. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent or foreign partners is treated as a current account outflow but effectively requires audited financial statements, a complete PRC tax clearance demonstrating 10% withholding on royalties or a reduced treaty rate under the HK-China Double Taxation Arrangement, and adequate foreign currency residing in the current account FX account. Practical hurdles include the bank's independent commercial review of each remittance, the requirement to reconcile the underlying copyright licence with actual invoicing, and the need to finalise SAFE and tax filings before dividends or liquidation proceeds can flow out; hence treasury planning must match operational cash generation with tax clearance timelines to avoid trapped cash and overdue SAFE registrations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the post-2012 SAFE regime, no statutory percentage of export foreign-currency receipts must be compulsorily converted into RMB; however, 100% of export proceeds and service-trade income, including film licensing royalties, must be repatriated onshore within the prescribed period of 90 days from the trade or service declaration date, extendable to 180 days for specified licensing and film service categories upon SAFE filing. Once credited, the FIP may retain the foreign exchange in its current account FX account up to the SAFE-approved cap, or voluntarily settle into RMB at the prevailing onshore market rate; balances exceeding the cap must be converted promptly. All receipts must be matched against the customs or SAFE trade verification system prior to release.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability and legally constrained: the People's Bank of China and the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) do not grant fintech electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences to offshore digital banks; therefore, providers such as Airwallex, Revolut, or an HK-licensed virtual bank cannot lawfully open an onshore operating/RMB current account for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership or act as the settlement bank for SAFE-supervised flows. Offshore EMIs may serve the Hong Kong treasury or the foreign parent entity, but any attempt to channel FIP royalty income or capital through such rails without passing through a Chinese domestic designated bank would breach SAFE's foreign-exchange receipt and verification rules and money-laundering reporting obligations. Consequently, the FIP's cross-border treasury must be anchored on a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; fintech EMIs are restricted to ancillary FX analytics, payment initiation functionality via authorised third-party payment institutions only where the underlying flow remains subject to a domestic bank's compliance oversight. Overall feasibility rating: Low for the onshore entity, Medium only for the offshore Hong Kong treasury shell.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign capital injection to the FIP must follow the SAFE FDI registration protocol at the local SAFE administration or through the settlement bank's online SAFE acceptance system. The Hong Kong parent or foreign partner remits the subscribed capital via SWIFT MT103 with the balance-of-payments purpose code 622011 (foreign direct investment capital injection), referencing the FIP's SAFE FDI registration code in the payment narrative. The domestic designated bank credits the funds to the FDI capital settlement account, performs the foreign-exchange receipt and verification against the SAFE registration, and permits RMB settlement only for approved business expenditures, including advanced royalties to copyright licensors, dubbing and mastering services, marketing costs, and working capital. Within six months of injection, the FIP must obtain a statutory capital verification report issued by a licensed PRC Certified Public Accountant, confirming that the capital contributions are fully paid in compliance with the partnership deed and SAFE record.Outbound disbursements for film-rights licensing, sublicensing fees, dubbing and mastering production services, and distribution advances should clear via SWIFT MT103 single-customer credit transfers carrying the SAFE service-trade BOP purpose code for intellectual-property royalties (122030), supported by the signed licence agreement, the SAFE service-trade payment and exchange application, and the non-resident beneficiary's tax clearance certificate evidencing 10% withholding on royalties or the reduced treaty rate after the HK-China Double Taxation Arrangement treaty-benefit application is approved. For physical media deliverables such as mastered hard drives, digital cinema packages, and promotional materials crossing customs, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit opened by the FIP's Chinese settlement bank is the preferred rail, with trust receipt release and SWIFT MT7xx messaging, while documentary collections with documents against payment or documents against acceptance provide a pragmatic fallback for established low-risk counterparties in Hong Kong and other contracted territories. All outbound transactions must reconcile with tax invoices raised onshore and must not be split below reporting thresholds to evade review.Onshore RMB/foreign-currency forward contracts with genuine trade background, executed via the settlement bank under SAFE registration and requiring a real underlying licence or trade exposureOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only through HK-licensed banks at the Hong Kong treasury centre; onshore NDFs are not legal for the mainland FIP entityCross-currency swaps are permissible strictly for hedging capital-injection repayment and dividend-remittance exposure, subject to SAFE registration, board-level hedging policy approval, and prohibition on speculative positions

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability and legally constrained: the People's Bank of China and the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) do not grant fintech electronic-money-institution (EMI) licences to offshore digital banks; therefore, providers such as Airwallex, Revolut, or an HK-licensed virtual bank cannot lawfully open an onshore operating/RMB current account for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership or act as the settlement bank for SAFE-supervised flows. Offshore EMIs may serve the Hong Kong treasury or the foreign parent entity, but any attempt to channel FIP royalty income or capital through such rails without passing through a Chinese domestic designated bank would breach SAFE's foreign-exchange receipt and verification rules and money-laundering reporting obligations. Consequently, the FIP's cross-border treasury must be anchored on a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank; fintech EMIs are restricted to ancillary FX analytics, payment initiation functionality via authorised third-party payment institutions only where the underlying flow remains subject to a domestic bank's compliance oversight. Overall feasibility rating: Low for the onshore entity, Medium only for the offshore Hong Kong treasury shell.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$750 million in annual consolidated group revenue (threshold for multinational enterprise groups triggering Local File and Master File documentation under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018, effective from tax year 2018-19).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the territorial source principle requires an FIP to demonstrate substantive economic activity to claim offshore treatment. For film licensing, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include contract negotiation, execution, risk management, and decision-making. The IRD may ignore transactions lacking substance under the anti-avoidance provisions of IRO s.61 and s.61A, potentially re-characterizing income as Hong Kong-source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(1) – willful tax evasion with intent to defraud: on conviction, a fine of HK$50,000 and treble the tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for a term of up to 3 years. Under IRO s.80(2), knowingly making a false return carries the same penalty.

Director personal liability: In a limited partnership (FIP), the General Partner (GP) has unlimited liability for the partnership’s tax. If the GP is a corporate entity, its directors may be personally liable as parties to the evasion; the court may pierce the corporate veil to order restitution of underpaid tax. Directors may face arrest and a travel restriction (Blacklisting), disqualification from holding directorships for up to 5 years, and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Failure to File / Non-Filing of BIR52

Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(1) – failing to furnish a return without reasonable excuse: a fine of HK$10,000 and an additional daily fine of HK$2,000 per day of continuing failure. The IRD may issue a best-of-judgment assessment, and an additional 10% surcharge is imposed on tax unpaid on the due date. Willful non-compliance amounts to evasion under s.82, with fines of HK$50,000 plus treble tax and up to 3 years’ imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate GP cannot hide behind the corporate shell. If the non-filing is attributable to their willful neglect, they are liable to a penalty personally. The IRD may seek a civil restraining order on their assets and property, impose a travel ban, and the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.275 permits disqualification of a director involved in fraudulent trading, with criminal liability for imprisonment up to 15 years under the same ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Shell Arrangement

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61 and s.61A empower the IRD to disregard the artificial scheme and reconstruct the transaction at an arm’s length price; the FIP may be assessed on the resulting understated profits. In addition, penalties under s.82A for incorrect returns have a maximum of 100% of the tax undercharged. Missing TP documentation can result in a fine of HK$100,000 per document.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate GP who knowingly approve the non-arm’s length rates are personally liable for the tax deficiency. The corporate veil can be pierced under s.275 of the Companies Ordinance if the structure is a façade. Directors may be subject to personal bankruptcy proceedings initiated by the IRD, a freezing order over assets, a blacklisting travel ban, and imprisonment of up to 2 years for knowingly signing false TP documentation.
