Hong Kong generally permits a registered office to be maintained by a licensed service provider or corporate services firm, and a virtual office address may be acceptable for basic Companies Ordinance filing purposes. However, for a Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in commercial 3D animation production with mainland revenue flows, a virtual mailbox is highly likely to be rejected by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department and by Hong Kong banks when determining 'place of effective management', permanent establishment exposure, and genuine economic substance. Tax authorities and banks will require a physical rented office or co-working suite with dedicated workstations, production infrastructure, staff, and evidence of management decisions occurring in Hong Kong. A virtual office that only forwards mail will undermine the partnership's pass-through tax status and may trigger mandatory registration of a mainland establishment.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the Electronic Tax Bureau portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

Registration for a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is performed through the integrated administrative platform of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the STA. The FIP must first obtain a business license from SAMR, which embeds the registration information and automatically assigns a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), serving as the functional Tax Identification Number (TIN). Foreign partners are not required to obtain a separate TIN if the entity is the taxpayer. The FIP must complete supplemental tax registration information via the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, providing bank account details, accounting system information, expected annual turnover, and partner allocation details. A VAT registration must be completed with the in-charge tax authority if the FIP supplies taxable goods or services in China. The tax authority will issue a separate VAT taxpayer identification number and register the FIP under the General VAT Taxpayer or Small-Scale Taxpayer regime as applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

Under Chinese income tax rules, an FIP is not subject to enterprise income tax on its income; profits are passed through to partners. Domestic legislation does not afford an elective pass-through classification akin to the U.S. check-the-box regime. However, the FIP may elect between General VAT Taxpayer status and Small-Scale Taxpayer status for indirect tax purposes, subject to turnover thresholds, and may elect the deemed simplification methods allocated to foreign-invested partnerships in special economic zones or pilot free-trade zones where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5 million (500万人民币) in annual taxable sales for services; however, small-scale taxpayers with annual turnover below RMB 5 million may apply simplified levy methods. Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered once annual turnover exceeds the statutory threshold, unless the entity qualifies for small-scale treatment; voluntary registration is allowed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim 0% VAT on exported services, including commercial 3D animation services provided to non-resident clients, if the services are rendered to an overseas recipient, the beneficial economic consumption occurs entirely outside China, and the consideration is received from overseas in foreign currency. The FIP must qualify as a general VAT taxpayer to apply zero-rating. If the service is provided effectively and wholly in mainland China to an overseas customer, the transaction may still qualify under cross-border service zero-rating provisions, provided the FIP retains prescribed documentation evidencing the offshore benefit and oversees payment. The service must not be a 'domestic consumption service' listed as non-zero-rated by Ministry of Finance and STA announcements.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Bill of Export) for goods or the service-contract-based export declaration for cross-border services
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border movement where goods are involved
  • Official foreign exchange inwards remittance receipt or bank statement certified by the Bank, evidencing receipt of payment from overseas
  • Signed contract or agreement demonstrating the overseas service recipient and the Chinese entity's obligation
  • Export service invoice and the FIP's VAT zero-rating export filing form
  • Evidence of service delivery and offshore consumption, such as delivery milestones, e-mail chain, digital handover certificates, or client acceptance records

All VAT taxpayers must use the nationwide electronic invoicing system integrated with the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV (Jinshui Phase IV) platform. Issuance of invoices requires real-time digital clearance: invoice data must be transmitted via STA-approved APIs (or through the Electronic Tax Bureau interface) to obtain a unique invoice code and anti-counterfeiting stamp before issuance. For fully digital electronic invoices (shuzi fapiao), the e-invoice must be issued through the STA platform or through certified third-party channels. No invoice is valid for input VAT deduction unless digitally cleared and included in the STA's real-time invoice database. Non-compliant issuance or manual invoice forging triggers automated anti-fraud alerts through the statewide VAT asset risk control system.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to ex-post audit by STA and customs authorities. If documentation is incomplete, inconsistent, or if foreign exchange is not received within the 180-day statutory repatriation deadline (unless extension is granted due to force majeure or state-approved circumstances), the zero-rated treatment is retrospectively revoked. The FIP must pay standard-rate VAT with daily late surcharge of 0.05% from the due date. Fraudulently obtaining zero-rating or fabricating export documents constitutes tax fraud punishable by fines from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, confiscation of illegal gains, and criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for export tax rebate fraud, which can lead to imprisonment. Additionally, the entities credit-rating and customs credit classification may be downgraded, blocking future export declarations and refund facilitation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR maintains full current and capital account convertibility for HKD, USD, EUR and CNH under the Linked Exchange Rate system; the HKMA does not impose exchange controls, compulsory conversion, or profit repatriation restrictions. The onshore China FIP, however, sits inside the PRC's controlled FX system supervised by PBOC/SAFE. The FIP's current account receipts from overseas animation clients are convertible and creditable without central approval, but must be settled through a SAFE-designated bank on a real receipts basis, with supporting contracts and invoices. Capital account transactions such as foreign partner capital contributions, shareholder loans, equity buybacks, and liquidation distributions are not freely convertible and require SAFE registration for FDI and a dedicated capital or escrow account. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong partner are treated as current remittances after annual audit and tax clearance under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax / partnership withholding rules; the declared amount can be remitted in HKD or USD after the bank verifies the tax payment certificate. Hong Kong's lack of controls converts the HK treasury entity into a financially open liquidity bridge between global clients and the PRC FIP, but all money crossing the PRC border must respect the PRC capital-versus-current account boundary and comply with PBOC's anti-money laundering and cross-border RMB reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC current-account foreign exchange rules, the Chinese FIP exporting animation and VFX services must repatriate foreign currency income to a SAFE-registered current account at a domestic-designated bank; there is no mandatory fixed-percentage surrender to the central bank in force today, but the full proceeds must be brought onshore and may be retained in a foreign currency account or voluntarily settled to CNY. The statutory repatriation limit under the Verification of Export Proceeds and Foreign Exchange Administrative Measures is generally 90 days from the date of export or service fulfilment unless an extension is approved by SAFE. In Hong Kong SAR, there is no statutory surrender rule; the HK treasury entity may freely retain, withdraw, lend, or remit HKD, USD, or CNH proceeds without exchange control approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore China Foreign-Invested Partnership, together with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for the Hong Kong SAR treasury corridor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise B2B is viable only as a secondary collection and disbursement rail for low-value service fees from global CGI and 3D animation clients and contractor payments, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 commercial bank for the PRC FIP or HK treasury entity. In Hong Kong, Wise is overseen under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance as a Money Service Operator and/or stored value facility licensee; it is not a licensed bank, does not take deposits, and cannot offer SAFE-registered capital accounts, trade finance, FX forwards, or documentary credits. In mainland China, fintech EMI-style services such as Wise are not licensed to operate domestically for the FIP's onshore RMB settlement; the FIP must use PRC domestic banks for SAFE reporting and RMB clearing. Therefore the mandated route is a Tier-1 bank core account with Wise B2B as an auxiliary collection rail for small SME receivables, not as a sovereign or corporate treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign exchange investment from the HK partner into the PRC FIP must flow through a dedicated capital account opened after completing the PRC FIP establishment filing with the local Administration for Market Regulation and obtaining the foreign exchange business registration from SAFE via the designated bank. The HK entity should remit via SWIFT MT103 using the purpose of Foreign-Invested Partnership capital contribution, and the receiving PRC bank will credit the capital escrow account only after SAFE registration has been matched; a registered Chinese CPA must issue a capital contribution verification report after each injection. For RMB injections, use CIPS with an RMB capital contribution purpose; for USD or HKD injections, the receiving bank may settle to CNY at the applicable foreign exchange reference rate only after SAFE registration and after the FIP submits the required declarative form. The 3D animation and VFX activity is not on the negative list for foreign investment, so ordinary FDI filing and SAFE registration is sufficient.For production costs, royalties, and independent contractor payments abroad, the FIP should make outbound remittances through its PRC current account via SWIFT MT103 supported by service contracts, invoices, and tax invoices; each remittance must be filed under SAFE current account foreign exchange business with the correct international trade purpose code such as Royalties and licence fees for animation IP or Business travel for production expenses. Documentary collections are practical only for physical goods and not for continuous 3D animation services; commercial LCs should be reserved for equipment or hardware imports such as render servers, motion capture devices, and high-end workstations, and can be opened by the HK treasury entity against the onshore FIP's purchase orders. The HK entity will execute onward payments from its HSBC or BOCHK accounts to global vendors using SWIFT MT103 and SWIFT gpi for real-time tracking; for high-value service settlements, MT103 plus a commercial invoice and statement of work is the safest clearing rail.For the PRC FIP: deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts and CNY/HKD cross-currency swaps with domestic banks for genuine commercial hedging of contracted animation revenue, supported by an underlying commercial exposure and not speculative; standard forwards should stay under a 1-year tenor and swaps over 12 months require SAFE approval if the notional is above the prescribed threshold.For the HK treasury entity: HKD/USD OTC forward contracts and CNH/HKD deliverable cross-currency swaps are permissible through HKMA-licensed banks because CNH is deliverable; NDFs referencing onshore CNY should not be booked by the PRC FIP because onshore entities are prohibited from participating in offshore CNY NDF markets, but the HK entity may use NDFs only where there is a proven underlying restricted-currency exposure.Currency options and exotic structured products are not permissible without a clear documented risk-management justification; passive hedging limited to 90-120% of forecast FX exposure is acceptable, and all hedging instruments must pass the bank's suitability, conduct, and anti-speculation policies.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Wise B2B is viable only as a secondary collection and disbursement rail for low-value service fees from global CGI and 3D animation clients and contractor payments, but it cannot replace a Tier-1 commercial bank for the PRC FIP or HK treasury entity. In Hong Kong, Wise is overseen under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance as a Money Service Operator and/or stored value facility licensee; it is not a licensed bank, does not take deposits, and cannot offer SAFE-registered capital accounts, trade finance, FX forwards, or documentary credits. In mainland China, fintech EMI-style services such as Wise are not licensed to operate domestically for the FIP's onshore RMB settlement; the FIP must use PRC domestic banks for SAFE reporting and RMB clearing. Therefore the mandated route is a Tier-1 bank core account with Wise B2B as an auxiliary collection rail for small SME receivables, not as a sovereign or corporate treasury counterparty.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (PRC Enterprise Income Tax standard rate; the Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership is fiscally transparent at entity level, so the 25% rate applies to resident corporate partners on allocated profits, while non-resident HK corporate partners face 10% PRC withholding on China-source passive income unless PRC-HK DTA relief applies)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC dividend withholding on payments by Chinese resident enterprises to non-resident enterprise shareholders; under PRC-HK DTA, reduced to 5% for a beneficial-owner HK company holding directly at least 25% of the capital. Because the FIP is tax-transparent, outbound profit allocations to a HK partner are analyzed as the partner's share of China-source income: if the partner has no PE, the passive component may be subject to 10%; if a PE exists, net profits are taxable at 25% and remittances of after-tax profit are not subject to additional WHT.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million for 'other related-party transactions' (including animation/VFX service fees) triggering Local File; RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible-property and financial-asset transfers; RMB 1 billion aggregate related-party transactions triggering Master File; RMB 5.5 billion consolidated group revenue triggering CbCR under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but applies a statutory substance-over-form / GAAR regime under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice [2009] No. 7. For the HK corporate partner to access DTA benefits, it must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: substantive employees, physical premises, management functions and risk control. A shell or letterbox HK entity is not a beneficial owner, will be denied treaty relief and may be recharacterized as a conduit, causing PRC-source income to be adjusted and taxed at 25% or 10% with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Fictitious Intercompany Service Fees

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 (related-party adjustment), Article 48 (special tax investigation), Tax Collection Law Article 63 (50%-500% of unpaid tax plus late surcharge), Criminal Law Article 201 (fine plus imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent tax evasion).

Director personal liability: The general partner's directors and managing partners are jointly and severally liable for restitution of unpaid tax and penalties. The FIP's limited partner who participates in daily management loses limited liability under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law Article 76. The responsible officers may be blacklisted under the Taxpayer Dishonesty Joint Punishment mechanism, subject to travel bans, high-consumption restrictions, and criminal imprisonment.
Treaty Abuse Without Beneficial Ownership or Economic Substance in Hong Kong

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits with recapture of tax at the 10% statutory rate, interest, and penalties under SAT Public Notice [2019] No. 35 Article 16 and Tax Collection Law Article 63; withholding-agent penalty of 50%-300% of un-withheld tax under Article 69.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use a sham HK entity may be personally liable for the taxes and penalties through veil-piercing; they face asset freezes, exit restrictions, one-to-three year fixed-term imprisonment, or imprisonment up to seven years in aggravated tax evasion cases under Criminal Law Article 201.
AML / Shell-Company Abuse in Cross-Border Payment Routing and Foreign Exchange Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 62 (failure to file: RMB 2,000-RMB 50,000 fines), SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42 (TP documentation penalties), SAFE foreign exchange penalties up to 30% of the illegal payment amount, and Criminal Law Article 191 for money laundering (up to 10 years imprisonment plus fines) if the proceeds are criminal.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: the GP and directors must restore diverted assets and are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution; the tax authority may impose travel bans and blacklisting restrictions; criminal liability extends to responsible officers and beneficial owners involved in directing the sham payment structure, with terms of imprisonment and fines.
