E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the foreign non-resident partnership is carrying on a business in Hong Kong under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) and the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); if such nexus exists, business registration is compulsory within one month of commencement regardless of turnover. Step 2: If the Foreign-Invested Partnership is structured as a limited partnership, register the partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) with the Hong Kong Companies Registry before applying for business registration; general partnerships do not require Companies Registry formation. Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, a partnership is automatically treated as transparent: the partnership itself is not the taxpayer and partners are assessed on their respective shares of partnership profits. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales and use tax regime in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is limited to excise duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and first registration tax on motor vehicles under the Motor Vehicles (First Registration Tax) Ordinance (Cap. 330).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST turnover threshold exists in Hong Kong. Business registration is mandatory for any person, including a foreign non-resident partnership, carrying on a business in Hong Kong within one month of commencement, irrespective of turnover; the effective threshold is zero.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

No VAT/GST export zero-rating regime is available in Hong Kong because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. The analogous protection is the territorial-source principle under the Inland Revenue Ordinance: export sales of goods shipped from Hong Kong to overseas customers are generally offshore income, and fees for automated subtitle transcription and AI voice-dubbing services are not chargeable to profits tax where the services are performed, managed, and concluded wholly outside Hong Kong. The claim is fact-based and not an election; the taxpayer must document the non-resident status of the customer, the place of contract negotiation and acceptance, the place of service performance and server/studio location, the movement of goods if exported, and banking evidence of foreign currency proceeds.

  • Electronic Export Declaration lodged with the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) through the Trade Single Window or an authorised EDI service provider
  • Commercial invoice describing the services or goods, buyer details, quantities, prices, and payment terms
  • Packing list for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Hong Kong to the foreign destination
  • Signed proof of delivery, overseas customer acceptance certificate, or delivery/performance evidence for AI subtitle and dubbing services
  • Service agreement and correspondence demonstrating that the digital services are supplied to a non-resident customer and performed outside Hong Kong
  • Bank credit advice or SWIFT payment confirmation (e.g., MT103) evidencing foreign exchange receipt from the overseas customer; an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is not a Hong Kong statutory document

Hong Kong does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, government pre-approval, or anti-fraud digital stamping. The IRD accepts paper and electronic invoices if they satisfy the record-keeping standard under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For cross-border goods movements, electronic export and import declarations must be filed with the Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) through the Trade Single Window or an authorised EDI service provider, generally within 14 calendar days; dutiable commodities require permits under Cap. 109.

Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, there is no VAT clawback mechanism. For profits tax, an unsubstantiated offshore-source claim may be recharacterised as Hong Kong-sourced income, resulting in additional profits tax assessments plus interest. Penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance for wilful evasion or false statements include up to treble the tax undercharged and additional fixed fines, with criminal fines and imprisonment possible in serious cases. Assessment periods may be extended to 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. False export declarations under Cap. 60E may attract fines and imprisonment. Records must be retained for at least 7 years. Hong Kong imposes no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is an internal documentary-control convention for this dossier.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a common-law special administrative region with no foreign exchange control regime and maintains full convertibility for both current and capital account transactions involving the Hong Kong dollar, US dollar, and other major currencies. There is no requirement for surrender of foreign currency receipts, and profit remittances from a Hong Kong entity to its foreign shareholders or partners are freely permitted without prior approval from the HKMA. However, the underlying structure being a China-connected Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) triggers the sovereign People’s Republic of China (PRC) foreign exchange regulatory framework, which divides the balance of payments into current account and capital account categories. Current account items such as service trade, royalty, and licensing fees related to the AI subtitle business can be settled via designated foreign exchange banks in China after evaluating trade authenticity under SAFE’s “Principle of Full Truthfulness and Comprehensiveness”. Capital account items, notably the injected partnership capital from Mainland Chinese investors into Hong Kong, fall under the ODI (Overseas Direct Investment) or QDLP/QDIE quota mechanisms, requiring SAFE registration, NDRC (National Development and Reform Commission) filing, and approval from the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM) when applicable. Once the funds are legally offshore, Hong Kong imposes no restriction on conversion or remittance; yet any subsequent distribution or repayment back to China is treated as inbound foreign investment at the Chinese receiving entity, subject to FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE and the domestic bank’s capital account monitoring. The practical implicaion is that liquidity within the Hong Kong partnership is fully unimpeded, but the cross-border bridge between CN and HK is the choke point governed by Chinese capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule applies within Hong Kong SAR. A Hong Kong partnership may freely retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency receipts (e.g., USD from subtitle licensing) without any mandatory conversion ratio or timeline. For the Chinese partners or related mainland entities, the PRC State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) requires that export proceeds in foreign currency (when exports are booked on the Chinese books) be repatriated to China within 180 days of the transaction. Under the current settlement system, Chinese exporters may either keep the foreign currency in a current account foreign exchange deposit or convert it to CNY at a designated bank; there is no forced conversion percentage, but all receipts must be truthfully declared under the Trade Monitoring System. Reinvoicing through Hong Kong does not alter the underlying obligation if the Chinese entity is the actual exporter of goods or services; therefore, the FIP’s cross-border treasury policy must include a reconciliation mechanism to prove economic substance and avoid deemed association penalties.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) / People’s Bank of China (PBOC) for sovereign China cross-border capital flow control

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and payment fintechs—including Airwallex, Neat, Statrys, and licensed money service operators—are operationally feasible for a Hong Kong-incorporated partnership because the HKMA does not prohibit non-bank financial technology companies from servicing legitimate corporate customers. However, they operate under a lighter regulatory perimeter (e.g., the Money Service Operators Ordinance or the Stored Value Facilities License) and cannot offer all traditional treasury products such as trade finance, check clearing, or documentary collections. Stripe and Wise (via Wise Asia Pacific Pte. Ltd. or its licensed HK entity) are available as payment facilitation rails for incoming global subscriptions and low-value disbursements; yet they are not substitute for a corporate bank account because they lack overdraft, capital escrow, and direct SWIFT correspondent relationships. The legal constraint is that the FIP must maintain a ’parent’ corporate bank account with a Tier-1 Hong Kong bank to satisfy anti-money-laundering accountability; EMIs can only serve as auxiliary receivable/payable gateways. Engagement with EMIs also requires separate due diligence under the Hong Kong Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) Cap.615, including assessment of enhanced due diligence measures for any Chinese-director involvement.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard Hong Kong profits tax rate for unincorporated businesses (partnerships), with two-tiered rates: 7.5% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits and 15% on the excess; corporate partners are separately assessable at 16.5% / 8.25% two-tiered rates on their share of partnership profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no withholding tax on dividends paid by Hong Kong companies, and a Hong Kong partnership is not a corporation; partnership profit distributions are not dividends and are not subject to any Hong Kong withholding tax.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership is required to prepare and maintain a Local File when the aggregate amount of its controlled transactions with associated persons exceeds HK$44,000,000 for a basis period; a Master File must be prepared when the global MNE group has consolidated turnover of at least HK$6,800,000,000 (approximately EUR 750 million) and the partnership has controlled transactions above the de minimis level. Even below these thresholds, IRD requires every taxpayer to keep sufficient transfer-pricing documentation to demonstrate arm’s length pricing; penalties can be imposed for failure to keep adequate records.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD applies a substantive territorial-source doctrine, the place-of-effective-management doctrine, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under IRO ss61 and 61A to reject arrangements lacking genuine Hong Kong economic substance. For this AI subtitle and voice-cloning partnership, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be performed in Hong Kong: development and maintenance of AI dubbing algorithms, negotiation and signing of client contracts, server hosting keyed to the Hong Kong operation, final audio/subtitle quality control, and strategic management of the partnership. The mere existence of a Hong Kong registration and a mail-forwarding address will not pass the economic-substance doctrine; the partnership must maintain a real office, locally resident employees, bank accounts, and minutes of partner/board decisions. Inbound treaty and outbound royalty benefits will be denied if the IRD or Chinese tax authorities characterize the FIP as a shell or conduit arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Offshore Source Claim

Statutory sanction: Contravenes the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), particularly s82 (willful evasion) and s82A (incorrect returns without reasonable excuse). Criminal liability: on conviction, a fine of up to HK$100,000 plus treble the amount of tax evaded and imprisonment for up to 3 years. Civil penalties: the IRD may impose additional tax of up to 100% or treble the undercharged tax, plus cancellation of loss carryforward and offshore claims. Repeated or sophisticated evasion can lead to seizure of funds from Stripe and Wise accounts via garnishee orders.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the partnership is used as a façade to disguise the true earning activity; general partners, managing directors, and de facto controllers can be made jointly and severally liable for the entire unpaid tax, penalties, and costs. Personal civil exposure includes asset attachment, bankruptcy, judicial travel restriction, court-ordered departure prevention orders, and company director disqualification for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance. Criminal exposure includes imprisonment of the individual signatory of the false BIR52 return.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Violates the transfer pricing provisions contained in Chapter 17B of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (ss50AA-50AAS). The IRD may adjust taxable profits to arm’s length outcomes and issue penalty assessments under s82A: an additional tax of up to 100% or treble the tax undercharged; where documentation is deliberately falsified, criminal prosecution under s80 and s82 applies with fines and imprisonment. The IRD also has power to impose a fixed penalty of HK$10,000 for non-compliance with a notice to furnish documentation, plus daily penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who sign declarations that the transfer pricing is arm’s length when they know it is not expose their personal assets to restitution orders; they may be assessed as the responsible persons for the partnership and held personally liable for the tax, surcharges, and legal costs. Courts can pierce the limited liability shield if the partner used the partnership to evade tax or misrepresent the commercial terms; personal consequences include civil asset seizure, loss of future directorship status, travel bans pending assessment payment, and, in deliberate cases, imprisonment for up to 3 years.
Shell Company and AML/Counter-Financing of Terrorism Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: May be prosecuted under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for false statements in returns (liable to imprisonment up to 2 years and a fine of HK$300,000); under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) for failing to maintain beneficial ownership and record-keeping requirements, carrying fines up to HK$2 million and imprisonment up to 7 years where the entity is a suspected conduit; and under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) for fraud, with penalties up to imprisonment for 14 years. Additionally, IRD can invoke domestic CFC-like anti-avoidance and assess the Mainland controlling partner as if the partnership income arose directly to that partner.

Director personal liability: Courts will lift the separate personality of the corporate general partner or any shell partner to impose unlimited personal liability on directors who controlled the fraudulent operation. Personal civil remedies include disgorgement, civil asset forfeiture, and compensation to the revenue; personal criminal remedies include imprisonment up to 14 years, extradition risk for cross-border AML fraud, disqualification as a director, freezing of personal bank accounts, blacklisting by Hong Kong’s financial intelligence unit, and refusal of renewal of the business registration certificate. The IRD may also issue a departure prevention order under the tax recovery regime to prevent the directors from leaving Hong Kong until the outstanding tax and penalties are secured.
