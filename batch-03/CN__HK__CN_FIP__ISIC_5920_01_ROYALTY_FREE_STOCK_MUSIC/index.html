Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR Government; online electronic portal: IRD e-Tax via GovHK (https://www.gov.hk/en/apps/irdetax.htm) and the IRD website (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/).

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) carrying on business in Hong Kong, the partnership is not a separate taxpayer but must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The IRD issues the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory tax identifier used for all tax administration. If the FIP is formed as a limited partnership, it must first be registered under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or, if structured as a fund vehicle, under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637) through the Companies Registry electronic filing services; the IRD BRN is then obtained. Foreign non-resident partners are not separately required to apply for a Hong Kong TIN or VAT ID solely on account of foreign status; each partner is assessable in its own name on its share of the partnership's Hong Kong-source profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A non-resident partner deriving no Hong Kong-source profits has no IRD filing obligation and no Hong Kong TIN unless the IRD opens an assessment file. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, and therefore no VAT ID exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or any comparable entity classification election. A partnership is mandatorily pass-through under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112): assessable profits are computed at partnership level as if the partnership were a separate person, but tax is charged on each partner in proportion to its partnership share. No election to treat the FIP as a corporation for Hong Kong profits tax purposes is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales Tax — Hong Kong has no general consumption tax, value added tax, goods and services tax, or sales/use tax; the only indirect excise duties are levied on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No turnover threshold in HKD or any other currency triggers VAT/GST registration because no such tax exists. A partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must still register for a Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 within one month of commencement; that registration is not an indirect tax registration and has no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to VAT/GST because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST; consequently there is no 0% export VAT rate, no export VAT zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism. For profits tax purposes, a royalty-free stock music FIP may claim offshore profits treatment under the territorial source principle of section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance if the licensing contracts are negotiated and executed outside Hong Kong, the music is hosted on servers outside Hong Kong, customers are sourced outside Hong Kong, and subscription payments are collected outside Hong Kong. This is an income-tax exemption possibility, not an indirect-tax zero-rating, and it must be supported by facts. No Hong Kong tax statute imposes a mandatory foreign exchange repatriation deadline or FIRC-based deadline for such digital export income.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates without foreign exchange controls; the HKD is freely convertible through authorized institutions and capital flows into and out of HK are unrestricted. However, the PRC sovereign regime imposes a strict distinction between current and capital account transactions for the Mainland-resident FIP. Current account flows, such as cross-border royalties, audio license subscription fees, service-related outsourced processing, and merchant settlement derived from the royalty-free stock music platform, are conditionally convertible at a SAFE-designated bank after an authenticity-and-compliance review; copies of agreements, invoices, platform payout reports, and tax vouchers are required. Capital account flows, including the HK investor partner’s capital contribution, shareholder loans, assignment of partnership interests, outward guarantees, and the final return of the FIP’s invested capital, are not freely convertible and require SAFE registration/filing, opening of capital-settlement and FDI RMB settlement accounts, and post-transaction reporting. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is treated as investment income under the current account framework: it requires the FIP’s audited financial statements, partnership profit-distribution resolution, the FIP’s tax payment certificate/withholding certificate and, where applicable, the China-HK double-tax arrangement residence certificate. Hong Kong’s lack of exchange controls enables the external HK holding vehicle to hold all license receipts in CNH/USD/HKD and to hedge offshore, but conversion into the onshore FIP is still channelled through SAFE-registered current/capital account rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE’s post-2012 trade administration framework, there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio for export/service-trade foreign exchange receipts; a PRC FIP may retain the foreign currency in a current-account FX account or convert it to CNY at a designated bank at the prevailing onshore spot rate. For goods export, SAFE historically requires the exporter to collect and verify the export payment within the matching declaration period, generally 90 days from customs release for high-risk transactions and up to 360 days for permitted open-account terms, with overdue items requiring a written explanation and possible downgrade of trade category. For the royalty-free stock-music digital service trade, the statutory control is not a percentage conversion but SAFE’s transaction-authenticity-by-voucher rule: the FIP must submit the license contracts, Stripe/PayPal settlement statements, and a service-trade foreign-exchange application within each bank’s current processing cycle, and the bank will report the proceeds into the SAFE service-trade monitoring system. In practice, export earnings must be repatriated and either settled or lodged in the FIP’s FX current account before the balance-sheet date; no purely offshore retention is allowed for a Mainland-resident FIP unless the income is earned and lawfully retained by the Hong Kong partner vehicle.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its statutory State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the controlling central-bank authorities for the onshore Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), while the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central bank for Hong Kong dollar clearing, CNH liquidity, and free-market fungibility in the Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for treasury capital and statutory corporate-banking obligations because offshore digital EMI licensees are not authorised by SAFE or PBOC to open capital-account, RMB basic settlement, or foreign-exchange current accounts for a China-resident FIP. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable only as online payment acquirers for low-value merchant collection, not as KYC-substitute banks. For Hong Kong-side settling of the same Stripe/PayPal flows, an HK-licensed payment institution may be used as a collection aggregator if the ultimate remittance is swept in MT103 into the onshore FIP’s domestic Tier-1 bank account. If the FIP has no HK branch, it should not rely solely on a fintech EMI for cross-border disbursement because PBOC rules treat any foreign payment institution without a PRC payment license as providing illegal payment clearing services for Mainland accounts. A viable hybrid is to use PBOC-licensed cross-border payment service providers to convert platform inflows into CNY after each transaction, while the FIP retains its legal-entity bank relationship in the Tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Low for treasury capital and statutory corporate-banking obligations because offshore digital EMI licensees are not authorised by SAFE or PBOC to open capital-account, RMB basic settlement, or foreign-exchange current accounts for a China-resident FIP. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable only as online payment acquirers for low-value merchant collection, not as KYC-substitute banks. For Hong Kong-side settling of the same Stripe/PayPal flows, an HK-licensed payment institution may be used as a collection aggregator if the ultimate remittance is swept in MT103 into the onshore FIP’s domestic Tier-1 bank account. If the FIP has no HK branch, it should not rely solely on a fintech EMI for cross-border disbursement because PBOC rules treat any foreign payment institution without a PRC payment license as providing illegal payment clearing services for Mainland accounts. A viable hybrid is to use PBOC-licensed cross-border payment service providers to convert platform inflows into CNY after each transaction, while the FIP retains its legal-entity bank relationship in the Tier-1 bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10,000,000 aggregated non-loan controlled transactions or HK$5,000,000 aggregated loans/interest where the entity's annual turnover exceeds HK$100,000,000 (per DIPN 59/60). Smaller entities are generally exempt from mandatory documentation but must retain basic evidence.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies a source-based territorial tax system. Under IRO s.14, profits are taxable only if arising in or derived from Hong Kong. For copyright royalty income, Section 21A deems a taxable source where the IP is used in Hong Kong. To support offshore claims, the FIP must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as contract negotiation, execution, and licensing decisions took place outside Hong Kong. Economic presence in Hong Kong with substantive staff, office, and management usually negates offshore claims and subjects profits to Hong Kong tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Claim Abuse

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82 - Penalty of three times the tax undercharged plus HKD 50,000 fine; criminal prosecution for tax evasion carrying imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who were knowingly complicit may be personally liable for the underpaid tax and penalties; the court may issue a travel ban (removal-restriction order) to prevent departure from Hong Kong where outstanding tax exceeds a threshold; the offending director faces disqualification as director for up to 5 years and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAS allows the Commissioner to adjust income, with penal interest and additional tax of up to 100% of the undercharged tax under s.82A for careless/negligent errors.

Director personal liability: Directors may be assessed personally as the persons responsible for enabling the wrong deduction or income shifting; the institution of civil recovery proceedings to recover diverted partnership assets; a blacklisting or travel-restraint order pending determination; criminal liability if the arrangement constitutes fraud, with imprisonment up to 7 years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership (Shell Entity Misuse)

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) - s.5: business without correcting registration; penalties up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years. The HKIRD may also compute profits on a deemed basis and impose additional tax.

Director personal liability: Courts may lift the corporate veil if the partnership is used as a façade; directors may be liable for civil restitution of assets, held jointly and severally for fines; personal travel ban imposed to secure debt (Blacklist of travel), and criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years for filing false statutory declarations.
