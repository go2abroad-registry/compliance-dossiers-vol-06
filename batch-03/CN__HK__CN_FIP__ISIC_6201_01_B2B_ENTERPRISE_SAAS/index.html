Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR, including the IRD Business Registration Office; online portal: IRD eTAX and Business Registration e-Service.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong partnership must file the partnership business registration application with the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business. The application must identify the person-in-charge and all partners, provide certified copies of foreign incorporation documents or passports, appoint a Hong Kong resident authorised representative or local agent, and supply a local business address for service. The IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the statutory taxpayer reference number for the partnership. For non-resident partners, the IRD may issue individual Tax Reference Numbers when the partnership return is opened; the partnership must also file the prescribed partnership return (BIR52) and the person-in-charge form. No separate corporate TIN application is required because the partnership is transparent and not treated as a separate legal taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed float with capital account controls regime. RMB is convertible on current account items (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties) after the presentation of authentic supporting documents and tax certificates. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, partner capital contributions, and equity injections, are subject to detailed registration, approval, and verification by SAFE or authorized banks under a delegated filing system. Under the July 2015 reform, the old pre-approval for FIP FX registration was largely replaced by bank-direct registration through the Capital Item Information System, but the actual receipt of foreign funds still requires a designated capital account and a bank-level authenticity check. An FIP engaged in B2B enterprise SaaS must strictly segregate current-account service revenue from capital-account contributions. Surrender requirements no longer mandate a fixed percentage; however, all offshore foreign-exchange proceeds must be repatriated, booked, and either retained as foreign currency balances or settled into RMB at the enterprise's will via a bank-verified voluntary settlement mechanism. Profit remittance abroad is permissible for dividends or partnership distributions only after the annual audit, tax clearance, and the completion of an outbound payment tax filing collected by the remitting bank. Liquidity cannot be freely moved out for non-operational purposes; any overseas payment must be grounded in genuine trade or service contracts. The conversion of partner capital into RMB must adhere to the negative list national treatment framework; proceeds cannot be used for activities outside the registered business scope, securities or loans prohibited by the FIP partnership agreement or China's investment regulations. The persistence of administrative quirks, such as a two-tier interbank rate (onshore CNY vs offshore CNH) and FX quotas in the interbank market, further underscores that direct RMB hedging and cross-border cash pooling have strict volume ceilings and counterparty eligibility requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current Goods/Service Trade FX Administration rules (State Administration of Foreign Exchange Circular No. 30 of 2012, as amended), cross-border export receipts in foreign currency must be credited to a foreign-exchange settlement account or the 'export proceeds monitoring account' within 30 calendar days from the submission of the customs declaration or, for intangible services such as SaaS, from the date of invoice or the contractually defined payment due; despite this baseline, banks may accommodate agreed credit terms subject to documentary verification. There is presently no fixed mandatory conversion ratio: the 'surrendered' quota was abolished after 2012, and enterprises can voluntarily decide whether to convert their foreign exchange into RMB or retain it as a foreign-currency deposit, provided that the entire amount first enters the domestic banking system. Any offshore retention beyond the period requires the prior approval of SAFE. For FIP investments under the FDI quota, a separate capital account registration and a smaller ratio of currency mixing may apply; however, operational receipts are fully free from explicit conversion mandates, subject only to authenticity and anti-money-laundering reviews by the designated Tier-1 bank.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated regulatory execution by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) do not hold a Chinese mainland banking license and are legally barred from opening resident accounts, handling RMB payments, or providing on-balance-sheet local-currency clearing for a China-registered FIP. Their role is strictly limited to (i) offering a non-resident foreign-currency current account to a Hong Kong affiliate on a 'traveller's cheque' basis; and (ii) acting as a payment aggregator for cross-border receipts if integrated with a licensed Chinese bank via SWIFT. For the underlying CN_FIP treasury, the bank account must be opened with a Tier-1 Chinese financial institution. The overall viability of using an EMI as the primary liquidity rail is therefore 'Low' for domestic settlement, but 'Medium' as an overlay for foreign-currency collection if nested atop a domestic bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign partner(s) must first complete FDI foreign-exchange registration for the FIP with the local SAFE office or an authorized large bank, which will issue a 'FDI Basic Information Bank Confirmation'. The capital injection must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the bank-designated FDI Capital Account using the purpose/remark field 'Foreign-invested partnership registered capital contribution — CAUTION: No other payment codes allowed'. The following documentary package must be submitted simultaneously: (i) the partnership agreement, (ii) the FIP business licence, (iii) the FDI registration code / CIIS ID, (iv) certificates of partner identity, and (v) an affidavit confirming the funds are not derived from sanction-list or criminal activity. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the purpose code and books the funds into the capital account. For each disbursement, the FIP must submit a fund-use letter and, for conversion to RMB, a verified payment order under the voluntary settlement plus negative list rule. For pure cloud services, the recommended channel is bank-to-bank SWIFT MT103 with the BOP purpose code for 'telecommunications, computer, and information services'; remitting banks may request underlying contracts, invoices, and proof of service delivery. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (e.g., Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud) do not hold a Chinese mainland banking license and are legally barred from opening resident accounts, handling RMB payments, or providing on-balance-sheet local-currency clearing for a China-registered FIP. Their role is strictly limited to (i) offering a non-resident foreign-currency current account to a Hong Kong affiliate on a 'traveller's cheque' basis; and (ii) acting as a payment aggregator for cross-border receipts if integrated with a licensed Chinese bank via SWIFT. For the underlying CN_FIP treasury, the bank account must be opened with a Tier-1 Chinese financial institution. The overall viability of using an EMI as the primary liquidity rail is therefore 'Low' for domestic settlement, but 'Medium' as an overlay for foreign-currency collection if nested atop a domestic bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 2.5 million threshold for each category of transaction? Actually, Hong Kong’s Transfer Pricing (TP) rules under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance introduce the Local File and Master File requirements. A Hong Kong entity is required to prepare a Local File if its related-party transactions exceed HKD 2.5 million for property transfers or HKD 5 million for financing transactions; for the Master File, the group’s consolidated turnover threshold of HKD 680 million and related-party transaction thresholds apply. The exact thresholds: for Large Enterprise (consolidated revenue > HKD 680 million), must prepare Master File; Local File required if (a) total amount of transactions of a specified class exceeds HKD 2.5 million (for property other than financial assets) or HKD 5 million (for financial transactions), or (b) the entity enjoys a tax concession and the transaction exceeds HKD 5 million. For B2B SaaS, typical intercompany payments include subscription fees, cloud service fees, IP licensing, and management charges. Even if below statutory threshold, prepare contemporaneous TP documentation to substantiate arm’s length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like certain offshore jurisdictions. However, the Inland Revenue Ordinance's source principle inherently requires substantive economic activities in Hong Kong to claim territorial source profits or treaty benefits. The IRD applies the 'overseas income exemption' only if the core profit-generating activities (CIGA) are performed outside Hong Kong. For an FIP acting as a pure IP holding vehicle, it will be subject to scrutiny under the anti-avoidance provisions and the recent IRD guidance on intellectual property (Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes - DIPN 58/59/60). For non-resident partners, if the partnership lacks substance or simply channels income, the IRD can assess under the control and management test. Also, the China-Hong Kong arrangements and BEPS 2.0 Pillar Two may affect the group if revenue exceeds EUR 750 million. Maintain records of board meetings, dealer management decisions, staff presence, and CIGA performed in HK.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Fraudulent Understatement of Hong Kong Source Profits (Passive vs. Active Business)

Statutory sanction: IRO Section 82: Evasion or intent to evade tax – maximum fine of HKD 100,000, plus treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to 3 years. Additionally, IRO Section 80 (failure to file or reporting incorrect information) may apply with fines of HKD 10,000 and additional treble tax. The IRD can issue assessments within 10 years (extended from the usual 6-year statute of limitations).

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced if the partner/director knowingly signed false tax returns; personal civil liability for the tax, penalties, and treble tax; criminal prosecution as principal or accomplice. Directors of the FIP's general partner (if a corporation) can face imprisonment and disqualification as director under the Companies Ordinance; a travel ban can be imposed by IRD or court preventing departure from Hong Kong while tax liabilities are outstanding. Under the IRO Section 13, individual partners are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities; miscreant directors can be personally assessed under Section 82A on the tax arrears of the entity.
Failure to Withhold Tax on Royalties or Interest Paid to Non-Residents (or Claiming DTA Relief Without Basis)

Statutory sanction: IRO Section 20O, 21A and section 23 (tax deduction source): failure to withhold and remit within 30 days triggers a fine of up to HKD 10,000 and possible additional penalty of 10% of the tax due. Under Section 82A, the liability may be recovered from the payer as agent; personal liability of the managing partner or director arises if the withholding was in their control. DTA misuse (treaty abuse) is attacked under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) incorporated into the HK-China CDTA, and penalties for incorrect DTA claims can amount to treble tax; the offence may be treated as fraud with imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners are jointly and severally liable to the extent they knowingly participated in arranging the payment without withholding. The IRD may issue a Director’s Assessment under IRO Section 82A against any person (including a director) who is responsible for the default, for the unpaid tax amount plus penalties. Criminal prosecution for false representation in DTA relief applications (e.g., submitting a false Tax Residency Certificate) is punishable by imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of HKD 100,000; civil asset restitution includes freezing of personal assets and a travel ban under the Tampering with Tax Laws (civil) provisions.
Economic Substance Violation / Shell Entity Misuse (False Offshore Claim)

Statutory sanction: IRO Section 61 and Section 61A anti-avoidance provisions allow the IRD to disregard the arrangement and compute tax as if the transaction had not been entered into or using a just and equitable adjustment, resulting in assessable profits at 16.5% plus potential penalties of up to 100% of tax undercharged (Section 82A). Additionally, the Inland Revenue Ordinance Schedule 17D (Transfer Pricing) also permits adjustments. Criminal sanction: if the shell arrangement is part of a tax evasion conspiracy, fraud at common law applies. The Companies Ordinance section 2 disqualification may be imposed; Hong Kong courts can issue an 'asset preservation order' and freeze bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who passively sign the tax return while knowing that the company has no substance are deemed to have aided and abetted evasion; they can be personally liable for the whole tax due as an assessment under IRO Section 82A. Criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment up to 3 years, personal fines up to HKD 100,000, and restitution of the evaded tax. A director who is a non-resident may be subjected to a travel ban as a matter of IRD enforcement, and as a regulatory matter, the Companies Registry may strike off the entity, allowing creditors and IRD to proceed against the director personally for fraudulent trading under section 358 of Companies Ordinance.
