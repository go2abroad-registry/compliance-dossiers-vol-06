Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk) including the Business Registration Office e-services

E-government filing portal

For the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in the Hong Kong SAR, the statutory tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The process is: (1) file Form IRBR 1 - Application for Business Registration within one month of commencement of business via the eTAX portal or in hard copy; (2) submit the partnership agreement, identification documents for each general and limited partner, and evidence of the local registered business address; (3) where the partnership has no physical presence in Hong Kong, appoint a Hong Kong resident agent or law firm to receive official correspondence and service of documents; (4) obtain the Business Registration Certificate and use the BRN as the partnership's TIN for all tax correspondence, customs/trade declarations, and banking records. No separate VAT or GST identification number exists in Hong Kong. The partnership itself is transparent for profits tax; each partner is separately assessed on their Hong Kong-source share of partnership profits and, where required, the partner provides their home jurisdiction TIN in filing documents.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a current-account convertible regime: genuine trade and service payments can be settled by authorized banks after verification, but capital account transactions require SAFE registration, quota, or specific approval. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) may receive foreign currency capital contributions from Hong Kong partners, but the funds must enter through a SAFE-registered capital project account and may be converted into RMB only after the bank verifies the underlying FDI record. RMB conversion is not unconditional: the settlement proceeds must be used for the FIP's approved business purposes, cannot flow into prohibited sectors such as unlicensed securities investment or speculative real estate, and cannot be used to repay domestic RMB loans in a manner that violates SAFE circulars. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is generally permitted after an annual audit, tax clearance, and a certified public accountant statement of distributable profits, but pass-through taxation under the partnership regime may impose withholding obligations on Chinese-source income attributable to non-resident partners. The Hong Kong jurisdiction is fully convertible, with the HKD pegged in the 7.75 to 7.85 range against the USD and no surrender or approval requirements for dividends, trade receipts, or capital returns. The practical friction is therefore concentrated in the PRC capital account, not in Hong Kong convertibility, and requires tight documentary alignment before any cross-border movement of funds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the 2012 PRC foreign exchange reform, the mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds is effectively zero; exporters are not required to convert foreign currency into RMB. However, all overseas export/service receipts must be repatriated into mainland China, and the statutory benchmarking period for goods export proceeds is generally 180 days from the date of customs declaration. Service trade income, including AI and software API fees, may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted at the bank's spot rate, provided the underlying contract, invoices, and tax certificates are available. Surplus foreign currency retained beyond the designated account limit must be reported to SAFE through the bank's foreign exchange monitoring system, and any conversion into RMB is voluntary. Hong Kong imposes no surrender rule for export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), empowered by the People's Bank of China (PBOC); for the Hong Kong leg, Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) operates the Linked Exchange Rate System with no foreign exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low to conditional-low for the PRC FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Wise, and Revolut Business are not legally authorized to open principal settlement accounts for enterprises in mainland China or to process CNY clearing through PBOC's payments system. Stripe may operate only as a front-end card acceptance gateway at the Hong Kong treasury node if the underlying funds settle to a fully licensed Hong Kong bank and are subsequently remitted to the FIP through SWIFT. Cryptocurrency-denominated USDC settlement is not viable for the PRC FIP's books; the Hong Kong node may convert USDC to HKD/USD through a licensed Hong Kong virtual-asset service provider, after which only fiat HKD/USD may be transferred onshore. The FIP should therefore treat domestic Tier-1 banks as the mandatory backbone and fintech EMIs as auxiliary invoicing tools, not as regulated banking rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK partner should execute a SWIFT MT103 or, for larger HKD transfers, a HKD CHATS settlement instruction from its licensed HK bank account to the FIP's SAFE-registered Capital Project Foreign Exchange Account at Bank of China Limited, ICBC, China Construction Bank, or China Merchants Bank. The remittance must name the FIP as beneficiary, the registered HK partner as remitter, and use the bank-approved SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contribution, generally referenced as SAFE transaction code 601 for FDI equity or partnership capital. The bank will require the SAFE FDI registration receipt, the FIP's partnership certificate, the capital contribution schedule, and an internal instruction letter before crediting the account. After receipt, the bank initiates capital verification through the SAFE FDI reporting system and a licensed CPA issues or updates the capital verification statement. RMB settlement from the capital project account is permitted only after the bank files a settlement-under-capital-project entry and verifies that the funds are deployed for the FIP's approved AI/LLM API business operations.Recurring AI API subscription fees from overseas customers should be repatriated via SWIFT MT103 with the correct service trade purpose code, supported by commercial invoices and the completed SAFE current-account verification declaration. For large customized AI model deployment contracts, a Commercial Letter of Credit can be used when the contract has measurable delivery/acceptance milestones and verifiable software export documentation. Documentary Collections, including D/P and D/A, are suitable for maintenance renewals, model version releases, and milestone-based licensing instalments where the counterparty requires shipment evidence. The FIP must do all onshore outbound settlement through direct member bank clearing and cannot route USDC or other crypto directly through the mainland banking system. The Hong Kong treasury node may convert USDC to HKD/USD through a licensed Hong Kong VASP and then transmit via CHATS/SWIFT to the mainland bank for onshore conversion.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts are permitted onshore with a genuine underlying trade or capital exposure, typically with a tenor up to 12 months and a hedge ratio not exceeding 100% of the committed receipts or payments.Offshore non-deliverable forwards in offshore CNY (CNH/USD NDF) are permissible only for the Hong Kong treasury node, not for the PRC FIP; onshore NDFs are not permitted in mainland China.Cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible only when a real debt or cross-border financing flow exists, such as a HK partner term loan converted into RMB, and the notional amount must match the underlying contract, require board approval, and be recorded with SAFE where applicable.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to conditional-low for the PRC FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Wise, and Revolut Business are not legally authorized to open principal settlement accounts for enterprises in mainland China or to process CNY clearing through PBOC's payments system. Stripe may operate only as a front-end card acceptance gateway at the Hong Kong treasury node if the underlying funds settle to a fully licensed Hong Kong bank and are subsequently remitted to the FIP through SWIFT. Cryptocurrency-denominated USDC settlement is not viable for the PRC FIP's books; the Hong Kong node may convert USDC to HKD/USD through a licensed Hong Kong virtual-asset service provider, after which only fiat HKD/USD may be transferred onshore. The FIP should therefore treat domestic Tier-1 banks as the mandatory backbone and fintech EMIs as auxiliary invoicing tools, not as regulated banking rails.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: annual controlled tangible-asset transactions exceeding RMB 200 million, intangible-asset/royalty/financial transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, or other controlled-services transactions exceeding RMB 40 million (STA Announcement [2016] No. 42). Master File threshold: group-wide related-party revenue above RMB 1 billion with associated-party transactions; contemporaneous TP documentation must be submitted with the annual CIT package (Form A100000 plus related-party forms G100000 and schedules).

Economic substance scope: The FIP and its HK partner must demonstrate real personnel, premises, decision-making and risk-bearing capacity under China's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Art. 47) and the Beneficial Owner substance-over-form checks. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for an AI LLM API platform include model training, fine-tuning, API pricing, customer contract execution, billing and risk management; these cannot be delegated to a shell affiliate merely to claim treaty benefits or offshore profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent Pass-Through Allocation and WHT Failure

Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 63 imposes recovery of underpaid tax plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% and a fine from 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; TCAL Art. 69 makes the FIP, as withholding agent, liable for the uncollected WHT; PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 creates a criminal threshold at RMB 100,000 of avoided tax or 10% of tax payable for the filing year.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner are personally liable if they knew of and directed the false allocation; the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Art. 20, exposing personal assets; civil asset restitution is ordered by the tax authority; a departure-restriction travel ban or blacklisting is imposed on the legal representative and directors; and in aggravated cases the directors face imprisonment up to 7 years.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse (Beneficial Ownership Fraud)

Statutory sanction: Denial of tax treaty benefits under STA Announcement [2019] No. 35, followed by a final withholding assessment at the statutory 10%; Hong Kong IRD may withdraw the Certificate of Resident Status where the company is not managed and controlled in Hong Kong; mainland penalties under TCAL Art. 44 for failure to supply information and a fine up to RMB 50,000 are also triggered.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Hong Kong nominee company and the mainland GP are exposed to piercing of the corporate veil; the nominee director's signature on false beneficial-ownership declarations is a criminal false statement offence under HK Crimes Ordinance Cap. 200 s. 36, carrying imprisonment up to 14 years; mainland-based directors are placed on the tax debt blacklist, subject to travel bans and asset freezes until the underpaid 10% WHT plus penalties are settled.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Art. 41 mandates arm's length adjustments; the tax authority may reattribute profits and impose 100% of the profit adjustment plus interest; TCAL Art. 32 imposes a 0.05% daily late-payment interest on underpaid tax for the years under audit; TCAL Art. 63 adds a 50% to 5x civil penalty; deliberate falsification of contemporaneous documents may be treated as tax fraud under criminal law.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed the artificial pricing are accessories to the tax evasion; they may be required to pay compensation for the company's shortfall under Company Law Art. 21 and Art. 20 veil-piercing rules; a travel ban and blacklist are imposed on all directors, officers and beneficial owners in the tax enforcement stage; if convicted, the sentencing range for fraudulent transfer pricing is imprisonment up to 3 years where the evasion exceeds the criminal threshold and 3 to 7 years where the amount is major (PRC Criminal Law Art. 201).
