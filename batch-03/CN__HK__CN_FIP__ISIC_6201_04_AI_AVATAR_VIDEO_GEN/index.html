Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong. There is no requirement to place any capital in a blocked escrow account or to obtain a capital verification certificate. The partners must agree the capital contribution schedule in the Limited Partnership Agreement, and the general partner may draw down subscribed capital when required by the FIP business. A limited partner who receives a return or withdrawal of capital may become liable to third party creditors if the withdrawal renders the partnership unable to pay its debts or reduces the statutory creditor protection available under Cap. 37.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX at https://www.etax.gov.hk. The State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China does not administer Hong Kong taxes under the one-country two-tax-systems principle.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not operate a separate TIN or VAT ID. The statutory tax identification number for an entity carrying on business in Hong Kong is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). A foreign non-resident partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must submit Application for Business Registration (Form IRBR1) to the Business Registration Office of the IRD within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, either online through eTAX or in person. Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate displaying the 8-digit BRN, which is used for all Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) filings and eTAX transactions. A partnership with no place of business in Hong Kong and no Hong Kong-source income is generally outside the Profits Tax net and does not require tax registration, but a partnership physically carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain the BRN regardless of the partners' non-resident status.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or sales tax in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is limited to specific duties, such as dutiable commodities and motor vehicle first registration tax, and Stamp Duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not impose value-added tax, GST, or sales tax and therefore has no economic turnover threshold triggering indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong does not levy VAT, GST, or sales tax, there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim and no input VAT refund mechanism. Hong Kong instead applies the territorial source principle of Profits Tax; export sales of goods and qualifying services are generally outside the scope of Hong Kong Profits Tax where the profits are not derived from a Hong Kong source, but this is an income tax rule and not an indirect tax zero-rating regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS), administratively managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). The Hong Kong dollar (HKD) floats within a guaranteed convertibility band of 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar, with the HKMA committing unlimited convertibility on both current and capital account transactions. Unlike mainland China's SAFE-governed model, Hong Kong has no statutory foreign exchange registration, quota approvals, or retention mandates. Profit remittance from a Hong Kong-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) to non-resident partners is unrestricted: dividends, distributable profits, and the return of partnership capital may be repatriated freely without prior central-bank authorization, provided the underlying source-of-funds records are bank-compliant. Foreign-currency revenue derived from the AI avatar video-generation subscription platform in USD, EUR, or other settlement currencies can be retained offshore in any denomination, converted into HKD or CNH through regulated banking channels, or used directly to pay international cloud vendors and contractor disbursements. The only material hindrance arises for capital entering from mainland China, where the People's Bank of China and SAFE impose outbound-investment filing obligations on the Chinese investing entity itself; however, once such capital lands in Hong Kong, no further exchange-control barrier, surrender obligation, or profit-remittance tax-obstacle exists beyond standard creditor-protection and capital-preservation loan-to-value norms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory timeline: Not applicable. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% (nil). The Hong Kong Monetary Authority does not impose any surrender, forced conversion, or repatriation timeline for export foreign-currency receipts. An FIP engaged in the cross-border sale of AI video-generation software subscriptions may hold all export proceeds indefinitely in USD, EUR, or HKD without any obligation to convert to the domestic currency. This position contrasts directly with mainland China's SAFE regime, where eligible export proceeds under the trade-in-services framework ordinarily require repatriation offshore and balance within reasonable grace periods; however, such mainland rules apply only to mainland-domiciled entities and do not attach to a Hong Kong SAR-person partnership.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Medium and legally constrained. Under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance, only institutions licensed by the HKMA may accept deposits and conduct banking business; unlicensed fintech EMIs operating under the United Kingdom's e-money regime or the EU's PSD2 passport are not legally authorized to hold corporate deposits or open virtual accounts for Hong Kong-domiciled partnerships without obtaining a recognized Money Service Operator (MSO) license. The HKMA has issued eight virtual banking licenses (ZA Bank, Mox Bank, WeLab Bank, Airstar Bank, Fusion Bank, etc.), which are fully regulated deposit-taking institutions and can serve as onboarding banks, but their corporate banking product suites remain narrower than Tier-1 commercial banks in terms of trade finance, FX hedging, and cross-border clearing. Stripe and Paddle represent payment aggregators and are classified as payment facilitators - they can route SaaS subscription receipts into a licensed Hong Kong settlement account, but they do not replace the core banking rails required for capital escrow, partner distributions, and FX hedging. Therefore the recommended structure is to maintain primary treasury operations with a Tier-1 commercial bank while leveraging Stripe/Paddle exclusively as upstream payment gateways.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Medium and legally constrained. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Hong Kong profits tax for an unincorporated business, including a partnership, is 7.5% on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits and 15% on the excess, subject to the two-tiered rates regime and eligibility restrictions. A corporate partner is generally subject to 8.25% and 16.5% under the corporate two-tiered rates regime.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Hong Kong withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong entity, including dividends paid to non-resident partners or shareholders. Hong Kong generally does not impose withholding tax on ordinary dividend distributions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong adopts a materiality and exemption framework rather than one universal threshold. A Hong Kong entity is generally exempt from preparing a Master File and Local File if it satisfies any two of these tests: annual revenue not exceeding HKD 400,000,000, total assets not exceeding HKD 300,000,000, or no more than 100 employees. Even where the entity-level exemption applies, specified transaction thresholds are relevant: HKD 220,000,000 for transfers of tangible goods, HKD 110,000,000 for financial transactions, HKD 110,000,000 for transfers of IP, and HKD 44,000,000 for other transactions, including services. Exceeding a relevant category threshold can trigger Local File documentation for that category. A Hong Kong constituent entity may also have Country-by-Country reporting obligations where the multinational group meets the applicable consolidated-revenue threshold, generally EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a single generally applicable economic-substance regulation equivalent to the broadest offshore economic-substance regimes. However, economic substance is relevant under the foreign-sourced income exemption regime for foreign-sourced interest income and certain other passive income, under transfer-pricing and anti-avoidance rules, and in determining source, beneficial ownership, treaty entitlement, and whether an arrangement is artificial or abusive. A qualifying entity claiming an exemption for foreign-sourced passive income may need adequate qualified employees and operating expenditure in Hong Kong, or must satisfy the applicable participation or nexus requirements. An AI video SaaS business should document development, platform operation, customer support, contracting, risk control, IP ownership, and decision-making functions by jurisdiction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax return

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance may result in a fine, imprisonment, recovery of undercharged tax, additional tax, and investigation costs. Wilful evasion or fraud attracts materially more severe criminal exposure than negligent error.

Director personal liability: Partners, responsible officers, and persons who consent to or participate in the conduct may be prosecuted personally. Civil recovery may target the partnership and liable partners. Travel restrictions are not automatic but may arise from court orders, bail conditions, immigration action, or enforcement proceedings; imprisonment is possible for criminal conviction.
Artificial offshore structuring and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: The Inland Revenue Department may deny offshore or treaty treatment, recharacterize income, impose tax, interest, penalties, and additional tax, and invoke general anti-avoidance or transfer-pricing provisions where statutory conditions are met.

Director personal liability: Individuals directing or knowingly implementing an abusive arrangement may face personal tax penalties or criminal liability where false statements, obstruction, or evasion is established. Corporate separateness is not a defense to personal participation in wrongdoing.
Transfer-pricing misstatement

Statutory sanction: The Inland Revenue Department may make compensating adjustments, impose tax and interest, reject deductions, require documentation, and apply penalties for incorrect returns or failure to furnish information.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, and tax representatives may be personally exposed where they knowingly authorize false records or obstruct an inquiry. Ordinary transfer-pricing adjustment alone does not automatically create personal criminal liability.
Unlicensed or misleading AI-content commercialization

Statutory sanction: Potential civil injunctions, damages, account of profits, delivery-up orders, licensing claims, administrative orders, and criminal penalties under applicable copyright, privacy, personal-data, consumer-protection, and fraud laws.

Director personal liability: Responsible individuals may be liable for knowing infringement, fraud, or unauthorized disclosure. Directors may face civil restitution and criminal prosecution where they personally authorized or participated in the conduct; travel bans are not an automatic sanction.
Anti-money-laundering and proceeds-of-crime exposure

Statutory sanction: Potential asset restraint and confiscation, account freezing, fines, criminal prosecution, imprisonment, and regulatory consequences for relevant intermediaries. Payment processors may terminate services and file suspicious-transaction reports.

Director personal liability: Partners, directors, beneficial owners, and compliance officers may face personal criminal liability if they know, suspect, facilitate, or fail to report conduct when a reporting duty applies. Confiscation and restitution may reach personally controlled assets; travel restrictions may be court-ordered.
Sanctions, export-control, and restricted-customer breach

Statutory sanction: Potential criminal fines, imprisonment, asset blocking, transaction prohibition, licensing consequences, contractual termination, and enforcement under Hong Kong, foreign, or extraterritorial sanctions regimes applicable to the transaction.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly authorize or facilitate prohibited dealings may be prosecuted personally. Civil restitution, asset restraint, and court-ordered travel restrictions may apply; liability depends on the applicable sanctions regime and conduct.
Books, records, and beneficial-ownership failure

Statutory sanction: Statutory fines, estimated assessments, disallowance of deductions, additional tax, prosecution, and adverse evidentiary inferences may result. Financial institutions and service providers may suspend accounts or terminate relationships.

Director personal liability: Partners and responsible officers may be fined or prosecuted for deliberate destruction, concealment, or production of false records. Personal civil exposure may include unpaid tax, penalties, restitution, and costs where the individual acted outside lawful authority.
Improper profit repatriation and disguised distributions

Statutory sanction: Potential denial of deductions, transfer-pricing adjustment, deemed income or distribution treatment, tax recovery, interest, penalties, and claims by partners or creditors.

Director personal liability: Partners may be personally liable under the partnership agreement and applicable partnership law for unauthorized withdrawals, breach of fiduciary duty, or wrongful distributions. Fraudulent transfers may support restitution, asset recovery, and criminal prosecution.
Failure to file and failure to cooperate

Statutory sanction: Late-filing penalties, additional tax, estimated assessment, interest, court recovery proceedings, prosecution, and enforcement against assets or liable partners.

Director personal liability: Authorized signatories, partners, and persons responsible for compliance may be personally prosecuted for false declarations or obstruction. Travel bans and imprisonment are not automatic for an ordinary late filing but may follow court enforcement or criminal conviction.
