Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox address is not acceptable as the registered office for a Hong Kong-incorporated or Hong Kong-registered partnership where tax and bank substance are required. The Companies Registry expects a physical location in Hong Kong for service of process; the Inland Revenue Department and licensed banks reject mailing-only addresses as indicative of shell activity without real presence. For the mobile utility app industry, a serviced or co-working office with a dedicated desk, a lease in the name of the partnership, a named responsible person, and accessible records is the minimum viable commercial substance. Without such substance, the entity risk rating should be elevated and bank account opening may be declined.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic registration and filing are performed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau online portal, with local administration by provincial and municipal tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For the Foreign-Invested Partnership, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Procedure: (1) register the partnership with the State Administration for Market Regulation under the Foreign-Invested Partnership registration rules and obtain the business license incorporating the USCC; (2) within 30 days after issuance of the business license, complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting the business license, partnership agreement, foreign partner identity and residency documentation, and the registered business address; the system then activates the partnership's VAT and income-tax profile; (3) if the partnership qualifies as a General VAT Taxpayer, complete the registration for issuance of Special VAT Invoices; if it remains a small-scale taxpayer, no special invoice registration is required unless voluntarily applied for; (4) for a non-resident foreign partner that does not carry on business in China through a permanent establishment, no separate TIN is required, and the partnership or payor must perform withholding registration for Chinese-source income; (5) if the non-resident partner does carry on business through the Foreign-Invested Partnership, the partner must be registered with the STA through the non-resident taxpayer identification procedure before receiving distributions.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — For the Foreign-Invested Partnership, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Procedure: (1) register the partnership with the State Administration for Market Regulation under the Foreign-Invested Partnership registration rules and obtain the business license incorporating the USCC; (2) within 30 days after issuance of the business license, complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting the business license, partnership agreement, foreign partner identity and residency documentation, and the registered business address; the system then activates the partnership's VAT and income-tax profile; (3) if the partnership qualifies as a General VAT Taxpayer, complete the registration for issuance of Special VAT Invoices; if it remains a small-scale taxpayer, no special invoice registration is required unless voluntarily applied for; (4) for a non-resident foreign partner that does not carry on business in China through a permanent establishment, no separate TIN is required, and the partnership or payor must perform withholding registration for Chinese-source income; (5) if the non-resident partner does carry on business through the Foreign-Invested Partnership, the partner must be registered with the STA through the non-resident taxpayer identification procedure before receiving distributions.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity elections; a Foreign-Invested Partnership is mandatorily treated as pass-through for Enterprise Income Tax purposes, with corporate and individual partners taxed on their allocated shares.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover-based exemption from VAT registration: tax registration is mandatory within 30 days of business license issuance. If annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000, the Foreign-Invested Partnership may remain a small-scale taxpayer using the simplified levy at 3%; above RMB 5,000,000 it must register as a General VAT Taxpayer and apply standard VAT rates. For digital software and app-store supplies, the applicable domestic VAT rate is generally 6% unless the export zero-rating condition is met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and the STA cross-border service zero-rating rules for qualifying internationally traded software and digital services that are supplied to a non-resident customer or an overseas end user, are consumed entirely outside the PRC, and are paid for in foreign currency from outside the PRC. For sales through Apple App Store and Google Play Billing, qualifying evidence includes end-user store account jurisdiction, billing address, payment instrument indicating a non-mainland-China jurisdiction, app-store commission and settlement statements for overseas stores, and repatriation of net proceeds into a PRC foreign-exchange account.

  • For physical goods: Customs Export Declaration and bill of lading or air waybill; not required for pure digital app downloads.
  • For digital services: app-store channel agreement with Apple/Google, including store-specific payout and settlement statements.
  • For digital services: Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing receipt from outside the PRC.
  • For digital services: cross-border service transaction schedule and zero-rated export service invoice issued under the Full Digital Invoice framework.
  • For all exports: underlying contract, purchase or service order, and evidence of settlement in convertible foreign currency, retained for at least five years.

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV and Full Digital Invoice (FDEI) e-invoicing framework. VAT invoices, including the Special VAT Invoice for general taxpayers and the ordinary VAT e-invoice, must be issued through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau or an STA-approved e-invoice service provider and carry the statutory 20-digit invoice code and anti-counterfeiting verification number. Invoice data are uploaded to STA systems in near real time and are cross-matched against export declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, and Apple/Google payout reports. The system does not use a European-style real-time polling clearance model; instead, the Foreign-Invested Partnership must submit invoice issuance within the statutory declaration period, use STA-approved API integration or portal entry, and retain the digital audit trail of invoice-state updates and anti-fraud verification signals.

If zero-rating is claimed without the required documentation or with falsified export or app-store evidence, the STA will disallow the zero-rating, assess VAT at the applicable domestic rate (6% for software services or 13% for tangible software goods), and impose late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day plus an evasion penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law of 50% to five times the underpaid tax. Fraudulent invoice issuance and export refund claims may also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law. The tax authority may retrospectively claw back refunds and reassess liabilities for three years generally, five years where underreporting exceeds statutory tolerance without willful misconduct, and without limitation for fraud or wilful evasion.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls. Under the current account, convertibility is largely unrestricted for trade and service transactions, consistent with IMF Article VIII obligations. However, the capital account remains heavily regulated; cross-border capital flows such as equity investment, loan repayments, and non-trade transactions require case-by-case SAFE approval or registration. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), capital contributions in foreign currency must be paid into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, then converted to RMB through the bank under the 'payment-against-use' rule. Profit repatriation by non-resident partners is permitted provided the audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and capital verification reports are submitted. Dividends and distributed profits are subject to withholding at the applicable rate unless Treaty relief is claimed. The RMB exchange rate is determined by market supply and demand with reference to a basket of currencies; however, enterprises face a crawling daily band of +/-2% for the USD/CNY spot rate. Surrender requirements have been phased out; entities may retain foreign currency in domestic accounts or convert at will, but must justify the authenticity of every transaction. This creates a dynamic environment where treasury management must strike a balance between regulatory compliance and liquidity efficiency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's trade foreign exchange administration, all export proceeds must be repatriated to the PRC within 180 days of the export customs declaration date, unless delayed payment terms beyond that period are registered with the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System (TFEMS). The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% — exporters enjoy full voluntary settlement of foreign currency receipts into RMB. However, any conversion must be executed through a designated foreign exchange bank with the underlying customs declaration, commercial invoice, and logistics documents scanned into the bank's verification system. Failure to repatriate in time may trigger a lower-tier regulatory status (Category B or C), requiring full advance deposit for every trade transaction.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-viable. China's FX control framework does not permit unlicensed fintech EMIs or offshore digital-only banks to operate as principal offshore account providers for PRC-onshore entities. The PBOC and SAFE mandate that all cross-border receipts and payments be settled through banks authorized to handle foreign exchange. Operating an EMI account abroad may be permissible for ancillary cash-flow collection, but it cannot serve as the primary clearing rail for the FIP's PRC banking and tax compliance. Legal constraints include the absence of a banking license, inability to integrate with CIPS/SWIFT as a direct member, and rejection by PRC tax authorities for documentary evidence.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound foreign currency capital contributions must be routed via SWIFT to the FIP's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a designated onshore bank. The remitting bank should include a purpose code for foreign direct investment, e.g. '901010' or 'investment by non-resident,' and the receiving bank will verify the payment against the SAFE filing. After receipt, the entity may convert the foreign currency into RMB at the spot rate pursuant to the 'Payment-Use' rule, but the RMB must be used for the declared business purpose within 180 days or revert to foreign currency. The bank will issue a capital account transaction confirmation. The PRC State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and SAFE filings must be completed before the remittance arrives; otherwise, the funds may be returned.For outbound payments to overseas contractors, suppliers, or app store platform costs, the FIP will use domestic bank channels for RMB-to-foreign-currency purchases under current account purposes. Remittances above a threshold must be supported by a tax withholding certificate, service agreement, and invoice. For goods trade, SWIFT MT103 via CIPS or the SWIFT network is standard; for larger capital purchases, an irrevocable confirmed Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) can be issued, while Documentary Collections (DP/DA) are permissible for simpler settlement. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: A Hong Kong incorporated company is subject to statutory audit regardless of size, except for certain dormant private companies that obtain an exemption from the Registrar of Companies under sections 358-360 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). The simplified reporting regime for small private companies requires total revenue not exceeding HKD 25 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 25 million, and not more than 50 employees, but even such companies must have audited financial statements unless dormant exemption is granted. For non-private companies, the thresholds are total revenue not exceeding HKD 2 million and total assets not exceeding HKD 2 million. There is no quantitative turnover, asset, or headcount threshold above which audit is legally mandated because the mandate applies to every company; however, the Inland Revenue Department may require audited accounts to support tax filings for any partnership. If the Hong Kong foreign-invested partnership operates a mainland China branch or subsidiary, PRC statutory audit thresholds apply under the applicable accounting regulations, generally requiring a certified audit for all enterprises regardless of size except micro enterprises exempted by local rule.
