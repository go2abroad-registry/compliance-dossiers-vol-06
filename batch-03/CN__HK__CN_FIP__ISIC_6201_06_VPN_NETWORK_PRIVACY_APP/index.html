Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic services are available through the GovHK portal and the eTAX system (Electronic Tax Filing and Payment System), with business registration handled by the Business Registration Office of the IRD.

        
E-government filing portal
1. The foreign-invested partnership is established under either the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) or the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37), or as a Limited Partnership Fund (LPF) under the Limited Partnership Funds Ordinance (Cap. 637), depending on the intended scope. 2. Within one month of commencing business, the partnership must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under Section 5 of the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) by submitting Form IRBR 105 (Application for Business Registration) to the Business Registration Office of the IRD. 3. The application must include the full partnership name, business description, principal office address in Hong Kong, and the particulars of every partner (including foreign partners' legal names, passport numbers, tax identification numbers of their home jurisdictions, and residential addresses); a certified copy of the partnership agreement must also accompany the application. 4. Upon approval, the IRD issues a BRC bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the partnership's primary tax identification number for Profits Tax and Business Registration purposes. 5. Each partner, whether resident or non-resident, is separately assessable to Profits Tax; non-resident partners without a Hong Kong Identity Card must obtain a Hong Kong Taxpayer File Number by completing Form IRBR 105 or by applying directly to the IRD for issuance of an individual tax reference number, providing passport and address evidence. 6. If the vehicle is structured as an LPF, initial registration must be completed with the Companies Registry as the LPF Registrar under Cap. 637, followed by the BRC application with the IRD. 7. There is no minimum capital requirement for partnership structures in Hong Kong, and the tax registration process is completed without a public audit or license requirement for VPN/network privacy SaaS activities.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible — Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a partnership is not a separate taxable entity; all profits are deemed to accrue directly to the partners in their agreed profit-sharing ratios. The territorial source principle applies: only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are subject to Profits Tax. For unincorporated partnerships, the two-tiered Profits Tax rates are 7.5% on the first HKD 2 million of assessable profits and 15% on the remainder, applied at the partner level.

        
Available election forms
None — Hong Kong has no check-the-box or entity-classification election under its tax code. The pass-through treatment of partnerships is statutorily mandatory. Partners may individually elect for Personal Assessment under Part VII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to offset certain deductions, but this election does not alter the partnership's transparent status.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No general VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax is levied in Hong Kong; selective excise duties apply to a limited list of dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), but digital VPN/encryption SaaS services are outside the scope of all indirect taxes.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable — Hong Kong imposes no indirect tax registration threshold because no VAT/GST regime exists. However, the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) mandates that all persons carrying on any business in Hong Kong apply for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement, regardless of annual turnover or revenue volume.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable under an export VAT framework because Hong Kong has no value-added tax. For direct tax purposes, revenue derived from the export of digital services, including VPN and network privacy SaaS subscriptions, qualifies for exemption from Hong Kong Profits Tax under Section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) provided the following statutory-criteria are met: (a) the profit-earning operations and the services are performed outside Hong Kong; (b) the contracts are concluded and registered outside Hong Kong; (c) the customers are located exclusively outside Hong Kong; and (d) the consideration is received in foreign currency from non-Hong Kong bank accounts. The offshore nature of the revenue must be supported by contemporaneous documentary evidence.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC maintains full current-account convertibility for genuine trade, service, and profit remittance transactions, but the capital account remains controlled. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership, inward registered capital must be booked through a SAFE-verified capital account, and every conversion from foreign currency into RMB must be supported by a genuine expenditure purpose and filed with the designated bank. Export proceeds are current-account receipts subject to trade verification and repatriation, but there is no mandated surrender ratio; the FIP may keep legitimate export receipts in foreign-currency settlement accounts. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is permitted as a current-account outward remittance only after audited financial statements, tax filing and payment, and a positive net distributable profit; the bank will require the FIP's registered partnership agreement and historical tax receipts to prove the distribution is not disguised capital repatriation. The Hong Kong SAR layer, if used for treasury concentration, is fully convertible under the HKD-USD linked exchange rate system administered by the HKMA. The principal compliance challenge is to demonstrate economic substance, genuine trade documents, and a clean flow of funds between the PRC FIP and the Hong Kong treasury account.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
For the PRC FIP, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic current-account foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days of the export declaration date; there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio, and the FIP may retain 100% of valid export proceeds in foreign currency or settle them through onshore banks at the market rate. For the Hong Kong SAR treasury overlay, there is no surrender obligation; HKD/USD funds may be held freely in licensed banks.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the Hong Kong SAR treasury clearing layer

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Low as a corporate treasury and clearing rail; Medium only as a SaaS merchant acquiring backend. Under PRC law, offshore digital EMIs are not licensed banks and may not open domestic corporate current accounts or clear capital-account flows. Stripe and Paymentwall may collect end-user card payments but must settle through an authorized cross-border payment institution and ultimately into a licensed PRC bank account; they cannot replace a Tier-1 bank relationship. Hong Kong-licensed stored value facilities are similarly not full banks and cannot provide the statutory clearing, FX, or capital verification services required for a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership.

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: Under Hong Kong law, a plain foreign-invested partnership (limited partnership under Cap.37) is not automatically subject to an annual independent audit. Mandatory audit arises only when: (a) the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap.637, in which case audited financial statements must be prepared under HKFRS or IFRS and delivered within 6 months of the financial year end; (b) the partnership carries on a regulated activity requiring a licence under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap.571), the Insurance Ordinance (Cap.41), or the Banking Ordinance (Cap.155); or (c) the Inland Revenue Department issues a specific notice requiring audited accounts under Section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112). In practice, the IRD issues such notices where gross revenue exceeds HKD 5,000,000 (approx. USD 641,000), total business assets exceed HKD 10,000,000 (approx. USD 1,282,000), or headcount exceeds 50 employees. Once triggered, the audit must be conducted by a certified public accountant or a firm of CPA practitioners registered under the Professional Accountants Ordinance (Cap.50), and the financial statements must comply with Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS), which are fully converged with IFRS; a limited partnership may alternatively apply HKFRS for Private Entities if it is not a listed or public-interest entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% (Hong Kong profits tax rate for unincorporated partnerships); corporate partners are separately taxed at the standard corporate rate of 16.5% on their distributive share of assessable profits.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
0% (Hong Kong does not impose any dividend withholding tax on profit distributions; dividends and partnership profit distributions are outside the charge to profits tax).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is strongly required under IRD DIPN 58 when the value of a controlled transaction or series of controlled transactions exceeds HK$5,000,000; the statutory safe harbour for conclusively documenting an arm's length position is the exceeding of these monetary benchmarks. A Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) notification obligation applies to non-resident parented MNE groups with annual consolidated group revenue of at least HK$6.8 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the territorial source principle and the general anti-abuse rules in IRO s.61 and s.61A require that the FIP have demonstrable Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong - including contract negotiation, VPN server management, software development, and strategic management - for any claim of offshore exemption, treaty benefits, or favourable source treatment. A partnership that exists only as a shell or conduit, while the real decision-making and management are located abroad, will be disregarded or re-characterised, and its profits will be reassessed as Hong Kong-source assessable income.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Offshore Claims
        
Statutory sanction: The IRD will invoke IRO s.82A to impose additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged, together with a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for three years for serious evasion. Knowingly making a false return under s.80(1) is punishable by a fine of up to HK$50,000 and six months' imprisonment; the IRD may also issue estimated assessments at double the estimated profit and levy a 10% late payment surcharge.

        
Director personal liability: Where the general partner or managing director knowingly submits the fraudulent offshore claim, the court may pierce the partnership/LLP veil under the Limited Partnership Ordinance and the Inland Revenue Ordinance, rendering the individual personally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and interest. Criminal conviction exposes the individual to a travel ban and immigration blacklisting under Hong Kong law, asset garnishment, disqualification from acting as a director or authorized representative, and civil restitution claims by the IRD for the shortfall.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Base Erosion
        
Statutory sanction: The IRD will apply the arm's length principle and the anti-avoidance provisions under IRO s.61A, which permit the IRD to nullify the tax benefit, imputed profits, and reassess the FIP as if the non-arm's length transaction had not occurred. Failure to produce transfer pricing documentation upon demand under s.51C carries a fine of up to HK$100,000; the tax undercharged may be subjected to additional penalties up to treble the amount, plus a 10% late payment surcharge.

        
Director personal liability: Managers who execute the artificial pricing scheme and sign the tax return without disclosing the related-party terms may be personally liable along with the partnership under joint and several liability provisions. The directors may be required to restore diverted income, face civil penalties and professional disciplinary sanctions, and in egregious cases a criminal conviction under IRO s.82A can lead to imprisonment for up to three years and a travel ban preventing them from leaving Hong Kong.

      

      
      

        Anti-Money Laundering and Sanctions Evasion
        
Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) Cap 615, a failure to conduct CDD and record-keeping carries a maximum fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for two years. Dealing with known proceeds of crime under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO) Cap 455 is an indictable offence with up to 14 years' imprisonment; the Customs and Excise Department and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority may freeze funds, revoke payment licences, and impose administrative fines.

        
Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who consent to, or are wilfully blind to, the AML failures are personally liable under AMLO; they may face criminal imprisonment for up to seven years for aggravated offences, civil asset forfeiture and restitution orders, professional disqualification and a travel ban (blacklisting) by the Immigration Department. The court may also pierce the partnership veil to impose a personal liability order on the beneficial owner for the laundered sums.
