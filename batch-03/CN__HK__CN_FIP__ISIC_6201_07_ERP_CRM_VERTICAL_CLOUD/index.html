Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: For a Hong Kong registered partnership, there is no automatic statutory audit imposed solely by the partnership form. However, an independent certified public accountant audit is legally mandatory when any one of the following quantitative thresholds or conditions is reached: (1) annual gross receipts or gross turnover exceeds HKD 1,000,000 (approximately USD 128,200), in which case the Inland Revenue Department requires audited financial statements to accompany the Form BIR52 Profits Tax Return; (2) the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund or carries on a financially regulated activity under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, where audited financial statements are unconditionally required regardless of turnover; (3) the Inland Revenue Department issues a specific notice under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance calling for audited accounts; (4) the partnership is a parent or subsidiary within a Hong Kong corporate group that is required to present consolidated audited accounts under the Companies Ordinance; or (5) a banking lender, investor, or financing agreement imposes an audit covenant irrespective of statutory requirements. No headcount-based audit trigger exists for partnerships under Hong Kong law, but once employees are engaged, separate employer tax reporting obligations arise.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where annual turnover exceeds HKD 100 million and specified intercompany transactions exceed HKD 10 million; Master File required where group consolidated turnover exceeds HKD 600 million. For FIPs below these thresholds, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is still strongly recommended for the vertical CRM/ERP cross-border services.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, the IRD applies a real economic substance test in determining the source of profits: the FIP must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong if it claims Hong Kong tax residence, including local office, employees, decision-making, IP ownership and cloud hosting. A shell FIP benefiting from treaty provisions without substance will be attacked under sections 61A or 61 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance and may be denied treaty benefits by the other contracting state.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82(1): on indictment, a fine of up to HKD 50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged and imprisonment up to 3 years; on summary conviction, a fine up to HKD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for knowingly concerned directors and general partners: they face civil restitution of tax and interest, a departure prevention order or travel ban from Hong Kong, seizure of personal assets, disqualification as company director, and criminal imprisonment up to 3 years.
Late Filing / Wilful Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: s.80(2) IRO: fine up to HKD 10,000; the IRD may raise estimated assessment and recover interest plus a further penalty up to 10% of the tax undercharged; non-compliance also triggers a failure to comply notice under s.80(1).

Director personal liability: If the general partner or director is the one responsible for filing, the court can order personal payment of the FIP's outstanding tax debts under piercing principles; a mandatory departure prevention order may be imposed under the Inland Revenue Ordinance to stop the director from leaving Hong Kong while tax is unpaid; repeated offences carry imprisonment up to 6 months.
Shell Company / Sham Transfer Pricing Arrangement

Statutory sanction: Section 61A IRO anti-avoidance: assessments adjusted as if the transaction had not been entered into; penalties up to 100% of the undercharged tax; transfer pricing adjustments under s.50AB; in egregious criminal cases, common law conspiracy to defraud or money laundering prosecutions (e.g., Drugs Trafficking and Organized Crime Ordinance) with fines up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the veil to hold directors/general partners personally liable for restitution of extracted profits, monetary penalties and legal costs; the IRD may apply for a Mareva injunction freezing personal assets and a travel ban; imprisonment can reach 7 years where the shell vehicle is used as part of a tax fraud conspiracy and 14 years where money laundering charges are proven.
