Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a limited partnership and no blocked escrow account is required at statutory registration. Capital contributions are determined contractually in the partnership deed and evidenced by the internal capital account ledger. The only relevant condition is that each limited partner's liability is limited to the amount actually agreed to be contributed to the partnership and recorded in Form LP1.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the national electronic tax service platform (e-Tax) and local State Tax Service bureaus; the Hong Kong SAR Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTAX portal are separately competent for the Hong Kong investor jurisdiction layer.

E-government filing portal

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its local equivalent. Upon approval, SAMR issues a Business License with an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the sole statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number for PRC tax purposes. Within 30 days of Business License issuance, the FIP must complete tax registration in the STA e-Tax portal by uploading the Business License, partnership agreement, partner identification documents, registered address proof, and bank account books; the e-Tax system creates the tax record and links it to the USCC. The FIP must then apply for VAT general taxpayer status if estimated rolling 12-month taxable supplies exceed CNY 5,000,000, or voluntarily if it expects to issue VAT special invoices. It must also apply for e-invoice quotas and a Tax U-Key digital signature device. Non-resident partners, including Hong Kong corporate partners, do not automatically receive a separate PRC TIN by reason of partnership status; their distributive share income is reportable by the FIP under the pass-through regime, and a non-resident partner separately registers with the STA only if it has a permanent establishment or direct PRC-source withholding obligation. Hong Kong partners seeking treaty relief under the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Double Taxation Arrangement must obtain a Certificate of Resident Status from the Hong Kong IRD and file it with the STA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The FIP is registered for tax from Business License issuance irrespective of turnover. Compulsory registration as a VAT general taxpayer is triggered on CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable supplies in any rolling 12-month period; below that threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it makes a voluntary general taxpayer election. Domestic supplies of cybersecurity SaaS are generally subject to the 6% modern-services VAT rate, while export supplies may be zero-rated where the statutory conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for exported goods and qualifying cross-border services under PRC VAT law. For this cloud vulnerability scanning and penetration testing SaaS business, 0% VAT applies when the services are supplied to an overseas recipient, consumed entirely outside mainland China, qualify under the STA's zero-rating categories for information technology and software services, are paid with foreign exchange repatriated to the PRC through a licensed bank, and are separately identified in the VAT return and service-export records. The FIP must document that no beneficial use of the services occurs inside mainland China. Domestic supplies remain taxable at 6%.

  • Customs Export Declaration / export goods declaration accepted by the General Administration of Customs of China, required for physical goods; for pure SaaS services this is replaced by the electronic service export contract and service delivery report
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill evidencing physical departure from the PRC, required for physical goods only
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank/SAFE electronic foreign-exchange receipt voucher confirming repatriation of export proceeds into the PRC
  • Signed commercial contract with the overseas customer, zero-rated VAT invoice, and a written substantiation of offshore consumption for the SaaS/cybersecurity services

Mandatory use of the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. All VAT invoices must be transmitted to the STA in real time, carry a digital tax stamp and unique invoice code, and be signed through the Tax U-Key or equivalent fiscal device. The FIP must separately report zero-rated, exempt, and taxable supplies in the VAT filing schedules and, for physical exports, reconcile invoice data with the China International Trade Single Window and customs export records.

Unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating is treated as underpayment of VAT. The STA may claw back the full zero-rated VAT together with a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the statutory due date, and may impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Fraudulent export tax refund claims and false export declarations may be referred for criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law, leading to fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment. The exporter may also lose zero-rating eligibility, be suspended from export tax refund processing, and face customs penalties for inaccurate export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a freely convertible currency regime with no foreign exchange controls imposed by the HKMA. The Hong Kong dollar (HKD) is fully convertible for both current and capital account transactions, backed by the Linked Exchange Rate System pegged to the US dollar within a band of 7.75 to 7.85 HKD per USD. There are no surrender requirements for export proceeds, no restrictions on profit repatriation, and no prior approval mandates for capital flows into or out of the territory. However, because the ultimate sovereign is China, cross-border RMB remittances between Hong Kong and the mainland remain subject to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules, including the Macroprudential Regulation of Cross-border Financing and the filing of foreign direct investment (FDI) or outbound direct investment (ODI) documentation where applicable. For a Hong Kong based Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) engaging in cybersecurity SaaS, inbound equity injections from mainland shareholders require compliance with SAFE registration via a designated bank, while operational proceeds may be held in any freely convertible currency. The absence of exchange controls in Hong Kong itself makes treasury management highly streamlined, but audit trails must differentiate between HKD, USD, and CNH accounts for clean statutory reporting and bank due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency proceeds in Hong Kong. Exporters may retain 100% of their foreign currency receipts in any permitted currency without any requirement to surrender to the HKMA. Entities are free to hold multi-currency accounts and manage FX positions at their own discretion, provided that internal accounting records are adequately maintained for taxation and anti-money laundering (AML) compliance.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and virtual banks have limited but role-specific viability in Hong Kong. Licensed virtual banks under the HKMA, such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, or Mox Bank, are legally authorized to service local corporate entities, but only as full-fledged banks under the Banking Ordinance. Non-bank fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, or Payoneer) are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in Hong Kong; they operate under Money Service Operator (MSO) licenses or rely on sponsor bank arrangements, which legally bars them from offering primary corporate deposit accounts or capital escrow services. For an FIP handling low-risk SaaS receivables, an EMI can be leveraged only as a payment aggregation rail for high-frequency small-ticket transactions, while core treasury and capital verification must reside with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The HKMA's Regulatory Sandbox permits certain cross-border payment experiments, but such arrangements are subject to strict transaction volume caps and quarterly compliance audits; accordingly, the viability of fintech EMIs for long-term treasury holding is rated 'Restricted' and cannot substitute for a licensed bank.

The wire transfer must include the Standard Settlement Instruction (SSI) of the Hong Kong beneficiary bank and reference the unique FIP corporate registration number. The correct cross-border payment purpose code for the mainland sending bank is '7210' (Overseas Investment Settlement) for equity injections into overseas enterprises. The receiving Hong Kong bank will perform capital verification by obtaining the inward remittance advice, the partnership deed, and a legal opinion on the FIP's power to accept the funds. For funds in USD or CNH, the routing can be done via CHATS (Clearing House Automated Transfer System) in Hong Kong or via SWIFT for cross-border wires; funds in RMB must be channeled through the Hong Kong Offshore RMB Clearing System overseen by the Hong Kong Clearing House, with the receiving bank confirming the CNH classification. After receipt, the surplus funds cannot be immediately deployed into overseas securities or real estate without specific regulatory approval; they must remain in either the capital escrow account or the operational current account pending confirmed trade disbursements.Settlements for outbound trade disbursements shall be executed via SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor payments, with detailed remittance information embedded in field 70 to align with the ISIC 6201 software licensing model. For high-value contracts exceeding HKD 1.5 million, a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) is recommended to reduce counterparty risk, with the FIP as applicant and the Hong Kong bank issuing an irrevocable LC in favor of the overseas software reseller. Documentary collections (DP/DA) are permissible for lower-risk service disbursements, but given the digital nature of the SaaS business, the most efficient clearing rail is the instant cross-border payment system via the Hong Kong Faster Payment System (FPS) link to global clearing networks, provided the beneficiary bank supports FPS. All outbound payments must be accompanied by a certificate of commercial invoice and, where applicable, the underlying sales contract to satisfy the bank's end-use verification under the HKMA's anti-money laundering and counter-financing of terrorism (AML/CFT) guidelines.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and virtual banks have limited but role-specific viability in Hong Kong. Licensed virtual banks under the HKMA, such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, or Mox Bank, are legally authorized to service local corporate entities, but only as full-fledged banks under the Banking Ordinance. Non-bank fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, or Payoneer) are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions in Hong Kong; they operate under Money Service Operator (MSO) licenses or rely on sponsor bank arrangements, which legally bars them from offering primary corporate deposit accounts or capital escrow services. For an FIP handling low-risk SaaS receivables, an EMI can be leveraged only as a payment aggregation rail for high-frequency small-ticket transactions, while core treasury and capital verification must reside with a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank. The HKMA's Regulatory Sandbox permits certain cross-border payment experiments, but such arrangements are subject to strict transaction volume caps and quarterly compliance audits; accordingly, the viability of fintech EMIs for long-term treasury holding is rated 'Restricted' and cannot substitute for a licensed bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation must be maintained when cross-border connected transactions exceed HK$10 million for property or non-financial services transactions, and HK$5 million for financing transactions; Master File and Local File are required where the multinational group has relevant revenue of at least HK$680 million and the transaction thresholds are met. Even below these thresholds, the FIP must keep sufficient books and records to reconstruct exempt offshore profits or intra-group dealings.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but economic substance remains decisive for treaty entitlement and source-of-profit analysis. The IRD applies sections 61A and 61B of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to disregard artificial transactions and schemes lacking bona fide commercial substance.
