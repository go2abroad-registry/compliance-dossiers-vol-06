Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under PRC rules applicable to foreign-invested partnerships, the registered address must be a real and verifiable place where the partnership carries on management and operations. A virtual office or commercial mailbox, standing alone, does not satisfy the actual business address requirement applied by SAMR and the tax authorities, and licensed banks will reject it for corporate account opening because it lacks documentary evidence of business substance. For a digital-health fitness-app foreign-invested partnership whose operations are largely online, the tax authority may accept a serviced office or coworking unit with a physical lease and workstation capacity, but not a bare mailbox. The registered address must also match the address printed on contracts, invoices, and bank statements. A pure virtual office is therefore impermissible as the sole registered address.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Regulation on Foreign Exchange Control (2008) and SAFE operational guidelines, the RMB is convertible for current account items, while capital account transactions remain subject to a complex system of registration, quotas, and regulatory approval. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is permitted to receive foreign currency capital contributions from its overseas partners only after SAFE has issued a foreign exchange registration certificate. The contributed funds may subsequently be settled into RMB in compliance with the positive list of permissible uses for partnership operating expenditures. Current account receipts, such as subscription proceeds collected by Apple or Google through app store platforms, must be repatriated through a designated foreign-exchange bank and are subject to an authenticity review supported by invoices, service contracts, and platform payment statements. Profit distributions remitted to non-resident partners are allowed after completion of the statutory annual audit, payment of applicable enterprise income tax, and submission of the tax clearance certificate to the remitting bank; the bank must also verify that distributable profits exist under the partnership agreement. Capital account outflows, including the return of capital, redemption of partnership interests, and liquidation proceeds, require separate SAFE approval and an amended foreign-exchange registration. In practice, RMB convertibility for the FIP is therefore full on the current account side, constrained and compliance-intensive on the capital account side, and heavily dependent on the availability of a verifiable transactional paper trail, tax filings, and an unblemished anti-money-laundering compliance profile.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's consolidated trade and services foreign-exchange rules, all export and service revenue received by the FIP in foreign currency must be repatriated into the PRC and reported to the designated bank through the Trade Cross-Border Foreign Exchange Monitoring system within 30 calendar days after the actual date of receipt. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain the full amount in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert all or part of it into RMB at the prevailing mid-rate quoted by the designated bank. For digital service income from app stores, the FIP must provide the underlying bill of service, the software copyright certificate, and the app store remittance statement. The bank will complete a record of received export proceeds, and the FIP must ensure that the proceeds are credited to the domestic account within 90 days from the billing month of the underlying export declaration; any longer collection period requires a reasoned written application submitted to and approved by SAFE.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar unlicensed fintech providers cannot serve as the primary corporate treasury account for a PRC-domiciled FIP because they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions or designated foreign-exchange banks under PRC law. SAFE-regulated capital contributions cannot be received by an unauthorized third-party entity, and the FIP will be unable to complete capital verification or foreign-exchange settlement through such platforms. EMIs may only function as auxiliary collection facilitators for app store proceeds if they are licensed cross-border payment institutions recognized by the PBOC and if the funds ultimately settle into a domestic bank account via a licensed payment provider. They are legally barred from safeguarding FIP capital escrow, performing onshore RMB settlement, or acting as a correspondent for SAFE reporting. Overall viability rating: Low; limited to supplementary upstream merchant collection arrangements, never as a replacement for the designated PRC bank account.

All inbound capital injections to the FIP must be routed as SWIFT MT103 messages from the overseas partner's account to the FIP's designated foreign-exchange capital account held at a SAFE-authorized PRC bank. The payment instruction must include the exact SAFE purpose code for a foreign-invested partnership capital contribution, and the remitter name must exactly match the foreign partner recorded on the SAFE foreign-exchange registration certificate. After the funds are credited, the FIP must instruct a Chinese certified public accountant to perform a statutory capital verification, and the bank will then allow the conversion of funds into RMB under the payment-to-use supervision framework. The bank must screen the proposed RMB use against the negative list for foreign investment and confirm that the ultimate purchase is a genuine operational expenditure such as payroll, cloud infrastructure, software licensing, or marketing costs. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar unlicensed fintech providers cannot serve as the primary corporate treasury account for a PRC-domiciled FIP because they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions or designated foreign-exchange banks under PRC law. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: IRO s.51C read with IRD DIPN 58: a Local File and Master File are mandatorily prepared for a large taxpayer with annual gross turnover at or above HK$200,000,000; administrative de minimis thresholds for controlled cross-border transactions are HK$50,000,000 for tangible property and financing transactions and HK$10,000,000 for services and intangible property transactions (including fitness app IP, user data licensing and software royalties); the IRD may request contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for any non-arm's length transaction regardless of size if there is a risk of tax leakage

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate UK-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code like Caribbean jurisdictions, but the territorial source principle embedded in IRO s.14 and the anti-avoidance provisions in IRO ss.61, 61A, and the transfer pricing rules under ss.58A-58E impose a de facto economic substance requirement; to support the Hong Kong-source status of profits from the health/fitness app, the entity must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including strategic management, marketing, app development and updates, revenue-collection controls, customer contracting, and hardware/server administration; a Foreign-Invested Partnership with no Hong Kong office, no employees, no independent bank account and no management decision-making is at risk of being treated as a shell, having its profits recharacterised as sourced elsewhere, and being denied DTA relief

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Offshore Claim or Omission of Assessable Profits

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82A (Evasion of tax): upon summary conviction a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; in addition, IRD may demand up to 200% of the tax undercharged as a penalty under s.82A(4) and may raise estimated assessments under s.59(3); the offending partner/director is also exposed to prosecution under the Crimes Ordinance for false accounting and common law conspiracy to defraud if fraudulent statements are used

Director personal liability: Under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) s.13, every partner is jointly and severally liable for the firm's debts and tax liabilities, piercing the corporate veil where the entity is a general or limited partnership without separate legal personality; IRO s.85 renders any officer, partner or director who consented or connived in the offence personally liable criminally; the IRD may issue a departure prohibition order under IRO s.77 barring the individual from leaving Hong Kong until all unpaid tax, penalties and interest are secured; civil asset restitution and compulsory bankruptcy/winding-up exposure follow
Failure to File Profits Tax Return or Repeat Non-Compliance with Notices

Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(2) imposes a fine of up to HK$10,000 for failure to provide a return; if tax has been undercharged, the court may order a further penalty up to 200% of the unpaid tax; late payment surcharge of 5% is added on tax unpaid after six months and an additional 10% after another six months under IRO s.71A; IRD may issue a best-of-judgment assessment with no prior audit, and interest continues to accrue

Director personal liability: The responsible partner or director who controls the entity is personally liable for the default under IRO s.85(2) if the offence was committed with their consent or attributable to their neglect; directors and partners can be personally assessed for unpaid partnership tax where the partnership assets are insufficient; departure prohibition orders under IRO s.77 may be issued; recalcitrant taxpayers are 'blacklisted' by IRD, resulting in civil restraint, travel restrictions and enhanced audit surveillance; imprisonment terms of up to 3 years apply under s.82A for fraud-related failures
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Shell/Substance Abuse for IP Royalties

Statutory sanction: IRO ss.58A-58D empower IRD to recompute the profits and disallow the excessive deductions on an arm's length basis; IRO s.61 and s.61A allow IRD to disregard artificial or fictitious transactions that have a tax avoidance purpose; penalties under IRO s.80(6) and s.82A can reach up to 200% of the adjusted tax differential; where false declarations are made to the Chinese tax authority to secure the DTA rate, the mainland China State Taxation Administration may impose interest and penalty under the PRC Tax Administration Law (up to 50% to 5 times of the underpaid tax in egregious cases), and may deny the entire treaty benefit

Director personal liability: Directors and controlling partners who orchestrate the shell arrangement or sign false beneficial ownership declarations face personal civil asset restitution for the unpaid tax and interest; if the transfers constitute money laundering, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) exposes individuals to fines up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years for dealing in the proceeds of crime; the IRD may apply IRO s.77 departure prohibition orders, while Chinese authorities may impose exit bans and criminal investigation; the corporate veil is pierced where the partnership is found to be a sham or facade, and the individuals behind the entity are jointly and severally liable for the entire tax liability
