Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the sub-national Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB) online filing portal of the competent provincial-level tax authority; the Hong Kong SAR maintains a separate Inland Revenue Department (IRD) eTAX regime, but the PRC formation is registered with STA for all Mainland tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in Mainland China, the statutory TIN/VAT ID is obtained through the following procedure: (1) file the partnership formation application with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its provincial/municipal branch together with the partnership agreement, foreign investor constitutive documents, identity evidence of the general and limited partners, and the required foreign-investment information report; (2) SAMR issues the Foreign-Invested Partnership Business License containing the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the TIN and VAT registration number for all PRC tax filings; (3) SAMR transmits the registration data to STA through the one-license-one-code inter-agency data-sharing mechanism, automatically effecting tax registration without a separate tax-registration application; (4) the FIP activates its taxpayer account on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, confirms tax classification, files bank account information, and applies for issuance/management of VAT invoices; (5) if the FIP’s taxable turnover crosses or it voluntarily elects the VAT general taxpayer regime, it must complete the general VAT taxpayer registration and enable the relevant VAT invoice control module; and (6) non-resident foreign partners do not require a separate PRC TIN to form the FIP, but where the FIP is obliged to withhold on income allocable to non-resident partners, the withholding is recorded under the FIP’s USCC/TIN and the foreign partner is issued appropriate withholding vouchers and tax residency certificates for foreign tax credit relief where applicable.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box election for partnerships, and the FIP is statutorily transparent for Enterprise Income Tax purposes. There is no standalone hybrid mismatch election available under PRC tax law. The only optional administrative elections are voluntary registration as a VAT general taxpayer before exceeding the RMB 5,000,000 turnover threshold and the documentary application for export zero-rating/exemption status on qualifying cross-border services and goods.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; all business entities issuing taxable supplies must complete basic tax registration upon business establishment irrespective of turnover, with small-scale taxpayer classification available below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods exports, zero-rating is available when the goods are actually exported from the PRC customs territory, the exporter files a valid customs export declaration, title and risk pass to an overseas buyer, the export transaction is genuine, and the sales proceeds are received in foreign exchange from outside the PRC. For qualifying cross-border software and SaaS/digital services, zero-rating/exemption applies when the service is supplied to a non-mainland client, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, the service is within the STA and Ministry of Finance cross-border taxable services zero-rating/exemption catalogue, the service contract clearly identifies the offshore customer and offshore consumption, and the consideration is received in convertible foreign currency from a non-resident party. A VAT general taxpayer status is required to claim output VAT zero-rating with input VAT refund for exports; where the taxpayer remains a small-scale taxpayer, export supplies may only be exempt without input credit unless general VAT taxpayer registration is elected.

China mandates the use of the national unified VAT invoice management system. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through STA-authorised channels, using certified tax control UKey devices or the Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice (FDEI) module on the Electronic Tax Bureau platform, with invoice data transmitted to STA in real time or near-real time upon issuance. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the jurisdiction applicable to this Foreign-Invested Partnership (CN_FIP), the RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions upon verification of genuine underlying trade in goods, services, and dividends. The capital account, however, remains administratively restricted. Incoming capital contributions from the Hong Kong partner must be registered with SAFE via the domestic custodian bank and placed in a dedicated capital settlement account before conversion into RMB for approved operating purposes. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate, prior capital verification, and a partner resolution to declare dividends. While the mandatory surrender system in China was abolished in 2012, all export and service proceeds must still be repatriated into China within the applicable statutory period; the enterprise is free to hold the foreign currency in a current account or convert it to RMB at its discretion, provided the source is matched to authentic export documentation. The 'real need' principle governs all FX conversion, meaning any settlement must be backed by contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and comparable evidentiary records. For payroll automation software exports, regular service fee collections are monitored under code 211010, while licensing royalties fall under code 231020. The entity must therefore maintain a dual treasury strategy: current account convertibility for operating cash flows, and a rigorous SAFE compliance path for capital account events.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs declaration. For services and payroll software exports, repatriation is required within 120 days from the date the service is rendered and invoiced. There is no mandatory conversion ratio imposed by SAFE; an entity may retain up to 100% of its foreign currency proceeds in a current foreign exchange account or voluntarily settle them into RMB. Any conversion, whether voluntary or via interbank market, must satisfy transaction authenticity checks and match the correct SAFE purpose codes. The bank is obliged to ensure the export receipt matches the corresponding contract and customs record under the export revenue reconciliation system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Revolut, and Payoneer do not hold a deposit-taking licence in mainland China and are therefore legally barred from opening CNAPS-linked RMB settlement accounts or serving as the primary custodian for a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership capital escrow account. Under the PRC Law on Commercial Banks and the regulations for foreign-funded banks, only licensed Chinese commercial banks and approved foreign-bank branches inside China or a designated Free Trade Zone may accept demand deposits and execute RMB clearing. Offshore EMIs may be used only as supplementary collection or disbursement rails for cross-border invoices outside the SAFE banking network, but any injection of paid-in partnership capital or intercompany loan proceed must traverse a licensed domestic bank that is registered with SAFE. Overall viability as the primary cash management and clearing counterparty is Low; as an auxiliary cross-border collection tool, viability is Medium subject to legal contracting and prerequisite validation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor must first complete an FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE through the domestic bank where the capital account is to be opened. The bank underwrites the FIP partnership agreement, business licence, HK investor's incorporation documents, and valid identity proofs. After receiving the FDI registration form, the bank sets up a capital settlement account and issues a Payment Notice for Capital Account. The inbound remittance is executed through SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent or an HK-licensed bank, using the SAFE purpose code for foreign-invested capital contribution and quoting the FDI registration number in the field 72. On receipt, the bank performs AML screening, verifies the Payment Notice, and credits the capital escrow account. An independent PRC CPA firm then issues a capital verification report confirming full payment, after which the funds may be converted into RMB and transferred to the operating basic RMB account. Any exchange of foreign currency into RMB is processed at the negotiated bank's spot rate, with the transaction ensuring strict compliance with the Foreign Investment Law and the negative list for market entry.The payroll SaaS platform will execute outbound vendor payments, software licensing fees, and dividend distributions through the standard Chinese banking clearing rails. For low-value and high-frequency payments, SWIFT MT103 wire transfers are used, each backed by an invoice, contract, and SAFE payment purpose code such as 211010 for computer services or 322091 for payroll processing. For high-value infrastructure procurement or cross-border acquisition of software assets, a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by a Chinese Tier-1 bank in favour of the offshore vendor serves as the primary settlement instrument, with documents delivered under a Documentary Collection (D/P at sight or term) for medium-risk transactions. The bank conducts dual verification of the underlying transaction and the sanctions screening on every counterparty, ensuring no routing through restricted jurisdictions. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong statutory law imposes an unconditional audit requirement on every limited company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622) and every Limited Partnership Fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap 637), irrespective of turnover, total assets, or headcount. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registered under the Partnership Ordinance (Cap 38) or the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap 37), no mandatory audit is imposed solely by size. However, a compulsory CPA audit is triggered if any of the following applies: (i) the FIP is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund, where an audit by a practising CPA is mandatory each financial year with no size threshold; (ii) the FIP holds a licence under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap 571) or the Money Service Operators Ordinance (Cap 615) (licensed Money Service Operators must audit their financial statements annually regardless of volume); (iii) the Inland Revenue Department, acting under Section 51(5) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, serves a written notice requiring audited accounts because the partnership has a gross annual turnover exceeding HK$5,000,000 (USD 641,000) or net business assets exceeding HK$2,000,000 (USD 256,000). These quantitative triggers are derived from the IRD's internal assessment parameters and are applied on a case-by-case basis. Additionally, if the FIP is governed by a partnership agreement that expressly requires an audit, or if the FIP is part of a multinational group whose parent requires audited financial statements for consolidation under IFRS, a voluntary statutory-type audit will become a practical necessity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% standard Hong Kong Profits Tax rate; a two-tiered rate of 8.25% applies to the first HKD 2 million of assessable profits and 16.5% applies to the excess, subject to connected-entity restrictions.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Hong Kong withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong resident company or business. Partnership profit allocations are not generally subject to a separate Hong Kong dividend withholding tax, although the partners may have tax exposure in their residence jurisdictions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong local-file and master-file exemptions are tested by transaction type and size. A local file is generally not required where the relevant category does not exceed HKD 44 million for property transactions, HKD 22 million for financial transactions, HKD 11 million for transfers of intangibles, or HKD 44 million for other transactions. A master file and local file are generally not required where the entity satisfies at least two of these three size criteria: total annual revenue not exceeding HKD 200 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 200 million, and no more than 100 employees. Separate documentation and filing requirements may apply to country-by-country reporting groups with consolidated revenue of at least EUR 750 million.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the regimes adopted by certain offshore jurisdictions. However, source-of-profits principles, the general anti-avoidance rule, transfer-pricing rules, beneficial-ownership requirements, the foreign-sourced income exemption regime, and international tax transparency standards require genuine functions, people, assets, decision-making, and risk control. For foreign-sourced passive income received in Hong Kong, qualifying economic-activity, participation, nexus, or subject-to-tax conditions may be relevant under the FSIE regime. A payroll SaaS business should maintain evidence of software development, product management, sales, customer support, cybersecurity, data governance, and strategic control activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Potential prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, fines, imprisonment, additional tax, and a penalty of up to three times the tax undercharged. Civil recovery of unpaid tax, interest, and penalties may also apply.

Director personal liability: Partners, responsible officers, and authorized signatories may face criminal liability where they consented to, connived in, or negligently permitted the offence. Civil restitution and enforcement against personal assets may follow. A travel restriction is not automatic but may arise through court enforcement or bail conditions; imprisonment is possible for serious offences.
Failure to File or Failure to Keep Records

Statutory sanction: Prosecution, fines, additional tax, estimated assessment by the Inland Revenue Department, disallowance of unsupported deductions, and interest on overdue liabilities.

Director personal liability: Responsible partners, directors, and officers may be prosecuted where the legislation attributes the default to their consent, connivance, or neglect. Personal imprisonment is possible for aggravated or repeated non-compliance.
Transfer-Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Primary adjustments, corresponding-adjustment disputes, denial of deductions, penalties for incorrect returns, record-production orders, and interest on resulting tax shortfalls.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may face personal exposure for deliberate misstatements or obstruction. Corporate separateness is not a defense to a personal statutory offence or fraudulent conduct.
Permanent Establishment and Source Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Profits tax assessment on Hong Kong-sourced profits, penalties, interest, reassessment of prior years within applicable limitation periods, and possible treaty competent-authority proceedings.

Director personal liability: Personal liability generally requires separate misconduct, but directors may be liable for false filings, destruction of evidence, or knowing participation in evasion.
Unlawful Profit Repatriation and Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty relief, reassessment, withholding-tax exposure in the relevant source jurisdiction, anti-avoidance penalties, interest, and possible criminal investigation for fraud or evasion.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly participate in sham arrangements may face criminal prosecution, confiscation or restitution orders, and imprisonment. Travel bans are not an automatic consequence and depend on applicable court or immigration measures.
Anti-Money Laundering and Suspicious Payment Activity

Statutory sanction: Suspicious transaction reporting obligations, account restrictions, asset restraint or confiscation, regulatory sanctions, fines, and imprisonment under applicable Hong Kong anti-money-laundering and organized-crime legislation.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, compliance officers, and employees may be personally prosecuted for knowing participation, tipping off, dealing with criminal property, or consent and connivance. Confiscation and restitution may reach personal assets.
Personal Data and Payroll Compliance

Statutory sanction: Enforcement notices, investigation, prosecution for specified offences, compensation exposure, contractual claims, and reputational damage under the Personal Data (Privacy) Ordinance and applicable overseas data-protection laws.

Director personal liability: Personal criminal exposure may arise for responsible officers where statutory conditions are met. Directors may also face fiduciary, employment, and negligence claims for knowingly approving unlawful data practices.
Unlicensed or Mischaracterized Business Activity

Statutory sanction: Regulatory prohibition, fines, prosecution, disgorgement, customer restitution, account closure, and loss of banking access.

Director personal liability: Responsible individuals may be prosecuted for carrying on regulated business without authorization. Corporate form does not shield directors or partners from personal statutory liability for unauthorized conduct.
