Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and registration portal: GovHK eTAX – https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/tax/eta/index.htm

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident partnership conducting business in Hong Kong is required to obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The application is made to the IRD on form IRBR 37 (Application for Business Registration) accompanied by the partnership's constitutional documents, a certified copy of the partnership agreement, identification and residential/registered address of each partner (including non-resident partners), and a Hong Kong business address. Submission can be done online via the GovHK eTAX portal using a valid IRD digital certificate (eXOTS) or through an authorised representative. The IRD issues the BRC bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the tax identifier for both revenue and profits tax. For tax filings, each partner (individual or corporate) is separately assessed; the partnership itself does not pay profits tax but must file Partnership Return (Form BIRS51) if required. Non-resident partners are assessed on their share of Hong Kong-sourced profits and may appoint a tax representative in Hong Kong.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales tax (Hong Kong imposes profits tax on territorial income only; customs duties apply exclusively to dutiable commodities such as tobacco, alcohol, hydrocarbons, and motor vehicles. No general indirect tax system operates.)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Hong Kong has no VAT registration threshold. Exemptions apply only to businesses earning gross annual turnover not exceeding HKD 10,000 (or other proclaimed limits, currently suspended).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under an indirect tax regime, as Hong Kong has no VAT to zero-rate. For profits tax purposes, income derived by a partnership from services (including software-as-a-service and low-code web application builder subscriptions) is taxable only if the profits arise in or derive from Hong Kong (IRO s.14). The statutory test for offshore-sourced services income requires that all or substantially all of the operations, contract negotiation, and performance occur outside Hong Kong; the taxpayer must prove non-HK source on a facts-and-circumstances basis. Where billing is handled via Stripe or Paddle from a Hong Kong entity to an end customer abroad, the IRD will examine the location of profit-generating activities. To substantiate offshore treatment, the partnership must demonstrate that service development, hosting servers, support, and client engagement activities are performed outside Hong Kong, and that the revenue is not 'export' zero-rated but exempt from territorial taxation as non-HK-sourced.

  • Not applicable to exports of services; for goods, a Customs Export Declaration (CEEL) would be required, but for this digital service model no customs documentation applies. Instead, the following documentary bundle is mandatory to prove offshore source and foreign exchange repatriation:
  • 1. Executed service contract (or clickwrap terms) with overseas counterparties.
  • 2. Commercial invoice issued by the partnership to each external customer, showing service description and amount in foreign currency.
  • 3. Bank inward remittance statements / SWIFT MT103 or third-party payment settlement reports (Stripe, Paddle) clearly identifying the counterparty and date of receipt.
  • 4. Server logs and IP-location records demonstrating that digital service delivery platforms and content delivery networks are situated outside Hong Kong.
  • 5. Operational records (e.g., meeting minutes, emails, project logs) evidencing that all profit-generating activities occurred outside Hong Kong.

None. Hong Kong does not impose real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing APIs, or anti-fraud digital stamping. Taxpayers are required to keep sufficient records (in English or Chinese) of receipts, payments, and business transactions for at least 7 years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Invoices may be issued electronically or in paper form; no statutory format is prescribed, though a properly documented invoice should include the supplier's name, BRN, date, description, and amount.

The IRD may disregard offshore-sourcing claims and assess additional profits tax if the evidence is inadequate or the substance is found to be in Hong Kong. Interest and penalties apply under sections 82A and 82B of the Inland Revenue Ordinance: a taxpayer who understates profits (by wrongfully treating Hong Kong-source income as offshore) is liable to a penalty of up to 50% of the undercharged tax for negligent errors, and up to 100% (or in aggravated cases 3 times) for wilful suppression. In cases of intentional fraud, criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment. The IRD can also reopen closed years (normally up to 6 years, or no limit for fraud) and recover underpaid tax plus interest. Additionally, failure to retain required records (including foreign exchange receipts) is an offence punishable by a fine of up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 6 months; the burden of proving offshore source lies squarely on the taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and a current-account RMB convertibility commitment. The Foreign-Invested Partnership's software licensing and subscription revenue from non-resident clients is a current-account receipt; it can be retained in a foreign-exchange settlement account or converted to RMB by a designated bank after the bank verifies the underlying transaction through contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, and payment gateway reconciliation. Capital-account flows, by contrast, are not freely convertible. All foreign capital contributions to the FIP must be registered with SAFE through the designated bank before funds enter China; the registration determines the permitted contribution amount and the disbursement purpose. The bank will open a foreign-currency capital account, and each inbound wire will be checked against the registered investment schedule. The converted RMB can be applied only to self-operating expenditures within the business scope, such as research and development, cloud hosting, employee payroll, bandwidth, and software procurement; funds cannot be used for equity investment, debt investment, non-self-use real estate, prohibited securities activity, or lending to other entities. Profit distribution and remittances to overseas partners require annual audited financial statements, enterprise income tax payment or tax clearance proof, a duly adopted partner distribution resolution, and evidence that the profit arose from the no-code website builder's operating revenue. In practice, the bank also performs a negative-list review and may require the FIP to disclose any future capital flows passing through an intermediate Hong Kong treasury company, ensuring such flows are not restructured as undisclosed offshore loans.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE has abolished the old mandatory percentage-based surrender system for current-account receipts. The FIP may retain qualifying export or software-service receipts in a foreign-currency current account or sell them to a designated foreign-exchange bank at the market rate. The controlling requirements are: all export and software-service receipts must be received back into China through approved banking channels and reported to SAFE under the correct transaction code; for goods trade, export proceeds should normally be collected within 90 days from customs declaration unless the FIP obtains an extension or registration; for services trade, including no-code website builder revenue, proceeds should be collected no later than the agreed payment date in the underlying contract and settled without unreasonable delay; there is no statutory conversion ratio, but the designated bank applies an authenticity-and-consistency review and may require a natural proportion of revenue to be settled into RMB if RMB operating costs are not otherwise funded. All receipts must be coded under the appropriate SAFE service-trade transaction code for computer and software services.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange administrator; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) governs the Hong Kong treasury booking side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for primary account opening. A mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership cannot open an operating bank account with an offshore digital EMI because offshore EMIs do not hold PBOC banking licenses and cannot provide SAFE reporting, FDI registration, capital-account custody, RMB clearing, or direct central-bank settlement. Some PBOC-and-SAFE-licensed cross-border e-commerce payment institutions can act as ancillary collection channels for Stripe or Paddle receipts, but they must be wired to a core domestic bank account and do not replace the bank. Stripe and Paddle are payment facilitators, not banking rails; their settlement flows must ultimately enter the FIP's mainland bank account through a licensed cross-border payment institution or a direct SWIFT remittance. Relying on an unlicensed offshore EMI to bypass China's capital controls would violate SAFE rules, trigger a suspicious-transaction report, and expose the FIP to administrative penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital for the FIP must follow SAFE direct-investment rules. Before any remittance, the FIP must obtain its Foreign-Invested Partnership business license and complete SAFE FDI registration at the designated bank, which will issue the foreign-exchange business registration certificate. The overseas investor then wires funds to the FIP's foreign-currency capital account using the SAFE direct-investment remittance purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise paid-in capital. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the remittance against the registered FDI quota and enters the transaction into SAFE's foreign-exchange and direct-investment monitoring system. The FIP then applies for foreign exchange settlement into RMB by submitting the partnership resolution, underlying expense contracts, and a written commitment that the RMB will be used for self-operated business expenditures. A PRC certified public accountant verifies the paid-in capital and issues a Capital Verification Report. All future injections must be matched against the registered schedule, and any intermediate Hong Kong treasury entity must be disclosed in the investment chain so that the bank can record the ultimate non-resident partner accurately.Outbound payments from the FIP to foreign vendors, licensors, or service providers should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for low-value and contractually evidenced service payments after applicable withholding tax is assessed. For recurring SaaS distribution, royalty, or technical-support payments, the FIP may use bank cross-border RMB settlement through CIPS or SWIFT using the appropriate SAFE service-trade BOP code for computer and software services. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are generally unsuitable for no-code website builder services because those instruments are designed for physical goods trade; however, if the FIP eventually sells hardware bundles or physical appliances with embedded software, a commercial LC can be used subject to the underlying sales contract and transport documents. Stripe and Paddle are acceptable only as front-end collection facilitators for inbound customer payments and must be reconciled to the bank settlement account; they should not be used as the primary outbound disbursement rail. For all outbound payables, the FIP must keep invoices, contracts, delivery or service-completion evidence, and tax withholding certificates to satisfy SAFE's post-event verification.Onshore RMB deliverable forward contracts with genuine exposure, matching forecast software-service receipts or payables and prohibited from speculative trading.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) with SAFE-verifiable underlying investment or operating debt to convert RMB liability or revenue streams into USD or HKD cash flows on a real-demand basis.RMB/USD or RMB/HKD vanilla currency options for premium-only risk management, subject to bank due diligence and proof of an underlying commercial transaction.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore for RMB because the RMB is a deliverable currency in China; the compliant alternative is a deliverable onshore forward, and any offshore NDF position should be avoided for the mainland FIP.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary account opening. A mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership cannot open an operating bank account with an offshore digital EMI because offshore EMIs do not hold PBOC banking licenses and cannot provide SAFE reporting, FDI registration, capital-account custody, RMB clearing, or direct central-bank settlement. Some PBOC-and-SAFE-licensed cross-border e-commerce payment institutions can act as ancillary collection channels for Stripe or Paddle receipts, but they must be wired to a core domestic bank account and do not replace the bank. Stripe and Paddle are payment facilitators, not banking rails; their settlement flows must ultimately enter the FIP's mainland bank account through a licensed cross-border payment institution or a direct SWIFT remittance. Relying on an unlicensed offshore EMI to bypass China's capital controls would violate SAFE rules, trigger a suspicious-transaction report, and expose the FIP to administrative penalties.
