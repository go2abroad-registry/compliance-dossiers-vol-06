Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office; electronic filing and registration portal: IRD eTAX and the GovHK Business Registration e-Service. The State Taxation Administration (STA) of China is not the competent registration authority for this Hong Kong offshore foreign-invested partnership unless a separate PRC permanent establishment creates a PRC tax filing obligation.

E-government filing portal

The statutory Hong Kong tax identifier for a Foreign-Invested Partnership (constituted as a limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance, Cap. 37) is the Business Registration Number (BRN) printed on the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310. Procedure: (1) ensure the limited partnership is registered with the Companies Registry and obtain the Certificate of Registration of Limited Partnership; (2) complete and submit the Commissioner's prescribed Business Registration application; (3) attach the executed partnership deed, register of partners, identity documents for each individual partner (HKID or passport), and corporate documents for each corporate partner (certificate of incorporation, register of directors, and non-resident/foreign ownership statements); (4) state the Hong Kong principal place of business and the date of commencement of business; (5) file through IRD eTAX, the GovHK Business Registration e-Service, or in person at the Business Registration Office within one month after commencement of business; (6) upon approval, receive the BRC stamped with the BRN. The BRN is the only business tax ID used for Profits Tax, employer returns, and customs/trade declarations. There is no separate VAT or GST registration because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, Cap. 112, the partnership is transparent for Profits Tax purposes, with assessable profits computed in the name of the partnership but made chargeable on the partners in proportion to their profit-sharing ratios. Hong Kong also applies a territorial source principle, so only profits sourced in Hong Kong are taxable.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no hybrid-mismatch classification election, and no tax-transparent entity election. An individual partner may elect Personal Assessment under section 41 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, Cap. 112, to obtain allowances and deductions; any Hong Kong double taxation agreement relief is applied at the partner level.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Hong Kong does not operate a VAT, GST, Sales and Use Tax, or digital services tax. The only indirect tax regimes are excise duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109 (liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol) and stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance, Cap. 117.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable because no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. Under the Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310, any person carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate within one month, regardless of turnover. A small-business fee and levy exemption applies when monthly average turnover does not exceed HKD 10,000 for service businesses or HKD 30,000 for other businesses, but this is a business-registration fee exemption, not an indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST on goods or services, so no 0% export VAT rate exists to claim. Exports of physical goods from Hong Kong are generally duty-free, except for dutiable commodities under Cap. 109. The LegalTech SaaS/e-signature service is a cross-border supply of services; because there is no Hong Kong indirect tax on services, the FIP simply needs to preserve documentation supporting the offshore-source position for Profits Tax purposes. For the FIP to treat the export/offshore service income as outside Hong Kong's territorial tax net, it should retain evidence that the contracting, negotiation, performance and customer-facing activity took place outside Hong Kong.

  • Electronic Export Declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, Cap. 60E, submitted through the Customs and Excise Department and Trade and Industry Department electronic trade system if physical goods are exported; this is not triggered by pure cloud SaaS/e-signature service exports
  • Executed LegalTech SaaS contract and commercial invoice identifying supplier, customer, jurisdiction of use, service description, contract amounts, currency, and payment rails such as Stripe or credit card

  • Bank and foreign-exchange settlement evidence, including SWIFT/TT credit advices, Stripe payout statements, card-acquirer settlement reports, and bank in-currency receipts proving that proceeds were actually repatriated to the Hong Kong account within the 180-day benchmark
  • Partner and customer correspondence, board minutes, intercompany agreements, and email or portal communications demonstrating that business negotiations and contract performance occurred outside Hong Kong; all records should be retained in English or Chinese with English translation available for IRD audit

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE delegated powers, the renminbi is convertible on the current account for trade in services, royalties, dividends and interest once the underlying transaction is genuine and documented. Capital-account flows, including the contribution of foreign capital to a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership by non-resident partners, are not freely convertible: every inward remittance must be preceded by SAFE FDI registration and must land in a designated capital-account deposit at a domestic authorised bank. After conversion of HKD or USD into CNY, the funds may be used only for purposes within the FIP's approved business scope and in compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List; the funds may not be used for securities, entrusted loans or non-business real estate. Profit remittances and partnership distributions to non-resident partners require an audited financial statement, income/liquidation tax clearance, a service-trade or dividend-remittance purpose code, and where applicable a withholding tax certificate; no blanket convertibility is granted for intercompany balances or unspecified treasury master accounts. Onshore CNY and offshore CNH are distinct deliverable markets with different price formation; a Hong Kong clearing bridge does not eliminate the onshore SAFE verification and balance-of-payments reporting obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The former mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratios have been abolished for goods trade under the SAFE goods-trade monitoring framework. Ordinary export proceeds must be repatriated to a designated onshore bank account within 90 days of the customs export declaration, unless delayed-receipt registration has been filed with SAFE; approved deferrals may extend to 180 days for receivables and up to 365 days for validated long-term receivables. The exporter may retain the foreign currency in a current-account FX deposit account or sell it to a designated bank for RMB at market rate; no mandatory conversion percentage exists. For cross-border service trade such as the SaaS subscription and e-signature revenue of the FIP, surrender rules do not apply, but each receipt must be matched with a signed service agreement, an invoice and a service-trade balance-of-payments declaration to the receiving bank, and funds must be repatriated into the onshore account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the controlling Chinese foreign-exchange authorities, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervising the Hong Kong dollar and offshore CNH clearing leg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

After the FIP obtains its business licence, the Chinese bank applies for SAFE FDI registration under the Foreign-Invested Partnership rules and opens a Foreign Exchange Capital Deposit Account. Non-resident partners must remit HKD/USD via SWIFT MT103 with the remittance information field showing the SAFE FDI registration number and the registered partner's name; no third-party remittance, credit card funding or Stripe transfer is acceptable for capital injection. Upon receipt, the bank updates the SAFE monitoring system and, if required, converts the funds to RMB at the onshore market rate. A PRC certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report crediting the partners' partnership capital. The converted RMB is held in a basic deposit or operational expense account and used strictly in line with the partnership agreement and the Foreign Investment Negative List; funds cannot be used for speculative financial products, securities investments or on-lending to unrelated parties.The FIP should use standard SWIFT MT103 for direct settlement of software licensing fees, royalties, cloud infrastructure costs and subcontractor services, with SWIFT MT202 COV only as the interbank cover for benefiting branches. Commercial letters of credit are generally unsuitable for pure SaaS and e-signature services; documentary collections or open-account with invoice matching are more appropriate where the counterparty has an established credit relationship. Every outbound service-trade payment must be accompanied by the signed contract, proforma invoice and, when the amount exceeds the bank's due-diligence screening threshold, tax and SAFE verification certificates. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and largely prohibited for onshore China: Stripe, Airwallex, Revolut, Wise and similar digital EMIs do not hold a PRC banking licence and cannot open or service a SAFE-monitored capital account. They cannot act as the primary clearing bank for a mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership and cannot accept FDI capital injections. Such payment providers may be used only as merchant acquiring or aggregation rails for low-value SaaS e-commerce receipts in Hong Kong or overseas markets, after funds are converted into HKD/USD and repatriated through a licensed onshore bank. Viability rating: low for the Mainland FIP; medium for the separate Hong Kong treasury vehicle.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate; the CN_FIP is a pass-through Foreign-Invested Partnership, so the Hong Kong corporate partner is assessed at 25% on PRC-source profits attributable to a permanent establishment, while a non-resident partner without a PE is subject to a 10% statutory withholding tax on China-source distributive income

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

PRC statutory withholding tax on outbound profit distributions or dividends to a non-resident Hong Kong corporate investor is 10%; because the CN_FIP is pass-through, the FIP must withhold at the statutory rate on the Hong Kong partner's distributive share of China-source profits; Hong Kong itself imposes no dividend withholding tax
