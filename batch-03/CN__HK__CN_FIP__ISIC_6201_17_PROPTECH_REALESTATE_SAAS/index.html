Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law imposes no minimum registered or paid-up capital on a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The partners define their subscribed contributions exclusively in the Partnership Agreement; no blocked escrow or pre-deposit capital account is required by the registration regime. However, foreign partners must remit subscribed capital in convertible currency through a licensed bank after the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration code is issued by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (SAT), acting through the locally competent tax service bureau of the place of business registration; online access via the SAT Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform and the official China Tax Service portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign-invested partnership must first complete commercial registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation to obtain a Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code, which serves as the entity's PRC Taxpayer Identification Number. The tax authority creates the taxpayer record through the SAMR-tax information-sharing mechanism, but the FIP must complete formal taxpayer registration, filing-profile setup, legal representative identity verification, and any required foreign-exchange and withholding registrations at the competent tax service bureau within 30 days after Business License issuance. A non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment that needs a direct registration or a separate withholding taxpayer record must submit the partnership agreement, foreign investor business registration evidence, Hong Kong or other jurisdiction tax-residence certificate, and a signed authorization appointing a tax representative, after which the competent tax authority will issue a non-resident taxpayer identification number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the Chinese FIP is not subject to Enterprise Income Tax at the entity level; taxable income flows through and is taxed at the partner level).

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or voluntary hybrid-mismatch classification for partnerships. Pass-through treatment is mandatory; general anti-avoidance rules may recharacterize hybrid arrangements, but no election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic PRC tax registration; every foreign-invested partnership must be tax-registered from formation. For general VAT taxpayer status, mandatory registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that amount the FIP may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to simplified VAT treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For cross-border SaaS and software-related services supplied by a PRC VAT general taxpayer to a non-resident recipient, 0% VAT zero-rating is available when the service is consumed wholly outside mainland China, no domestic real-estate or goods-related benefit is conferred, and the FIP maintains contemporaneous evidence of the offshore service contract, offshore delivery, and overseas collection. Qualifying cross-border services may alternatively be VAT-exempt, but zero-rating additionally allows refund of input VAT. Physical exports of goods are generally eligible for 0% VAT upon completion of customs clearance out of China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment outside Chinese customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds collection voucher
  • Export contract and commercial invoice made out to the offshore purchaser
  • Proof of foreign exchange repatriation through the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system or current-account declaration
  • Cross-border taxable services zero-rating filing package and offshore service recipient confirmation (for SaaS supplies)

The FIP must issue VAT invoices through the SAT National Electronic Invoice Service Platform, using fully digitalized electronic invoices for mainland-China taxable supplies. Real-time or near-real-time fiscal clearance is performed by the Golden Tax IV system; taxpayer invoicing systems connect through approved APIs, and the tax authority validates, digital-stamps, and archives every invoice before it can be used for input-tax credit. Special VAT invoices remain subject to separate approval and issuance reconciliation, and any mismatch between invoice, VAT return, and tax payment will trigger automatic tax control exceptions.

Where export zero-rating is claimed without the required customs documentation, freight evidence, offshore service-delivery records, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence, SAT will claw back the zero-rated output tax benefit and any refunded input VAT, impose late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and apply an evasion penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax undercharged. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims may additionally trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, inclusion in the national tax-credit blacklist, and loss of future zero-rating and refund eligibility.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a supervised current-account convertibility regime and a capital-account control framework that continues to require regulatory filing or approval for most capital movements. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) can collect USD, HKD or CNH subscription revenue from bona fide overseas PropTech customers through a licensed domestic bank on the current account without a separate SAFE permit, but the bank must validate the authenticity of the service contract, the invoicing trail and the cross-border SWIFT narrative. Capital-account flows are not freely convertible: an inbound capital contribution from the Hong Kong investor must be matched against the FIP's SAFE foreign-invested entity registration, routed into a dedicated capital account, and supported by a mainland CPA capital-verification report before funds can be deployed in the FIP's permitted operating scope. Profit remittance to the foreign partner is a legally accepted transaction only after audited accounts show realised distributable profits, the FIP has satisfied its corporate income tax or partnership-level withholding obligations, the bank receives the partnership distribution resolution, and the remittance is coded with the correct international-balance-of-payments purpose. In every case the underlying documents are examined item-by-item under the bank's economic-substance and round-tripping controls; the absence of a genuine platform, real users or a commensurate operating cost base will usually cause the bank to reject the flow as a disguised capital transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a mandatory export-proceeds surrender ratio. The FIP may retain 100% of eligible cross-border service-trade receipts in a SAFE-designated current-account FX account or convert any portion to RMB at its discretion. The statutory discipline is repatriation: all export and service-trade foreign currency must arrive through a licensed domestic bank and be reconciled in the bank's trade and FX monitoring system, generally within 30 days of the date the transaction is registered for verification, or longer where the underlying contract expressly permits deferred payment; the bank will require the SaaS contract, invoice, delivery or access log, and customer confirmation. Any receipt remaining unreconciled for a protracted period can trigger downgrade from A/B/C classification, enhanced KYC and potential capital-control review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign exchange regulator, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the offshore Hong Kong banking and clearing node used for cross-border settlement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - not legally viable as the primary onshore treasury or account-holding rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, Nium, or Wise B2B are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to provide domestic current or capital accounts to a mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership, and any RMB clearing or foreign-exchange settlement inside China must be executed by a licensed Chinese commercial bank or a PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institution. Such EMIs may be used only as an auxiliary treasury interface for overseas collections in HKD, USD or CNH, and only if the EMI itself is operated by an entity licensed in an acceptable jurisdiction and the FIP maintains its operative account at a real Chinese Tier-1 bank for statutory capital, tax and FX compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor must transmit the capital via SWIFT MT103 from its own licensed HK bank account directly to the SAFE-registered capital account of the FIP at Bank of China, ICBC or another designated Tier-1 bank. The MT103 narrative must reference the FIP's SAFE foreign-invested entity registration number, name the recipient as the Foreign-Invested Partnership, and use China's cross-border BOP direct-investment purpose code 622011 for the initial capital injection, or the bank-mapped equivalent for an increase in contribution. The remittance cannot be applied against future SaaS receivables, and the bank will not permit conversion into RMB or movement to the general account until the FIP has completed the capital-verification report through a licensed PRC accounting firm and the bank has entered the transaction in the SAFE monitoring system. For CNH, USD or HKD multi-currency capital subscriptions, the bank will apply the registered foreign-investment negative list to confirm the PropTech property-software activity is not restricted, and any excess capital contribution beyond the filed amount will be returned or made subject to a fresh SAFE registration.Operational outflows for cloud hosting, third-party licensing, vendor fees, marketing and overseas technical support must be cleared by SWIFT MT103 from the FIP's onshore RMB/FX current account, with each payment carrying the correct BOP service-trade purpose code and a supporting file containing the contract, invoice, service-receipt evidence and any applicable withholding tax clearance. For physical hardware purchases under the property-technology build-out, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued by ICBC or BOC provide the primary settlement rail when the vendor requires title protection, while Documentary Collections are acceptable for moderate-value and repeat shipments; both instruments require the usual transport, insurance, customs and inspection documents, and all proceeds and acceptances must be reconciled against the SAFE goods-trade monitoring database. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate applicable to corporate partners on allocated profits; the Foreign-Invested Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and has 0% entity-level Corporate Income Tax)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on gross dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise under EIT Law Articles 3(3) and 37. For a fiscally transparent FIP, outbound profit shares remitted to a non-resident corporate partner are treated as China-source income and are likewise subject to a 10% final withholding tax absent treaty relief. The China-Hong Kong DTA reduces the rate to 5% for profits/dividends where the Hong Kong partner is a company beneficially owning at least 25% of the FIP capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File and annual related-party transaction reporting are mandatory when any of the following thresholds are exceeded in a fiscal year: RMB 40 million for related-party services, RMB 200 million for related-party tangible goods transactions, RMB 100 million for related-party intangible asset transactions, RMB 100 million for related-party financial asset transfers, or RMB 300 million for related-party equity transfers. A Master File is additionally required when the Chinese enterprise is part of a group whose prior-year consolidated revenue reaches RMB 5 billion or whose annual related-party transaction volume reaches RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies a robust substance-over-form doctrine through Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), Article 45 (Controlled Foreign Corporation), and the transfer pricing rules in SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42. There is no separate Economic Substance Regulation in China, but the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is central to determining both tax residency and treaty eligibility. For the Hong Kong partner, CIGA means that product development, pricing, risk management, software maintenance, and client contracting must be actually undertaken in Hong Kong with employees, premises and board authority; if those activities remain in the Chinese FIP or in a Mainland China affiliate, the Hong Kong partner is a mere nominee and treaty relief will be denied. The FIP's general partner must also have substantive economic presence and decision-making power over contracts and investments, or the tax authorities may recharacterize the entire structure as a sham and impose domestic 25% taxation plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Treaty Claim and False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and TCAL Article 63 impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus a daily 0.05% late payment surcharge. The tax authority will deny treaty benefits, recalculate the gross remittance at the statutory 10% or 25% rate, and apply GAAR recharacterization. If the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes a false tax declaration, the case is referred under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with up to 7 years' imprisonment and unlimited criminal fines.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20(3) and hold the directors of the corporate general partner and the Hong Kong partner jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest and penalties. Directors also face travel bans (exit control), asset freezes, blacklisting under the Major Tax Violation Disclosure Measures, confiscation of proceeds, and criminal imprisonment from 3 to 7 years. A foreign director may be declared person non grata and subject to extradition/interpol alert in egregious cases.
Transfer Pricing Compliance Failure – Missing Documentation and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42, the tax authority may recharacterize the transactions, disallow the excessive deductions, compute taxable income on an estimated basis, and impose special tax investigation interest plus up to 5 additional percentage points over the ordinary rate. If the false pricing is deemed intentional tax evasion, TCAL Article 63 applies with penalties of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. The failure to file the related-party transaction schedule itself attracts separate penalties under TCAL Article 62.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the non-arm's-length fees become personally liable for the tax adjustment to the extent they caused the concealment. The tax authority may freeze their personal bank accounts, impose exit bans, put them on the punitive joint-credit blacklist, and pursue civil restitution under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine. If the TP adjustment is accompanied by fabricated contracts or fake invoices, the directors face criminal fraud or tax evasion prosecution with imprisonment of up to 7 years.
AML / Shell Company Structure and Failure to Report Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines on the institutions and responsible individuals, and the State Administration for Market Regulation may revoke the FIP's registration. The PBOC may freeze the bank accounts, and the SAT will jointly punish the entity and its directors through blacklisting and denial of all tax benefits. If the conduct crosses into money laundering, PRC Criminal Law Article 191 provides imprisonment of 5 to 10 years and confiscation of property. The China-Hong Kong DTA relief will be denied, and the unpaid tax becomes immediately assessable at 10% to 25%.

Director personal liability: Directors who used the shell to conceal ownership or to launder the proceeds of the SaaS business lose limited liability protection. Civil liability requires full restitution of the remitted amounts, interest, penalties and legal costs. Directors face travel bans and passport seizure, personal asset freezes, blacklisting, and imprisonment under both tax evasion and money laundering statutes. Foreign resident directors may be barred from entering China and their home jurisdictions may be notified through mutual legal assistance channels.
