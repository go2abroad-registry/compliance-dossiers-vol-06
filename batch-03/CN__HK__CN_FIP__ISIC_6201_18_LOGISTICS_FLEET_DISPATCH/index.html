VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No monetary VAT-registration threshold: a PRC-incorporated FIP must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance. General VAT taxpayer registration becomes mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are currently VAT-exempt for ordinary sales but still must be registered.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT under the VAT Law applies to exported goods physically leaving the PRC customs territory and to qualifying cross-border services, including the FIP logistics SaaS and telematics services, where the recipient is located wholly outside the PRC, the services are consumed and performed outside the PRC, and the overseas recipient bears the service cost. The FIP must maintain contemporaneous records showing place of supply, place of performance, overseas customer identity, service delivery evidence, and direct receipt of funds from outside the PRC.

  • PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration Form for physical goods shipments
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill or other transport documentation evidencing export from the PRC
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation or export proceeds verification record from a SAFE-authorized bank
  • Signed cross-border service agreement with the offshore customer and scope and delivery memorandum
  • E-invoice or VAT invoice for the export transaction
  • Telematics and SaaS delivery evidence such as customer dashboard access logs and route-data server records demonstrating performance and consumption outside the PRC

The FIP must issue electronic invoices through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. VAT special invoices and VAT ordinary invoices are generated in OFD format with a cryptographic anti-counterfeiting two-dimensional code and real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA. The fully digitalized electronic invoice process is mandatory for general VAT taxpayers in the relevant pilot provinces and is required for cross-border export documentation. The platform supports API-based system-to-system integration for the FIP telematics SaaS billing engine, but every invoice must be digitally signed and stamped before it is legally valid.

If the STA disallows the zero-rating, the FIP is assessed the uncollected output VAT at the applicable rate, plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and an administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export claims, forged customs or foreign-exchange documents, or abuse of Hong Kong-related offshore arrangements trigger retrospective clawback of any VAT refund, denial of future simplified export-refund procedures, and potential criminal liability for tax evasion or fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. In fraud cases the STA may recover the tax without a statute-of-limitations bar.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Control Regulations, the CNY is not freely convertible on the capital account, while current-account items are convertible once the underlying transaction is verified as genuine. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (CN_FIP) established in mainland China must treat inbound partner capital contributions as foreign direct investment (FDI) and complete SAFE foreign exchange registration before the remitting bank will credit a dedicated capital account. After the conversion of the registered FDI capital into RMB, the funds may be used only for permitted operating expenditures within the partnership's registered business scope; prohibited deployments include entrusted loans, wealth-management products, acquisition of equity or real estate outside the approved scope, and repayment of pre-existing domestic RMB debt with no direct project link. Profit distributions to non-resident partners are current-account remittances but face strict documentary hurdles: the remitting bank requires a non-resident income tax withholding certificate, audited financial statements, a partnership profit-distribution resolution, and evidence that the distribution is not a disguised capital withdrawal. In the Hong Kong SAR leg, the HKD is fully convertible under the HKMA currency-board discipline; no surrender, conversion approval, or profit-remittance restriction applies to HKD/USD transactions. However, any conversion between onshore CNY and offshore HKD/CNH must be executed at a SAFE-licensed bank, with the true purpose code stated on the SWIFT instruction, and the bank must monitor the operation against the FIP's actual logistics-software business activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a mainland-incorporated FIP, all export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to the PRC through a designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days from the date of export declaration or completion of the exported service, unless SAFE has approved an overseas retention facility. There is no fixed statutory conversion percentage for retained export FX since the 2007 current-account foreign-exchange retention reform; the bank will establish a current-account FX retention quota based on the FIP's actual trade receipts over the previous 12 months. Any FX proceeds above that approved retention quota must be sold to a designated foreign exchange bank for CNY, effectively a mandatory 100% conversion of the excess. Advance export receipts must be booked through the designated bank's receipt registration and the underlying export must be completed within the normal trade period of not more than 180 days unless extended with SAFE approval. For the Hong Kong SAR treasury entity, no surrender rules apply: HKD/USD proceeds may be held freely offshore, and no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed by the HKMA.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the banking and monetary regulator for the Hong Kong SAR treasury node; and for the mainland-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the fx-control authority. The HKMA operates the HKD Linked Exchange Rate System and does not impose exchange controls on HKD/USD flows, while PBOC/SAFE controls CNY convertibility and capital-account transactions on the mainland.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital banks and fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, Stripe Treasury, or Neat are not designated foreign exchange banks under SAFE and therefore cannot open or maintain the mandatory capital account, cannot perform FDI foreign exchange registration, cannot verify current-account trade documents, and cannot act as the issuing bank for commercial letters of credit. In Hong Kong, non-bank payment institutions are regulated either as licensed banks, restricted licensed banks, deposit-taking companies, or Stored Value Facility (SVF) licensees; an SVF or EMI may process e-commerce collections but is not permitted to hold the operational treasury of a cross-border FIP as a full bank. Therefore, a HKMA-licensed Tier-1 bank must be the settlement and clearing bank. Stripe may be used only as a collection front-end for the SaaS invoicing and subscription business, with mandatory daily settlement sweeping into the licensed bank. Airwallex may be used for low-value ancillary cross-currency payments from a Hong Kong treasury entity only to the extent permitted by its Hong Kong money service operator and payment institution licences, but it cannot replace the SAFE-compliant mainland banking relationship.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All FDI capital contributions to the CN_FIP from the Hong Kong treasury entity must be remitted by SWIFT to the FIP's SAFE-designated foreign-currency capital account at a licensed mainland bank. The remittance must include the beneficiary exactly as registered in the FDI registration, a narrative 'FIP capital contribution - [purpose code]', and the bank-approved SAFE transaction purpose code for a foreign-invested partnership capital subscription. The receiving bank will validate the FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Number, book the funds as frozen capital, and upon presentation of the FIP's capital-account settlement application with genuine contracts, invoices, or lease agreements, convert the foreign currency to RMB at the onshore spot rate and credit the basic RMB account. A PRC CPA must issue a capital verification report to be filed with the local Administration for Market Regulation. All in-country RMB spend must comply with the negative list and SAFE circular restrictions; prohibited uses include wealth management, entrusted loans, and repayment of unrelated domestic loans. If the mainland FIP instead draws an intercompany loan from the HK entity, the loan contract must be registered as foreign debt with SAFE and the proceeds must enter a separate external-debt account with matching maturity and currency-mix eligibility.For outbound operating disbursements, the mainland FIP will instruct its designated bank to purchase FX at onshore spot after submitting the underlying contract, commercial invoice, tax payment record, and service-trade remittance application; the bank will send a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for high-value settlement to the beneficiary bank using the appropriate service-trade purpose code. Trade-based payments for telematics hardware or fleet infrastructure can be routed through a sight or usance commercial letter of credit issued by the mainland bank in favor of the overseas supplier, or through documentary collections

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary treasury rail. Offshore digital banks and fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, Stripe Treasury, or Neat are not designated foreign exchange banks under SAFE and therefore cannot open or maintain the mandatory capital account, cannot perform FDI foreign exchange registration, cannot verify current-account trade documents, and cannot act as the issuing bank for commercial letters of credit. In Hong Kong, non-bank payment institutions are regulated either as licensed banks, restricted licensed banks, deposit-taking companies, or Stored Value Facility (SVF) licensees; an SVF or EMI may process e-commerce collections but is not permitted to hold the operational treasury of a cross-border FIP as a full bank. Therefore, a HKMA-licensed Tier-1 bank must be the settlement and clearing bank. Stripe may be used only as a collection front-end for the SaaS invoicing and subscription business, with mandatory daily settlement sweeping into the licensed bank. Airwallex may be used for low-value ancillary cross-currency payments from a Hong Kong treasury entity only to the extent permitted by its Hong Kong money service operator and payment institution licences, but it cannot replace the SAFE-compliant mainland banking relationship.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 4; however, the Foreign-Invested Partnership itself is fiscally transparent and is not a CIT taxpayer; the 25% rate applies to corporate partners on their allocated partnership income)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

China's statutory WHT on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise is 10% (EIT Law Article 27 and Implementation Regulation Article 91). Under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA, the rate is reduced to 5% for a beneficial owner that is a Hong Kong-resident company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the payer. For a pass-through FIP, profit distributions to partners are not dividends; instead, the allocated income is taxed at the partner level: 25% net basis if a PRC PE exists, or 10% gross basis if the partner has no PE and no treaty relief.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Related-party transaction reporting and Local File preparation are mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million for intercompany services and other transactions, RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers, and RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers (SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016). Master File and Country-by-Country Report may be required for larger multinational groups. A logistics SaaS FIP transacting with a Hong Kong parent should maintain full TP documentation even below these thresholds if the arrangement is high-risk.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations by name, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47, Implementation Regulation Article 123) and the SAT Beneficial Ownership Framework (Announcement No. 9 of 2018) require substantive economic activity. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) concept is applied through PE analysis under the China-HK DTA: if the Hong Kong partner merely holds the FIP interest while fleet dispatch, software development, and client contracting are carried out in mainland China, the Hong Kong entity will be found to lack CIGA and will be denied treaty benefits. The FIP must demonstrate that people functions, capital employment, and risk control are located where the income is reported.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Partnership Allocation

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (TCAL) Article 63 imposes recovery of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, and a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years for tax evasion if the amount involved and the percentage of unpaid tax meet criminal thresholds.

Director personal liability: The managing partner or executive officer who directs the false allocation can be held jointly and severally liable for the FIP's unpaid tax and penalties under TCAL Article 8 and the Supreme People's Court interpretation on corporate veil piercing. The individual is also subject to an exit ban (travel restriction) and inclusion on the Joint Punishment List for Persons with Serious Tax Violations, which restricts credit, banking services, and the ability to serve as a senior executive. Criminal liability: imprisonment of 3 to 7 years for intentional tax evasion.
DTA Abuse / Beneficial Ownership / Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018 and the GAAR provisions of EIT Law Article 47 plus Implementation Regulation Article 123, treaty benefits are denied, the transaction is recharacterized as a direct payment to the ultimate Chinese principal, and additional tax is assessed at 10% on gross income or 25% on net income if a PE is attributed. Late interest and a 50% to 150% special tax adjustment penalty may also apply. The withholding agent will be ordered to pay any shortfall in WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who execute the treaty-shopping arrangement are jointly and severally liable as withholding agents for the unpaid WHT under TCAL Article 69, including penalties of 50% to 300% of the unpaid amount. The responsible individuals face blacklisting for serious tax violations, travel bans, disqualification from serving as directors in mainland China, and, where false declarations are intentional, criminal liability for tax evasion or tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 205.
Shell Company / Transfer Pricing Abuse / Disguised Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: PRC Special Tax Adjustment Measures (SAT Announcement No. 6 of 2017) and EIT Law Article 41 permit the tax authority to restate the IP transfer at fair market value, deny the royalty deduction, impose a 5-year back-assessment or 10-year period for willful evasion, and tax the adjusted profit at 25%. The cross-border fund movement without SAFE approval also triggers Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Article 39, which requires confiscation of the illegal exchange and a fine of 30% to 100% of the illegal amount. Penalties for tax evasion under TCAL Article 63 may reach 500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Chinese courts may pierce the partnership veil under the Partnership Enterprises Law Article 39, holding the managing partner personally liable for all unpaid debts of the FIP with personal assets. Directors of the Hong Kong shell are subject to civil restitution to the tax authority, travel bans and blacklisting on the Credit China platform, and, if the foreign exchange or tax evasion is intentional, criminal prosecution for tax evasion or illegal foreign exchange offenses, with imprisonment terms up to 10 years in aggravated cases.
