Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the FIP is not a separate taxpayer for Hong Kong Profits Tax; each partner is assessed on their allocated share of partnership profits, while the partnership must file an information-bearing Profits Tax Return for partnerships.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election; assessability is governed by the territorial source principle under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and a taxpayer can only substantiate an offshore-claim position on the facts.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Hong Kong SAR levies no VAT, GST, or sales-and-use tax; the only indirect consumption duties are selective excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on liquors, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Hong Kong; every business must obtain a Business Registration Certificate, which is a business registration, not an indirect-tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on exports, so there is no 0% export VAT claim. For Profits Tax, exported goods or qualifying offshore digital services (EMR SaaS) are exempt from tax only if the profits do not arise in or derive from Hong Kong and are not derived from a trade, profession, or business carried on in Hong Kong (Inland Revenue Ordinance section 14). Substantiation requires proving that the contracts were negotiated, executed and performed outside Hong Kong, with no local staff, office, or sales force involved, and that consideration was paid outside Hong Kong.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China administers a managed and only partially convertible FX system. Current account payments and receipts are generally permitted for bona fide trade in goods and services, but banks must verify invoices, contracts, customs declarations and BOP declarations. Capital account operations, including foreign direct investment into a Foreign-Invested Partnership, partnership equity transfers, foreign currency loans and liquidation proceeds, are not freely convertible; they require registration with SAFE or SAFE-designated banks, use of designated capital accounts, and strict compliance with the Foreign Investment Information Report system. The Hong Kong investor may inject capital only into a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, after which the FIP may convert those funds to RMB for permitted business purposes. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is allowed only after the FIP completes its annual statutory audit, files tax returns and proves the profit is genuinely distributable, with withholding or clearance certificates submitted to the paying bank. Additional frictions arise from rules prohibiting capital funds from being used for speculative domestic investments, entrusted loans, or non-business equity investments. As a result, the FIP must treat convertibility as a controlled privilege, not an unencumbered right.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE has abolished mandatory export-proceeds surrender. An FIP that exports EMR software or receives cross-border cloud/technology service fees may retain the full amount of foreign currency in a domestic Foreign Exchange Settlement Account or convert 100% to RMB at a designated bank; there is no statutory minimum conversion ratio. The legal obligation is to repatriate and declare the proceeds: for goods export, payment must normally be received within 90 days from customs declaration; for services, the invoice and contract must show a reasonable payment cycle, and any longer credit period must be reported through the Trade/Service Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Enterprises failing to meet collection timelines must submit a revised collection plan and supporting documentation, while retained foreign currency remains subject to the genuine-transaction and non-speculation rules.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI accounts are legally barred from serving as the primary banking or foreign exchange clearing account for a mainland China FIP. Institutions such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or other Hong Kong/UK-licensed EMIs do not possess a PBOC commercial banking license and cannot open a domestic RMB or foreign currency account in the FIP's own name. They may be used only as an ancillary merchant collection gateway for overseas clinic B2B payments, and even then final settlement must be routed into a licensed tier-1 commercial bank account in mainland China. The regulatory feasibility rating is Low for core treasury clearing; Medium for supply-chain receivables collection where the EMI is contractually obliged to batch-settle to the FIP's bank account. Relying solely on an EMI for Stage 4 clearing would fail SAFE's entity account and capital verification requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Use SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong investor to the FIP's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a SAFE-designated tier-1 bank. The remittance must include the correct SAFE/BOP transaction classification for foreign direct investment capital injection. Before opening the account, the FIP must complete the Foreign Investment Information Report, file the partnership agreement through the relevant market regulation system, and provide the Hong Kong investor's incorporation and KYC documents. After receipt, the bank verifies the transaction in the Foreign Investment Information System and releases funds to the RMB basic account only for the business scope; capital verification by a PRC CPA must be completed and the amount recorded in the FIP's books. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI accounts are legally barred from serving as the primary banking or foreign exchange clearing account for a mainland China FIP. They may be used only as an ancillary merchant collection gateway for overseas clinic B2B payments, and even then final settlement must be routed into a licensed tier-1 commercial bank account in mainland China. The regulatory feasibility rating is Low for core treasury clearing; Medium for supply-chain receivables collection where the EMI is contractually obliged to batch-settle to the FIP's bank account. Relying solely on an EMI for Stage 4 clearing would fail SAFE's entity account and capital verification requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong requires a Local File and Master File where total consolidated revenue is HKD 400,000,000 or more and controlled transactions exceed HKD 5,000,000 with non-Hong Kong associates (or HKD 30,000,000 with Hong Kong associates). For the PRC side, under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Local File is required where related-party transactions exceed RMB 10,000,000 for tangible property transfers, RMB 40,000,000 for intangible property transfers, or RMB 10,000,000 for services; a Master File is required where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 100,000,000. Cross-border intercompany arrangements must be monitored against both thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a statutory Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) regime equivalent to BVI/Cayman; however, the IRD applies economic substance scrutiny to treaty benefit claims and requires the beneficial owner to have substantive operations. For an FIP, the IRD will examine whether core income-generating activities (CIGA) for EMR SaaS are performed in Hong Kong. In the PRC, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 permits re-characterization of structures lacking commercial substance. The PRC-HK DTA requires substance for beneficial ownership; a mere shell or conduit will be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / willful submission of false profits tax return or false offshore claim

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82(1), willful tax evasion with intent to defraud is punishable by a fine of HKD 10,000, a further fine of three times the tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to three years. Under IRO s.80(2), failure to file without reasonable excuse attracts a fine of HKD 10,000 and additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged. In the PRC, under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63, a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax underpaid may apply; criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 203 applies where underpaid tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes 10% of the tax due, with fines and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: A partnership has no directors; each partner is jointly and severally liable for partnership debts and taxes. A partner who commits tax evasion may be personally subject to fines and imprisonment. In the PRC, the general partner is jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law Article 39; personal assets may be seized. Travel bans (blacklisting) may be imposed for unpaid tax liabilities exceeding RMB 100,000. Criminal imprisonment of up to three years in Hong Kong and up to seven years in China may apply.
Transfer pricing violation / non-arm's-length intercompany pricing and failure to maintain TP documentation

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.51F and s.51G, failure to prepare and retain TP documentation results in penalties of HKD 50,000 on first conviction and HKD 100,000 on subsequent convictions, plus up to three times tax undercharged. Under PRC Special Tax Adjustment Rules, the tax authority may adjust income and impose interest at benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points (first year) or 8 percentage points (subsequent years), plus penalties of 50% to 5 times tax underpaid under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63. The IRD may also issue additional assessments of up to six years for fraudulent misstatements.

Director personal liability: For an FIP, the general partner or managing partner who knowingly signs false tax computations or TP documentation may be personally assessed for undercharged tax and interest. Under PRC law, the general partner is jointly and severally liable with the partnership; personal assets may be pursued. Directors or managing partners may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving China where unpaid tax exceeds thresholds. Criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 may result in imprisonment up to seven years and fines.
Beneficial ownership abuse / treaty shopping / false DTA claim without substance

Statutory sanction: Under PRC GAAR (EIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Articles 120-123) and STA Announcement 2018 No. 9, treaty benefits may be denied and the conduit re-characterized as a mainland China resident, imposing 10% withholding on gross income plus late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and penalties of 50% to 5 times tax underpaid. In Hong Kong, under IRO s.61 and s.61A, the IRD may disregard artificial transactions and assess as if the transaction had not occurred. Criminal prosecution under IRO s.82 may follow for false tax residency declarations, with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Individuals controlling the conduit partnership, including nominal partners or beneficial owners, may be personally liable for restructured tax assessments, interest, and penalties. Under PRC Partnership Enterprise Law Article 39 and Company Law Article 20, veil-piercing may make shareholders or partners personally liable for unpaid tax. Criminal exposure under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 or 203 may lead to imprisonment up to seven years, fines up to five times tax evaded, and travel bans preventing exit from mainland China until settlement.
