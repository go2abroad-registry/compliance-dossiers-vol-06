Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must have a physical registered office in the Mainland PRC evidenced by a lease agreement, property ownership certificate, or lettable use certificate issued by the local sub-district office. A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for initial SAMR registration nor for tax registration with the State Taxation Administration; local tax authorities may reject a virtual address because daily management of a helpdesk SaaS business requires personnel and equipment at that address. However, if the FIP lawfully obtains a 'registered address + tax receipt address' split under certain pilot free trade zones (e.g., Shanghai FTZ or Qianhai) permitting paid shared-office administrative addresses, such arrangement is permissible only if the FIP also leases a physical operation or cloud-serviced fulfillment center and posts a conspicuous sign. Banks will likely reject a virtual office because the required face-to-face interview and original documentary inspection under PBOC Order No. 3 requires physical presence; the FIP must present the actual place of business lease and utility bills to validate the address. The Hong Kong holding entity, by contrast, may use a registered office service under Section 658 of the Companies Ordinance, but that is separate and does not cure the Mainland FIP physical address requirement.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; registration and filings are executed through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portals linked to the STA's national online taxpayer service system

E-government filing portal

After the Foreign-Invested Partnership is registered with the local office of the State Administration for Market Regulation under the Registration Administration Measures for Foreign-Invested Partnerships, tax registration is effected through the Multi-License Integration reform: the Unified Social Credit Code printed on the business license is automatically activated as the taxpayer identification number for VAT and income tax purposes. Within 30 days of license issuance, the executive partner or authorized agent must complete the new-taxpayer onboarding process on the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau, comprising real-name authentication, taxpayer status election (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer), financial and accounting system filing, bank deposit account reporting, payroll and social insurance filing, and e-invoice quota and format authorization. A foreign non-resident partner is not issued a separate Chinese TIN solely for holding a partnership interest; its home-jurisdiction TIN and tax residence certificate are recorded in the FIP registration file for treaty relief and distribution withholding. If a foreign non-resident partner later establishes a taxable presence or permanent establishment in China, it must separately complete non-resident taxpayer registration with the competent STA office.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available for cross-border helpdesk/SaaS, software, and information-technology services supplied to customers outside mainland China (including Hong Kong SAR) under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36, Annex 4, read with STA implementing measures for cross-border taxable services, provided the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the recipient is an overseas entity or individual, the consideration is received from outside China, and the FIP maintains contemporaneous documentary evidence. If the service does not qualify for zero-rating, exempt treatment may still be available for certain offshore outsourcing categories; the FIP must verify the exact service code and applicable STA classification before claiming the zero rate.

  • Pure helpdesk/SaaS services: no customs export declaration or bill of lading is required because no tangible goods leave PRC customs jurisdiction; electronic delivery records substitute for customs clearance
  • If the FIP also exports physical devices or embedded software media, the mandatory bundle must include the customs export declaration (export goods customs declaration), the ocean bill of lading or airway bill, and the bank export proceeds settlement receipt
  • Cross-border services contract and statement of work evidencing the overseas customer, service description, billing terms, and offshore consumption location
  • Bank foreign-exchange repatriation proof: the authorized bank's export collection/remittance receipt and the foreign-exchange income declaration report showing settlement of the export proceeds
  • Technical audit trail: helpdesk ticket logs, conversational-bot interaction statistics, server log extracts, and customer acceptance certificates proving service performance and consumption outside mainland China

The FIP must issue invoices through the STA's digital fully digitalized e-invoice platform and the VAT Invoice Management System. Under the nationwide fully digitalized e-invoice model, each invoice is assigned a tax-authority-controlled digital invoice number, issued in the uniform OFD/XML data format, transmitted to the STA in near real time, and cleared by the STA's fiscal anti-counterfeiting system without a physical countersignature. General VAT taxpayers must additionally process special VAT invoices through the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform for credit certification. Legacy fiscal control devices and tax-control U-keys remain recognized for existing taxpayers, but new registrants in most pilot regions are onboarded on the fully digitalized e-invoice channel.

The STA and its local tax bureaus audit export zero-rating claims through the export tax rebate control system and invoice-flow anomaly analysis. Failure to produce the required documentation within the repatriation period, or use of forged or inconsistent supporting documents, triggers clawback of the zero-rated VAT amount, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, administrative fines from 50% to five times the tax amount under the PRC Tax Administration Law, suspension or revocation of export tax refund qualifications, and referral to public security authorities for criminal liability for export-rebate fraud and false-invoice crimes. The executive general partner and ordinary partners may be jointly liable for the FIP's unpaid tax and penalties to the extent provided by partnership law and the applicable tax agreement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, RMB is substantially convertible on the current account for bona fide trade, service, dividend, interest and royalty transactions, consistent with the IMF Article VIII status of the currency. However, the capital account remains regulated. In the case of a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the entity must first complete SAFE foreign-exchange registration under the FDI framework at the receiving commercial bank. Foreign capital injection can then be credited to a designated capital settlement account and converted into RMB only for actual-use purposes that are permitted under the Foreign Investment Negative List. The conversion is a post-registration operation and it cannot take place before the use plan, the partnership deed and the FIP's compliance undertakings are accepted by the bank. Export earnings of the FIP must be repatriated to China before any internal distribution, while profit remittance to non-resident partners is generally permitted as a current-account outflow once corporate income tax, partnership-level pass-through tax, withholding tax and a tax clearance certificate are evidenced. Chinese banks will not authorise such remittance without a completed Balance of Payments reporting code, authenticated transaction documents and a valid tax payment receipt, especially where the beneficiary is a Hong Kong partner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts generated by the FIP must be repatriated to mainland China within the statutory monitoring time window applicable to goods exports, generally 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless an extended period has been approved by the local SAFE branch for documented longer financing terms. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero, meaning there is no statutory requirement to convert a fixed percentage into RMB. However, the proceeds must be pooled into the FIP's current-account foreign exchange account and may either be retained in foreign currency or converted into CNY at the bank's prevailing spot rate. Under the SAFE Trade Monitoring System, every receipt must be reconciled with customs data; if proceeds are held abroad without a valid approval or written waiver, SAFE will require late return and may impose monetary penalties, order the bank to stop handling the FIP's current-account business, and include the matter in the bank's compliance watch list.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the applicable offshore settlement authority for Hong Kong-dollar clearing channels

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for the mainland FIP as the primary treasury account holder. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie and Revolut Business do not maintain PBOC payment licences or SAFE foreign-exchange settlement licences and are legally barred from acting as the statutory corporate bank account of a PRC-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. Stripe Billing and PayPal are permitted only as global collection gateways, not as bank deposit or FX settlement rails, and any funds captured by these gateways must be repatriated into the FIP's onshore licensed bank account via a SAFE-regulated cross-border payment institution or the bank's single-window trade settlement module. A Hong Kong subsidiary or treasury vehicle may use EMIs and gateways more flexibly under HKMA requirements, but that offshore vehicle cannot substitute for the PRC account, and all profit or capital returns must still flow through the mainland FIP's SAFE-registered account before tax clearance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound RMB or USD capital injection from a Hong Kong bank to the mainland FIP must be remitted via SWIFT with the applicable SAFE Cross-Border Payment Transaction Code under the capital-account category for foreign direct investment liabilities, selecting the sub-category for foreign investor equity or partnership capital contribution. The remitter must be the partner named in the FIP's SAFE registration record. The receiving bank will credit the funds only to a designated capital settlement account; RMB conversion requires submission of the SAFE registration form, the utilisation plan, the notarised partnership agreement, and evidence of continuing compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List. If a capital verification report is required, an independent PRC CPA must verify the contribution within 30 days of receipt and file the capital verification conclusion with the local market-supervision authority. The funds must not be commingled with operating-account liquidity before the capital settlement account conversion is completed.Outbound settlement from the FIP should be routed through SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments, service fees, royalty remittances and small-value current-account disbursements where full authenticity documentation is available. For high-value goods imports with defined delivery dates and transport documents, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) is preferred because it provides documentary protection and bank-controlled settlement. Documentary Collections (DP/DA) are acceptable for low-value, trusted counterparties and tested repeat flows. All outbound transactions must be supported by an underlying contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration for goods, or tax payment certificates for services; the bank will assign the correct current-account or capital-account purpose code in the SAFE monitoring system and will halt any transfer where the documentation does not satisfy the real-transaction authenticity test.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts with a SAFE-licensed commercial bank, executed only on verified real-demand trade or current-account exposure and settled at maturity through a documented underlyingFX swap and cross-currency swap instruments used for the management of the FIP's capital-account timing gap or interest-rate exposure, subject to SAFE hedge-ratio rules and an ISDA-style master agreement with the bankPlain-vanilla FX options or zero-cost collar combinations approved by the licensed bank, with the hedge amount limited to the outstanding underlying exposure and with no speculative net open positionOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not a permitted instrument on the mainland FIP balance sheet; any NDF hedging must be executed by a separately capitalised Hong Kong treasury vehicle under HKMA regulations and cannot be booked against the PRC entity

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the mainland FIP as the primary treasury account holder. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie and Revolut Business do not maintain PBOC payment licences or SAFE foreign-exchange settlement licences and are legally barred from acting as the statutory corporate bank account of a PRC-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. Stripe Billing and PayPal are permitted only as global collection gateways, not as bank deposit or FX settlement rails, and any funds captured by these gateways must be repatriated into the FIP's onshore licensed bank account via a SAFE-regulated cross-border payment institution or the bank's single-window trade settlement module. A Hong Kong subsidiary or treasury vehicle may use EMIs and gateways more flexibly under HKMA requirements, but that offshore vehicle cannot substitute for the PRC account, and all profit or capital returns must still flow through the mainland FIP's SAFE-registered account before tax clearance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 0; no de minimis statutory threshold for Master File or Local File, but the IRD's Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (DIPN 59) and the transfer pricing (TP) rules under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 require contemporaneous documentation for all cross-border transactions unless the taxpayer is an SME eligible for reduced documentation; any cross-border transaction with an associated enterprise (excluding intra-Hong Kong transactions) must be supported by a profit attribution study and transfer pricing documentation showing alignment with the arm's length principle; maintain documents if total turnover exceeds HKD 100 million and intra-group transactions exceed HKD 10 million (for full compliance), otherwise a basic TP file is recommended.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not part of Hong Kong's statute; instead Hong Kong enforces substance through the DTA residence test, the general anti-avoidance rule (GAAR), the preferential regime for corporate treasury centres and aircraft leasing, and the OECD BEPS minimum standards; a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA), including management decisions, customer support operations, Helpdesk platform hosting and risk-taking, occur in Hong Kong; if the partnership is only a shell or has no physical presence, the IRD may disregard the entity, reassign the income to the substantive offshore entity, and deny treaty benefits on the ground of absence of beneficial ownership.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Sham Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) sections 61 and 61A: the IRD may disregard the artificial transaction, reduce the tax benefit, and assess profits of the substance-less entity as taxable Hong Kong income; IRD may impose additional tax of up to 100% of the undercharged tax under IRO section 82A; fraudulent evasion under IRO section 82 exposes the entity to a fine of HKD 50,000 and treble tax in addition to the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Under IRO section 82, directors, partners or controlling officers who knowingly and wilfully submit false returns or direct the sham structure are personally liable to imprisonment for up to 3 years, a fine of HKD 50,000, and treble tax; civil recovery under common law for breach of fiduciary duty; an Inland Revenue / Department of Justice travel restriction and blacklisting order may be issued preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong; if the arrangement also violates mainland Chinese tax law (EAT or GAAR), the director may face extended audit and extradition risk in a cross-border criminal tax proceeding.
Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Mispricing of Digital Services

Statutory sanction: Transfer pricing rules under IRO sections 50ABI to 50ABT and DIPN 59 allow the Inland Revenue to adjust the arm's length profit, disallow excess deductions, and re-characterise the intercompany payments; Hong Kong imposes a penalty of 10% or 100% of the tax undercharged for incorrect returns under IRO section 82A; in a Mainland-linked audit, the Chinese tax authority (SAT) may make secondary adjustment and charge a 5-year arrears interest of approximately 6-10% plus a 0.5% monthly late fee under Article 33 of the Measures for Special Tax Adjustments.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the tax return knowing that the intercompany pricing is misstated commit an offence under IRO section 82A and are subject to treble tax, a fine of up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years; the corporate veil may be pierced for negligence or fraudulent misrepresentation; the director may also be held personally liable for the civil damages of the Chinese foreign-invested enterprise's shareholder or lender under the PRC Company Law anti-fraud doctrine; an asset-preservation injunction and travel blacklist can be issued by both HK and mainland tax bureaus.
Tax Evasion and False Declaration / AML Conduit in Recurring Digital Payments

Statutory sanction: Fraudulent tax evasion under IRO section 82 carries a fine of HKD 50,000, treble the tax evaded, and imprisonment up to 3 years; making an incorrect return without reasonable excuse under IRO section 80(2) is a criminal offence subject to a fine of HKD 10,000 plus treble undercharged tax; for failing to maintain adequate records, Section 51C imposes a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment to 6 months; under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) and the United Nations (Anti-Terrorism Measures) Ordinance, money laundering carries imprisonment up to 14 years and a fine of HKD 5,000,000.

Director personal liability: Directors, tax representatives and compliance officers are personally criminally liable if they knowingly fail to file or assist in the false return; the court will pierce the corporate veil under common law and the PRC doctrine of separate legal personality may be ignored where the partnership is used as a façade for fraud, leaving the director exposed to full personal asset restitution, a court-civil penalty, travel ban (blacklisting) at the airport, and imprisonment for up to 14 years for money laundering; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority or Customs and Excise Department can impose a record-keeping and AML penalty, and the director's disqualification under the Companies Ordinance may extend to 15 years.
