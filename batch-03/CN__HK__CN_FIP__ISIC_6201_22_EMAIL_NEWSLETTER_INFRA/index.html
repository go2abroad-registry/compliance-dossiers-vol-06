Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permitted as the sole registered office for a Foreign-Invested Partnership under Hong Kong law. The Companies Registry requires a physical address in Hong Kong where legal documents can be served. The Inland Revenue Department and banks will reject a virtual office as the principal place of business for tax registration and bank account opening, particularly for an email infrastructure platform that processes payment card data. A serviced office or co-working space with a dedicated meeting room, physical signage, and a named person authorized to receive legal process may be accepted. However, a pure virtual commercial mailbox (e.g., a mailbox at a commercial mail receiving agency without a street-level reception) will be treated as non-compliant. For this industry, due to the need to meet PCI DSS requirements, client contracts, and LLM-based merchant underwriting, the IRD and the HKMA expect a physical office location or at minimum an office address controlled by the general partner. The partnership should lease a serviced office with a minimum term of 12 months, maintain a physical server/co-location contract (even if cloud-based), or at least enter into a local data processing agreement that demonstrates commercial substance. Banks will conduct a site visit for a new FIP opening an account; a virtual mailbox fails that inspection. Therefore, virtual office may be tolerated for the name reservation and incorporation stage, but pre-incorporation due diligence must plan for immediate transition to a physical address before bank account opening and tax filings.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Regulations on Establishment of Foreign-Invested Partnership Enterprises impose no statutory minimum paid-up capital. Partners must agree on the amount, form, valuation and payment deadline of their capital contributions in the partnership agreement. There is no blocked escrow account requirement for a general FIP formation, except that non-cash contributions must be fairly valued, and any inbound foreign-exchange contribution by a non-resident partner or Hong Kong partner must be channelled through a domestic foreign-exchange-designated bank and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the foreign direct investment information rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); online electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: If the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is constituted as a limited partnership, register it with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37) to obtain the certificate of registration. Step 2: Apply for Business Registration with the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business; the application can be made through eTAX or by paper and must be accompanied by the partnership agreement, limited partnership registration certificate (if any), registered office address, and particulars of all partners, including the overseas tax identification numbers of non-resident partners where available. Step 3: The IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN); the BRN is the entity-level tax identification number of the FIP for business registration, trade declarations, import/export matters, and IRD correspondence. Step 4: For profits tax, the FIP is fiscally transparent; the partnership itself is not the taxpayer. Each partner, including each non-resident partner, is separately chargeable on its share of Hong Kong-source profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A non-resident partner without an existing Hong Kong tax file must apply to the IRD through eTAX for a Tax Reference Number (TIN) so that the partner can file returns and pay tax attributable to its partnership share. Step 5: Maintain the BRC and renew it annually on the statutory renewal date; notify the IRD within the prescribed period of any change in partnership particulars, partners, address, or cessation of business.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax. Indirect taxes in Hong Kong are limited to specific regimes such as duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117), neither of which applies to the cross-border email newsletter infrastructure business.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax law and therefore no turnover threshold for indirect-tax registration. A Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 is required once business is carried on in Hong Kong, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for indirect-tax purposes because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating to claim and no input VAT refund mechanism. For profits tax, the territorial-source principle under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) applies: income from services is taxable only if the profit arises in or is derived from Hong Kong. The bulk transactional email API and newsletter hosting platform should retain objective evidence that key profit-generating activities, including contract negotiation, service delivery, and client acceptance, occur outside Hong Kong to support an offshore profits treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China is not fully capital-account convertible: RMB is convertible on current-account items such as legitimate trade and services, while capital transactions such as direct investment, intercompany loans, equity contributions and profit repatriation require SAFE registration, designated bank routing, purpose-code declaration and underlying documentary evidence. Hong Kong SAR is fully convertible under the Linked Exchange Rate System and does not restrict capital or current account inflows, outflows, HKD conversion or foreign-currency retention. Hong Kong also imposes no surrender requirements on export receipts. Profit remittance from a China-based foreign-invested partnership to foreign partners requires the partnership to settle applicable PRC taxes, complete SAFE foreign-exchange verification for the income distribution, and provide audited financial statements or tax payment vouchers. Remittance from Hong Kong to the China parent is unrestricted at the HKMA level but will be reviewed by SAFE once received onshore. The practical hurdle is not HKD convertibility but rather the documentation around ultimate beneficial ownership, underlying commercial contracts, source of funds, economic substance and tax treatment, especially where the partnership elects pass-through taxation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory export-proceeds surrender rules and no mandatory conversion ratio; all export foreign-currency receipts are freely deposit-able, retainable and convertible. For the Mainland China leg, export trade receipts must be repatriated through a SAFE-designated bank and are generally required to be collected no later than 90 days after the export customs declaration unless SAFE-approved extension applies; conversion of those receipts into RMB is voluntary rather than mandatory, subject to the trade verification system and anti-circumvention supervision.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), for onshore CNY control; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central bank and foreign exchange authority for Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Medium as a primary treasury clearing rail. Offshore fintech EMIs incorporated outside Hong Kong and not licensed under the Banking Ordinance cannot lawfully provide Hong Kong domestic clearing, local currency accounts, trade finance or FX hedging to this entity. Hong Kong virtual banks with a full HKMA banking licence can support basic collections and digital onboarding, but they are not a substitute for Tier-1 commercial bank clearing for cross-border capital injections, documentary collections and wholesale FX execution. Merchant-of-record services such as Stripe Billing may be used as a complementary payment aggregator for subscription revenue, but the operating account, capital escrow and trade settlement facilities must rest with a fully licensed HKMA-authorised bank.

Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), an independent certified CPA audit is mandatory for a private company that fails to qualify as a small company. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 50,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions threshold in a year of assessment (or HKD 3,000,000 for loan/financing transactions) triggers mandatory Local File preparation for a Hong Kong entity; Master File is required when group global revenue exceeds HKD 6,000,000,000 with cross-border controlled transactions exceeding HKD 500,000,000; non-resident partners must maintain sufficient documentation showing arm's-length pricing for email infrastructure services, newsletter hosting, API subscription fees, licensing and intra-group marketing support; failure to file a TP report when notified attracts HK$50,000 – HKD 200,000 penalties plus a daily fine and potential TPD adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but economic substance is a decisive factor in obtaining offshore profit exemption and treaty relief; the IRD will scrutinise whether core income-producing activities (CIGA) taking place in Hong Kong include strategic decision-making, contract execution, IP ownership substance, staff control, and server infrastructure for the SaaS email newsletter platform. A Hong Kong FIP acting as a mere invoice conduit while all CIGA are performed elsewhere will be denied offshore treatment and may be considered a pure shell lacking beneficial ownership for China/HK DTA benefits; IRD may apply s.61A (general anti-avoidance) to disregard artificial arrangements; directors/partners must ensure the partnership maintains a Hong Kong office, local employees, independent management, and adequate capital to support the transactional volume.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82A(1): on summary conviction, a fine of HK$10,000 and imprisonment for 6 months; on indictment, a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years plus 3 times the amount of tax evaded; IRD may also issue estimated assessments and recover 10% default surcharge under s.82C.

Director personal liability: Under s.82B IRO, the court can make a director or partnership general partner personally liable for unpaid penalties and can issue a departure prevention order / travel ban; civil asset restitution for evaded taxes; on conviction for fraud, director faces disqualification from managing a company under the Companies Winding Up Ordinance and criminal imprisonment for up to 14 years if conspiracy to defraud is charged.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Sections 50AA and 51C(3) IRO; IRD may make transfer pricing adjustments increasing assessable profits and impose a fine of HK$50,000 for non-compliance with a notice; additional assessments may carry a 10% surcharge; intentional non-compliance with substantial net tax understated leads to prosecution under s.82A (tax evasion) with up to 3 years' imprisonment and triple tax penalty.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or causes the filing of a false TP declaration is personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and penalties; IRD can pierce the corporate veil and seek restitution from the individual; directors in flagrant cases may be subject to an absolute disqualification order and a travel restriction issued by the High Court; criminal imprisonment for directors for aiding and abetting evastion can reach 3 years.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse & AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61A anti-abandon has retroactive effect and allows IRD to ignore the shell arrangement and tax the profits at 16.5%; for money laundering aspects the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO) s.25 punishes persons who deal with crime proceeds – penalty up to HK$500,000 and 14 years' imprisonment; failure to register a foreign-controlled business with the Companies Registry could expose the directors to a fine of HK$50,000 plus a daily default fine; if the shell is used to facilitate tax evasion for a PRC beneficiary, Chinese tax authorities can apply CFC and anti-abuse rules under tax treaties.

Director personal liability: The court may impose personal civil liability for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties, as well as an order to repay any funds extracted from the shell; directors can be blacklisted (travel restriction) under s.82B IRO; criminal charges for conspiracy to defraud the revenue can lead to imprisonment up to 10 years; under OSCO, directors who knowingly launder proceeds receive up to 14 years' imprisonment and forfeiture of their personal assets; the corporate veil can be pierced on grounds of sham and fraud.
