Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic registration and filing portal: eTAX (https://www.ird.gov.hk/etax); the Business Registration Office under the IRD administers the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Hong Kong SAR is a separate tax and customs jurisdiction from Mainland China; the State Taxation Administration of China has no authority over Hong Kong profits tax, business registration, or indirect tax.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong does not obtain a VAT ID or TIN from a central registry. The functional tax identifier is the Business Registration Number issued by the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Procedure: (1) within one month of commencing a business in Hong Kong, submit the prescribed business registration application via the IRD Business Registration e-Service in eTAX/GovHK or in person at the Business Registration Office; (2) provide the foreign entity constitutive documents, all partners full names, nationality or incorporation country, passport or certificate numbers, principal place of business, description of the SEO/MarTech-SaaS activity, estimated turnover, and expected business duration; (3) if the partnership elects limited liability, register with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or the Limited Partnerships Fund Ordinance, as applicable, and attach the certificate issued by the Companies Registry to the IRD application; (4) receive the Business Registration Certificate and the 8-digit Business Registration Number from IRD; (5) display the certificate at the Hong Kong place of business and file an amendment with the IRD within the statutory notice period for any change in partners, business address, nature of business, or cessation. No VAT/GST registration is required because Hong Kong does not levy a general value-added tax, goods and services tax, or sales and use tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a Hong Kong partnership is not a separate taxpayer. Profits tax is computed on the partnership assessable profits and each partner is assessed in their individual capacity on their share according to the partnership agreement; losses are similarly allocated.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in Hong Kong. The only relevant statutory election is the Personal Assessment election under Part VII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), which individual partners may make to aggregate partnership profits with salaries and property income and to claim personal allowances. Partnerships may also submit agreed apportionments of partnership profits and capital allowances to the IRD under the partnership return.

Not_Applicable_No_VAT_GST_Threshold_HKD_0; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax and therefore no economic turnover threshold triggering indirect-tax registration. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), any person carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for business registration from the date of commencement, irrespective of turnover, unless a specific exemption in the ordinance applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable; Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, so there is no statutory 0% export VAT rate, no input tax credit, and no VAT export refund mechanism. For profits tax, income derived by a Hong Kong partnership from the export of software-as-a-service and SEO analytics is outside the scope of Hong Kong profits tax if the profit-producing activities are performed outside Hong Kong, as explained in the IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes No. 21 (DIPN 21); this offshore income treatment is an income-tax source question, not an indirect-tax zero-rating or refund.

  • No Hong Kong VAT export zero-rating or FIRC obligation applies because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST and no foreign-exchange controls; the documents below serve as supporting evidence for customs declarations and offshore profits tax claims
  • Importer/Exporter Registry Certificate issued under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) by the Trade and Industry Department, where goods are exported through Hong Kong
  • Electronic export trade declaration submitted through a Customs and Excise Department approved Electronic Data Interchange service provider for any physical export of goods
  • Bill of Lading, Master Air Waybill, or courier consignment note evidencing movement of goods from Hong Kong (if applicable; not required for pure SaaS/cloud analytics export)
  • Commercial invoices, subscription order forms, and executed SaaS service agreements identifying the overseas customer and place of supply
  • Bank credit advices and remittance documents evidencing receipt of export or service proceeds in the partnership Hong Kong bank account
  • DIPN 21 offshore-claim file containing contracts, negotiation records, server logs, and operating evidence demonstrating that the profit-producing activities occurred outside Hong Kong, where offshore profits treatment for profits tax is sought

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing API, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies to VAT/GST, because no VAT/GST exists. The IRD eTAX platform is used for electronic filing of profits tax, salaries tax, property tax, and employer returns, and the Business Registration Office issues digital Business Registration Certificates. For instruments subject to stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117), the IRD provides electronic stamping services, but this is not an indirect tax clearance mechanism. Records supporting business transactions must be retained as required by the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

There is no VAT export zero-rating or clawback to administer because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. False or unsupported trade declarations can be penalized by the Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E). For profits tax, a partnership or partner claiming offshore income treatment without adequate evidence can be audited by the IRD; additional tax may be assessed under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and penalties for incorrect returns without reasonable excuse or with willful intent to evade tax may run up to treble the amount of tax undercharged, in addition to potential criminal prosecution. The IRD also applies transfer-pricing and anti-avoidance provisions to recharacterize artificial arrangements for Hong Kong-sourced benefit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system with full convertibility for current account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement. The capital account remains heavily regulated, requiring SAFE certification for foreign direct investment, cross-border financing, and overseas outbound investments. For the foreign-invested partnership (FIP), registered capital in the form of foreign currency must be remitted into a designated domestic capital account and subsequently settled into RMB using real-transaction verification procedures. Although China abolished the mandatory surrender of export proceeds in 2012, all foreign currency receipts must be reported through the SAFE monitoring system and are subject to authenticity checks via contracts, invoices, and shipping or service delivery evidence. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong parent is fully permitted but requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and a documentary trail proving the declaration of income. Practically, the conversion of RMB profits into foreign exchange at prevailing market rates is usually possible within 1-3 business days once the compliance file is accepted, but any suspicious pattern (e.g., rapid round-tripping) will trigger KYC upgrades and delay. The overall assessment confirms that current account transactions are unimpeded, while capital flows (including equity injections and dividend remittances) are administratively monitored but not prohibited, provided the enterprise maintains a clean compliance record and submits the mandated reporting within 30 days of any transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As of the 2012 SAFE reform, there is no statutory mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds. The FIP may retain all foreign currency receipts or voluntarily convert them into RMB. However, all receipts must be declared in SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system within 5 working days of clearing, and the corresponding underlying trade/service vouchers (contracts, invoices, customs declarations or service completion certificates) must be retained for at least 10 years for post-verification audits.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Direct onboarding of the FIP with offshore EMI platforms such as Stripe or Paddle is not legally feasible because these entities lack a Chinese payment institution license issued by PBOC. The FIP must use a domestic bank or a licensed Chinese PSP (e.g., Alipay Global or UnionPay International) for inbound and outbound settlement. An alternative route is to channel settlement through the Hong Kong parent company, opening a merchant account on Stripe or Paddle in Hong Kong and then moving funds under a SAFE-registered intercompany service agreement. This adds operational friction and requires transfer pricing documentation, but remains legally viable. Hence the overall feasibility of direct fintech EMI service for the mainland FIP is rated Low, with a medium viability for an indirect Hong Kong-supported overlay.
