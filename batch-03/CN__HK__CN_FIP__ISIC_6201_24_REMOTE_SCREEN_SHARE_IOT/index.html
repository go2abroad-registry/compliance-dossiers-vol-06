Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with filings made through the STA Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform; for the Hong Kong SAR element, the separate competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the IRD e-Tax portal.

E-government filing portal

For a China-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP): first, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the Foreign-Invested Partnership Business License and the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which automatically becomes the PRC tax identification number; second, the FIP is required to activate tax registration in the STA e-Tax platform and ensure the registered scope covers remote screen-share/IoT telemetry software services; third, a foreign non-resident partner that has no fixed place of business in China must either obtain a non-resident tax identification number from the competent STA office or be subject to withholding filing by the partnership; the documentary package includes the foreign partner's certificate of incorporation, tax/residence certificate, notarized power of attorney if applicable, the partnership agreement, SAMR business license, and beneficial owner proof supporting any tax treaty claim. For the HK jurisdiction node, the FIP should apply to the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance for a Business Registration Certificate and may be required to file Profits Tax returns only if it has Hong Kong-sourced profits; the Hong Kong Business Registration Number is the documentary identity used on invoices issued from that node.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None under PRC law: China does not provide a US-style check-the-box election. The FIP is statutorily fiscally transparent; the partnership does not pay enterprise income tax, and each partner reports distributive share. Partnership allocations are respected only where they have substantial economic effect and genuinely reflect the partners' economic risk. Hybrid mismatch and anti-abuse rules may recharacterise cross-border payments or disallow double-dip outcomes. No entity-level C-corporation type election is available to a partnership.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (annual taxable sales) is the mandatory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale taxpayer with simplified levy, but VAT registration and the collection of Special Value-Added Tax Invoices are required once the threshold is exceeded or if voluntary registration is desirable for input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The FIP may claim zero-rating for qualifying exports of remote screen-share and IoT telemetry software services where the service is supplied to an overseas customer, the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China, the software is delivered purely by electronic download or cloud access, and consideration is paid in foreign currency and repatriated through a licensed bank. Pure software downloads do not require a customs declaration, but physical IoT hardware or software carriers leaving China qualify for the goods export zero-rate only with a valid customs export declaration; input VAT attributable to zero-rated exports is refundable, whereas VAT on exempt cross-border services would not be refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued inward remittance receipt
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract or purchase order
  • Chinese export VAT invoice (zero-rated export invoice)
  • State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) export collection record
  • Software delivery report, server log, IP address evidence, or remote session log for digital services

The Golden Tax IV system requires all VAT invoices, including Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoices (FDED), to be issued through the STA electronic invoicing platform. Invoice data must be transmitted to the tax authority in near-real-time, each invoice receives a unique tax-control number and digital stamp, and no offline or unauthorised local invoice can be used. B2B sales require Special Value-Added Tax Invoices to allow the buyer's input VAT credit, and each special invoice must be cleared and matched through the national e-invoice database.

If the export zero-rating is not supported at audit, the tax authority will restore output VAT at the applicable domestic rate (6% for services or 13% for goods) and impose late-payment interest at 0.05% per day. A false or fraudulent substantiation package may result in administrative fines equal to 50% to 500% of the tax underpayment, denial and clawback of any input VAT refund, and criminal liability for false VAT invoices or export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law, including custodial penalties in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating RMB regime with current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) may freely settle export/service proceeds, pay genuine trade-related expenses, and remit after-tax profits through the designated onshore account; prior approval is not required for such current-account transactions, but SAFE-licensed banks must verify authenticity on the basis of contracts, invoices, customs/VAT declarations and remittance applications. In contrast, capital-account convertibility is still controlled. Foreign partner capital contributions, equity transfers, intragroup loans and foreign-investment liquidation remittances require FDI foreign-exchange registration, capital account opening and post-registration; RMB settlement of foreign-invested partnership capital must be within the scope of the registration and may not be used for prohibited investments such as domestic equity lending. Profit remittance from the FIP is permitted after annual audit and Chinese tax clearance, and the bank will check the residence certificate, tax settlement certificate and payment order. Because Hong Kong is a separate SAR, cross-border flows between the HK parent and the mainland FIP are subject to the same SAFE filing requirements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio. The statutory rule is that export/service proceeds must be repatriated to onshore China and credited to the FIP's designated foreign exchange settlement account as soon as the exporting entity receives them, and in no event later than the contractual settlement maturity date; overdue receivables must be reported to the bank with a commercial justification. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent - the FIP may retain 100% of foreign currency in its settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB at the prevailing market rate. For remote software/IoT services, export proceeds are not supported by export customs declarations; the bank instead requires the executed master subscription agreement, invoices, evidence of service delivery, and the SAFE service trade payment code.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and its subordinate State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Under PBoC and SAFE rules, foreign fintech EMIs such as Stripe and Paddle are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot legally open or maintain settlement accounts for a Chinese FIP. They may be used only as overseas payment aggregators or billing front-ends; the FIP must repatriate collections through a licensed domestic acquiring bank and/or Tier-1 bank. Any arrangement where a foreign EMI holds the FIP's operating capital or settles its revenue through a commingled wallet would create severe unlicensed payment business and AML risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial capital contributions from the HK partner must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's onshore foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-authorized Tier-1 bank. The remittance narrative must identify the FIP and contain the SAFE FDI registration code issued by the receiving bank. The receiving bank verifies the foreign-investment registration, applies the correct SAFE transaction code for foreign-invested enterprise capital contribution, and upon clearing performs RMB settlement subject to the capital-account use restrictions and the foreign investment negative list. The FIP must then obtain a capital verification report from a licensed Chinese CPA and retain the SAFE capital account registration form; the injected funds cannot be used for prohibited transactions such as RMB lending to domestic parties or speculative investments outside the registered business scope.For trade and operating disbursements, the FIP uses SWIFT MT103 wire transfers through its Tier-1 bank after submitting a current-account remittance application, invoice, underlying contract, tax withholding certificate, and where applicable proof of service delivery. Larger software/hardware procurement can be settled through an irrevocable at-sight commercial letter of credit or documentary collections, with the supplier presenting compliant documents against a bank trade desk. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

Hong Kong Profits Tax: 8.25% on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits and 16.5% on the excess for a qualifying entity under the two-tiered rates regime. The 8.25% rate is generally unavailable where the entity is connected with another entity claiming the two-tiered rates or where an election is not properly made. Hong Kong does not impose a separate corporate income tax, value-added tax, capital gains tax, dividend withholding tax, or general interest withholding tax.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% Hong Kong withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong entity. Hong Kong generally does not impose withholding tax on dividends, including dividends paid to non-residents. Partnership profit allocations are not dividends; their treatment depends on the partner's status, source, and applicable foreign tax law.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong local-file and master-file documentation requirements generally apply unless an exemption is available. A Hong Kong entity is generally exempt from preparing a master file and local file if it does not exceed at least two of these three size thresholds: HKD 400,000,000 annual revenue, HKD 300,000,000 total assets, and 100 employees. Separate local-file transaction thresholds generally exempt controlled transactions not exceeding HKD 220,000,000 for transfer of financial assets, HKD 220,000,000 for transfer of intellectual property, HKD 440,000,000 for transfer of goods, or HKD 110,000,000 for other transactions, subject to statutory aggregation and classification rules. Records must generally be retained for at least seven years.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no general standalone economic-substance regulation equivalent to the economic-substance legislation of certain offshore jurisdictions. However, substance is material to source-of-profits analysis, beneficial ownership, treaty access, transfer pricing, anti-avoidance, and the Foreign Source Income Exemption regime. For foreign-sourced passive income received in Hong Kong, qualifying entities may need to satisfy economic-substance requirements for interest income or disposal gains, or participation-exemption requirements for dividends and equity disposal gains. Relevant substance generally includes adequate qualified employees and adequate operating expenditure in Hong Kong, proportionate to the activities generating the income. Outsourcing may be accepted only with adequate supervision, control, and records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and false tax return

Statutory sanction: Criminal prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance may result in a fine of up to HKD 50,000 per offence, a further fine equal to three times the tax undercharged, and imprisonment of up to three years, in addition to assessment, recovery, and penalties.

Director personal liability: Partners, authorized signatories, agents, and responsible officers may be prosecuted where their own consent, connivance, neglect, or participation is established. Civil recovery and tax assessment may continue against the taxpayer. Travel restrictions are not an automatic tax sanction but may arise from separate court or criminal procedures.
Late filing, non-filing, and non-payment

Statutory sanction: Penalties may include up to HKD 10,000 for filing default, additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged, estimated assessment, payment surcharge, civil recovery, prosecution, and enforcement against assets or bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Personal liability is not automatic merely because an individual is a director or partner. It may arise for statutory offences personally committed or where separate guarantees, fraud, misfeasance, or insolvency-law breaches are established.
Transfer-pricing manipulation

Statutory sanction: The Inland Revenue Department may adjust assessable profits, deny deductions, impose compensating assessments, apply penalties for incorrect returns, and pursue anti-avoidance or tax-evasion proceedings where intentional conduct is present. Documentation failures can independently create compliance exposure.

Director personal liability: Responsible persons can face prosecution for knowingly false information or participation in evasion. Ordinary TP adjustments generally impose entity-level tax rather than automatic personal liability.
Artificial offshore-profit claim

Statutory sanction: The Inland Revenue Department may treat profits as Hong Kong sourced, deny the offshore claim, assess tax and penalties, and apply anti-avoidance principles. Foreign-source passive income received in Hong Kong may also be taxable under the Foreign Source Income Exemption rules unless an exemption condition is met.

Director personal liability: Individuals directing false source records, sham arrangements, or misleading filings may face criminal exposure. Corporate separateness does not protect deliberate fraud or statutory offences.
Beneficial ownership and treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Treaty relief may be denied, domestic withholding or source-country tax may apply, and interest, penalties, assessments, and anti-avoidance consequences may follow.

Director personal liability: Individuals responsible for false residence certificates, beneficial-ownership declarations, or sham structures may face civil and criminal consequences in the relevant jurisdictions.
Unregistered or inaccurate business-registration status

Statutory sanction: Failure to comply with the Business Registration Ordinance may result in prosecution, fines, and possible imprisonment under the applicable provisions, separate from profits-tax assessments.

Director personal liability: The responsible individual or partner may be prosecuted for the default. Corporate or partnership status does not immunize a person from a personal statutory offence.
AML, sanctions, and suspicious-payment exposure

Statutory sanction: Applicable obligations may arise under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance, United Nations sanctions legislation, payment-services rules, and contractual platform controls. Consequences can include account freezing, transaction rejection, regulatory investigation, fines, and imprisonment for serious offences.

Director personal liability: Individuals who knowingly facilitate money laundering, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion, or false onboarding information may face personal criminal liability, confiscation, restraint orders, and imprisonment. Travel restrictions may be imposed by a competent court in related criminal proceedings.
Unlicensed regulated activity

Statutory sanction: Operating a regulated activity without the required licence may result in injunctions, business interruption, fines, prosecution, and sector-specific imprisonment provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, and managers who knowingly authorize unlicensed operations may be personally prosecuted and may be subject to disqualification or other regulatory orders.
Intellectual-property and source-code misappropriation

Statutory sanction: Potential consequences include injunctions, damages, account of profits, delivery-up or destruction orders, criminal copyright penalties in applicable cases, contract termination, and tax deduction denial for unsupported payments.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may arise for knowing infringement, dishonest assistance, fraudulent misrepresentation, or breach of fiduciary duty. Ordinary inadvertent infringement is generally an entity-level civil matter.
Corporate insolvency and asset diversion

Statutory sanction: Transactions may be challenged or reversed under insolvency law, with contribution orders, restoration of assets, fines, disqualification, and criminal sanctions for fraudulent trading or misconduct.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may face personal contribution orders, civil restitution, disqualification, and imprisonment where fraudulent or reckless conduct is proven. A separate legal personality does not shield fraudulent asset diversion.
