Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China - National Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax); for Hong Kong SAR component: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) - eTAX portal and Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

For the China foreign-invested partnership (FIP): the entity must first be registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued at SAMR registration is automatically the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and is synchronized into the STA electronic taxpayer registration. The FIP must complete tax registration, VAT general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer status confirmation, payroll and social insurance employer registration, and withholding tax registration for non-resident partners through the STA e-Tax portal. For a Hong Kong SAR element, a foreign non-resident partner or entity must file an application for Business Registration through the Inland Revenue Department's eTAX channel and obtain a Business Registration Certificate; the Business Registration Number (BRN) constitutes the Hong Kong taxpayer identification number for the partnership. Where a non-resident partner has no Hong Kong establishment, the partner may obtain a Hong Kong TIN through the IRD's taxpayer record procedures when Hong Kong Profits Tax filing or withholding obligations arise.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - the PRC FIP is not an Enterprise Income Tax payer under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law; each partner is taxed in its own capacity on allocated income from the partnership. For Hong Kong SAR, the partnership is transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) for Profits Tax purposes, with territorial source principles applying to the taxation of partners.

Available election forms

None - PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or analogous entity-classification election for partnerships; the partnership is mandatorily pass-through for income tax, although the partnership itself remains a VAT taxpayer. Hong Kong also provides no check-the-box election; a Hong Kong partnership is mandatorily transparent. No hybrid mismatch election is available under either regime, although BEPS-aligned rules may recharacterize an offshore entity for specific treaty or anti-abuse purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China: general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold small-scale taxpayer status applies with simplified rates and no input-credit mechanism, and VAT registration is also required before issuing tax invoices. Hong Kong SAR: not applicable; no indirect tax registration threshold exists, but a Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 is mandatory for all business carried on in Hong Kong regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China: zero-rating or VAT exemption is available for (a) export of goods actually discharged from customs; and (b) cross-border sale of certain taxable services, including qualifying software and digital services supplied to an overseas enterprise or individual where the services are consumed entirely outside China. The anti-cheat engine supplied as software-as-a-service or as a digital license to an offshore gaming operator may qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption under the cross-border taxable services policy, provided the FIP retains evidence that the recipient is outside China, the service is performed offshore, and payments are received from outside China. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT exists; for Profits Tax, export-related income may be treated as offshore-sourced and thus outside Hong Kong's territorial tax base, but this is not a VAT zero-rating mechanism.

China: if the exported goods or cross-border service zero-rating is not substantiated by customs declarations, logistics documentation, contracts, invoices, and the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation or receipt evidence, the STA may claw back the 0% treatment, re-assess VAT at the applicable standard rate (generally 6% for software services or 13% for goods), impose daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05% of overdue tax from the due date, and assess administrative penalties of 50% to five times the tax shortfall under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime in which current-account transactions are nominally convertible while the capital account remains tightly supervised. Trade in goods, software licensing, services, and profit distributions are considered current-account flows, yet each remittance must pass through SAFE's cross-border monitoring system and be supported by authentic underlying documents such as contracts, invoices, customs declarations, service trade vouchers, or audited financial statements. The capital account is heavily controlled; foreign direct investment into a Mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership is admitted through FDI registration and a dedicated capital FX settlement account, but portfolio flows, quasi-capital injections, short-term cross-border loans, and speculative instruments require separate PBOC or SAFE approval. RMB convertibility for trade and services operates under the real-demand principle, meaning conversions are permissible only when a genuine commercial transaction exists and is executed through a licensed domestic bank. Profit or capital remittances out of the FIP encounter heightened scrutiny: SAFE and the local market regulator require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, proof of compliance with the partnership agreement, and confirmation that all foreign-currency tax obligations have been discharged. Non-resident partners must evidence lawful source of funds and demonstrate the FIP's substance, board authorisations, and registrations. Export proceeds are not subject to a fixed mandatory surrender ratio today; however, all export-related foreign exchange must be repatriated to a domestic banking account within the statutory period and may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily converted into RMB after verification. The net effect is a supervised-convertibility system rather than free convertibility: treasury and clearing structures must be designed around documented, pre-approved transactional corridors rather than open-market currency mobility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current-account rules, export proceeds must be repatriated into a licensed Mainland bank account within 90 days of the export customs declaration date, with an extendable window up to 180 days for bona fide service and software exports upon documented application. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the FIP may retain 100% of export foreign currency in a foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB, provided that every receipt is supported by customs declarations, shipping or service performance records, contracts, and SAFE-compliant reporting forms.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Very low / non-viable. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to maintain domestic corporate accounts, execute renminbi clearing, or serve as the FX gateway for a Mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. The State Council's payment-service rules and SAFE's cross-border payment administration require any entity providing settlement or foreign-exchange services to a domestic corporate client to hold a payment-institution licence and a SAFE cross-border payment record. No offshore EMI holds these licences; thus an offshore EMI cannot lawfully onboard the FIP, cannot receive its FDI capital, cannot convert RMB on its behalf, and cannot disburse trade settlements in compliance with SAFE. The FIP may use an offshore EMI only at the Hong Kong affiliate level for offshore receipts falling outside China's domestic bank rails, but never as a substitute for a tier-1 domestic banking relationship. Legal rating: prohibited for onshore treasury operations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very low / non-viable. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or the National Financial Regulatory Administration to maintain domestic corporate accounts, execute renminbi clearing, or serve as the FX gateway for a Mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Controlled transactions between associated enterprises must be documented when the aggregate amount of intercompany transactions exceeds HK$20,000,000 per year; Master File and Local File are mandatory for any Hong Kong entity with group turnover exceeding HK$680,000,000 and cross-border related-party transactions above HK$20,000,000

Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) legislation, the IRD applies the OECD BEPS treaty context: an FIP seeking treaty benefits must demonstrate real economic substance in Hong Kong, including Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as or originated from the integration of anti-cheat algorithms, server infrastructure, contract negotiation, and managerial decision-making conducted locally; the presence of only the entity name with all CIGA performed in China will trigger treaty shopping rejection by the IRD and Chinese tax authorities

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82, the partnership, partners, and responsible directors are liable to a fine of HK$100,000 plus treble the tax undercharged; under s.82A, fraudulent evasion carries imprisonment for up to 10 years; the IRD may also reopen assessments without time limits when fraud or wilful neglect is established

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced if the court finds the partner-directors used the FIP as a mere façade; each director may be jointly and severally liable for the full tax arrears, civil restitution of diverted profits, forfeiture of business assets, a HK travel restriction order under the protection of revenue provisions, and an imprisonment term of 3 to 10 years
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under China’s General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) and the China-HK arrangement’s Limitation on Benefits provisions, the tax authorities may deny treaty benefits, impose a 10% withholding under China CIT law (or higher domestic withholding if no treaty), levy late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose a penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the tax originally underpaid under Article 63 of the Chinese Tax Collection Law; in Hong Kong, the IRD may revoke the TRC and assess all deemed HK-source profits

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute offshore payment flows through a shell FIP may be personally liable under common law conspiracy involving tax fraud; courts can pierce the veil to hold directors personally for the entire underpaid tax plus statutory surcharges; directors can be blacklisted from leaving Hong Kong and China, have their assets frozen, and face criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent tax evasion under the joint HK-China mutual legal assistance framework
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong’s Inland Revenue Ordinance (Amendment) No. 6 of 2017 imposes penalties for failure to maintain adequate transfer pricing documentation: a default penalty of HK$50,000 upon first conviction and HK$5,000 per month of continuing default; the IRD may adjust the taxpayer’s profits and assess additional profits tax up to 16.5% of the adjusted amounts; China may correspondingly adjust its taxation and impose penalties under the Chinese Transfer Pricing Rules and Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation, ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid

Director personal liability: Where documentation failure is part of a deliberate scheme to divert profits, the directors can be personally assessed under s.82A for fraudulent purposes; the corporate veil can be pierced, rendering directors personally liable for aggregate unpaid tax, penalties, and sanctions of up to treble the diverted tax; criminal penalties for individual officers include imprisonment of up to 5 years, and the directors may be personally blacklisted and subjected to HK Immigration Department travel prohibitions
