Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership operating a dedicated offshore software engineering and maintenance hub, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is rejected by local tax authorities, market regulators, and banks. The SAMR requires a lawful, fixed business premises matching the registered address; the tax authority requires actual premises for invoice issuance, audit, and site verification; banks and payment service providers, including Wire, Wise B2B, and Payoneer, will not complete KYC and cross-border payment routing on a virtual office unless a recognized Free Trade Zone formally approves clustered registration and the tax bureau issues written acknowledgement. Even under an approved clustered registration, banking physical inspection risk and tax challenge risk remain high. A physical, staffed office with the FIP's name on the door, a lease in the FIP's name, utility records, and software development equipment is commercially mandatory for the ODC model.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Registration Department, Limited Partnerships Register, 14/F, Queensway Government Offices, 66 Queensway, Hong Kong; and Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, 4/F, Revenue Tower, 5 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong.

Governing statute

The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is formed as a Hong Kong registered limited partnership rather than as an incorporated company. Primary governing statutes are: (i) the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), sections 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8; (ii) the Partnership Ordinance (Cap 38), sections 3 and 4; and (iii) the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310), section 6. The Companies Ordinance (Cap 622) is not the primary formation statute for the FIP, but Cap 622 applies to any body corporate that is appointed as the corporate general partner. The FIP is treated as an offshore special territory vehicle for this multinational structure because it is established in Hong Kong SAR with foreign or non-Hong Kong partners permitted.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form LR1 (Statement of Limited Partnership) under section 5(1) of the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), together with IRC Form 1 (Business Registration Application) under section 6 of the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310), and the executed Partnership Deed.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital, no mandatory issued share capital, and no blocked escrow account on a Foreign-Invested Partnership registered as a limited partnership. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing through the STA electronic tax bureau (e-Tax) portal, with the national public portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn. Hong Kong SAR's separate tax authority, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), is relevant only to any separate Hong Kong offshore vehicle in the structure, not to the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership itself.

E-government filing portal

Establish the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) by registering with the local Market Supervision Administration; the issued business license carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the entity's statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Next, complete the tax information confirmation filing in the STA e-Tax portal within the statutory period after issuance of the business license, select VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), and file the partnership particulars including all foreign partners. For a non-resident foreign partner without a permanent establishment in China, the competent PRC tax authority will open a non-resident taxpayer file and issue a non-resident taxpayer identification number when the China-sourced income is first reported under withholding procedures; the foreign partner must provide its home-state tax certificate, partnership agreement, declarations as to beneficial ownership, and notarised/apostilled corporate identity documents.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible (the FIP is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of FIP profit; the FIP remains a VAT taxpayer for PRC VAT, so pass-through applies only to income tax). — Establish the PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) by registering with the local Market Supervision Administration; the issued business license carries the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the entity's statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the FIP is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer; each partner is taxed on its allocated share of FIP profit; the FIP remains a VAT taxpayer for PRC VAT, so pass-through applies only to income tax).

Available election forms

None. The PRC tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections for Foreign-Invested Partnerships. Application of any treaty or arrangement relief is based on facts, not an elective entity classification; no optional entity-level taxation is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration. A FIP carrying on any taxable supply in China is required to complete VAT registration from the first supply; below the threshold it may be treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer at the simplified rate, but a FIP seeking export zero-rating should register as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Tangible goods exported from China qualify for zero-rating/exemption with export VAT refund if they are physically shipped under a China Customs export declaration, sold to an overseas buyer, and foreign-exchange consideration is received from outside China. Offshore software engineering, development and maintenance services qualify for zero-rating or exemption when supplied to a non-resident enterprise, consumed wholly outside China, and covered by the STA's cross-border taxable services zero-rating/exemption catalogue; the FIP must prove the recipient, place of performance, and place of consumption are outside China.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (export bill-of-entry equivalent) bearing the customs declaration number
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or combined transport document evidencing physical export from China to the overseas consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank foreign-exchange receipt advice evidencing cross-border payment from the overseas buyer
  • Signed export or cross-border service contract, commercial invoice, and the FIP's VAT e-invoice issued for the zero-rated supply

China operates real-time VAT clearance through the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Full digital e-invoices must be issued through the STA electronic invoice service platform; invoice tax-control data is uploaded by API in near-real time to the national tax database. Each e-invoice bears an STA digital anti-counterfeit tax-control number, and input VAT credit is blocked until the fiscal system validates the counterpart invoice. Automated risk screening flags suspicious, high-frequency, or high-value invoices for audit.

Zero-rated export documentation is cross-validated against China Customs, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, and banking remittance records. If an audit disallows the zero rating, the FIP must repay VAT at the domestic rate (13% for goods, 6% for services) plus late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Penalties for inaccurate or fraudulent declarations may range from 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid tax, with retrospective clawback within the statutory assessment period and no time limit for fraud. Criminal liability, including imprisonment and asset confiscation, applies to fraudulent export VAT refund claims based on false customs declarations, forged transport documents, or fabricated foreign-exchange remittance evidence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully free capital account and a convertible currency regime anchored by the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the Hong Kong dollar trading in the 7.75 to 7.85 band against the US dollar. There are no surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, no exchange control approval for current or capital account transactions, and no statutory restrictions on profit repatriation or dividend remittance for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The HKMA supervises licensed banks as the principal channels for foreign exchange execution, while the Companies Ordinance and the Census and Statistics Ordinance impose only ordinary corporate filing and trade declaration duties rather than exchange controls. For the Offshore Development Center, convertibility risk is therefore driven almost entirely by sanctions screening and anti-money laundering compliance rather than by jurisdictional FX restrictions. The Chinese parent company remains subject to PRC SAFE foreign direct investment review for capital outflows, but Hong Kong imposes no domicile or currency limits on inbound capital, service receipts, or operational disbursements. As a result, Hong Kong ranks at the highest practical level of convertibility for full corporate treasury activity, subject only to robust KYC documentation and bank-level financial crime clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceed surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. All foreign currency receipts and software development service proceeds may be retained in USD, EUR, CNH, or any convertible currency, freely converted at commercial rates, or used directly to settle supplier and operating liabilities. Exporters must only maintain underlying trade documents such as invoices, contracts, and bank statements for audit and, where physical goods are involved, file import/export declarations under the Import/Export (Registration) Regulations; pure software services do not carry a foreign exchange surrender window.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), operating under the Exchange Fund Ordinance and the Banking Ordinance, with the Financial Secretary retaining exchange rate policy oversight

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility is medium-high for ancillary operational payouts, but low as a replacement for a Tier-1 banking relationship. Fintech EMIs such as Wise and Payoneer are not legally barred from servicing Hong Kong entities, but they must hold either a Money Service Operator license under the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Ordinance or a Stored Value Facility license under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. For an Offshore Development Center, these platforms can handle small-dollar payroll, vendor payments, and software licensing fees with strong payment-rail efficiency. However, they cannot provide a true capital escrow account, issue banker’s guarantees, support CPA capital verification, or serve as the routing corridor for inbound Chinese FDI or loan remittances. Banks will treat EMI statements as secondary supporting evidence only, and any transaction above USD 50,000, or any transaction forming part of tax or equity documentation, should clear exclusively through a licensed Hong Kong authorized institution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Chinese parent must clear only through HKMA-licensed authorized institutions using SWIFT MT103 or MT202 messages, with full remitter and beneficiary legal names, registered addresses, and the bank-defined purpose code for overseas direct investment or service fee funding. The Chinese parent must complete SAFE ODI/FDI registration before remittance, and the Hong Kong FIP must book the contribution in its capital ledger and retain the stamped bank statement. Capital verification is performed by a Hong Kong certified public accountant, substantiated by a receipt certificate, the SWIFT trail, and the executed contribution agreement. No fintech EMI or aggregated payout rail may be used for capital injection or equity verification purposes.Outbound trade and operational disbursements should use SWIFT MT103 for recurring low-value payments, payroll, and software license fees; commercial letters of credit issued through SWIFT MT700/MT707 for high-value hardware, infrastructure, and third-party vendor purchase contracts; and documentary collections with full transport documents for established counterparties. For same-day Hong Kong dollar and USD obligations, CHATS and the Faster Payment System (FPS) support local clearing, while all cross-border USD, EUR, CNH, and HKD transfers settle via SWIFT or CHATS USD. Fintech EMIs such as Wise and Payoneer are not legally barred from servicing Hong Kong entities, but they must hold either a Money Service Operator license under the Anti-Money Laundering and Terrorist Financing Ordinance or a Stored Value Facility license under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. For an Offshore Development Center, these platforms can handle small-dollar payroll, vendor payments, and software licensing fees with strong payment-rail efficiency. However, they cannot provide a true capital escrow account, issue banker’s guarantees, support CPA capital verification, or serve as the routing corridor for inbound Chinese FDI or loan remittances. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory threshold is not fixed in the Inland Revenue Ordinance; the IRD imposes a comprehensive obligation to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions. The CbC reporting notification threshold is HK$7.5 billion annual consolidated group revenue. For the ODC, a Local File and Master File are mandatory if the Central Management and Control of the partnership is exercised in Hong Kong and the aggregate cross-border intercompany charges exceed HK$5 million in any basis period.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not implemented the Caribbean-style ESR codes, but the IRD applies a codified economic substance threshold for any offshore claim, tax treaty entitlement, and transfer pricing defense. For an offshore development center to avoid fatal re-characterisation, it must show that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - including software deployment, maintenance decision-making, contract negotiation, and risk-bearing - physically occur in Hong Kong. An ODC that merely invoices from Hong Kong but performs all code development and server management in Mainland China may fail CIGA and be treated as a shell taxable in both jurisdictions, with source-conflict and double taxation risk.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understated Offshore Development Revenue

Statutory sanction: Offence under Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82A: upon conviction, fine of HK$20,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus an additional fine of HK$500 for each day the offence continues. Under s.82B, the court may order the offender to pay a penalty not exceeding 3 times the amount of tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: The Hong Kong court may pierce the corporate veil where the partnership is used as a façade or sham. General partners and shadow directors who signed the returns, approved the allocation, or issued the invoice are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. The Inland Revenue Department may list the defaulting entity and its directors on the blacklist, apply emigration control to prevent travel, freeze bank accounts under the Tax Recovery Ordinance, and impose a maximum 3-year imprisonment sentence.
DTA Abuse / False Beneficial Ownership Certificate

Statutory sanction: Knowingly providing false or incorrect information for a tax exemption or treaty claim is an offence under IRO s.80(1) and s.80(2); the penalty is the greater of HK$10,000 or the amount of tax undercharged, up to 3 times. The IRD can also apply General Anti-Abuse Rules to deny the treaty claim retrospectively and charge interest on the full difference.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who executed the beneficial ownership declaration are personally exposed. Chinese courts, alongside Hong Kong proceedings, may impose civil restitution of all treaty relief claims, impose an asset preservation order, and add the directors to a cross-border travel ban under article 282 of the PRC Criminal Law where fraud has occurred. Hong Kong disqualification and a 5-year directorship ban apply where fraud is proven before the District Court.
Shell Company / Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The IRD may re-assess profits under the transfer pricing provisions of IRO s.50A and s.50B using an arm's length price, and impose an additional penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged for failure to maintain sufficient records and contemporaneous documentation. Criminal fines of up to HK$20,000 and imprisonment for 3 years apply to willful obstruction or false reporting under s.82A.

Director personal liability: The 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine is applied where directors act improperly or use the entity to avoid tax. Each director who signed the invoice and the tax return is jointly and severally liable for the adjusted TP amount. Personal assets may be attached to satisfy the undercharge. The individual may be blacklisted by the IRD, subjected to immigration control, and disqualified under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance; in the most severe cross-border fraud cases, the Chinese tax authority will coordinate criminal referral leading to up to 7 years of imprisonment under PRC tax evasion statutes.
