Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account requirement for partnerships. The partners may subscribe capital in cash, server hardware, or other assets as expressly stated in the executed partnership deed; however lending banks may contractually require a segregated escrow arrangement for callable capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Certification required from the Fire Services Department for fire service installations, emergency lighting, and fire escape routes; structural load clearance from the Buildings Department for raised floors, server racks and battery rooms; electrical safety approval from the Electrical and Mechanical Services Department for high-voltage and low-voltage systems; and Occupational Safety clearance from the Labour Department for working in confined spaces, manual handling operations, and data hall environmental safety.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account requirement for partnerships. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; Business Registration Office under IRD; online portals: IRD eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk) for electronic filing and GovHK business registration services for Business Registration Certificate applications.

E-government filing portal

1) Determine whether the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is carrying on a business in Hong Kong; any partnership formed for data center colocation leasing in Hong Kong must register under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement. 2) Complete and submit Form IRBR 1 (Application for Registration of Business) to the IRD Business Registration Office together with the prescribed business registration fee and levy. 3) Attach a certified copy of the partnership agreement, identity documents and foreign addresses for all general and limited partners, proof of the Hong Kong business address (such as the data center lease or rack licence), and a declaration of the principal place of business. 4) Foreign non-resident partners should appoint an authorised representative in Hong Kong to receive IRD correspondence and should state the representative contact details on Form IRBR 1. 5) On approval, IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) carrying an 8-digit Business Registration Number (BRN); the BRN is the tax identifier for all IRD filings, Profits Tax Returns, and employer compliance. 6) If the partnership changes name, partners, business address, or class of business, file Form IRBR 2 (Application for Change of Business Registration Particulars) within 30 days. 7) The partnership must then file the Partnership Profits Tax Return; non-resident partners are assessed separately on their share of profits, and IRD may issue each partner a direct assessment using the BRN or another tax file reference.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (Territorial_Scope applies): The Hong Kong partnership is tax-transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the FIP is not a separate taxpayer. Each partner is separately assessed on its allocable share of Hong Kong-source assessable profits, while offshore-source profits remain outside the territorial scope of Hong Kong profits tax.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no limited liability company tax classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election for a partnership. Pass-through treatment is mandatory under the Inland Revenue Ordinance; tax avoidance provisions apply to artificial arrangements designed to exploit hybrid mismatches.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. Hong Kong retains narrow indirect duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117), but neither applies to data center colocation leasing or bare-metal hosting services unless a lease instrument is separately chargeable to stamp duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no consumption tax registration threshold. Under the Business Registration Ordinance, any business operating in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate regardless of turnover; no economic threshold must be crossed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. There is no 0% export VAT rate, no export zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism. Cross-border data center colocation and bare-metal server hosting services are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect taxation. If the customer is a Mainland China enterprise, PRC VAT may be withheld by the Mainland recipient under the PRC VAT Circular (Caishui [2016] 36) if the place of supply is deemed to be Mainland China; the Hong Kong FIP itself has no Hong Kong VAT registration requirement.

  • No statutory VAT-export document bundle exists in Hong Kong; if hardware is physically exported, retain the Hong Kong Import/Export Declaration filed through the Trade Single Window and the cargo manifest as export evidence.
  • Bill of Lading, House Air Waybill, or Master Air Waybill showing consignor, consignee, and destination for any physical equipment movements.
  • Data center colocation agreement, rack lease order, service scope, billing amount, and service delivery schedule evidencing that the services were performed at the Hong Kong facility.
  • Bank SWIFT credit advice or foreign exchange receipt record evidencing customer remittance in the invoiced currency; an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) is not issued by Hong Kong banks, and IRD accepts bank-issued transaction advices.
  • Rack access logs, handover certificates, site entry records, and bandwidth utilisation reports substantiating that the IT services were physically performed in Hong Kong for offshore-source profit analysis.

No statutory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, government e-invoice API, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists in Hong Kong. IRD accepts paper and electronic invoices in any format, provided records are kept in English or Chinese and retained for at least seven years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For physical movement of hardware, Hong Kong Trade Single Window and Census and Statistics Department import/export declaration rules apply, but these are trade statistics and customs control measures, not tax clearance mandates.

There is no VAT clawback because no VAT is chargeable in Hong Kong. However, IRD may audit the partnership to determine whether profits from the colocation business have an accurate Hong Kong source. If a partnership or partner uses false export documents, fabricated foreign receipts, or artificial arrangements to misclassify Hong Kong-source income as offshore income, IRD may issue additional profits tax assessments, charge interest under section 60 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and impose penalties under section 82A of up to 300% of the tax undercharged. Knowingly submitting false returns or statements is a criminal offence under section 80, carrying substantial fines and imprisonment. The 180-day remittance period is not a statutory Hong Kong deadline, but it is used as an internal compliance benchmark; longer remittance periods do not automatically invalidate the transaction but will increase audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates a fully convertible currency regime for capital and current account transactions. The Hong Kong dollar is freely traded under the Linked Exchange Rate System with the US dollar in the 7.75 to 7.85 band. There are no surrender requirements, no prior exchange control approval for normal trade-related receipts, and no profit remittance restrictions for Hong Kong-domiciled partnerships. Dividends or partnership profit distributions to foreign partners may be freely remitted provided proper CRS and tax documentation is maintained. However, because the Chinese mainland maintains a controlled capital account, an FIP with mainland Chinese investors must isolate and document every injection of RMB or foreign currency from mainland China. Such funding must trace to SAFE-registered overseas direct investment or cross-border settlement through recognized banks; otherwise conversion into HKD can be blocked by the receiving bank. Operational receipts from mainland customers in RMB may be held as CNH under the offshore RMB clearing arrangement, allowing convertibility to HKD without mainland SAFE approval. Profit repatriation from the Hong Kong FIP to mainland parent firms remains subject to mainland capital account rules, including dividend remittance through registered current account procedures and tax clearance under the People's Republic of China territorial tax system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Hong Kong law there is no statutory requirement to surrender export proceeds or convert them into HKD; exporters and service providers may retain foreign currency in designated accounts. If export or rental proceeds are collected from Mainland China by a mainland connected company, SAFE operates an export collection and settlement system: proceeds generally must be verified via the State Administration of Foreign Exchange digital monitoring platform and repatriated into China within 180 days of the declaration date, with discretionary extension if bona fide delays are documented. Mandatory conversion ratios have been abolished; however, banks may require sale to RMB when proceeds are mobilized under trade credit facilitation quotas or when the proceeds are used to settle mainland taxes.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and, for cross-border fund flows involving Mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Moderate for supplementary payment processing only; low as the primary bank. In Hong Kong, no standalone digital EMI is licensed as an authorized institution for corporate deposit-taking. Airwallex, Currenxie, Neat, Statrys and similar operators are registered money service operators or partner with licensed banks; they cannot operate a capital escrow account, issue guaranteed settlement, or provide the full safeguarding expected for data-center colocation clearing. They are legally permissible for transactional payments and expenses where the underlying FX is pre-funded from the FIP's bank account, but they are barred from acting as the sole liquidity rail for inbound capital injection, capital verification, or profit repatriation, because their licenses do not cover deposit-taking or custody of partnership capital. Therefore, onboarding one or two Tier-1 banks is mandatory; an EMI can be used as a convenience layer for small monthly utility-style payments only if its partner bank's due-diligence requirements are met.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the Hong Kong FIP from foreign partners should be settled through SWIFT MT103/MT202 from a licensed source bank to one of the Tier-1 clearing banks. The payment memo and forwarding instruction should state the purpose as 'Capital contribution to Foreign-Invested Partnership' or 'Paid-in capital for partnership operations', together with the FIP's legal entity identifier and the underlying commercial purpose code recognized by SAFE. If the sender is a mainland China corporate entity, the injection must be predicated on a SAFE ODI filing or cross-border RMB settlement agreement, with bank approval reference numbers included in the remittance details. Within Hong Kong, the FIP must maintain the contributed records in a Capital Escrow or verified Capital Contribution account until a certified public accountant issues a capital verification report confirming receipt from the correct partner and matching the business plan. For data-center rack leasing, the capital amount should be proportional to committed leases and projected power usage; a bank may require proof of lease deposits and rack installation contracts before releasing funds from escrow.Outbound operational trade disbursements should be cleared through the FIP's designated transaction account using SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier transfers, commercial letters of credit where imported equipment is procured directly from foreign manufacturers, and documentary collections for deferred payment term trades with escrowed shipping documents. Rack rental and cross-connect invoices to tenants should be received via ACH or SWIFT wire using invoicing descriptions matching the colocation contract. For disbursements to mainland China, use Offshore Renminbi CNH clearing through Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited, which reduces SAFE approval friction and avoids onshore CNY conversion. All outgoing payments must be supported by the underlying colocation lease, power usage statement, equipment invoice, or utility bill, and the treasury function must reconcile every MT103 with the corresponding commercial certificate to sustain audit trail integrity.Deliverable FX forwards on HKD/USD and CNH/USD to lock rental income and power costs, with tenors tied to underlying lease contracts and margin collateral through an ISDA 1992 Master Agreement.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) referencing onshore USD/CNY for exposure to mainland-derived receivables when the FIP lacks onshore CNY bank accounts and cannot settle deliverable CNH.Cross-currency swaps between HKD and CNH/USD with HKMA-regulated licensed banks for long-term asset-liability matching, excluding speculative or uncovered positions unless the trade is supported by colocation lease receivables and verified power bills.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Moderate for supplementary payment processing only; low as the primary bank. In Hong Kong, no standalone digital EMI is licensed as an authorized institution for corporate deposit-taking. Airwallex, Currenxie, Neat, Statrys and similar operators are registered money service operators or partner with licensed banks; they cannot operate a capital escrow account, issue guaranteed settlement, or provide the full safeguarding expected for data-center colocation clearing. They are legally permissible for transactional payments and expenses where the underlying FX is pre-funded from the FIP's bank account, but they are barred from acting as the sole liquidity rail for inbound capital injection, capital verification, or profit repatriation, because their licenses do not cover deposit-taking or custody of partnership capital. Therefore, onboarding one or two Tier-1 banks is mandatory; an EMI can be used as a convenience layer for small monthly utility-style payments only if its partner bank's due-diligence requirements are met.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No statutory audit is mandated for Hong Kong unincorporated partnerships, including a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP). Legal audit requirements apply only to companies incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). For such companies, small private companies may qualify for audit exemption where annual turnover ≤ HKD 2,000,000, total assets ≤ HKD 2,000,000, and number of employees ≤ 50. Since an FIP is not a company, no statutory quantitative thresholds trigger a mandatory audit. However, independent certified CPA audits may be contractually or regulatorily required if the partnership maintains banking covenants, investor agreements, or operating licenses that impose audit obligations beyond statutory minima. In practice, lenders often require an audit when annual turnover exceeds HKD 10,000,000 or gross assets exceed HKD 5,000,000, but these are not statutory triggers.
