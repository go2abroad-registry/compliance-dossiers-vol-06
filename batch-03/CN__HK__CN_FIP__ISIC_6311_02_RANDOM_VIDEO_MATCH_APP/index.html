Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Hong Kong Companies Registry, Partnerships Registration Section, operating with the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for Business Registration Certificate issuance and annual renewal.

Governing statute

The primary governing statutes are not a Companies Act but the Partnership Ordinance (Cap 38), the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), and the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310) of the Laws of Hong Kong SAR. The relevant operative sections include sections 3, 17 and 18 of Cap 38, sections 4 through 7 of Cap 37, and sections 5 and 6 of Cap 310, together with the Finance Act provisions that fix the annual Business Registration fee and levy for a Foreign-Invested Partnership carrying on data processing activities under ISIC 6311.

Formation / constitutional filings

Limited Partnership Statement (Form LP1) filed under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), followed by the Business Registration application (Form IRBR 1) filed under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account requirement on a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Capital contributions, whether in cash, technology assets, or intellectual property, are contractually governed by the Partnership Deed and recorded in the capital contribution schedule, which binds each partner to its commitment.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — there is no physical facility, storage area, electrical workshop, chemical inventory, or high-load structure requiring Civil Defense fire certification, structural load inspection, or workplace safety approval. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; partnership profits flow through to the partners for Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes, and the territorial source limitation applies so that only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are chargeable.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box entity classification election, no elective pass-through regime, and no hybrid mismatch election. The fiscally transparent treatment of a partnership is mandatory under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and the offshore income claim is a factual and legal substantiation based on the territorial source principle rather than a statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax regime. Hong Kong only imposes narrow excise duties on liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; no indirect tax registration threshold exists. A Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 is mandatory once a business is carried on in Hong Kong, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating or refund mechanism. For Hong Kong Profits Tax, an FIP supplying real-time video matching and cross-border social telephony services to users outside Hong Kong can only exclude profits if those profits are derived from outside Hong Kong under the territorial source principle. The IRD will evaluate where the services are performed, where the contracts are negotiated and executed, where the app platform and servers are operated, where management and technical decisions are taken, and where the revenue is economically generated; the taxpayer must demonstrate that the entire course of business, or the relevant profit-earning activity, is offshore. Mere use of a Hong Kong billing address or payment processor such as Apple IAP or Paymentwall does not in itself establish an onshore source.

Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, there is no VAT clawback or zero-rating penalty. However, if the IRD rejects an offshore profits claim, the FIP partners are assessed to Hong Kong Profits Tax on the full onshore-source profit, with interest and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance for inaccurate returns or failure to provide information, and criminal liability for tax fraud. Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China applies a managed floating exchange-rate regime and maintains RMB current-account convertibility, while the capital account remains subject to SAFE registration, licensing, and post-verification. For this structure, the operative entity is a China-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership and the Hong Kong SAR vehicle is the non-resident investor; therefore PBOC/SAFE rules supersede HKMA banking flexibility. The HK partner's capital contribution is a capital-account inflow requiring a negative-list clearance, SAFE foreign-exchange registration, and an onshore foreign-exchange capital account. The FIP may voluntarily settle registered foreign currency into RMB at spot through the opening bank, but the RMB must be held in a settlement-pending account and used only for bona fide operational expenditures; unauthorized equity investments, offshore loans, or wealth-management deployments are prohibited. Current-account items, including app-store subscription remittances, hosting fees, and Apple/Paymentwall settlement flows, are convertible with transaction-authenticity review and tax withholding verification. Profit distribution to the Hong Kong partner requires audited financial statements, a tax-clearance certificate, and SAFE-compliant remittance documentation. Additional restrictions apply because real-time video-matching and social telephony implicate cybersecurity, data localization, and telecom licensing requirements; the FIP must show compliance before banks clear cross-border funds. Overall, current-account convertibility is operationally fast; capital-account convertibility is highly conditional and documentary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory foreign-exchange surrender. The FIP may retain 100% of export or service-trade receipts in a foreign-currency account or convert voluntarily, but all such receipts must be repatriated onshore to the FIP's designated bank account within the applicable SAFE declaration window; advance receipts requiring export performance beyond 30 days must be registered, and deferred export receipts exceeding 90 days from the export declaration date require prior SAFE registration. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero; the statutory obligation is repatriation, accurate transaction declaration, and preservation of supporting trade credentials for bank verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

In-depth feasibility rating: Non-viable/Low. PBOC and SAFE do not issue EMI banking licenses to pure fintech providers, and SAFE does not recognize offshore EMI/PSP accounts as corporate treasury accounts for a mainland China FIP. Apple IAP and Paymentwall may serve only as downstream consumer collection/payment gateways; settlement must ultimately be collected into a licensed Chinese bank account. An offshore EMI cannot provide RMB deposit accounts, cannot process FDI capital-account settlement, cannot execute SAFE-monitored foreign-exchange hedging, and cannot produce the regulatory reporting required for profit remittance. Any attempt to route capital contributions or partner distributions through an EMI would be rejected at bank clearing and may trigger AML investigation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner should remit HKD or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's foreign-exchange capital escrow account at a Tier-1 designated bank. Before remittance, the FIP must complete the SAFE direct registration through the opening bank, obtain the foreign-exchange capital account approval, and confirm the investment is outside the foreign-investment negative list. The remittance must reference the FIP name, investor name, and the SAFE-compliant direct-investment purpose code, generally 622011 for inward capital injection. Upon receipt, the bank will verify the transaction and report it to SAFE; the FIP may then voluntarily settle the capital into RMB into a settlement-pending account. A Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming the actual paid-in capital, and the FIP must update its partnership registration and SAFE files with the paid-in capital amount before using the funds for operating expenses or making remittances to the Hong Kong partner.For operational outflows such as Apple App Store commissions, Paymentwall gateway fees, hosting, advertising, and technology licensing, the FIP should use SWIFT MT103 through its domestic relationship bank or, for cross-border RMB settlement, the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System. Each payment must be supported by a signed agreement, commercial invoice, proof of service delivery, and a valid SAFE/tax declaration; service-import remittances also require the tax withholding certificate and the SAFE service-trade registration form. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: In-depth feasibility rating: Non-viable/Low. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10,000,000 for intangible property and other transactions; HK$20,000,000 for financial transactions; HK$50,000,000 for tangible property transactions; if the partnership's intercompany transactions exceed any one of these thresholds, it must prepare and retain Master File (group consolidated revenue above HK$680 million) and Local File contemporaneously.

Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime as in the UAE or BVI, the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the 2020 Transfer Pricing (Deduction for Expenses) amendments require a substance-over-form analysis. The IRD applies the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test to determine the source of profits and to deny DTA anti-abuse claims. For a partnership operating an AI-driven video-matching app, the CIGA are: server and algorithm hosting, user interface maintenance, licensing of matching software, negotiation of Apple IAP and Paymentwall terms, and management decisions on pricing. If these activities are physically performed in Hong Kong, profits are HK-source; if delegated to a mainland China affiliate, the partnership must demonstrate its own personnel with adequate skills and decision-making authority. The partnership must also prepare an annual TP documentation file even if below thresholds, because the IRD requires a general consistency note for all cross-border connected-party transactions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Falsely Claiming Offshore Status

Statutory sanction: Violation of Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Section 82 (Fraudulent evasion) and Section 82A (Additional tax in respect of false or misleading statements); financial penalties up to three times the amount of tax undercharged, plus a fine of HK$500,000; the IRD can also amend assessments for up to 10 years under Section 82A.

Director personal liability: Under IRO Section 82, the general partner and any managing director who knowingly signs a false partnership return are personally prosecuted: on conviction upon indictment, imprisonment for 3 years and a fine of HK$500,000; on summary conviction, imprisonment for 6 months and a fine of HK$50,000; the directors' personal assets can be seized under the Civil Procedure (Recovery of Tax) Ordinance to satisfy the partnership's tax arrears; directors may also face a travel ban (Blacklisting) and disqualification from acting as corporate directors for 5 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance Abusing the DTA

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits under the Multilateral Instrument (MLI) Article 7 and Hong Kong's domestic General Anti-Abuse Rule (IRO Section 61A); 50% additional assessment on the partnership's taxable profits plus a fine of HK$100,000; the IRD may also re-characterize the transactions under the Transfer Pricing Rules (50AAB-50AAE) and impose a penalty of 10% of the amount of the pricing adjustment.

Director personal liability: The general partner and the shadow director (the individual who actually controls the Chinese operating company) are personally liable for the partnership's tax undercharge under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; civil asset restitution is ordered to the IRD for unpaid taxes; the Inland Revenue Ordinance authorizes the District Court to issue a Blacklisting Order prohibiting the director from leaving Hong Kong and freezing his bank accounts; criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years is possible under IRO Section 80(2) for failing to notify chargeability to profits tax when the partnership has no economic activity.
Money Laundering via Digital Payment Rushes and Cross-Border Pass-Through

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) Sections 5, 6 and 20; on conviction on indictment, a fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 14 years; the partnership's assets are subject to confiscation under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap 455) Section 24; the IRD joint-assessment unit with the HK Police Financial Investigation Division can freeze all partnership bank accounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and the designated compliance officer are personally criminally liable if they failed to exercise reasonable due diligence; under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622) Section 465, a director who knowingly causes the partnership to engage in money-laundering risks personal criminal liability; civil disgorgement of bonus and salary from the past 5 years is ordered; the court may impose a travel ban and issue a warrant of arrest for the director; in the event of bankruptcy of the partnership, the director remains liable for the fine and confiscation order, and cannot discharge the debt through liquidation.
