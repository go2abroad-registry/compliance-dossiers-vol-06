Competent corporate registry

Municipal Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) at the jurisdiction of the Foreign-Invested Partnership's registered address, operating under the unified supervision of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the local People's Government; online submissions are made through the national enterprise registration portal known as the E-License One-Stop Enterprise Registration System.

Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law does not impose a statutory minimum registered capital or minimum paid-up capital requirement on a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Capital contributions are fixed solely by the Partnership Agreement and may be in cash, in-kind, intellectual property, technology, or other lawful property rights. No blocked escrow account rule applies to the formation of the partnership. If the Hong Kong partner contributes foreign currency, the contribution must be brought into China through a licensed bank under SAFE foreign exchange registration procedures and may be credited to the partnership's foreign exchange capital account or converted into RMB for lawful business use.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: eTAX at https://www.ird.gov.hk. The Business Registration Office within the IRD administers the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) and issues the statutory Business Registration Certificate.

E-government filing portal

A Foreign-Invested Partnership operating in Hong Kong should: (1) register the partnership with the Companies Registry if formed as a Limited Partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or as a Limited Partnership Fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637); (2) apply to the Business Registration Office using the statutory Application for Business Registration (Form IR-BR) within one month of commencing business; (3) where a foreign non-resident entity is a partner, appoint an authorized representative or local agent in Hong Kong and submit certified copies of the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration, identity documents of the authorized representative, and a written Hong Kong address for service; (4) receive the Business Registration Certificate, which contains the Business Registration Number used by the IRD as the taxpayer identifier; (5) if the partnership or a foreign partner is carrying on business in Hong Kong as a non-Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), separately register the local place of business with the Companies Registry and update the Business Registration record with the branch or place-of-business details; and (6) use the Business Registration Certificate and Business Registration Number for bank account opening, invoicing, and all IRD correspondence. Hong Kong does not issue a separate TIN for partnerships; the Business Registration Number is the operative tax registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no partnership tax election, and no hybrid mismatch election mechanism. The partnership itself is not a profits tax taxpayer; each partner is separately assessable under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) on their share of the partnership's Hong Kong-sourced profits. For a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637, the fund may apply for the profits tax exemption for qualified private funds, but that is a fund-level exemption election, not a partnership classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, Sales and Use Tax, or other general indirect tax is levied in Hong Kong. The only similar indirect taxes are selective excise duties on tobacco, alcoholic liquor, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/Sales Tax registration threshold because no such indirect tax exists. Every person carrying on business in Hong Kong is required to hold a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, export supplies cannot be zero-rated and there is no VAT refund mechanism. Financial market news aggregation and syndication feed services delivered offshore via API are outside the scope of any Hong Kong indirect tax. For profits tax purposes only, income derived from offshore services may be treated as offshore-sourced and not chargeable to profits tax if the services are performed outside Hong Kong and the customer is outside Hong Kong, subject to the territorial source principle and documentary substantiation; this is an income tax concept and not an indirect tax zero-rating provision.

The IRD requires taxpayers to retain sufficient records under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and electronic invoices are acceptable as business records, but there is no statutory e-invoicing clearance platform.

No VAT clawback or export zero-rating penalty is applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. However, if a taxpayer incorrectly claims offshore profits tax treatment or fails to keep proper records, the IRD may reopen assessments under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) for up to 6 years after the end of the relevant year of assessment, or up to 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion, and may impose additional tax of up to 300% of the tax undercharged under section 82A. For customs declaration offences, the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) provides fines and imprisonment for false declarations or failure to lodge required declarations, with penalties enforceable by the Customs and Excise Department.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China's FX legislation vests control in PBOC and SAFE. The RMB is convertible on current account for trade and service flows with genuine supporting documents, but capital account operations remain registered and administratively constrained. For a China-based Foreign-Invested Partnership with a Hong Kong corporate or general partner, each capital injection is a capital account transaction. The remitting bank in HK will send SWIFT MT103 into the FIP's designated foreign currency capital account at a domestic Tier-1 bank; the receiving bank must verify the link to the FIP's SAFE-filed foreign investment information, book it into the FDI registered quota, and permit RMB settlement only for sanctioned operating uses. Current account surpluses, including subscription income from a financial newswire API, are not subject to compulsory conversion, but service export receipts must be repatriated into domestic designated banks and supported by contracts and invoices when sold for RMB. Profit remittance to non-resident partners is an outbound distribution; the paying bank requires audited accounts, income tax withholding certificate or treaty relief documentation, the partnership distribution resolution, and a written statement of no false declaration. The main practical hurdles are timely SAFE registration, the negative-list business scope restriction, and proving that the foreign contribution has been received, invested, and taxed in China before any outward remittance is entered.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a mandatory conversion ratio on export foreign exchange receipts; the surrender requirement is effectively 0% for ordinary commercial goods and service exporters. The statutory repatriation timeline is anchored in SAFE's Trade in Goods monitoring system: each export declaration must be matched to an inward remittance within the collection verification cycle declared on the reporting record, and any delay must be reported through SAFE's deferred-receipt reporting module. Where the underlying payment term exceeds 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, the exporter must file a deferred-receipt report before the due date. For service exports, the FIP's financial newswire and API subscription revenues must be repatriated into a domestic bank account and be supported by the contract, invoice, and delivery evidence; there is no fixed conversion ratio, but the proceeds remain subject to SAFE's authenticity and anti-money-laundering review. Failure to repatriate or verify can result in a downgrade under SAFE's A/B/C classification and compressed FX facilitation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Not viable for serving as the FIP's primary onshore banking and treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC as payment institutions or commercial banks in mainland China, and they cannot access CNAPS or CIPS RMB clearing, nor can they connect to SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administration Information System. As a result, such an EMI cannot accept the Hong Kong partner's FDI capital contribution, cannot settle export or API subscription proceeds in RMB, and cannot execute profit remittance with tax clearance. A global payment facilitator such as Stripe may collect subscription payments under a Merchant of Record arrangement for digital services, but this does not replace the domestic designated-bank liquidity rail required for regulatory capital, SAFE registration, and outbound distributions. Viability rating: Low.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner instructs its bank to remit USD or HKD by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's SAFE-registered FDI capital foreign-exchange account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. The remittance instruction must identify the FIP, reference the foreign-invested partnership registration, and use the bank's foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contribution. The receiving bank verifies the FIP's SAFE foreign investment information, books the receipt under the registered FDI quota, and settles the proceeds into RMB through the onshore FX market only after a lawful purpose check. The RMB funds must remain in the FIP's capital account and be used only for approved operating expenses, payroll, rent, and third-party payments. The FIP must then complete capital verification with a mainland licensed CPA, issue a capital verification report, and report the contribution through the annual FDI information system. Any change in contribution amount, partner, or value requires an updated SAFE registration before subsequent remittances.Outward payments for financial newswire subscriptions, API licensing, cloud hosting, and data services should be executed by SWIFT MT103 through the domestic Tier-1 bank. The FIP must submit the SAFE payment application form, the underlying commercial contract, a valid invoice, proof of digital service delivery such as an API entitlement or usage report, and any applicable tax voucher. For RMB payments, the bank may route through CNAPS or the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) with clean MT103-equivalent messages. For hardware or equipment embedded in the financial data backbone, an import Commercial Letter of Credit or Documentary Collection may be opened at the domestic bank if shipment documents are required; however, for pure API and newswire subscriptions, letter of credit and documentary collection rails are operationally unnecessary and should be avoided to preserve the low-risk service trade profile. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for serving as the FIP's primary onshore banking and treasury rail. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10 million aggregate face value of controlled transactions in a given year for mandatory Local File and Master File preparation; HKD 6.8 billion consolidated group revenue for Country-by-Country Reporting; taxpayers with controlled transactions below HKD 10 million should still retain contemporaneous transfer pricing evidence

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply the Gulf Cooperation Council Economic Substance Regulations verbatim, but the 'economic substance doctrine' is embedded in the FSIE regime, the arm's-length rule under IRO s.50AA, and the non-resident withholding framework. For the FIP to preserve treaty and FSIE benefits, the partnership must maintain a Hong Kong office, employ sufficient locally based qualified staff, make strategic decisions in Hong Kong where Hong Kong-source income is asserted, and perform the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in the jurisdiction where the income is claimed to be sourced. For software and financial data licensing revenue, the OECD-modified nexus approach must be applied to allocate R&D expenditure against qualifying intellectual property income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(1) and s.82A impose criminal fines up to HKD 20,000 or greater upon indictment, imprisonment up to three years (summary) and up to ten years (indictment), plus additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged; the IRD may also assess estimated profits and deny the exemption entirely

Director personal liability: The courts may pierce the partnership veil where the general partners or directors of the corporate partner treated the FIP as their alter ego. Directors and general partners can be held personally liable for the unpaid profits tax, ordered to disgorge hidden syndication fees, subjected to asset freezes and charging orders, placed on the IRD enforcement blacklist, and prevented from leaving Hong Kong under departure-control procedures. Fraudulent trading may further expose directors to civil compensation and disqualification under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.902.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Artificial Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AA empowers the IRD to adjust profits to the arm's-length result; failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation is punishable under s.80(2) with penalties up to HKD 50,000 and additional tax up to three times the undercharged amount. Deliberate transfer pricing manipulation with an intent to evade tax triggers criminal prosecution under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who know that the FIP lacks commercial rationale for intercompany pricing may be treated as joint tortfeasors or shadow partners. Civil liability includes restitution of the shifted profits, contribution to tax and penalties, and disqualification from managing companies in Hong Kong. Hong Kong courts may also grant worldwide freezing orders and travel bans where assets are being dissipated through the syndicated finance payment rails.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure with AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Claiming FSIE without economic substance is denied under the Hong Kong territorial rules, leaving the full profits assessable at 15%/16.5% plus additional tax of up to three times under s.82A. Failure to keep adequate books and records is separately penalised under IRO s.80. Where fund movements constitute dealing with proceeds of crime, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) imposes up to 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced if the FIP is a facade for personal profit. Directors and general partners may be jointly and severally liable for all unpaid tax, interest, and penalties, required to restore diverted digital-asset and wire-transfer receipts, and blacklisted by the IRD and immigration authorities. Criminal liability includes disqualification, confiscation orders, and imprisonment for money laundering and fraudulent evasion of tax.
