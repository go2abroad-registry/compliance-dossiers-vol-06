Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, 3/F, Revenue Tower, 5 Concorde Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong; online electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (www.etax.gov.hk).

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident entity structured as a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP), the statutory tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued by the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). If the FIP is constituted as a limited partnership, the partnership must first be registered with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or the Limited Partnerships Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637) as applicable; after partnership registration is complete, an application for Business Registration must be lodged with the IRD's Business Registration Office within one month of commencement of business. The non-resident general partner or its authorised Hong Kong representative must supply the partnership deed, a Hong Kong registered office address, identity and address proofs (passport for individuals, certificate of incorporation and good standing certificate for corporate partners), and the partnership's principal business description. The IRD will then issue the 8-digit BRN, which acts as the de facto TIN for Profits Tax, employer returns, DTA treaty claims and banking AML onboarding. There is no separate VAT ID because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST. Non-resident partners are not subject to mandatory HK tax registration unless a business is carried on in Hong Kong or a Profits Tax filing obligation arises; a pure passive equity-holding SPV that only receives offshore dividends should carefully document its offshore character to avoid unwanted registration and assessment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Source (the FIP is transparent and each partner is chargeable only on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong; passive offshore equity dividends and disposals by a pure holding SPV are generally outside the territorial source for Profits Tax).

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not administer check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch elections; the pass-through result is mandatory under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) sections 2 and 22, and no entity-level relief election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST or Sales and Use Tax (only narrow excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Hong Kong SAR. Business registration under Cap. 310 is required only where a trade or business is carried on in Hong Kong; a very small income exemption under section 5A of Cap. 310 is not an indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because no VAT/GST is chargeable on supplies made in Hong Kong; a 0% VAT export zero-rating claim therefore cannot arise. To the extent the FIP must demonstrate offshore profits for direct-tax purposes, the IRD accepts documentary proof that the relevant goods were sold, delivered and paid for outside Hong Kong. For a pure equity-holding SPV, dividends and capital gains from non-Hong Kong subsidiaries are generally treated as outside Hong Kong's territorial source of assessment.

None - the IRD does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping system and does not mandate any API-connected e-invoicing platform. Electronic invoices and records are legally valid under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance (Cap. 553). For cross-border goods movement, the FIP must file electronic Import/Export Declarations through the Hong Kong Trade Single Window for customs statistics, but this is not an indirect-tax clearance mechanism.

There is no VAT clawback because no VAT was chargeable. However, where export/offshore-income substantiation is required for Profits Tax, the IRD may within six years (or ten years where fraud or wilful evasion is alleged) reopen assessments and claw back under-assessed profits. Knowingly making a false declaration or furnishing incorrect information is an offence under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) sections 80 and 82, punishable by a fine of up to HKD 50,000 plus treble the tax undercharged and imprisonment; false export declarations may also be prosecuted under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60). Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the documented contractual/financial-statement period does not create VAT liability, but it weakens the offshore-income evidentiary chain and may expose the FIP to reassessment, penalties, and adverse anti-money-laundering scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB exchange-rate regime with full current-account convertibility for genuine trade, dividends, interest and service transactions, but strictly controlled capital-account convertibility. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is an onshore Chinese partnership, and every inbound equity contribution, conversion of foreign currency into RMB, and outbound dividend remittance must be documented against the SAFE foreign-investment registration and the FIP registered business scope. Equity capital from the Hong Kong partner must enter through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank; conversion into RMB is allowed only for permitted business uses and against real underlying documents. Dividend remittances to non-resident partners require audited financial statements, tax clearance and withholding certificates, and a partner resolution; the bank must verify that the funds are current-account profits, not unreported capital. There is no discretionary convertibility for speculative or undeclared transactions. The treasury should therefore treat RMB/FX conversion as a documentary, transaction-by-transaction process requiring SAFE reporting and, for any capital-account matters, prior registration or approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The pure-equity holding FIP has no goods-export receipts; for any current-account receipts, including dividends, funds must be credited to the designated domestic current-account FX account and, where applicable, reconciled with underlying tax and financial documents. SAFE no longer requires mandatory surrender of export proceeds; the conversion ratio is 0% mandatory and the entity may retain FX in a permitted account or voluntarily sell it to a licensed bank at market exchange rates. For actual merchandise exports by any affiliated operating entity, proceeds must be repatriated to China and matched in the SAFE/Customs trade-monitoring system within the standard collection period (generally 90 days for general trade, extendable to 180 days under approved credit terms).

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary banking or clearing rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and virtual banks do not hold an onshore commercial banking licence from PBOC/SAFE and are legally barred from executing the SAFE reporting, BOP declaration, and FX capital settlement duties required for an onshore Chinese FIP. They may be used only as secondary interfaces if they are partnered with a licensed onshore bank, but the account and all regulatory reporting must sit with a licensed Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its own corporate account, with the payment in HKD or USD (or RMB if using a cross-border RMB channel) into the FIP's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account. The remittance instruction must state 'FDI capital contribution to the Foreign-Invested Partnership' and use the appropriate SAFE/BOP transaction code for foreign direct investment capital contribution. The receiving bank will verify the MT103 against the SAFE foreign-investment registration, complete the BOP direct reporting, and, when RMB conversion is required, request a capital-settlement instruction and permitted-use declaration. A registered CPA capital contribution verification report may be required before settlement of capital into RMB or before certain outward remittances; the process should be completed at the same designated bank that performed the SAFE registration to avoid dual reporting.For a pure equity holding SPV, the primary outflows are dividends, loan principal, and service fees, not goods trade. Dividends remitted to the Hong Kong partner are executed by SWIFT MT103 after deduction of the applicable 10% dividend withholding tax (or a reduced rate under the Mainland-HK tax treaty if the 25% ownership and substance requirements are satisfied), with supporting partner resolution, audited financial statements, and tax payment vouchers. If the FIP later refinances or makes working-capital advances to subsidiaries, intercompany loan repayments also travel via MT103 against an executed loan agreement and SAFE debt registration. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The profits tax return is generally issued on 1 April each year for the year of assessment ending 31 March. The filing deadline is typically 1 month after the issuance date (extendable to 3 months if filed electronically through eTAX by a tax representative). Where the accounting period does not align with 31 March, the return must be filed within the timeframe specified by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), usually 1 to 3 months from the issue date. All returns must be filed within the statutory deadlines; failure is subject to penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Transfer Pricing) Ordinance (Cap. 112I), transfer pricing documentation is mandatory for intercompany transactions that exceed: (a) HK$ 4 million for property transfers (tangible goods), (b) HK$ 500,000 for financial services transactions, (c) HK$ 250,000 for intangible property transfers (royalties, licence fees); the aggregate threshold for a controlled transaction is HK$ 5 million if no specific category is met. A pure equity-holding SPV with intercompany loans or licensing of IP above these amounts must prepare a Local File. Master File is required only if the multinational enterprise group's consolidated revenue is at least HK$ 680 million (or otherwise meets the OECD threshold) and the Hong Kong entity engages in covered transactions. For a pure equity-holding SPV with no intercompany transactions (only shareholding), no TP documentation is required, but economic substance is still assessed under the Hong Kong ESR regime.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong implements Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) through the Inland Revenue (Profits Tax Exemption for Funds) Rules and the broader exemption for offshore funds, but for a pure equity-holding SPV, the relevant doctrine is the 'economic substance' test under the OECD's Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 5 and the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department's interpretation of 'central management and control'. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for a holding company are: (i) holding of the shares/investments; (ii) making operational decisions on the disposal/acquisition of the subsidiary; (iii) managing the investment portfolio; (iv) preparing corporate reports and minutes; (v) appointing directors; (vi) maintaining statutory books and records. If these activities are performed outside Hong Kong, the SPV may not be tax resident in Hong Kong and may lose DTA benefits. If the SPV is a passive investment vehicle that merely holds shares and receives dividends, the IRD will still require that the company has an adequate office, staff, or a registered agent with substance. The IRD issues 'Heads of Tax' with administrative guidance; a pure equity-holding SPV without books, records, or board meetings is presumed to be a shell company. CIGA analysis under the HK Economic Substance Regulations (effective 1 January 2021 for certain offshore entities) applies to tax-resident companies; a pure equity-holding SPV is not a 'geographically mobile' business, but if it claims treaty benefits under the China–Hong Kong DTA, the IRD will verify substance on a 'substance-over-form' basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fatal Tax Evasion – Substantive Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Business Records

Statutory sanction: Sections 80 and 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) – penalty of treble the amount of tax undercharged and a fine of HKD 100,000; upon prosecution, imprisonment up to 3 years. Section 80(1) and (2) fine of HKD 10,000 per offence. Section 85 provides that aiding or abetting evasion is also an offence. Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), failure to keep proper accounting records is an offence punishable by a fine of HKD 300,000 and imprisonment for up to 2 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under common law if the director actively conceals the company's true income. Directors may be personally liable for the unpaid tax and the treble penalty under section 82, and may be disqualified as directors. Civil asset restitution: the Director of Audit / IRD may recover the tax and penalties from the director's personal assets. Travel ban: the IRD can apply to the District Court for an order prohibiting the director from leaving Hong Kong under section 76 (departure of person about to leave Hong Kong) if tax is unpaid. Criminal imprisonment: up to 3 years for evasion; if conspiracy to defraud the IRD is charged, the maximum imprisonment may be 14 years under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210).
Fatal Treaty Abuse – Shell / Conduit Company without Substantial Business Purpose

Statutory sanction: Under PRC's EIT Law, the tax authority may disregard the SPV and recharacterise the dividend as paid directly to the ultimate parent, imposing a 10% WHT instead of 5%, plus interest and penalties. In Hong Kong, the IRD may deny the foreign-sourced exemption and deem the profits Hong Kong-source, initiating an assessment at 16.5% on the full dividend or interest received, plus 5% additional tax under Section 82A for failure to file. If the SPV is a Hong Kong-incorporated offshore-fund-type vehicle, the IRD may apply the modified economic substance test under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2020; failure to meet economic substance triggers compulsory deregistration and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors of a treaty-shopping conduit can be held personally liable for the additional tax and penalties under both Hong Kong and PRC law. Piercing the corporate veil is applied where the SPV is an 'instrumentality'; the directors may be sued by the tax authorities for collusion. Bans: Under the IRD's administrative measures, a director who does not settle a default assessment may be disqualified from holding any office in any company for up to 12 years (Companies Ordinance, section 168). Travel ban applies as above. Criminal liability: if the director signs false declarations (e.g., Form IR1313A stating a beneficial ownership position that is a sham), he/she may be prosecuted for perjury under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) – unlawful oaths and false statements, maximum imprisonment of 7 years.
Fatal AML / Corporate Veil – Money Laundering through Unjustified Intercompany Loans or Dividend Forfeiture

Statutory sanction: Under Cap. 615, section 5 – knowingly structuring transactions to avoid reporting is an offence, punishable by a fine of HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Under the Organised and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455), money laundering faces a maximum fine of HKD 5,000,000 and 14 years' imprisonment. The Companies Registry can strike off the company for non-compliance with the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), and the IRD can issue a penal assessment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign loan agreements or dividend resolutions with knowledge of the illegal flow of funds may be prosecuted for money laundering. The corporate veil is pierced under section 306 of the Companies Ordinance (disqualification for fraudulent trading) – a director may be made personally liable for the company's debts if the business was carried on with intent to defraud creditors. Under the Law of the PRC on Offenses Endangering National Security, but more directly, the PRC's Anti-Money Laundering Law (2021) imposes civil and criminal liabilities on directors of foreign-invested partnerships. Asset restitution: the court can order the director to repay the full amount laundered. Travel ban: the Hong Kong SAR Commissioner of Police may apply for a travel ban on the director while the money laundering investigation is ongoing. Criminal imprisonment: up to 14 years' imprisonment under Cap. 455; in Mainland China, money laundering is punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment under Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law.
