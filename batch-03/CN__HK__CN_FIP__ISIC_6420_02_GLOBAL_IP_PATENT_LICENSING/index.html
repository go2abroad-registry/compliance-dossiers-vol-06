Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region jurisdiction: Inland Revenue Department (IRD); online portal: eTAX at https://www.etax.gov.hk. Business registration is administered by the Business Registration Office (BRO), a functional arm of the IRD.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine whether the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is carrying on a business in Hong Kong. A Hong Kong-source IP licensing business creates a registration nexus; a purely passive non-resident IP holder receiving royalties from Hong Kong without carrying on business in Hong Kong does not need a Hong Kong business/tax ID, though the non-resident remains potentially assessable to profits tax on Hong Kong-source royalty income under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Step 2: If the FIP is a Hong Kong partnership, or a foreign partnership carrying on business through a Hong Kong branch, apply to the Business Registration Office using the prescribed application for business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement. Step 3: Provide the partnership deed, all partners’ legal names and foreign tax identification numbers, addresses of all partners, the Hong Kong business address, the nature of the IP licensing trade, and the date of commencement. If the entity is structured as a limited partnership, registration under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) is also relevant. Step 4: When the application is accepted, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the Hong Kong tax identifier for profits tax, employer returns, and official correspondence. Step 5: The FIP must file profits tax returns in the name of the partnership when issued by the IRD, and should notify the IRD of any appointed Hong Kong tax representative or authorised person. The concept of a TIN/VAT ID used in VAT jurisdictions does not apply; the BRN is the statutory tax identifier for a business in Hong Kong.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box election, a US-style entity classification election, or a hybrid mismatch election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong. The general indirect tax rate is zero because no such tax is imposed; limited indirect taxation exists only on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), and it does not apply to patent licensing.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable in Hong Kong dollars because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST system. The relevant registration obligation is the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), which requires all persons carrying on business in Hong Kong to register within one month and has no general turnover threshold; there is an administrative fee/levy exemption for qualifying small businesses that is not an indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong imposes no VAT, there is no 0% export VAT rate, no VAT zero-rating claim, and no VAT export refund mechanism. For profits tax purposes, a taxpayer seeking offshore status for cross-border IP royalty income must establish that the profits arise outside Hong Kong; the IRD assesses the totality of facts relevant to negotiation, execution, enforcement, and the economic activity generating the licensing rights. The burden of proof is on the taxpayer.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible and open currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the Hong Kong dollar pegged to the US dollar in the band of HKD 7.75-7.85. There are no surrender requirements for current or capital account proceeds, and both residents and non-residents can freely remit HKD or foreign currencies through licensed banks. However, for the Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership acting as a global IP patent licensing HoldCo, onshore RMB convertibility remains governed by the PBoC/SAFE regulatory framework. Current account royalty and trademark licence fee receipts from overseas licensees are treated as trade-in-services income and are generally convertible after the submission of a valid IP contract, SAFE contract registration, a withholding tax clearance certificate, and authentic transaction documents. Capital account inflows (foreign partner capital contributions, loans, or capital repatriation) require separate SAFE approvals, a foreign exchange capital account compliance registration, and a subsequent capital verification report from a Chinese certified public accountant. Profit remittances and dividend-like distributions from the FIP to non-resident partners are classified as current account investment income and are remittable after audited financial statements are produced, the corresponding PBoC/SAFE filings are made, and the Chinese tax withholding or settlement has been completed. Practical hurdles include a conservative approach to valuations of patents and trademarks, strict beneficial ownership documentation, and SAFE's requirement that the commercial substance and purpose of the Hong Kong treasury centre be clearly evidenced in the transfer pricing and tax filing records of the FIP.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 107 (2012), the mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign exchange proceeds was abolished; an FIP may retain 100% of its goods export or IP licensing royalty proceeds in a Current Account Foreign Exchange Deposit Account or settle them into RMB at designated banks. All cross-border foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through the SAFE Foreign Exchange Receipts and Payments Verification system, and there is a statutory repatriation window for IP royalty or trademark licensing receipts of generally 30 days from the invoice date or the legal maturity date of the receivable, with a maximum extension to 120 days for service trade contracts if a documented reason such as staged payment terms is provided. Goods-related trade proceeds are subject to a separate 90-day duration, also extendable to 180 days in exceptional circumstances with prior SAFE bank approval. Any failure to observe the timelines triggers administrative penalties, revocation of the bank's direct reporting privilege, and enhanced audit scrutiny in subsequent cross-border transactions.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the effective central bank for the Hong Kong jurisdiction; the People's Bank of China (PBoC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) exercise supervisory authority over all cross-border RMB flows involving Mainland China, which is the ultimate sovereign home of the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, Currenxie, or eMoney issuers) are not licensed by the PBoC or SAFE to open onshore RMB accounts for Chinese FIPs, and they are legally barred from providing foreign exchange clearing services for current-account royalty transactions involving mainland China. Such EMIs may offer Hong Kong-based multi-currency digital wallets, but they cannot act as direct participants in the SAFE Trade in Foreign Exchange Verification System, cannot issue capital verification reports, and do not provide documentary collection, commercial letters of credit, or onshore RMB settlement infrastructure. The HKMA's Stored Value Facility regime under Cap 155 restricts their ability to accept large cross-border corporate wires without a licensed bank sponsor. The FIP should therefore treat any fintech EMI as a supplementary low-value disbursement rail only after the primary banking relationship at a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank has been established, and even then, EMI onboarding viability is extremely low for the core treasury structure.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong ordinary partnerships are not subject to the Companies Ordinance audit regime and there are no numeric balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount thresholds that compel an audit of an ordinary partnership under the Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 38) or the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 337). A statutory audit becomes mandatory solely in the following cases: (1) the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637, in which event audited financial statements must be produced annually without regard to size; (2) one or more partners is a Hong Kong-incorporated company, because that corporate partner must itself comply with the mandatory audit provision in section 406 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); or (3) the Inland Revenue Department exercises its statutory power under section 51(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to require the partnership to produce accounts and tax computations that include a report from a certified public accountant (practising) on grounds of inadequate records, prolonged losses, or transfer-pricing risk. For a global IP patent-licensing holding vehicle, the customary safe practice is a voluntary audit when annual royalty turnover exceeds HK$10 million (US$1.28 million) or total IP assets exceed HK$20 million (US$2.56 million), although these are not statutory thresholds.
