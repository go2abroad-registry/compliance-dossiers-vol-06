Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and registration portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.ird.gov.hk); Business Registration Office under the IRD administers business registration.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity forming or investing in a Hong Kong partnership must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of the partnership commencing business in Hong Kong. The application is submitted through eTAX or directly to the Business Registration Office; the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN). The BRN is the Hong Kong tax identification number for the partnership and for profits tax reporting by its partners. Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT ID because no VAT/GST is imposed. The partnership itself is not the taxpaying entity; each partner reports its assessable share of profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A non-resident partner with no Hong Kong presence and no Hong Kong-source assessable profits may not be separately required to register, but the partnership must register if it carries on business in Hong Kong.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong provides no check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime; partnership pass-through treatment is mandatory under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Hybrid mismatch rules, where applicable, are mandatory and do not provide an election mechanism. A qualifying single family office may, however, be able to rely on the Family-owned Investment Holding Vehicles (FIHV) profits tax concession in the Inland Revenue Ordinance where the statutory ownership and operation conditions are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong SAR)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales and use tax and therefore has no indirect-tax registration threshold or local currency turnover threshold. The Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) requires registration for any business carried on in Hong Kong without a small-business turnover exemption.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT claim or export VAT refund to substantiate. Goods exported from Hong Kong are outside the domestic indirect tax net; exports may still require an export declaration for trade statistics and customs control under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60). Qualifying offshore services supplied by a single family office to non-residents are not subject to any Hong Kong indirect tax. Hong Kong does not impose a statutory export foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day figure is retained as the internal documentary benchmark for cross-border proceeds substantiation.

  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing cross-border shipment
  • Commercial invoice setting out exporter/importer, description, quantity and value
  • Packing list
  • Bank inward remittance credit advice or bank receipt evidencing foreign exchange repatriation; this serves as the Hong Kong equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, which is not a Hong Kong statutory instrument

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not operate a real-time fiscal clearance, government e-invoicing, API-based clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping system for commercial invoices. The IRD eTAX platform is used for tax returns and payments, not for invoice clearance. Books and records must substantiate transactions for profits tax purposes, but there is no VAT e-invoice mandate.

There is no VAT export zero-rating to claw back in Hong Kong. False or incomplete export documentation can attract penalties under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Customs and Excise Ordinance (Cap. 342). The IRD may impose penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) where export revenues and related foreign-exchange receipts are misreported or undocumented. No retrospective VAT clawback exists because no VAT is chargeable in Hong Kong.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

On the mainland side of this cross-border structure, RMB remains only conditionally convertible: the current account is freely convertible for genuine trade and service transactions, while the capital account remains controlled. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) requires SAFE foreign-exchange registration, a bank-designated capital account, and a compliant purpose code for each inbound injection from its Hong Kong parent. Funds settled from capital accounts may be applied only to the approved investment scope, cannot flow into prohibited sectors, and cannot be used for unapproved offshore loans or entrusted lending. The Hong Kong dollar and RMB are freely convertible in Hong Kong, but when funds are routed into mainland China they are subject to PRC regulations, including the negative-list and anti-round-tripping rules. Profit remittance is permitted only after the PRC FIP entity has completed annual audit and tax filings, paid withholding tax on distributable income, and presented a tax clearance certificate and audited financials to the settlement bank. A statutory fixed conversion ratio is not imposed; all conversions must be effectuated through designated foreign-exchange banks against genuine transaction documentation. The overall assessment is a controlled convertibility regime requiring a documented, self-balancing treasury architecture.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods-trade monitoring rules, any export foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated and lodged with a designated bank in China within 180 days of the customs declaration date unless a SAFE extension is granted. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent for the forced sale into RMB, but the full 100 percent of export proceeds must be brought onshore and either settled into RMB or retained in a foreign-currency account that is covered by the enterprise's trade monitoring system. For this FIP, no export proceeds are anticipated because the scope is private investment holding; if incidental trade is added, the FIP must perform customs declaration and trade receipt reporting, retain underlying contracts, invoices, Bills of Lading, and must maintain a B-class or A-class trade closure rating to avoid enhanced supervision.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBoC), together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), governs mainland Chinese exchange controls; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises banks in Hong Kong but does not operate exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for the onshore PRC FIP. PRC law requires foreign-exchange and RMB settlement to run through licensed domestic banks; digital-only cross-border EMI providers such as Revolut, Wise, Airwallex, and similar neo-banks are not licensed to open SAFE-registered capital accounts or operational settlement accounts for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership. The Hong Kong parent may use an EMI only for supplementary offshore disbursements, never as a primary clearing rail or as a recipient of the FIP's capital account funds. Legal assessment: Low for offshore use, minimal for onshore, and not acceptable for the capital-escrow leg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound flow: (1) obtain SAFE foreign-exchange registration for the FIP with the Hong Kong parent as the investing partner; (2) open a capital-escrow foreign-currency account at the tier-1 domestic bank; (3) instruct the HK parent to remit via SWIFT MT103 using the precise SAFE-approved inward remittance purpose code for foreign direct investment, with the remitter name and ultimate beneficiary matching registration records; (4) upon receipt, present the MT103 confirmation, capital injection agreement, and partnership resolution to the bank for capital verification; (5) engage a PRC certified public accountant to issue the capital verification report; (6) settle the funds into RMB only for permitted working capital or investment outlays within the negative-list; (7) prohibit withdrawal for purchasing real estate or non-business securities except where expressly permitted. All flows must be captured in the SAFE capital project information system.For current-account operating payments such as family-office management fees, external custody charges, audit and legal fees, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 with the underlying service agreement, invoice, and SAFE payment purpose code. Capital-account outbound flows other than profit distribution after tax clearance, registered external loans, or SAFE-approved cross-border guarantees are prohibited. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$50 million (aggregate controlled transactions threshold triggering mandatory Local File preparation); Master File and CbCR for multinational groups with consolidated revenue ≥ HK$6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply a generic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime, but the FIHV concession requires the entity to carry on 'core income-generating activities' (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including strategic decision-making, risk management, and engagement of sufficient qualified staff with adequate premises. The IRD applies the 'central management and control' doctrine to determine the source of profits; a mere booking or paper office will not suffice. For partnerships, each partner's residence is considered for treaty purposes, and the partnership should maintain an economic substance file documenting board minutes, investment mandates, and employment records to withstand IRD challenge.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Misdeclaration of Hong Kong-Sourced Profit as Offshore

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82A: A person who willfully and with intent to evade tax is liable on conviction to a fine of HK$100,000, imprisonment for 3 years, and treble the amount of tax undercharged. IRO s.82A(3) and (5) extend liability to every partner and, where a partner is a company, to every director.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner and managing partners are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; the IRD may order a travel ban (blacklisting) under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Tax Disputes) Ordinance, and assets of individuals can be frozen. Upon conviction, directors face criminal imprisonment up to 3 years and disqualification from acting as directors.
AML – Dealing with Proceeds of Crime (Money Laundering)

Statutory sanction: Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25: any person who deals with property knowing or having reason to believe it represents proceeds of an indictable offense commits an offense, punishable by a fine and imprisonment of up to 14 years. Regulatory fines may also be imposed under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615).

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly authorize, permit, or fail to prevent money laundering transactions are criminally liable, subject to imprisonment up to 14 years, unlimited fines, forfeiture of personal assets, and debarment from bank custody services. Additionally, the High Court may impose a travel restriction s25J of the Criminal Procedure Ordinance (Cap. 221).
Shell Company / Economic Substance Non-Compliance (FIHV Concession Abuse)

Statutory sanction: The IRD will deny the concession, assess profits at the standard 16.5% corporate rate (or 15% for individuals), impose additional 6-year assessment on the profits, and levy penalties for incorrect returns under IRO s.82A, including a fine of up to HK$100,000 and treble tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the declaration are personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties; may be subject to a travel ban and asset freeze under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (Tax Disputes) Ordinance; if the false declaration is proven willful, they face imprisonment up to 3 years and disqualification as directors.
