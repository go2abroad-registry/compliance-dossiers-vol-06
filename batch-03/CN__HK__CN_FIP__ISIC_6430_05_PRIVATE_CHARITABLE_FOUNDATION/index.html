Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal and the relevant provincial STA e-Tax systems; tax registration data is integrated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). For the Hong Kong SAR non-resident interest, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong SAR is the competent authority for issuing resident person certificates under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement through the eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

Under the PRC multi-license integration regime, the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is first registered with the local SAMR office; the issued business license contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for mainland China. No separate tax registration application is normally required because SAMR automatically transmits registration data to the STA. The FIP must then activate its account on the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau, complete real-name authentication, update taxpayer classification (small-scale or VAT general taxpayer), apply for invoice quotas and digital invoice systems, and complete any required charitable foundation or fund registration with the competent ministry if the entity will act as a private charitable foundation. Foreign non-resident partners do not receive a separate PRC TIN for the partnership, but their identity, residency, and profit-allocation percentages must be reported to the STA in the partnership registration and annual information returns; a Hong Kong partner that seeks treaty benefits must obtain an IRD Certificate of Resident Person and file it with the STA before reduced withholding rates are applied.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with rigorous foreign exchange controls administered by SAFE. The Renminbi is freely convertible on the current account for legitimate trade, service, and non-trade payments, including grants and charitable transfers, provided the underlying authenticity and documentation satisfy SAFE's business authenticity and compliance review. However, the capital account remains tightly regulated; a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) receiving capital injections from Hong Kong must register the inbound remittance through the SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) information system, and the foreign currency funds must be held in a designated capital account pending verification. Once registered, the FIP may convert the capital to RMB for permitted operational expenditures under the national negative list regime, but any alteration in the use of funds or onward capital transfer to a third party requires a supplementary filing. From a treasury perspective, distributions to foreign partners or cross-border grant payments are treated as current account outflows that require only evidence of tax clearance and action by the managing partner, not prior SAFE approval. Large or frequent recurring philanthropic flows from an overseas related foundation may, however, be re-characterised as capital account transactions by the bank's compliance department, necessitating additional approvals. Overall, profit or donation remittances are suspended only when sanctioned partners or unverified recipient jurisdictions are involved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Rules on Foreign Exchange Administration of Export Proceeds (SAFE Circular 2012/1), a Chinese enterprise, including a FIP with an export licence, must repatriate all export proceeds into a domestic foreign exchange current-account account within 180 days of the export customs declaration; the deadline may be extended to 12 months if the enterprise is rated A-class by the customs/SAFE joint accreditation. There is no mandatory fixed percentage conversion to RMB; instead, the enterprise may retain the foreign currency up to an SAFE-approved settlement account quota, selling all amounts above the quota to a designated bank at the market mid-price. For a charitable endowment entity without export operations, the rule has no application, but the FIP must nevertheless observe any other repatriation obligations triggered by foreign interest or donation income.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs), whether domestic or offshore, cannot serve as the primary banking infrastructure for a mainland-incorporated FIP. Under the Banking Supervision Law and the Regulations on the Administration of E-money Payments, only licensed commercial banks may maintain clearing accounts and provide foreign exchange conversion services; EMIs are limited to supporting prepaid instruments and payment aggregations without deposit-taking functions. Therefore, any Hong Kong or offshore digital EMI proposal, including those offering virtual accounts or multi-currency wallets, is legally barred from replacing a Tier-1 PRC bank. An EMI may be considered only as an ancillary channel for highly controlled pass-through payments, but the FIP's treasury policy should require that all settlement rails terminate at a licensed Chinese bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funding from Hong Kong or other offshore sources should be routed via SWIFT MT103 into the FIP's foreign exchange capital account at a designated Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must carry the SAFE-specified code for a foreign direct investment capital contribution (identified as FDI Capital Injection in the bank's BOP reporting system) and include the FIP's name, the SAFE registration number, and a clear payment reference to the partnership agreement. Upon receipt, the bank will conduct a compliance review and hold the funds for mandatory capital verification by an independent PRC certified public accountant, after which the funds are available for conversion to RMB and permitted expenditure. Any change to the investment amount must be pre-registered with SAFE via the relevant filing platform.Grant disbursements and trade-related payments should be executed through the FIP's settlement bank using SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for donations, scholarships, or scientific grants; the payment must reference the underlying grant agreement and use the applicable SAFE outbound purpose code for philanthropic contributions. If the FIP's endowment engages in trade finance arrangements (such as purchasing equipment or funding proof-of-concept trials), it may also utilise Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) or Documentary Collections (MT400) through the same bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs), whether domestic or offshore, cannot serve as the primary banking infrastructure for a mainland-incorporated FIP. Under the Banking Supervision Law and the Regulations on the Administration of E-money Payments, only licensed commercial banks may maintain clearing accounts and provide foreign exchange conversion services; EMIs are limited to supporting prepaid instruments and payment aggregations without deposit-taking functions. Therefore, any Hong Kong or offshore digital EMI proposal, including those offering virtual accounts or multi-currency wallets, is legally barred from replacing a Tier-1 PRC bank. An EMI may be considered only as an ancillary channel for highly controlled pass-through payments, but the FIP's treasury policy should require that all settlement rails terminate at a licensed Chinese bank.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% standard rate; 8.25% concessionary rate for first HK$2 million assessable profits under two-tiered profits tax regime

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Hong Kong imposes 0% withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong entity to both residents and non-residents. If the partnership is treated as a Chinese enterprise for PRC tax purposes (e.g., central management and control in China), dividends distributed to non-China shareholders may be subject to China's 10% dividend withholding tax under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, subject to treaty relief.
