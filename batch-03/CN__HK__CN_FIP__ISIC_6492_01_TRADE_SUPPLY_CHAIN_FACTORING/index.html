Competent corporate registry

Local Municipal Market Supervision Administration exercising jurisdiction over the FIP's registered office (under the State Administration for Market Regulation, SAMR), operating through the National Integrated Online Government Service Platform and the municipal One-Stop Enterprise Start-up Window (一网通办); for the Hong Kong SAR investor-partner, the Hong Kong Companies Registry and a China-appointed Notary Public form the auxiliary attestation chain.

Governing statute

Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (adopted 1997, revised 27 August 2006, effective 1 June 2007), Articles 5, 8, 9(1), 14, 15-17, 18, 24, 33, 61-67, 85-90; Administrative Measures for the Establishment of Foreign-Invested Partnerships in China (State Council Order No. 567, effective 1 March 2010), Articles 2-6 and 9; Regulations of the PRC on the Administration of Registration of Market Entities (State Council Order No. 746, effective 1 March 2022), Articles 3, 14(2), 17, 19 and 24; and CBIRC General Office Notice No. 205 (2019) (Notice on Strengthening the Supervision of Commercial Factoring Enterprises), applied by the local Financial Regulatory Bureau upon delegated authority from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA).

Formation / constitutional filings

Form 1: 'Application for Registration of Establishment of a Foreign-Invested Partnership' (外商投资合伙企业设立登记申请书), filed together with the Partnership Agreement, the Capital Contribution Commitment Deed, and the statutory annexes prescribed under the Market Entity Registration Administration Regulations; the completed filing results in issuance of the Unified Social-Credit-Code Business License (营业执照).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Foreign-Invested Partnership under the Partnership Enterprise Law; the amount, form, and schedule of capital contributions are fixed exclusively by the Partnership Agreement, which may provide for cash, in-kind, or services contributions. However, for the commercial factoring license filing, the local Financial Regulatory Bureau commonly applies a paid-in operating capital guideline of RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD 700,000), and capital remitted by the Hong Kong SAR partner must be received through a designated bank into a verified escrow or capital account under SAFE FDI registration until the contribution is confirmed.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office; primary digital portal: e-Tax via GovHK (https://www.ird.gov.hk); trade declaration platform: Hong Kong Trade Single Window (TSW); the IRD also operates the AEOI/CRS registration portal for affected Financial Institutions.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: If structured as a Limited Partnership, register at the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap.37) or, for a fund-style vehicle, under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap.637); obtain the Certificate of Registration and the Partnership Agreement. Step 2: Within one month of commencing business, file Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) at the IRD Business Registration Office; attach the limited partnership certificate, the executed partnership deed, and certified identity/address documents of every general and limited partner (non-resident partners: passport plus bank or utility proof of address); no minimum capital is required and 100% foreign ownership is permitted. Step 3: The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) with a Business Registration Number that serves as the counterparty TIN for Hong Kong tax administration; renew annually (fee HK$2,000 per annum; the HK$150 levy is currently suspended). Step 4: The IRD will then establish a Profits Tax file for the partnership and issue Form BIR52 (Profits Tax Return) approximately 18 months after commencement; each non-resident partner must also obtain a Hong Kong Tax File Number for separate assessment under IRO s.22A and for treaty-related claims. Step 5: If the factoring activity amounts to money lending, a Money Lender's Licence under Cap.163 must first be obtained from the Registrar of Money Lenders (Companies Registry) and the licence number disclosed to the IRD; all transaction records must be retained for at least seven years under IRO s.51C.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible_Territorial_Scope — The Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is fiscally transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112); the partnership itself is not chargeable to Profits Tax. Each partner is separately/collectively assessable on its distributable share of Hong-Kong-source profits (IRO ss.22–22A). The territorial source doctrine limits taxation to profits 'arising in or derived from Hong Kong'; offshore factoring income is effectively exempt, but must be specifically claimed and supported. There is no entity-level profits tax, no capital gains tax, no business asset tax, and no net wealth tax for the partnership; a sub-tax distinction exists in that the IRD issues one aggregate assessment in the partnership name, while each partner's liability is computed at the relevant partner-level rate (corporate 16.5%, unincorporated individual 7.5% on first HKD 2 million and 15% on the balance, with progressive alternatives). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible_Territorial_Scope — The Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is fiscally transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112); the partnership itself is not chargeable to Profits Tax. Each partner is separately/collectively assessable on its distributable share of Hong-Kong-source profits (IRO ss.22–22A). The territorial source doctrine limits taxation to profits 'arising in or derived from Hong Kong'; offshore factoring income is effectively exempt, but must be specifically claimed and supported. There is no entity-level profits tax, no capital gains tax, no business asset tax, and no net wealth tax for the partnership; a sub-tax distinction exists in that the IRD issues one aggregate assessment in the partnership name, while each partner's liability is computed at the relevant partner-level rate (corporate 16.5%, unincorporated individual 7.5% on first HKD 2 million and 15% on the balance, with progressive alternatives). Non-resident partners are taxable only on the Hong-Kong-source share.

Available election forms

Available elections and rulings: (a) IRO s.22A election — any partner may within two years after the end of the year of assessment (or such longer period as the assessor may allow) give written notice to the IRD to be assessed separately on its share, which is especially relevant for a PRC corporate partner seeking a distinct DTA position; (b) IRO s.88A advance ruling — the FIP may apply to the IRD for a binding ruling on the treatment of its cross-border factoring profits, including the offshore claim, before filing the first return; (c) no US-style 'check-the-box' regime exists in Hong Kong; entity classification is mandatory and derives from Cap.112 and the partnership deed; (d) under the PRC/HK Double Tax Arrangement (2006 as amended by the 2019 Protocol), a PRC tax-resident partner may elect to rely on DTA provisions, but the partnership itself cannot obtain a Certificate of Residence because it is not liable to tax; (e) Hong Kong has not enacted domestic hybrid-mismatch election rules; anti-avoidance for hybrid structures is handled by the general anti-avoidance rules (IRO ss.61 and 61A) and, from 2025, the 15% domestic minimum top-up tax for large MNE groups under the global minimum tax framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, GST, or general sales-and-use tax; Hong Kong applies only narrow indirect levies: Stamp Duty (Cap.117) on chargeable instruments and excise duties (Cap.109) on alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oil. Assignment of book debts and factoring agreements are not generally chargeable to stamp duty unless they convey Hong Kong stock or immovable property; loan capital is expressly outside the charge. Hotel Accommodation Tax has been 0% since July 2008.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Zero threshold for any indirect tax registration because no VAT/GST exists. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap.310), every person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover, with an annual fee of HK$2,000 (the HK$150 levy is suspended); a Profits Tax Return must be filed even for offshore or zero-profit years.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not directly applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT/export VAT zero-rating mechanism. The functional equivalent is the territorial source exemption for offshore profits. The FIP may treat cross-border factoring income as non-taxable if it satisfies the IRD's permanent source rules under DIPN 21: (i) negotiation, execution, and enforcement of the factoring agreements occur wholly outside Hong Kong; (ii) essential operational functions — credit due diligence, receivables purchase, funding, ledger management, and collections — are performed outside Hong Kong; (iii) no counterparty is a Hong Kong resident and no purchased receivable is owed by a Hong Kong debtor; (iv) consideration is received offshore or, if received into a Hong Kong bank account, is immediately traceable to the offshore service; (v) the offshore claim is made in the Profits Tax Return and supported transaction-by-transaction. There is no refundable VAT credit mechanism; the 'relief' is an exclusion from assessable profits.

No statutory electronic invoicing, no real-time fiscal clearance, no API integration to the IRD for commercial invoices, and no digital fiscal stamping. Electronic records are legally recognized if they can be reproduced and are retained for seven years (IRO s.51C). Trade declarations for physical exports/imports are submitted electronically through the Trade Single Window (TSW), with phased mandatory use for certain declaration types since 2022–2025. The IRD's e-Tax portal supports electronic filing of Profits Tax Returns, and voluntary Peppol/EDIFACT e-invoicing is accepted as business evidence, but never for fiscal clearance.

The IRD may open field audits within six years of the year of assessment, and without time limit in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. If an offshore claim is rejected or partially denied, additional assessments are raised and additional tax under IRO s.82A can be imposed in an amount of up to 300% of the tax undercharged; criminal prosecution for wilful evasion or false statements can result in fines and imprisonment of up to three years. The IRD may also apply the general anti-avoidance rules (s.61/61A) to re-characterize transactions and claw back claimed exemptions retroactively. For cross-border transactions with PRC affiliates, the PRC State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose non-repatriation penalties and the China/HK DTA and BEPS-based limitation-on-benefits provisions may deny treaty benefits; intercompany factoring fees must be supported by contemporaneous transfer-price documentation to avoid adjustment and related withholding tax recharacterization.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains capital account controls with partial convertibility; current account transactions are generally convertible while capital items require SAFE approval. FIP profit remittances face regulatory hurdles including tax clearance and SAFE filing for amounts exceeding approved thresholds.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must surrender 100% of foreign exchange export proceeds to designated banks within 180 days of shipment date. Non-compliance triggers penalties, account restrictions, and mandatory conversion at official rates.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% for corporate partners; 15% standard rate for individual partners; two-tiered concessional rate 8.25% applies to the first HKD 2 million assessable profits.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% – Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends, partnership distributions, or remittances of profits to non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Return issued after the year of assessment (1 April to 31 March). For accounting period ended 31 December, due by 15 April of the following calendar year; for 31 March year-end, due by 15 May; extension to November is available for e-filing and where the facts of the trade require it.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Same statutory cycle as BIR53; returns are issued concurrently with the partnership return and must be filed within the stated response period (normally one month from issue date, extendable to three months via e-filing).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 200 million annual gross income triggers mandatory transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) for controlled transactions exceeding HKD 5 million per category; CbC report is required where the consolidated group revenue reaches HKD 6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations; however, the IRD applies substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance rules (IRO ss.61A/61B) to negate artificial tax schemes, and the Mainland/HK DTA requires actual management and economic activity in Hong Kong to secure treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Criminal liability under IRO s.82: fine up to HKD 100,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, and three times the tax avoided; civil penalty under s.80 can reach 300% of the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and effective directors can be personally assessed for unpaid tax arising from fraudulent filings; travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification orders are authorised under IRO s.36/37 and the Companies Ordinance.
Shell Company / Abusive Offshore Income Claim

Statutory sanction: Reassessment to Hong Kong profits tax on the entire profit with a late payment penalty of 10%; additional penalty up to 200% of undercharged tax under IRO s.82A; potential strike-off of the entity under the Companies Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Where the partnership is found to be a sham, the courts may pierce the veil and hold general partners and effective directors jointly liable for the tax debt, including asset tracing and travel restraint orders.
Transfer Pricing / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Adjustment by the IRD to the arm's length profit; penalty up to 10 times the undercharged tax where documentation is materially false; recharacterisation under anti-avoidance provisions may deny the transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors of the general partner are liable for negligence if they sign false TP declarations; personal assessment is possible under IRO s.63A, and directors may be banned from acting in Hong Kong companies.
Money Laundering / Unlicensed Factoring

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed deposit-taking carries fines up to HKD 5 million and imprisonment up to 2 years; AML breaches under the AMLO attract fines up to HKD 1 million and imprisonment up to 7 years; assets derived from the crime are forfeitable.

Director personal liability: General partners are personally liable under the Partnership Ordinance; individuals responsible for AML compliance who fail to report suspicious transactions face imprisonment and personal monetary penalties.
Unauthorized Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may deem the remittance as China-source income and impose 10% or 5% withholding; the payer becomes liable under CIT Art. 37 for unpaid withholding plus a 0.05% daily surcharge; SAFE may impose a forex penalty up to 30% of the amount remitted.

Director personal liability: General partners involved in the forex or tax evasion can be personally liable under Chinese and Hong Kong law; travel bans are imposed on tax debtors under PRC exit control measures, and directors may face restoration orders.
