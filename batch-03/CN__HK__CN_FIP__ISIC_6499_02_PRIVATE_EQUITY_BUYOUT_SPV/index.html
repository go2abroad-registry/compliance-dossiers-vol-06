Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office addresses are generally not accepted for registration of a limited partnership in Hong Kong; the registered office must be a physical address in Hong Kong. However, a service provider's address may be used if it is a physical location and the provider is licensed. For banking and tax authorities, a virtual office is likely rejected as it does not demonstrate commercial substance. The Inland Revenue Department may challenge tax residency if there is no substantive office operations. Banks, especially for private equity SPVs, require a physical address for account opening. Therefore, a virtual office is not permissible; a physical office or serviced office with meeting room and staff is recommended.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The LPF has no statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement and no blocked escrow account obligation under Cap. 637. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) accessible at the STA national website www.chinatax.gov.cn, administered by the local competent State Tax Bureau at the FIP's registered address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC); SAMR automatically shares registration data with the STA. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days of business license issuance, log into the e-Tax portal, confirm the preliminary tax registration details, and file the partnership agreement, business license, investor passports or foreign entity certificates, resident manager/responsible person identification, registered business premises evidence, and bank account particulars. Step 3: The USCC becomes the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification code; e-Tax will determine whether the FIP is a small-scale or general VAT taxpayer, and will activate the relevant tax obligations. Step 4: For each non-resident partner whose income creates a PRC tax withholding obligation, complete a non-resident taxpayer registration through e-Tax (Non-Resident Taxpayer Code) or file the foreign investor's identity and tax residence certificate with the withholding agent; treaty benefits under the Hong Kong-China DTA require a Hong Kong Certificate of Tax Resident Status. Step 5: If the FIP will export goods, complete Foreign Trade Operator Filing with the Ministry of Commerce, Customs registration at the exporting customs office to obtain the Customs Registration Code, and register the export business in the China International Trade Single Window before any customs declaration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: the FIP is a partnership under PRC law and is not an Enterprise Income Tax taxpayer; its income passes through to resident and non-resident partners who are separately taxed on their profit shares. The FIP itself is nonetheless a VAT and customs duty registrant for indirect taxes.

Available election forms

None: the PRC does not provide check-the-box entity elections or hybrid mismatch elections; the only election relevant to the FIP is the voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer for a small-scale taxpayer with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (annual taxable sales threshold requiring mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration); all FIPs must complete tax registration within 30 days of establishment irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for goods actually exported from China where the FIP has valid export capacity (Foreign Trade Operator Filing, customs registration, general VAT taxpayer status), the goods are physically exported and cleared by customs, the export sale is supported by invoices and contracts, foreign exchange or RMB cross-border settlement is received from the overseas buyer, and the cargo is not on the prohibited/restricted export list. Qualifying offshore digital or cross-border services supplied to non-resident clients outside China, consumed entirely outside China, and evidenced by offshore service contracts and overseas remittance may also qualify for zero rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form; the export equivalent of a bill of entry, including customs release number and security code)
  • Export commercial invoice and the VAT e-invoice (or VAT special invoice where applicable)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing export of goods by sea or air
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation issued by the receiving bank
  • Export foreign exchange settlement receipt and SAFE cross-border receipt record
  • Export sales contract or purchase order and export packing list
  • China International Trade Single Window export clearance record and customs release receipt

The FIP must issue VAT invoices through the STA-approved e-invoicing platform. Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (e-invoices) are transmitted in real time to the STA for fiscal clearance; VAT special invoices are generated by the tax-control system (Golden Tax IV) and carry an encrypted anti-fraud code/digital stamp. Invoice data, customs declaration data, and foreign-exchange settlement data are cross-matched by the STA in its anti-fraud compliance system.

The STA and General Administration of Customs will cross-verify the e-invoice flow, customs export declarations, logistics documents, and SAFE foreign-exchange collection records. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export, without foreign-exchange receipt, or on false/forged documentation, the VAT refund/exemption will be clawed back in full with late-payment surcharge (0.05% per day) and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under PRC Criminal Law, suspension of export tax refund qualifications, and taxpayer integrity blacklist penalties also apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime built on current-account convertibility and controlled capital-account liberalisation. RMB is convertible for genuine current-account transactions, including trade, services and dividend remittance, but only after the relevant bank verifies the underlying transaction documents and tax-clearance status. Capital-account items such as the FIP's inbound equity contribution, foreign-currency borrowing, profit distribution and liquidation proceeds remain heavily ring-fenced and must be executed through dedicated SAFE-monitored capital accounts. Each capital-account movement requires a BOPS declaration and must satisfy the real-demand principle before conversion into RMB. For this PE buyout SPV, inbound capital from Hong Kong partners is permissible but its use is restricted to the acquisition and designated operating purposes described in the partnership agreement; general treasury speculation is prohibited. Profit remittances are permissible but are subject to a formal distribution resolution, audited financial statements, proof of enterprise income tax payment at the partnership level or partner level, and, where HK treaty relief is sought, a Hong Kong tax residency certificate. Any attempt to use the capital account for speculative FX purposes, on-lending, or round-tripping with mainland-controlled funds will be rejected and may trigger a SAFE penalty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Although the FIP itself is a buyout SPV and not a trading exporter, any export receivables routed through its Chinese bank account must be repatriated into mainland China within 180 days of the date of customs clearance for goods or the contractual performance date for services. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the entity may retain the foreign currency in its export settlement account or convert to RMB at its discretion. The bank will release the funds only after matching the electronic trade-integrity data and the customs and SAFE declarations.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), exercising delegated statutory foreign exchange control and direct investment registration authority for mainland China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 40,000,000 (China Local File threshold for other related-party transactions; higher thresholds of RMB 200 million for tangible assets and RMB 100 million for intangibles/financial assets apply); Hong Kong Local File threshold: HKD 100,000,000 per category of controlled transaction; HK Master File threshold: group revenue above HKD 7,500,000,000 and controlled transactions above HKD 300,000,000.

Economic substance scope: China's General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) may recharacterize the FIP if it lacks real substance and is a pure conduit. Hong Kong applies the Economic Substance Requirement for offshore funds claiming profits-tax exemption under the DIPN 15H / DIPN 15A regime; the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must occur in Hong Kong, including strategic asset management, board meetings, and decision-making. The FIP must maintain sufficient employees, office premises, and financial records in the jurisdiction where it claims tax residence; otherwise, treaty benefits may be denied under the Limitation on Benefits / beneficial ownership clause and directors can be personally exposed.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and criminal liability for evading tax above statutory thresholds. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 82 imposes a maximum fine of HKD 50,000, treble the tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to 3 years for the offense of deliberate tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate veil is pierced, directors and general partners are personally liable for the unpaid tax, civil penalties, and criminal fines; their personal bank accounts and properties may be frozen; Chinese tax authorities may impose exit bans (blacklisting) under the 'Measures for Managing Tax-Related Security Deposits', and Hong Kong courts may grant a Mareva injunction and committal orders; criminal conviction can lead to custodial sentences and disqualification as a director.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: PRC Special Tax Adjustment regulations under Articles 41-44 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 112 of the Implementation Rules: the tax authority may make an adjustment, impose interest of 5% per annum over the benchmark rate plus daily 0.05% surcharge, and levy a penalty from 10% to 50% of the unpaid tax; Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 80 and Regulation 15H also allow penalties up to HKD 50,000 and 3x tax underpaid for transfer pricing non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation may be sued for breach of fiduciary duty, and assets in their personal names can be recovered to satisfy corporate tax liabilities; additionally, Hong Kong has a director liability regime whereby a director of an insolvent company is liable for fraudulent trading; Chinese tax authorities can count the director's personal property as security for the tax under Collection Law Article 65, and aggravated circumstances attract criminal sanctions under Article 203 of PRC Criminal Law.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: PRC GAAR under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law allows recharacterization of the entity and adjustment of tax payable; Hong Kong's territorial tax rules under DIPN 15H deny offshore exemption unless the entity satisfies the economic substance requirements; if the FIP is registered in an offshore jurisdiction, Economic Substance Regulations impose penalties, e.g. in Crest Island-type laws, but for Hong Kong the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2019 enforces material substance requirements for IP income; failing the substance test results in loss of treaty relief and additional taxes with surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners may be held personally liable for restitution of tax evaded through the sham structure; courts can pierce the corporate veil if the entity is found to be an alter ego, and in cases involving money laundering or fraud (concurrent AML violation), directors face criminal prosecution under the AML Ordinance Cap. 615, with fines up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years; CANAM - internationally they are placed on tax blacklists, leading to visa denials and extradition risks in aggravated cross-border tax crimes.
