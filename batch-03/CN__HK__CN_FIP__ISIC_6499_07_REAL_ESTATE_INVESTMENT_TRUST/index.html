Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; registration is administered through the Business Registration Office, with electronic submissions and case communication conducted via the GovHK portal and the eTAX electronic tax system.

E-government filing portal

A non-Hong Kong entity that carries on business in Hong Kong, including a foreign-invested partnership with a Hong Kong place of business, must, within one month of commencement of business, apply under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) to the Commissioner of Inland Revenue. The Business Registration Office issues a Business Registration Certificate and a unique Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number in Hong Kong. If the entity is or may become chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax, written notice of chargeability must also be given to the Inland Revenue Department under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), after which a tax file reference is assigned. A non-resident entity deriving Hong Kong-sourced real estate income but not carrying on a trade in Hong Kong must nonetheless obtain a tax file number and appoint an authorized representative in Hong Kong for service of process and correspondence. Hong Kong does not issue a VAT identification number because no VAT regime exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch elective classification. A validly constituted partnership is fiscally transparent: partners, not the partnership, are separately assessed on their shares of Hong Kong-sourced partnership profits. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax is imposed by the Hong Kong SAR. The only indirect consumption-style taxes are the duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold. Any person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST on exports. Export of goods is outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax. For profits tax purposes, the territorial-source principle excludes profits derived outside Hong Kong; a Hong Kong REIT cannot claim VAT zero-rating because no VAT system exists. Cross-border real estate rental or disposal profits are outside Hong Kong profits tax only where the economic activities generating the profits are performed outside Hong Kong.

  • No VAT zero-rating substantiation bundle is required. If the Hong Kong entity moves goods physically through Hong Kong, the statutory record is the Import/Export Declaration filed under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60A).
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill retained as transport evidence.
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the underlying goods movement.
  • Bank payment advice and foreign-exchange credit records retained as supporting evidence for source-of-profit determinations, where applicable.

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time fiscal invoice clearance, API-based tax-authority clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping. Invoices and underlying commercial records are retained by the taxpayer as supporting evidence for Hong Kong profits tax and Business Registration compliance.

No VAT clawback can arise in Hong Kong because no VAT is chargeable. However, false statements or returns made to the Inland Revenue Department to obtain a tax benefit attract civil penalties and prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), including fines, imprisonment, and additional tax of up to 300% of the tax undercharged. The Inland Revenue Department may reopen assessments within six years in ordinary cases and without time limit in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; the RMB is fully convertible for trade and service transactions, but capital account convertibility is subject to a progressive, quota-based system under SAFE. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) retains the right to convert operating revenues at a domestic designated bank for genuine current account payments, but any inbound capital injection constituting registered partnership capital must be housed in a dedicated foreign exchange capital account and can only be applied to verified business operating costs, land acquisition, construction, or income-generating real estate assets. Dividends and rental profits remittable to foreign partners are treated as current account items after tax settlement, but the remittance is blocked until the annual audited financial statements are filed and the bank confirms the accuracy of the capital verification report. The principal hurdles are the mandatory pre-registration of each capital injection with SAFE, the need for a non-receipt-of-loan certificate, and the temporary freezing of any excess working capital converted from CNY to FX. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation and total-amount verification, while cross-border property disposal proceeds from Hong Kong buyers are classified as capital items and require separate SAFE project-level approval. Practical convertibility for a REIT is at strong tier for operating rents (current account), but at a controlled tier for property sales and capital distributions, requiring layered regulatory filings and bank-level documentary compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods trade administration, all export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 30 calendar days from the date of the export declaration or the contract-specified settlement date. The enterprise may surrender 100% of the realised RMB receipts at a designated bank at the prevailing market rate, or retain the foreign currency in an export foreign exchange settlement account, provided the full amount is captured under the total-amount verification system. The statutory timeline for export proceeds is mandatory, and any delay beyond the 30-day window triggers an automatic reduction in the enterprise's cross-border trade quota and potential administrative penalty. For a REIT, rental income from Hong Kong tenants is not an export proceeds item, but if the FIP sells a real estate asset to a Hong Kong or overseas purchaser, the cross-border inflow is treated as a current account transfer from disposal of property, requiring the bank to verify the underlying sale contract, obtain SAFE approval for the settlement, and convert the funds to RMB only after the due foreign exchange registration is completed.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its administrative arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); for the Hong Kong SAR clearing leg, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) serves as the de facto central bank and cross-boundary settlement regulator.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Infeasible. Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut, Wise Business, Airwallex) are not licensed by SAFE or the PBOC to hold, convert, or clear RMB, nor can they open the mandatory foreign exchange capital account for a Foreign-Invested Partnership. The Chinese legal framework restricts all capital account banking services to domestic approved deposit-taking institutions. Such EMIs may provide ancillary expense cards or invoicing tools, but the core inbound capital injection, FX settlement, and outbound dividend remittance must clear through a tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The FIP cannot rely on an EMI as its primary account bank to satisfy corporate treasury and regulatory capital verification obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign partner instructs its overseas bank to wire the committed capital from an established international bank account to the FIP's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at Bank of China or ICBC using SWIFT MT103. The payment must include the proper SAFE purpose code (e.g., 'FDI - Partnership Equity Contribution') and reference the FIP's FX registration number. Upon receipt, the account bank verifies the remitter's name against the registered partners, checks the credit notification, and performs a capital verification with the local SAFE branch. After approval, the funds are credited to the capital account and can be converted to RMB for land acquisition, construction, or operating expenses. The conversion is registered under the 'Four-Step' mechanism: capital verification, transaction background check, settlement, and post-event random audit.For periodic distributions to foreign partners, the FIP will execute a SWIFT MT103 from its RMB or foreign currency operational account to the recipient's overseas bank, with the payment purpose listed as 'Profit Distribution - Real Estate Investment Trust'. The bank requires the audited financial statements, a resolution by the general partner declaring the distribution, proof of tax settlement, and the latest foreign investment filing. For rental payments from local tenants, clearing is done through the domestic China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or the Hong Kong Faster Payment System (FPS) for the HK clearing leg. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$4 million aggregate per controlled transaction category (goods, services, intangibles, financing) is the statutory threshold for mandatory contemporaneous documentation. Higher thresholds apply: HK$8 million for property transfers and HK$2 million for royalties/intangibles. The IRD typically requires entities with turnover exceeding HK$20 million to maintain a Local File and a Summary Report. If the FIP transacts with related parties in China exceed these thresholds, it must prepare Hong Kong Profit Tax Return schedules accompanied by a Transfer Pricing Report (DIPN 59).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but it enforces the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the MLI and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) of IRO s.61 and s.61A. For a Hong Kong FIP to enjoy treaty benefits under the Mainland/HK Double Taxation Arrangement, it must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. These include: active management of the real estate portfolio, employment of local staff, holding of board meetings in Hong Kong, execution of lease contracts, property maintenance inspections, and strategic decision-making at the partnership level. The IRD will deny treaty relief where the FIP is a shell, has no premises, no employees, and only signs documents through a representative. Taxpayers must compile Substance Reports to evidence CIGA. For REITs, the underlying property assets must be genuinely controlled from Hong Kong; if the property is in Mainland China and the FIP merely holds a passive investment, the economic substance requirement remains but may be satisfied by having a Hong Kong serviced office and an investment manager.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Deliberate Understatement of Rental Income

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(2) - Fraud or wilful evasion: fine up to HK$50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, plus an additional penalty equal to three times the amount of tax undercharged. Under s.82(1), making an incorrect return without intent to evade: fine up to HK$10,000 and triple tax surcharge. The IRD may also impose default daily penalties under s.80(2). Criminal conviction is possible if the difference in assessable profit is materially understated, commonly >30% of total income.

Director personal liability: A general partner or director of the corporate general partner is personally liable if he assists in preparing the false return. The IRD can issue an assessment in the name of the partnership and then recover from the partner's personal assets under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance. Under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, if a director uses the partnership as a mere facade to evade tax, the corporate veil will be lifted. Directors may also be prohibited from leaving Hong Kong (travel blacklisting) until tax is settled, and criminal imprisonment terms apply on conviction. The IRD may seek a personal tax demand against the director as the 'responsible person' for the partnership under s.52.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance - Failure to Provide Arm's Length Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.61A, the IRD may adjust the assessable profits to reflect an arm’s length price and impose additional tax. Failure to produce documents on demand is an offense punishable under s.51C with a fine of HK$10,000. Where tax avoidance is established, a further penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged may be added. For a partnership, the allocable profit adjustments are attributed to partners, potentially increasing their HK profits tax liability.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate general partner who deliberately disregard the transfer pricing requirements may be personally liable for civil penalties up to HK$200,000 plus tax shortfall. The IRD can apply to the High Court for an order disqualifying the director from acting as a director for up to 15 years (Companies Ordinance s.168E). In gross cases of fraudulent pricing, imprisonment up to 2 years may be imposed. Additionally, the director may be held personally liable for a civil action in restitution brought by the partners if his negligence reduces the partnership’s tax penalty immunity.
Treaty Abuse - Shell Company Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD will apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule (IRO s.61) and the MLI Principal Purpose Test to deny DTA benefits. The full profits tax at 16.5% (or 15% for non-corporates) may be imposed on deemed assessable profits, plus a penalty under s.20B of the Tax Law (where applicable). In Hong Kong, circumvention of DTA provisions triggers additional tax of 100% of the undercharged amount and a fine of up to HK$10,000. The IRD is empowered to issue a penalty assessment under s.82A; a director found knowingly concerned in the abuse faces a fine not exceeding HK$200,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced if the FIP is shown to be a sham or a mere conduit. Directors may be held jointly liable for the full tax amount and penalties; the IRD may procure a winding-up order and subsequently issue a bankruptcy petition against the directors. Civil asset restitution may be obtained through a court order under the common law of knowing assistance if the director assisted in draining assets from the real estate trust. A director also faces a travel ban (blacklisting) and may be arrested upon attempted departure while tax liabilities remain unpaid. Criminal imprisonment for up to 3 years is possible for participation in fraudulent treaty abuse.
