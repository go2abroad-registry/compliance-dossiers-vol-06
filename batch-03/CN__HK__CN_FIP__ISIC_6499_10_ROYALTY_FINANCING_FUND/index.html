Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in revenue-based financing for digital brands, a virtual office or commercial mailbox that merely provides an address without any staff, business records, bank communication, or physical oversight will be rejected by SAMR for registration because the FIP is required to maintain an actual business premises within the administrative area of the registration authority. Tax authorities (State Taxation Administration) and the local tax bureau will also reject a virtual-only address for general taxpayer designation and for issuance of VAT invoices. Banks designated for the FIP's operating account and capital verification require a physical presence, lease agreement, utility invoice, and on-site inspection. However, a genuine serviced office or shared business center with a binding office lease, a dedicated meeting room, physical file storage, and an on-site administrative or accounting presence can satisfy the registered office requirement for this non-licensed fintech business class, provided the address can receive legal service and the tax authority is notified of the actual place of business. The FIP should maintain a physical desk, a domicile plaque, and at least one part-time administrative officer at the address to avoid characterization as a shell or purely mail-forwarding operation.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), with the China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) web portal (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) serving as the exclusive digital channel for national tax registration, VAT filing, and export refund applications for the PRC mainland.

        
E-government filing portal
The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) must obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at formation; the USCC is automatically treated by STA as the entity's Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Procedure: (1) Register the FIP with SAMR and receive the business license bearing the 18-digit USCC. (2) Within 30 calendar days of issuance, perform the new-entity tax-information confirmation filing through the e-Tax portal; STA then activates income tax, VAT, and local surtax registrations by reference to the USCC. (3) For foreign non-resident partners not engaged in a PRC permanent establishment, the FIP must file a non-resident taxpayer registration with the competent tax authority using the e-Tax portal before any China-source royalty, financing, or loan payment is exported; required recordals include the partner's original corporate registration certificate (or apostilled equivalent), a certified English translation of the certificate, the FIP partnership agreement, a power of attorney or board resolution, and a VAT taxpayer information schedule. (4) The FIP must undertake VAT status assessment: if annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, registration as a General VAT Taxpayer is mandatory; if turnover is below that threshold and the FIP exclusively supplies financial services within mainland China, it may register as a Small-Scale VAT taxpayer (which is functionally ineligible for zero-rating export treatment). (5) After validation, the TIN is entered into the State Taxation Administration's unified information system and becomes the single reference key for invoice issuance, VAT filings, export declaration, foreign-exchange verification, and export VAT refund applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
None. PRC tax law provides no check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch election for partnerships; the FIP is statutorily transparent at entity level for income tax purposes, and all partners are taxed on their distributive shares. The STA retains anti-avoidance discretion to recharacterise any hybrid financing arrangement (e.g. royalty financing that is treated as equity elsewhere) to prevent double non-taxation; no elective classification other than the mandatory General/Small-Scale VAT taxpayer status is available.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable turnover is the mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration threshold for the FIP; below-threshold entities must nevertheless complete statutory tax registration with the STA and are classified as Small-Scale VAT taxpayers, but they cannot enjoy export zero-rating unless they voluntarily elect general taxpayer status.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rated VAT applies to exported goods when the goods are physically removed from mainland China through a Chinese customs port and the FIP holds the original export customs declaration, transport documentation, and full foreign-exchange receipt. For qualifying offshore services supplied by the royalty-financing fund (including revenue-based financing, royalty financing, and loan arrangement services) to a recipient in Hong Kong SAR or another non-resident jurisdiction, zero-rating is available only if: (a) the recipient is a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in mainland China; (b) the service falls within the zero-rate cross-border services catalogue published under Finance and Taxation [2016] No. 36 and subsequent STA interpretive circulars; (c) the FIP executes a written cross-border services agreement identifying the non-resident client and the foreign-currency amount; (d) payment is received in a convertible currency through SAFE-authorised banking channels; and (e) all export substantiation documents required for the VAT zero-rating claim are preserved and disclosed electronically. Domestic revenue-financing transactions with Mainland digital brands attract VAT at the standard financial-services rate and cannot be zero-rated.

China mandates real-time fiscal clearance of all VAT invoices through the Golden Tax Phase IV system. The FIP must issue full digital e-invoices through the STA-approved Full Digital Electronic Invoice platform; every invoice must carry an encrypted anti-fraud digital stamp and be transmitted in real time to the STA central database via machine-to-machine API connectivity. The FIP is required to integrate its billing system with the e-Tax platform for invoice authorisation, number allocation, cancellation, and credit-note reporting. Any zero-rated export invoice must be linked in the STA export refund database to the underlying customs declaration, transport document, and foreign-currency remittance certificate before the refund application can be lodged.

      
STA performs post-refund audit scrutiny using the national export-tax reconciliation and risk-control system. If STA finds the zero-rated claim unsupported by the mandatory documentation bundle, misdescribes the service destination, or fails to meet the foreign-exchange repatriation deadline, it will impose a retrospective clawback of the entire refunded VAT plus overdue interest, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the unrepaid VAT, and an administrative penalty ranging from 50% of the underpaid tax (for non-fraudulent omissions) to 500% of the false refund amount (for fraudulent or wilfully false zero-rating claims). Cases involving deliberate false export documentation are referred to the criminal justice authorities under the PRC Criminal Law and applicable export rebate supervision rules, with potential imprisonment, confiscation of illegal gains, and cancellation of the FIP's General VAT taxpayer registration. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may additionally impose fines of up to 30% of the unlawful foreign-exchange amount for any repatriation violation or concealment of export proceeds beyond the mandatory 180-day deadline.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China operates a dual-tier foreign exchange regime. Current-account items, including trade in goods and services, are generally convertible, while capital-account items remain subject to strict registration, verification, and approval gates. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in China must complete SAFE foreign direct-investment basic registration before receiving any foreign capital contribution. The levied capital must be parked in a foreign-currency capital settlement account at a licensed Chinese bank; conversion to RMB is permitted after the bank has reviewed the underlying contract and SAFE code. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong corporate partner are treated as current-account payments but require certified audited financial statements, a prior year-end tax settlement clearance, and an evidence packet demonstrating distributive share authenticity. A major hurdle arises from the royalty-financing model because the bank may query whether fees and revenue-share payments are true service trade or disguised capital flight. Surrendering export proceeds has been substantially liberalised: exporters and collective receivable owners must repatriate proceeds within 180 days but no fixed mandatory conversion ratio applies; funds can be retained in foreign-currency accounts or converted at will. Nevertheless, the SAFE electronic payment verification system will match every outward remittance against the underlying transaction evidence. Hong Kong itself imposes no exchange controls, so the treasury layer can use Hong Kong as a free-floating cross-border liquidity gateway, but the PRC FIP entity must remain fully documented under SAFE.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the SAFE Verification and Administration Measures for Export Proceeds, all export foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated to China no later than 180 days after the date of export declaration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio at present; proceeds may be credited to a foreign-currency settlement account or converted into RMB at the receiving commercial bank. The bank must verify the electronic trade-verification files, customs declaration forms, invoices, and transport documents. For the FIP's royalty-financing receivables, such inflows are technically current-account receipts under service trade; they must be protected by execution-ready contracts and complete billing documentation, and any conversion must follow the bank's SAFE registration table.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central monetary authority; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the monetary authority for Hong Kong SAR

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Ebury, or Currencycloud are not licensed to serve as the principal banking rail for an onshore PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership. They cannot hold RMB current accounts, cannot access CIPS or China's RTGS system, and are not recognised by SAFE as qualified foreign-exchange settlement banks. They may function only as supplementary collection or disbursement vehicles for Hong Kong treasury entities outside the PRC regulatory perimeter. The feasibility rating for the PRC FIP's onshore capital injection, trade settlement, and profit remittance is Low; all regulated treasury flows must be executed through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank with SAFE filing capabilities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital from the Hong Kong partner must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from a licensed HK bank to the FIP's capital escrow account at a Chinese Tier-1 bank. The SWIFT message must carry the appropriate SAFE transaction purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise contribution and must include the SAFE FDI basic registration reference. The receiving bank will verify the partnership establishment certificate, the SAFE registration confirmation, the shareholder resolution, and the underlying remitter-payee relationship. Upon receipt, the foreign currency may be converted to RMB and credited to the operating account. A capital verification procedure, either via the bank's system or a certified CPA report, will confirm the full injection and update the FIP's capital ledger within the prescribed statutory period.For operating disbursements and profit distributions to Hong Kong or other offshore parties, the FIP should utilise SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct current-account payments, commercial letters of credit for large or shipment-linked trade obligations, and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-value documentary trade invoices. Every outward payment must be pre-filed in the SAFE service-trade payment verification system with the underlying contract, customs declaration, invoice, tax certificate, and any approval or registration certificate required by the bank. Payment instructions must use the exact SAFE transaction-purpose code corresponding to the payment class to avoid automatic rejection.Onshore RMB forward contracts with real trade or investment exposure background executed with a licensed PRC commercial bankCross-currency swaps or interest-rate swaps for FX liabilities arising from the FIP's onshore borrowing or long-term capital movements, requiring underlying exposure documentationOffshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts executed through the Hong Kong treasury layer for hedging RMB exposure outside China, as onshore NDF is not permittedNo speculative options, exotic derivatives, or leveraged forwards are permitted by PBOC rules without an underlying current-account or capital-account exposure
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Ebury, or Currencycloud are not licensed to serve as the principal banking rail for an onshore PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership. They cannot hold RMB current accounts, cannot access CIPS or China's RTGS system, and are not recognised by SAFE as qualified foreign-exchange settlement banks. They may function only as supplementary collection or disbursement vehicles for Hong Kong treasury entities outside the PRC regulatory perimeter. The feasibility rating for the PRC FIP's onshore capital injection, trade settlement, and profit remittance is Low; all regulated treasury flows must be executed through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank with SAFE filing capabilities.

Stage 06
Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

      
Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

    

    

      
Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
15% for Hong Kong Profits Tax on unincorporated partnerships; 16.5% for corporations; the Foreign-Invested Partnership is a pass-through entity, so the entity-level corporate income tax rate is 0%, but partners are taxed on Hong Kong-sourced profits. On PRC-source flows to a non-resident corporate partner, the statutory PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate is 25% with a 10% withholding rate on dividends, interest, and royalties unless treaty-reduced.

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
Hong Kong does not impose statutory withholding tax on dividends. For distributions from a PRC foreign-invested partnership to a Hong Kong corporate partner, the PRC statutory withholding rate is 10% on non-resident enterprise income, reduced to 5% under the Mainland/HK Double Taxation Arrangement if the Hong Kong partner is the beneficial owner and held at least 25% of the relevant capital for the period required by the Arrangement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong: prepare a Local File if the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds HK$50,000,000 in the basis period; prepare a Master File if the worldwide group revenue exceeds HK$1,000,000,000. PRC: under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be maintained if total related-party transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000, or if a single related-party transaction category exceeds RMB 40,000,000. The FIP must also maintain a special tax adjustment file for cross-border financing and royalty arrangements.

      
Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong does not have a codified Economic Substance Regulation identical to offshore financial centers, the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the PRC general anti-avoidance rules require the FIP to meet a Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test. The FIP must demonstrate that strategic management, investment decisions, contracting, risk assumption, and human capital are located in Hong Kong. If the activities are predominantly in the Chinese mainland, the PRC tax authorities may recharacterize the financing or royalty as sourced in China, deny DTA benefits, and treat the Hong Kong FIP as a paper entity for tax administration and treaty abuse prevention.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: Under Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 82, fraudulent or wilful evasion is punishable by imprisonment for up to 3 years, a fine of HK$100,000, and an additional penalty of treble the tax undercharged. Under PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63, the taxpayer may be ordered to pay the underpaid tax plus a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall and daily late interest of 0.05%.

        
Director personal liability: A director of the corporate general partner who signed or approved the false return can be personally assessed for the underpaid taxes and penalties; courts may issue asset preservation orders, travel restrictions, and blacklisting against the director. In aggravated fraud or cross-border money laundering, the director may face imprisonment of up to 7 years under Hong Kong and PRC criminal law.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Thin Capitalization Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: The Hong Kong IRD may adjust profits under Section 50AAF of the IRO and impose penalties of up to 100% of the additional tax when transfer pricing documentation is inadequate. The PRC tax authority under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 44 may re-determine taxable income using a deemed profit method, impose a 5% interest surcharge per annum on the shortfall, and levy a penalty up to 50% of the underpaid tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's-length royalty or financing rates can be held jointly and severally liable for the additional PRC and Hong Kong tax; civil recovery may extend to personal bank accounts and assets. Criminal exposure arises if the transfer pricing structure is knowingly used to conceal true profits, with potential imprisonment for tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 and disqualification orders under Hong Kong company law.

      

      
      

        Beneficial Ownership and Shell Company Abuse / Anti-Avoidance
        
Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the financing as equity or illegal tax avoidance, and adjust the taxable income with interest and a penalty up to 10% of the adjusted tax amount. The Hong Kong IRD may also refuse to issue a Tax Residency Certificate and may impose penalties under IRO Section 82A for the false claim of residence.

        
Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil is applied when the Hong Kong entity is a sham: the general partner and its directors are personally liable for the unpaid Chinese and Hong Kong taxes, statutory interest, and penalties. Courts may order civil asset restitution, freeze personal assets, impose travel-bans and blacklisting, and issue arrest warrants where the conduit arrangement is used to perpetrate tax evasion or money laundering, carrying prison terms up to 10 years under PRC Criminal Law and Hong Kong anti-tax evasion legislation.
