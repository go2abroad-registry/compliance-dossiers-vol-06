Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the Electronic Tax Service Platform (eTax), and the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the eTAX online portal for Hong Kong Business Registration and Profits Tax registration

E-government filing portal

The PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is formed under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Administrative Measures for Foreign-Invested Partnerships. Upon issuance of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the partnership must complete tax registration with the local STA through the Electronic Tax Service Platform. The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier; it is used for all VAT, enterprise income tax withholding, and payroll tax filings. For VAT, the FIP should register as a General VAT Taxpayer when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, while below that threshold it may elect Small-Scale Taxpayer status if eligible. Because captive reinsurance is a financial service with large premium flows and the need to recover input VAT on purchased services, General VAT Taxpayer registration is the commercially appropriate default. Foreign non-resident partners not maintaining a permanent place of business in China do not separately register as enterprise income tax taxpayers, but they must obtain a China non-resident tax identification code through the withholding agent for China-source income; the withholding agent must file the statutory contract registration and any applicable Double Tax Agreement (DTA) benefit application with STA. If a Hong Kong entity or branch is also created, the Hong Kong Business Registration Office of the IRD must be notified through the Business Registration application, and the resulting Business Registration Number constitutes the Hong Kong TIN; the eTAX login credentials and taxpayer profile must be activated for Profits Tax filings, employer returns, and any property tax obligations, with the IRD territorial-source rules governing Hong Kong tax exposure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Neither the PRC nor Hong Kong maintains a US-style check-the-box election regime, and there is no statutory hybrid mismatch election for the FIP. The partnership is mandatorily transparent for PRC Enterprise Income Tax purposes, with each partner taxed on its distributive share, while the FIP itself remains a taxable person for VAT. Hong Kong Profits Tax follows territorial sourcing principles, and the Hong Kong captive reinsurance SPV may claim offshore profits treatment only if the underwriting operations and profit-generating activities are conducted outside Hong Kong.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold that triggers VAT registration in the PRC; any entity with taxable activity must tax-register upon receiving its business license. The mandatory General VAT Taxpayer turnover threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. The FIP may remain a Small-Scale VAT Taxpayer below that threshold, subject to reduced compliance obligations and temporary SME exemptions, but as a captive reinsurance SPV it will normally be required or advised to register as a General VAT Taxpayer in order to issue special VAT invoices and claim input VAT credits. Hong Kong has no indirect tax registration threshold because no VAT/GST is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT rules, goods are zero-rated only if they are actually exported from the PRC customs territory and the export proceeds are received in foreign exchange by the exporting entity. Qualifying offshore services, including certain cross-border digital services, are zero-rated or exempt only if the recipient is located overseas and the service is wholly rendered and consumed outside China, and only if the service falls within the statutory zero-rated cross-border service list issued by the Ministry of Finance and STA. For the captive reinsurance SPV, a 0% or exemption position is available only for reinsurance risk assumed from non-PRC cedents, with the contract risk, premium flows, underwriting decisions, claims handling, and management functions all performed outside the PRC. If the reinsurance service is supplied to a PRC cedent or the risk is situated in China, the transaction is taxable at 6% and cannot be zero-rated merely because the SPV is connected to Hong Kong.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form / Bill of Entry for export consignment) issued by PRC Customs confirming actual exportation of the goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing that the goods left the PRC customs territory to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase/sales contract, and export VAT refund electronic filing records
  • Foreign exchange inward remittance certificate (FIRC / bank receipt voucher) or SAFE-compliant bank remittance evidence confirming that export proceeds have been repatriated to China within the statutory deadline
  • For reinsurance and digital services: signed reinsurance/service contract, proof of the non-resident recipient status, statements of performance and consumption outside China, underwriting and claims files, and any applicable zero-rating or exemption application records

The PRC operates a mandatory electronic invoice system through the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform. Qualified taxpayers issue fully digitalized electronic invoices, whose issuance data is transmitted to the STA tax-control and anti-fraud system in near-real time. The FIP must use STA-approved e-invoicing channels or an ERP/accounting system with API integration to the regional STA e-invoice platform. Each fully digital e-invoice carries a unique tax-control invoice number and digital signature, facilitating automatic matching with sales declarations, corporate income tax data, and external anti-fraud analytics. Red-letter reversal invoices are subject to strict pre-clearance rules, and invoice quota limitations are applied automatically where risk indicators appear. Hong Kong does not impose e-invoicing or fiscal clearance mandates; IRD accepts electronic records, but the HK SPV should preserve ordinary business records for Profits Tax.

PRC tax authorities and Customs will scrutinize export zero-rating through cross-referencing of the Customs export declaration, transport documents, invoices, and SAFE remittance data. If zero-rating is claimed but not substantiated, the tax authority will claw back any refunded VAT, impose output VAT at the applicable rate, deny input tax credits, levy late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax in cases of fraud or intentional evasion. Export tax rebate fraud may also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the PRC, while SAFE may impose separate foreign exchange penalties for unrepatriated export proceeds or false receipts. Records supporting export zero-rating must be retained for no less than five years, and for at least ten years in fraud cases. In Hong Kong, any false offshore profits claim can be reopened by the IRD within six years, or ten years in cases of fraud or gross negligence, and may result in additional Profits Tax plus penalties of up to 300% of the tax undercharged depending on culpability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the HKD trading within the 7.75 to 7.85 per USD band. There is no distinction between current and capital account treatment for most non-bank corporates in Hong Kong; profits, dividends, partnership distributions, and capital repatriation can generally be remitted without exchange control approval. For the Foreign-Invested Partnership captive reinsurance SPV, the primary convertibility friction lies not in Hong Kong but in the Mainland China side. Inbound capital injection from a PRC parent requires NDRC and MOFCOM overseas investment filings and SAFE foreign exchange registration. Outbound payments from the HK FIP to Mainland China, whether for reinsurance claims, retrocession premiums, or service fees, must pass SAFE genuine-transaction verification and comply with capital account item restrictions. The HK FIP may freely hold USD, HKD, CNH, or other currencies; there are no surrender requirements in Hong Kong. However, when CNH is converted to onshore CNY, the offshore bank must use the CNH market and adhere to CIPS messaging and anti-money-laundering transaction screening. Overall, HKD convertibility is assessed as unrestricted, while RMB cross-boundary convertibility is conditional on documentary proof of commercial substance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio for foreign exchange receipts. A captive reinsurer may retain all premium and claims receipts in USD, HKD, or any freely convertible currency without repatriation or mandatory sale to the HKMA. For cross-boundary flows to or from Mainland China, SAFE requires underlying reinsurance contracts, tax invoices, and service trade declarations to evidence genuine transactions, but no surrender or conversion ratio applies to the Hong Kong-resident entity.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for Hong Kong SAR; for any cross-boundary RMB or foreign exchange flows involving Mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China exercises extraterritorial filing, approval, and monitoring powers.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is assessed as low for this captive reinsurance SPV. Hong Kong licensed EMIs and stored value facilities are not full banks and cannot provide regulatory escrow, underwriting reserve segregation, commercial letters of credit, or direct SWIFT/CHATS clearing membership. The Hong Kong Insurance Authority requires captive reinsurers to maintain policy liabilities and reserves in authorized institutions or licensed banks. Fintech EMIs may only support ancillary payment initiation if the underlying clearing is executed through a Tier 1 licensed bank. Therefore, relying on an offshore digital EMI as primary treasury rail is not legally viable and would be rejected by the AML compliance committee.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the PRC parent must be prefaced by NDRC overseas investment filing, MOFCOM certificate, and SAFE foreign exchange registration for overseas direct investment. The remittance should be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with narrative tag clearly stating 'Capital contribution to Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong', referencing the approved ODI code and the partnership capital escrow account. The Hong Kong receiving bank will require the MT103 to match the CPA capital verification report and the treaty name if the funds represent initial reinsurance premium reserves. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard Hong Kong profits tax rate applicable to the foreign-invested partnership (FIP); corporate head-line rate would be 16.5%, with a two-tiered first HK$2,000,000 assessable profits concession of 7.5% for non-corporate FIPs and 8.25% for corporations.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Hong Kong does not levy dividend withholding tax on distributions made by companies or partnerships).

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Profits Tax Return is issued on a block-issue basis; the FIP must file within one month of the issue date, unless granted an extension by the IRD for cases represented by a tax representative; ordinary extensions are granted to 15 August for financial year ending 31 December, to 15 November for ending 31 March, and to 31 December for ending 30 June.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$20,000,000 aggregate annual covered intercompany transaction value triggers the mandatory preparation of the Hong Kong Local File; a Master File is required where the multinational group has consolidated annual turnover of HK$200,000,000 or more and covered transactions exist in the group (the IRD applies a transactional threshold of HK$20,000,000 for goods, HK$11,000,000 for financial transactions and intangibles, and HK$4,400,000 for services in certain cases).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a UAE-style ESR code, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance applies a territorial-source doctrine and the anti-abuse rules in s.61A. A captive reinsurance FIP claiming Hong Kong profits tax class or DTA protection must direct Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: active underwriting, actuarial pricing, reserve management, risk governance, and board decisions. The IRD may challenge the arrangement if premium income is simply ceded from a Shanghai parent, a Shenzhen investment committee approves every risk, and HK personnel are absent; such facts cause the profit to be attributed to the PRC enterprise and the HK FIP to be treated as a transparent shell with no economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Underwriting and Reinsurance Accounts

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112) s.82(1): on summary conviction, a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for three years; s.82A additionally requires the FIP to pay a further three times the amount of tax undercharged. For false returns, the IRD may impose a civil penalty of 100% of the undercharged tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Where fraud is proven, the court disregards the limited liability of the partnership and the corporate veil of any participating HK manager: directors and controlling PRC partners are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties; asset freeze orders and the statutory departure detention (Blacklisting) under s.77 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance may be issued prohibiting travel; directors face personal criminal imprisonment up to three years and disqualification from directorship for a period deemed fit.
Shell Company / Sham Economic Substance for Captive Reinsurance

Statutory sanction: IRD may apply s.61A anti-avoidance to recharacterize the arrangement, assess the FIP on full 15% to 16.5% profits tax plus 3x penalty, and deny the China-HK Arrangement residence certificate. The Hong Kong Insurance Authority may revoke the captive insurer's license under Insurance Ordinance (Cap.41) s.128-133 for carrying on insurance without substantial local risk transfer; a licensed entity failing substance commits an offence punishable by a fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to two years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the captive SPV who signed sham board minutes are personally civilly liable to restore funds into the insolvent estate, and cannot shelter behind the corporate veil where the company is a mere facade; their HK bank mandates are blocked, they are blacklisted by the Immigration Department for departure; criminal liability for false declaration attracts imprisonment up to three years and, if proved fraudulent trading, up to seven years under the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance.
AML - Unlicensed Insurance and Structured Wire Payments

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap.615) s.5 imposes a fine of up to HK$3,000,000 and imprisonment for up to seven years for a person carrying on money service or designated non-financial business without AML controls; moreover, carrying on insurance business without an insurance license under Insurance Ordinance (Cap.41) s.128(1) subjects every unauthorized wire to a separate offence with a fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment of the relevant responsible person; the IRD also applies the 'no economic substance' presumption and denies tax exemptions.

Director personal liability: Where the offshore SPV is a conduit and controllers knowingly processed red-flagged premium wires designed to evade bank scrutiny, the corporate veil is pierced: directors and designated non-executive officers are jointly liable to satisfy customer and creditor claims, the Financial Intelligence Unit will include their names in the HK Money-Laundering and Terrorist Financing Blacklist, bank accounts will be closed, travel passports can be confiscated by the court, and a criminal sentence of up to seven years' imprisonment together with confiscation of personal assets will be imposed following national AML enforcement proceedings.
