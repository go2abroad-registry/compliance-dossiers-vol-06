Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its official online electronic tax registration portal, the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax / 电子税务局), operated at the provincial level; for Hong Kong SAR, the competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) with its eTAX portal

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in Hong Kong SAR with mainland China cross-border operations: Step 1 - Register the partnership with the Hong Kong Companies Registry (CR) and obtain the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the Inland Revenue Department, which serves as the primary tax identification number (BRN). Step 2 - Apply for a Certificate of Good Standing and, if required, a Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) via the IRD's eTAX system. Step 3 - For mainland China tax nexus, apply for a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 统一社会信用代码) by registering the foreign-invested partnership with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), which simultaneously creates the tax registration record in the STA's Jin San system. Step 4 - Complete the tax registration within 30 days of obtaining the business license through the provincial Electronic Tax Administration portal, including uploading the partnership agreement, the Hong Kong BRC, the designated responsible person's passport copies, and the proof of registered address. Step 5 - Activate the e-tax account and obtain the tax registration certificate (税务登记证) or its electronic equivalent, which integrates with the USCC. Step 6 - For jurisdictions where the FIP is treated as pass-through, apply for a Certificate of Resident Status for tax treaty relief if needed, and register for withholding tax obligations on behalf of non-resident partners.

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None specific under mainland China's partnership taxation regime; partnerships are not subject to entity-level enterprise income tax but are pass-through to partners, with each partner taxed individually; Hong Kong SAR does not provide check-the-box elections for partnerships, but partnerships may elect to be taxed under Profits Tax on a territorial source basis; hybrid mismatch rules under mainland China's General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in the Enterprise Income Tax Law apply, but no formal statutory check-the-box election exists for FIPs.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland China: RMB 5,000,000 annual sales for general VAT taxpayer registration; lower thresholds may apply depending on industry and local implementation, with small-scale taxpayers at RMB 500,000 annual sales being subject to simplified VAT rates; Hong Kong SAR Business Registration is mandatory irrespective of turnover, but Profits Tax applies only to profits sourced in Hong Kong, and there is no VAT registration threshold

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from mainland China qualifies for VAT zero-rating (0%) or VAT exemption with refund under the current export VAT rebate policy, provided the goods are physically exported through customs declaration, the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the export transaction is genuine and supported by consideration in foreign currency, and the exporter has received the full export proceeds through designated foreign exchange settlement; for cross-border services provided to overseas clients, zero-rating applies to internationally traded services such as transportation, logistics, and certain consulting services if the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China and the recipient is a non-resident enterprise; legal services related to cross-border commercial litigation qualify if the service is rendered to an overseas entity and the benefit is derived outside China, subject to the prevailing STA administrative guidance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (出口货物报关单 / Export Declaration Form with customs stamp or electronic customs clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (出口收汇核销单) or bank foreign exchange receipt record (银行结汇水单) evidencing repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and sales contract
  • VAT special invoice or ordinary invoice issued in accordance with the electronic invoice mandate
  • Export VAT rebate application form (出口退税申报表) filed through the STA's export tax rebate system

Mainland China enforces the mandatory use of the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice System (公共发票服务平台) for all VAT transactions; general VAT taxpayers must issue digital invoices through the e-tax platform using the Unified Social Credit Code as the tax identifier; the system requires real-time automated data transmission to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV (金税四期) system, which applies advanced data analytics for anti-fraud purposes; special VAT invoices (增值税专用发票) and ordinary digital invoices must be generated with a unique invoice code and anti-counterfeit QR code; Hong Kong SAR uses eTAX for tax filing but does not mandate electronic invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance for commercial invoices.

The STA conducts post-export audits using Golden Tax Phase IV data matching between export declarations, foreign exchange receipts, and VAT invoice issuance; if the export zero-rating is found to be fraudulent or unsupported by actual customs clearance and foreign exchange repatriation, the taxpayer must repay the refunded VAT or the exempted tax with interest, and is subject to a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax amount involved pursuant to the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law; criminal liability may arise under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax rebates, with penalties ranging from imprisonment and fines; additionally, the taxpayer's export tax rebate qualification may be suspended for a period of up to three years, and the legal representative and responsible persons may be blacklisted in the tax credit system.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates under the HKMA's linked exchange rate system, with no exchange controls on current or capital account transactions. The Hong Kong dollar trades freely within the 7.75-7.85 band against the US dollar, and a Foreign-Invested Partnership can convert HKD into USD, RMB/CNH, EUR or other currencies without prior approval. Profit distributions, litigation recoveries, fee income and liquidation proceeds may be repatriated offshore without surrender requirements or conversion quotas. Mainland China, however, maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime: current account receipts such as legal fees and trade proceeds are generally free and are processed through SAFE-registered banks, while capital account flows such as partner capital injections, cross-border loans and equity transfers require ODI/FDI registrations, approvals, or filing with MOFCOM/NDRC and SAFE. Profit or surplus remittance from China to Hong Kong requires audited financial statements, tax filing evidence, a formal distribution resolution and a valid FX transaction purpose code. The practical hurdle in the Chinese system is not convertibility itself, but documentary substantiation, sanctions screening, and economic substance verification, because banks will reject wires that lack a legitimate underlying transaction or a demonstrable ownership chain.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China: export proceeds from goods must be repatriated to a domestic bank account through the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System within 180 days of customs declaration, but there is no mandatory conversion ratio; an exporter may retain the foreign currency in an approved account or sell it to a bank. Hong Kong SAR: zero surrender obligation; export-service or litigation-fee receipts may be held in HKD, USD, CNH or any freely convertible currency, with no statutory repatriation timeline.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the central bank authority and banking supervisor for Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary treasury or client-money rail in Hong Kong. Under the Banking Ordinance, only an authorized institution can take deposits or hold client money, and a law-firm IOLTA/trust account must be maintained at a licensed bank. Hong Kong-licensed e-money and stored value facilities can support ancillary payments, but they cannot lawfully serve as a depositary for litigation settlement funds, cannot provide RTGS clearing, and create AMLO scope creep if used for client funds. The recommended architecture is therefore a Tier-1 bank as legal/trust account holder, with any EMI used only as a non-discretionary payment gateway where the bank requires it.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital inflows should be routed by SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the mainland investor's SAFE-designated bank to the HK FIP's Tier-1 account, using the SAFE foreign-exchange transaction category for Outbound Direct Investment (ODI) and the payment reason equity contribution / capital injection. The receiving bank must hold the ODI registration certificate, the signed partnership/subscription agreement, the FIP Business Registration Certificate and the opening bank's legal undertaking. HKSAR imposes no regulatory capital-verification escrow, but if the FIP is also subject to PRC reporting, an appointed accountant may perform capital verification by obtaining bank confirmation letters and matching the inflow to the capital contribution schedule. A CNH inflow can be processed with the same documentation; HKD or USD inflows are converted at the current market rate.Outbound disbursements for cross-border litigation should be executed by SWIFT MT103 for foreign counsel fees, court lodgements, expert witnesses and enforcement agents, while HKD-denominated payments to Hong Kong counterparties clear through CHATS/RTGS. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for legal services; documentary collections are equally unsuited and should not be drawn against an IOLTA account. Turnover: For a Hong Kong-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership under Cap. 637, there is no statutory balance-sheet, turnover or headcount threshold that automatically imposes a full independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit at the partnership level. The FIP is not a company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); therefore the mandatory audit requirements under Cap. 622 that apply to Hong Kong companies do not apply to the FIP solely by reason of its size. However, two mandatory CPA engagements arise from the FIP's business scope as a licensed legal practice: first, an annual Accountant's Report under the Solicitors' Accounts Rules is required whenever the firm handles client money, regardless of revenue or asset size; second, the Hong Kong Law Society may require a certified audit or agreed-upon procedures report in respect of client accounts as a condition of the annual practising certificate. If the FIP later incorporates as a private company, that company would be required to appoint a Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA) auditor and to file audited financial statements under Cap. 622, with no small-company audit exemption available for active companies; a dormant company exemption applies only where no significant accounting transactions occur.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Hong Kong Transfer Pricing Documentation Rules (effective from tax year 2022/23), a Master File and Local File must be prepared if the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 680 million AND the Hong Kong entity's controlled transactions exceed either HKD 7 million (for non-financial transactions) or HKD 11 million (for financial transactions).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong operates a territorial source-based taxation system, not a general economic substance doctrine. However, the Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime effective from 1 January 2023 requires any multinational enterprise entity (including a partnership that is part of an MNE group) claiming tax exemption on offshore passive income (dividends, interest, IP income, and disposal gains) to satisfy an economic substance requirement in Hong Kong. The entity must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) – e.g., strategic decisions, risk management, and operational direction – take place in Hong Kong, supported by adequate employees and premises. If the partnership is merely a shell vehicle with no real Hong Kong substance, the FSIE exemption will be denied and the offshore passive income will be subject to 16.5% profits tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Return

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82, the maximum penalty upon summary conviction is a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus a further fine of treble the amount of tax undercharged. On indictment, the fine increases to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years. The Commissioner may also add a 5% late-payment surcharge and additional 5% after six months, and can assess additional tax on a best-of-judgment basis.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a director, general partner, or managing partner knowingly approves the false return. The individual can be held personally and civilly liable for the unpaid tax, penalties and treble additional tax, may be disqualified from acting as a director or partnership manager for up to 5 years, may be issued a departure prevention order (travel ban) by the Commissioner under IRO s.77, and is subject to criminal imprisonment up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.58G, the court may impose a fine of up to HK$100,000 for failure without reasonable excuse to produce required TP documentation. The Commissioner may also adjust the assessable profits to the arm's length amount and impose a penalty of 10% of the resulting tax undercharge. If the failure is accompanied by fraud or willful neglect, the criminal penalties under s.82 apply (HK$50,000 plus treble tax and imprisonment).

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners who knowingly direct or participate in profit shifting through abusive transfer pricing may be personally liable for the additional tax plus the 10% surcharge and civil damages to revenue. They can be barred from leaving Hong Kong pending investigation, disqualified as directors under the Companies Ordinance, and, in cases amounting to cheating the public revenue, prosecuted under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) with imprisonment up to 14 years.
AML / Beneficial Ownership / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Under Cap. 615, contravention is an offence carrying on summary conviction a maximum fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years; on indictment, a fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years. Regulatory authorities may also deregister the partnership under the Business Registration Ordinance, and the Department of Justice may apply for confiscation orders, restraint orders, and asset freezing orders under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners and beneficial owners face personal civil liability for any assets traceable to AML breaches; the court can pierce the corporate veil where the partnership is used as a sham to evade AML obligations. Individuals may be subjected to travel bans (immigration hold), disqualification orders, and criminal imprisonment up to 7 years. They are also personally liable to disgorge any improper profits obtained through the concealment of beneficial ownership.
