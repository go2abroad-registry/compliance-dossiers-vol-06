Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the local Municipal Market Supervision Administration where the partnership's registered office is located; online filings are submitted through the national government-service enterprise registration portal and the Unified Social Credit Code system.

Governing statute

Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (promulgated 1997, amended 2012), Articles 2, 14-16 and 62-63; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (effective 1 January 2020), Articles 2, 12 and 31; Regulations on the Administration of Market Entity Registration of the People's Republic of China (State Council Order No. 746 of 2021), Articles 8, 15-20; Measures for the Registration of Foreign-Invested Partnerships (promulgated by the State Administration for Market Regulation on 12 January 2010, last amended 2017), Articles 3-7.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form FIP-1: Application for Establishment Registration of a Foreign-Invested Partnership, filed electronically with the local Municipal Market Supervision Administration, together with the Partnership Agreement, capital contribution schedule, notarized Hong Kong investor certificates, registered office proof and Foreign Investment Negative List self-declaration.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

PRC law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital on a foreign-invested partnership. The partnership agreement may specify any contribution amount, currency and payment schedule; contributions may be cash, tangible property, intellectual property, land-use rights, services or other property. No blocked escrow account or prior deposit is required for registration. The only capital-related requirements are that the partnership agreement list each partner's contribution and payment deadline, that each partner truthfully contribute within the agreed term, and that the FIP report paid-in status accurately in the annual report on the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to industrial civil defense or high-risk chemical approvals. The FIP remains subject to the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China; the office must obtain a building fire-safety inspection or occupancy fire-safety certificate, maintain functional fire extinguishers, emergency lighting, smoke detectors and unobstructed exits, and keep in place an emergency evacuation plan. The Work Safety Law requires a safe office environment, electrical safety and emergency evacuation training for employees. Because no manufacturing, storage or hazardous installation exists, no Civil Defense hazardous-installation permit is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the Electronic Tax Bureau online taxpayer platform for mainland China; Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTAX electronic filing system for the Hong Kong SAR investment holding layer.

E-government filing portal

The foreign-invested partnership (FIP) is first registered with the local Market Supervision Administration (SAMR), which issues an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that functions as the statutory taxpayer identification number. Within 30 days of establishment, the FIP must complete consolidated tax registration with the competent local STA office using the Electronic Tax Bureau platform, at which point tax categories are assigned, including Value-Added Tax, Enterprise Income Tax withholding and Stamp Duty. Foreign non-resident partners that are otherwise not required to maintain a PRC establishment must obtain a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number through the FIP withholding agent registration module or by filing direct non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority. For the Hong Kong layer, the investor must obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance; the BRC number serves as the Hong Kong tax reference. No minimum capital requirement applies and foreign ownership is permitted subject to the National Negative List for Foreign Investment Access.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None available under PRC law. Under Caishui [2008] No. 159, a Chinese partnership is transparent and each partner is taxed on its allocable share; there is no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election. In Hong Kong, the Inland Revenue Ordinance taxes partners directly on their respective shares of partnership profits; a partnership may file a territorial-source offshore claim, but that is a sourcing claim rather than an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of taxable turnover over any consecutive 12-month period for mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer in mainland China; all established enterprises including the FIP must complete taxpayer registration upon formation regardless of turnover. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT registration threshold because no indirect tax is imposed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from mainland China qualify for the 0% VAT export rate under the PRC export VAT rebate scheme when the seller is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods physically depart the PRC customs territory, and all customs export procedures are completed. Qualifying cross-border services, including outsourced VCFO and multicurrency bookkeeping services, are eligible for VAT exemption where the recipient is a non-resident outside China, the recipient has no PRC establishment and the service is wholly consumed outside China under Caishui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4 and STA Announcement [2016] No. 29; services within the statutory zero-rated cross-border list may instead apply 0% VAT with input credit repayment. The FIP must file the cross-border taxable activity exemption or zero-rating declaration with the competent tax authority and retain all supporting contractual, payment and bank documentation.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Customs Bill of Entry/Export Declaration Form) with the 18-character declaration number and customs release evidence
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, air waybill or multimodal transport document evidencing cross-border movement for goods exports
  • Signed cross-border supply contract or purchase order identifying the overseas non-resident recipient
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued through the STA digital e-invoice platform
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent foreign exchange receipt confirmation issued by a PRC designated bank evidencing full repatriation of export proceeds

China mandates the use of the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform for fully digitalised electronic invoices; invoice data are transmitted to the national e-invoice database in real time, and each valid invoice receives a unique national invoice number, tamper-evident QR code and anti-fraud tax-control digital watermark. The platform supports API-based integration with enterprise billing systems, replacing physical fiscal device clearance with online real-time validation. Hong Kong SAR has no equivalent e-invoicing, fiscal clearance or anti-fraud stamping mandate because no VAT exists.

Unsupported or fraudulent zero-rating is subject to retrospective clawback of the repaid input VAT and uncollected output VAT, plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day under Articles 32 and 52 of the PRC Law on Tax Collection and Administration. False export claims are penalised under Articles 63 to 66 of that Law with fines of 50% to 500% of the tax involved; criminal fraudulent export tax rebate behaviour is prosecuted under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law. The STA triangulates invoice, customs and foreign exchange receipt data to detect phantom and inflated exports, and the statutory reassessment period is extended to five years where fraud is established.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is convertible for current account transactions under Article 8 of the IMF Articles of Agreement, meaning bona fide payments for services, including outsourced virtual CFO advisory fees, may be remitted freely through licensed banks once the underlying transaction is verified. However, the capital account remains subject to structured administrative approvals and registrations. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established in China with a Hong Kong SAR partner, inbound capital contributions must be registered with SAFE through the relevant commercial bank, deposited into a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, and strictly allocated to the partnership's registered business scope. The convertibility of capital funds is constrained by purpose-based management, anti-circumvention rules, and cash flow checks; capital cannot be freely deployed for domestic equity investment, real estate, or entrusted loans unless separately authorized. Profit and distribution remittances are treated as current account outbound transfers provided that the FIP has completed annual audits, paid all corporate taxes, and obtained a tax clearance where applicable. In practice, the China FIP will need to submit audited financial statements, capital verification reports, and tax payment vouchers to the remitting bank, and the bank will apply the SAFE 'actual transaction background' test before releasing the funds. Surrender requirements have been eased since 2012; export receipts and other current account collections may be settled at market rates voluntarily or held in foreign currency within a domestic account, but all receipts must be repatriated to China and reconciled through the goods and services trade verification system. Therefore, the FIP can access full current account convertibility for its operating revenue and distributions, but capital account flows, including partner capital injections and loan principal repayments, remain strictly file-driven and subject to bank-level and SAFE compliance reviews.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration (Huifa [2016] No.16) and the prior Measures for Foreign Exchange Administration of Trade in Goods, there is no mandatory ration-based conversion of export foreign currency receipts. The FIP or its conduit trading affiliate must repatriate export proceeds into a domestic bank account within a time frame consistent with trade terms, generally without unreasonable delay, and then either retain the foreign currency or settle into RMB at the prevailing market rate. All receipts must be matched against the corresponding export customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and collection documentation in the SAFE verification system; any advance payment or deferred settlement beyond statutory thresholds requires a separate trade credit report.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) retains supervisory jurisdiction for Hong Kong SAR banking counterparties but does not administer mainland FX controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex have very low viability as the primary banking and clearing counterparty for a China-regulated FIP. The PBOC's Administrative Measures for Non-bank Payment Institutions and SAFE's current account verification framework prohibit non-bank entities from operating an independent corporate settlement rails for a Chinese legal person without a licensed Chinese banking partner as the direct intermediary. Such EMIs cannot legally hold the FIP's capital account, cannot receive foreign exchange capital contributions under SAFE, and cannot issue a purpose-of-payment code accepted by the Chinese customs and tax systems. The FIP therefore must maintain a fully regulated domestic bank relationship for onshore treasury, while an EMI may only serve as a supplementary receivable voucher for foreign clients in low-risk jurisdictions, subject to RMB quota and compliance restrictions that realistically make this channel unattractive.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK investing partner shall initiate an SWIFT MT103 remittance to the FIP's SAFE-designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must clearly identify the payment purpose as 'Foreign Exchange Capital Contribution' and reference the FIP's SAFE capital registration number or the bank-assigned capital code. The bank will validate the funds electronically against the SAFe Capital Projects Information System, confirm the identity of the remitter as the registered foreign investor, and record the transaction for capital verification. Upon receipt, the FIP must engage a licensed Chinese CPA within the statutory time frame to issue a capital verification report confirming the actual contribution. The RMB conversion of the capital injection is executed at the onshore spot market rate, and the resulting RMB funds may only be used for the FIP's stated business operations, i.e., the outsourced virtual CFO services, and must never be diverted to prohibited capital account items.For current account service payments, including the settlement of subcontracted CFO consultancy or software fees, the FIP will utilize SWIFT MT103 transfers with standard current account purpose codes such as 'service trade fees' or 'management consulting fees'. The remitting bank will require the underlying service contract, tax invoices, and proof of service completion before releasing foreign exchange via the SAFE verification system. Merchanting trade settlements or physical goods payments under commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not relevant to this virtual CFO services business scope; if the FIP later adds trade activities, those rails must be separately opened under the trade in goods verification framework. International payment fees and correspondent bank charges will be allocated in accordance with the participating bank's standard schedule, and all outbound disbursements must maintain alignment with the FIP's documented business model.Onshore CNY/USD forwards (deliverable) with a real current account or capital account exposure registered under SAFEOnshore CNY/USD foreign exchange swaps used strictly for cash-flow hedging of projected service fee receipts or paymentsCross-currency swaps with a genuine underlying commercial exposure and prior approval/reporting through the licensed Chinese bankStructural hedging through matched multi-currency pricing in the virtual CFO contracts, avoiding speculative derivative productsOffshore non-deliverable forward (NDF) contracts are not permissible for a mainland-regulated FIP as a standalone hedge and will be rejected by SAFE; only onshore deliverable instruments with documented trade background are acceptable

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Cross-border fintech EMIs such as Wise or Airwallex have very low viability as the primary banking and clearing counterparty for a China-regulated FIP. The PBOC's Administrative Measures for Non-bank Payment Institutions and SAFE's current account verification framework prohibit non-bank entities from operating an independent corporate settlement rails for a Chinese legal person without a licensed Chinese banking partner as the direct intermediary. Such EMIs cannot legally hold the FIP's capital account, cannot receive foreign exchange capital contributions under SAFE, and cannot issue a purpose-of-payment code accepted by the Chinese customs and tax systems. The FIP therefore must maintain a fully regulated domestic bank relationship for onshore treasury, while an EMI may only serve as a supplementary receivable voucher for foreign clients in low-risk jurisdictions, subject to RMB quota and compliance restrictions that realistically make this channel unattractive.

Turnover: Hong Kong has no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption for incorporated private companies: every Hong Kong company must appoint a Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant (practising) to audit its financial statements annually, except only for a dormant-company exemption actively granted by the Companies Registry. For an unincorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong, the statutory audit trigger is not a size threshold but a structural or regulatory trigger: (i) any Hong Kong corporate partner that is itself an active company will require an audited financial statement to satisfy its own Companies Ordinance filing obligation; (ii) if the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund or holds a Securities and Futures Commission licence for regulated activities such as asset management or advisory services, audited financial statements are a mandatory licence condition; (iii) if the Inland Revenue Department serves a written notice under section 51(3) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance requiring audited accounts, audit becomes legally enforceable; and (iv) where a licensed bank, authorised money service operator, or payment processor imposes a balance-sheet threshold on the partnership's financial statements, the independent certified audit becomes a commercial condition of continued operation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10 million per connected cross-border transaction, or HK$50 million annual aggregate connected transactions, for mandatory Local File; Master File required when the multinational group's consolidated revenue exceeds HK$500 million.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted formal Economic Substance Regulations analogous to BVI/Cayman. Nevertheless, for offshore profit claims and CN-HK treaty benefits, the IRD requires factual evidence of economic substance: staff, premises, management decisions and business functions located in Hong Kong. A shell entity with no real presence will be denied treaty relief and offshore treatment, and its profits may be fully re-characterized as Chinese-source under the Mainland tax authorities' beneficial ownership test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False HK Profits Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82A: fine of up to HK$10,000 and additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged; criminal conviction may lead to imprisonment up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of the corporate partner or the partnership's authorized signatory are personally liable if the offence is committed with consent or connivance; civil recovery may pierce the corporate veil and allow seizure of personal assets and travel restrictions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Arm's Length Transactions

Statutory sanction: Chinese tax authority may reassess profits, impose a 5-year look-back adjustment, levy a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid EIT, and add daily interest at 0.05% of the overdue tax.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax; they may face travel bans and placement on the 'serious tax violation' list preventing outbound border crossing.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits, recharacterization of profits as China-source, imposition of Chinese EIT at 25% or dividend WHT at 10% without reduction, and penalties under the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR).

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners may be jointly liable for the resultant tax; assets may be frozen and a court may order disqualification from serving as a director or manager.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Violation Using Digital Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Under Hong Kong CAP.615, unlicensed dealing or aiding ML can attract a maximum fine of HK$5 million and imprisonment up to 7 years; under PRC Criminal Law, regard to 10 years imprisonment and fines for money laundering.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally criminally liable if they control or knowingly benefit from the laundering, and may lose their professional licenses; bank accounts and personal properties subject to freezing orders.
Failure to Keep Sufficient Books and Records

Statutory sanction: HK IRO s.51C: maximum fine of HK$10,000 and up to HK$50,000 for continued non-compliance; PRC EIT law imposes a RMB 2,000–10,000 filing penalty and the tax authority may assess income by deeming at 3% to 10% of gross receipts.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and may be barred from leaving the jurisdiction until records are produced; disqualification from company management can follow.
