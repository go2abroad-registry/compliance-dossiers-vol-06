Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) - National Electronic Tax Bureau at etax.chinatax.gov.cn (the official online e-filing portal for national and local tax matters)

E-government filing portal

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) established within mainland China, the non-resident investors must first file for formation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Unified Social Credit Code, which automatically serves as the enterprise's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). The FIP must then complete tax registration at the competent local STA office within 30 days of business license issuance, under the Measures for Tax Registration Administration; the tax registration is integrated with the business registration process via the 'one-form, one-window' service, and the Unified Social Credit Code is the sole national TIN. If the foreign enterprise itself (non-resident, without a permanent establishment in China) needs a TIN for withholding or VAT collection purposes, it must apply for a non-resident tax registration code under the 'Regulations on Non-resident Enterprise Tax Registration' by submitting the corporate formation documents, board resolutions, and a tax representative power-of-attorney to the local competent tax authority, which then issues a temporary taxpayer code (starting with 99) for filing and payment obligations. All TIN applications are processed free of charge; the relevant forms are available on the e-Tax Bureau portal and require a valid China Mobile/Unicom/Telecom phone number and the FIP's legal representative identity verification via the coded tax control device or e-identification token.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None; under the current Chinese Partnership Enterprise Law and Enterprise Income Tax Law, a partnership is not an 'enterprise income tax taxpayer' as a separate entity and no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists. The entire taxable profit is allocated to the partners under the 'distribution-after-tax' (先分后税) principle, with corporate partners subject to enterprise income tax at 25% and individual partners subject to individual income tax on business income (5%-35% progressive rates). No entity-level tax classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities engaging in taxable sales of services or goods in China are required to obtain a TIN and complete VAT registration at the local STA office. The mandatory threshold for classification as a general VAT taxpayer (with full input-output VAT deduction rights) is annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD 690,000). Below this threshold, taxpayers may register as small-scale taxpayers, initially eligible for a simplified rate of 3% (subject to temporary reduction to 1% where applicable) and exempt from VAT on annual sales up to RMB 1,800,000 if the sales volume remains within the statutory limits. However, cross-border supply of tax advisory services to overseas clients is not subject to this threshold for the zero-rating/exemption assessment; it is assessed on the service's consumption place and the payer's location.

China has implemented a mandatory nationwide digital invoice system, known as the All-Electronic Invoice (全电发票) / Digital Electronic Invoice reform, which requires every VAT taxpayer to issue invoices through the STA e-invoice service platform using a digital tax stamp and real-time encrypted fiscal code. Since the system is fully real-time, every issued invoice must be reported to the State Taxation Administration immediately over the secured in-house banking-and-tax API, with mandatory digital stamping (including the invoice code, invoice number, and message digest) under the 'Fiscal Invoice Security Control Machine' (税务UKey) environment. Enterprises must integrate their enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems with the e-seal API (电子印章接口) to ensure automatic issuance and transmission of compliant invoices; failure to use the official digital stamp or to report within the prescribed time renders the invoice invalid and results in aggregate penalties. The new system also establishes the taxpayer's digital account umbrella (税务数字账户) which centrally matches purchase and sale invoices, automatically identifies anomalies, and triggers inspection where the VAT returns do not reconcile with the e-invoice data.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with the renminbi only partially convertible. The current account is convertible in principle under SAFE's current-account administration framework: genuine service and trade foreign-exchange payments can be processed by designated domestic forex banks against authentic invoices, contracts, customs declarations and tax-payment receipts. The capital account, however, remains controlled. An inbound capital injection into a foreign-invested partnership must be registered with SAFE or filed through the designated bank, opened through a SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account, and settled in RMB only after the bank validates the capital item. Profit remittance is permitted after the FIP completes its annual audit, files or withholds applicable tax on partnership profits, updates its foreign-investment information report, and presents audited financial statements plus a partner distribution resolution; the remittance is categorized as a current-account distribution and must clear tax and BOI verification before leaving China. Subsidiary hurdles include transfer-pricing documentation for related-party management or advisory fees, monthly/quarterly FX monitoring reports, field inspections by SAFE, and the need to demonstrate real economic substance in mainland China. The Hong Kong arm is fully unrestricted on current and capital movements, but the mainland FIP remains bound by PBOC/SAFE liquidity and conversion controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE trade monitoring rules, China no longer imposes a mandatory percentage surrender of export foreign-exchange receipts to the central bank. Export proceeds for goods or services must generally be repatriated into China through a licensed domestic bank within a statutory window, normally 180 days from the date of export declaration or receipt, and must be reported in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System with supporting customs declarations, invoices, logistics documents and contract evidence. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain proceeds in a foreign-exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB at their operating discretion, provided the receipt is genuine, timely and traceable under the receipt-verification framework. Any overdue repatriation without a SAFE-approved exception can trigger monitoring, administrative penalties or suspension of current-account FX services.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) separately governs the Hong Kong leg where no China-style exchange controls apply.
