Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; official online filing and registration portal is the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform.

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident investors must provide notarised and legalised identity and constitutional documents, after which the Foreign-Invested Partnership is formed with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). SAMR issues the Partnership Business License containing the statutory 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the definitive Tax Identification Number for the partnership. Immediately after issuance, the partnership must complete the mandatory tax information registration confirmation on the electronic tax bureau, linking the USCC to tax filing, VAT invoice issuance, withholding obligations, export refund eligibility and bank account details. No separate paper TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is the taxpayer identifier for all PRC tax reporting and customs and export transactions.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under Chinese FX law, the renminbi is convertible on the current account for trade and service payments, subject to a truthfulness documentation review performed by designated foreign exchange banks. However, the capital account remains heavily regulated and non-convertible without SAFE approval or a quota mechanism. For the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) receiving investment from Hong Kong, the injected foreign currency is treated as a capital account transaction and therefore must be brought onshore through a SAFE-registered foreign currency capital account, strictly aligned with the partnership agreement and the FDI negative list of the relevant Free Trade Zone or domestic area. Once the capital has been verified as payable by a Chinese certified public accountant, the FIP may convert into RMB via the counterpart settlement mechanism. Profit remittance hurdles include the need to produce audited financial statements, tax settlement certificates, proof that the distribution was made from after-tax retained earnings, and compliance with the annual reporting of foreign investment via the MOFCOM/SAFE system. Export proceeds from future consulting services, if any, must be repatriated to an onshore foreign exchange account within the statutory timeline; failure to observe surrender obligations or misdeclaration of purpose codes will trigger administrative fines and downgrade the bank's compliance risk rating. Additionally, any offshore loans obtained by the FIP must be registered under the macroprudential cap and are subject to strict debt-to-capital ratios.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Notice No. 3 of 2020, exporters, including service providers, must repatriate export foreign exchange proceeds into China within 180 days from the date of customs declaration for goods, or from the contractual service completion date. The historical mandatory conversion ratio for trade proceeds has been abolished; firms may retain all proceeds in foreign currency accounts at designated banks, but must effect foreign exchange settlement in accordance with actual transactional needs. However, any conversion of such proceeds must be supported by the relevant trade documents and recorded in the foreign exchange account information system. For capital account receipts, a 100% settlement is effectively required into RMB unless the enterprise obtains specific SAFE approval to retain the balance in foreign exchange. Service receivables from the FIP's market-entry consulting activities are treated as current account receipts and are subject to the same 180-day repatriation rule.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise or Revolut cannot act as primary clearing institutions for this Chinese FIP. They are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC and are legally barred from providing RMB-denominated domestic accounts or settling Chinese current/capital account transactions. Their underlying banking infrastructure may be used only for ancillary international services outside China, but any attempt to route the partnership's capital injection, salary, or trade settlement through these EMIs would breach the SAFE bank-dedicated account requirement. Moreover, Chinese anti-money laundering rules require the applicable entity to maintain a beneficial ownership ledger directly with a designated foreign exchange bank; an offshore EMI cannot perform the mandatory Chinese tax withholding, customer due diligence, or filing obligations. Therefore, fintech EMIs are commercially impractical and legally non-compliant for the onshore treasury of the FIP. They may be employed as a supplementary settlement instrument on the foreign side, but only for non-Chinese related expenses and never for statutory capital flows.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Root cause: Capital injection from a Hong Kong shell company with opaque beneficial ownership

Defense evidence: Provide a full shareholding diagram with identity passports of all natural persons, Notarial Certificate of Incorporation and Certificate of Incumbency, bank references from the investor's home bank, a source-of-funds sworn affidavit with wealth accumulation evidence, and a legal opinion from a Hong Kong counsel confirming no shell or asset-stripping structure.
Inconsistency between the partnership business scope (market-entry consulting) and the expected transaction pattern of high-value cross-border capital flows

Root cause: Inconsistency between the partnership business scope (market-entry consulting) and the expected transaction pattern of high-value cross-border capital flows

Defense evidence: Submit an annual business plan with detailed revenue streams, a certified copy of the partnership agreement, past enterprise consulting contracts or letters of intent from target clients in Hong Kong or overseas, and a projection of receivable and payable flows with settlement terms. Root cause: Potential round-tripping arrangement due to prior off-balance-sheet borrowing or cross-border related-party lending

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% headline Hong Kong corporate profits tax rate; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is, unless an election otherwise applies, taxed as an unincorporated business at the two-tier rate of 7.5% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits and 15% on the excess

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends or on partnership profit distributions, even when paid to non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The BIR52 must be filed within one month from the date of issue of the return; IRD publishes an extension table for tax representatives, commonly extending filing to 15 August for accounting periods ending 31 December and to 15 November for accounting periods ending 31 March

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 - Business Registration and Partner Particulars Legal name of FIP, Business Registration (BR) number, principal place of business, industry code (ISIC 7020 / NAICS 541611), date of commencement, and full identification of every general and limited partner with their tax residency and partner tax ID numbers The FIP must be registered under the Business Registration Ordinance; for non-resident partners, disclose full foreign tax identification numbers, residence addresses and Hong Kong tax file numbers. If the FIP is a transparent partnership, the IRD will assess the partners individually, so the return must allocate the correct share to each non-resident partner to avoid estimated assessments and penalty exposure. Late filing of the BIR52 exposes the FIP to a fine of up to HK$10,000 under IRO s.80(2), plus an additional tax charge of up to three times the tax undercharged if IRD issues estimated assessments. Knowingly making a false return or declaration is a criminal offence under IRO s.82A, punishable on conviction by a maximum fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to three years, together with an order to pay three times the tax undercharged. IRD may also apply departmental penalties, asset-preservation procedures and garnishment of third-party debts.

A non-resident partner cannot use foreign basis periods to defer Hong Kong assessment; Hong Kong source rules apply independently. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: There is no single statutory monetary threshold in the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Note (DIPN) 59 establishes safe-harbour documentation expectations: contemporaneous Local File documentation is expected where a controlled transaction category exceeds HK$20,000,000 for goods/services or HK$8,000,000 for financing and intangible transactions; a Master File is generally expected for multinational groups above the BEPS global consolidated revenue threshold and with material intercompany flows. Even below these safe harbours, the IRD may still require a reasonable basis for intercompany pricing.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have zero-tax Economic Substance Regulations comparable to Caribbean offshore centres, but economic substance is decisive in two ways: first, an offshore-source profits claim can be defeated if the core income-generating activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong; second, treaty relief under the Mainland/Hong Kong DTA will be denied to an FIP lacking real Hong Kong substance, offices, staff, decision-making, risk and bank accounts. The PRC tax authorities may recharacterise the FIP as a conduit and apply the General Anti-Abuse Rule under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Art.47 if the substance is artificial.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112) s.82A: on conviction, maximum fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to three years, plus an order for additional tax of up to three times the undercharged tax; failure to file under s.80(2) attracts a separate fine of HK$10,000 and additional tax. Fraudulent evasion cancels the ordinary six-year tax assessment limitation period.

Director personal liability: Where the FIP has a corporate general partner, its directors and managers who consent to or knowingly facilitate the false return are personally liable; the IRD can pierce the partnership's veil, seek asset-restraining orders, have the directors/proprietors blacklisted for travel exit restrictions, commence bankruptcy/insolvency proceedings, and obtain criminal convictions leading to disqualification as directors for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance Part 8A (ss.50AAM-50AAMJ) empowers the IRD to adjust transfer prices to the arm's length value, recompute assessable profits, deny deductions, recharacterise finance and intangibles, and justify penalties; artificial avoidance transactions may also be countered under IRO s.61A and s.61B. If negligence or fraud is established, the additional tax on undercharge can be three times the tax deficiency, with a HK$50,000 fine and imprisonment under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are exposed to personal and criminal liability if they approve false transfer pricing returns or cause the partnership to omit required documentation; courts may impose personal civil liability for the tax deficiency as a fraudulent preference in insolvency, issue worldwide Mareva asset freezes, order disqualification, and permit extradition/mutual legal assistance with PRC authorities under the China/HK arrangement.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC tax authorities will deny the beneficial owner claim, re-characterise the FIP as a contract-conduit and impose the full 10% PRC dividend withholding tax (or statutory 20% where appropriate), with unpaid tax interest and a late-payment penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Art.63; SAT Bulletin 2018 No.9 and the principal purpose test will collapse the DTA claim; Hong Kong IRD may similarly attack the entire structure under IRO s.61A and assess all deemed income as Hong Kong-sourced.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced for the persons who designed or operated the sham: directors and precedent partners may be held jointly and severally liable for the additional PRC tax, statutory surcharges and penalties; PRC courts may freeze personal bank accounts, impose exit bans on foreign principals, issue civil restitution orders for diverted dividends, and initiate criminal charges for fraudulent tax evasion where the conduit arrangement was knowingly fictitious.
