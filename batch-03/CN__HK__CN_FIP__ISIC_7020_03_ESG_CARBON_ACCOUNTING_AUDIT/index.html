Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole registered office for a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in ESG carbon auditing and offset brokerage. PRC partnership law and SAMR registration rules require an actual business premises in China with a lease contract, physical signage, and capacity to receive legal and tax documents. Local tax authorities and banks treat advisory and professional-service entities as high-risk for registration arbitrage; a virtual address will likely be rejected for tax invoice authorization and corporate bank account opening. A physical office is mandatory, and its details must match the partnership agreement and business license.

Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry of Hong Kong SAR, 14/F, High Block, Queensway Government Offices, 66 Queensway, Hong Kong, together with the Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, 4/F, Revenue Tower, 5 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong.

Governing statute

Not a company vehicle. The Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) will be constituted under Hong Kong partnership law, principally the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38 of the Laws of Hong Kong) and, if limited liability is sought for passive partners, the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37), together with the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), section 5(1), and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), Part 2. The name 'Foreign-Invested Partnership' describes non-Hong Kong partner participation; Hong Kong has no separate foreign-invested partnership incorporation statute, so formation is by partnership deed followed by business registration and, where applicable, registration of a limited partnership.

Formation / constitutional filings

Business Registration Application Form IRBR 1 and, for limited liability, the Limited Partnership Statement prescribed under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 37), together with the executed Partnership Deed and the Pro-Forma First Board Resolution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free capital account with no exchange controls. The HKD is fully convertible and maintained within a 7.75 to 7.85 band against USD under the Linked Exchange Rate system; current-account and capital-account transactions are not subject to general convertibility restrictions or surrender requirements. Hong Kong law does not impose mandatory conversion of export proceeds, nor any remittance blockage for partnership profits, interest, or capital redemption. The principal treasury friction within the composite CN-HK structure is entirely outbound from the Mainland: an onshore investor subscribing to an HK foreign-invested partnership must obtain SAFE ODI registration or Circular 37 filing before wiring funds to the HK FIP bank account, and the payment must carry a valid investment purpose code. Once funds sit offshore in HK, the FIP can open multi-currency accounts and settle freely. Profit repatriation from HK to Mainland partners is not restricted by HKMA; dividends, distributions, and liquidation proceeds may be remitted using standard banking channels. However, a mainland recipient will trigger PRC tax withholding and SAFE bank verification, so the FIP must maintain complete audited accounts, distribution resolutions, and source-of-funds dossiers. In practice, the HK FIP should be treated as an unrestricted offshore treasury hub; the only hurdles are documentation, AML/KYC, and the counterparty clearing rails touching Mainland China.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong: there is no statutory timeline, surrender percentage, or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. HKMA requires only proper record-keeping and suspicious transaction reporting under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance. For export proceeds collected on behalf of a Mainland China exporter, SAFE export verification and electronic receipt matching still apply onshore, but the HK FIP's own export-related HKD/USD/CNH receipts are fully free from any forced conversion or retention limit.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and, for Mainland China capital-account cross-border flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for core treasury functions. Offshore non-bank electronic money institutions are not authorized institutions under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and cannot provide licensed CHATS clearing, SWIFT correspondent hedge capacity, trade finance instruments, or SAFE-compliant inbound wires. HKMA-licensed virtual banks may onboard the FIP for simple transactional accounts, but their single-account limits, lack of LC/guarantee products, and restrictive counterparty lists make them unsuitable as the primary banking rail for cross-border corporate treasury and carbon offset brokerage. A licensed tier-1 commercial bank with an established mainlane corporate client channel and physical compliance team remains mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from Mainland China must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the onshore investor's SAFE-designated bank to the HK FIP licence bank, with the remittance narrative explicitly identifying the SAFE ODI approval or Circular 37 filing number and using the approved outward investment foreign exchange purpose code. The HK receiving bank will credit only the capital escrow or designated investment account; funds must be segregated from trading receipts until a CPA capital verification certificate and updated partnership register are issued. For non-mainland inbound capital, no prior foreign exchange approval is required in HK; the FIP should maintain a board/partnership resolution, bank reference, and source-of-funds memorandum to smooth the receiving bank's compliance review.Use SWIFT MT103 wires for high-value discretionary disbursements; commercial letters of credit for carbon offset procurement and brokerage where performance risk exists; and documentary collections for low-risk open-account trade settlements. Each outgoing payment must be matched to an invoice, contract, carbon registry serial number, and, when the beneficiary is in Mainland China, a SAFE-supported purpose code and customs/verification record. Payments in HKD and CNH should settle via the HKMA CHATS rails, while USD wires clear through the correspondent banking network.USD/HKD forward contracts up to 12 months for balance sheet hedging under HKMA-licensed bank ISDA/CSA documentationUSD/CNH deliverable forwards on the offshore CNH market for hedging mainland-related receivables and payablesUSD/CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) when onshore CNY hedging access is unavailable due to structural restrictionsHKD/USD/CNH cross-currency basis swaps for long-term liability or capital redemption exposurePlain-vanilla call/put option structures on trade-linked carbon credit price indices, only if approved by the treasury and risk committee

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Hong Kong limited partnership (including a Foreign-Invested Partnership) is not subject to a statutory audit by virtue of the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37) or the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112). However, an independent certified audit becomes compulsory in any of the following situations: (i) the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap 637) and the fund agreement or the provisions of that Ordinance require an audit; (ii) the partnership or any of its partners is a licensed corporation under the Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap 571) and holds a regulated activity licence; (iii) the partnership operates as a licensed money service operator under the Money Service Operators Ordinance (Cap 615); (iv) the partnership is a licensed insurance broker or agent under the Insurance Ordinance (Cap 41); or (v) the partnership is required by an external financing, investor or joint-venture agreement to produce audited financial statements. Absent such triggers, the IRD accepts unaudited accounts attached to the BIR52; if gross income for the basis period does not exceed HK$500,000 (US$64,103), the partnership may submit a simplified statement of accounts instead. The IRD retains the right under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to direct that the accounts be examined and certified by a certified public accountant where the accuracy of the accounts is in doubt.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (Hong Kong profits tax standard rate; two-tiered rates apply: 8.25% on first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits and 16.5% on the excess for unincorporated partnerships; corporations effectively pay 8.25% on first HKD 2,000,000 and 16.5% above, subject to eligibility rules). For a Foreign-Invested Partnership, profits are assessable in the hands of each partner individually at the partner's applicable rate, not the partnership entity rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%: Hong Kong does not impose dividend withholding tax on distributions made by a Hong Kong entity to its shareholders or partners. A Foreign-Invested Partnership is generally not subject to withholding tax on profit distributions to non-resident partners.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally within 1 month from the issue date of the Profits Tax Return; if accounts are prepared to a date other than 31 December, the deadline may be extended to the specified due date stated in the notice, often up to 3-4 months after the return issue date. For a partnership, the return is issued in the partnership name and must be filed together with a partnership return of income and each partner's share details; the practical due date is the date specified on the return form.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong Transfer Pricing Rules under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2018: Contemporaneous documentation is required for transactions that are not exempt from the arm's length principle. A Local File containing full transactional documentation must be prepared if the amount of all controlled transactions exceeds HKD 30 million per year. A Master File must be prepared if the multinational enterprise group's consolidated group revenue exceeds HKD 750 million EUR-equivalent (approximately HKD 6.8 billion) and the group has covered transactions. A Country-by-Country Report filing threshold applies when the group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 6.8 billion. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership in the carbon advisory sector, all intercompany service, management, licensing, and financing transactions must be supported by a local file if the aggregate exceeds the HKD 30 million threshold.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong imposes no formal Economic Substance Regulations analogous to the UAE ESR, but the IRD applies the 'economic substance' concept through: (1) the source of profits doctrine, which requires core income-generating activities to be performed in Hong Kong; (2) the anti-avoidance provisions under sections 61A and 61B IRO, which allow the IRD to disregard transactions entered into for tax avoidance; (3) the separate entity principle under Hong Kong Transfer Pricing Rules under Part 8F of the IRO; and (4) the business/trademark/patent beneficial ownership tests for claiming treaty benefits. A Foreign-Invested Partnership must demonstrate actual decision-making, staff presence, contract negotiation, and operational execution in Hong Kong to claim Hong Kong tax residence and to support the source of profit position. The IRD may challenge an offshore claim if the directors or partners are located outside Hong Kong and all commercial functions are performed in mainland China, causing the profits to be sourced outside Hong Kong and potentially assessed by mainland China under its tax rules, leading to double taxation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Defraud of Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under section 82(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), willful tax evasion with intent defraud the Crown is liable upon conviction on indictment to a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years. Under section 82A, incorrect returns carry a penalty of up to three times the tax undercharged, plus a fine of HKD 10,000. The IRD may also issue a return assessment under section 59 and impose additional tax under section 82A(2) of up to 100% of the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors and partners may be jointly and severally liable if they knowingly signed and filed false returns. The corporate veil can be pierced under common law where the entity is used as a sham or mere façade. A partner who actively participates in tax evasion may face criminal prosecution as a principal offender, confiscation orders under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, travel restrictions including departure prohibitions under section 82A(10), freezing of personal bank accounts, and extradition exposure in cases of cross-border fraud. Civil restitution to the Revenue can attach to personal assets.
Shell Company / Absence of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may deny the tax residence certificate and treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test of the China-Hong Kong Arrangement. Under sections 61A and 61B IRO, the IRD may disregard any transaction whose sole or main purpose is to obtain a tax benefit, and recompute the assessable profits at the arm's length level. The Commissioner can impose additional assessments up to six years after the end of the year of assessment, or unlimited in cases of fraud. For a shell partnership, the IRD may also apply the general anti-avoidance rule to add back expenses and disallow the offshore claim. There is no statutory ESR fine in Hong Kong, but the de facto penalty is full cancellation of the offshore claim, re-characterization of profits, interest on underpaid tax, and a possible penalty of up to 100%.

Director personal liability: The directors or general partners who control the shell entity may be personally assessed to profits tax under section 20A(2) IRO if the non-resident partner is not chargeable and cannot be recovered. Directors who create sham documents may be prosecuted for conspiracy to defraud the Revenue, carrying imprisonment up to 14 years under the Theft Ordinance, and may be subjected to director disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance. A travel ban may be imposed by the Commissioner under section 82A(10) pending the outstanding tax assessment. Personal guarantees on operating leases and bank facilities may be enforced to recover unpaid taxes.
AML / Anti-Money Laundering Compliance Breach in Carbon Credit Brokerage

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) requires any entity engaged in financial business or DNFBP activities to implement customer due diligence and record keeping. Hong Kong does not currently license unregulated carbon advisory firms under AMLO unless they engage in virtual asset trading or payment services; however, if the Foreign-Invested Partnership handles third-party funds through trust accounts, it may be classified as a money service operator under the Customs and Excise Act, requiring a license. A breach of AMLO is a criminal offense with on conviction a fine of HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years for failure to maintain proper records. In the context of carbon credits, money laundering charges may also be brought under the Drug Trafficking and Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, providing for prison terms of up to 14 years and forfeiture of the full proceeds of the offense.

Director personal liability: Directors, partners, or compliance officers who knowingly allow the partnership to receive tainted funds are personally liable for money laundering offenses. The corporate veil may be pierced under the doctrine of'alter ego' where the partnership is inextricably linked to personal bank accounts. Individual directors may face seizure of personal bank accounts, property restraint orders, revocation of business registration, travel bans, and extradition requests from mainland China. In serious cases, a Hong Kong court may impose imprisonment of up to 14 years and order restitution to the victims or to the government. Additionally, under the Companies Ordinance, a director who is convicted of an offense involving dishonesty may be disqualified for up to 15 years.
