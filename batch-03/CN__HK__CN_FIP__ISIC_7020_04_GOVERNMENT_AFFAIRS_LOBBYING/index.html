Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portal: IRD eTAX (through GovHK).

        
E-government filing portal
Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no separate VAT registration or VAT ID. A foreign-invested partnership (FIP) carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The application must be lodged with the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business, using a Hong Kong business address and, where no Hong Kong-resident partner exists, a notified tax representative. The supporting file must evidence the partnership deed, the overseas registration or incorporation certificate, the partners' particulars, and the principal place of business; if the partnership is a limited partnership, prior registration under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or the Limited Partnership Funds Ordinance (Cap. 637) is required. Upon approval, the IRD issues a BRC containing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory tax identifier for Profits Tax, employer returns and bank onboarding. If the FIP does not carry on business in Hong Kong, no tax registration is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Pass_Through_Eligible

        
Available election forms
None equivalent to a check-the-box election. Hong Kong partnerships are fiscally transparent by default; the IRD assesses partnership profits in the partnership name but attributes chargeability to partners. Individual partners may elect Personal Assessment under section 41 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and limited capital-allowance elections may be made on partnership succession; there is no hybrid mismatch or entity-classification election.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
No general VAT, GST or sales and use tax; Hong Kong imposes only selected excise duties on liquor, tobacco and hydrocarbon oil under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and stamp duty on specified instruments.

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable for VAT/GST. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), registration is compulsory when a business is carried on in Hong Kong; a small-business fee/levy waiver is available where monthly gross turnover does not exceed HK$150,000, but this does not remove the registration obligation.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Not applicable — Hong Kong has no VAT/GST and therefore no export zero-rating or export VAT refund claim. For Profits Tax, the territorial-source principle applies: profits from services, including cross-border trade policy and public-affairs advisory services, are chargeable only if they arise in or are derived from Hong Kong. A taxpayer seeking to treat profits as offshore must prove the place of negotiation, performance and conclusion, and that the profit-making activities occurred outside Hong Kong.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The operative headquarters entity is a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registered in mainland China; therefore the RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account, while the current account has been substantially liberalised. Inbound foreign currency contributions from Hong Kong partners are treated as foreign-direct-investment capital and must be registered with SAFE; the funds are held in a designated FDI capital account and converted to RMB only against verified operating expenses under the payment-verified-use principle. Current-account items such as retainer fee remittances and profit distributions may be settled freely through a licensed bank after submission of real transaction documentation, but profit remittances require an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificates, and partnership tax return filings. Outbound dividends for foreign partners are subject to a 10% withholding enterprise income tax (if no tax treaty reduction) and VAT-related levies. There is no blanket approval for converting RMB into capital-account foreign exchange for offshore investments, overseas loans, or securities unless a separate SAFE registration or quota is obtained. The HK corridor adds the distinction between onshore CNY and offshore CNH: funds in Hong Kong may move via the Hong Kong clearing system and be repatriated as FDI only when satisfying both SAFE and HKMA anti-money-laundering requirements.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under PRC trade-in-goods foreign exchange rules, export proceeds must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign currency current account or converted at a bank; the statutory repatriation window is 180 days from the date of export declaration. There is no mandatory percentage surrender ratio; SAFE uses a monitoring and verification system and enterprises may keep 100% of export receipts in foreign exchange subject to truthfulness checks. For services income (trade policy analysis and lobbying retainer fees), receipts must be collected under the service contract and reported in the BOP declaration, but no fixed surrender deadline applies other than the contractual payment maturity and no mandatory conversion is imposed.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), an administrative agency under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), for the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership entity, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the monetary supervisory authority for the Hong Kong SAR clearing corridor

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut Business are not legally permitted to maintain the onshore RMB capital deposit account or the FDI foreign-exchange capital account of a mainland China FIP, because only licensed commercial banks with SAFE registration can accept capital-account deposits and perform FX settlement. These EMIs may provide an offshore HK wallet for the Hong Kong parent or remittance initiation, but they cannot act as the FIP's primary clearing bank; attempting to route FDI through an EMI would be treated as a third-party payment not recognised for capital verification and may trigger violation of SAFE regulations. A licensed HK bank or the HK branch of a mainland state-owned bank is the acceptable cross-border channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound contribution from Hong Kong: use a single SWIFT MT103 from the registered partner's bank account (never from a third-party aggregator or EMI) to the FIP's SAFE-registered FDI capital deposit account at the mainland bank. The remittance advice must include the SAFE foreign-investment registration code and the purpose text 'FDI equity contribution - BOPS code 710010 - Foreign-Invested Partnership capital deposit'. After receipt, the bank will report through the SAFE capital-projects information system; an independent PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report before the FIP may settle the foreign currency into RMB. The converted RMB funds are to be released only against invoices, contracts, payroll, rent, taxes, and other operating expenditures; the bank may require a detailed drawdown schedule and downstream transaction receipts for each conversion. For cross-border RMB from HK, route via CIPS with the same registration reference; do not mix trade and capital remittances in one instruction.For overseas advisory retainers, consultant fees, and regulatory research payments, use SWIFT MT103 (sometimes MT202 for corresponding bank charges) with BOP service code 200026 ('management and public relations consultancy') or the applicable services classification. Attach the service agreement, tax invoice, and proof of service delivery; tax withholding receipts from the State Taxation Administration must accompany each outward remittance over the threshold. For physical trade supplies, commercial documentary letters of credit and documentary collections are permissible only for documentary trade flows and should be routed through the same Tier-1 bank under its trade-services system; however, for a services company, the primary clearing rails are CNAPS for domestic RMB, CIPS for cross-border RMB, and SWIFT for offshore foreign currencies.Onshore deliverable FX forwards between CNY and USD/HKD with a licensed SAFE-regulated bank, supported by a genuine current-account or capital-account exposure.FX swaps between CNY and foreign currency for temporary cash-flow matching of trade or capital receipts and payments, provided the underlying exposure is real and non-speculative.Cross-currency swaps only if the FIP has registered cross-border financing or debt; plain vanilla currency options may be permitted when a real underlying exposure is documented, while offshore NDFs and speculative derivative products are not permitted for the onshore entity.

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: No statutory audit is mandatory for a Hong Kong limited partnership as a non-corporate vehicle. The Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) and the Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38) impose no balance-sheet, turnover, revenue, or headcount threshold requiring the partnership itself to appoint independent certified public accountants. A mandatory audit is triggered only in narrower circumstances: (a) if the partnership is operated through, or establishes, a Hong Kong incorporated company as the general partner or in the same group, that corporate entity must be audited under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) regardless of size; (b) if the partnership is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund (Cap. 637) under a fund structure whose constitutive documents or regulator require audited accounts; or (c) if a government contract, lender, investor, or professional licence overrides the absence of a statutory audit duty. In those cases the auditor must be a practising Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant (CPA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation supporting the BIR51 is mandatory where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds HK$5,000,000 in the year; a master file may be required where group consolidated turnover exceeds HK$500,000,000 and controlled transactions exceed the local file threshold; the IRD may require contemporaneous documentation for any taxpayer regardless of size where there is high risk of profit shifting, especially shell-type lobbying or advisory pass-through structures.

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulations filing regime like Caribbean offshore territories, but the IRD applies the OECD/CIGA substance doctrine in transfer pricing examinations and treaty benefits. Core Income-Generating Activities for a government-affairs and lobbying advisory entity must occur in Hong Kong: substantive decision-making, licensed lobbying operational management, physical office, employed qualified researchers and lobbyists, contract execution, policy-report preparation and risk-holding through real capital and bank accounts. An entity that merely receives retainer wires in Hong Kong while advising on mainland China trade policy has no Hong Kong CIGA, and the IRD will recharacterise the arrangement under sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance or attribute profits to a permanent establishment of the non-resident principal. The Mainland-Hong Kong Arrangement also contains a principal purpose anti-abuse rule; shell companies without economic substance will be denied residency and treaty benefits.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / False Offshore Claim
        
Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), sections 80(1) and 82(1); a fine not exceeding HK$50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years on conviction, together with an additional sum of three times the amount of tax undercharged and interest; civil adjustments and substantial negligence penalties may apply in a four-level penalty regime.

        
Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil to hold signing directors personally and jointly liable for the understated tax, penalties and unpaid interest; directors may be named in bankruptcy/disqualification proceedings, subjected to a travel ban, placed on the IRD prosecution blacklist, restrained from leaving Hong Kong under asset-restraint orders, and receive custodial sentences of up to 3 years for fraudulent tax evasion.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Deterioration / Shell Profit Shifting
        
Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61 and 61A (recharacterisation of artificial transactions and transfer pricing adjustment); the Taxpayer is required to prepare contemporaneous documentation under DIPN 58; failure to keep records may result in a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment; the IRD may impose additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax plus interest, and may remove the benefit of the two-tiered rate and capital allowances.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who execute the inflated management-fee agreements and sign false tax returns cannot rely on corporate personality because transfer-pricing adjustments may be collected from the delinquent taxpayer only, but if assets are dissipated or accounts are falsely maintained, a director may be personally liable for restitution, receive a restraint/forfeiture order, be blacklisted from future government contracts and lobbyist licences, be subject to a travel ban, and face imprisonment of up to 3 years for knowingly providing false information to the IRD.

      

      
      

        Corruption, Bribery in Government Lobbying and AML Offences
        
Statutory sanction: Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201), section 9: an agent/officer who corruptly accepts or solicits an advantage in connection with his principal's affairs is liable on conviction to a fine of HK$500,000 and imprisonment for 7 years; organized and serious crimes confiscation, restraint and travel-ban orders may be pursued under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455).

        
Director personal liability: The corporate and partnership veil is pierced for intentional criminal participation in corruption: directors are personally liable for fine repayment, asset confiscation, civil restitution of the bribe amount and the FIP's funds dissipated, they are prohibited from leaving Hong Kong and may be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Companies Registry as unfit controllers, and are liable to imprisonment for up to 7 years; where mainland nexus exists, the PRC Criminal Law makes directors liable for bribery of PRC officials, ranking in criminal deterrence, extradition, asset seizure and personal terms of imprisonment potentially beyond 10 years for grave legislative corruption.
