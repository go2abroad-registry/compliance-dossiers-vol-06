A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the registered office of an FIP in Hong Kong because Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 and Business Registration Ordinance Cap. 310 require a physical Hong Kong street address for service of process and statutory records. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the national electronic tax bureau portal (e-Tax/New Electronic Tax Bureau); for the Hong Kong SAR side, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong and its eTAX portal remain the competent authority for any Hong Kong-resident counterparty.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident investor forming a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in China first obtains registration from the local Administration for Market Regulation, which issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that automatically becomes the FIP's tax identification number. The FIP must complete tax registration with the competent STA office through the e-Tax portal, using the official tax registration procedure for foreign-invested enterprises, within the statutory period after issuance of the business license. Where the foreign partner itself has no establishment in China and will derive Chinese-source income, it must obtain a non-resident enterprise TIN through the STA's non-resident taxpayer registration/withholding registration procedure and must appoint a withholding agent for China enterprise income tax on passive income and on business profits attributable to a Chinese permanent establishment.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A non-resident investor forming a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in China first obtains registration from the local Administration for Market Regulation, which issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that automatically becomes the FIP's tax identification number. The FIP must complete tax registration with the competent STA office through the e-Tax portal, using the official tax registration procedure for foreign-invested enterprises, within the statutory period after issuance of the business license. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under PRC law. The FIP is statutorily transparent for enterprise income tax; partners are taxed on their allocable share. For VAT, however, the FIP is a taxable person and may elect, where legally eligible, to register as a small-scale taxpayer (simplified levies) or as a general taxpayer (normal input-output VAT credit method), subject to the annual turnover threshold and STA rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; every entity that conducts taxable business in China must complete tax registration upon commencement and, below this threshold, qualifies for small-scale taxpayer status, with monthly taxable sales at or below CNY 100,000 potentially exempt from VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China qualify for 0% VAT and export tax rebate only if the export transaction is genuinely completed, the goods leave China through a regulated customs procedure, the exporter receives the export proceeds in foreign exchange, and the underlying paperwork matches the customs declarations. Qualifying cross-border services, including AEO supply-chain security audit and compliance services supplied entirely to a non-resident enterprise and consumed outside China, may qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption under the STA cross-border services framework (including the Caishui [2016] No. 36 pilot rules) if the service recipient is outside China, the service is wholly performed/used outside China, and complete transfer documentation is retained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration / export Bill of Entry) with customs declaration number and exporter/factory match
  • Bill of Lading (or airway bill/hand-carried goods stamp) proving the goods have left China
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and signed export contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or PRC bank foreign exchange receipt confirmation demonstrating export proceeds have been repatriated to China
  • VAT special invoice/purchase invoice for the exported goods (where rebate is claimed)
  • Export tax rebate declaration produced through the STA export tax rebate management system
  • For cross-border services, contract, service delivery documentation, counterparty tax-residency certificate, and proof of foreign-currency settlement

China mandates the use of the VAT invoice management system for taxable supplies. General VAT taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices using tax control equipment or the STA-approved digital electronic invoice platform, with real-time or periodic upload of invoice data to the STA; the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice pilot and the Golden Tax System Phase IV platform impose invoice numbering, tax declaration reconciliation, anti-counterfeiting QR/digital stamping, and API-based issuance/clearance requirements on qualifying taxpayers. The compliance audit services under ISIC 7020 are generally subject to VAT at 6% as modern services when supplied domestically, with e-invoicing required in the ordinary course.

The STA and Chinese Customs conduct coordinated audits of AEO-certified exporters; where zero-rating or rebates are found to be based on false declarations, fictitious exports, or unrepatriated proceeds, the tax authorities will claw back the amount with statutory daily interest surcharge and impose penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection (including fines of 50% up to five times the tax evaded) and may refer the case for criminal liability under the Criminal Law on fraudulent export tax refunds. Customs may also revoke AEO certification and impose enhanced control measures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains full currency convertibility and imposes no surrender or repatriation restrictions on the Hong Kong dollar. However, the substantive treasury exposure of this entity passes through Mainland China as the foreign-invested partnership homeland. Therefore, the binding external-account constraint is the RMB capital-account convertibility regime administered by SAFE. Current-account transactions, including consulting fees, management fees, and dividends derived from the AEO supply-chain audit business, are freely convertible if genuine documentation, invoices, tax certificates, and bank-verified underlying transactions are presented. Capital-account transactions such as inbound FDI capital injections, offshore equity acquisitions, and cross-border loans require SAFE registration, capital verification by a licensed CPA, and compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit remittance from the Mainland FIP to Hong Kong requires audited financial statements, Chinese corporate income tax settlement clearance, withholding tax proof, and a SAFE foreign-exchange settlement record. In practice, hard-currency liquidity should be held in Hong Kong, while RMB current-account payments can be settled onshore, but RMB capital movements remain heavily controlled and documented.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Mainland China export proceeds earned by the FIP, SAFE requires repatriation of proceeds through a domestic designated bank generally within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a verified export proceeds account or convert into RMB at a bank. Each receipt must be reconciled with the export verification system. Hong Kong imposes no export surrender rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR, with coordinated supervision over cross-border RMB flows by the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of Mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Wise are viable only on the Hong Kong treasury side, where Wise holds a Hong Kong Money Service Operator licence and can process low-value foreign-currency payments such as CNH, USD, and EUR disbursements. Wise cannot lawfully open an onshore RMB settlement account in the Mainland Chinese banking system, cannot execute SAFE-registered FDI capital injections, and cannot issue any China-legal RMB payment instrument. The legal boundary is strict: fintech EMIs can serve only as ancillary FX and disbursement rails for outbound Hong Kong liquidity, never as primary clearers for capital account transactions or SAFE-supervised trade settlements. All inbound and strategic outbound flows must migrate over licensed commercial bank rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong partner into the Mainland Chinese FIP must follow the SAFE FDI framework. The FIP must first complete foreign-exchange registration at a domestic designated bank. The Hong Kong funding account then transmits the equity capital via SWIFT MT103 with the reference field clearly stating FDI registered capital injection, together with a bank-specific purpose code for inbound direct investment. After receipt, the FIP opens a renminbi capital account or foreign-currency capital account, obtains a CPA capital verification report, and files it with SAFE through the receiving bank. Each subsequent disbursement or currency conversion must be accompanied by underlying real-use documents such as invoices, contracts, payroll records, and tax certificates. For capital flows destined only for Hong Kong, there are no foreign-exchange controls, but the source of funds and economic substance of the Hong Kong account must be disclosed during bank onboarding.Outbound trade disbursements should prefer bank-cleared SWIFT MT103 for non-documentary service fees and subcontractor settlements. For procurement of security systems or physical audit hardware, commercial letters of credit or documentary collections are appropriate where third-party verification of shipping documents is needed. All outbound payments from the Mainland FIP must be supported by tax invoices, service-completion certificates, customs declarations, shipping bills, or comparable compliance instruments, and must pass the bank's SAFE verification workflow. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent certified CPA audit is statutorily or legally mandatory in the following circumstances: (a) the partnership's balance-sheet total exceeds HK$10 million; (b) annual gross turnover exceeds HK$20 million; (c) average headcount exceeds 100; (d) the partnership is a public-interest entity, holding company, regulated financial institution, insurance business, or licensed customs AEO-certified operator subject to GACC conditions; (e) the IRD requires audited accounts to verify the BIR52 return; (f) the PRC local market regulator or MOFCOM requires an audit report for annual foreign-investment reporting; or (g) a partner, lender, or contract imposes audit as a governance condition. Where none of the thresholds is met, the partners may still commission a voluntary audit for credit, compliance, or operational assurance; submission of unaudited financial statements is acceptable only to entities that are not otherwise caught by the above thresholds.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

BIR52 must be filed within one month from the date of issue. For a 31 December fiscal year-end, the IRD typically issues the return in the following April; if filed electronically through eTAX by a recognized tax agent, the filing deadline is generally extended to three months from the issue date.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 50 million in aggregate controlled transactions per annum is the minimum administrative threshold requiring a contemporaneous Local File and Master File; intercompany financing or royalty flows above HKD 10 million per annum must be covered by a dedicated transfer-pricing benchmark. All AEO supply-chain audit fees charged to related parties must be supported by third-party comparables.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation code, but both the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the China-HK DTA apply substance-over-form. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of this FIP are client negotiation, physical supply-chain security surveys, workpaper preparation, AEO certification analysis, final audit opinions and post-certification monitoring. Those activities must be performed by locally present employees and partners in Hong Kong. If a Mainland shareholder directs audit staff and makes management decisions in Shenzhen or Shanghai, the FIP will be assessed in Hong Kong on the profits and simultaneously exposed to a PRC permanent establishment claim, resulting in double taxation and treaty anti-abuse penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Offshore-Source Declaration

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 82 and 82A: additional tax of up to three times the tax evaded, a fine of up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; the IRD may reopen all open assessment years and impose back-duty assessments with a 100% surcharge.

Director personal liability: The general partners are jointly and severally liable for unpaid profits tax, penalties and costs. Where the Mainland controlling partner uses the FIP as a mere conduit, the court can pierce the partnership veil and order civil asset restitution over the controller's Hong Kong and Mainland assets. Dishonest directors and controlling partners may be blacklisted, subjected to a travel ban, disqualified from acting as directors for up to 15 years, and imprisoned for up to 7 years on common law conspiracy to defraud charges.
Customs AEO Certification Fraud and Supply-Chain Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Customs and Excise Duty Ordinance (Cap. 109) section 18: forfeiture of goods plus monetary penalty up to HKD 1,000,000 and six times the value of the goods or duty evaded. AEO certification is immediately revoked, the entity is removed from the AEO trusted-trade programme, and the Customs authority may issue civil penalties for each false declaration made during the supply-chain audit.

Director personal liability: Directors, general partners and compliance officers who knowingly or recklessly issue false AEO security declarations are personally liable to Customs for compensation and civil penalties. Criminal exposure includes fines up to HKD 500,000 and imprisonment for up to 5 years. The individuals will be blacklisted from operating any AEO-authorized entity, face travel restrictions across Hong Kong and Mainland China, and are subject to disqualification proceedings under the Companies Ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation, Thin Capitalization and AML/Shell-Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRD transfer-pricing adjustments under Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 50AAJ and 50AAF; disallowance of non-arm's length interest, royalty, management fee and subcontract charges; additional tax assessments plus penalties. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), misuse of payment rails in the course of tax evasion is a criminal offence carrying a fine of up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years, followed by section 50AAK penalties for failure to keep transfer-pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: The court may recharacterize the shareholder loan as equity and pierce the partnership veil to recover the diverted profits from the controlling partners and their related parties. Directors face civil restitution and freezing orders over assets in Hong Kong and Mainland China under mutual legal assistance arrangements, company disqualification for up to 15 years, travel bans while liabilities remain unpaid, and criminal imprisonment for up to 7 years under Cap. 615 and fraudulent trading provisions if the FIP is used as a shell to conceal beneficial ownership and evade tax.
