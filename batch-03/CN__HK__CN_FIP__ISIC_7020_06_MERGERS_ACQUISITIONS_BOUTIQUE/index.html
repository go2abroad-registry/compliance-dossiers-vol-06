Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Registrar of Companies, 14/F, Queensway Government Offices, 66 Queensway, Hong Kong SAR, operating through the e-Registry online portal (www.e-Registry.com.hk); together with the Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, Revenue Tower, 5 Gloucester Road, Wan Chai, Hong Kong SAR.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), Laws of Hong Kong, sections 4(1), 7, 11(1), 12(1) and 37; Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 38), section 3; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), section 6(1); Securities and Futures Ordinance (Cap. 571), sections 114, 116, 117 and 180, applicable only to the regulated Type 6 advisory activity. The label Foreign-Invested Partnership is not a separate statutory vehicle in Hong Kong; it is implemented as a Hong Kong limited partnership whose partnership base includes non-resident foreign investors, which is expressly permitted under the open-access provisions of the Limited Partnerships Ordinance.

Formation / constitutional filings

Statement of Limited Partnership (Form LP 1) under section 7(1) of the Limited Partnerships Ordinance, executed by the general partner and each limited partner; accompanied by the executed Limited Partnership Agreement, the registered office consent, and Form IRBR 1 (Business Registration Application) for the mandatory Business Registration Certificate.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Hong Kong law. The Limited Partnerships Ordinance and Partnership Ordinance impose no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a foreign-invested partnership. The capital is solely contractual and fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement; each partner's capital subscription may be paid in cash, in-kind assets, or services, and must be received as cleared funds or validly transferred before the corresponding capital account is credited. Annual business registration fees apply under the Business Registration Ordinance, and commercial banks may require evidence of initial operating funds for non-resident partners under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority AML/CFT regime, but these are bank credit conditions and not statutory capital requirements.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal is the IRD eTAX platform (www.etax.gov.hk). For vehicles constituted as limited partnerships, the Companies Registry acts as the commercial registrar under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap 37) or the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap 637), with tax registration then completed with the IRD Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a partnership in Hong Kong: (1) Determine whether the partnership is carrying on business in Hong Kong; a non-resident that merely invests or receives passive income without a Hong Kong economic nexus does not require business registration. (2) Where the vehicle is a limited partnership, first file the prescribed statement with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnership Ordinance or the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance, as applicable. (3) Submit a business registration application to the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310) within one month of commencement of business, either online through eTAX or using the prescribed paper registration procedure. (4) Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the statutory 8-digit Business Registration Number, which serves as the partnership's Tax Identification Number for profits tax and employer returns. (5) Each partner retains their own IRD taxpayer identification reference for personal or corporate taxation. (6) No separate VAT or GST identification number is required because Hong Kong does not impose value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election, hybrid mismatch election, or similar elective tax classification mechanism. A partnership is transparent for profits tax purposes: the partnership itself is not a taxpayer, and each partner is separately assessed on their share of partnership profits that are derived from or arise in Hong Kong under the territorial source principle of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales Tax

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no general indirect tax, and therefore there is no economic turnover registration threshold for VAT, GST, or sales tax. Business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310) is nevertheless mandatory for any person or partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in VAT terms because Hong Kong does not levy VAT, GST, or sales tax; consequently, there is no export VAT zero-rating regime, cash refund mechanism, or digital-services deemed-supply rule. For profits tax purposes, fees derived from cross-border M&A advisory services are outside the charge to Hong Kong profits tax when the services are negotiated, contracted, performed, and delivered wholly outside Hong Kong, and the profit arises from activities outside Hong Kong. The territorial source principle under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112) governs this assessment.

  • Not applicable to VAT export zero-rating; no Hong Kong VAT zero-rating return or refund claim exists.
  • For profits tax offshore-source substantiation: signed engagement letter, M&A advisory contract, and master services agreement demonstrating negotiation, acceptance, and performance outside Hong Kong.
  • Detailed success-fee invoices and fee notes showing the billing principal as a non-Hong Kong counterparty.
  • Bilateral bank transfer confirmations, SWIFT records, and foreign exchange receipts evidencing fee settlement in a non-Hong Kong account.
  • If physical ancillary deliverables or goods are exported, the exporter is required to lodge an export declaration with the Customs and Excise Department under the Imports and Exports (Registration) Regulations (Cap 60E); however, this is not a VAT zero-rating document.
  • Working papers, meeting records, and partner/board resolutions establishing that no Hong Kong economic activity generated the revenue.

No real-time fiscal clearance or electronic invoice stamping mandate exists in Hong Kong. The IRD does not operate a pre-clearance or anti-fraud digital stamping system for commercial invoices. Businesses are subject to the general record-keeping obligation under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112) to retain adequate accounting records, including invoices and receipts, for a period of at least seven years.

Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST export zero-rating, there is no VAT clawback mechanism. If a taxpayer successfully relies on the offshore-source profit treatment and the IRD subsequently determines that the services were in fact rendered or economic activity was performed in Hong Kong, the IRD may reopen assessments and assess the full amount as Hong Kong-sourced profits. In the absence of full and true disclosure, penalties of up to three times the amount of tax undercharged may be imposed under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112), together with fines and, in cases of fraudulent or willful evasion, criminal prosecution. For customs declaration failures under the Imports and Exports (Registration) Regulations (Cap 60E), separate fines and penal provisions apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency under the linked exchange rate system that maintains the HKD at 7.75 to 7.85 per USD. There is no legal distinction between current account and capital account convertibility for Hong Kong entities, meaning the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) may freely convert HKD, USD, CNH and other major currencies, remit distributions, and settle cross-border success fees without prior exchange control approval from the HKMA. The same freedom does not extend to mainland China flows. If the FIP is a mainland-incorporated foreign-invested partnership or routes funds into mainland China, SAFE requires full documentation for current account payments such as M&A advisory services, while capital account transactions including capital injections must be registrated with SAFE and deposited into a designated domestic foreign exchange account. Profit or partnership capital repatriation from mainland China is permitted after tax settlement and bank verification, but inherently subject to SAFE post-verification and anti-round-tripping scrutiny. No surrender obligations exist in Hong Kong, but mainland-sourced export or service proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic account and can be retained in foreign currency or converted to RMB at the market rate. Overall convertibility is unrestricted in Hong Kong, with medium regulatory friction only at the China border.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under Hong Kong law there is no statutory timeline, mandatory conversion ratio, or surrender requirement for export proceeds. Foreign currency receipts are freely negotiable and may be credited directly to any Hong Kong dollar or multi-currency operating account. If the FIP derives export service revenue from mainland China, SAFE current account administration requires repatriation of such proceeds to a domestic bank account within a commercial settlement cycle consistent with trade authenticity verification; the legacy 180-day export verification window remains the practical banking benchmark. There is no fixed surrender percentage: the FIP may retain all proceeds in foreign currency, convert at market rates, or use them to offset documented trade expenses. Any repatriation to mainland China must be matched by invoices, contracts, customs declarations or service transaction evidence acceptable to the settlement bank.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for mainland China foreign exchange control

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary treasury operations, Low for regulated bank functions. Airwallex, Currenxie and Neat are HKMA-licensed stored value facilities and may open multi-currency business accounts for Hong Kong entities, which makes them acceptable for low-value payroll, SaaS payments, and small success fee settlements. They are not licensed banks under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and cannot issue documentary credits, provide URC522 collections, maintain SAFE-registered capital accounts, or serve as custodian for inbound investment capital. A mainland FIP additionally cannot rely on a foreign fintech EMI for export proceeds repatriation or SAFE capital registration because mainland regulatory counterparty rules require an onshore or licensed cross-border bank. Therefore EMIs are viable only as secondary liquidity rails, never for capital escrow, trade finance, or FX hedging requiring bank counterparty status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital into the Hong Kong FIP should be settled by SWIFT MT103 from the investing entity's bank directly to the FIP's capital escrow account. The remittance must reference the partner contribution and use an appropriate bank purpose code, typically PURP/GIF or a bank-specific equity contribution code. No SAFE approval is needed while funds remain outside mainland China. If the capital is destined for a mainland Foreign-Invested Partnership, the investor must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system, open a foreign exchange capital account at a domestic designated bank, deposit the remittance, and obtain a capital verification report from a PRC CPA before settlement into RMB or disbursement for operating purposes. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate; the FIP itself is fiscally transparent and net profits are allocated to partners for tax at each partner level)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding tax on dividends and deemed distributions of PRC-source profits to non-resident enterprises. For a transparent FIP, a non-resident partner's allocable PRC-source profit is generally subject to withholding at 10%, unless a China-HK DTA benefit reduces the rate to 5% for a qualifying direct investor holding 25% or more of a PRC resident company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory in RMB where related-party transaction values exceed: RMB 40 million for related-party service fees (including management, brokerage, and advisory fees), RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers or loans/financial transactions, and RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers. A Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue in the prior year exceeded RMB 5.5 billion. The annual related-party transaction reporting schedule must be filed with the A100000 return regardless of local file threshold if any related-party transaction occurred.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not formally call its rules Economic Substance Regulations, but CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 implement a substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance rule. An HK partner claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate real business presence, staff, decision-making, and CIGA in Hong Kong. China will deny the Hong Kong DTA claim if the arrangement has no reasonable commercial purpose or if the HK recipient is merely a shell used for treaty shopping. The HK economic substance test for carried interest and offshore profits also requires the entity to maintain sufficient CIGA in Hong Kong; absent this, the income will be recharacterized as China-source and subject to CIT and WHT.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax, plus late-fee interest and compulsory recovery; Article 62 imposes a separate filing penalty. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes liability for tax evasion where the evaded amount is significant, with fines and imprisonment; the Supreme People's Court threshold for criminal filing is generally RMB 100,000 evaded with more than 10% of taxable income.

Director personal liability: The managing partner, general partner, or de facto director may be held personally liable for corporate/partnership tax fraud where the entity is used as a facade; courts may pierce the limited liability veil under Company Law Article 20, freeze personal assets, impose restitution orders, impose outbound travel bans and blacklisting through the tax non-faithful list, and sentence responsible officers to imprisonment for up to three years for ordinary evasion and three to seven years for particularly large amounts.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Articles 40 to 48 authorize the SAT to adjust taxable income using arm's length methods, impose interest at benchmark rates plus a 5-percentage-point penalty interest, and disregard transactions lacking commercial substance under SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6. If the HK entity is deemed wholly artificial, the China-HK DTA benefit is denied and the payer is liable for the 10% withholding WHT on dividends/interest/royalty plus underpayment penalties of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly designed or implemented the artificial structure can be declared jointly and severally liable for the tax adjustment and penalties; they may face civil restitution for diverted profits, blacklisting and travel bans preventing them from leaving China, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion if the adjusted tax exceeds the criminal threshold, with imprisonment terms of up to seven years in aggravated cases.
Withholding Tax Evasion and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 69 penalizes the withholding agent with a fine of 50% to 3 times the unwithheld amount and requires full payment of the tax; Article 64 also applies to the non-resident recipient. The Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes administrative fines on the institution of up to 10 million RMB and on responsible persons up to 500,000 RMB for serious failures, plus Criminal Law Article 191 money-laundering liability with imprisonment of five to ten years and criminal fines.

Director personal liability: When the partnership is used as a vehicle for laundering the proceeds of tax evasion, the corporate veil will be pierced; directors and general partners are subject to personal asset tracing, civil damages, court-ordered confiscation of property, travel bans, and blacklisting under tax and anti-money-laundering enforcement systems, plus personal criminal liability with imprisonment for money laundering of up to ten years and disqualification from managing business entities.
