A Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership must maintain a physical registered office at a permitted mainland address within the administrative territory of the local SAMR registration. Hong Kong virtual office addresses cannot satisfy PRC formation, tax, or banking substance tests; commercial mailboxes are rejected by the tax authority and major Chinese banks for invoice issuance, pass-through tax filings, and corporate account opening. Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), acting through the local Market Supervision and Administration Bureau (Foreign-Investment Registration Window) in the city where the registered office of the foreign-invested partnership is located.

Governing statute

Not a company: the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is governed by the Partnership Enterprise Law of the People's Republic of China (2006 revision), Articles 2, 5, 8, 11, 12, 14, 19, 26-41 and 62; the Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (effective 1 January 2020), Articles 2, 4, 15 and 31; the Administrative Measures for Foreign Enterprises or Individuals to Establish Partnership Enterprises in China (State Council Order No. 567, effective 1 March 2010, as amended), Articles 2-11; and the Regulations on the Administration of Market Entity Registration (SAMR Order No. 59), Chapter II. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) of Hong Kong SAR may govern the corporate status of a Hong Kong body corporate partner but does not govern the FIP itself.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foreign-Invested Partnership Establishment Registration Application Form submitted through the SAMR online enterprise registration portal.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 31 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and the Foreign-Invested Partnership rules impose no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for a foreign-invested partnership. The partners are free to agree the capital contributions, timing and form (cash, intellectual property, services or other property) in the partnership agreement; contributed capital is reflected in the partnership asset account and registered with the Market Supervision Administration.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - no covered civil defense fire permit is required because the enterprise has no industrial or warehousing premises; however, the office lease must comply with the Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China and local building fire codes, and any employer with staff must complete general workplace safety registration and fire safety evacuation certification.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; Business Registration Office (BRO) under the IRD; online electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (Electronic Services for Tax) at www.gov.hk/etax

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity, including a Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership, carrying on business in Hong Kong, the statutory tax identifier is the Business Registration Number (BRN) obtained under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The foreign entity must file Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) with the Business Registration Office within one month after the date of commencement of business; if the operation is structured as a branch of a foreign entity, Form IRBR2 applies. The application must be accompanied by: (i) a certified copy of the partnership agreement or equivalent constitutive document; (ii) an original certificate of registration or incorporation excerpt from the foreign registry, with a certified English translation if necessary; (iii) identification documents for every partner and the responsible person, such as passport or national identity card for individuals, or certificate of incorporation for corporate partners; (iv) proof of a local Hong Kong business address, or a confirmation from a local authorised representative if no physical office is maintained; and (v) the prescribed registration fee and levy set out in the Business Registration Ordinance and the Business Registration Regulations (Cap. 310A). The application may be submitted electronically through GovHK eTAX using a recognised Digital Certificate or by paper at the Business Registration Office. Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing the 8-digit BRN. The BRN is the single tax identification number for Hong Kong profits tax, employer returns, business registration renewal, and stamp duty; no separate VAT or GST identification exists. The foreign entity must notify the BRO in writing of any changes in business particulars within one month and display the Business Registration Certificate at the place of business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no hybrid mismatch election, and no entity classification election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A partnership is statutorily transparent: profits are assessed under the name of the partnership but the charge is on the partners in their respective shares. Individual partners may separately elect personal assessment under Part VII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but this election does not change the partnership's pass-through classification and is not an entity-level tax election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not applicable - Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or sales and use tax. The indirect tax system is limited to stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117) and duties on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no turnover threshold for VAT or GST because no such tax exists. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), the trigger is the carrying on of a business in Hong Kong, not the attainment of a turnover threshold; the Business Registration Regulations (Cap. 310A) prescribe fee and levy exemption thresholds for very small businesses, but those exemptions do not remove the obligation to register.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT or GST, so there is no zero-rating mechanism for exports of goods or services and no indirect-tax refund claim to substantiate. For profits tax, the territorial source principle determines whether income is taxable; income arising in or derived from Hong Kong is taxable, while offshore profits are generally outside the charge to profits tax, as interpreted in IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes No. 21. A virtual concert or Metaverse service sold to overseas customers will be assessed on its source, utilising contracts, place of performance, server and event location logs, payment receipts, and operational documentation, but this is a profits-tax offshore-income analysis rather than an export VAT zero-rating claim. The 180-day value for foreign-exchange repatriation in this dossier is a global pipeline default; no statutory FX repatriation deadline is imposed by Hong Kong tax law.

  • Not applicable for pure virtual services: no physical goods cross the boundary, so no Hong Kong customs export declaration is required for the virtual concert or digital ticket tokenisation transaction.
  • If the entity also exports physical merchandise, the following Hong Kong customs and trade documents apply under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E): electronic Export Declaration (Form 2A) lodged via the Road Cargo System (RCSC) or Air Cargo System (ACSC), commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, and certificate of origin if preferential tariff treatment is claimed.
  • For profits-tax offshore-income substantiation of cross-border digital services, retain the underlying service agreement, evidence of the place of performance and delivery, server and event location logs, platform ticket sales records, transaction ledgers, and bank or payment-gateway statements.

No mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping applies in Hong Kong for indirect tax purposes. The IRD's eTAX electronic portal supports voluntary electronic filing and payment of taxes and business registration applications; commercial invoices and receipts are ordinary private documents and do not require IRD pre-clearance, fiscal certification, or digital stamping. For virtual asset transactions, any applicable Securities and Futures Commission licensing and record-keeping obligations are separate and do not create an indirect-tax invoicing mandate.

No VAT or GST zero-rating can be clawed back because no such consumption tax exists in Hong Kong. In the profits tax context, the IRD may deny offshore treatment if the taxpayer fails to substantiate the foreign source of income; penalties may be imposed under sections 80 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) for incorrect returns without reasonable excuse, and the tax undercharged may be recovered with an additional penalty of up to three times the tax evaded. For business registration, failure to apply for registration is an offence and may result in a fine and imprisonment under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with partial, real-demand convertibility. Current-account items, including royalty income, virtual-performance service fees, and digital-ticket proceeds, may generally convert into CNY after the designated bank verifies the underlying contract, invoice, and tax declaration. Capital-account flows, such as inbound capital contributions to the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) from Hong Kong investors and profit remittances, remain subject to SAFE registration, foreign debt quotas, and a strict use-of-funds list. The Hong Kong SAR itself imposes no foreign-exchange restrictions and permits unrestricted settlement, but any balance-sheet link to the mainland FIP must pass through a licensed mainland bank. Profit distribution from a mainland FIP to a Hong Kong partner is treated as capital-account repatriation; it requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, SAFE FDI profit-remittance registration, and evidence that all statutory withholding tax has been settled. Funds received in USDC must be flattened into a fiat currency in an offshore licensed account and wire-transferred onshore through SWIFT-compliant channels before any CNY conversion, because mainland banks will not accept stablecoin settlement directly.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio for export or cross-border service proceeds under current SAFE rules. The statutory obligation is repatriation: all trade-related foreign-currency receipts of the mainland FIP must be brought back onshore through a designated FX current account within the earlier of the contract deadline or the 180-day export-receipt monitoring period. Within that account, the FIP may freely retain the foreign currency or convert it into CNY at market rates at the bank's discretion. For the virtual-concert business, Stripe and USDC collections must be collected and converted to USD/HKD in a licensed offshore settlement account, then remitted onshore with the relevant service-trade purpose code so that the bank can complete SAFE ownership verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the mainland FX regulator, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the counterpart monetary authority for the Hong Kong treasury/intermediary layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs, including Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Currenxie, are not licensed as banking institutions in mainland China and cannot hold CNY deposits or execute SAFE-registered FX transactions for a mainland FIP. They may only be used as an auxiliary Hong Kong collection/settlement vehicle to receive Stripe and USDC proceeds and then convert to fiat before the funds are remitted to the mainland banking system. Direct use as the FIP's principal treasury is Low-viability; using an EMI for the HK intermediary is Medium-viability because the HK entity can lawfully settle there, but final onshore onboarding still requires a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor must first complete the mainland FDI registration with SAFE and obtain the Foreign-Invested Partnership establishment certificate. The FIP then opens a capital account at a Tier-1 bank, and the Hong Kong partner wires the capital injection by SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE FDI registration code and the approved capital-injection purpose code. Funds must stay within the registered contribution quota and business scope. Upon arrival, the FIP may leave the FX in the capital account or settle into CNY under the 'capital project settlement' process, submitting a payment-use declaration, board/partner resolutions, and underlying contracts. A PRC CPA must issue a capital-verification report after the inbound funds are received and before the FIP may use them for operational disbursements.For virtual-concert royalty/licensing payments to the Hong Kong treasury entity or other overseas rights holders, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate remittance with a compliant SAFE service-trade purpose code (such as royalties or software/platform fee), supported by the service agreement, performance evidence, and tax withholding certificate where applicable. Commercial letters of credit are not normally used for intangible virtual services; documentary collections are only relevant when the FIP imports physical stage hardware or audiovisual equipment.
