Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, Limited Partnerships Section, Registrar of Companies, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with ancillary Business Registration Office, Inland Revenue Department, Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

Governing statute

Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37), sections 3, 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8; Partnership Ordinance (Cap 38), sections 2 and 3; Business Registration Ordinance (Cap 310), sections 5 and 6; Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap 117) where applicable; Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615), Schedule 2, where applicable. Formation / constitutional filings

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a limited partnership under Cap 37 or a general partnership under Cap 38. The Partnership Agreement governs the amount, timing, form and conditions of capital contributions. No blocked escrow account is required under Hong Kong legislation; however, if a China-based partner remits contributions, such remittance must first satisfy applicable PRC outbound investment (NDRC), Ministry of Commerce filing, and SAFE foreign exchange registration, and funds should only be deposited into the partnership bank account after that account is lawfully opened and operated under the banking mandate.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and account administration are conducted via the GovHK eTAX portal (https://www.etax.gov.hk) and the IRD public website (https://www.ird.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) formed in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using Form IRBR 1 (Application for Business Registration) within one month of the commencement of business. The application is submitted to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department and must disclose all individual and corporate partners, including non-resident partners. Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing a Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the partnership. If any non-resident partner has no Hong Kong address, a Hong Kong tax representative or authorized representative must be appointed to receive statutory correspondence. The Business Registration Certificate must be renewed annually or triennially according to the prescribed issuance cycle. For profits tax purposes, the partnership itself is fiscally transparent under section 22 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). The partnership does not file a standalone profits tax return; each partner is separately assessed on their share of Hong Kong-sourced partnership profits. The IRD assigns each partner a separate Taxpayer Reference Number (TRN) upon registration or upon filing of the partner's first Profits Tax Return.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a 'check-the-box' entity classification election or an elective transparency regime under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Partnerships are mandatorily treated as pass-through for profits tax purposes. No hybrid mismatch election is available. The IRD may apply the general anti-avoidance provisions in sections 61 and 61A of the IRO to disregard or recharacterize artificial transactions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax is imposed in Hong Kong. The indirect tax regime is limited to specific duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), which do not apply to engineering CAD/BIM services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no VAT/GST/sales-tax registration threshold in Hong Kong because no broad-based indirect tax exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, and therefore there is no 0% export rate, no statutory zero-rating mechanism, and no input tax credit or refund system for cross-border services. For profits tax purposes, income from cross-border engineering CAD/BIM services may be treated as outside the territorial scope of Hong Kong taxation if the services are performed and the contracts are negotiated and enforced outside Hong Kong, but this is a territorial-source analysis under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), not an indirect-tax zero-rating claim.

  • Not applicable to indirect tax - Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST and therefore requires no customs export declaration, bill of entry, bill of lading, or inward remittance certificate for zero-rated export VAT purposes. For profits tax territorial-source substantiation, the FIP should retain service contracts, invoices, bank statements, remittance confirmations, project work logs, and evidence that all service performance activities occurred outside Hong Kong.

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing pre-authorization, tax-API integration, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists under Hong Kong tax law. Electronic invoices and records have legal validity under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance (Cap. 553), and the IRD accepts electronically generated business records for tax purposes provided they are complete, accurate, and retained in accordance with the record-keeping obligations in the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Not applicable to VAT/GST export zero-rating because no indirect tax is levied. However, in a profits tax audit, if the IRD determines that partnership profits are Hong Kong-sourced rather than offshore-sourced, the partners become liable for profits tax on their respective shares. Incorrect returns and fraudulent or willful tax evasion are subject to penalties under sections 80 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), including fines and additional tax of up to three times the amount undercharged, as well as imprisonment in serious cases. The FIP must therefore maintain full documentary evidence of offshore service delivery, contract execution, client communications, and foreign exchange receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current account under IMF Article VIII, but strict capital-account controls are administered by SAFE. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with a Hong Kong treasury overlay cannot freely move capital into or out of mainland China. Inbound capital is only compliant through a SAFE-registered FDI foreign-exchange capital account; once converted to RMB, the funds must be used for the FIP's normal operating purposes. Partner loan and equity contributions are subject to capacity and maturity covenants. Profit and partnership distributions require audited financial statements, full tax settlement proof, bank confirmation of capital verification, and a SAFE-compliant remittance application; the Hong Kong remittance leg may face additional bank sanctions screening and source-of-funds scrutiny. Current-account revenues from CAD/BIM outsourcing are generally convertible and may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB, but every receipt must be accompanied by the service contract, invoice, tax vouchers and bank submission to SAFE. Historically mandatory surrender ratios have been abolished, so the largest practical hurdle is documentary precision and the bank's discretionary risk appetite rather than an absolute conversion ceiling. Currency convertibility in practice is therefore conditional and flow-specific.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-monitoring rules, export/current-account foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated to the PRC and credited to a designated bank account within 180 days of the date the export/service obligation is completed and invoiced, unless an offshore retention account has been previously approved. Mandatory conversion ratios are currently set at 0% for normal trade; the FIP may elect to keep 100% of receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert any portion to RMB at a licensed bank's spot rate. In cases of abrupt capital-flow pressure, SAFE may temporarily reintroduce a compulsory surrender ratio, and the published ratio must then be observed. For BIM/CAD services exports, the 'export' trigger is the acceptance certificate or milestone invoice rather than a customs declaration; the bank will treat missing evidence as an unfiled trade receivable and may block the account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the HK-side authorised institution supervisor.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Not viable as a core treasury rail. Offshore digital EMIs (including Wise) do not hold a mainland Chinese banking licence, are not SAFE-designated banks, and cannot open a capital foreign-exchange account for an FIP, accept FDI capital injections, perform SAFE trade- and capital-account reporting, or issue clean SWIFT corporate MT103 with proper purpose coding. Mainland banks will reject upstream/downstream payments that route through an unlicensed EMI because the sending institution is not a regulated bank and the underlying FX narratives are incomplete. For the Hong Kong ancillary vehicle, Wise may be used only for negligible administrative expenses outside the regulated capital/trade pipeline, subject to HKMA payment service provider rules; it cannot replace the authorised bank wire, LC, CIPS or documentary collection rails. Feasibility rating: Low (core flows), Incidental (HK-side petty expenses).

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route all inbound funding from the HK treasury vehicle to the FIP's designated SAFE capital foreign-exchange account via SWIFT MT103. The MT103 must show the remitter as the registered Hong Kong SAR partner or investor, the beneficiary as the exact FIP legal name, and in Field 70 a purpose statement referencing the SAFE cross-border transaction category for inbound direct investment paid-in capital; the receiving bank will map the corresponding BOP/FX purpose code in its SAFE reporting and cannot accept generic personal or investment notes. After funds clear, the bank verifies under the capital-cap account payment mechanism against the FIP's business licence, partnership deed and SAFE FDI registration; for RMB settlement, the FIP must submit the relevant contract, invoice, payroll list or purchase order. If the capital comes in CNY from Hong Kong, use the RMB clearing line via CIPS or the Hong Kong RMB clearing bank. Capital verification for the Chinese FIP is evidenced by the bank-issued confirmation letter, not only by an audit report.Use SWIFT MT103 with full contract and invoice references in Field 70 for direct service payments, subcontractor fees, royalties and operating expenses. For large civil-infrastructure procurement, issue an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit through the FIP's Tier-1 bank (MT700/MT707) with the beneficiary's draft, shipment/performance documents, and insurance as required. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for medium-risk suppliers or repeat vendors where LC cost is not justified. Cross-border RMB disbursements should be routed via CIPS; USD and HKD payments clear through CHIPS/Fedwire and HKCHATS respectively. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: An independent certified CPA audit is legally mandatory in Hong Kong when the entity cannot rely on the small private company audit exemption, namely when any of the following thresholds is exceeded: annual turnover above HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,000), total assets above HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000), or headcount above 100 employees. For a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership, the Inland Revenue Department also requires audited financial statements to accompany the Partnership Profits Tax Return where gross income exceeds HKD 5,000,000 (approximately USD 641,000), where the partnership claims a tax loss, where non-resident partners are allocated Hong Kong profits, or where IRD issues a written notice requiring audited accounts. The audit must be performed by a certified public accountant registered with the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council and must comply with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard Profits Tax rate for unincorporated businesses/partnerships; first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits is taxed at 7.5% under the two-tiered regime

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends, including profit distributions by a partnership. The partnership itself is not taxed at entity level; profits are taxed in the hands of partners. Thus, repatriation of profits from Hong Kong to non-resident partners is generally free of dividend withholding tax, but the partners must file Profits Tax returns and pay tax at their applicable rate before distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under DIPN 58 (Transfer Pricing Documentation), a Local File is required if the total amount of controlled transactions in any single category exceeds HK$4,000,000 for services, intangible property, or financial transactions, or exceeds HK$8,000,000 for goods purchases or sales. A Master File is required where the ultimate parent entity's group consolidated revenue exceeds HK$680,000,000 and the Hong Kong entity engages in controlled transactions with foreign related parties.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted dedicated Economic Substance Regulations similar to the BVI, Cayman, or UAE. However, the Inland Revenue Ordinance, in particular the General Anti-Abuse Rule under s.61A, the anti-treaty-shopping provision under s.9A, and DIPN 60 on offshore profits, impose functional and substance analysis. The partnership must demonstrate real and substantive economic activity in Hong Kong: a physical office, full-time employees with BIM/CAD expertise, local decision-making on contracts and pricing, and maintenance of key records. A partnership that merely invoices from Hong Kong while performing all Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in mainland China will fail the economic substance test; the IRD may recharacterize the profits as Hong Kong-source, impose penalties, and disallow treaty benefits under the BEPS multilateral instrument.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: Under s.82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), any person who knowingly and willfully makes a false return or neglects to report assessable profits is liable to a penalty of three times the tax undercharged, in addition to a fine of HK$50,000. Under s.80(2), conviction on indictment triggers a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; summary conviction carries a fine of HK$10,000 and imprisonment for 6 months. The Commissioner may also raise additional assessments for the statute-barred years if fraud is proven.

Director personal liability: The general partner or managing partner of the FIP is personally and jointly severally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities under s.71 IRO. The doctrine of piercing the corporate veil applies where the partnership is used as a facade to avoid tax: the IRD may disregard the partnership structure and assess the controlling partners directly. Subject to forfeiture orders, the partners' personal bank accounts and other assets can be seized; the Commissioner may also seek a travel restriction (blacklisting) through the High Court, preventing departed directors or partners from leaving Hong Kong until tax and penalties are paid. In egregious criminal cases, the relevant partner faces imprisonment of up to 3 years, and under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, the court can impose a confiscation order equal to the benefit derived from tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing - Unjustified Cost-Plus Markup on Intercompany CAD Services

Statutory sanction: The IRD has the authority under s.20AAA, s.50AAM, and s.50AAP of the IRO to adjust the income to reflect an arm's-length consideration. Upon adjustment, an additional assessment is raised with a penalty of up to 100% of the extra tax under s.82A. Moreover, failure to prepare contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation as required by DIPN 58 incurs a fixed penalty of HK$50,000 under s.51D and an additional penalty for each day of continuing default. The China State Taxation Administration (STA) may also conduct a correlative adjustment and impose underpayment penalties at 25% punitive corporate tax plus late fee surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors (or general partners) who knowingly sign false TP documentation or approve non-arm's-length invoices are liable to personal penalties under the IRO; the IRD can pierce the partnership to recover the additional tax and penalty directly from directors' personal assets. In fraudulent trading scenarios, the Companies Ordinance s.458 may impose criminal liability on directors who participate in business carried on with intent to defraud creditors, with a maximum imprisonment of 15 years and unlimited fines. The IRD also shares information with the China tax authorities under the bilateral exchange of information; a trans-border transfer pricing investigation can result in double tax consequences, blacklisting of the directors in both jurisdictions, and criminal prosecution for making false written representations to tax authorities.
AML and Shell Company - Economic Substance Deficiency and Money Laundering Through Trade Payments

Statutory sanction: The IRD may treat the partnership as a sham under s.61A of the IRO and disregard all transactions, assessing the Chinese resident partner as if the income had been received directly in China. The Financial Intelligence Office and the Commercial Crime Bureau may impose penalties under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) with fines of up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years for non-compliance with AML due diligence and record-keeping requirements. The court may also impose a control order under the Prevention of Bribery and Corruption Ordinance.

Director personal liability: For limited partnerships, the limited partners have capital protection only if they refrain from participating in management; the general partner who establishes and controls the shell FIP is personally liable for all tax debts, penalties, and legal costs. Piercing the corporate veil is automatic because a partnership is not a separate legal person in Hong Kong; creditors and the tax authority can execute directly against the general partner's personal estate. In laundering proceedings under the Drug Trafficking and Organized Crime Ordinance, the general partner may be sentenced to life imprisonment and fined HK$5,000,000; a confiscation order covers the full amount of the laundered payments, and the partner's Hong Kong property, accounts, and passport can be frozen and revoked.
