Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local Municipal Tax Service at the registered office of the Foreign-Invested Partnership; all electronic registration, filing, invoice, and refund actions are processed through the official STA Electronic Taxpayer Hall portal and its approved application programming interfaces.

E-government filing portal

After the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) business license with an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), the USCC is automatically the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number for all PRC tax purposes. The legal representative or an authorized tax agent must, within 30 calendar days of business license issuance, complete the electronic taxpayer enrolment in the STA Electronic Taxpayer Hall: (1) perform real-name authentication of the legal representative and financial officer through the STA mobile application or bank-channel identity verification; (2) file the partnership agreement, SAMR business license, registered office proof, business scope, accounting and cost-calculation methods, financial-period end, bank settlement account, responsible tax officer, and authorized delegates; (3) record every foreign partner's name, tax-residence jurisdiction (for example Hong Kong SAR), ownership percentage, and apostilled or notarized certificate of incorporation or identity document, with a Chinese translation where required by law; (4) select VAT taxpayer status: small-scale taxpayer status is available while annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000, and general taxpayer status is adopted upon application or once the mandatory turnover threshold is exceeded so that special VAT invoices and input VAT credits can be used; (5) apply for the digital invoice quota and fully digitalized e-invoice registration through the invoice module; and (6) complete customs import/export enterprise registration if the FIP will export goods. A foreign non-resident partner that does not have an establishment in mainland China is not separately VAT-registered merely because of its FIP interest; the FIP itself withholds any applicable taxes on distributions and is the VAT taxpayer for its own taxable supplies. If the foreign partner is subsequently found to have a permanent establishment in China, it must separately obtain a Chinese taxpayer identification code through the non-resident registration function of the Electronic Taxpayer Hall.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China applies an asymmetric convertibility model: the current account is substantially convertible for all legal residents, while the capital account is heavily administered. For a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in the battery-safety testing industry, fee income earned from UN38.3 drop tests, thermal abuse, and CE-RED electromagnetic laboratory work is a current-account service receipt. Such revenue may be credited to the FIP's current-account foreign-currency business account and converted into RMB at any designated bank in exchange for authentic commercial invoices and test certificates. The bank will submit the relevant remittance information to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the Customs trade platform. By contrast, capital contributions from the Hong Kong partner or any foreign investor must be injected through a dedicated FDI capital account opened at a SAFE-designated bank, after completion of a Foreign Investment Information Report. Once settled into RMB, the capital proceeds are subject to an expenditure negative list: they cannot be used for securities or wealth-management products, intercompany lending, real property outside the partnership's operations, or any activity inside the prohibited industries. Profit remittances to foreign partners are allowed only from the audited after-tax surplus and require (i) a PRC-certified audit report, (ii) a tax clearance certificate demonstrating that corporate income tax has been paid or withheld, (iii) the prevailing official remittance application, and (iv) a bank KYC check on the Hong Kong payee. In addition, the FIP must report all capital receipts in the national Foreign Investment Information System and complete an annual SAFE parity review. Any crypto-based remittance or informal non-bank channel is illegal and will jeopardize the bank's license.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Administrative Measures for Foreign Exchange in Trade, the FIP must repatriate all export receipts for goods and services to an onshore bank within 180 days from the date of export declaration (goods) or from the service completion date (testing services). Since the abolition of compulsory surrender of foreign exchange in 2012, there is no fixed statutory percentage of export proceeds that must be converted; the FIP may retain the entire receipt in a current-account foreign-currency account or convert it through the bank's settlement system. However, for any RMB conversion above USD 50,000 (or the bank's internal limit), the FIP must present the underlying testing contract, commercial invoice, and (if applicable) customs declaration so the bank can input the transaction into SAFE's cross-border receipt and payment platform. The bank will reconcile the proceeds with the CHINAPORT customs data, and a discrepancy exceeding 5% will trigger a documentary inquiry. Non-repatriation or late repatriation without a valid reason is a penalty offense under the Administration of Foreign Exchange Act.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - entirely infeasible for onshore operations. The bank may also require an external auditor's management letter confirming the purchase was booked as plant and equipment, and no round-robin back-through to the FIP occurred.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign-currency capital from the Hong Kong partner must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's FDI capital account at the onshore bank, with the beneficiary exactly matching the registered FIP name and a purpose code prescribed by SAFE for foreign direct investment in non-listed partnerships. Before the bank accepts the transfer, the FIP must have completed the Foreign Investment Information Report through the national integrated investment system and presented its unified social credit code. After credit, the funds cannot be withdrawn in foreign exchange without further approval; instead they must be settled into CNY through the bank's 'capital settlement for payment' module to pay only approved operational items. The FIP must then engage a PRC-certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report (within the statutory period, generally 30-60 days after injection) and the bank must file the receipts with SAFE's FX balance-of-payment statistics. Any use of an offshore EMI or crypto route voids the bank's ability to provide the compliance endorsement.For overseas purchases of UN383 testing calibration equipment and consumables, the FIP may use: (1) SWIFT MT103 for straightforward advance or open-account payments to suppliers, with bank verification based on the import contract and commercial invoice; (2) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) for high-value shipments of specialized battery-testing rigs or gases, with the FIP as applicant and original documents (bill of lading, certificate of origin, inspection certificate) negotiated through its tier-1 bank; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P, D/A) for repeat suppliers in stable relationships, allowing the bank to transfer documents against acceptance. All outbound RMB or FX remittances for goods over a threshold must be matched with customs declarations and reported to SAFE in the trade platform, and for services the underlying test contract and proof of delivery must be maintained.Deliverable CNY/USD forward contracts onshore (up to 12 months) with a commercial bank, requiring underlying trade invoices for testing exports or equipment imports; HKD/CNY forwards are also available where HKD cash flows arise from Hong Kong remittances.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for CNY are only permitted in the offshore market (e.g., Hong Kong via the parent investor's dealer); the mainland FIP cannot register for onshore NDFs, and SAFE limits onshore hedging to real trade deliverables.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) available for converting a USD/HKD foreign-currency term loan into CNY, subject to SAFE's foreign debt registration and full documentation of the loan agreement.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - entirely infeasible for onshore operations. Offshore digital EMI providers such as Wise (TransferWise), Revolut Business, and Airwallex are not licensed banks or payment institutions under the PBOC Payment Service License framework and cannot open CNY-denominated accounts, receive FDI capital, or service payroll and VAT payments for the FIP. Airwallex does operate a Hong Kong MSO licence, but that only supports offshore HKD/USD transaction banking for the Hong Kong leg; it cannot be used to settle inside mainland China. The FIP therefore should use Wise or similar only for pre-arranged pre-funding by the Hong Kong partner into an offshore collection account, followed by a standard MT103 transfer to the onshore FDI account. Any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI for onshore RMB settlement would violate the Administrative Licensing for Payment Services and carry fines and reputational risk.
