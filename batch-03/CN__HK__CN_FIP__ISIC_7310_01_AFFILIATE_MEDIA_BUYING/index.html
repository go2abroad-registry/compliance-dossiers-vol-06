Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: PRC Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn), supplemented by provincial and municipal electronic tax bureaus for specific filing and registration operations.

E-government filing portal

Upon formation of the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the partnership obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as its statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes. Within 30 calendar days from the date of business license issuance, the FIP must complete tax registration with the competent local tax authority through the online Electronic Tax Bureau portal. The online New Taxpayer Startup Package submitted must include: (1) the partnership business license; (2) the executed partnership agreement; (3) valid identification documents for the general partner and foreign partners, including notarized and legalised or apostilled certificates of incorporation, passports, or beneficial ownership evidence; (4) the partnership's registered address, bank account details, and contact information; (5) a description of the principal business activities and intended tax categories; and (6) any foreign investor's tax residency certificate or supporting documentation needed for treaty relief. The local tax authority then finalises the tax registration, issues the tax registration record, determines the applicable taxes (VAT, CIT/PTT, IIT, stamp duty, surcharges), and assigns filing frequencies. Non-resident partners without a fixed place of business in China may require an additional non-resident tax identification registration through the STA's non-resident tax system for withholding or refund purposes.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and Caishui 2000 No.91, the FIP itself is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes and is not a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) taxpayer. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - Under the PRC Partnership Enterprise Law and Caishui 2000 No.91, the FIP itself is fiscally transparent for income tax purposes and is not a Corporate Income Tax (CIT) taxpayer. Each partner is taxed separately on their distributive share: corporate partners pay CIT up to 25%; individual partners pay Individual Income Tax (IIT) on business income at progressive rates up to 35%; foreign partners are taxed on China-sourced income in accordance with their tax classification and applicable treaty provisions. However, the FIP remains a distinct VAT taxpayer and must separately register, invoice, and discharge VAT obligations on taxable supplies within China.

Available election forms

None - PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election for partnerships; fiscal transparency is mandatory and irrevocable. The FIP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation, and no partnership anti-abuse elections under US check-the-box rules are available under Chinese tax law. Only the passive versus active income treatment for partners is determined at the partner level, subject to general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) under the PRC CIT regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis VAT registration threshold exists for a newly formed PRC entity; the FIP must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining its business license irrespective of turnover. For VAT status classification, annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 generally qualify the taxpayer as a small-scale VAT taxpayer (currently eligible for a reduced levy rate or 1% as introduced by periodic relief policies); when annual taxable sales reach RMB 5,000,000 or more, registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory, enabling input VAT credits and the issuance of special VAT invoices. Given the advertising/media-buying service scope, the general VAT taxpayer status is usually advisable to claim input credits on media procurement and platform costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Caishui 2016 No.36 Annex 4, a VAT zero rate with input credit/refund is available to general VAT taxpayers for: (i) international transportation services; (ii) aerospace transport services; and (iii) enumerated offshore services supplied to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China, including R&D services, energy performance contracting services, design services, broadcast and TV programme production/distribution services, software services, circuit design and testing services, information system services, business process management services, offshore service outsourcing, and technology transfer services. The FIP's affiliate media-buying services (digital advertising arbitrage) are not enumerated in the zero-rated list; they may qualify for VAT exemption (not zero-refundable) if the advertising placement is wholly consumed outside China. To claim zero-rating for qualifying goods or an eligible offshore digital service, the FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer, maintain separate books for export transactions, and file a zero-rated export declaration in the period VAT return with the statutory documentation bundle attached. If the service is merely advertising/media buying, it is prudent to treat it as exempt rather than zero-rated unless restructured into an enumerated zero-rated service category such as software service or information system service.

  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list issued by the FIP
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) or equivalent international transport document evidencing the goods were exported from China

The PRC operates the Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice system under Golden Tax Phase IV. VAT invoices are issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which performs real-time fiscal clearance by assigning a unique digital invoice code, applying an anti-fraud QR stamp, uploading invoice data to the STA in near real time, and reconciling issuance and receipt through the taxpayer's Tax Digital Account. No separate fiscal hardware device is required, but the taxpayer must integrate an API-based e-invoicing interface or use the web-based portal, ensure accurate VAT service tax codes, provide line-item descriptions, and respond to automated risk-control prompts from the Golden Tax big-data platform. Special VAT invoices must be declared in the ordinary VAT return and, for export zero-rating or input refunds, matched against receipt-side digital validation.

Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating/refund claims are treated as export tax fraud. Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, the tax authority will recover the entire refunded VAT amount, impose daily late-payment interest at 0.05% of the overdue tax, and levy a fine of 100%-500% of the tax obtained by fraud. The STA may also revoke the taxpayer's export tax refund qualification, disqualify the entity from issuing special VAT invoices, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution under the crime of defraudulently obtaining export tax rebates, which carries imprisonment and criminal fines. SAFE enforcement for non-repatriation or falsified FX receipts can add fines of 5%-30% of the involved amount and suspension of the foreign exchange trade monitoring filing. Golden Tax Phase IV continuously cross-matches customs declarations, VAT invoices, FX receipts, logistics data, and bank settlement records; any mismatch, delayed repatriation, or artificial transaction chain will trigger automated audit alerts, freeze pending refunds, and permit retrospective clawback for up to ten years in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China controls CNY through a current-account liberalization and capital-account registration system. A Foreign-Invested Partnership established under PRC law is onshore; therefore all cross-border capital flows must be filed with SAFE through designated banks. Current-account payments such as advertising service fees, affiliate commissions and media placement costs are convertible after the bank validates the underlying contract, invoice and customs/tax declaration. Capital-account items, including partner capital injection, foreign-partner buyout, liquidation proceeds and offshore loans, require SAFE permission, a dedicated escrow account, and sometimes an inbound/outbound FDI filing. Hong Kong's legal territory is distinct: HKD and CNH are freely convertible, there are no surrender requirements, and the use of HK USD/HKD accounts is unrestricted; however, a mainland FIP cannot legally use Hong Kong accounts as the primary settlement system for onshore revenue. The practical treasury model must maintain onshore CNY/foreign currency current-account balances for SAFE compliance and offshore HK multi-currency accounts for affiliate payables and media payments. Profit remittance from China demands an audited annual report, complete tax clearance or withholding certificate, and partner distribution resolution; otherwise safe banks will block the overseas telegraphic transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC current-account rules, foreign-currency export receipts from digital advertising services must be repatriated to a domestic SAFE-designated foreign-currency account within 180 days from the later of the service delivery date or the contracted payment due date; all monies receivable from a cross-border trade or service transaction must be returned to China (100% repatriation) and cannot be retained offshore unless an SAFE-approved offshore account exists. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio is currently 0%; the FIP may hold export proceeds in foreign currency in its current-account foreign-exchange account or convert to CNY at a designated bank, though certain provincial branches may internally require conversion of pro-rata tax and administrative expenses.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the autonomous Hong Kong SAR monetary authority for HKD/CNH clearing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low-to-Medium for an auxiliary HK disbursement wallet; not viable as the primary bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex or Mercury are not legally permitted to service a mainland China FIP as the main operating account because PRC foreign-exchange law requires the FIP's cross-border receipts and payments to clear through SAFE-supervised banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions holding restrictive PBOC licenses. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong’s Transfer Pricing (TP) regulations (ss.50AA-50AO of the IRO) require a Master File and Local File when the taxpayer’s group consolidated annual turnover exceeds HKD 7.5 billion AND related-party transactions exceed the following de minimis thresholds: (i) transfers of property – HKD 220 million; (ii) financial asset transactions – HKD 220 million; (iii) transfers of intangible property – HKD 110 million; (iv) all other transactions (including media buying services, commissions, and lead generation fees) – HKD 44 million. Where the partnership is part of a multinational group generating performance marketing revenues, it must maintain contemporaneous TP documentation if intercompany service fees (e.g., payments to media inventory suppliers or affiliate network providers) exceed these amounts.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a discrete Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) akin to BVI or Cayman; however, the principle of substantive economic activity is embedded in the territorial source doctrine, the beneficial ownership rules for treaty relief, and the anti-abuse provisions of the HK-Mainland DTA. For a Hong Kong Foreign-Invested Partnership engaged in affiliate media buying, the IRD will examine whether the core income-generating activities (CIGA) — such as entering into advertising contracts, operating the media-buying platforms, managing affiliate networks, and negotiating revenue shares — are physically performed by employees or partners based in Hong Kong, or whether the business is merely booked in Hong Kong. A lack of office, staff, decision-making autonomy, or a low level of value-added functions will cause the IRD to re-characterize the partnership as an offshore shell, deny offshore-profit exemption under the IRD’s Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (DIPN 57), and for treaty purposes, deny the beneficial ownership status, thereby exposing the entire profit to Hong Kong profits tax plus penalties. The OECD’s BEPS Action 5 substantive activity requirement applies, and the partnership must maintain payroll, lease agreements, and board/partner meeting minutes evidencing real management.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), s.82A(1). On conviction on indictment, the offender is liable to a fine of HKD 50,000, three times the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for three years. In addition, per s.80(2), failure to file a correct return may incur a fine of HKD 10,000 and three times the tax undercharged. The IRD may impose a summary assessment (s.59) with a 5% surcharge for late payment, and interest at 3% per month on outstanding tax after six months. Where the fraud also involves Chinese mainland tax evasion, the China Tax Administration Collection Law (s.63) imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the amount of underpaid tax, and the Criminal Law (arts. 201-204) prescribes up to seven years’ imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: For a partnership whose managing partner is a corporate entity, directors can be personally named in the IRD’s prosecution as aiding and abetting (s.82A(3)). Courts have pierced the partnership veil where the general partner is a company with no substance, rendering the company’s directors personally liable for the tax debt under s.74 of the Partnership Ordinance and the doctrine of fraud. Personal assets, including bank accounts and real estate, may be garnished. The IRD may apply to the court for a domestic travel ban against the directors and managing partners (Control of Departure Ordinance, Cap. 72), forbidding them from leaving Hong Kong until the tax and penalties are paid. If the fraud is accompanied by the use of forged invoices, directors may face additional charges under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) s.73 for forgery, punishable by imprisonment for up to 14 years.
Treaty Abuse / Dishonest Beneficial Ownership Claim

Statutory sanction: Under the HK-Mainland DTA (Article on ‘Entitlement to Benefits’), the mainland tax authority may deny the treaty reduction and collect the full statutory withholding tax of 10% on the gross payments, plus a late-payment surcharge of 5% per month (0.05% per day) per Art. 32 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. If the denial arises from a false Beneficial Ownership Declaration, the mainland authority can impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded under CTAL s.63. The IRD may also disallow any claimed offshore status and assess the entire profit as Hong Kong-sourced with a 100% penalty if it finds a deliberate attempt to avoid tax. In civil proceedings, the mainland tax authorities may recover the additional tax from the ultimate beneficial owner through mutual administrative assistance.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who sign the Beneficial Ownership Declaration and TRC applications without ensuring verifiable substance are personally liable for misrepresentation under the Misrepresentation Ordinance (Cap. 284). Civil restoration orders may require the director to use personal assets to settle the mainland tax liability. The mainland State Tax Administration publishes a ‘serious dishonest taxpayer’ blacklist, which imposes travel bans and restriction on high-consumption activities within mainland China. For criminal purposes, participation in a scheme to obtain treaty benefits by deception can be prosecuted as conspiracy to defraud under the Crimes Ordinance, with a maximum term of 14 years. The partnership’s limited partners may lose their limited liability if the corporate veil is pierced; they will then be treated as general partners with personal liability for the full tax debt.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: Pursuant to ss.50AA-50AO of the IRO, the IRD may adjust the partnership’s assessable profits to the arm’s length amount. If the TP documentation is absent or materially incomplete, the taxpayer may be subject to a default penalty of HKD 10,000, plus 3 times the amount of additional tax assessed, under s.80(2) or s.82A if the omission was intentional. China’s Special Tax Adjustments (STAR) regime (CTTAT arts. 121-124) can impose an adjustment of up to 10% of the turnover, plus interest at the one-year lending rate and a penalty of 5% per annum on the unpaid tax. For transactions that involve a paper loss-making entity used to shelter profits, the IRD may apply the general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under s.61A, which can increase the assessable profit by the full amount of the transaction and apply penalties of up to 100% of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directors of a corporate general partner are required, under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), to approve the partnership’s financial statements and TP disclosures. A director who fails to procure and retain the TP documentation may be disqualified from being a director for up to 15 years under s.168L if the court determines the failure to be persistent or dishonourable. The personal assets of the general partner can be reached for all additional tax, interest, and penalties due to the joint and several liability rules of the Partnership Ordinance s.13. In cross-border scenarios, mainland tax authorities can apply mutual administrative assistance to enforce personal liability, and may include the directors in the ‘serious untrustworthy’ blacklist, leading to restrictions on purchasing property, taking flights, and obtaining banking services. In cases of deliberate collusion to shift profits with the intent to defraud tax, the director may be charged criminally under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) s.16A (obtaining services by deception) and face up to 10 years’ imprisonment.
