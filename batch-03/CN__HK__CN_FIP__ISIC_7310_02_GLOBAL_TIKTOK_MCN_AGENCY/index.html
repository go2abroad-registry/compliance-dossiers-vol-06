Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk). The Business Registration Office forms part of the IRD for Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) registrations.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Confirm the Hong Kong business registration obligation under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). An entity described as a Foreign-Invested Partnership, where constituted as a partnership in Hong Kong, is treated by the IRD as a partnership. Step 2: Prepare the partnership deed or limited partnership agreement, passports or foreign registration certificates of all non-resident partners, proof of a Hong Kong registered office or service address, and, where the non-resident partners have no Hong Kong fixed place of business, an appointment letter for a Hong Kong authorised tax representative. Step 3: If the arrangement is a limited partnership, first register with the Companies Registry under the applicable Hong Kong limited partnership legislation, then file Form IRBR 1 with the Business Registration Office through the eTAX portal or in person, together with the prescribed fee. Step 4: Obtain the Business Registration Certificate and the partnership Tax File Number; the Business Registration Number serves as the Hong Kong TIN. Because Hong Kong has no VAT or GST, no separate VAT ID is issued and no VAT registration is made. Step 5: After registration, the IRD issues profits tax returns to the partnership; however, the partnership itself does not pay profits tax on the aggregate profit. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), each partner is separately assessable on their share of the partnership profit sourced in Hong Kong.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not_Applicable - Hong Kong has no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. Excise duty is chargeable only on a limited class of dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no indirect tax registration threshold in Hong Kong because no general indirect tax exists. Business registration under Cap. 310 is triggered by carrying on business in Hong Kong, not by turnover, and does not impose VAT or GST obligations.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not levy VAT or GST on exports or domestic supplies; therefore there is no 0% export VAT rate, no statutory export zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism. A Hong Kong MCN agency may invoice TikTok and creator clients without Hong Kong indirect tax. For customs purposes, only dutiable commodities require export duties or non-direct tax declarations; digital creator services do not pass through customs. If the Hong Kong entity's profits are not sourced in Hong Kong, the IRD applies territorial source principles, but this is a profits tax issue and not an indirect tax zero-rating matter.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The China-resident FIP operates within a stringent capital-account control environment. The renminbi is convertible on current-account items for genuine trade and cross-border service transactions once the bank has completed authenticity and conformity review, but capital-account flows remain governed by SAFE registration and operational boundaries. The Hong Kong leg enjoys free convertibility under the Linked Exchange Rate System, but the binding constraint for the FIP is the PRC Foreign Investment and Foreign Exchange administration framework. The FIP must open a SAFE-registered FDI foreign exchange capital account to receive the Hong Kong partner's capital contribution; funds cannot be freely used or settled into RMB unless the capital account payment is aligned with the registered commercial purpose and supported by contracts, invoices, or audits as applicable. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is a current-account distribution and requires audited financial statements, final tax settlement certificates, partnership profit-distribution resolutions, and Chinese bank verification under SAFE's current-account payment rules. Conversely, service income received from TikTok or overseas clients can be retained in foreign currency or voluntarily settled into RMB after the bank reviews the underlying service contract and platform statements. No surrender margin is imposed on the Hong Kong vehicle, but the PRC FIP must participate in the nationwide trade monitoring and declaration system for every cross-border receipt or payment. Thus the practical FX environment is a hybrid: full external convertibility at the Hong Kong treasury level and prudential, documentary-driven convertibility at the China FIP level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the PRC FIP, export and cross-border service proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-currency current account; after authenticity review, the FIP may retain the foreign exchange or voluntarily settle into renminbi. There is no fixed percentage surrender requirement under the current Chinese framework, but the bank and SAFE require the proceeds to be credited only after trade or service documentation is matched, and retention is subject to the enterprise's declared foreign-currency account status. For the Hong Kong treasury vehicle, no surrender obligation exists; proceeds may be held in any freely convertible currency with no prescribed timeline.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the China-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the separate monetary authority for the Hong Kong structuring and treasury vehicle.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low / Not viable for the China-resident FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise B2B, Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold a Chinese commercial banking licence and cannot provide renminbi current accounts, SAFE foreign-exchange registration, or capital-account services to a mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership. The FIP must use a licensed Chinese commercial bank for foreign currency receipt, settlement, and capital injection. The Hong Kong parent may use a licensed EMI or Money Service Operator for offshore creator payouts outside China, provided the EMI is registered with the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department as a Money Service Operator or is an HKMA-licensed institution; unlicensed offshore EMIs are not acceptable as a primary clearing rail. Therefore, the TikTok creator payout rail should be split: Hong Kong-based payouts via an authorised institution or licensed MSO, and all China-sourced amounts via a domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Hong Kong partner must be routed by SWIFT MT103 with the registered overseas partner as the sole remitter and the FIP's SAFE-registered FDI capital account as beneficiary. The payment instruction must carry the SAFE transaction declaration code for inbound direct investment in a foreign-invested partnership, the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Code from the FDI registration certificate, the FIP's full Chinese and English names, and the reference of the partnership's capital contribution schedule. The receiving bank will validate the remitter against the registered partner list, credit the funds to the foreign-exchange capital account, and then require the FIP to provide underlying contracts, capital contribution notices, and if required by the bank a PRC certified accountant

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low / Not viable for the China-resident FIP. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise B2B, Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer do not hold a Chinese commercial banking licence and cannot provide renminbi current accounts, SAFE foreign-exchange registration, or capital-account services to a mainland-resident Foreign-Invested Partnership. The FIP must use a licensed Chinese commercial bank for foreign currency receipt, settlement, and capital injection. The Hong Kong parent may use a licensed EMI or Money Service Operator for offshore creator payouts outside China, provided the EMI is registered with the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department as a Money Service Operator or is an HKMA-licensed institution; unlicensed offshore EMIs are not acceptable as a primary clearing rail. Therefore, the TikTok creator payout rail should be split: Hong Kong-based payouts via an authorised institution or licensed MSO, and all China-sourced amounts via a domestic bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under IRD's Transfer Pricing Rulings and the Income (Amendment) Ordinance 2018, a HK taxpayer must prepare a Master File and Local File if it meets all of the following: yearly total turnover ≥ HK$200 million and controlled transaction amounts exceed HK$5 million for property transfers, or HK$1 million for financial transactions, intangibles and services. Even below these thresholds, the partnership must maintain contemporaneous evidence for any cross-border related-party transactions (e.g. parent MCN revenue split with TikTok, inter-company creator contracts) to support arm's length pricing.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations; however, the source rule requires that the core income-generating activities (CIGA) of the MCN agency (contract execution, creator management, negotiation, billing and active decision-making) be performed in Hong Kong to justify the 'carrying on of business in Hong Kong' for profits tax. A pure booking shell that remotely directs operations from Mainland China will fail the source test, and the IRD may deem profits as taxable in Hong Kong, apply the anti-abuse rule under s.61A to disregard artificial transactions, and, where DTA relief is claimed, deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Profits Tax Return Fraud

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82: criminal liability for knowingly making a false return; fine of HK$50,000, treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to 3 years. IRO s.80(2) for an incorrect return without reasonable excuse: fine up to HK$10,000 plus treble tax.

Director personal liability: For a limited partnership, the general partner (GP) is deemed the responsible person and may be individually assessed. A court may order the GP/directors to restore the undercharged tax from their personal assets; IRD may seek a Mareva injunction to freeze assets, and an asset freeze order can ban the individual from leaving Hong Kong (travel restriction) until the tax is settled. Convictions carry deportation or extradition risk under mutual legal assistance.
Failure to Maintain Proper Accounting Records

Statutory sanction: IRO s.51C(2): on conviction, a fine of up to HK$100,000 plus a daily fine of HK$1,000 for each day the offence continues (in practice, courts order imprisonment for up to 6 months where the fine is unpaid).

Director personal liability: The GP, managing partner, and any appointed representatives are jointly and severally liable for the partnership's record-keeping failures. Civil liability includes the reversal of accounting evidence and reconstructive tax assessments; persistent non-compliance can support a finding of fraudulent trading under the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance, leading to disqualification from holding an office in any HK company for up to 15 years and an order to contribute personal assets to cover the tax shortfall.
Artificial Tax Avoidance / GAAR Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61A (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) empowers the IRD to disregard artificial transactions and re-allocate profits on a reasonable basis; an additional tax assessment of up to 100% of the tax avoided may be imposed under s.82A. In the PRC, similar avoidance provisions (Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 47) and the general anti-abuse rule (SAT) may recharacterize the arrangement.

Director personal liability: Under principle of piercing the corporate veil, courts may disregard the limited liability structure where the entity is trading fraudulently or as a sham. The GP or directors face personal civil liability for the full tax underpaid, damages for misrepresentation, and criminal charges of conspiracy to defraud the IRD. A travel ban (blacklist) may be issued by the court upon IRD's application for unpaid tax, prohibiting departure from Hong Kong until payment. In serious cases, extradition to Mainland China for tax fraud under the PRC-HK Mutual Legal Assistance Agreement can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years in PRC.
