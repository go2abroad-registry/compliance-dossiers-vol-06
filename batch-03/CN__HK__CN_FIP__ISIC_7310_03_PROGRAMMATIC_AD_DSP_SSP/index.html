Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Hong Kong SAR Government; electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk); Business Registration Office of the IRD for Business Registration Certificates.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: File for registration as a limited partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) or Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637) if an LPF structure is chosen; this is a legal prerequisite for the entity's existence and must be completed before tax registration. Step 2: Within one month of commencement of business, apply for Business Registration (BR) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using official Form IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) submitted to the Business Registration Office of the IRD. The application must include the partnership's registered name, address, and description of business. For a foreign non-resident partner, attach a certified copy of its overseas registration certificate, and provide a Hong Kong correspondence address for IRD notice purposes. Step 3: The IRD will issue the Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing an 8-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the partnership's de facto tax identification number. The BRN must be displayed at the business premises and used on invoices and correspondence. Step 4: The IRD will allocate an Inland Revenue file number to the partnership and issue Profits Tax Returns (Partnership) on a basis period. A 'Principal Partner' (or the partnership's authorized representative) is responsible for completing and filing the return. Step 5: Each partner (individual or corporate) must separately provide tax identification details: Hong Kong individuals use their HKID card numbers; foreign non-resident individuals must provide their passport number and a current foreign tax identification number; foreign corporate partners must provide proof of incorporation and their overseas tax registration number. The IRD uses this information to assess each partner on their share of partnership profits. No separate VAT/GST registration is required because Hong Kong has no such tax.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable – Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. The territory relies on direct taxes (profits tax, salaries tax, property tax) and selective duties (e.g., liquor, tobacco) which are outside the scope of this entity.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no turnover-based registration threshold for indirect tax because no indirect tax is levied. However, every business, including a service partnership, must register for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) regardless of turnover.

Cross-border services, including programmatic advertising provided from Hong Kong, are outside the scope of indirect tax. For profits tax purposes, income is chargeable only if sourced in Hong Kong under the territorial source principle; services wholly performed outside Hong Kong may be reported as non-taxable offshore income, supported by contracts, evidence of negotiation and execution abroad, and client communications.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible open capital account under the Linked Exchange Rate System. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible outside mainland China and there is no legal distinction between current account and capital account convertibility for ordinary treasury operations. Non-residents and foreign-invested partnerships may maintain offshore RMB (CNH), HKD, USD, EUR or JPY accounts without HKMA approval, fund them from abroad, and remit profits, dividends, or repayment of shareholder loans without surrender or mandatory conversion requirements. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in their original or any permitted currency, and no foreign exchange control or central bank filing is required before remitting for trade or financial purposes. The only meaningful friction lies at the PRC border: capital injections from mainland China must comply with the regulatory framework for outward direct investment, including NDRC filing and SAFE registration, and each remittance must satisfy genuine-transaction review by the Chinese remitting bank. Once the funds are received in Hong Kong, they can be freely diverted to operational expenses, publisher payments, tax, or shareholder distributions, and the HKD/USD exchange rate is stable because the HKMA maintains a tight convertibility band at 7.75 to 7.85. Therefore, treasury planning should prioritise pre-clearance of source-side PRC remittances rather than Hong Kong restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There are no statutory export proceeds surrender rules in Hong Kong. The HKMA does not impose any timeline for repatriation, forced conversion, or ratio requirement for foreign exchange receipts. An exporter may keep the full invoice amount in an operational HKD, USD, or CNH account, convert it at market rates, or use it to pay trade obligations without needing any central bank declaration. If the FIP settles advertising invoices from Chinese clients, the CNH proceeds can be held offshore and are not subject to mainland China surrender or settlement rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) in its capacity as the de facto central bank and Exchange Fund administrator for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; for cross-boundary RMB flows to and from mainland China, the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) also have regulatory authority over the onshore side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium as a primary account, medium as an ancillary collection rail. The Hong Kong Banking Ordinance restricts deposit-taking to licensed authorised institutions; pure offshore EMI licences do not permit deposit-taking in Hong Kong and are not regulated by the HKMA, so they cannot serve as the bank-of-record for a foreign-invested partnership. HK-licensed virtual banks, such as Mox Bank, WeLab Bank and Livi Bank, are authorised institutions and can open basic HKD/USD accounts, but their trade finance, FX hedging, CHATS clearing, and credit capabilities are limited and they generally impose lower transaction thresholds than tier-1 incumbents. An offshore EMI with only an EU/UK e-money licence is vulnerable to inbound remittance rejection by HK compliance, particularly when the ultimate beneficial owner sits in mainland China. Consequently, the partnership should maintain its primary treasury and capital-escrow relationship at a tier-1 commercial bank, and may use a regulated money service operator or virtual bank only for small-ticket disbursements with full reporting to the principal bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The PRC parent should have completed NDRC project filing and SAFE ODI registration for its investment in the Hong Kong FIP before opening the HK bank account or remitting funds. At execution, the onshore bank remits via SWIFT MT103 with a narrative referencing partnership capital contribution, the SAFE ODI certificate number, and the purpose code for outbound direct investment equity or quasi-equity capital. The HK receiving bank will not require Hong Kong Monetary Authority approval, but will ask to see the remitter's identity, the underlying partnership agreement, and a board or general partner resolution approving the contribution. For capital verification, a Hong Kong certified public accountant can issue a certificate after receipt of funds; this certificate is useful for the PRC parent's annual ODI monitoring, Hong Kong profits tax filing, and future dividend or return-of-capital remittances. No minimum capital is statutorily required for the Hong Kong FIP, but most tier-1 banks expect initial operating deposits of at least HKD 500,000 or equivalent to justify account economics and support the capital-escrow classification. The funds may remain in HKD, USD or CNH and can be freely converted into other convertible currencies once inside Hong Kong.For outbound disbursements to publishers, data partners and technology vendors, the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for individual cross-border transfers above HKD 100,000. For Hong Kong domestic payments in HKD, the clearing rail is CHATS; USD and CNH payments also have dedicated CHATS clearing systems that are faster and more cost-effective than legacy correspondent wires. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are only relevant if the FIP is financing physical trade or importing hardware for ad-serving or hardware-based exchanges; in that case a standard confirmed irrevocable L/C may be issued through HSBC or Standard Chartered. In every disbursement, the beneficiary name, bank account, and invoice reference must be explicitly stated on the wire narrative; payments to a pooled wallet belonging to an intermediary or affiliate should be avoided unless supported by a written treasury instruction and a reconciliation report. ACH-style direct settlement is acceptable for payroll and low-value publisher micropayments, but the partner's core trade settlement ledger should retain MT103 or CHATS confirmations so that the documentary evidence is sufficient for internal audit, customs, and tax inspections.Deliverable FX forwards between HKD, USD, EUR, JPY and CNH to hedge committed advertising service revenue and publisher obligationsOffshore RMB (CNH) non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) for addressing exposure to onshore CNY conversion risk, especially where mainland customer receipts are pending conversionCross-currency swaps (CCS) to restructure HKD, USD, and CNH financing and swap fixed or floating interest rate exposures for long-term capitalVanilla FX options and zero-cost collars with approved HKMA-licensed or international-domiciled counterparties under a standard ISDA credit support annex

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-medium as a primary account, medium as an ancillary collection rail. The Hong Kong Banking Ordinance restricts deposit-taking to licensed authorised institutions; pure offshore EMI licences do not permit deposit-taking in Hong Kong and are not regulated by the HKMA, so they cannot serve as the bank-of-record for a foreign-invested partnership. HK-licensed virtual banks, such as Mox Bank, WeLab Bank and Livi Bank, are authorised institutions and can open basic HKD/USD accounts, but their trade finance, FX hedging, CHATS clearing, and credit capabilities are limited and they generally impose lower transaction thresholds than tier-1 incumbents. An offshore EMI with only an EU/UK e-money licence is vulnerable to inbound remittance rejection by HK compliance, particularly when the ultimate beneficial owner sits in mainland China. Consequently, the partnership should maintain its primary treasury and capital-escrow relationship at a tier-1 commercial bank, and may use a regulated money service operator or virtual bank only for small-ticket disbursements with full reporting to the principal bank.
