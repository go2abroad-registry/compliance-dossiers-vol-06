Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's foreign exchange regime remains asymmetric: current account transactions are widely convertible after genuine-business verification, while capital account transactions are still subject to registration, quota, and use restrictions. The Foreign-Invested Partnership will earn service export revenue from ASO, SEO and app-growth localization work; that revenue qualifies as trade-in-services and can be repatriated into a foreign-exchange current account and converted to RMB using the bank's verification of contract, invoice and balance-of-payments declaration. The main hurdle is separately registering any capital injection from Hong Kong as inbound direct investment under SAFE, opening a foreign exchange capital account, and converting the incoming foreign currency strictly under the capital settlement rules. Converted RMB must not be deployed into securities, entrusted loans, off-balance-sheet lending, real-estate speculation or any negative-list activity; each conversion requires a valid use document and the funds must be placed in a designated settlement-pending-payment account. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is a current account item but requires audited PRC financial statements, tax payment clearance, and the bank's verification of underlying service income. The absence of a minimum capital requirement in Chinese partnership law does not reduce the evidentiary burden: the partnership's actual capital contribution plan, the identity of general and limited partners, and the economic substance of the business remain decisive for all onboarding and FX decisions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio in the PRC current-account regime; the effective ratio is 0% for verified export and service trade receipts. Export/service proceeds must be received into the FIP's foreign exchange current account at a licensed bank, and the payee must complete the SAFE international receipts declaration within five business days of the bank's notification. The bank will match the proceeds against the commercial contract, invoice and service deliverables; if the FIP is classified as an A-list trade enterprise, conversion may proceed immediately, while B/C-list enterprises may face pre-settlement checks, delayed credit, documentary audits and, in severe non-compliance cases, enforced conversion after regulatory order.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the implementing authority for foreign exchange controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Payoneer Fit: High

Low as a primary onshore treasury rail. Wise_B2B and Payoneer are not licensed PRC commercial banks and cannot perform the SAFE foreign-exchange registration, international receipts declaration, tax recordal certificate validation, or capital account supervision required for a China-domiciled FIP. They are also barred from acting as the receiving bank for FDI capital injections or as the bank of record for corporate outbound service payments, because such payments must be routed through a SAFE-designated domestic bank and accompanied by statutory tax and FX documentation. These EMIs can only be used as an auxiliary vendor-disbursement channel for small, non-core overseas marketing payments if funds have already been settled through a licensed bank and converted into foreign currency with prior tax and purpose-code filing; even then, the bank's stated trade payment process should remain the risk-controlled default.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor should remit via SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's SAFE-designated foreign exchange capital account at the chosen PRC bank. The remittance must include the investor name, the FIP full name, the SAFE FDI registration code, the purpose-of-payment description reading INBOUND DIRECT INVESTMENT CAPITAL CONTRIBUTION, and the applicable SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code for FDI capital injection. The receiving bank will perform an inward remittance notification, complete the international receipts declaration, verify the amount against the FIP's foreign investment record and, upon successful conversion, place RMB into the capital settlement pending-use account. A PRC licensed CPA should then issue a capital verification report using the bank credit advice, SWIFT message, SAFE registration certificate, and partnership bank statement. No payment into private accounts is permitted, and commingling with operational current-account traffic must be avoided until the capital verification and SAFE filing are completed.For service and intangible payments, SWIFT MT103 with full beneficiary details and purpose code is the primary clearing rail; commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not suitable for digital marketing services unless the payment covers purchase of physical promotional goods, in which case a standard trade LC or documentary collection through the same bank's trade services department would apply. For cross-border RMB payments into Hong Kong, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) with an MT103-equivalent instruction and the correct RMB clearing code. For USD payments to app stores and ad networks, the bank will route via its US correspondent through CHIPS or Fedwire; for HKD payments, use the Hong Kong dollar RTGS/CHATS clearing through the bank's Hong Kong arm. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% for corporations; 7.5%/15% for the unincorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) under Hong Kong two-tiered profits tax (first HKD 2,000,000 assessable profits at 7.5%, remainder at 15%; a corporate general partner would use 8.25%/16.5%)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends or profit distributions paid by a Hong Kong partnership or company. A profit distribution by the FIP to its PRC or non-resident partners is therefore not subject to Hong Kong dividend withholding tax. If the partners receive Mainland-source dividends from a PRC company through the Hong Kong entity, PRC WHT of 10% applies, reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA where the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the PRC payer's capital.
