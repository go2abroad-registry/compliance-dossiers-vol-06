Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: GovHK e-Tax and IRD eTAX (https://www.gov.hk/en/onlineservices/taxes/, https://www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), a foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply to the Business Registration Office (BRO) of the Inland Revenue Department within one month of commencing business. For a partnership, the designated general partner submits an application (Form IRBR1 for non-corporate partnerships) via GovHK/IRD eTAX or in person/paper, accompanied by the partnership agreement, identity documents of all partners (passports for individuals or Certificate of Incorporation and registered office details for corporate partners), the principal place of business in Hong Kong, and a description of the nature of business. The BRO issues a Business Registration Certificate with a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory tax identifier; there is no separate VAT ID or TIN in Hong Kong. If the partnership is to have limited liability, it must first be registered as a limited partnership with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 321) before applying for business registration. The IRD will then issue Profits Tax Returns (e.g. BIR52 for partnerships) and assess each partner on their share of Hong Kong-sourced profits through partner-level returns (BIR60 for individuals; BIR51 for corporate partners).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax. Hong Kong does not operate a general indirect tax system; only specific duties apply to certain dutiable commodities such as alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and motor vehicles.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for indirect tax purposes because there is no VAT/GST/sales tax. As a general business obligation, business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is required from day one regardless of turnover, so there is zero economic turnover threshold for registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT export zero-rating rule because Hong Kong imposes no VAT. For profits tax purposes, profits from goods or services supplied to overseas customers may be treated as derived outside Hong Kong only if the substance of the relevant activities was performed outside Hong Kong; the taxpayer must claim offshore nature through the Profits Tax Return and provide evidence sufficient to satisfy the IRD that the profits have a non-Hong Kong source.

  • For physical exports: Import/Export Declaration lodged with the Trade and Industry Department through the Government Electronic Trading Services (GETS) or the relevant electronic system, plus Bill of Lading or Air Waybill and commercial invoices/packing lists where applicable.
  • For offshore services: executed contracts with overseas clients, fee invoices, deliverables, correspondence, meeting minutes, and staff time/travel records demonstrating that all substantive performance activities were carried out outside Hong Kong.
  • Bank inward remittance advice/SWIFT MT103 or bank credit voucher evidencing receipt of the overseas consideration into a Hong Kong or foreign bank account.
  • A written offshore profits claim memorandum prepared for the Profits Tax Return describing the transaction flow, parties, location of negotiation and execution, and place of performance.

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time fiscal e-invoicing, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping for invoices. Businesses may issue ordinary invoices or receipts; the IRD requires adequate records to be kept in English or Chinese for at least seven years so that assessable profits can be determined.

There is no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline in Hong Kong. If the IRD determines that claimed offshore profits were in fact sourced in Hong Kong, or that the documentation is insufficient or false, it may raise additional profits tax assessments under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), with interest, and impose penalties under sections 80 and 82A. Willful tax evasion may result in a fine of up to HK$50,000, three times the tax undercharged, and imprisonment on indictment; summary convictions may attract a fine of up to HK$10,000 with additional tax penalties and imprisonment. The IRD may also reopen prior years and claw back any tax benefit previously allowed if the offshore exemption is unsupported.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Foreign-Invested Partnership established in mainland China operates under the strict capital and current account foreign exchange controls administered by SAFE. Renminbi is freely convertible on current account transactions, including service-trade receipts and payments, provided the underlying transaction is genuine, fully documented, and declared through a designated domestic bank under the trade-in-services payment and receipt verification system. Capital account items, such as foreign partners injecting capital into the FIP, remain subject to prior SAFE registration, a dedicated foreign currency capital account, and post-injection capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant. Profit repatriation by non-resident partners is treated as a current account remittance once the annual audited financial statements, tax settlement certificates, withholding tax payment evidence, and the partnership's foreign exchange registration are submitted to the remitting bank; however, SAFE may investigate red flags such as round-trip fund flow, nominee ownership, or inconsistent invoicing. Hong Kong, by contrast, maintains full convertibility under the Basic Law and has no exchange controls, but the mainland FIP cannot rely on Hong Kong convertibility to circumvent SAFE domestic regulation. Practical treasury management therefore requires the FIP to keep onshore capital and operating accounts at mainland Tier-1 banks, maintain separate offshore collection accounts in Hong Kong only for overseas retainer receipts, and ensure every cross-border movement matches an economic contract and a SAFE purpose code. The overall assessment is a partially convertible system: liberal current account, controlled capital account, and strict documentary verification despite the low AML risk tier of a public relations agency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service trade export proceeds derived from overseas public relations and communication services, mainland China currently does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio into Renminbi. The FIP must repatriate the foreign currency receipts into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account or a designated bank within a commercially reasonable period, generally expected to be within 180 days from the date of service completion or the agreed invoice settlement date. The FIP may retain the foreign currency in the settlement account subject to its bank-approved balance quota, or may voluntarily sell the proceeds to the bank at the prevailing market rate. The bank must verify the service contract, invoice, delivery evidence, and the corresponding SAFE service trade transaction code before crediting the received funds. If the FIP maintains a Hong Kong treasury account, all export proceeds attributable to mainland operations must be repatriated into the mainland onshore account and cannot be permanently parked offshore for domestic cost settlement without declaring an underlying current account need.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) governs the Hong Kong clearing and treasury leg

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms, including global neobanks and virtual account providers, are not legally viable as the primary clearing or settlement infrastructure for a mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership. Mainland China banking regulations require all domestic legal entities to maintain their principal RMB and foreign exchange accounts at a licensed commercial bank or a licensed payment institution authorized by the PBoC and SAFE for cross-border transactions. Offshore EMIs do not possess Chinese payment institution licenses and cannot connect directly to CNAPS, CIPS, or identify the FIP with a valid mainland tax registration and SAFE entity code. At most, a fintech EMI can be used as an auxiliary receivable collection tool in Hong Kong for foreign client retainers, provided the FIP remains the beneficial owner, all funds are reflected in audited accounting records, and the amounts are subsequently repatriated into the mainland onshore bank account. Reliance on such EMIs for payroll, trade settlements, or outbound vendor payment processing would expose the FIP to SAFE penalties, bank account freezes, and reputational damage. Therefore the underwriting viability rating is Low for operational clearing and Medium only for licensed Hong Kong-based virtual banking arrangements if operated under an HKMA banking license and with transparent downstream wires to mainland accounts.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from foreign partners must be routed through SWIFT MT103 from the partner's licensed bank directly into the FIP's dedicated SAFE-monitored capital escrow account at a mainland Tier-1 bank. The remittance must carry the approved foreign exchange purpose code for partnership capital contribution and must reference the FIP's capital account registration number. The paying bank will complete the SAFE foreign exchange registration, log the transaction into the Banking Security System, and release the funds only after receiving the FIP's capital call resolution and partnership agreement. Within a statutory period generally measured in accounting days after the credit, a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming that the funds were received from the correct foreign partner in the agreed currency and amount and that no proceeds have been used for any prohibited purpose. All subsequent capital-based payments, including repatriation to foreign partners upon exit or liquidation, require a matching SAFE deregistration or amendment filing.The FIP's outbound settlements for overseas public relations services and media retainers should be executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 from the onshore foreign currency settlement account or cross-border RMB settlement account, with the underlying service contract, invoice, and proof of delivery archived at the bank. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms, including global neobanks and virtual account providers, are not legally viable as the primary clearing or settlement infrastructure for a mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership. Mainland China banking regulations require all domestic legal entities to maintain their principal RMB and foreign exchange accounts at a licensed commercial bank or a licensed payment institution authorized by the PBoC and SAFE for cross-border transactions. Offshore EMIs do not possess Chinese payment institution licenses and cannot connect directly to CNAPS, CIPS, or identify the FIP with a valid mainland tax registration and SAFE entity code. At most, a fintech EMI can be used as an auxiliary receivable collection tool in Hong Kong for foreign client retainers, provided the FIP remains the beneficial owner, all funds are reflected in audited accounting records, and the amounts are subsequently repatriated into the mainland onshore bank account. Reliance on such EMIs for payroll, trade settlements, or outbound vendor payment processing would expose the FIP to SAFE penalties, bank account freezes, and reputational damage. Therefore the underwriting viability rating is Low for operational clearing and Medium only for licensed Hong Kong-based virtual banking arrangements if operated under an HKMA banking license and with transparent downstream wires to mainland accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master/Local File obligatory where the MNE group’s annual consolidated revenue is at least HK$6.8 billion; Local File additionally required where the Hong Kong entity’s cross-border related-party transactions exceed: HK$200 million (property), HK$160 million (financing), HK$220 million (intangibles), HK$400 million (other transactions) as prescribed in DIPN 59.

Economic substance scope: No codified Economic Substance Regulations exist in Hong Kong. However, the territorial-source principle and Section 61A anti-avoidance look-through require the FIP to demonstrate genuine commercial activity and substantive decision-making in Hong Kong. A passive booking shell, without employees, premises, or operating functions, may be disregarded and profits recharacterized as Hong Kong-sourced.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Returns

Statutory sanction: On indictment, a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years under Section 82 IRO; additional tax up to three times the amount undercharged may be ordered. On summary conviction, fines and additional tax also apply.

Director personal liability: General partners and controlling officers may be jointly and severally assessed for evaded tax; courts may issue travel restrictions, allow asset freezing orders, and commence bankruptcy proceedings.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement lacking Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the arrangement under Section 61A, re-characterize profits as chargeable in Hong Kong, impose the applicable profits tax rate, and levy penalties up to three times the undercharged tax. DTA benefits may be denied for lack of beneficial ownership.

Director personal liability: Beneficial owners and general partners who knowingly participated in the sham may be held personally liable; asset forfeiture, bankruptcy petitions, and criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the revenue may follow.
AML / Anti-Money-Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, conviction for money laundering carries a fine of up to HK$5 million and imprisonment for up to 14 years; confiscation orders may be made against the proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Directors or general partners who knowingly consent or are wilfully blind may be criminally liable; travel bans, freezing orders, and personal confiscation orders can be imposed.
