Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mailbox address is not permissible as the sole registered office for a China FIP in this industry. The PRC Partnership Enterprise Law requires a business premises, and PBOC/SAMR Order No. 3 together with tax anti-fraud rules require physical presence, original record access, payroll substance, invoice issuance capability and data-processing capacity. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

PRC: State Taxation Administration (STA); online taxpayer portal is the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau, accessed through www.chinatax.gov.cn and the provincial e-tax nodes. Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD); online portals are GovHK e-Tax and Business Registration Online via www.ird.gov.hk.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP): (1) complete establishment registration with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Partnership Business License bearing the 18-character Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) complete tax registration with the STA through the data-sharing mechanism or separately in the electronic tax bureau within 30 days, with the USCC serving as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for PRC tax purposes; (3) determine VAT registration status with the local tax authority, including general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer classification; (4) for each non-resident foreign partner, obtain and submit the partner's home-jurisdiction Tax Identification Number, together with the partnership agreement and foreign capital information report, to support pass-through allocation and PRC withholding compliance; (5) if a Hong Kong hub is used, the non-resident investor or HK operating entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement, and the IRD will issue a Business Registration Number used as the Hong Kong taxpayer reference; (6) where offshore profits status is intended, document substantive business decisions and service recipients outside Hong Kong and, if necessary, appoint an authorized tax representative for IRD correspondence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC: any entity engaged in taxable business must complete tax and VAT registration; mandatory general VAT taxpayer status applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, with small-scale taxpayer status available below that threshold. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT regime and therefore no economic turnover threshold; only Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance applies to any person carrying on business in Hong Kong.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods: 0% VAT export rate applies where physical goods leave the PRC customs territory and the export sale is substantiated by customs declaration and foreign-currency receipt repatriation. For services: qualifying cross-border services, including market research, consumer polling, and SaaS-type research platforms supplied to recipients outside mainland China, may qualify for zero-rating or exemption under the PRC cross-border taxable services rules, provided the service is genuinely rendered for and consumed outside mainland China and the consideration is received from outside the PRC. Hong Kong SAR: because no VAT is levied, zero-rating is structurally not applicable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical goods exported from the PRC
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-currency receipt/verification evidencing export proceeds repatriation
  • Cross-border service agreement and commercial invoice for the market research/SaaS service outlet
  • Electronic tax bureau filing record of the cross-border service zero-rating/exemption claim or VAT refund application

PRC: digitalized e-invoices are issued through the national electronic invoice service platform under the fully digitalized e-invoice reform; special VAT invoices and ordinary invoices are uploaded in real time to the Golden Tax system, with a tax-control unique code and electronic seal authenticated by the tax authority. Hong Kong SAR: no electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists.

The tax authority may deny zero-rating, require repayment of VAT at the applicable PRC rate, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% until paid, and levy administrative penalties from 50% to five times the tax under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent export VAT refund or zero-rating claims may trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, retrospective clawback of any refund, revocation of taxpayer status, and market-access blacklisting. For Hong Kong, an unsupported offshore claim may be recharacterized as Hong Kong-source profits and subjected to profits tax plus penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating RMB exchange rate regime and a strictly supervised two-tier convertibility framework. Current-account receipts and payments are in principle freely convertible once the underlying transaction is real, verified, and consistent with SAFE reporting; for the consumer panel survey and cross-border market research platform, SaaS subscription fees, panelist honoraria, contractor payments, advertising costs, and license royalties are treated as service-trade items and may be settled through designated banks on a genuine transaction basis. Capital-account items remain tightly controlled: inbound FDI capital contributions must first complete SAFE foreign investment registration, be received into a foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank, and be settled into RMB only for permitted operating expenditures consistent with the registered business scope; there is no automatic fungibility between capital-account funds and current-account working capital. Profit remittance by the Foreign-Invested Partnership to the Hong Kong partner is allowed only after the annual statutory audit, taxation of taxable partnership income, withholding tax settlement on treaty-eligible passive income, and submission to the remitting bank of the audited financial statements, partnership distribution resolution, tax payment proof, and SAFE declarations. Surrender requirements have been reformed: exporters and service suppliers may retain eligible foreign currency receipts in FX settlement accounts, but all such proceeds must be repatriated to onshore banking channels and cannot be parked offshore indefinitely. CNH in Hong Kong is freely convertible, but CNY/CNH arbitrage is ring-fenced by the two-pool system; the onshore FIP must keep its operational cash in CNY and use its HK treasury vehicle for CNH/USD/HKD optimization without crossing into speculative capital-account activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Trade Monitoring System, goods-export proceeds must be repatriated through designated banks within the statutory monitoring window, generally within 90 days from the customs export declaration date, or within 30 days from the date of an advance receipt, unless an extension is approved by SAFE. Service-trade export proceeds such as market research fees and survey platform subscriptions must be received under the commercial payment terms and declared in the SAFE service-trade reporting system, with a practical repatriation expectation of no later than 30 days after invoice maturity. There is no binding mandatory conversion ratio in the current reform framework; the FIP may retain eligible export and service receipts in foreign currency in its FX settlement account or voluntarily convert them to RMB. Retained foreign exchange must be used for genuine current-account purposes or approved capital-account uses, and may not be deployed offshore for securities, real estate, or speculative treasury products.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the offshore clearing and regulatory authority for CNH and HKD flows

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as a primary onshore clearing rail for the PRC-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership. PayPal Mass Payout and Wise are licensed electronic money institutions in Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK, and other common law markets, and they can lawfully service the Hong Kong parent or offshore treasury vehicle. Neither holds a PRC commercial banking licence nor a SAFE cross-border RMB settlement licence, and neither may receive FDI capital, settle service-trade receipts, or disburse RMB payroll and panelist payments for an onshore Chinese entity. Using them as an onshore clearing rail would violate PBOC third-party payment rules, SAFE foreign exchange controls, and commercial bank AML expectations. If mass micro-payouts to consumer survey respondents are required within China, they must be routed through a PBOC-licensed third-party payment institution or the designated commercial bank's bulk-payment module. Wise and PayPal may only be used by the Hong Kong vehicle for non-mainland panelists, incidental low-value HKD/USD payouts, or offshore treasury flows that are clearly segregated from the mainland entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong partner into the PRC FIP must follow the SAFE-instituted FDI lifecycle: first, complete the SAFE foreign investment establishment registration using the FIP's business licence and partnership agreement; second, open a foreign exchange capital account at a designated commercial bank; third, receive the cross-border remittance via SWIFT MT103/MT202 from the partner's Hong Kong bank account, using the direct investment capital contribution purpose code in the SAFE/BOP reporting system; fourth, allow the receiving bank to validate the remitter name, transaction code, and underlying contract; fifth, engage a PRC CICPA to issue a capital verification report confirming the partnership capital contribution where required by bank policy; sixth, settle the foreign currency into RMB through the capital project settlement account only for permitted operating uses, such as payroll, rent, panelist compensation, software procurement, and market development expenses. The FIP must retain the SAFE registration certificate, the original remittance instructions, bank statements, capital verification report, and expenditure invoices for at least five years for regulator inspections.Outbound disbursements for trade and operational settlements should be executed over bank-grade clearing rails: use SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border payments of vendor fees, contracting payments, and repatriation of after-tax partnership profits to Hong Kong, with the correct SAFE payment purpose code attached; use commercial letters of credit (L/C) for high-value imports of survey hardware, software infrastructure, or licensed data assets; use documentary collections (D/P or D/A under URC 522) for contract-based trade where documentary risk control is required; and use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or bank RMB clearing for all onshore-offshore RMB payments to avoid undue SWIFT conversion friction. For panelist and mass payouts, use the designated commercial bank's bulk disbursement module or a PBOC-licensed third-party payment institution, with tax withholding applied where required and SAFE reporting embedded in each payment batch.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD foreign-exchange forward contracts with genuine current-account or approved capital-account exposure, written with a designated onshore bank under SAFE real-demand principles and no speculative net open positionOnshore RMB/USD foreign-exchange swaps for liquidity conversion between RMB and USD, used only for matched operational funding flows and not for arbitrageCross-currency interest rate swaps (CIRS) for long-term USD-denominated capital funding, capped by the notional amount and maturity of the SAFE-registered external debt or foreign investment capital planOffshore CNH/CNY non-deliverable forwards (NDF) are not permitted for the onshore PRC FIP; such instruments may be used only by a separate Hong Kong-incorporated treasury vehicle through an HKMA-authorised bank, with strict legal ring-fencing from the mainland entity's bank accounts, liabilities, and SAFE registrations

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Not viable as a primary onshore clearing rail for the PRC-domiciled Foreign-Invested Partnership. PayPal Mass Payout and Wise are licensed electronic money institutions in Hong Kong, Singapore, the UK, and other common law markets, and they can lawfully service the Hong Kong parent or offshore treasury vehicle. Neither holds a PRC commercial banking licence nor a SAFE cross-border RMB settlement licence, and neither may receive FDI capital, settle service-trade receipts, or disburse RMB payroll and panelist payments for an onshore Chinese entity. Using them as an onshore clearing rail would violate PBOC third-party payment rules, SAFE foreign exchange controls, and commercial bank AML expectations. If mass micro-payouts to consumer survey respondents are required within China, they must be routed through a PBOC-licensed third-party payment institution or the designated commercial bank's bulk-payment module. Wise and PayPal may only be used by the Hong Kong vehicle for non-mainland panelists, incidental low-value HKD/USD payouts, or offshore treasury flows that are clearly segregated from the mainland entity.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% Hong Kong Profits Tax on HK assessable profits of a corporation; 25% PRC Corporate Income Tax on the profit share allocated to a corporate partner of a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP); 15% unincorporated rate applicable to sole proprietors/partnerships in HK.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Hong Kong imposes no dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders; however, if the FIP is treated as a PRC Foreign-Invested Partnership and distributes earnings to a non-resident corporate partner, the PRC statutory withholding rate on China-sourced dividends is 10%, reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement where the beneficial owner is a company directly holding at least 25% of the capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$50,000,000 aggregate controlled-transaction value for the Hong Kong Local File under IRD DIPN 58; Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated turnover reaches HK$750,000,000 or where a PRC related-party filing is triggered; cross-border market-research and SaaS transactions with mainland Chinese group companies should be benchmarked regardless of threshold because the PRC related-party transaction filing threshold can be as low as RMB 40,000,000 for certain transactions.

Economic substance scope: The Hong Kong Foreign-Source Income Exemption (FSIE) regime, effective January 2023, taxes offshore passive income (dividends, interest, IP royalties, and disposal gains) arising in Hong Kong unless the taxpayer carries out the core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong; although HK does not apply the UAE Economic Substance Regulations, the CIGA test requires demonstrable local staff, management decisions, board meetings, and risk-taking for the FIP; IP income is further restricted to the nexus-ratio benefit so the FIP cannot book data-panel royalties in HK without local developers, contract negotiators, and strategic oversight.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Failure to Report China-Sourced Income

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63, the underpaid tax is collectible with daily interest of 0.05% and a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax; in Hong Kong, IRO s.82A adds additional tax of up to three times the tax evaded for false returns; directors who cause the false report are jointly liable for civil restitution.

Director personal liability: Civil asset seizure, bank account freezing, and director blacklisting (travel ban) in both Mainland China and Hong Kong; criminal prosecution may result in imprisonment of up to 7 years under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 and/or up to 3 years under the Hong Kong Crimes Ordinance for fraudulent tax documentation.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Sham Intercompany Fees

Statutory sanction: The IRD may adjust the consideration to arm's length under IRO s.61/61A and supplementary TP rules, with penal additional tax of up to 300% of tax undercharged; the Chinese tax authorities may make a special tax adjustment with interest and a 50%-500% penalty under Articles 41 and 63 of the Tax Administration Law; Country-by-Country and DIPN 58 non-filing exposure also increases audit risk.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers may be held personally liable for restitution if they arranged the sham fees for tax avoidance; asset freezing, denial of exit permits, and director disqualification orders can be issued; criminal charges for false accounting can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years in Hong Kong and up to 7 years in China for tax evasion.
Shell Company / No Economic Substance / Loss of Treaty Benefits

Statutory sanction: Under the Hong Kong FSIE regime, the passive income is treated as Hong Kong-source and taxed at 16.5% (or 8.25% on the first HK$2m for qualifying companies); the Mainland-HK DTA beneficial-ownership clause will deny the 5%/7% reduced rates, reverting to the 10% PRC statutory WHT; PRC tax authorities may recharacterize the HK entity as a conduit and assess a 10% WHT on the full gross amount, plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal liability for making false substance declarations, blacklisting by the Inland Revenue Department and tax authorities, civil asset restitution for unpaid taxes, and criminal penalties for conspiracy to defraud the revenue, with imprisonment terms normally ranging from 3 to 10 years in serious cross-border cases.
