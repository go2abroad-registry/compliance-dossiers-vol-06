Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident establishing the CN_FIP in Hong Kong must first comply with the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The partnership, through its partners or local representative, must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business, using the eTAX portal or a prescribed paper application. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate and a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the partnership's taxpayer identification number. If the partnership is set up as a limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37), the partnership deed must also be registered with the Companies Registry before business registration. Each partner, if not already holding a Hong Kong TIN or BRN, must provide a certified copy of passport or identification and any overseas tax identification number. No VAT or GST registration is required because Hong Kong has no general indirect tax. The BRN must be displayed at the principal place of business, and any change in partnership particulars must be notified to the IRD within the statutory notice period.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box or equivalent pass-through election. A partnership is mandatorily transparent under the Inland Revenue Ordinance; profits tax is chargeable on each partner's share of profits at the applicable rate. The only available planning is the contractual profit-sharing ratio, subject to Part IVIA anti-avoidance and the general anti-avoidance rules if the arrangement is artificial.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales tax in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is confined to selective duties on dutiable commodities, motor vehicle first registration tax, and betting duty.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no VAT/GST is imposed. Business registration under Cap. 310 is required for any person carrying on business in Hong Kong irrespective of turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong does not impose VAT or GST, no supply can be zero-rated for indirect tax purposes and no input VAT refund mechanism exists. Design subscription services and goods exports are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax; the only relevant question is whether profits from the exported design services are sourced outside Hong Kong for profits tax purposes.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a linked exchange rate system and maintains full current and capital account convertibility. The HKD is freely convertible within the 7.75 to 7.85 band against the USD; there are no surrender requirements, no restrictions on repatriation, and no prior approval regime for partnership profit distribution or capital injection. Because the FIP is formed in Hong Kong, it can hold USD, HKD, EUR and offshore RMB (CNH) in multi-currency accounts and settle cross-border receipts without central bank permission. The only structural friction arises when funding is sourced from mainland China: a mainland corporate or individual partner injecting capital into the Hong Kong FIP must complete outward direct investment filing and SAFE registration where applicable, and the onshore RMB cannot be freely wired to the FIP without the underlying approval. Once the funds are in Hong Kong, they enjoy unrestricted convertibility and can be hedged through HKMA-regulated banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil under Hong Kong law. There is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export or service foreign-currency receipts in Hong Kong; receipts may be retained in the original currency indefinitely. Mainland Chinese affiliates, if any, remain subject to SAFE rules requiring current-account export proceeds to be repatriated into China, generally within 90 days from customs declaration unless an extension is approved, and effectively requiring settlement through the onshore banking system. This surrender rule does not apply to the Hong Kong FIP.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), the de facto central bank and monetary authority for Hong Kong; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) regulate mainland China cross-border RMB flows when they touch the PRC side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Stripe Billing and Wise are not prohibited in Hong Kong and can support low-risk multimedia design subscription collections, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions. They are legally barred from holding statutory capital, issuing capital verification certificates, providing commercial credit, or acting as the FIP's principal banker under the Banking Ordinance. Their viability is high only for treasury-lite merchant collection and payment-disbursement rails, provided funds are swept daily to a tier-1 bank account; they cannot replace the Hong Kong bank for compliance, FX hedging, or capital-contribution control.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All partner capital must be injected through SWIFT MT103 into a segregated Capital Escrow account at a tier-1 Hong Kong bank, referencing the FIP registration number and the purpose 'Capital Contribution to Hong Kong Partnership.' The remitter must pass KYC, provide source-of-funds statements, and, if the remitter is a mainland entity, include the SAFE and ODI registration reference. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% for unincorporated partnership profits; 16.5% for profits allocable to a corporate partner; effective rate after two-tiered concession: 7.5% on first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits for unincorporated businesses and 8.25% for corporate profits

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong imposes no domestic withholding tax on dividends, irrespective of whether the recipient is a Chinese tax resident or a Hong Kong company; the FIP's profit distribution to foreign partners is not subject to any Hong Kong dividend WHT, but the distributing profits must first be sourced from assessable (or exempt under FSIE) earnings.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong follows the OECD TP package under IRO s.50AAK–50AAZ. The Local File / Master File documentation thresholds, in HKD, are: single-party covered property transactions exceeding HKD 10 million per year; financing transactions exceeding HKD 5 million per year; intra-group service transactions exceeding HKD 1 million per year; and a general threshold of HKD 5 million in total business income with any covered transaction. A CbC Report is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of HKD 5.8 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong is not subject to the EU/OECD Economic Substance Regulations as a zero-tax locale; however, the HK Foreign-Source Income Exemption (FSIE) regime – effective 1 January 2023 – requires a Hong Kong FIP receiving foreign-sourced passive income (e.g. subscription royalties from a Chinese parent) to satisfy substantial economic substance requirements. For a UI/UX design subscription agency, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must be exercised in Hong Kong: strategic design planning, negotiation and execution of design service contracts, financial management of subscription revenue, and the key hiring/performance review of the design team. A mere letterbox office, outsourced design development in Shenzhen, and bookkeeping only in Hong Kong will break the substance test, causing the foreign-sourced income to be deemed taxable in Hong Kong, with penalties of 10% of the reassessed tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent or False Declaration of Foreign-Sourced Fee Income

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s.82(1) – knowingly making a false statement or entry in a tax return. Sanctions: immediate criminal prosecution; fine of HKD 50,000; additional tax up to 300% of the tax undercharged; asset seizure order up to the undercharged tax plus penalty; any partner who signs the return is jointly and severally liable for the additional tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate partner's directors are personally liable if they knowingly participated in the false declaration. Criminal consequences include imprisonment up to 3 years; the Hong Kong District Court may order disqualification as company director for up to 15 years under the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance. Travel bans (blacklisting at Hong Kong immigration) are imposed for outstanding tax assessment of HKD 150,000 or more, preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong until tax and penalties are settled; the Court may also issue a worldwide asset freezing order.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation – Artificial Profit Shifting Through Intercompany Royalties

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAK – the Commissioner may re-characterise or adjust the royalty payment to an arm's-length amount; further penalty under s.50AAR for failure to maintain documentation: HKD 500,000 plus additional tax of 100% of the undercharged tax; if the arrangement is deemed artificial, s.61 or s.61A imposes a 3x additional tax; the adjustment may be treated as a deemed distribution or unpaid interest, triggering falsified DTA relief claims.

Director personal liability: Under the piercing-the-corporate-veil doctrine, directors who authorise payments knowing they are not arm's length are personally liable for the uncovered tax, penalties, and interest. The IRD may apply to the High Court for an order requiring the director to compensate the FIP for the lost tax; criminal charges under s.82 can result in imprisonment up to 3 years. Directors may be prohibited from leaving Hong Kong and listed on the Hong Kong Trade-and-Industry blacklist, preventing them from representing any new company with IRD or Companies Registry.
AML and Shell Company – Use of Foreign-Invested Partnership as a Pass-Through Conduit for Money Laundering of Digital Subscriptions

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) – a financial institution or designated non-financial business must conduct customer due diligence; the HK FIP is not a licensed financial institution but the use of payment rails such as Wise and Stripe subjects it to the PSP's compliance requirements. Failure to cooperate triggers a civil penalty up to HKD 10 million and, for the benefiting partner, prosecution under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) for dealing with property known or believed to be proceeds of crime – imprisonment up to 14 years and a fine of HKD 5 million. Inland Revenue Ordinance s.80 and s.82 are also applied in parallel, since the false invoices create fictitious deductible expenses.

Director personal liability: The Court will pierce the limited liability veil on the grounds that the FIP is a mere facade. Directors and general partners are joint and severally liable for all corporate debts and tax underpayments. The Chinese parent's director, even if resident in Mainland China, becomes subject to Hong Kong arrest warrants and a red notice via Interpol if an arrangement triggers the Mutual Legal Assistance in Criminal Matters Ordinance. The Hong Kong Immigration Department will blacklist the director, preventing re-entry into Hong Kong and Macau; civil confiscation orders can reach assets in Mainland China through the China-HK mutual enforcement arrangements, including the transfer of forfeited funds within 90 days.
