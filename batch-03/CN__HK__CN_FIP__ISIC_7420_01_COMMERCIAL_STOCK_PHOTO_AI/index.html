Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China for mainland China tax matters; Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong SAR for Hong Kong profits tax matters. Online electronic portals: STA Electronic Tax Office (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and Hong Kong IRD eTax Portal (https://www.gov.hk/etax).

E-government filing portal

1) Register the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, obtaining the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). 2) The SAMR registration record is automatically synchronized to the STA, creating the taxpayer registration file; no separate paper tax registration is required for the entity itself. 3) For a mainland China establishment, the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for enterprise income tax and VAT purposes; the FIP must separately complete VAT taxpayer classification as a small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer through the STA Electronic Tax Office. 4) A foreign non-resident investor or parent without a permanent establishment in China must obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration number by filing the applicable registration particulars with the competent STA office or through its Chinese withholding agent. 5) For Hong Kong tax purposes, each partner carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate with the IRD and file the applicable profits tax returns; the FIP itself is not a separate Hong Kong profits tax entity because partnership profits are allocated to partners.

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — 1) Register the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, obtaining the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). 2) The SAMR registration record is automatically synchronized to the STA, creating the taxpayer registration file; no separate paper tax registration is required for the entity itself. 3) For a mainland China establishment, the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for enterprise income tax and VAT purposes; the FIP must separately complete VAT taxpayer classification as a small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer through the STA Electronic Tax Office. 4) A foreign non-resident investor or parent without a permanent establishment in China must obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration number by filing the applicable registration particulars with the competent STA office or through its Chinese withholding agent. 5) For Hong Kong tax purposes, each partner carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate with the IRD and file the applicable profits tax returns; the FIP itself is not a separate Hong Kong profits tax entity because partnership profits are allocated to partners.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax regime automatically treats partnerships as pass-through entities and does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election. Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Ordinance likewise has no check-the-box election; the assessable profits of a partnership are allocated to and taxed in the hands of the partners. No additional hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In mainland China, all entities engaged in taxable sales must complete VAT registration; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, while below that threshold the FIP may remain a small-scale taxpayer under a simplified levy regime. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the China VAT framework, including Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and the STA administrative measures for cross-border taxable services, zero-rating or VAT exemption applies to qualifying cross-border supplies where the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China, the recipient is an overseas non-resident, and the FIP retains contemporaneous documentary evidence of the contract, actual offshore provision, and payment. Digital stock-photo licensing and related AI-generated visual asset services may qualify when the buyer is offshore and the rights are exercised outside mainland China. For physical export goods, zero-rating is available when the customs export declaration is filed, the goods depart the PRC customs territory, and export foreign exchange is repatriated.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (customs export declaration with assigned customs declaration number)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or equivalent transport document evidencing departure from China customs jurisdiction
  • Export sales contract or purchase order identifying the overseas buyer and delivery terms
  • Foreign exchange receipt confirmation from a licensed bank, inward remittance certificate, or SAFE cross-border receipt record evidencing repatriation

Mainland China requires electronic VAT invoice issuance through the national VAT invoice management system and the electronic invoice public service platform; invoice data is transmitted in near real time to the STA Golden Tax System for cryptographic validation, anti-counterfeit verification, and fiscal clearance. General taxpayers must use the tax control or fully digitalized electronic invoice mechanism, and the FIP must reconcile invoice data with tax returns via the STA Electronic Tax Office. Fully digital electronic invoices do not require paper stamping but carry a unique tax-controlled verification code. Hong Kong does not mandate e-invoicing or VAT clearing because it levies no general VAT.

If the zero-rating or export refund claim fails audit verification, the STA will disallow the 0% rate, claw back the input VAT credit or refund already granted, assess the unpaid VAT, and impose late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Administrative penalties may range from 50% to five times the tax evaded. Fraudulent export refunds using false customs declarations, fabricated invoices, or unverified foreign-exchange receipts attract criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law provisions on export tax fraud, including heavy fines and imprisonment for responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a hybrid convertibility regime: the current account for goods, services, dividends, and non-trade remittances is substantially convertible, while the capital account remains heavily regulated. A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with an HK parent can inject capital after completing SAFE foreign-exchange registration; the capital contribution must enter through a specially supervised capital account and can only be converted into RMB on a payment-by-use basis, requiring underlying contracts, invoices, and tax records. Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China within the statutory trade-receipt monitoring window but may be retained in a foreign-currency account or converted voluntarily; there is no mandatory surrender ratio. Profit remittances are feasible only after the annual audit, the posting of profits, payment of enterprise income tax and, where applicable, withholding tax, submission of the audited balance sheet and profit distribution resolution to the remitting bank, and completion of SAFE reporting. Persistent hurdles arise from the need to prove each conversion purpose through real transaction documents, the strict disallowance of pre-mature capital-account settlement, and the bank's independent compliance review before any outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade in foreign exchange regulations, export-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into a domestic enterprise foreign-currency account within 180 days of the export declaration or within the regional reporting period, and must be matched with customs and trade monitoring data. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may keep the proceeds in foreign currency or sell them to a bank voluntarily at the market rate. Strict reconciliation applies to each invoice and customs declaration.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Stripe and PayPal cannot act as the FIP's primary cross-border treasury clearing rail because they are not licensed under PBOC as payment institutions and have no direct settlement relationship with SAFE. They may only offer front-end pay-in collection for e-commerce sales; the collected funds must be routed to a licensed domestic bank account of the FIP. Their inability to open a capital account or issue RMB payments in compliance with SAFE capital account rules makes them legally unsuitable for inbound capital injection, export proceeds settlement, or profit remittance. The FIP should treat EMIs as accessory invoicing portals, not as licensed clearing banks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Opaque HK-parent ownership chain: 100% of the FIP capital flows from a Hong Kong entity whose ultimate beneficial owner is not visible from the partnership registration certificate.

Root cause: Opaque HK-parent ownership chain: 100% of the FIP capital flows from a Hong Kong entity whose ultimate beneficial owner is not visible from the partnership registration certificate.

Defense evidence: Notarized and apostilled Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and updated Gazette pages; a group ownership chart tracing each UBO through the HK holding company; beneficial ownership declarations signed by every natural person; audited financial statements for the last two years; proof of economic substance in Hong Kong such as lease, payroll and director engagement records; and a written explanation of the commercial rationale for the HK intermediate holding structure.

Root cause: Source of funds mismatches for the initial capital injection, especially when the HK parent's bank statements show third-party funding shortly before the transfer.

Geographic exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions: overseas stock photo contributors or payees are located in OFAC, UN, EU or PRC-sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions, triggering a bank's sanctions filter.

Root cause: Geographic exposure to sanctioned jurisdictions: overseas stock photo contributors or payees are located in OFAC, UN, EU or PRC-sanctioned or high-risk jurisdictions, triggering a bank's sanctions filter.

Defense evidence: An automated sanctions-screening log demonstrating no match against OFAC, UN, EU, and PRC sanctions lists; completed counterparty risk assessments for each high-risk contributor; negative news checks and politically exposed person screening; an internal compliance policy prohibiting transactions with sanctioned persons; and a written board-level risk acceptance memorandum approved by the FIP's compliance committee.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent should instruct its bank to send the capital contribution by SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's SAFE-registered capital account at a designated PRC Tier-1 bank, using remittance purpose code for shareholder capital contribution and carrying the full FIP legal name, SAFE foreign exchange registration number, and capital contribution schedule. The receipt must be registered in the bank's capital account information system; the FIP then submits underlying contracts, invoices and tax declarations each time it needs to convert foreign currency into RMB under the payment-by-use rule. A PRC certified public accountant performs capital verification and issues a capital contribution verification report before the capital is used.For cross-border trade disbursements, the FIP should use SWIFT MT103 for recurring low-to-mid value royalty or contributor payments, Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value acquisition of exclusive photographic collections, and Documentary Collections for well-established distributors where the bank holds a repayment master. Every disbursement must be matched to a signed licensing agreement, commercial invoice, proof of delivery or tokenization record, and the relevant SAFE service-trade purpose code, followed by tax withholding clearance if a non-resident beneficiary receives royalty income.Onshore RMB forward contracts are permissible for hedging genuine current-account trade and service flows with physical delivery in RMB.Non-deliverable forwards are permissible only on the offshore HK side for the parent treasury; the onshore FIP is prohibited from settling offshore NDFs against its PRC capital account.Cross-currency swaps are permissible for hedging registered capital or shareholder loan cash flows, subject to SAFE registration and real exposure verification.Plain-vanilla onshore foreign currency options are permissible for documented current-account receivable or payable exposure under a bank-approved derivative master agreement.

Turnover: Hong Kong does not impose quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount thresholds that automatically trigger an audit for an entity formed as a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37). A mandatory certified CPA audit is triggered, without a size threshold, if: (1) the FIP is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap 637, which requires audited annual accounts irrespective of revenue, assets, or partners; (2) the Inland Revenue Department expressly requires a certified profit-and-loss statement or audited accounts due to the size or complexity of the business; (3) the partnership deed, a financial institution, Stripe, PayPal, or a regulatory licensing body contractually mandates it; or (4) the entity is in fact a Hong Kong company registered under Cap 622, in which case every private company must be audited unless dormant-company conditions are satisfied. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 and the Transfer Pricing Rules, a Local File is mandatory if: (i) the aggregate value of any intercompany goods transactions exceeds HKD 5 million; (ii) the aggregate value of intercompany services or royalty transactions exceeds HKD 1 million; or (iii) the aggregate value of financing transactions exceeds HKD 1 million. A Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 1 billion and the Hong Kong entity's revenue is at least HKD 100 million. Country-by-Country reporting applies for groups with consolidated revenue of at least HKD 6.8 billion (subject to EU/global adoption standards).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the Inland Revenue Department applies a territorial source doctrine and anti-abuse rules. A Foreign-Invested Partnership must demonstrate real economic presence and conduct Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong – for a stock-photo AI business, these include key commercial decision-making, contract negotiation, server management, R&D in generative AI, and the employment of management staff. If such activities occur outside Hong Kong, the profits may be treated as offshore-sourced and not taxable in Hong Kong; conversely, where a structure has no genuine substance and merely claims treaty benefits (treaty shopping), the IRD may invoke the General Anti-Abuse Rule (IRO s.61A) to disregard the structure and re-characterise the income, denying treaty benefits and assessing tax at the statutory rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent False Declaration

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(1): conviction upon indictment – fine of up to HKD 50,000, imprisonment of 3 years, and additional tax not exceeding three times the amount undercharged. IRO s.80(2): summary conviction – fine of up to HKD 10,000 and up to three times the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil of corporate partners and hold the directors personally liable for the partnership's tax liabilities if they orchestrated or knowingly abetted the fraud. Directors face criminal imprisonment up to 3 years, disqualification as directors, asset seizure, travel bans (blacklisting) restricting departure from Hong Kong, and civil restitution orders to compensate the revenue authority.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure & Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRO s.58C and s.80(5): failure to provide information or documents to the Inland Revenue Department upon notice is an offence attracting a fine of up to HKD 100,000 (on indictment) and imprisonment of 6 months; for summary conviction, a fine of up to HKD 10,000 and a daily default fine of HKD 300 for continued non-compliance. In addition, the IRD may adjust the profits to reflect arm's length prices and assess additional tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors or designated partners who knowingly cause the entity to breach transfer pricing documentation requirements may be personally assessed for penalties under IRO s.82A. In severe cases, courts may lift the corporate veil and hold directors jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties. They may also face travel bans and disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance for Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The anti-abuse provisions of the double taxation arrangement, combined with the General Anti-Abuse Rule (IRO s.61A), authorise the taxation authority to disregard the artificial steps, deny treaty benefits, and re-assess the profits at the full statutory rate of 16.5%. Additional penalties up to 100% of the tax avoided may be levied under IRO s.61B. The partnership may also be struck off the Business Register and face winding-up proceedings under the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Directors or partners who participate in creating or maintaining a sham structure face personal liability for the tax avoided plus penalties. If they sign a false declaration of economic substance (e.g., by completing Box 8 of the return falsely), they are liable for perjury under the Crimes Ordinance and IRO s.82, with imprisonment of up to 2 years. The IRD may issue a travel ban (blacklisting) to prevent the individual from leaving Hong Kong during investigation, and may freeze personal bank accounts to secure the recovery of unpaid taxes.
