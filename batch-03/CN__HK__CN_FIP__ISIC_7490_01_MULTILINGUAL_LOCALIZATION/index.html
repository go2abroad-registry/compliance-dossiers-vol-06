The procurement procedure for a foreign non-resident partnership is: (1) if the entity is a limited partnership, first register with the Companies Registry under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37) and obtain the limited partnership certificate; (2) within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, file the prescribed business registration application with the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department, either online through GovHK Business Registration e-Service or by paper through a Hong Kong authorised representative, stating the English business name, principal place of business in Hong Kong, nature of business, date of commencement, full particulars of every general and limited partner including overseas addresses and foreign tax identification numbers, and appointing a Hong Kong address for service of notices; (3) no minimum capital contribution is required and non-resident ownership is permitted without any local residency condition; (4) the IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) displaying the 8-digit BRN, which functions as the Hong Kong tax identification number for profits tax, business registration, and all IRD correspondence; (5) after receipt of the BRC, the partnership must display it at the principal place of business and renew it in accordance with the Business Registration Ordinance; (6) when the IRD issues a Profits Tax Return for the partnership, the return must be completed on a fiscal-transparency basis reflecting each partner's agreed profit share, and each partner, including non-resident partners, will be separately assessed or may elect an authorised tax representative to handle the Hong Kong tax compliance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax. Hong Kong has no broad-based consumption tax; only selective excise duties are levied on specified dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Hong Kong in Hong Kong dollars or any other currency. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), any person, including a foreign non-resident partnership, carrying on business in Hong Kong must register with the Business Registration Office upon commencement of business regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, there is no statutory export VAT zero-rating claim, no place-of-supply regime for VAT, and no VAT refund process. Cross-border multilingual localization and media-dubbing services are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax. For physical goods exported through Hong Kong, the only relevant indirect duty exposure is for listed dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109); a VAT zero-rating entitlement cannot arise because no VAT exists.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account items, including trade in services such as professional multilingual translation and media dubbing, provided a bona fide commercial transaction is evidenced to the domestic bank through a service agreement, commercial invoice and tax declaration. Capital account items, including foreign partner capital injections into the Foreign-Invested Partnership, remain highly regulated: they must be registered with SAFE through a designated domestic bank, and each inward remittance is verified against the approved FIR registration and the partnership agreement. Profit remittances to non-resident partners are permissible after the annual audited financial statements are completed, the Chinese withholding tax (generally 10% on distributive profits of a non-resident partner, reduced under applicable double taxation agreements) has been settled, and the remittance is treated as a current account profit outflow. The FIP must first offer the banks a tax certificate and a resolution of partners. Export proceeds from localization services must be repatriated to a domestic foreign currency current account maintained with a licensed bank, and SAFE applies a surrender and settlement monitoring framework rather than an inflexible conversion ratio. Enterprises may retain foreign currency receipts in eligible accounts, but any failure to repatriate or any unexplained breach of the declared service value may trigger penalties, make the account subject to suspension, and obstruct future outbound payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from cross-border services, including translation and media dubbing, must be repatriated to a SAFE-registered domestic foreign currency account within 180 days from the contractual due date or actual receipt date, whichever is later. The current mandatory conversion ratio is effectively zero for export proceeds: the FIP may retain 100% in foreign currency in an eligible current account or convert to RMB at a domestic bank. However, each repatriation must be matched against the declared commercial invoice and filed under the balance of payments reporting system, and the FIP must obtain a state-administered foreign exchange settlement and sale notice. Any anticipated delay requires a written extension application to SAFE before the 180-day deadline ends; otherwise the bank will place a stop-limit on the account and the FIP will be subject to administrative investigation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the operative foreign exchange control authority for the PRC-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acting as the prudential supervisor for any Hong Kong clearing corridor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

The feasibility of Wise B2B and Payoneer as primary or statutory clearing rails is LOW, and using them as the sole banking interface is legally barred for a mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership. These offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in China, cannot open a RMB basic deposit account, and have no direct connection to SAFE capital verification, balance-of-payments reporting, or CIPS settlement. They are also not able to write the required tax and withholding remittance certificates for PRC fiscal purposes. At best they may operate as a complementary collection channel through a licensed domestic cross-border payment partner, but all statutory capital injections, export receipts, and outbound trade disbursements must flow through a domestic bank account controlled by the FIP. Any FIP relying on a pure EMI rail would fail its account compliance audit and expose the partners to penalties under the Administration of Payment Services rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: The feasibility of Wise B2B and Payoneer as primary or statutory clearing rails is LOW, and using them as the sole banking interface is legally barred for a mainland Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% partner-level enterprise income tax for resident corporate partners; the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is fiscally transparent and pays no entity-level tax; non-resident enterprise partners are subject to a 10% final withholding tax on Mainland-source allocable income unless a Double Taxation Arrangement reduces the rate.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law on profit distributions to non-resident enterprise partners before treaty relief; individual non-resident partners are generally subject to 20% individual income tax unless a DTA reduces the rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory for the Chinese FIP when annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, when intangible-related transactions or financial-asset transactions exceed RMB 100 million, or when any other related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is required when a multinational group's cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 2 billion or the group meets the consolidated-revenue threshold under SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: Although the PRC does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations to a Chinese partnership, the State Taxation Administration applies substance-over-form, general anti-abuse rule in Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, and the beneficial-ownership doctrine. The Hong Kong partner must perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, including strategic contracting, pricing decisions, invoice approval, fund management, and risk assumption, or the Chinese tax authority may recharacterize transactions, deny treaty benefits, and impose tax at the statutory 10% withholding rate with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes confiscation of illegal gains and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; false invoicing triggers Criminal Law Article 205 with fines and imprisonment; the State Taxation Administration may publish the taxpayer as a tax blacklist and impose exit-control bans.

Director personal liability: Managing partners and de facto directors who sign false invoices or direct concealment are personally jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties when partnership assets are insufficient. They face civil asset restitution, administrative blacklisting, travel bans under the Exit Administration Law, and criminal imprisonment up to seven years for tax evasion or false special VAT invoice offenses.
Treaty Abuse and Shell Conduit Structure

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test and GAAR, reassess at the statutory 10% withholding rate, charge late interest at the base lending rate plus five percentage points for transfer-pricing noncompliance, and impose penalties under Tax Administration Law Article 63 if the arrangement was concealed.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong conduit and the managing partner of the Chinese FIP can be personally liable for the recovered tax shortfall where the corporate veil is pierced for fraud or sham; they may be named in tax blacklists, subjected to cross-border travel restrictions, and exposed to criminal prosecution for intentional tax avoidance if they knowingly designed or executed the shell scheme.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 authorizes a primary transfer-pricing adjustment to the arm's-length result; Article 44 permits a deemed taxable income estimate for lack of documentation; SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 imposes a 5% interest uplift on underpaid tax; and Tax Administration Law Article 62 penalizes failure to report related-party information.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who approve the mispricing or obstruct documentation requirements face personal civil liability for the adjusted tax and interest if the partnership cannot satisfy the assessment. They may be disqualified from leaving China while more than RMB 100,000 of tax arrears is outstanding, and they may be criminally liable for facilitating the underdeclaration of tax through contrived intercompany charges.
