Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally permissible for a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) in Hong Kong, provided that the registered office address is a physical location in Hong Kong where legal documents can be served. However, for a patent prosecution agency, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and the Companies Registry may scrutinize the substance of the operation. The IRD requires that the partnership has a sufficient level of economic substance in Hong Kong, including a physical office, employees, and business records. A virtual office alone may be rejected for tax residency purposes and could trigger a higher risk of audit. Banks also typically require a physical office for account opening, especially for professional services firms. Therefore, while virtual offices are legally permissible, they are not recommended for this industry without a physical presence.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a limited partnership and does not require a blocked escrow account. The amount and timing of capital contributions are fixed by the Limited Partnership Agreement; each subscribing partner must contribute the agreed cash or in-kind capital, and the general partner may call capital as provided in the agreement.

Commercial office fire installations including portable extinguishers, emergency lighting, and means of escape shall be certified by a registered fire-service installation contractor.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; official electronic portal: eTAX (the IRD's electronic tax filing service). Business Registration is administered by the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for registration with the Business Registration Office within one month of commencement. The application is submitted through eTAX or at the Business Registration Office, accompanied by the partnership formation deed, identification documents for all partners, the partnership name, a local Hong Kong business address, and the statutory registration fee. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the entity's Practical Tax Identification Number for Profits Tax purposes. Because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, no VAT registration identifier is separately required. Foreign non-resident partners may be assigned a Taxpayer Identification Number / file reference by the IRD; if the partnership does not carry on business in Hong Kong but derives Hong Kong-source income, it should nonetheless notify the IRD and comply with the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Tax ID type & application path: Pass_Through_Eligible — A foreign non-resident partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong must apply for registration with the Business Registration Office within one month of commencement. The application is submitted through eTAX or at the Business Registration Office, accompanied by the partnership formation deed, identification documents for all partners, the partnership name, a local Hong Kong business address, and the statutory registration fee. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or comparable entity classification election. Partnerships are transparent under Hong Kong Profits Tax; individual partners may elect Personal Assessment under Part V of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), but this does not alter the partnership's pass-through classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong. Limited excise duties apply under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), and stamp duty is separately imposed under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Any person carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) regardless of turnover; there is no HKD turnover threshold to express for indirect tax purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no 0% export VAT rate, no zero-rating application, and no indirect tax refund claim mechanism. Cross-border services such as PCT patent prosecution and international trademark registration fall outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect taxation. For Profits Tax, the territorial source principle in the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) generally excludes profits arising outside Hong Kong; this is an offshore profits tax analysis, not an export VAT zero-rating claim.

None. Hong Kong does not operate a mandatory e-invoicing system, real-time fiscal clearance mandate, API stamping regime, or anti-fraud digital stamping system for VAT/GST. Taxpayers must retain ordinary business records sufficient to compute Profits Tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Separate Customs and Excise Department permit and licence controls apply only to dutiable, prohibited, or strategic commodities.

No VAT clawback regime applies because no export VAT relief is granted. However, an incorrect claim or unsupported position that Hong Kong Profits Tax is not chargeable on fees for patent prosecution or trademark services can be challenged by the Inland Revenue Department. Penalties for fraudulent or negligent incorrect tax returns under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) include additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax and potential prosecution; failure to register a business under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) also carries statutory penalties.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates a free capital account regime with no quantitative FX restrictions; as a Special Administrative Region it is not subject to SAFE exchange controls. The HKD is a freely convertible currency managed under the Linked Exchange Rate System between the HKMA and the US Dollar in the 7.75 to 7.85 band. Current account transactions such as service fees, patent office charges and royalties, as well as capital account transactions such as partner capital injections, distributions and loan disbursements, may be converted and remitted freely without HKMA approval. Profit remittance is therefore unrestricted at the Hong Kong level; no surrender or conversion ratio applies. The main constraint appears at the Mainland China interface: when a mainland partner contributes capital or when a mainland-based entity earns IP-related revenue, the remittance must comply with SAFE registration and obtain tax clearance, and the underlying transaction must be verified under SAFE current-account rules. Within Hong Kong itself, the treasury function may hold multi-currency balances and settle in HKD, USD, EUR, GBP or offshore CNY without post-conversion restrictions. The compliance burden lies in documented economic substance and UBO disclosure, not in exchange control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR. The HKMA does not statutorily require surrender, conversion, or time-bound repatriation of export proceeds or service receipts; a partnership may retain all foreign currency in its operating account. In the specific case of a PCT/IP agency, government fee refunds or WIPO disbursements may be received in any convertible currency and held without restriction. Where a Mainland Chinese counterparty is involved, the onshore remitting entity must comply with SAFE current-account verification, generally reporting within 30 days after settlement, but no mandatory conversion percentage applies in the current regime.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), as the de facto central bank and monetary authority for the Hong Kong SAR; for any onshore-leg remittance touching Mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the controlling authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

High viability for ancillary payment rails but legally constrained from serving as the primary banking anchor. Hong Kong permits licensed money service operators and stored-value facility / electronic money institutions; therefore Wise and comparable EMIs can handle low-value cross-border fee remittances such as WIPO or USPTO official payments. However, an EMI is not an authorized deposit-taking institution in Hong Kong, it cannot hold client trust monies as a licensed bank, and funds held with it are not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme. A true client-money trust account must be maintained at a Tier-1 authorized institution. The FIP should therefore use Wise only as an approved secondary rail after the Tier-1 bank has cleared the beneficiary list, transaction purpose codes and per-transaction thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Partner capital contributions should be settled by SWIFT MT103 from the remitting partner's bank to the FIP's Tier-1 HK account, with the beneficiary field containing the exact account-holder name and reference field stating the purpose as partnership capital contribution, the contributing partner name and the capital call date. No HKMA approval or statutory capital verification is required because Hong Kong has no exchange control; the receiving bank may require a CPA-certified statement of capital contribution to substantiate the FIP's books. If the contribution originates from Mainland China, the onshore remitting bank must classify the transfer under SAFE's capital-account framework for foreign-invested partnership equity contributions, supported by the entity's registration certificate and SAFE foreign-investment registration; the HK beneficiary account may be credited in HKD or CNH after standard conversion.The primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 from the FIP's Tier-1 bank account to the beneficiary patent office, IP office or foreign law firm. Each instruction must reference the official application or registration number and the associated client matter/case ID to support audit trail. Low-value official fee payments under the bank's approved threshold can be batched through Wise or another licensed EMI after the Tier-1 bank has whitelisted the beneficiary and approved the purpose code, but client-trust funds must not be routed through EMI accounts. Partnership Profits Tax Return (Form BIR52) The IRD issues a BIR52 return each year of assessment (1 April to 31 March); the return must be filed within one month from the date of issue (electronic filing through e-Tax may extend the deemed deadline to two months) and, where a different accounting date is used, within the IRD-specified issue-block due date. $0 USD Late filing exposes the general partner to a fixed penalty of HKD 10,000 and, where tax is undercharged, an additional penalty of up to three times the undercharged tax; if the return remains outstanding, the IRD can issue best-of-assessment tax assessments and prosecute the partnership in the District Court.
Employer's Return of Remuneration and Pensions (Form BIR56A) and individual employee statements (Form BIR56B / IR56 series) Annually issued on 1 April following the year of assessment; due one month after the date of issue (elective e-Tax filing is normally allowed up to 31 May) for all Hong Kong employers, including licensed partnership firms with employees. $0 USD Late or inaccurate submission is an offence under the Inland Revenue Ordinance: maximum penalty HKD 10,000 and additional penalty of three times the tax undercharged; an employer that fails to file may also be liable to prosecution and imprisonment, and the IRD may issue estimated assessments against the partnership.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong does not provide a quantitative audit-exemption threshold for companies under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); however a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) registered as a limited partnership is not itself a 'company', and no independent statutory audit is mandatory solely on the basis of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount. An independent certified CPA audit becomes mandatory where (i) the partnership is a licensed entity (e.g., a trust or corporate service provider, patent/trademark agency practice subject to professional body rules) whose licence imposes an audit condition; (ii) the IRD specifically requests audited accounts or an auditor's report in connection with a profits tax assessment; (iii) a general partner that is a Hong Kong company must audit its own accounts under Cap. 622 and the FIP is consolidated into that partner's audited group financial statements; or (iv) the partnership agreement, a lender, a key client, or international PCT/trademark-related contractual counterparty requires certified financial statements. For IRD purposes, financial statements need not be audited for a small partnership, but the IRD retains discretionary power to require audited accounts where the turnover, gross income, or accounts complexity makes verification necessary.
