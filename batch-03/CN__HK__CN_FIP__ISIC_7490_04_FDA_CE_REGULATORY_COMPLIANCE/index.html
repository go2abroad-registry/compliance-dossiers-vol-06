Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Procedure: (i) confirm that the partnership carries on business in Hong Kong; (ii) submit the prescribed business registration application form to the Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business; (iii) provide the partnership deed, identity and address evidence for each foreign partner, overseas registration or incorporation certificate, and the Hong Kong principal place of business address; (iv) if the foreign partners have no Hong Kong presence, appoint an authorized representative with written authority; (v) pay the applicable business registration fee and levy; and (vi) upon issuance of the Business Registration Certificate, use the BRN for profits tax correspondence, business records, and governmental filings. There is no separate VAT identification number because Hong Kong does not operate a VAT/GST system.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible (the Foreign-Invested Partnership is treated as transparent for Hong Kong profits tax purposes; each partner is separately assessable on their share of Hong Kong-sourced partnership profits, subject to the territorial source principle)

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide check-the-box elections, entity-classification elections, or hybrid mismatch elections. The partnership is transparent by operation of the Inland Revenue Ordinance; taxpayers may make a fact-based offshore profits claim for profits sourced outside Hong Kong, but this is not a statutory entity-classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong SAR has no general indirect tax system; limited duties exist only on certain dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/Sales Tax, so no economic turnover threshold triggers indirect tax registration. Every business carrying on business in Hong Kong is generally required to register under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), irrespective of turnover, unless a specific exemption applies.

Any exclusion of export profits from taxation is an income tax offshore-source claim under the territorial source principle, not an indirect tax zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Given that the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) is domiciled in mainland China, the RMB is not fully convertible on the capital account. The current account is largely convertible in accordance with IMF Article VIII, permitting trade in goods and services and profit remittances, subject to genuine transaction verification and SAFE reporting. However, capital account items such as foreign direct investment injections, equity transfers, and loans require prior registration and approval through SAFE. All inbound foreign exchange contributions to the FIP's capital base must be routed through a designated capital account at a domestic bank and converted via a valid RMB-FX transaction. Profit distribution to non-resident partners is permitted under current account rules but requires an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificate, and evidence of declared taxes. Surrender requirements no longer mandate full conversion of export proceeds, but all foreign exchange receipts from current account transactions must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign exchange account or settled into RMB via a licensed bank, with post-event reporting within 30 days. De facto convertibility hurdles arise from documentary compliance, banks' due diligence, and time-lags in verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE regulations, current account foreign exchange receipts (including consultancy fees) must be repatriated to China within the prescribed period (normally 180 days) from the date of collection. While there is no mandatory conversion ratio, all FX inflows must be either sold to a designated bank or retained in a designated foreign exchange settlement account; retention limits are set by the bank based on a company's operational needs and verified transaction history. Each receipt must be matched with a contract, invoice, and BOP declaration code (e.g., service trade code 221010). Non-compliance may trigger administrative penalties and loss of the 'A-class' customs and FX status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility. Mainland China does not recognise or license offshore EMI providers such as Revolut, Wise, or Airwallex for opening domestic multi-currency accounts for FIPs. Foreign-invested partnerships are mandated to conduct all FX transactions through SAFE-designated domestic banks. EMI platforms cannot fulfil documentary verification under SAFE's 'See-Through' supervision, nor can they obtain the necessary BOP codes. Moreover, the PBOC's Payment & Clearing Network does not interconnect with such EMIs. Therefore, all treasury operations must remain onshore with Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound foreign direct investment capital contributions must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the partner's bank, with the remittance purpose code fixed at 'FDI' (e.g., code 622101 for capital injection). The funds must be denominated in a freely convertible currency and directed to the FIP's dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. Within 15 business days of receipt, the bank will perform a statutory capital verification and issue a foreign exchange business confirmation. The funds cannot be deployed for operating expenses until the SAFE registration and capital verification are completed. For profit remittances, use purpose code '721121' (profit distribution) with an accompanying audited balance sheet and tax payment certificate.For service-related outbound payments (e.g., subcontracting regulatory testing), settlement rails may include SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for amounts under USD 500,000, with transaction narration supported by a contract and invoice. Larger trade disbursements can be executed via Documentary Collections (no draft or with draft) or Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) if the payment is tied to tangible deliverables. Business Registration Certificate Annual Renewal (Certificate of Business Registration under Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310) Annually before the expiry date shown on the current certificate; for a one-year certificate this is the day before the registration anniversary. The renewal form and fee are payable on or before the expiry date, with a de facto grace period of one month during which IRD may accept renewal without prosecution in practice. $276 USD Carrying on business without a valid Business Registration Certificate is an offence under Cap. 310: each partner may be liable to a fine of up to HK$5,000 (about USD 641) and imprisonment for up to one year; the Inland Revenue Department may assess estimated profits by best judgment and impose additional tax; repeated non-renewal can cause the business registration to lapse and, for a registered limited partnership, the Companies Registry may strike the entity off under section 53 of the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 32) for non-filing.
Profits Tax Return - Partnership Return (Form BIR52) IRD issues Form BIR52 on the first working day of April each year. The return is due within one month of the issue date; where the accounting period ended on 31 December, an approved tax representative may obtain an automatic extension to 15 August of the same year. $0 USD Late filing may lead to an estimated (best judgment) assessment under Inland Revenue Ordinance, Cap. 112 section 59; on conviction, fines up to HK$10,000 and three times the amount of tax undercharged for non-compliance with section 51C; interest on overdue tax is charged at the official rate (currently 8% per annum) under section 71.
Employer's Return of Remuneration and Pensions (Form BIR56A and individual IR56B forms) For each year of assessment ending 31 March, the Employer's Return is usually issued in early April and must be filed by 30 April (one month after the issue of the return). A nil return must be submitted if the FIP had no employees during the year. $0 USD Failure to file an employer's return or failure to notify cease-in-employment within the prescribed 14-day window is an offence; penalties include a fine up to HK$10,000 and three times the tax that would have been undercharged; IRD may also estimate the employer's salary expenses and disallow deductions.
Notice of Change of Particulars of Business or Limited Partnership (Form IRBR3111A / Companies Registry Form LPC1) Within 30 days after any change in the partnership's name, principal place of business, nature of business, partners, capital contributions, or other registered particulars; in addition, a limited partnership must notify the Companies Registry within 7 days of the change. $0 USD Non-filing of a notifiable change is an offence under Cap. 310 and the Partnership Ordinance; conviction may result in a fine of up to HK$5,000 for each offence and further daily default fines; the Registry may record the foreign-invested partnership as defunct and proceed with administrative strike-off after the partnership ceases business or fails to comply with a registered-office confirmation.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No quantitative HK balance-sheet, gross-receipts, or headcount threshold mandates a statutory independent audit for a Hong Kong foreign-invested general or limited partnership. An audit is compulsory, regardless of size, only if the FIP is also incorporated as a private company under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 or is registered as a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637 (which must engage a certified public accountant to audit annual financial statements unless the fund qualifies for an explicit Cap. 637 audit exemption). For an ordinary trading or consulting FIP, the IRD requires sufficient business records under Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap. 112 section 51C, but no statutory audit certificate is mandated solely by revenue or asset size.
