E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT ID; the statutory tax identification number for a non-resident person or partnership carrying on business in Hong Kong is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). A foreign non-resident entity desiring to form or invest in a partnership must: (i) determine whether the partnership is carrying on business in Hong Kong; (ii) if so, file the prescribed business registration application with the Business Registration Office of the IRD within one month of commencement, via the GovHK online e-service or in person; (iii) supply the partnership’s full English and Chinese names, principal place of business, particulars of all partners (including non-residents), nature of business, and documentary proof of the foreign partner’s legal existence such as a certificate of incorporation or certificate of registration in its home jurisdiction; (iv) pay the statutory business registration levy and fee; and (v) obtain the Business Registration Certificate, which bears the BRN used for subsequent tax filings, correspondence, and Inland Revenue Ordinance assessments. A foreign non-resident entity with no Hong Kong business presence has no mandatory Hong Kong tax registration obligation.

Not applicable in Hong Kong: because no VAT/GST is levied, there is no 0% export VAT claim or refund mechanism. For cross-border professional services (including anti-dumping trade-defense consulting), no export VAT arises; under the territorial source principle, profits derived by non-residents from services performed outside Hong Kong are outside Hong Kong profits tax, and the taxpayer must document that the significant profit-generating activities were performed outside Hong Kong.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible on the current account for genuine trade in goods, services, salaries, and profit remittance, but capital account operations remain tightly controlled and require separate SAFE registration or approval. For the CN_FIP, the Hong Kong partner's capital subscription must be registered with SAFE before any inbound remittance; the bank will check that the contribution is consistent with the signed partnership agreement and the registered partnership scope. Once the capital is booked into the FIP's capital account, conversion into RMB is permitted only for verifiable operating payments, such as lease, payroll, consulting fees, and taxes, under the payment-of-real-transactions audit. Current account outflows, such as distributions of partnership profits to Hong Kong partners, are not freely remittable without audited financial statements, a partners' capital allocation resolution, tax settlement proof, and bank-level anti-money laundering review. Since the Hong Kong SAR has no exchange controls and the HKD/USD market is free, the treasury can hold foreign exchange offshore; however, any flow into the PRC must use a licensed banking channel, and any RMB profit repatriation must be documented as a real claim based on certified accounts. These dual constraints make the FX approval tier the central liquidity risk for the structure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export and cross-border service foreign exchange receipts, including fees for anti-dumping trade defense consulting, must be repatriated into the PRC banking system within 180 days from the date of customs declaration, invoice issuance, or completion of service performance, unless SAFE has approved offshore retention. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under current regulations; the FIP may retain 100% of the export proceeds in a foreign exchange current account or convert any amount to RMB at market rate through its designated bank. The bank must register every receipt in SAFE's online monitoring system, and the FIP must keep underlying contracts, invoices, delivery documents, and proof of service performance for at least five years. Failure to repatriate on time is treated as a foreign exchange reporting violation, can trigger fines and revocation of the bank's convenience quota, and will block subsequent hedging and profit remittance applications.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the separate Hong Kong SAR authority for the cross-border HKD/USD corridor

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is extremely low for the PRC entity. Offshore digital banks and payment institutions such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, or Currenxie do not hold a deposit-taking banking licence from the National Financial Regulatory Administration and cannot legally open settlement accounts, issue RMB debits, or execute foreign currency conversion for a mainland-registered Foreign-Invested Partnership. If used at all, an EMI may only serve the Hong Kong side ledger or collect small administrative deposits outside China, but all substantive clearing and FX settlement for the CN_FIP must be conducted through a SAFE-approved domestic commercial bank. Recommending an EMI as the primary treasury rail would be non-compliant and would result in account freezing and regulatory penalty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection routing begins with the SAFE Foreign-Invested Partnership registration and filing through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System or local counterparts; the HK partner then instructs its Hong Kong licensed bank to make a SWIFT MT103 remittance to the FIP's designated FDI capital account at the PRC Tier-1 bank. The application must be annotated with the SAFE-mandated purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise capital contribution and must quote the FIP's unified social credit code; no split wires or personal transfers should be used. The receiving bank will freeze the funds until the CPA capital verification report and the partners' capital call resolution are filed. Conversion into RMB may then occur under the capital-use-by-truth system, with the bank verifying each downstream payment is for payroll, lease, taxes, or anti-dumping consulting costs and is within the registered business scope. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 7.5 billion consolidated group revenue triggers the obligation to prepare a Master File and Local File. For the Local File specifically, the Hong Kong entity must prepare documentation when its controlled transactions exceed: HKD 500 million for goods; HKD 125 million for services; HKD 125 million for intellectual property or financing transactions. As this FIP is engaged in trade remedy defence consulting (ISIC 7490), its intercompany services with related Chinese entities would fall under the HKD 125 million services threshold if applicable.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no codified Economic Substance Regulations like zero-tax jurisdictions, but the territorial source principle under s.14 IRO imposes an economic substance requirement: profits are chargeable only where the operations producing them are performed in Hong Kong. For anti-dumping defence consulting, the partnership must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) — client engagement, advisory decision-making, negotiation, and issuance of reports — are conducted by staff physically based in Hong Kong, with supporting records. Offshore claims without such substance are vulnerable to challenged assessments, additional taxes, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Offshore Income Claim Without Substance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), s.82 – Upon conviction, a fine of HKD 10,000 and liability to additional tax of up to three times the amount undercharged, plus imprisonment for up to 3 years. Civil fraud assessments may extend the tax period to 10 years under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Partners (including general partners) have unlimited liability for partnership tax debts. Where a partner knowingly participates in the evasion, the corporate veil is pierced automatically under partnership law; the partner's personal assets are subject to civil restitution and confiscation. The IRD may issue a departure prevention order under s.77A preventing the partner from leaving Hong Kong if unpaid tax exceeds HKD 200,000. Imprisonment is a personal criminal sanction; in a limited partnership, a limited partner who engages in management or perpetrates the fraud loses limited liability status.
Transfer Pricing Documentation and Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Sections 58A-58F IRO (as added by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No.6) Ordinance 2018) – Failure to prepare or retain required documentation attracts a fine of up to HKD 50,000 for each category of non-compliance. Where documentation is false or misleading, the fine is increased to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years. The assessor may also adjust the controlled transaction and impose additional tax of up to 100% of the shortfall.

Director personal liability: The general partners who are responsible for the filing are personally liable for penalties if they knowingly or negligently fail to comply. Civil liability includes the immediate recovery of the additional tax and penalties from the partners' personal estates. A departure prevention order may be issued to secure payment. Criminal liability attaches to the individual signatory of the misleading documentation; imprisonment is served in personal capacity. The piercing of the veil is automatic under partnership law; even limited partners may be exposed if they actively participated in the transfer pricing policy.
Failure to Withhold Royalty Tax and Unauthorised DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: Under s.21A IRO read with s.20A, the payer is deemed to be the agent of the non-resident and is personally liable to account for the tax that should have been withheld. Penalties for failure to deduct under s.82A may amount to 100% of the tax undercharged. If the incorrect claim is deemed fraudulent, s.82 penalties apply: HKD 10,000 fine plus three times undercharged tax and imprisonment for up to 3 years.

Director personal liability: Partners, as withholding agents, are jointly and severally liable for the unremitted tax, and the IRD may pursue civil recovery against the partners' personal assets. For wilful non-compliance, a criminal prosecution may be initiated against the responsible partners; a conviction results in personal fine and incarceration. It is also a basis for issuing a departure prevention order if the tax liability remains outstanding. The corporate veil is pierced because the partnership is not a separate legal entity; partners' personal liability is direct and unlimited in respect of the statutory obligation.
