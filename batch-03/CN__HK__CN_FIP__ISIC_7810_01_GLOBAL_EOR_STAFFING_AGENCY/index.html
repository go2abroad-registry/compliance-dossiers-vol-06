Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - no statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account rule applies to a Limited Partnership Fund under Cap. 637. The partners fix the capital in the Limited Partnership Agreement; contributions may be paid directly into the designated partnership bank account and are callable or repayable only in accordance with the Limited Partnership Agreement and the preservation-of-assets and solvency protections in Cap. 637. For PRC investor outbound filing, any capital remittance must be reported under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and foreign direct investment regulations if routed through mainland China, but that condition is not a formation capital blockade at the Hong Kong registry level.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China acting through the National Electronic Tax Service portal; for Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTax portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor files the establishment application with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its local counterpart to register the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP); upon approval, the FIP receives a business license and a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: Within 30 days after the business license is issued, the FIP completes taxpayer registration through the provincial State Taxation Administration (STA) e-Tax service; the USCC becomes the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for e-filing and VAT purposes. Step 3: If a foreign partner is a non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment, a non-resident enterprise tax record must be established under the Administrative Measures for Tax Registration of Non-Resident Enterprises, either directly at the STA office exercising jurisdiction over the payer or via the e-tax platform, to support withholding, services, dividends, royalties, and other China-source income compliance. Step 4: The FIP must apply for a digital certificate (CA key) for the electronic tax system to enable authorized online declarations and payments. Step 5: If the FIP also maintains a Hong Kong business registration presence, the IRD Business Registration Office will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC); for Hong Kong profits tax and filing obligations, the IRD eTax system is used.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None for income-tax classification. A Chinese Foreign-Invested Partnership is fiscally transparent by default for enterprise income tax purposes; there is no check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election available under Chinese domestic law. For VAT purposes, the FIP may elect small-scale taxpayer simplified treatment until annual taxable turnover exceeds the mandatory general taxpayer threshold. Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box election for this entity.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China VAT: mandatory general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; all active enterprises must complete basic tax registration from business commencement regardless of turnover. Hong Kong: no VAT registration threshold because no VAT/GST exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under China VAT rules, exported goods are zero-rated when they are declared to China Customs and physically leave Chinese territory. Cross-border services, including international EOR and payroll support provided to an overseas principal, may qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption if the service is consumed wholly outside China, the recipient is located outside China, and the transaction satisfies the statutory place-of-supply and beneficial enjoyment conditions. The zero-rated supply must be supported by a fully digitalized zero-rated VAT invoice and documented evidence of foreign currency settlement received in China.

  • Export Customs Declaration (Bill of Entry) duly accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving the goods physically left Chinese territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher
  • Cross-border service agreement or EOR staffing contract with the overseas principal
  • Place-of-supply and beneficial owner evidence, including service completion records, milestone reports, and payroll timesheets
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued through the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system

China mandates the State Taxation Administration's Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system under the Golden Tax System Phase IV architecture; VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved electronic channels, transmitted in near real-time to the tax authority's clearance platform, and authenticated with unique invoice numbers and digital anti-fraud stamps. Hong Kong does not impose a corresponding general real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate.

Export zero-rating is subject to strict audit scrutiny. If the supporting documentation is incomplete, false, or the services are found not to be genuinely consumed outside China, the zero-rating is reversed and the tax authority will claw back the unpaid VAT plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties may range from 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall under the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may also trigger criminal liability, unlimited statutory recovery periods, and imprisonment under the PRC Criminal Law provisions governing tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates as a separate monetary jurisdiction with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as its de facto central bank and the Hong Kong dollar (HKD) freely convertible under the linked exchange rate system. However, because the subject entity is a Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) formed in mainland China with cross-border payments into and out of Hong Kong, the relevant foreign exchange control authority for RMB current and capital account items is the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) in the People's Republic of China. RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account; capital injections into the FIP require SAFE registration, foreign exchange quota approval, and conversion through a designated domestic bank under the Foreign Invested Partnership foreign exchange registration regime. Current account items such as trade-related service fees, payroll disbursements, management fees, and employer-of-record service fees are generally convertible upon submission of authentic supporting documentation (contracts, invoices, customs or service verification), but the payer must complete a SAFE 'Direct Reporting' transaction code and retain full documentary trails for five years. Profit remittances and dividend-like distributions from the FIP to non-resident partners require an annual audit report, tax payment proof (corporate income tax withholding where applicable), and a SAFE foreign exchange payment application, making outbound distributions subject to material administrative friction. Surrender requirements apply to export proceeds for goods trade, but for a staffing agency primarily exporting services, foreign currency receipts derived from cross-border service revenue must be repatriated and converted into RMB according to the prevailing SAFE rules unless a verified offshore retention account is approved. The Hong Kong receiving entity is unrestricted in holding and deploying HKD and foreign currency liquidity, and may aggregate global EOR payroll liquidity in Hong Kong as a treasury hub, provided that onshore RMB funding flows into China are properly structured as current account service payments with underlying employment contracts and payroll agency agreements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For service-export proceeds received into mainland China, the FIP must repatriate receipts to its domestic SAFE-designated foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory timeline (generally within 180 days of receipt for current account items or as stipulated in the registration certificate, unless an extension is approved for bona fide commercial reasons). The mandatory conversion ratio historically was 100 percent surrender for allowable export receipts under the old rules; after SAFE reforms, enterprises may retain receipts in foreign currency accounts or convert voluntarily, but capital verification and RMB funding for operational expenses typically require settlement at the prevailing spot rate through the designated bank, with all transactions reported under the correct service trade code such as 'payroll and employment services' or 'management consulting services' in the SAFE digital monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks such as Wise (TransferWise) are not licensed to provide regulated corporate banking services to mainland China onshore entities, and cannot serve as the primary settlement bank for the FIP's domestic RMB obligations, SAFE-registered capital flows, or tax payment processes in China. Their viability in Hong Kong is higher: licensed stored value facilities or money service operators may be used for low-value global payroll disbursements, but they remain supplementary because they do not offer full corporate credit lines, trade finance, SAFE-compliant RMB conversion, or capital account intermediation. For the global EOR staffing agency treasury structure, the recommended approach is a hybrid: use a tier-1 Hong Kong licensed bank as the core liquidity clearing and payroll disbursement hub, and use Wise only for marginal non-regulated beneficiary payments in corridors where FX efficiency matters, provided the Hong Kong entity is the contractual payor and each remittance has underlying payroll or contractor agreements to satisfy AML screening.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections into the mainland China FIP must be routed through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange settlement account at a tier-1 Chinese commercial bank (e.g., Bank of China Shanghai branch) using the following protocol: (1) execute the FIP partnership agreement and obtain the business license; (2) complete SAFE foreign exchange establishment registration for the FIP and obtain the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate; (3) effect the wire transfer from the Hong Kong treasury company through SWIFT MT103 citing purpose code 'grant of other equity capital' (currently aligned with SAFE classification 622081 for investment inward remittance) or the appropriate capital project code; (4) ensure the remitter is the registered foreign partner and the beneficiary is the FIP's capital escrow account; (5) upon receipt, the domestic bank will conduct capital verification through a certified public accountant and issue a capital verification report confirming the foreign exchange settlement and RMB conversion; and (6) convert foreign currency into RMB using a SAFE-designated spot transaction, ensuring compliance with the negative list and the Foreign Investment Law negative list restrictions (the staffing agency industry should be analyzed against the latest market entry negative list, though HR and EOR services are generally permitted unless involving specific Chinese listing or dispatch licensing thresholds). The Hong Kong paying entity must maintain all originator and beneficiary details, remittance contracts, and underlying partnership agreements for five years to satisfy bank replay queries.For outbound global payroll and EOR disbursements from the Hong Kong treasury hub, the recommended clearing rails are: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct beneficiary payments to employee bank accounts and contractor accounts in countries outside mainland China, with full structured remittance information (employee name, payroll reference, contract number, and purpose code 'wages, salaries, and other remuneration'); (b) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) are not appropriate for payroll, but may be used only if the EOR platform engages in procurement of software or tangible goods ancillary to staffing services; for such goods, a confirmed irrevocable documentary credit opened by the Hong Kong entity from a tier-1 bank provides settlement security; and (c) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are viable only for trade-based invoices to corporate clients for recruitment or workforce management services, but for pure payroll pass-through flows the SWIFT MT103 credit transfer rail is the dominant, lowest-risk method; use professional clearing via the HK Clearing House Automated Transfer System (CHATS) in HKD and USD, concurrent with RMB clearing via the Hong Kong RMB Real-Time Gross Settlement (RTGS) system for onward transfers to mainland China.Onshore FX forwards for period up to 12 months available to the FIP from a SAFE-designated domestic bank, requiring real trade or settlement background and the submission of the underlying service or payroll contract for the bank's authenticity review.Hong Kong non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on CNH for hedging RMB exposure without onshore SAFE registration, permissible for the Hong Kong entity as an offshore participant with maturity generally up to 2 years.Currency swaps between HKD, USD, RMB, and other payroll currencies for cross-currency treasury matching, executed only with licensed counterparties under ISDA documentation, ensuring that no derivative transaction creates a leveraged speculative exposure contrary to SAFE's derivatives real-demand principle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs and neobanks such as Wise (TransferWise) are not licensed to provide regulated corporate banking services to mainland China onshore entities, and cannot serve as the primary settlement bank for the FIP's domestic RMB obligations, SAFE-registered capital flows, or tax payment processes in China. Their viability in Hong Kong is higher: licensed stored value facilities or money service operators may be used for low-value global payroll disbursements, but they remain supplementary because they do not offer full corporate credit lines, trade finance, SAFE-compliant RMB conversion, or capital account intermediation. For the global EOR staffing agency treasury structure, the recommended approach is a hybrid: use a tier-1 Hong Kong licensed bank as the core liquidity clearing and payroll disbursement hub, and use Wise only for marginal non-regulated beneficiary payments in corridors where FX efficiency matters, provided the Hong Kong entity is the contractual payor and each remittance has underlying payroll or contractor agreements to satisfy AML screening.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under HK Inland Revenue Ordinance, a taxpayer must prepare a Local File if the annual turnover exceeds HKD 500 million and the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 100 million. A Master File is required if the MNE group has consolidated revenue of at least HKD 6.8 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue ≥ HKD 6.8 billion and operations in multiple jurisdictions. These thresholds apply to partnerships where controlled transactions are conducted with associated enterprises.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no codified Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) like traditional offshore centres. However, under the territorial source principle and IRO s.61A general anti-avoidance rule, taxpayers claiming offshore income treatment must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) (for an EOR: client acquisition, contract negotiation, payroll processing, employee onboarding/compliance, and risk management) are performed outside Hong Kong. If the CIGA are performed in Hong Kong, the corresponding profits will be taxable onshore. A mere shell bookkeeping arrangement will be disregarded, and profits will be adjusted to reflect the substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Misrepresentation of Offshore Profits

Statutory sanction: Upon conviction under IRO s.82: fine up to HKD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years (summary conviction) or up to 5 years (indictment), in addition to a further fine of 3 times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also impose a s.82A additional tax of 10% of the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: Managing general partner (or director-equivalent) is personally and criminally liable if they signed the false return or directed the arrangement. Civil liability includes repayment of all avoided tax and penalties; assets can be charged under a court order. A departure ban (travel ban / blacklist) may be issued under IRO s.80H. Imprisonment terms as per sanction.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61A permits the IRD to disregard the arrangement and recharacterise profits as onshore, subject to tax at the top rate. Additionally, a penalty of 10% of the tax undercharged is imposed under s.82A. If the sham is found to be fraudulent trading, the Companies Court may wind up the entity and impose fines under the Companies Ordinance.

Director personal liability: The partner may be personally liable for the full tax and penalties; the court may order restitution of the assets extracted from the partnership. Director disqualification up to 15 years could occur if fraudulent trading is proven. A travel ban and blacklisting by the IRD may be issued, and the partner could be subject to criminal imprisonment for up to 5 years under s.56C of the Crimes Ordinance if fraud is committed.
AML Compliance Failure in Global Payroll Channel

Statutory sanction: Cap. 615 (AMLO) s.21 – failure to apply CDD measures: fine up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment up to 2 years per offence; continued non-compliance may attract a daily penalty. The company may be deregistered/relicensed. If laundering is established, the proceeds of crime act may impose additional fines.

Director personal liability: Directors/general partners can be criminally prosecuted for wilful neglect of AML duties; they face the full fine and imprisonment (up to 2 years). They may be collectively and severally liable for compensable losses, and asset restraint orders can be issued under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance. A travel ban and blacklisting by the banking sector are practical consequences, with potential extradition if funds involved foreign criminal proceeds.
