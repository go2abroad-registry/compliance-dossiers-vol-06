Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the electronic tax service portal etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with telephone hotline 12366; where Hong Kong SAR-source tax matters arise, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong (IRD) via its eTax electronic services portal at www.ird.gov.hk.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: For the foreign-invested partnership (FIP), file establishment with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), attaching notarised and authenticated identity documents of all foreign investors and the partnership agreement. Step 2: Obtain the FIP Business License bearing the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC); under PRC law the USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Step 3: The AMR automatically transfers registration data to the STA; verify or complete supplementary tax registration on the STA e-tax portal within 30 calendar days. Step 4: Obtain the electronic tax registration certificate and digital certificate for e-tax filings. Step 5: If annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, register as a VAT general taxpayer through the integrated e-tax platform; otherwise remain a small-scale taxpayer with simplified VAT filing. Step 6: For each foreign non-resident partner, apply separately for a non-resident taxpayer identification number with the competent STA tax office so that allocated partnership profits can be subject to withholding or self-assessment; disclose the partner's home-country TIN to the FIP. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible: Under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China, the FIP is fiscally transparent and is not itself an enterprise income tax payer; profits are allocated to partners and taxed in their hands under corporate or individual income tax rules. For Hong Kong SAR tax purposes, the territorial source principle applies, so only profits sourced in Hong Kong are taxable; profits sourced outside Hong Kong may be offshore if the substantive control and key functions are located outside Hong Kong.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box or elective entity-classification regime for partnerships; the FIP is pass-through by statutory operation and cannot elect entity-level taxation. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under PRC law. In Hong Kong, partners may assert an offshore claim under territorial source principles, but the partnership itself has no elective tax classification mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover triggers mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration in the PRC. Any FIP making taxable supplies in mainland China must complete VAT tax registration from the date of business licence issuance regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border supply of study abroad and immigration visa consultancy may qualify for 0% VAT export treatment only where the service is supplied to a client located outside mainland China, is wholly consumed offshore, and falls within the statutory cross-border zero-rated service list issued by the Ministry of Finance and the STA. If the consulting service is not on that list, the supply remains taxable at 6%. For physical goods, zero-rating requires actual export and customs clearance from mainland China. For qualifying offshore digital services, the recipient must be a non-resident and the place of supply and consumption must be outside mainland China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for the People's Republic of China
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill issued by a licensed carrier
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a PRC-designated foreign-exchange bank evidencing foreign currency receipt from the overseas client
  • Cross-border service contract or sales agreement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Evidence of overseas consumption or delivery, including client onboarding records, visa/immigration service status reports, and delivery acknowledgements

Under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV system, all VAT invoices must be issued through the national integrated electronic tax platform. Fully digitalised electronic invoices (FDEI) are generated online with a unique invoice number and anti-counterfeit digital stamp, and invoice data is transmitted to the STA in near-real time. VAT special invoices require digital stamping and entry into the VAT invoice management system; API-based reporting is available to taxpayers, and abnormal invoices are automatically flagged for anti-fraud review. Paper invoice reliance is being replaced by the fully digital e-invoice regime.

PRC tax authorities will examine the documentary trail supporting the 0% rate. If the export or cross-border service claim is unsubstantiated, fraudulent, or repatriation of foreign exchange does not occur within the statutory period, the VAT exemption/zero-rating is retrospectively clawed back with late-payment interest at 0.05% per day and a fine of 50% to five times the tax evaded under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export refund claims and false VAT invoice issuance are also subject to criminal referral under Articles 204 and 205 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland RMB current account is de facto convertible for genuine service transactions, including the education-advisory fees earned by the FIP, but every inward and outward remittance is screened against the SAFE enterprise classification and the genuine-transaction principle. The capital account is not freely convertible: any foreign capital injection into the FIP from the Hong Kong partner must first be registered with SAFE through the settlement bank and credited to a restricted direct-investment capital account; the amount and entry purpose cannot be changed without filing an amendment. Conversion of those capital-account funds into RMB is allowed only on a payment-by-payment basis against approved business use, not as a free conversion facility. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong partner is a current-account distribution once the FIP satisfies the partnership tax filing, has audited or CPA-reviewed financial statements, and passes the bank's distribution checklist; the bank will look for unpaid VAT/income tax, a partner meeting resolution, and consistency with historic operating revenue. In Hong Kong SAR, by contrast, there are no surrender or convertibility controls, and HKD/offshore RMB can be freely used for settlement, which allows the HK treasury hub to concentrate liquidity while the regulated activities remain booked in mainland China.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the PRC trade-in-goods foreign exchange control regime, all export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China through a bank within 180 days after the date of customs export declaration. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: exporters and service providers, including the FIP, may retain the entire receipt in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert it to RMB at the market rate, provided the transaction is genuine and supported by contracts, invoices, customs declarations or service delivery records. For the FIP's study-abroad and immigration advisory services, service-trade receipts are subject to the same current-account repatriation principle and are monitored under SAFE's service-trade verification system; no surrender or sale requirement applies, but any delayed receipt older than the bank's classification threshold will trigger enhanced due diligence.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for the mainland China FIP; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR treasury side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-Viable as a regulated treasury rail for the mainland FIP. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open RMB or foreign-currency current/capital accounts for Chinese resident entities, cannot perform SAFE direct-investment registration, cannot provide the required capital-account payment-verification reporting, and cannot issue documents acceptable to PRC tax authorities. The FIP may use an EMI only for non-regulated ancillary Hong Kong liquidity concentration, but every substantive inbound capital injection, client escrow, tax payment, and FX conversion must remain on the books of a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong partner must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 (for USD, HKD, or EUR) or CIPS (for offshore RMB) to the FIP's SAFE-registered direct-investment capital account at a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank. The remittance must quote the SAFE FDI registration number for the foreign-invested partnership and use the capital-account foreign-exchange purpose code for direct investment into an FIP; any credit that exceeds the registered amount or lacks the registration reference will be rejected or held in a pending account. After crediting, a PRC-registered CPA must issue a capital verification report, and all onward RMB conversion or settlement must pass the bank's capital-account payment-verification checklist against invoices, contracts, and the approved business scope.Outbound disbursements will use SWIFT MT103 for service fees, university tuition deposits, immigration authority fees, and refunds, with each instruction bearing a genuine current-account purpose code and the bank retaining the underlying contract/invoice. Escrow payments will be segregated in a dedicated ledger and released only under dual-signature authorization plus evidence of service delivery or visa outcome. Commercial letters of credit are not appropriate for intangible consultancy services but may be used only for a bank-issued performance guarantee or standby LC when an overseas university or immigration principal demands security. Documentary collections may support performance-related shipping documents in ancillary education-supply contracts. Hong Kong transfers will clear through HKD/CHATS, USD/CHATS, or RMB RTGS, and all other jurisdictions through SWIFT with end-to-end tracking.Onshore RMB/FX forward contracts through SAFE-licensed banks, limited to genuine current-account service receipts and payments with a maximum one-year tenor and no net speculative open position.Onshore FX swaps for same-day/spot liquidity matching of non-RMB escrow disbursements, subject to underlying trade/service documentation and bank approval.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) booked only by the Hong Kong treasury company/partner; NDFs are not permitted for the mainland FIP and cannot be used to circumvent onshore SAFE regulations.Cross-currency swaps are prohibited for this service-sector FIP unless linked to a separately approved bilateral loan and fully documented for hedge accounting.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-Viable as a regulated treasury rail for the mainland FIP. Offshore fintech EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open RMB or foreign-currency current/capital accounts for Chinese resident entities, cannot perform SAFE direct-investment registration, cannot provide the required capital-account payment-verification reporting, and cannot issue documents acceptable to PRC tax authorities. The FIP may use an EMI only for non-regulated ancillary Hong Kong liquidity concentration, but every substantive inbound capital injection, client escrow, tax payment, and FX conversion must remain on the books of a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank.

Turnover: No statutory audit is mandated for a Hong Kong partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 321), the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), or the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

15% standard Profits Tax on unincorporated partnership assessable profits; corporate partners taxed at 16.5%; two-tiered concession applies at 7.5% for unincorporated business and 8.25% for corporations on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profit.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends or partnership profit distributions; no statutory dividend WHT arises on repatriation from the FIP to a non-resident partner.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual Profits Tax Return is issued after the fiscal year of assessment, normally due within one month from the date of issue; e-filing returns are generally granted a one-month extension. Provisional Profits Tax is payable in two instalments, typically in November and April, with the exact statutory deadline stated on the Demand Note.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 4.5 billion group consolidated revenue for CbC reporting; HKD 400 million annual turnover for the Hong Kong entity and related-party transactions exceeding HKD 220 million for goods, HKD 110 million for financial/intangible property transactions, or HKD 44 million for other related-party services such as management fees and referral commissions.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not adopt the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD applies a robust economic substance doctrine through IRO s.61 (sham), s.61A (general anti-abuse rule), and the OECD principal purpose test in tax treaties. For a study-abroad/golden visa FIP, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) are client sourcing, suitability assessment, visa strategy, contract execution, and post-landing advisory support; these must be actively performed by Hong Kong-located officers if the entity is to be recognised as a Hong Kong resident and is to enjoy treaty protection. A mailbox office with no local employees, no substantive board or partner meetings, and no local records will be pierced.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by artificial offshore profit diversion

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82 - fraudulent tax evasion is punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000, imprisonment for three years, and an additional tax liability of three times the undercharged tax; s.80 and s.82A provide civil penalties of 50%, 100%, or 200% of the tax deficiency depending on conduct.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate/partnership veil and treat the controlling director or managing general partner as the person who knowingly furnished false tax returns. The individual faces civil asset restitution, freezing orders, a travel ban / blacklisting by the Inland Revenue Department, disqualification from acting as a director, and criminal imprisonment for up to three years.
Shell company abuse and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: The IRD will disregard the shell under IRO s.61A and invoke the Principal Purpose Test under the Mainland-HK Arrangement and the multilateral instrument, denying treaty benefits, recharacterising the transactions, and imposing additional assessments and penalties equal to 100% or more of the tax avoided.

Director personal liability: Directing partners who sign declarations that omit material facts are personally liable for false statements under IRO s.82; the veil is pierced because the entity was a facade; the director can be blacklisted, prohibited from leaving the jurisdiction, ordered to make civil restitution of diverted profits, and criminally imprisoned.
AML and immigration visa fraud

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) creates criminal liability for breaches of customer due diligence and record-keeping, with fines and imprisonment; the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) adds up to seven years' imprisonment for dealing with proceeds of unlawful conduct; Immigration Ordinance (Cap. 115) penalises false representation with severe fines and up to 14 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors and general partners cannot hide behind the partnership: they face personal arrest, asset forfeiture, a travel ban and blacklisting, civil restitution to defrauded clients, and imprisonment of up to seven years (or 14 years for immigration fraud).
