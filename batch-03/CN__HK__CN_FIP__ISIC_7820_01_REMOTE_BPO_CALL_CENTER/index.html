Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital on a Foreign-Invested Partnership. Capital contributions are fixed by the Partnership Deed. For a PRC corporate partner, the actual cash contribution must be registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange under the outbound investment regime, and the contributed funds must be parked in a Hong Kong bank account in the partnership name until the statutory registers and beneficial ownership records are finalized. No blocked escrow account is required by Hong Kong law beyond normal account opening client due diligence.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the cross-border supply of remote BPO call-center services from mainland China to a Hong Kong SAR customer, the FIP may apply the VAT 0% export rate or VAT exemption if the services are provided to a non-resident enterprise, consumed entirely outside mainland China, properly contracted, and paid for by the offshore customer. The FIP must be a general VAT taxpayer and must hold contemporaneous evidence that the service is rendered for a non-mainland economic operator; otherwise the standard 6% VAT rate applies.

  • Export service agreement or purchase order identifying the Hong Kong SAR recipient and stating the cross-border nature of the remote call-center services
  • Commercial invoice issued by the FIP showing zero-rating or exemption treatment
  • No China Customs export declaration is required for pure services; if goods are incidentally exported, the electronic Customs Export Declaration from the China Single Window would be required
  • Foreign exchange repatriation proof: bank inward remittance receipt, bank-issued foreign exchange settlement or receipt record, or SAFE export proceeds monitoring record evidencing receipt of payment from the Hong Kong customer
  • Service delivery proof: call detail records, remote-agent workstation logs, time-zone shift schedules, quality monitoring reports, and written confirmation from the customer that the BPO services were consumed outside mainland China

Mandatory electronic invoicing through the STA nationwide e-invoice system integrated with Golden Tax System Phase IV. Invoices are transmitted to STA in real time and must be issued through the e-Tax Bureau or a certified e-invoice service provider using a U-Key or secure digital certificate. Each invoice carries an encrypted verification code and enters the STA national invoice database; no traditional physical fiscal stamping is used.

The STA will scrutinize export zero-rating and exemption claims through Golden Tax System cross-checking with banking and customs data. If the export documentation is false or insufficient, the claimed 0% rate will be reversed with VAT at 6% plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day; fraudulent export tax refunds may attract penalties from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid and criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but capital account items remain heavily administered. For a foreign-invested partnership (FIP) whose contributing investor is domiciled in Hong Kong SAR, all capital inflows are classified under the capital account and must follow SAFE registration and verification procedures rather than the simpler current-account rules. Profit remittances are treated as current account outflows and are, in principle, permissible after verification of audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and a valid SAFE certificate for foreign direct investment registration. However, practical hurdles include the need to source the remittance from actual after-tax distributable profits, evidence of complete settlement of Chinese taxes, and clawback risk if the underlying service contracts are challenged as lacking economic substance. Export or service-trade proceeds generated by the BPO operations are subject to repatriation into mainland China through designated bank accounts and are placed under the Goods and Services Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring system; no mandatory conversion to renminbi is currently imposed, but the receipts must be retained in, or converted through, a licensed bank. Funds held in foreign currency accounts may be used only for permitted payments, and unused balances must be recorded with SAFE. The existence of the Hong Kong partner introduces double-layer risk because capital injected to the FIP is classified as inbound direct investment and recorded on the SAFE direct investment system; subsequent distributions to Hong Kong require matching evidence of the prior capital-inflow file to avoid reclassification as illegal outward remittance. Furthermore, the FIP must not use the Hong Kong partner as a mere conduit for foreign funds; SAFE will assess the ultimate beneficial owners and the genuine business purpose of the BPO entity, which could otherwise trigger anti-structuring and round-tripping (reverse investment) scrutiny under the 2015 Foreign Investment Law and the 2020 Negative List. In summary, convertibility is real for trade and services, but any capital account or suspicious profit outflow will face layered compliance checks and possible quarantine of funds pending bank review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular [2012] No. 38 and the subsequent goods and services trade administration framework, export receipts must be repatriated to mainland China within 180 days from the relevant customs declaration date and must be reported to the SAFE monitoring system within 30 days of receipt. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; enterprises may retain 100% of verified export/service receipts in a foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily convert the proceeds to renminbi. However, any current-account receipt that is not supported by an underlying bona fide transaction will be suspended by the receiving bank and may be returned to sender.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), exercising delegated authority over all cross-border capital flows and foreign exchange transactions for entities domiciled in mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability for the mainland China FIP as the specified legal entity domicile is subject to PBOC and SAFE regulatory jurisdiction. Licensed domestic banks are the only authorised channels for receiving foreign exchange and converting RMB under the direct investment registration framework. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise Business or Payoneer do not hold a mainland China payment institution licence and cannot open a bank account with a mainland Chinese business registration number. Even if such EMI services are offered through a Hong Kong partner, their use for settlement of the FIP's own trade or service obligations would require a licensed onshore bank to act as the official settlement and reporting agent. The FIP would still need a domestic corporate bank account for SAFE filing, tax bureau registration and social insurance contributions. Consequently, fintech EMI rails are not a substitute but may be used only as a customer payout tool by the underlying BPO clients and must never be used to route FIP foreign exchange receipts or capital injections.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong partner must remit the injection via SWIFT MT103 from a bank in Hong Kong to the FIP's dedicated capital contribution foreign exchange account at a mainland Tier-1 bank, using the SAFE-approved international payments purpose code for inbound direct investment capital contribution (to be selected from the current SAFE code list, with the remittance reference stating 'capital contribution to a foreign-invested partnership'). The remittance reference must also contain the FIP's name and the SAFE FDI registration number. Before the transfer, the FIP must complete foreign investment registration and filing on the SAFE digital foreign exchange administration system, and the bank will report the transaction to SAFE under the cross-border receivables and payables statistical system. After receipt, the bank will issue a foreign exchange capital contribution proof. If capital is to be converted to RMB, the FIP must follow the independent settlement model and fill in the capital account payment use form; each RMB converted use must be for permitted operating expenses only. Capital verification for accounting and tax purposes must be performed by a licensed certified public accountant (CPA) within the time limit allowed by the partnership agreement, but the bank's SAFE verification is the operative regulatory check. All subsequent outbound remittances of dividends, partnership distributions, or refunds to the Hong Kong partner require matching the original capital injection registration and can be routed only after the CPA audited financial statements show distributable profits and after resident tax is withheld or satisfied.For BPO service fees payable to overseas vendors (e.g., cloud telephony, global network, software licences), the standard clearing rail is an MT103 SWIFT customer credit transfer from the FIP's foreign currency or RMB account, with a purpose code matching the service trade (e.g., telecommunication services or computer and software licences). If the payee requires settlement in RMB, the FIP may use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) through the correspondent bank, and the transaction will be reported under the RMB Cross-Border Payment Information Reporting system. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections apply only to tangible goods procurement, which are not core to a call centre but can be issued for hardware purchases if suppliers require them; an irrevocable confirmed LC would be opened at the FIP's bank with a margin covering the imported goods (e.g., telephony switches or servers). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for the mainland China FIP as the specified legal entity domicile is subject to PBOC and SAFE regulatory jurisdiction. Licensed domestic banks are the only authorised channels for receiving foreign exchange and converting RMB under the direct investment registration framework. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise Business or Payoneer do not hold a mainland China payment institution licence and cannot open a bank account with a mainland Chinese business registration number. Even if such EMI services are offered through a Hong Kong partner, their use for settlement of the FIP's own trade or service obligations would require a licensed onshore bank to act as the official settlement and reporting agent. The FIP would still need a domestic corporate bank account for SAFE filing, tax bureau registration and social insurance contributions. Consequently, fintech EMI rails are not a substitute but may be used only as a customer payout tool by the underlying BPO clients and must never be used to route FIP foreign exchange receipts or capital injections.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% PRC Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) on each corporate partner's allocable share of Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) taxable income; the FIP itself is fiscally transparent under Caishui [2008] No. 159 and is not a separate EIT taxpayer.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% final withholding tax on PRC-source dividend-equivalent profit distributions paid to a non-resident enterprise partner without a China PE; 5% reduced rate under Mainland-HK DTA Art. 10 where a Hong Kong resident company beneficially owns at least 25% of the capital; however, FIP profit allocations to a Hong Kong partner are characteristically governed by Art. 7 (Business Profits) where the mainland FIP constitutes a permanent establishment, making the ordinary 25% EIT regime applicable through the HK partner's mainland filing.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File mandatory where the enterprise's annual related-party revenues exceed RMB 1 billion; Local File mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for financial transactions, RMB 40 million for intangibles or other transactions (SAT Ann. 2016 No. 42, as updated by SAT Ann. 2022 No. 16); the FIP or corporate partner must also file the annual Related-Party Transaction Report Form (关联业务往来年度报告表) with the annual EIT return.

Economic substance scope: PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Art. 47; SAT Order No. 32) authorizes recharacterization of arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose; the Beneficial Ownership Doctrine (Ann. 2018 No. 9) requires the Hong Kong partner to demonstrate substantive business operations, personnel, premises, and decision-making functions; for remote BPO call-center models, substance is evidenced by mainland FIP employees, active management of client contracts, and risk-bearing capacity, while the Hong Kong parent entity must show real business activity in Hong Kong to retain DTA benefits; shell structures or artificial profit shifting to HK or a HAL (Hong Kong as agent-light) conduit will be disregarded.
