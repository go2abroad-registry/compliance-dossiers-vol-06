Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, acting through the Commissioner of Inland Revenue; electronic tax portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk). The Business Registration Office operates under the IRD pursuant to the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

Step 2: Within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, apply for Business Registration via eTAX or at the IRD Business Registration Office. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible

Available election forms

None comparable to a check-the-box election. A Hong Kong partnership is statutorily fiscally transparent: no profits tax is charged at partnership level; each partner is assessable on their share of Hong Kong-source partnership profits under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), subject to the territorial source principle. Hong Kong law does not permit a partnership to elect entity-level taxation. Individual partners resident in Hong Kong may elect Personal Assessment under Part VII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but that election does not alter the partnership's pass-through classification. There is no domestic hybrid-mismatch election regime in Hong Kong.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong has no general indirect tax; the only indirect duties are those under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), which do not apply to executive-search services).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - there is no indirect tax registration threshold in Hong Kong. The only statutory registration trigger is the commencement of a business in Hong Kong under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong because there is no VAT or GST to zero-rate. For profits tax purposes, cross-border executive-search fees may be treated as non-Hong-Kong-source offshore income if the services are performed, negotiated, and delivered wholly outside Hong Kong; eligibility depends on the substantive location of the headhunting work, not merely on the invoicing address or foreign client status.

  • No Hong Kong customs export declaration, Bill of Entry, or Bill of Lading is required for cross-border executive-search services, because no physical goods are exported and services are not subject to customs duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).
  • For an offshore-income claim, maintain: (a) the signed executive-search retainer agreement with the foreign client showing the engagement's place of performance; (b) itemised invoices issued by the Hong Kong partnership to the foreign client; (c) bank remittance receipts or SWIFT MT103 credit advices from the foreign client evidencing fee settlement; (d) work-product files, candidate sourcing records, interview notes, and time records evidencing that the headhunting activities physically occurred outside Hong Kong; and (e) email or meeting records demonstrating negotiation and execution of the engagement outside Hong Kong.
  • Hong Kong has no foreign-exchange controls and the IRD does not require an Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); a standard bank-in or remittance advice is sufficient evidence of payment.

Businesses may use paper or electronic invoices, and must keep records in English or Chinese sufficient to enable assessable profits to be readily ascertained under Section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

There is no VAT zero-rating to claw back because Hong Kong imposes no VAT or GST. If a partnership or partner claims offshore treatment and the IRD determines the services were performed in Hong Kong, the IRD may raise additional profits tax assessments under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). For incorrect returns without reasonable excuse, additional tax of up to 100% of the tax undercharged may be imposed under Section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and criminal penalties may apply for fraudulent tax evasion. The IRD may also impose business registration penalties under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). In audit, the IRD typically requests engagement contracts, time records, email communications, bank statements, travel records of personnel, evidence of the foreign client's location, and candidate interview records. No 180-day foreign-currency repatriation deadline applies in Hong Kong.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible and freely floating within the institutional window of 7.75 to 7.85 Hong Kong dollars per United States dollar under the Linked Exchange Rate System supervised by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. Hong Kong imposes no exchange controls; current-account and capital-account transactions are unrestricted for residents and non-residents, and foreign currency can be held, remitted, and settled without prior authorization. For the mainland Chinese nexus of this Foreign-Invested Partnership, the renminbi remains under a managed float with controls exercised by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and the People's Bank of China. Trade-related service fees, including executive-search retainer invoices, may be settled with genuine contracts and bank verification. Capital contributions into the mainland entity require FDI registration, a SAFE foreign-exchange registration certificate, and settlement through a designated domestic bank within the approved capital account limit. Profit distributions to offshore partners are subject to tax clearance and CRS reporting, but Hong Kong imposes no surrender or conversion requirement. The convertibility of CNY proceeds generated by the mainland FIP is conditional on supporting documentation and cannot be used to bypass SAFE capital controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. Export proceeds may be retained in any currency without compulsory exchange. For a mainland Chinese affiliated entity, SAFE requires goods-export foreign-currency receipts to be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange account within 90 days of the customs declaration date; conversion into CNY is voluntary after verified reporting through the Trade Monitoring System, and the enterprise may keep the foreign exchange in a settlement account up to the verified balance. Service-export proceeds, such as cross-border headhunting fees, follow the same repatriation and verification framework but must be declared under the service-trade foreign-exchange purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and People's Bank of China (PBOC) govern the mainland Chinese renminbi and capital-flow elements of this Foreign-Invested Partnership

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating is medium-high for the Hong Kong treasury node and low for the mainland China Foreign-Invested Partnership. In Hong Kong, licensed stored-value facility operators and money service operators under the HKMA and the Customs and Excise Department may hold operating balances and process cross-border payments; Wise B2B is acceptable only as an ancillary rail from the Hong Kong entity. In mainland China, the FIP cannot legally maintain its primary corporate settlement account with an overseas fintech EMI because SAFE and PBOC require corporate settlement, foreign-exchange verification, and tax declaration to be performed only by domestic designated banks. Therefore, regulatory capital injections, tax payments, and trade-verified disbursements must never be routed through Wise or any unlicensed EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 5 million in aggregate specified transactions triggers Local File preparation; global consolidated group revenue of HKD 680 million or more triggers Master File preparation, in accordance with the Inland Revenue Ordinance and DIPN 58.

Economic substance scope: The FIP must demonstrate real economic substance in Hong Kong. For the 2023 foreign-sourced income exemption (FSIE) and treaty benefits, the IRD will examine whether the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - such as candidate sourcing, reference checking, placement contract negotiation, fee invoicing and management decisions - are actually performed in Hong Kong. The FIP must keep fixed office premises in Hong Kong, have local employees/decision-makers, and maintain complete records proving the economic substance; a shell headhunting FIP with no CIGA is outside the exemption and full Profits Tax will apply.
