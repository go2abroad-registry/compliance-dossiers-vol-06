Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no minimum paid-up capital, share capital, or blocked escrow account requirement for a Foreign-Invested Limited Partnership registered under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap 37). Capital contributions are fixed contractually in the limited partnership agreement and are payable to the partnership bank account as committed; they are not subject to any company-law capital maintenance or escrow rules. The commercial capital of the partners is a private contractual matter between the general partner and the limited partners.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable - a pure company-secretarial and registered-agent office is not subject to an industrial Civil Defense fire permit. The premises must nevertheless comply with the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap 95) for fire safety and means of escape, and with the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap 509) for workplace safety, emergency lighting, and safe egress.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: GovHK Tax and Duties / IRD e-Tax (www.ird.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

A Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with non-resident partners is not a separate legal person under Hong Kong law; nevertheless, it must be registered for tax. The responsible partner or the registered agent under the offshore secretarial scope must apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance, Cap. 310. The application is submitted via the GovHK electronic tax registration service or the prescribed paper application. Mandatory attachments include: (a) a completed application signed by all general partners; (b) a certified copy of the partnership deed; (c) evidence of the Hong Kong registered address or place of business; (d) certified copies of non-resident partners passport or corporate register extracts; and (e) if applicable, the Limited Partnership Certificate issued by the Companies Registry. The IRD issues a Business Registration Number and a certificate valid for one or three years. For profits tax, the IRD will open a file for the partnership and issue tax returns to the designated representative partner. Each non-resident partner is separately required to file a Taxpayer Particulars Statement or profit tax individual assessment in respect of their distributable partnership share, and the IRD will issue a tax reference number to each partner where none previously exists.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible — Under Hong Kong territorial profits tax, a partnership is a transparent entity. The Foreign-Invested Partnership is not a separate taxable person; each partner is assessed to profits tax on their proportionate share, subject to the source principle. Profits derived from, arising in, or deemed to arise in Hong Kong are chargeable; genuine offshore income may fall outside the Hong Kong tax net.

Available election forms

None — Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. The FIP and its partners may, however, elect for the two-tiered profits tax rate applicable to unincorporated businesses for the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits, and non-resident partners may claim treaty relief or unilateral tax credit under applicable Double Taxation Agreements. No entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Hong Kong imposes no value-added tax (VAT), goods and services tax (GST), or retail sales and use tax. Limited excise or dutiable commodity duties apply to a closed list of goods under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — no VAT/GST registration threshold exists. A Dutiable Commodities License may be required for dealing in liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, or methyl alcohol, but that is not a general tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong because there is no value-added tax to zero-rate. For profits tax purposes, export income may be treated as offshore sourced only if the substantive trading operations, including contract negotiation, conclusion, and execution, are performed outside Hong Kong. For customs purposes, all exports of dutiable or controlled goods must be declared under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations, Cap. 60, and any claim of offshore sourcing must be substantiated by business records, correspondence, and board documentation.

  • Commercial invoice issued by the Hong Kong partnership to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list itemizing the export consignment
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from Hong Kong
  • Electronic export trade declaration submitted to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department via the designated Government Electronic Trade Services or Customs clearing facility
  • Original purchase orders, contracts, and payment instructions evidencing the transaction chain

None — Hong Kong does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or transactional e-invoicing submission to the Inland Revenue Department. Electronic invoices are commercially accepted, but are not subject to government clearance.

Hong Kong does not operate VAT, so no VAT clawback or export zero-rating penalty applies. However, if a taxpayer claims offshore profits treatment and the Inland Revenue Department reverses that classification on audit, the full profit becomes chargeable to profits tax, with additional tax, interest, and penalties up to three times the amount of undercharged tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For customs, false declarations, omitted export declarations, or failure to keep supporting records can expose the partnership and its registered agent to fines and prosecution under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains full current and capital account convertibility for both residents and non-residents. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and pegged within a band of 7.75 to 7.85 against the US dollar. There are no surrender requirements, no prior approvals for inward or outward remittances, and no restrictions on profit repatriation for Hong Kong-incorporated entities. However, due to the parent sovereign role of the People's Republic of China, cross-border flows between mainland China and Hong Kong are subject to the foreign exchange controls of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) in the mainland. This means a Chinese foreign-invested partnership (FIP) receiving capital contributions from a mainland resident or entity must ensure that the underlying remittance is properly registered under SAFE's FDI or ODI rules, and that each transfer is tagged with the correct payment purpose code. For the Hong Kong entity itself, all banking transactions are unencumbered; yet if the partnership is viewed as an extension of a mainland commercial operation, the bank may require evidence of SAFE registration for capital injections, and shareholders' dividends or distributions sent back to mainland China will trigger a withholding tax analysis and a cross-border remittance review under Circular 37, CIT 2017 No. 2, and the applicable double-taxation arrangement. The practical hurdles for profit remittance from Hong Kong to mainland are minimal from the Hong Kong side, but the mainland receiving entity must present a valid tax clearance and a formal board resolution before the inbound credit is accepted. Overall, the jurisdiction offers pristine currency convertibility at the sovereign level, but any interaction with mainland China introduces a secondary layer of administrative compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable within Hong Kong - no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds received by a Hong Kong entity. A Hong Kong business may retain foreign currency in any form, freely use it for onward payments, or convert it without any obligation to surrender to the HKMA. However, if the same entity is considered a resident exporter for mainland China purposes (e.g., a parent FIP in Shenzhen collecting export receivables in the mainland), then the mainland SAFE rules require those export proceeds to be repatriated back to the mainland within the prescribed time window (generally 30 to 90 days depending on customs classification) and the exporter must convert or hold the foreign currency in its foreign exchange settlement account in accordance with the prevailing margins and quotas set by SAFE. Since the target entity here is a Hong Kong offshore FS provider, no surrender rules are imposed by the HKMA.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) - while Hong Kong operates under the Linked Exchange Rate System with no statutory foreign exchange control, the HKMA remains the de facto monetary and financial stability authority, ensuring the free convertibility of the Hong Kong dollar and overseeing the licensed banking ecosystem.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Hong Kong permits licensed fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) to operate under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. However, for a regulated entity engaged in registered agent and offshore secretarial services, the core banking relationship cannot be substituted by an EMI. Fintech EMIs such as Neat, Airwallex, and Currenxie can provide ancillary transaction accounts and foreign exchange sweep tools, especially for receiving service fees from global clients via credit card or wire. Yet they generally do not offer escrow-grade custodial services, commercial letters of credit, or physical banking relationship management, and they often have lower transaction thresholds and faster account freezes under enhanced AML scrutiny. Moreover, the HKMA's banking etiquette expects a TCSP-licensed entity to maintain one primary relationship with a licensed, tier-1 commercial bank that can underwrite the partnership structure. Therefore, the fintech EMI route is viable only as a supplementary collection rail, not as a substitute for the local commercial banking backbone, and any EMI onboarding must still complete full KYC and pay a institutional onboarding fee.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Foreign-Invested Partnership in Hong Kong receiving capital contributions or loan proceeds from mainland China, the compliant routing begins with the mainland investor obtaining a foreign exchange settlement notice from the local SAFE branch under the foreign-invested partnership equity subscription framework. The remittance must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code '722019' (capital injection for partnership) or '621011' (repayment of loans borrowed from overseas) as applicable, with the remitter being the mainland entity's own account and the beneficiary being the Hong Kong FIP's named escrow account at a tier-1 bank. The Hong Kong receiving bank will require a copy of the partnership agreement, the capital verification report issued by a Hong Kong certified public accountant within 30 days of receipt, and the original FDI or ODI registration certificate if the mainland investor is a Chinese company or individual. If the capital is injected by way of a shareholder loan rather than an equity contribution, the loan agreement must be stamped and the interest rate must fall within the SAFE-prescribed band, and the Hong Kong partnership must register the loan under its corporate ledger to avoid any deferred tax or transfer-pricing challenge. Once the funds are credited, the partnership may also need to apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a certificate of resident status if subsequent interest payments to the mainland are to be protected under the Hong Kong-China double taxation arrangement.For the registered-agent and secretarial-services business, outgoing payments typically consist of professional fees to sub-agents, regulatory filing fees, and administrative expenses. The preferred clearing rail is direct SWIFT MT103 transfers from the partnership's Hong Kong bank account to third-party service providers, with the payment narrative clearly citing the underlying invoice number and service description to avoid clearing delays. If the partnership is used as a settlement hub for mainland clients procuring offshore incorporation services, the bank may also allow the use of a commercial letter of credit (LC) to support a trade transaction where the beneficiary is a Chinese supplier of corporate seal or compliance documentation; however, given the low physical goods content, banks will likely view an LC request as anomalous and may instead recommend a documentary collection (D/P) or straightforward wire. For recurring subscription fees collected from overseas clients via credit card, the partnership should arrange a merchant acquiring gateway that routes to its local bank, and the bank will monitor chargeback ratios and the merchant category code (MCC) for professional services to ensure the aggregate volume is consistent with the stated ISIC 8299 activity. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Hong Kong permits licensed fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) to operate under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance. However, for a regulated entity engaged in registered agent and offshore secretarial services, the core banking relationship cannot be substituted by an EMI. Fintech EMIs such as Neat, Airwallex, and Currenxie can provide ancillary transaction accounts and foreign exchange sweep tools, especially for receiving service fees from global clients via credit card or wire. Yet they generally do not offer escrow-grade custodial services, commercial letters of credit, or physical banking relationship management, and they often have lower transaction thresholds and faster account freezes under enhanced AML scrutiny. Moreover, the HKMA's banking etiquette expects a TCSP-licensed entity to maintain one primary relationship with a licensed, tier-1 commercial bank that can underwrite the partnership structure. Therefore, the fintech EMI route is viable only as a supplementary collection rail, not as a substitute for the local commercial banking backbone, and any EMI onboarding must still complete full KYC and pay a institutional onboarding fee.
