Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and business registration portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk); Business Registration Office e-services are accessed through the Business Registration section of the IRD website (https://www.ird.gov.hk).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Determine registration obligation. A foreign-invested partnership (FIP) that carries on business in Hong Kong SAR must register under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business; there is no minimum capital requirement and foreign non-resident ownership does not exempt the partnership. Step 2: Prepare partnership particulars, including all partners' legal names, addresses, offshore tax identification numbers where available, business commencement date, principal place of business in Hong Kong, and a description of the virtual office/CMRA mail scanning activity. Step 3: Submit a Business Registration Application to the IRD Business Registration Office, either authenticated through eTAX using a recognised digital certificate or iAM Smart login, or by paper. Step 5: For profits tax purposes, IRD will allocate a Tax Reference Number, an 8-digit tax file number, to the partnership. Because the partnership is fiscally transparent, a foreign non-resident partner is not required to obtain a separate BRN merely by being a passive partner; however, if that partner separately carries on business in Hong Kong through a branch, it must register separately. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible; under section 22 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a partnership is not taxed as a separate legal taxpayer. Each partner is assessable on its agreed share of the partnership's profits. The territorial scope under section 14 applies, so only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are chargeable. For a Hong Kong-based virtual office/mail scanning operation, scanning and administrative services physically performed in Hong Kong will generally produce Hong Kong-source profits, irrespective of foreign client location.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong SAR does not operate a check-the-box election, partnership classification election, or hybrid mismatch entity election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Pass-through treatment is mandatory for partnerships. If the FIP is constituted as a limited partnership under the Limited Partnership Ordinance (Cap. 321) or as a limited partnership fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637), the same mandatory transparent treatment applies for profits tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales and use tax regime; Hong Kong SAR imposes no general indirect tax. Excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) apply only to specified goods, not to virtual office or mail scanning services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; HKD 0. There is no economic-turnover threshold, VAT registration threshold, or indirect tax registration requirement in Hong Kong SAR.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST in Hong Kong SAR and therefore no statutory 0% export VAT rate or export VAT refund mechanism. Goods exported from Hong Kong are outside any general indirect tax; services supplied by the FIP to overseas clients are not zero-rated because there is no VAT charge. Profits tax exposure remains governed by the territorial source rule in section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China runs a hybrid convertibility regime. Current-account receipts derived from the FIP's virtual-office, mail-receiving and scanning service fees are broadly convertible into CNY once the designated bank verifies a real underlying service contract, invoice, and collection record. Capital-account movements, including inbound foreign-partner capital contributions, remain subject to SAFE registration, avoidance of the foreign-investment negative list, and the use of a dedicated FDI capital settlement account. The Hong Kong SAR side does not apply exchange controls and the HKD is freely convertible under the linked exchange rate system with the US dollar, but that does not remove the onshore FIP's SAFE obligations. For profit remittance to non-resident partners, the FIP must complete the annual statutory audit, compute partner-attributable income, settle any corporate income tax (or pass-through withholding in instances where the FIP is fiscally transparent), satisfy the 10% non-treaty dividend withholding tax if applicable, and produce the bank-validated tax payment certificate, audited financial statements, partnership agreement and partner identity documents. The practical hurdles are documentary: discrepancies between the MOFCOM foreign-investment filing, SAFE FX registration data and the bank's commercial evidence can delay conversion, and Chinese banks will not convert funds purely on an expectation of future service revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a PRC-resident FIP providing export-type mail-scanning services, there is no mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio under the current SAFE current-account framework; the FIP may choose to retain USD/HKD in an onshore current-account FX settlement account or convert to CNY at a designated bank. However, all export or cross-border service proceeds must be repatriated to an onshore designated bank on or before the contractual payment due date, and where no contract fixes that due date, within 90 days of the invoice date; any retention or conversion must be supported by the service contract, merchant settlement data, and a matching SAFE trade-in-services declaration. The bank applies no conversion ceiling, but amounts outstanding beyond the due date become delinquent and may trigger SAFE trade-monitoring review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBoC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) separately regulates the HKD banking system but imposes no foreign-exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW as a primary treasury rail for the onshore FIP. Chinese banking law and the SAFE/PBoC licensing architecture do not permit offshore digital EMI platforms, including Hong Kong virtual banks or neobank providers, to open lawful current or capital-account facilities for a mainland-registered foreign-invested partnership. An offshore EMI may only serve as an auxiliary card-acquiring gateway for Stripe-recurring and credit-card income if it operates through a PBoC-licensed onshore payment institution or a Chinese-incorporated licensed bank, with the funds swept daily to the licensed onshore designated bank via SWIFT/MT103 messaging and the full client-merchant identity chain disclosed; retaining an operational treasury balance offshore beyond the permitted foreign-currency account regime could be re-characterised as illegal capital flight. For the capital-injection side, EMIs are categorically not viable.

Chinese banking law and the SAFE/PBoC licensing architecture do not permit offshore digital EMI platforms, including Hong Kong virtual banks or neobank providers, to open lawful current or capital-account facilities for a mainland-registered foreign-invested partnership. An offshore EMI may only serve as an auxiliary card-acquiring gateway for Stripe-recurring and credit-card income if it operates through a PBoC-licensed onshore payment institution or a Chinese-incorporated licensed bank, with the funds swept daily to the licensed onshore designated bank via SWIFT/MT103 messaging and the full client-merchant identity chain disclosed; retaining an operational treasury balance offshore beyond the permitted foreign-currency account regime could be re-characterised as illegal capital flight. For the capital-injection side, EMIs are categorically not viable.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$55 million in aggregate controlled transactions triggers a Local File under IRD DIPN 59/2020; an HK member of a multinational group with consolidated revenue at or above HK$220 million is expected to prepare a Master File; transactions below HK$55 million still require contemporaneous file if the IRD issues a notice. A CbCR is required when the ultimate parent group has annual consolidated revenue of HK$6.8 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the OECD-aligned source doctrine embodied in IRO Division 4AA and the anti-avoidance sections s.61A and s.61B requires real economic substance. For a virtual-office/mail-scanning CMRA, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are the physical receipt of mail, scanning and digital archiving, record-keeping, monitoring of client instructions, contract administration, and the taking of management decisions. If these CIGA are performed in mainland China rather than Hong Kong, and the Hong Kong FIP acts only as a mail-forwarding mailbox, the IRD may treat the FIP as a front entity, assess no Hong Kong profits, or reallocate the fees to the mainland principal; conversely, the mainland principal may be treated as carrying on a Hong Kong permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / false or misleading profits tax return

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.80(1), a person who fails to file a return or furnishes an incorrect return is liable on conviction to a fine of HK$10,000 and to an additional fine of three times the amount of tax undercharged. Under IRO s.82A, an intentional tax-evasion scheme is punishable on summary conviction by a fine of HK$50,000 plus three times the undercharge and imprisonment for 3 years; on indictment, by a fine of up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment for 7 years.

Director personal liability: The general partner, investment manager, or responsible director who signs the return is personally and jointly liable for the undercharged tax, and the corporate veil is pierced where the FIP is used as a sham. The court may order civil restitution of profit distributions funded by the underpaid tax, issue travel bans and blacklisting under Hong Kong Immigration control, and impose disqualification orders of up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).
Artificial transfer-pricing and profit-stripping through related-party service contracts

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61A and s.61B empower the IRD to disregard artificial transactions and to make transfer-pricing adjustments. A notice may reallocate revenue and expenses on an arm's length basis, resulting in an additional tax liability. Under IRO s.80, failure to keep contemporaneous transfer-pricing documents attracts a Level 3 fine and threefold additional tax; reckless or intentional TP manipulation is prosecutable under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Directors and managing partners who knowingly authorize non-arm's-length transactions may become jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and for consequential interest. Courts may pierce the partnership or corporate veil, order repayment of dividends and distributions declared out of stripped profits, impose asset freezes, issue departure restrictions, and, where cross-border fraud is proven, apply sanctions that include extradition under the PRC-HK mutual legal assistance framework.
Shell-entity abuse / no economic substance / failure to notify chargeability

Statutory sanction: IRO s.51C and s.80(2) penalize failure to keep adequate business records and failure to notify chargeability within the statutory period; on summary conviction, fines of up to HK$100,000 apply, and the IRD may raise default assessments under s.59(3). The IRD can also invoke s.61A to nullify the arrangement and impose additional tax of up to 100% of the tax avoided; serious cases are prosecuted under s.82A.

Director personal liability: Where a director or partner uses a shell FIP to conceal the real beneficiary, the veil is pierced: the individual is personally liable for all unpaid tax, penalties and interest; the court may disqualify the director under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for up to 15 years, issue a blacklisting/travel restriction notice with Hong Kong Immigration, and if the conduct meets tax-fraud thresholds, impose imprisonment of up to 7 years and order civil restitution of all moved assets.
