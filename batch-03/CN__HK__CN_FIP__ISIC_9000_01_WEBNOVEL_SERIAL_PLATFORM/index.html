Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Hong Kong limited partnership under the Limited Partnerships Ordinance (Cap. 37). The partners must agree on capital contributions in the Partnership Deed, and contributions may be made in cash, property or services. No blocked escrow account or mandatory capital lock-up is required; however, the partnership must open and maintain operational bank accounts in Hong Kong for the segregation of partnership assets and for tax or regulatory tracking.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local county/district tax services bureau where the Foreign-Invested Partnership is registered, with online filing through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau; for any Hong Kong establishment, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD), Business Registration Office, through the eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

1. Register the Foreign-Invested Partnership (FIP) with the local Administration for Market Regulation under the PRC Foreign Investment Law, Partnership Enterprise Law and Market Entity Registration Regulations to obtain a Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code; that Unified Social Credit Code is the FIP's statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for mainland China. 2. Within 30 days of the Business License date, complete tax information confirmation through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau, registering the FIP for VAT, stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax and education surcharges; no separate paper tax registration certificate is issued because the Business License and Unified Social Credit Code function as the tax registration certificate. 3. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Pass_Through_Eligible - For PRC Enterprise Income Tax, the FIP is transparent and not taxed at the partnership level; taxable income is allocated to partners and taxed at the partner level (corporate partners under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; individual partners under the Individual Income Tax Law). For PRC VAT, the FIP is a taxpayer and must invoice in its own name. For Hong Kong profits tax, a partnership is also transparent, with each partner assessed on their respective share of partnership profits.

Available election forms

No check-the-box election exists in mainland China; an FIP cannot elect to be taxed as a corporation for income tax, and no hybrid-mismatch entity classification election is available. The only material elections are the VAT General Taxpayer versus Small-Scale Taxpayer election for turnover below RMB 5,000,000, and, for foreign partners, the application for tax treaty benefits (e.g., a Hong Kong resident partner claiming relief under an applicable double tax arrangement). Hong Kong also has no check-the-box election; profits tax follows the actual partnership structure and profit-sharing arrangement.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border zero-rating is claimable if the WebNovel serial platform is characterized as a cross-border taxable service (for example a cultural-creative service or digital/information service) or as an export of intangible digital content supplied to non-resident users, consumed entirely outside mainland China, and paid for in foreign currency directly or through Apple App Store / Google Play. The statutory basis includes Caishui [2016] No. 36, the Measures for Administration of Tax Refund (Exemption) for Taxable Services under Zero VAT Rate, and the applicable STA cross-border service zero-rating/exemption catalogue. The FIP must file the zero-rating or export-refund claim in the Electronic Tax Bureau and maintain evidence that no mainland user consumed the service.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration of the People's Republic of China - required only for physical exports such as printed books or promotional merchandise; not applicable to pure digital downloads.
  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - required only for physical export shipments; for digital services, substitute with server/traffic logs and IP-geolocation reports proving overseas consumption.
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt record generated through the SAFE trade foreign-exchange monitoring system, or Apple/Google app-store payout statements, evidencing collection and repatriation of foreign-currency proceeds.
  • Cross-border service contract, subscription terms, or digital license/order records together with the zero-rated digital e-invoice and the cross-border service zero-rating/export-refund declaration submitted via the Electronic Tax Bureau.
  • Transaction-level revenue ledger and user-jurisdiction report from the WebNovel platform showing user country codes, payment rails, consumption timestamps and app-store settlement amounts.

The FIP must issue Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform; invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time, and every digital e-invoice carries a unique invoice code and anti-counterfeit QR code. The former physical tax-control equipment method has been replaced by the fully digitalized e-invoice platform in covered regions. For Apple IAP and Google Play Billing transactions, the FIP must reconcile the app-store settlement statements, transaction-level extracts and payout records with the digital e-invoice reports, preserving evidence of the foreign-currency sales and the applicable 0% or 6% VAT treatment.

If zero-rating is claimed without adequate evidence of overseas consumption or foreign-currency repatriation, the tax authority will reverse the 0% rate and assess the applicable VAT (generally 6%), along with late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and recovery of any refunded input VAT. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict convertibility separation between the current and capital accounts. For the Foreign-Invested Partnership, ordinary current-account transactions such as web-novel subscription revenue, royalty payments, and host-app platform settlements are generally convertible once the transaction is genuine and supported by a written business contract and corresponding service-trade declaration. Capital-account items, however, require SAFE registration, a designated bank capital-settlement account, and a qualified business purpose. The partnership is not permitted to convert RMB freely into foreign currency for portfolio outbound investment, offshore deposits, or lending to affiliated parties unless such activity is explicitly approved. Profit and dividend remittances to the Hong Kong corporate partner may proceed only after the annual partnership financial audit is completed, relevant PRC taxes covering partner-level income tax and any withholding tax for non-resident partners are settled, and SAFE current-account verification is completed. In practice, the convertibility bottleneck is not usually the exchange rate movement but the evidentiary burden placed on platform revenue, IP-licensing contracts, and UBO-matched statement trails. As such, the FIP should maintain an onshore RMB operating account at a SAFE-designated bank, a foreign-currency capital settlement account, and a separate Hong Kong dollar or US-dollar collection account at an HKMA-authorized bank to enable clean clearing rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE regulatory framework, the FIP is not subject to a mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds from digital content services. The statutory regime requires all current-account foreign exchange receipts arising from cross-border provision of services to be collected in a designated current-account settlement account and repatriated onshore within the applicable SAFE-monitored deadline, which for services is normally aligned with the invoice maturity date or the contract payment terms and is monitored through the SAFE cross-border income and payment monitoring system. If the FIP receives goods-trade-related export proceeds from physical books or promotional merchandise, the default inward remittance deadline is 30 days from the date of collection; 100% of the foreign currency may be retained in a foreign-currency account or converted into RMB at the partnership's discretion, provided the underlying transaction has been reported in the trade receipts registration system. Any approved offshore retention is strictly quota-limited and conditioned on prior SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); for Hong Kong treasury channels, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the licensing and monetary oversight authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut Business are not licensed by SAFE to hold or route RMB on behalf of a mainland-incorporated Foreign-Invested Partnership. They cannot issue a lawful onshore settlement account nor execute capital-account conversion for the FIP's foreign-currency capital contributions. Their viable role is limited to offshore subsidiary cash management, such as collecting app-store remittances from Apple and Google via a Hong Kong or Singapore entity and then paying the FIP under an intercompany licensing or subscription-services agreement through a SAFE-designated bank. The statutory core treasury function must remain with licensed domestic commercial banks and HKMA-authorized banks. For a Hong Kong treasury vehicle, licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank and Mox are permissible but are not substitutes for the onshore FIP capital account. Overall fintech EMI viability for this group treasury is Low to Medium: legal for offshore legs but legally barred from servicing the onshore FIP entity's statutory FX and settlement accounts.

The Hong Kong partner will execute a SWIFT MT103 to the FIP's foreign-currency capital settlement account at the designated domestic bank. Tax evasion / underreporting platform revenue
