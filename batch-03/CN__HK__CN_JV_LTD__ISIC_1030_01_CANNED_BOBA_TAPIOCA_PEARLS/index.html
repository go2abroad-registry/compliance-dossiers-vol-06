Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with the online electronic portal being the STA National Electronic Tax Authority e-Tax platform; customs and export clearance administration is performed by the General Administration of Customs (GACC) through the China International Trade Single Window; for Hong Kong SAR aspects, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the competent tax authority, serviced through eTax and the Companies Registry for Business Registration Ordinance purposes.

E-government filing portal

In mainland China, the foreign investor and local partner first obtain a business license for the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). SAMR license data is automatically transmitted to STA; within 30 days the JV must complete tax registration and taxpayer qualification filings in the e-Tax portal. The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license is the national Taxpayer Identification Number for VAT and Corporate Income Tax. The JV then files for VAT general taxpayer status or small-scale-taxpayer treatment, applies for export-tax-refund status, and completes customs registration with GACC, obtaining the customs declarant registration code. Because the scope involves export of canned food, the JV must also complete GACC registration of export food manufacturing facilities. A foreign non-resident shareholder without a Chinese establishment does not normally receive a standalone PRC TIN; China-source income payable to that shareholder is reportable by the JV as withholding agent, using the foreign entity's home-jurisdiction TIN and a Certificate of Tax Residence when claiming treaty benefits under the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation. If a Hong Kong branch or entity is separately formed, it must file a Business Registration application with the IRD within one month of commencement, obtain a Business Registration Certificate and Business Registration Number, and complete employer registration if it hires staff; the Hong Kong Companies Registry provides the company registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed as a stand-alone corporation; it has no pass-through feature. Hong Kong territorial-source principles may additionally apply only if the JV or its Hong Kong branch derives profits sourced in Hong Kong, in which case Hong Kong Profits Tax is assessable on that separately identifiable source under the territorial-source rules of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Available election forms

None under PRC income tax law; there is no check-the-box election, partnership classification election, or hybrid-mismatch election in China. The JV may elect to be registered as a VAT general taxpayer (mandatory if 12-month taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; optional below that threshold), may elect to apply for export-tax-refund status with STA, and may make a claim for treaty benefits or Hong Kong offshore profit treatment. These are administrative status elections and claims, not entity tax-classification elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 0 for entity-level tax registration; every PRC enterprise must register for VAT after SAMR license issuance. Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration applies when 12-month taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; monthly taxable sales up to CNY 100,000 for small-scale taxpayers are VAT-exempt under the current policy through 31 December 2027. No indirect-tax registration threshold exists in Hong Kong.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported canned boba tapioca pearl products qualify for PRC zero-rated VAT treatment as exported goods under the PRC VAT Law if all of the following conditions are satisfied: goods are physically exported from mainland China and pass customs clearance; the JV is the exporter or consignor shown on the GACC customs export declaration; the JV holds valid VAT and export-tax-refund registration; the export sale is made to an overseas buyer under a genuine commercial contract; the JV retains the complete electronic export-file trail and upstream VAT special invoices; and the export consideration is collected in foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System unless an exemption or extension is approved. Digital and remote-services zero-rating is not applicable to the canned goods manufacturing scope. In Hong Kong, there is no VAT or GST, so export zero-rating does not arise; exports are instead offshore in Hong Kong Profits Tax terms if the profits are not sourced in Hong Kong.

  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing that the goods have left China

China operates real-time fiscal clearance through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform under the full digital electronic invoice framework. The JV must issue VAT invoices electronically through the national platform; each invoice receives a national electronic invoice code, a unique serial number, and a digital seal only after acceptance by STA servers. Invoice data, including the buyer's USCC, commodity tax classification codes, VAT rate, and amounts, are transmitted to STA in real time and automatically matched with VAT returns, input purchase data, and GACC customs declarations. For export sales, the JV must file customs declarations through the China International Trade Single Window; the Single Window shares clearance data with STA for export zero-rating and refund matching. Private standalone invoicing software and local tax-control devices are not substitutes for STA-issued digital e-invoice records.

The PRC tax authorities and GACC conduct post-clearance matching between export declarations, VAT e-invoices, input credits, and foreign-exchange receipts. If an export zero-rating or refund claim is not substantiated, or if foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days without an approved SAFE extension, STA may claw back the refunded VAT, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax, assess a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of tax underpaid, suspend or revoke the exporter's export-tax-refund treatment, and refer the case to the tax police for criminal investigation if false export or false VAT-special-invoice fraud is found. The exporter must retain the complete export documentation file for at least 10 years under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent export-tax-refund conduct can also lead to export-control blacklisting, customs complaint procedures, and, in serious criminal cases, imprisonment and fines under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with RMB convertible on the current account after documented real trade or service verification, but strictly controlled on the capital account. For this Sino-Foreign JV, inbound FDI registered capital must be routed through a SAFE-registered capital account at a designated Tier-1 domestic bank; conversion into RMB requires the investment be reported on SAFE's RCPMIS system and the bank to complete a capital-confirmation check. Export receipts, once declared under goods-trade monitoring, may be retained in a foreign-currency account or converted into RMB without mandatory surrender, but must be repatriated to mainland China within the regulatory collection cycle (generally 90 days from customs declaration, extendable to 180 days with commercial justification). Dividend remittances are permissible only after audit, tax clearance, and SAFE/account-bank verification of distributable profits. The Hong Kong shareholder may hold and pay CNH/HKD freely, but every cross-border capital movement entering the mainland JV is subject to PBOC/SAFE approval and purpose-code declaration. Therefore, RMB convertibility is effectively full for current-account trade transactions and conditionally medium/high for capital-account transactions, with each outflow subject to documentary underwriting, profit sustainability and tax compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory foreign-currency surrender ratio currently applies to goods-trade exports under SAFE's trade-facilitation framework. The JV may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in a domestic foreign-currency current account or convert them into RMB at the interbank market rate. However, all export receipts must be repatriated to mainland China, reported to the bank under SAFE's goods-trade FX receipt/payment monitoring system, and matched to Customs declarations; standard collection should be received within 90 days of export declaration, with up to 180 days allowed on application where commercial payment terms justify the extension.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland operating entity; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong treasury/settlement leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the mainland JV as a primary treasury rail. Offshore fintech/EMIs are not licensed deposit-taking or FX-settlement banks in mainland China, and SAFE/PBOC regulations do not permit them to open RMB current/capital accounts or process SAFE-verified cross-border payments on behalf of the JV. They may be used by the Hong Kong parent as a multi-currency collection wallet or for non-mainland working capital, but every inbound leg into the PRC JV must still land at a PBOC-supervised bank and be reported under the exact SAFE trade/payment code. An unlicensed EMI routing FDI or export proceeds would constitute illegal forex settlement under Articles 39/46 of the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File and Local File become mandatory in China when any of the following thresholds are crossed: annual related-party transaction value of RMB 1 billion or more (Master File); related-party tangible asset transfers of RMB 200 million or more; related-party intangible asset transfers of RMB 100 million or more; related-party financial asset transactions of RMB 100 million or more (Local File). The CNY-renminbi thresholds are prescribed under PRC transfer-pricing regulations (Announcement of the State Administration of Taxation No. 6 of 2016) and require contemporaneous documentation, benchmark studies, and economic analysis to be prepared no later than May 31 following the tax year.

Economic substance scope: Chinese GAAR and the Beneficial Ownership Doctrine apply to any transaction lacking genuine economic substance; arrangements using a Hong Kong mailbox company to book boba-manufacturing profits, claims for DTA-reduced withholding, or off-shore financing without Hong Kong CIGA will be re-characterised, denied treaty benefits, and may trigger a 50% penalty. Core Income-Generating Activities for canned quick-cook tapioca pearls include physical sourcing of raw agricultural tapioca starch, operation of food-safety production plants in the Mainland, quality control, risk of raw material cost fluctuations, demand forecasting, packaging design, and quarantine certification; for the Hong Kong affiliate the CIGA must include real transactional activity, locally managed distribution, invoicing, inventory decision-making, and ancillary financial functions performed by resident full-time employees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Intentional Tax Evasion and Fraudulent CIT Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes confiscation of evaded tax, an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, and, in serious cases, prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to 7 years and criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is a vehicle for fraud, courts will pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20(3); directors, supervisors and operational control persons who arranged the fictitious Hong Kong transactions are jointly and severally liable for undervaluation of tax debts; their personal assets in Mainland China and Hong Kong can be frozen, their names are placed on the tax blacklist imposing exit-entry travel bans, and custodial imprisonment for tax fraud is imposed under Criminal Law.
Shell Company and Tax Treaty Abuse Using Hong Kong DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: China's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47 and implementation rules) authorizes re-characterisation of the abusive arrangement, denial of treaty benefits, recomputation of income to the Mainland JV, late interest, and an administrative penalty of up to 50% of the additionally assessed tax; the HK shareholder may be disqualified from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance treaty-benefit rules.

Director personal liability: The veil will be pierced for fraudulent misuse of separate legal personality; directors and controlling shareholders are personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and its penalties; border control and blacklisting measures are imposed to enforce tax collection; if the shell is linked to laundering of proceeds from cross-border food-trade payments, directors face money-laundering criminal charges with imprisonment of not less than 5 years and up to 10 years under PRC Criminal Law Article 191.

Statutory sanction: PRC transfer-pricing regulations impose a reassessment at full statutory rates, imposition of the 5-year retrospective audit adjustment period, penalty surcharges under Tax Collection Law Article 64, and a 50% penalty on the underpaid tax for the failure to provide contemporaneous documentation; missing CIGA documentation also invalidates the Hong Kong treaty claim and exposes the JV to withholding shortfall of 3% to 5%.
