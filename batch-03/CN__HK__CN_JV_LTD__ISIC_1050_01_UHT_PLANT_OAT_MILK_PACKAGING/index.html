Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); mainland China online filing is performed through the STA's Electronic Tax Administration (e-tax) platform (chinatax.gov.cn). Hong Kong SAR, as the cross-border jurisdiction, is administered separately by the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR (IRD), with its own Business Registration regime.

        
E-government filing portal
Upon incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under PRC law, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues a Business License bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the entity's taxpayer identification number (TIN). Tax registration is integrated into the SAMR one-stop registration; the competent local STA office automatically completes the taxpayer record. If the automatic registration has not been completed, the JV must, within 30 days of the Business License date, apply for tax registration at the competent STA office. The JV must then complete the election or registration of VAT status: small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer. A foreign non-resident entity (including the Hong Kong SAR shareholder) that has no permanent establishment in mainland China but derives China-sourced income (dividends, interest, royalties, or capital gains) must complete a simplified non-resident enterprise tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Administration portal or through a withholding agent, receive a PRC non-resident taxpayer ID, and, if treaty protection is sought, file a treaty benefit claim with the STA under the mainland China-Hong Kong SAR tax arrangement.

Default tax classification

Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No turnover exemption for tax registration itself; every business entity conducting taxable sales must complete VAT tax registration.         Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under the PRC VAT legislation, goods exported from mainland China (and not prohibited from export) are taxable at 0%. A Sino-Foreign JV manufacturing UHT aseptic oat milk qualifies for 0% VAT on genuine outbound exports where title, risk and control are transferred to an overseas buyer, the goods physically leave PRC customs territory under customs supervision, the export sales are invoiced to the overseas customer, and the sales proceeds are collected in foreign currency or RMB under the lawful cross-border RMB trade settlement mechanism. The manufacturing exporter applies the exemption, credit, and refund method (exemption from VAT on output, credit of input VAT against domestic output, and refund of remaining input VAT). Qualifying offshore digital services, not applicable to this physical-goods scope, may be zero-rated only if the service is supplied to a recipient outside China and consumed entirely outside China, substantiated by contract, invoice and service-delivery evidence.

If the 0% rate or export VAT refund is claimed without complete and consistent documentation, fails the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation requirement, or is found fraudulent, the tax authority will deny the exemption or refund, claw back any refunded VAT, assess late-payment interest from the due date, and impose administrative fines of up to five times the tax under-collected or refunded (typically 100%-500% for fraudulent export tax refunds). Criminal liability for fraudulent export VAT refunds may be pursued under PRC Criminal Law, including imprisonment and fines for responsible persons, and the tax recovery limitation period is unlimited for fraudulent underpayments.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
Hong Kong operates as a wholly liberalised offshore special territory with no foreign exchange controls imposed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible and is managed under a linked exchange rate system pegged to the US dollar in a 7.75-7.85 band. Capital account and current account transactions are uniformly open; there is no distinction between resident and non-resident accounts, no surrender requirement on export proceeds, no repatriation ceiling on dividends or capital, and no approval filing for ordinary trade payments. The only formalities are accountability-based: banks must perform client due diligence, transaction monitoring, and suspicious transaction reporting under the AMLO and the HKMA Supervisory Policy Manual. In practice, a Hong Kong JV exporter can receive USD, EUR, RMB and CNH freely, hold foreign currency in any mix, and pay overseas suppliers without exchange control approval. For cross-border flows from the PRC parent, the effective constraint lies in the mainland SAFE regime: the outbound capital investment must be registered as ODI and supported by a tax certificate and audited financials. Once the funds clear into Hong Kong, FX conversion at commercial banks is unrestricted, and firms may access deliverable and non-deliverable hedging products without quotas.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
No mandatory surrender or conversion timeline applies in Hong Kong. Under HKMA's free convertibility framework, export proceeds received in any foreign currency may be retained indefinitely in HKD, USD, CNY/CNH or other currencies; the exporter may buy or sell foreign exchange at market rates through an authorised institution. If the exporting entity is a mainland manufacturing parent, the PRC SAFE current-account verification rules require export proceeds to be collected through the customs-declared trade settlement channel, with bank settlement records, but no statutory forced conversion ratio or deadline applies beyond the current account reconciliation cycle.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the PRC-side regulator for mainland-origin capital flows

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        For capital entering the Hong Kong JV from the mainland parent, the compliant route is: obtain SAFE ODI registration in the PRC; use a designated mainland bank to issue an outward remittance via SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code ODI and a reference to the MOFCOM certificate; transfer USD, CNH or HKD to the segregated Capital Escrow Account in Hong Kong. The receiving HK bank will credit the capital escrow account only after completing internal AML clearance. Upon receipt, a Hong Kong certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report; funds may then be switched to the operating account for capex, raw material procurement and payroll. However, for a Sino-Foreign JV with capital escrow, SAFE-registered inbound remittances, commercial letter of credit operations and UHT plant capex disbursements, pure fintech EMIs are not viable because they cannot open capital escrow accounts, cannot issue or confirm commercial LCs, and have restricted multi-currency clearing. Viability rating is low for primary banking; medium only as a supplemental payment rail for small customer receivables. Cross-border EMIs such as Airwallex or Currenxie may offer collection solutions, but the core capital and trade clearing must remain with a tier-1 bank in Hong Kong.

Turnover: Hong Kong law does not prescribe quantitative balance sheet, turnover or headcount thresholds for audit exemption. Under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622, every active Hong Kong incorporated company must appoint a certified public accountant as auditor and have its financial statements audited, regardless of size. The only exemption is for a dormant private company which has not begun business or has ceased business, has no significant accounting transactions, and has resolved to apply for exemption; an active manufacturing entity operating an aseptic UHT oat milk plant cannot rely on any small-company audit exemption. If the Hong Kong JV has a mainland Chinese corporate parent, the onshore entity's statutory audit obligations under PRC law and CASBE will apply separately to the underlying Chinese JV entity.
