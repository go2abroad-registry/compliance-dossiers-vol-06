Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China through the unified National Electronic Tax Administration platform (e-Tax); the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong SAR remains competent for any Hong Kong-source Profits Tax and Business Registration obligations of the Hong Kong investor

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is incorporated in the PRC and is therefore a PRC tax resident; upon obtaining its legal persona through the State Administration for Market Regulation, it is assigned an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code which simultaneously constitutes its enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier. The JV must complete initial tax registration in the e-Tax portal within 30 days after the issuance of its business license, filing its articles of association, incorporation certificate, registered address, legal representative, bank account details, and anticipated taxable activities. There is no separate VAT ID; the Unified Social Credit Code is used for VAT invoice issuance, Enterprise Income Tax filings, export tax rebate applications, and customs interactions. For a foreign non-resident entity that has no PRC establishment but derives PRC-source income, the foreign entity, or a qualified PRC withholding agent on its behalf, must register in the non-resident enterprise tax module of the e-Tax portal to obtain or be referenced by a non-resident taxpayer identification number before a withholding tax remittance is made. The Hong Kong investor should separately maintain its Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate if it commences a business in Hong Kong, but that registration is independent of the PRC JV's PRC tax registration.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered once annual taxable sales (excluding VAT) exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any consecutive 12-month period. Below that threshold, the JV may operate as a small-scale VAT taxpayer; general taxpayer status may be elected voluntarily. A foreign non-resident supplier with no PRC establishment has no registration threshold, but the PRC buyer must withhold VAT at the statutory rate on imported taxable services and cross-border supply of goods or services not covered by exemption.

The PRC mandates fully digital electronic invoicing under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. VAT-registered taxpayers must issue invoices through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which transmits each invoice in near-real time to the State Taxation Administration, assigns a unique digital invoice number and anti-counterfeiting QR code, and validates the invoice against the issuer's ledger. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC treats RMB as conditionally convertible: current-account trade payments are generally permitted against authentic documents, while capital-account transactions require specific SAFE approvals, registrations, and designated bank accounts. The Sino-Foreign JV will operate onshore in mainland China and therefore cannot enjoy Hong Kong's full convertibility; every inbound FDI capital contribution from the HK shareholder must be recorded with SAFE and deposited only into a capital settlement account. Export proceeds are repatriated into China through a designated foreign-exchange bank after customs declaration, and although conversion to RMB is permitted, the bank must verify the underlying trade. Profit remittances to the non-resident shareholder are current-account outflows once the annual audit and tax settlement are completed; the practical hurdle is documentary: the bank requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax payment certificates, board resolutions, and proof that all statutory reserves have been accumulated. Capital reductions, share transfers, liquidation proceeds and offshore loan repayments remain tightly controlled and cannot be executed without separate SAFE registration or approval. Therefore the JV must maintain strict fund segregation between capital, trade and operating flows to preserve auditability and avoid blocked remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated into mainland China through a designated bank within ninety (90) days from the customs export declaration date. There is currently zero mandatory conversion ratio; the JV may retain the receipts in an approved foreign-currency settlement account or convert them to RMB at the prevailing market rate through the designated bank. Every collection must be matched to the corresponding customs declaration, shipping documents, and commercial invoice; expected receipt periods beyond 90 days require advance SAFE registration for deferred export collection.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the mainland JV; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong treasury/investor vehicle.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW as a primary banking and clearing rail. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed as banks under the PBOC/SAFE framework and cannot open RMB capital settlement accounts, execute FDI capital verification, process SAFE-designated FX transactions, or provide onshore CNY clearing for the JV. Using such an EMI to receive export proceeds may violate collection and repatriation rules unless the funds are onward settled into the designated PRC bank account within 90 days. The Hong Kong treasury entity may use a licensed Hong Kong EMI or digital corporate account only for ancillary collection outside China, but every transfer to the mainland JV must be a SWIFT MT103 credited to a licensed Tier-1 bank account, not an internal EMI book transfer.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK investor must route the SWIFT MT103 from its Hong Kong corporate treasury account to the JV's SAFE-registered FDI Capital Settlement Account at the designated PRC Tier-1 bank. The payment instruction must clearly identify the JV name, SAFE FDI registration reference, the FDI capital contribution purpose code, and the supporting JV contract/board resolution reference. The receiving bank will verify the remittance against SAFE records, quarantine funds in the capital account, and permit release or FX settlement to RMB only for documented operating expenses, capex, wages, and procurement. A PRC-registered CPA firm must issue a capital verification report after receipt and file it with the Administration for Market Regulation. If the HK shareholder remits RMB instead of foreign currency, the transfer must clear through CIPS or SWIFT RMB with the PBOC cross-border RMB FDI filing reference, and the same capital verification applies.The JV will settle import payments for raw materials and packaging through three licensed clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer for post-shipment payments after matching import customs declarations, invoices, and the bank's SAFE trade verification; (2) irrevocable at-sight Commercial Letters of Credit issued by the PRC Tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT700 for high-value bulk purchases, with documents including clean bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for established suppliers, with the presenting bank releasing documents only against payment or acceptance. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous documentation) is mandatory where the annual aggregate amount of related-party tangible asset transactions with Hong Kong or other foreign affiliates exceeds RMB 200,000,000; Master File is required where the entire group's annual related-party transaction value reaches RMB 1,000,000,000 or where the group consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5,500,000,000 triggering CbC reporting obligations; a CbC report must be filed in China where ultimate parent group revenue exceeds RMB 5,500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not issue Economic Substance Regulations in the BVI or Cayman style, but the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) doctrine is embedded in the beneficial-ownership rules under SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018) and in the general anti-avoidance rule of CIT Law Article 47. The JV factory in China must demonstrate substantive production, employment, plant, equipment, inventory risk and entrepreneurial functions. The Hong Kong shareholder must not be a shell or conduit; it must have real economic activities in Hong Kong (staff, office, decision-making) to enjoy treaty benefits. If the sole purpose of inserting the Hong Kong entity is to obtain the 5% dividend or 7% interest/royalty rate, the tax authority may deny the treaty claim and impose the statutory 10% withholding plus interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through False Cost Invoices

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law requires payment of the unpaid tax and imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. Article 201 of the Criminal Law punishes evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax payable with up to three years imprisonment and a fine; if the evaded amount exceeds RMB 500,000 and exceeds 30% of the tax payable, the imprisonment range is three to seven years. The tax authority will also impose daily late surcharges of 0.05% and may refer the case for criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Under the current PRC Company Law (amended in 2023), the corporate veil may be pierced where a shareholder or director abuses corporate independence to evade taxes. Directors who personally caused or approved the false invoicing may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. The tax authority may impose an exit ban on the legal representative and the responsible financial officer under Article 44 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law, freeze personal bank accounts, block real estate transfers, and if a criminal conviction results, the director faces imprisonment, seizure of personal assets and disqualification from serving as a director for a period.
Failure to Withhold PRC Source Withholding Tax on Outbound Payments

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law provides that a withholding agent that fails to withhold is subject to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld. CIT Law Article 37 imposes a statutory obligation on the payer to pay the tax if the non-resident recipient does not pay it. The tax authority will also collect interest computed at the daily late surcharge rate of 0.05% and pursue the underpaid tax from the JV. Repeated violations can trigger criminal liability for unauthorised non-payment of tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who deliberately instruct the finance department to ignore withholding obligations may be held personally liable as abusers of the corporate form. Courts and tax authorities have pierced the veil where the JV is left with no assets and the shareholder has drained profits; the director's personal assets are then subject to attachment. Criminal prosecution can name the director as an accomplice or principal under the Criminal Law for tax evasion, leading to imprisonment and a personal fine. An exit ban and red-link dishonesty measures apply to the legal representative and the director in charge of finance.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Shell Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 47 permit the tax authority to re-characterise the transaction on an arm's length basis. Under the Special Tax Adjustment Implementation Measures (SAT Announcement No. 6 of 2016), the tax authority will make an adjustment, charge interest at the benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points for late payment, and impose a 5% penalty for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation. The re-characterised amount may be treated as a hidden dividend, triggering the 10% statutory withholding tax. The China-Hong Kong DTA anti-abuse clause will deny treaty benefits if obtaining the tax advantage was the primary purpose.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or signed the transfer pricing scheme may be personally liable under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil if they used the JV to evade tax for the benefit of the Hong Kong parent. The tax authority can seek collection of the adjusted tax from the directors' personal assets in cases of wilful misconduct. Criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law can result in up to seven years imprisonment for the director who orchestrated the abusive scheme. Additionally, the director may be placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist, leading to restrictions on aircraft, high-speed rail travel, access to bank credit, and real-estate purchases.
