Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC VAT general taxpayer that manufactures retort river snail noodles in mainland China may apply the 0% VAT export rate only if: (1) the goods are physically exported outside the PRC customs territory, including from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR, under a valid customs export declaration; (2) the exporter is the registered VAT taxpayer and holds export tax refund qualification; (3) the sale is made to an overseas buyer at arm's length, commercial title/risk has passed to the buyer, and the export price satisfies customs valuation rules; (4) the proceeds are received in foreign currency or RMB as approved and repatriated through a designated bank and matched to the customs declaration; (5) all input VAT related to the exported goods is substantiated by compliant STA e-invoices; and (6) the goods meet GACC export food producer registration and food safety/import-country certification requirements. Qualifying offshore digital services, if later added, would require a separate consumption-outside-China and non-resident-business-customer evidence file.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (export Bill of Entry) with validated export customs clearance number and customs statistics number
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement from a PRC customs port
  • Commercial Export Invoice and underlying export sales contract containing Incoterms, pricing, and buyer identity
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued Export Proceeds Receipt Record monitored by SAFE evidencing repatriation of the export sale proceeds
  • Packing list and, where applicable, certificate of origin, health certificate, and GACC export food certificate required for ISIC 1079 processed food shipments
  • STA e-Tax export tax rebate declaration record matching the customs, invoice, and FX receipt data

The STA mandates digitalized 'all-electronic invoices' (electronic special VAT invoices and electronic ordinary VAT invoices) issued through the STA's Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each invoice is cleared centrally in near real-time and authenticated with an STA-assigned digital invoice number, anti-counterfeiting code, and digital seal. Taxpayers must integrate internal ERPs with STA-approved APIs or use the e-Tax portal's direct invoicing function; invoice data including buyer tax ID, commodity/service code, quantity, price, VAT rate and amount are transmitted to the STA inventory/control system. Red-letter (offset) invoices, partial rejection, and input-VAT credit verification all require the STA platform. Paper invoices, if still encountered, require the legacy tax control equipment and cannot replace the digital clearance data used for export zero-rating.

The STA uses the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Digital Electronic Invoice Service Platform to cross-match export customs declarations, e-invoices, sea/air waybills, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipt records automatically. If export documents are incomplete, inconsistent, or the proceeds are not repatriated within the mandatory period, the 0% rate is denied, output VAT at the headline 13% rate is reassessed from the original filing date, input VAT refunds are clawed back, and daily late-payment surcharges accrue at 0.05% of the unpaid tax per day. Penalties for false export declarations or fraudulent export VAT refunds include an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the refunded/unpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection, forfeiture of the refund, and criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, which may include imprisonment, criminal fines, and liability of the responsible officers and legal representative.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility and a heavily licensed capital account system. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established on the mainland with a Hong Kong parent, all cross-border foreign exchange transactions must be registered with SAFE through a designated domestic custodian bank. Current account items, including import of raw rice, seasoning packaging materials, export sales of packaged retort noodle products, royalties under technology licenses, and ordinary operating disbursements, are generally freely convertible once the designated bank verifies transaction authenticity, invoices, customs data, and trade contracts. By contrast, capital account items, such as foreign direct investment paid-in capital, acquisition of shares, shareholder loans, and outward remittance of liquidation or investment-related proceeds, require pre-registration, a dedicated capital account, and post-entry verification by a certified public accountant. Export proceeds from noodle sales may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted into RMB, but must first be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of export declaration. Profit remittance is permissible only after the annual audit is completed, enterprise income tax is settled, the board resolution approving dividends is passed, and the bank obtains the CPA audit report, capital account registration certificate, and authenticated FIE documents. No automatic convertibility is available for speculative flows, so treasury must plan every currency leg with documentary evidence and purpose codes ready for SAFE transparency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory percentage surrender applies to export proceeds under the current trade-in-goods administration. The exporting JV may either settle 0% to 100% of collected foreign currency into RMB or retain it in an authorised foreign exchange settlement account. However, all export receivables from rice noodle shipments must be repatriated to a domestic SAFE-designated bank account within 180 days of the export customs declaration unless SAFE approves an extension for exceptional trade credit. The bank will match each receipt against the customs release filing, commercial contract, invoice, and packing list through the Trade in Goods Monitoring System before releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Onshore viability is LOW and NOT FEASIBLE for the mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign JV. A fintech EMI cannot replace a SAFE-licensed designated commercial bank because only domestic banks can maintain the account infrastructure needed for FDI registration, customs verification interfaces, international income declaration, and capital account monitoring. Digital EMI providers without a corporate banking licence in China are legally barred from opening RMB settlement or foreign exchange accounts for mainland-incorporated entities. Hong Kong-licensed EMIs could theoretically support the Hong Kong shareholder as an offshore concentration node before funds enter China, but they cannot clear onshore CNY trade payments or receive SAFE-approved FDI capital injections. Therefore the recommended treasury architecture is a domestic Tier-1 bank as the sole onshore clearing bank, while the Hong Kong parent uses its own Hong Kong licensed banking relationship for pre-injection funds. The fintech EMI role is limited to non-regulated ancillary visibility tools and must never hold client money for the JV.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All incoming capital from the HK parent must be remitted in USD, HKD, or approved convertible currency via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's SAFE-designated capital account at the chosen Tier-1 domestic bank. Prior to the first injection, the JV must complete FDI registration with SAFE and obtain the capital account confirmation and registration number. The remittance instruction must reference the approved business contract and the bank's SAFE purpose-code framework, generally corresponding to inbound FDI paid-in capital. The bank will conduct international income declaration, validate the inflow against the SAFE certificate, and place the proceeds in the capital account. No operational disbursement is permitted until a certified public accountant issues a capital verification report and the treasury completes a passive payment-to-use declaration demonstrating that funds will be applied to approved working capital, fixed-asset purchases, or operating expenses within the entity's licensed scope. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Falsified Export Revenue and False Invoices

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63: tax authorities recover the evaded tax, impose daily late-payment interest of 0.05%, and fine the taxpayer from 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 criminalises tax evasion with graduated imprisonment; Article 205 criminalises false VAT-invoice issuance with severe imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under the PRC Company Law when control of the JV is abused to escape tax liabilities; directors, legal representative and finance officers who knowingly arrange false invoices or conceal revenue are jointly liable for restitution and tax debts. Exit bans / blacklisting under TCAL Article 44 and the Social Credit System restrict travel; criminal conviction can impose imprisonment from 3 to 7 years for significant evasion, and up to life imprisonment for aggravated false-invoice crimes.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Missing Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41-46 authorise the tax authority to apply the arm's length principle and adjust taxable income; SAT Public Notice No. 6 (2016) imposes administrative penalties for missing documentation, and an underpaid tax adjustment accrues daily interest plus a 5% penalty on the adjusted tax. Misuse of transfer pricing to evade tax is prosecuted under TCAL Articles 63 / 64 and may convert to Criminal Law Article 201 fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign contemporaneous documentation containing false warranties or who direct the under-invoicing scheme are treated as actual controlling persons; they can be held personally liable for the company's adjusted tax, interest and penalties when separate corporate personality is abused. Cross-border exchange of tax information may expose them to asset restraint, travel bans, and criminal proceedings in both mainland China and Hong Kong.
Shell Company / Abuse of DTA Treaty Benefit

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership rule and the Principal Purpose Test, so the full 10% domestic withholding tax applies with late interest. CIT Law Article 47 / Article 120 (GAAR) permits recharacterisation of the tax avoidance structure.
