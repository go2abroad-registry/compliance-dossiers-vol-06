Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For mainland China: the State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the competent local tax office at the entity's registered address, with the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal as the statutory electronic registration, filing, and clearance channel. For the Hong Kong SAR nexus of the same multinational formation: the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR, using the eTAX electronic portal on GovHK as the corresponding authority for Hong Kong profits tax and business registration obligations.

E-government filing portal

(1) PRC-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture: incorporate at the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is simultaneously transmitted to the tax authority and becomes the entity's sole Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identifier; no separate tax-ID application is needed for a resident enterprise. (2) Foreign non-resident entity (including a Hong Kong investor or offshore seller): before deriving China-source income or making taxable supplies in mainland China, complete the non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority of the payer/withholding agent through the e-Tax portal or at the in-person counter; submit a certified copy of the foreign/Hong Kong certificate of incorporation or Business Registration Certificate, articles/constitutional documents, board resolution appointing the authorised person and tax filing agent, identity documents, and, when treaty relief is claimed, a Hong Kong tax residency certificate for the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement; the tax authority then activates a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identification number used for withholding returns and any VAT obligations. (3) For VAT specifically, a foreign non-resident seller making taxable supplies in mainland China must register directly with the local STA office or appoint a lawful withholding agent; the tax profile is calibrated for e-invoice issuance, and VAT registration/VAT ID is evidenced by the TIN/USCC or the non-resident tax code. (4) Hong Kong SAR: any corporate entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the Business Registration Office of the IRD within one month of commencing business; the Business Registration Number is the Hong Kong taxpayer reference key, and separate profits tax returns are filed through eTAX.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for the PRC-resident JV (taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, with no pass-through or consolidated return); Hong Kong SAR operations/investor vehicle are subject to Territorial_Scope under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, taxing only profits with a Hong Kong source; no Tax_Exempt_Zone treatment applies to this entity unless a later special customs supervision zone license is obtained.

Available election forms

None for PRC entity classification: the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law has no check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch pass-through election; the JV is automatically an entity-level taxpayer. Available statutory elections include voluntary general VAT taxpayer registration for eligible small-scale taxpayers (annual taxable turnover at or below CNY 5,000,000), the simplified output VAT method for small-scale taxpayers, and direct/indirect foreign tax credit elections to eliminate double taxation. For Hong Kong, no check-the-box election exists, but the taxpayer may elect to apply the Mainland-Hong Kong Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation, including mutual agreement procedures and foreign-tax-credit relief where applicable.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold in mainland China; below this threshold an enterprise may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer. Non-resident enterprises making taxable supplies in mainland China must complete tax registration or arrange a withholding agent from the first taxable supply, irrespective of turnover, unless a statutory de minimis or non-resident digital transaction exception applies. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no broad-based indirect tax exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer that actually exports qualifying goods from the PRC customs territory may apply the 0% export VAT rate and claim refund of the attributed input VAT. Eligibility requires: physical export of the self-heating instant food/meal product through customs under an accepted export goods declaration; the exporter is the actual seller and title/beneficial owner of the goods; the sale is made to an overseas buyer with risk transferring outside mainland China; export proceeds are collected and settled/repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported in the SAFE goods-trade foreign exchange monitoring system; the enterprise maintains complete input VAT special invoices or equivalent proof of VAT actually borne; and the transaction is not a domestic VAT sale. Qualifying exported processed food preparations are eligible unless prohibited by customs or health/safety regulations.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export customs goods declaration) bearing the customs declaration number, export trade receipt, and customs release/clearance evidence
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or rail/road transport document evidencing physical carriage from mainland China to the overseas buyer/port of destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Foreign exchange repatriation evidence: bank inward remittance receipt / designated bank foreign exchange settlement record / cross-border RMB settlement voucher (inward remittance certificate where applicable) matching the export declaration
  • Export sale contract or cross-border purchase order
  • VAT invoice record and input VAT special invoice set evidencing the input VAT borne on production, materials, packaging, and freight (as required for export VAT rebate calculation)
  • Export tax refund/exemption application data submitted electronically via the STA export rebate module

Mainland China mandates real-time digital VAT invoicing through the STA's National Unified e-Invoice Platform and the Golden Tax Phase IV data infrastructure. General VAT taxpayers must issue VAT special invoices through the approved e-invoice/self-service invoicing system; each invoice receives a unique multi-digit invoice code and is uploaded in real time for tax-authority matching, input credit control, reconciliation with customs export declarations, and anti-fraud digital authentication. Invoices lacking valid digital authentication or failing automated matching cannot support input VAT credits or export VAT refund claims. Export sales are cleared electronically via the export VAT rebate module, and no manual stamping is required after customs release, but the e-invoice data, customs export declaration data, and foreign exchange collection data must reconcile. Hong Kong does not operate a transactional e-invoicing clearance mandate; eTAX supports electronic profits tax returns while underlying business records remain the statutory evidence.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs jointly police export zero-rating through digital reconciliation of customs export declarations, VAT e-invoice data, input VAT credits, logistics/transport records, and foreign exchange collection data. Any fraudulent, fictitious, or substantively unverified export zero-rating claim is disallowed and clawed back retroactively in full; the enterprise must repay the refunded VAT with late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day under the Tax Collection Administration Law, and may be penalised at 50% to five times the tax evaded or erroneously refunded for tax evasion or false export refund conduct. Where fraud is criminal, Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China imposes imprisonment and criminal fines, with potentially life imprisonment for especially huge amounts; tax authorities may place the enterprise on the Serious Tax Violation List, revoke export tax refund facilitation status, restrict invoice quotas, and impose multi-agency joint punishments affecting financing, customs clearance rating, and exit travel for responsible officers. In deception or concealment cases, recovery is not barred by the normal limitation periods.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR itself has no exchange control regime, but the entity under formation is a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operating in mainland China; therefore the controlling regime is the PRC's managed floating exchange-rate system administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. The RMB is convertible for current-account transactions, including trade and service payments, after banks complete truthful-trade verification. The capital account remains controlled: foreign direct investment equity injections, cross-border financing, and dividend repatriation require SAFE registration, filing, or approval. A foreign-invested enterprise may open an FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a designated domestic bank; remitted capital may be converted into RMB upon presentation of capital verification records and use-purpose supporting documents, but such funds cannot be deployed for speculative domestic equity investments, entrusted loans, or non-business expenditures. Profit remittances are permitted after annual audit, tax settlement, and SAFE record filing, provided the entity has a verified current-account balance and no unresolved capital verification issues. Export proceeds must be repatriated into China; conversion ratios are no longer mandatory, but proceeds are subject to SAFE and bank monitoring.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For general trade, export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 90 calendar days of the customs export declaration date unless the branch approves a longer open-account collection term. There is currently no forced conversion ratio; 100% must be physically repatriated, but the JV may retain the foreign currency in the settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB through a bank.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong treasury structure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Not viable for the onshore PRC JV. PRC corporate banking law, bank-licensing rules, and SAFE regulations require the domestic operating entity to maintain its capital, settlement, tax, and trade accounts with a licensed commercial bank supervised by the National Financial Regulatory Administration. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, or Currenxie do not possess a PRC corporate banking license and are legally barred from opening a local FDI capital account or processing SAFE-governed RMB settlements for the JV. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 48 and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42, the tax authority may recharacterise the profit using the TNMM or Profit Split Method, impose an upward transfer pricing adjustment, and assess interest at the base lending rate plus 5 percentage points. Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law adds a penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax. If the manipulated profit shifts more than RMB 100,000 and exceeds 10% of the JV's total tax payable, criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 applies.

Director personal liability: Directors approving the artificial intercompany pricing may be personally liable for restitution of the JV's tax loss under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, enabling the court to pierce the corporate veil. Criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment of up to 3 years for large tax evasion and 3 to 7 years plus a fine up to 5 times the evaded tax for extremely large amounts. The tax bureau may also list the controlling persons on the tax blacklist and impose an exit ban (出境限制), preventing international travel.
Failure to Withhold on Outbound Remittances / Unpaid Dividend WHT

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes a fine of 50% to 300% of the unpaid withholding tax on the JV as withholding agent, plus late-payment interest under Article 32. The non-resident beneficiary remains jointly liable for the tax. Use of a fraudulent TRC or concealed remittance also triggers penalties under Article 63, and criminal referral can follow under Article 201 or Article 210 of the Criminal Law for fraudulent reduction of tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute an untaxed dividend flow are personally responsible for the shortfall if the JV is insolvent; the corporate veil will be pierced where directors use the JV as a mere conduit to strip assets. The tax authority may issue a civil travel ban and blacklist the responsible directors, and criminal conviction can result in imprisonment up to 7 years where the evasion is treated as organised cross-border tax fraud.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and False CIGA Declaration

Statutory sanction: PRC Public Notice [2018] No. 9 codifies the beneficial ownership denial: treaty benefits are refused, the statutory 10% withholding applies, and interest plus late-payment penalties are assessed. The entire Hong Kong structure may be recharacterised as an agent or conduit of the Chinese shareholder. If the Hong Kong entity files a false offshore claim, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance may impose criminal fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years for false statements under Section 82A. Mainland Chinese authorities may additionally impose penalties under the tax fraud provisions of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong affiliate who sign the false substance declaration face criminal liabilities in Hong Kong, including imprisonment and disqualification under the Companies Ordinance, plus civil liability to the Hong Kong government for costs. Directors of the Chinese JV who orchestrate the sham are jointly exposed to PRC personal asset restitution, travel restrictions, and criminal punishment of up to 7 years imprisonment if the treaty abuse deprives China of more than RMB 100,000 in tax. The courts will disregard both the Hong Kong entity and the JV's limited liability shield in favour of equity-holder accountability.
