Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online portal for mainland China VAT and Enterprise Income Tax. For any Hong Kong SAR branch or foreign entity layer, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR administers Profits Tax and Business Registration; Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department administers duties but not VAT.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is established as a PRC resident enterprise. Its tax registration is obtained in the following steps: (1) the Administration for Market Regulation registers the JV and issues a Unified Social Credit Code, which is simultaneously the entity's Tax Identification Number and VAT registration identifier; (2) the JV activates its tax account in the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and confirms its general VAT taxpayer status, especially because it will claim export VAT refunds on freeze-dried pet raw meat exports; (3) the JV obtains a Customs registration code through China Customs for electronic export declarations; (4) for the Hong Kong or other foreign non-resident shareholder that derives China-sourced income, the withholding agent must file non-resident tax registration with the competent STA office to obtain a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number before applying treaty benefits; (5) if a separate Hong Kong SAR entity is used in the structure, that entity must apply to the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement, and the Business Registration Number serves as the Hong Kong tax identity; no additional VAT ID is required because Hong Kong has no VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The JV may apply the 0% export VAT rate to freeze-dried pet raw meat sold to Hong Kong SAR or any other destination outside mainland China if the goods physically leave China under customs supervision, the JV is the statutory exporter named on the customs declaration, the export is an actual sale to a non-PRC-resident customer at arm's length, and the export proceeds are collected in a foreign currency or HK dollar through a PRC-designated foreign exchange bank from an account outside mainland China. The goods are exported from the PRC VAT territory; Hong Kong SAR is treated as outside the VAT territory for zero-rating purposes. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to non-resident enterprises may also be zero-rated or exempt, but the principal regime here is goods export.

  • Export Customs Declaration Form issued by China Customs (electronic customs declaration with the approved 18-digit customs declaration number and the export destination showing Hong Kong SAR or another jurisdiction outside mainland China)
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the JV fully matching the customs declaration value, quantity, unit price, HS code 2309 and invoice number
  • Packing List detailing gross and net weights of the freeze-dried pet raw meat and container or seal numbers
  • Bill of Lading (original or express release), Air Waybill or multimodal transport document evidencing dispatch from a China customs export port to the buyer or consignee outside mainland China
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order signed with the offshore buyer and identifying the payment terms, currency and Incoterms
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher or Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation issued by a PRC-designated foreign exchange bank showing the SWIFT TT receipt from the overseas buyer (in lieu of an Inward Remittance Certificate, which is not part of PRC statutory export documentation)
  • If exported via cross-border e-commerce, the corresponding e-commerce export customs declaration, customer order data, payment transaction serial number and logistics tracking record
  • Where applicable, Customs warehouse entry or bonded supervisory zone transfer receipt proving the goods moved into the export environment and were not consumed in China

The JV must issue all VAT invoices through the STA's fully digital electronic invoicing platform (Digital Electronic Invoice) integrated with the e-Tax Bureau. Invoice data are transmitted in real time to the STA national invoice database and carry a unique 20-digit electronic invoice number and anti-forgery clearance code. No local fiscal device stamping is required for general VAT taxpayers using the digital e-invoice platform, but the invoice must be tax-cleared before issuance. Large-volume exporters may integrate their ERP with the STA digital e-invoice API. The STA automatically cross-checks e-invoice data with China Customs export declaration data and SAFE foreign exchange declaration data to detect false zero-rating claims.

The STA may cross-audit export refund claims using third-party data from China Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange for five years, and for ten years where fraudulent intent is suspected. If the zero-rating is disallowed because the goods never departed, the customs declaration was falsified, the invoice was inflated, or export proceeds were not repatriated within 180 days without an approved extension, the tax authority claws back the refund, charges late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, and assesses a penalty of 50% to 500% of the VAT under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Tax Collection. Fraudulent export VAT refund schemes and false issuance of export invoices may be prosecuted as criminal tax fraud, potentially leading to fines, confiscation of property, imprisonment for the responsible officers, and in severe cases life imprisonment. In addition, the responsible directors of the JV and the Hong Kong shareholder could face personal liability and compliance restrictions on export tax refund registration status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls administered jointly by the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Current account transactions, including import/export trade settlements and service payments, are generally convertible once the underlying transaction is validated against SAFE regulations, but all cross-border receipts and disbursements require genuine trade documentation, pre-registration with designated banks, and the correct declaration of transaction codes. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment equity injections, shareholder loans, outward remittances for overseas treasury purposes, and profit repatriation distributions, are subject to a much more rigorous approval, filing and verification architecture. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture domiciled in Hong Kong SAR operating inside mainland China, the JV must convert registered capital, any lawful renminbi loans, and retained earnings in a compliant manner, and the post-establishment foreign currency inflows must pass through a capital settlement or foreign exchange settlement account at a SAFE-designated bank. Profit remittances to Hong Kong shareholders require a board resolution, an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificates such as the Certificate of Tax Payment, a foreign exchange registration certificate (FDI compliance), and proof that bank settlement and payment systems have not detected money laundering or tax evasion red flags. Although China has progressively opened its onshore FX markets, the convertibility is not unconditional: export proceeds must be surrendered or settled within a specified timeline, outbound payments must be justified by documentary trade evidence, and all project-level foreign debts must be filed under the NDRC/SAFE cross-border financing macro-prudential framework. The commercial entity engaged in freeze-dried pet raw meat manufacturing must therefore treat the Chinese mainland treasury as a domestically regulated cash pool with controlled convertibility, while the Hong Kong lending/treasury vehicle can act as the offshore liquidity aggregator. Operational liquidity should be funded from an underlying real-trade basis, avoiding speculative FX arbitrage or round-tripping structures that SAFE would classify as violations of the real-trade principle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China within 90 days of the ship date or agreed settlement date, and the exporter is required to complete bank receipt verification and settlement through the SAFE-designated domestic bank; while the compulsory surrender ratio has been relaxed to allow controlled retention in foreign currency accounts, any conversion to RMB must occur through the domestic settlement system with the correct trade item codes and supporting customs declarations.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file required if annual related-party tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million; intangible transfers, financial asset transfers, and service transactions have lower thresholds (RMB 100 million for intangibles/financial and RMB 40 million for other services). Master file is required when the consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion; Country-by-Country Report becomes mandatory when group consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and General Anti-Avoidance Rules rather than a distinct Bangladeshi/Caribbean ESR. The key substantive requirement is that the HK shareholder, not the Chinese JV, must be the true beneficial owner of dividends, interest, royalties, and disposals. Under Hong Kong's FSIE regime in force from 2023, the HK company must maintain adequate employees, offices, and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong to claim tax benefits and treaty reductions; a shell HK company with no real activity will be denied treaty relief, subject to Chinese GAAR, and may trigger automatic risk alerts on the CIT return.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: PRC Taxation Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of underpaid tax, daily surcharge at 0.05%, a civil penalty from 50% to 500% of evaded tax, retroactive special tax adjustment, and full audit opening of prior years; serious amount triggers PRC Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable where corporate independence is abused to evade taxes; courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law and order personal asset restitution, freezing of bank accounts, travel bans, and tax blacklisting; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment up to 7 years, fines, and prohibition of directorships.
DTA Abuse / Conduit Shell Company Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under PRC EIT Law Article 48 and GAAR; reclassification of dividends at the statutory 10%; additional tax, interest, and penalties of up to 500% of avoided tax; publication in tax risk blacklist and enhanced review for all future remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Chinese JV and the HK parent face joint and several liability for unpaid withholding taxes if they knowingly orchestrated the conduit structure; personal wealth attachment, travel bans, revocation of residence permits, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with up to 7 years imprisonment are all available sanctions.
AML / Underreporting / False Related-Party Disclosure

Statutory sanction: Under Taxation Administration Law Articles 63 and 64, the taxpayer faces tax recovery, daily surcharge, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; SAT can conduct retrospective transfer pricing adjustments and impose penal interest; false statements may trigger criminal tax fraud liability and public tax blacklisting under the national integrity system.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial director, and tax agent who signed the false return are personally blacklisted, international travel is curtailed, assets are frozen for tax recovery, and courts may impose criminal imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines. Piercing the corporate veil is authorized where directors use the company as a device to defraud state tax revenue, making director personal estates directly liable.
