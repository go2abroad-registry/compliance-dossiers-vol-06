Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (CNY) operates under a bifurcated convertibility framework. Current account transactions, including cross-border trade in goods and services, interest payments, and dividend distributions, enjoy full convertibility subject to verification of transaction authenticity and compliance with tax clearance procedures. Capital account transactions, notably foreign direct investment (FDI), equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border financing, are heavily regulated through the SAFE registration system, with each capital flow requiring a certified Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate and a nominal account purpose code. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in commercial shipbuilding, all foreign exchange capital inflows must be recorded in a Capital Account Settlement (FDI) system and converted into CNY only for the approved business scope. Profit remittance hurdles are significant: before any dividend outflow, the JV must complete annual audit, submit corporate income tax settlement certificates to the tax bureau, demonstrate that paid-up registered capital has been actually injected and verified, and obtain SAFE's approval for the remittance via a designated bank. Additionally, 25% of capital gains and 10% withholding tax on dividends may apply depending on the parent company's tax residency and treaty entitlements. The JV cannot freely convert CNY to foreign exchange for speculative purposes, and all exchange transactions must adhere to the real-demand principle, meaning the entity must prove an underlying hedging or settlement need. This makes treasury design complex: liquidity managers must pre-schedule conversion windows, maintain segregated CNY and USD accounts, and align payment cycles with regulatory submission deadlines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the sale of container vessels must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of the customs export declaration, although extended timelines are possible with SAFE pre-approval. Upon repatriation, enterprises are generally obligated to sell or settle surrendered foreign currency in accordance with the current account policy; however, genuine trade receipts may be retained in foreign currency accounts up to a certain cap determined by SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% for amounts exceeding the approved retention limit, and the JV must use the Trade Monitoring System to align each receipt with the corresponding export customs record to satisfy the authenticity and matching requirement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its subsidiary State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore financial technology EMI platforms, such as digital challenger banks or virtual account providers headquartered in Hong Kong or Singapore, have no legal authority to open primary bank accounts or provide foreign exchange settlement services directly for a mainland Chinese domiciled JV. China's banking regulatory framework under PBOC and SAFE does not recognize unlicensed EMIs as deposit-taking institutions or qualified foreign exchange banks. Any attempt to route operational liquidity through an EMI would violate the Uniform Capital Account Supervision rules and trigger immediate account freezing, forfaiting of the associated tax incentives, and potential administrative penalties. The JV must use a licensed domestic commercial bank for all CNY clearing and SAFE-registered FX transactions. The only permissible fintech role is as a supplementary payment aggregator for e-commerce-related micro-transactions, which is not applicable to a shipyard's high-value maritime installment payments. Therefore, cross-border EMI viability is rated 'Non-Feasible' for core treasury operations, though an EMI can serve as an informational dashboard aggregating balances from licensed bank accounts via API feeds without holding funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All foreign capital injections from the Hong Kong shareholder enter via SWIFT MT103/202 to the JV's Capital Account at a designated licensed bank. The instruction must carry SAFE purpose code 622 (Direct Investment – Equity Injection) and the FX registration number of the FDI contract. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the amount against the SAFE-registered paid-in capital schedule and checks that the remitter is exactly the same entity as the registered investor. After completion, the JV must apply for the Capital Account Settlement Bank Confirmation and then convert the foreign currency into CNY within 5 working days, using the current offer rate, provided that the conversion legality certificate has been filed. For fund verifiability, the JV must engage a qualified accounting firm to issue a capital verification report confirming the actual injection, which is then submitted to the local administration for industry and commerce, and simultaneously noted in the SAFE system. The JV must not pre-incur expenditure against the injection until the capital verification certificate is issued, to avoid reclassification as a shareholder loan.All outbound trade disbursements for shipyard supplies, subcontractors, and maritime equipment must be routed through the JV's operational account using either SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers, or documentary trade instruments such as an Irrevocable Letter of Credit (LC) or Documentary Collection (D/P or D/A). For the LC route, the issuing bank (e.g., Bank of China) authenticates the seller's draft against conforming documents including the bill of lading, commercial invoice, certificate of origin, and inspection certificate issued by an independent surveyor such as SGS or Bureau Veritas. For high-value stage payments under the shipbuilding contract, the JV should escrow each milestone payment using an installment LC where the paying bank releases funds upon presentation of the classification society's approval of the construction stage. For domestic CNY payments to local suppliers, the JV must use the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) or the newer RMB Cross-Border Payment System (CIPS) for any transactions involving offshore Chinese counterparts, with proper trade receipts. Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on the gross amount of dividends paid by a Chinese resident JV to a non-resident corporate shareholder under CIT Law Articles 3, 27 and 37. The China-Hong Kong DTA, Article 10(2), reduces the rate to 5% where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company that directly holds at least 25% of the equity capital of the JV. If the beneficial ownership or shareholding condition is not met, the 10% statutory rate applies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The mandatory Local File is triggered where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services. The Master File is required where aggregate related-party transaction amounts exceed RMB 1 billion in one tax year. The JV must file the annual related-party transaction declaration with the CIT annual return for all related-party transactions, and contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be prepared by June 30 of the following year.

Economic substance scope: China adheres to the economic substance and anti-abuse doctrines under CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Articles 120 and 121. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in ocean container vessel construction, the JV itself must carry out the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA): vessel design management, hull construction, procurement of marine equipment, project risk assumption, staged revenue recognition, and final delivery decisions. The Hong Kong parent claiming DTA benefits must have sufficient substance in Hong Kong—real premises, employees, board meetings, bookkeeping and risk control—to satisfy the beneficial ownership standard. If CIGA are effectively performed in mainland China or the HK entity is a mere shell, the Chinese tax authority may disregard the HK intermediary, reattribute the profit to the Chinese shipyard, deny treaty relief, and apply special tax adjustments plus interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Revenue Concealment Through Offshore Receipts

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63 authorizes recovery of the underpaid CIT plus 0.05% daily late interest and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. The State Taxation Administration will re-determine taxable income based on the shipyard's actual functional profile and may impose a deemed profit adjustment. Under SAT Public Notice [2015] No. 60 and Public Notice [2009] No. 601, all reduced DTA rates on any future dividend are revoked if the Hong Kong entity is a conduit. If the underpayment exceeds the criminal threshold, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes additional fines and imprisonment for the entity's responsible persons.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 where the director uses the JV to evade tax and abuses limited liability; the director is jointly and severally liable for restitution of the diverted sale proceeds and unpaid tax. The legal representative is subject to an exit ban and cross-border blacklisting under TCAL Article 44 until the tax debt is settled. Civil asset preservation orders may attach personal bank accounts, shares and vessels. A knowing director faces criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years for serious tax evasion, plus fines and supplementary property seizure.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation, Thin-Capitalization Interest Stripping, and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 47, Implementation Regulations Articles 120 and 121, and SAT transfer pricing rules empower the tax authority to make special tax adjustments, recharacterize the excessive interest as a deemed dividend, disallow the fees, and impose interest calculated at the market lending benchmark plus an additional 5 percentage points under Implementation Regulation Article 122. The taxpayer may be fined for false filing under TCAL Article 63 from 50% to 500% of the resulting underpaid WHT and CIT. The HK parent's treaty relief is denied if it fails the beneficial ownership or principal purpose test.

Director personal liability: A director who personally controls both the JV and the HK parent may have the corporate veil pierced under Company Law Article 20, making personal assets liable for the stripped profits and tax deficiencies. Tax authorities may place the director on the serious tax violation blacklist, restrict departure from China, and apply to courts for asset freezing and seizure. If the director signs false transfer pricing documentation or forged invoices to support the fees, criminal liability under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205 may result in imprisonment up to life for fraudulent VAT or tax evasion, together with monetary fines.
Sham Royalty Payments and Unlawful Withholding Tax Treaty Abuse / Foreign Exchange Fraud

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority will deny the DTA benefit under SAT Public Notice [2015] No. 60 and Public Notice [2009] No. 601, reclassify the royalty as a dividend or disallowed expense, and assess the 10% WHT deficiency plus daily late interest and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax under TCAL Article 63. The foreign exchange remittance made on the strength of fraudulent documents violates the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration, subject to a SAFE penalty of 30% to 100% of the illegal payment amount in serious cases, in addition to administrative currency restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the sham royalty agreement and caused the unlawful remittance are personally liable under Company Law Article 20 for restitution of the deducted expenses and WHT loss. The legal representative and operating directors face travel bans, blacklisting, and court-ordered civil asset preservation. A conviction for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 or fraudulent foreign exchange purchase and remittance under Criminal Law Article 190 can result in imprisonment for up to 10 years, criminal fines, and confiscation of proceeds derived from the offshore escrow fraud.
