Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Taxation) platform (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the 12366 national taxpayer service hotline; in the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region sub-territory, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the GovHK e-Tax electronic portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) must first obtain its Business License from the Market Regulation Administration; the license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings in China. Step 2: Tax registration is completed automatically upon issuance of the business license and the opening of the taxpayer electronic file at the competent district/county office of the STA where the registered address is located; the tax officer activates the Electronic Taxation Bureau filing account. Step 3: Because the JV has foreign and Hong Kong shareholders, the JV must also complete the withholding tax registration for each non-resident investor and file the Non-resident Enterprise Contract and Tax Payment registration through the Electronic Taxation Bureau; required documents include the joint venture contract, articles of association, the USCC business license, investor identity or certificate of incorporation, and passport or director identification of the legal representative. Step 4: Where a Hong Kong-incorporated related entity is used as the offshore distribution or investment vehicle, that entity must separately obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD of Hong Kong SAR under the Business Registration Ordinance within one month of commencing business and obtain a Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number; no dividend withholding tax is imposed on distributions paid out of Hong Kong to its shareholders.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election is recognised in China or Hong Kong SAR. Statutory elections that may be utilised by the JV include: (a) pursuant to Article 2 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China, a non-resident enterprise whose de facto management and control body is situated in China may elect to be taxed as a Chinese resident enterprise; (b) an election for special tax treatment (tax-neutral rollover) of qualified asset and share reorganisations under the Caishui [2009] No. 59 regulations; and (c) the Small and Low-Profit Enterprise preferential election, where qualifying, to apply the reduced progressive corporate income tax rates. In Hong Kong SAR, no election is needed to claim the territorial source principle; a taxpayer merely sustains the burden of proof that profits are sourced offshore.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China has no turnover exemption from VAT registration: every enterprise with taxable sales must register. The decisive statutory threshold is annual taxable sales of CNY 5,000,000: above it, VAT general taxpayer status is mandatory; below it, small-scale taxpayer status applies. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT so no registration threshold arises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods of the Chinese JV qualify for the 0% VAT rate and export tax rebate where: (1) a genuine sale is made to an overseas buyer; (2) the goods leave the People's Republic of China customs territory under a formal Export Goods Declaration; (3) export sales proceeds are received from overseas and repatriated through a designated settlement bank; (4) the transaction is commercially documented by contract, commercial invoice, bill of lading and bank receipts; and (5) the goods are not prohibited from export. Because the goods are all-terrain military tactical vehicles (ISIC 3040 / NAICS 336992), the exporter must also hold the statutory military and defense export licence and comply with end-user and end-use certification before 0% VAT may be applied. Qualifying offshore digital or cross-border services supplied to non-residents are zero-rated under the separate cross-border services schedule where consumed entirely outside China.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (cleared through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading (or Master Air Waybill) evidencing outbound shipment
  • Export Proceeds Receipt Confirmation issued by the settlement bank under SAFE goods-trade monitoring
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Military products export licence together with end-user and end-use certificate
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice and signed export sales contract

China mandates the Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice regime: VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the official Electronic Invoice Service Platform of the Electronic Taxation Bureau, transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV information system, and each invoice must carry a unique 20-digit invoice number and cryptographic anti-fraud code; buyers may claim input VAT credit only after the invoice is electronically cleared in the State Taxation Administration database. Enterprise resource planning systems may integrate through official tax-control API interfaces, and export declarations must be cleared through the China International Trade Single Window. The Hong Kong SAR does not operate an electronic fiscal clearance mandate; e-invoicing remains voluntary.

If the 0% export zero-rating is not substantiated by the required customs, shipping, licensing and foreign-exchange repatriation evidence, the tax authority will claw back the export VAT rebate and re-assess VAT at the standard 13% rate with a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the underpaid tax. Under Article 66 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection of the People's Republic of China, fraudulent procurement of an export tax refund attracts an administrative penalty of 50% up to 500% of the refunded amount, and where the amount is large, criminal liability under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund and foreign-exchange fraud, with imprisonment up to life imprisonment and monetary fines. Military and defense exports additionally trigger customs and state-security retrospective audits, including verification of the end-user certificate and the 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and the 2008 Foreign Exchange Control Ordinance. While trade-related foreign exchange payments and remittances are generally permitted after mandatory customs and tax verification, capital account transactions remain strictly controlled and require case-by-case approval from SAFE. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) engaged in the manufacture of all-terrain military tactical vehicles, capital contributions from the Hong Kong shareholder must be routed through a designated Foreign Currency Capital Account and verified by a certified public accountant. Profit remittance and dividend distribution to the Hong Kong parent are allowed only after annual audit, tax clearance, and submission of the deemed-paid foreign income tax credit documentation; any earlier repatriation would trigger an illegal capital flight investigation. For the export of armored security personnel transport vehicles, export proceeds must be repatriated to China and recorded in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, although enterprises are permitted a voluntary foreign exchange settlement policy. Given the defense-sensitive industrial classification, additional SAFE scrutiny on the authenticity of the underlying trade contract, the end-user certificate, and the non-restricted nature of the destination country is unavoidable. The convertibility of CNY for capital account items such as overseas direct investment by the JV or external debt amortization will require a separate SAFE filing and could be delayed by the obtaining of an approval certificate and the completion of a risk assessment. Overall, the currency regime is conditionally convertible: operational, trade-driven current account payments flow with relative ease, but all equity injections, debt servicing, and cross-border profit distributions sit under strict regulatory gates designed to prevent illegal cross-border capital flows and to preserve financial stability.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC FX rules, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts; exporters may retain the currency in a domestic foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily convert it to CNY through a designated bank. Export proceeds from invoiced shipments must be repatriated into a licensed domestic foreign exchange account via an approved bank without unreasonable delay, and all transactions must be reported under the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within 30 days of settlement. SAFE applies classified monitoring (A/B/C category) and may require earlier repatriation or documentary evidence if an exporter is rated B or C. In practice, standard A-class exporters must clear export receipts within 180 days of the customs declaration date unless a pre-approved extension is issued by the branch of SAFE covering the goods in question.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Revolut, or other offshore digital banks are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to provide domestic banking or FX conversion services to resident corporate entities in mainland China. These EMI platforms may only support cross-border payment initiations through a licensed Chinese clearing partner; however, they cannot offer the required capital escrow, SAFE-specific purpose codes, Customs document matching, or military end-use compliance verification needed for a high-risk defense-sector JV. Given the mandatory physical deposit-taking institution relationship and the statutory duty to report to SAFE under the Trade Foreign Exchange and Capital Account Information Systems, the viability of an offshore EMI is effectively zero. Only licensed tier-1 domestic commercial banks with full clearing capabilities and compliant integration with the PBOC Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) can legally serve this entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
