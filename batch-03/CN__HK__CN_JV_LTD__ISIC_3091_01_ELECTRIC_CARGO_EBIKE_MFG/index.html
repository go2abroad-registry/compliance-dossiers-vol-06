Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital on a private company limited by shares. The JV shareholders may authorize a nominal share capital in the Articles of Association and subscribe for shares as fully paid, partly paid, or nil-paid shares. No escrow or blocked capital account is required by the Companies Registry; however, if the Chinese parent remits equity consideration, the foreign exchange authority of the People's Republic of China may require a designated capital account and SAFE/PBOC registration before remittance.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense and fire clearance: Fire Services Department fire service installation certificate covering automatic sprinklers, hydrants, hose reels, alarms, emergency lighting, and an approved means of escape plan under the Fire Safety (Buildings) Ordinance (Cap. 572). If lithium-ion battery cells or aerosol solvents are stored, a Dangerous Goods Licence under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295) must be obtained and storage quantities must conform to the licensee's approved schedule. Structural load clearance from a Registered Structural Engineer is needed for assembly lines, mezzanine racks and battery test rooms; the floor live load must remain within the design limit and any alteration should be reported to the Buildings Department. Occupational clearance from the Labour Department includes an employee safety plan, personal protective equipment assessment, noise and ventilation controls, and a chemical safety data sheet register.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no statutory minimum paid-up share capital on a private company limited by shares. The JV shareholders may authorize a nominal share capital in the Articles of Association and subscribe for shares as fully paid, partly paid, or nil-paid shares. No escrow or blocked capital account is required by the Companies Registry; however, if the Chinese parent remits equity consideration, the foreign exchange authority of the People's Republic of China may require a designated capital account and SAFE/PBOC registration before remittance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), exercised through the provincial and municipal tax service bureaus where the JV is registered, with administrative and filing access through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau online service portal; Hong Kong SAR is an offshore special jurisdiction served by the Inland Revenue Department and Business Registration Office for the foreign investor, but the VAT registration of the mainland manufacturing entity is exclusively under STA for VAT and enterprise income tax purposes.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must first complete company incorporation and obtain a business license with the State Administration for Market Regulation; the Unified Social Credit Code of 18 characters is issued on the business license and serves as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for all PRC tax filings, including VAT and enterprise income tax. Within 30 days of issuance of the business license, the JV must complete tax registration on the STA Electronic Tax Bureau portal by submitting the Unified Social Credit Code, business license, bank account and settlement details, legal representative identity, equity structure, articles of association, joint venture contract and board resolution. The tax authority will activate VAT and CIT filing profiles and determine the VAT payer status. As a foreign non-resident investor, the Hong Kong SAR parent must also submit a certificate of Hong Kong tax residence to the competent PRC tax authority to qualify for reduced withholding rates under the Mainland and Hong Kong double taxation arrangement; the mainland JV acts as withholding agent for dividend, interest, royalty and service fee payments, and the non-resident parent is registered for non-resident tax contract records but is not issued a separate VAT TIN unless it creates a permanent establishment in mainland China.

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered general VAT taxpayer exporting electric cargo bicycles from mainland China, including exports to Hong Kong SAR, may apply VAT zero-rating and the export tax refund or exemption provided the goods are physically exported through a PRC customs port, the export goods customs declaration is made in the name of the JV, the overseas buyer pays in freely convertible foreign currency, and the export proceeds are repatriated through a bank within the statutory deadline. Qualifying services and offshore digital delivery are outside the scope of this manufacturing entity.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Goods Customs Declaration), representing the authoritative electronic export clearance record in the name of the JV
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR or the overseas destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Invoice matching the customs declared value
  • Packing List and export logistics documents
  • Export Sales Contract with the overseas buyer
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) proving collection of export proceeds from the overseas buyer

The JV must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices (Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices, known as FDEIs) through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which applies an electronic anti-fraud digital stamp and unique invoice code at issuance. High-volume issuers must integrate via system-to-system API or compatible client software so that invoice data is transmitted to the STA Golden Tax Phase IV information system for near-real-time clearance, invoice comparison, bank-customs-tax data reconciliation and audit surveillance.

The STA and customs authorities deploy data-matching between customs declarations, logistics manifests, bank remittance records and the Golden Tax Phase IV database. If export zero-rating or refund is not substantiated by the required documentation, or the foreign exchange repatriation deadline is missed, the STA will reverse the zero-rating or rebate, recover the VAT previously exempted or rebated, impose daily late-payment surcharges, and may assess administrative penalties ranging from 50 percent to five times the tax amount involved under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Collection; serious cases involving false customs declarations or forged FIRC documentation are referred for criminal investigation for VAT invoice fraud and export tax fraud. A retrospective clawback may extend to five years, or ten years in cases involving fraud or misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by SAFE. Current account items are generally convertible after verification by a designated settlement bank, enabling import settlement, export collection, and trade-related service payments provided the underlying invoices, customs declarations, contracts, and tax-paid certificates are presented. The capital account remains strictly controlled; foreign direct investment into a Sino-foreign equity joint venture requires an FDI registration, opening of a capital account foreign exchange settlement account, and remittance of equity consideration under a valid investment purpose code. Export proceeds must be repatriated and are subject to SAFE trade verification but, under the modernized framework, no fixed compulsory conversion ratio is imposed on legitimate merchandise receipts. Profit remittance is permitted through the current account if the JV demonstrates distributable profits on an audited PRC GAAP basis, has completed the statutory corporate income tax settlement, and holds a board resolution and proof that prior-year losses have been fully recaptured. The principal hurdles include PRC withholding tax on dividends, annual audit and capital verification requirements, SAFE information verification, and prohibition of remittal against unaudited books or unverified invoices. Overall, current account convertibility is conditionally open but capital account entry and exit remain strictly registered and policed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the electric cargo e-bike manufacturing JV must be repatriated to mainland China within 30 days after the customs export declaration, or within such SAFE-approved extension as may apply for scheduled production and staggered deliveries, not exceeding 90 days without specific approval. The statutory mandatory conversion ratio for verified trade receipts is effectively nil: 100% of the foreign currency receipt may be credited to the exporter's foreign exchange account and either retained or converted to CNY at the exporter's discretion, subject to trade verification and matching against the electronic customs export data. Where an export receipt cannot be matched or verified within the prescribed window, the collecting bank is required to freeze or force settlement under SAFE's trade verification system and the JV may be subject to administrative penalty, so the full supporting export declaration and BOP declaration must be completed by the bank and exporter.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the mainland China JV. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as cross-border fintech payment companies are not licensed depository institutions by the PBOC or SAFE and are legally barred from opening onshore PRC deposit accounts, executing foreign exchange settlements, or handling FDI capital verification for a Sino-foreign equity joint venture. A Hong Kong parent or treasury vehicle may hold an account at a Hong Kong-licensed bank or an HKMA-authorized stored value facility for its own working capital, but every capital injection into the mainland JV must pass through a designated domestic SAFE-registered bank; any scheme that attempts to bypass the FDI registration and BOP reporting via an EMI is non-compliant and will be rejected by the receiving bank. Therefore, reliance on fintech EMIs is not viable for the JV's onshore treasury or trade-clearing function.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity contributions from the Hong Kong investor must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the JV's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account at the designated Tier-1 bank. The remitter must quote the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment reporting purpose code for inward foreign direct investment equity capital, and the receiving bank will validate the FDI registration number, the JV contract, the board resolution, and the business license before releasing the funds. Immediately after the remittance is credited and settled into CNY, the JV must engage a PRC certified public accountant to issue a statutory capital verification report, which is filed with SAFE through the bank within the prescribed reporting window, usually within ten business days. The settled capital may be used only for operating expenditures within the approved business scope; the proceeds cannot be applied to offshore lending, equity investment, securities trading, or PRC real estate unless separately approved by SAFE.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard CIT rate; reduced to 15% for qualifying High and New Technology Enterprises, and 20% reduced rate applicable to small low-profit enterprises on taxable income up to RMB 3 million under the Small Low-Profit Enterprise preferential regime)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory rate on dividends paid to non-resident enterprises under Article 3(3) and Article 37 of the EIT Law; reduced to 5% under Article 10 of the China-Hong Kong Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income (China-Hong Kong DTA) if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company holding at least 25% of the Chinese JV’s capital and satisfies the substance requirements. The 5% treaty rate is available for dividends while interest generally qualifies for a 7% treaty rate (or 5% if the beneficial owner is a bank / financial institution) and royalties at 5% (or 7% for certain industrial / cultural copyright royalties).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The State Taxation Administration’s Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) when: (i) annual related-party transactions / intercompany transactions with controlled foreign parties exceed RMB 40 million (approximately USD 5.5 million); (ii) total related-party tangible asset transactions exceed RMB 400 million / intangible transactions exceed RMB 200 million / financial transactions exceed RMB 100 million; or (iii) any single related-party transaction exceeds RMB 100 million. For the electric e-bike manufacturer, if the JV sells finished e-bikes to the Hong Kong parent for distribution, the combined local sales and intercompany purchases will typically exceed the RMB 40 million threshold, triggering Local File / Master File / Country-by-Country reporting (CbCR) if the MNE group has consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese JV is a fully taxable resident entity with economic substance; however, if the structure involves a pure Hong Kong holding / procurement / licensing entity, the Hong Kong jurisdiction’s Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) under Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Part 9A apply only to Hong Kong tax resident entities and are framed as ‘economic substance requirements’ for specified income. For the Chinese JV, the relevant doctrine is the PRC ‘reasonable business needs’ and substance-over-form rules under Articles 47 and 123 of the EIT Law, and the OECD BEPS action plans as applied through SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 (beneficial ownership) and Announcement 2015 No. 60. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the manufacturer: design and engineering of electric cargo bikes, procurement of batteries and controllers, manufacturing and assembling, quality control, inventory management, and order fulfillment in China. For the Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits, CIGA must occur in Hong Kong (logistics, sales contract negotiation, distribution, and risk management). If the Hong Kong entity is a mere invoice issuer without employees or risk-bearing capacity, the Chinese tax authority can recharacterize the structure, deem the Hong Kong entity as a conduit, deny treaty relief, and impose 10% WHT on undeclared dividends / service fees.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent under-declaration of revenue (CIT fraud)

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administrative Law (TCAL): penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of underpaid tax; back-taxes plus daily 0.05% interest surcharge; Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC: crime of tax evasion punishable by detention / imprisonment up to 3 years (if underpaid tax is RMB 100,000–1 million and 10%–30% of taxable amount) or 3–7 years (if over RMB 1 million and over 30% of taxable amount) plus fines. The tax authority may also impose audit adjustments and refer the legal representatives for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law and the principle of piercing the corporate veil (Article 20(3) Company Law), directors who commingle personal assets with corporate assets, abuse the corporate form to evade tax, or intentionally cause the JV to under-declare profits are personally liable for the company’s unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest to the extent of the abuse. Directors may face civil asset seizure / freezing of personal bank accounts, court-ordered restitution to the corporate estate, travel restrictions and exit bans under the ‘exit control’ measures of the State Taxation Administration (blacklisting), and criminal detention / imprisonment. If the tax evasion exceeds RMB 1 million, the responsible director / CFO is exposed to 3–7 years of imprisonment plus a criminal fine and confiscation of illegal gains.
Transfer Pricing manipulation / False related-party documentation

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the EIT Law empowers the SAT to make a primary adjustment to the taxable income using arm’s-length pricing; Announcement 2016 No. 42 and Announcement 2017 No. 6 impose a 5% interest surcharge on the upward adjustment (currently 5% above the benchmark loan prime rate, but not less than the standard rate for underpaid tax), plus a 50% to 5x penalty if underpaid tax arises from negligence or fraud. Failure to submit the Local File / Master File may result in a RMB 10,000–50,000 fine and the use of adverse inferences / burden shift under the TP audit rules.

Director personal liability: Chinese tax authorities may assert joint and several liability against the director / actual controller under the ‘de facto controller’ doctrine and under Article 20 of the Company Law if the director directed the fabrication of TP documentation, knowingly signed false intercompany agreements, or transferred assets out of the JV to the Hong Kong parent before the tax assessment. Directors may be disqualified / blacklisted by credit and social credit systems (失信被执行人), banned from leaving China under the exit-control list issued by the SAT, and held personally liable for the corporate tax debt if piercing the corporate veil is established. Criminal liability may include tax evasion as described above, and if fake VAT special invoices are used, punishment under Article 205 of the Criminal Law for ‘false issuance of special VAT invoices’ is up to life imprisonment, with personal assets confiscated.
Improper DTA / WHT avoidance and beneficial ownership abuse (Shell company / AML)

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 (Beneficial Ownership) and Article 122 of the EIT Law, the tax authority will deny treaty benefits and apply a 10% statutory WHT on the under-withheld amount, plus late payment interest and a penalty of 50% of the underpaid WHT under Article 63 TCAL; the case triggers the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 EIT Law (recharacterization of the transaction), resulting in tax adjustment and potentially a zero-benefit re-determination. If the fraudulent TRC / false documentation is used, the approval of the tax bureau may be revoked and the case referred for criminal forgery of official instruments (Article 280 of the Criminal Law: imprisonment up to 3 years).

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders of the JV who sign false declarations, overpay intercompany invoices to launder the dividend out of China, or conceal the Hong Kong conduit’s beneficial owner may be subject to: (i) civil restitution of the avoided WHT with interest and penalties; (ii) personal blacklisting and exit bans under the Tax Collection Law and the SAT ’red list’ of serious tax offenders; (iii) money-laundering charges under Article 191 of the Criminal Law if the funds are laundered through the Hong Kong entity, with imprisonment from 5 to 10 years plus fines; and (iv) personal liability for offshore trust / holding structures set up to evade tax under the PRC’s individual income tax anti-avoidance rules. The director may also be banned from serving as a director in any Chinese company for 3 years and face administrative detention for up to 15 days under public security regulations.
