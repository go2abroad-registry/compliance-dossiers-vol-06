Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); however, the company must issue at least one share upon incorporation. The subscriber shares are generally allotted nil paid or fully paid as agreed. No blocked escrow account or statutory paid-up capital deposit is required in Hong Kong. For bank financing and letter-of-credit facilities, lenders may impose a voluntary cash-collateral or escrow arrangement, but this is a contractual matter and not a statutory requirement.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department approval under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) covering fire service installations and equipment, including manual alarm, extinguishers, hose reels, emergency lighting, and evacuation route diagrams; Buildings Department structural load inspection for the installation of injection moulding machines and rotating equipment, with a demonstrated floor loading capacity of not less than 5 kPa; Labour Department Occupational Safety and Health clearance requiring a safety statement, machinery guarding inspection, compressed air system certification, and industrial dust control verification before commencement of operations.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) portal and the Export Tax Rebate Management System of the STA. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) remains relevant solely for the Hong Kong parent/investor, not for the PRC-resident CN_JV_LTD.

E-government filing portal

Step 1. Register the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Step 2. Upon issuance of the business license, obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the official TIN in the PRC for income tax, VAT, customs, and foreign exchange filings. Step 3. Within 30 days of SAMR registration, complete consolidated tax registration with the competent STA office through the National Electronic Tax Bureau, selecting VAT, Enterprise Income Tax, urban surtaxes, and other applicable indirect taxes. Step 4. Apply for VAT general taxpayer status if annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, or earlier by election to recover input VAT. Step 5. If a foreign non-resident entity has no permanent establishment in China, it must obtain its own non-resident tax registration through the withholding agent before receiving Chinese-source dividends, interest, royalties, or fees; the withholding agent references the non-resident's TIN or the JV's USCC as required. Step 6. For export activities, complete customs export registration and, where applicable, cross-border e-commerce export customs filing in the name of the JV before claiming zero-rated export VAT treatment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not permit check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is mandatorily taxed as an entity. The only administrative election available is between VAT general taxpayer and small-scale taxpayer status, subject to turnover thresholds, which does not alter the entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory tax registration exists for all regulated enterprises without a turnover threshold. Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 may qualify for the VAT exemption, but still require tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered PRC VAT general taxpayer may apply 0% VAT to designer blind box toy goods that are physically exported from mainland China under a valid PRC Customs export declaration, sold to a non-resident buyer, and settled with foreign exchange returned through a SAFE-licensed bank. The exporter must be the same entity shown on the customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and bank receipt. Qualifying offshore digital services consumed outside China may also qualify, but they are not the primary activity of this ISIC 3240 manufacturing entity.

  • PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration (customs export clearance record showing exporter, HS classification, quantity, value, and customs regime)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical shipment from mainland China to the foreign destination
  • Bank-issued Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation (foreign exchange receipt / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) as accepted by the SAFE-designated bank)

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV real-time tax control system. All invoices, including fully digitalized electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA-approved National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform and transmitted to the tax authority in real time. Special VAT invoices include the STA anti-counterfeit tax control code and encrypted digital verification stamp; no separate fiscal clearance hardware or third-party stamping is recognized as an alternative.

The STA and PRC Customs jointly audit export declarations, shipping documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export or without verifiable repatriation of proceeds, the VAT refund is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest and penalties of 0.5 to 5 times the tax involved under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export tax refund claims trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC (punishable by fine and/or imprisonment up to life) and the exporter loses export tax rebate eligibility and customs credit rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime. The RMB is convertible for current account items including trade settlements and dividend remittances under verified documentation. However, capital account transactions remain subject to PBOC/SAFE approval, registration, and macroprudential supervision. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, initial share capital injection must be registered with SAFE and validated by an eligible local bank. Subsequent profit remittances require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board resolutions, and an 'FDI Profit Remittance' application. Surrender requirements: entities may retain foreign currency in designated accounts up to a certain cap, but must timely report cross-border receipts to SAFE under the Goods Trade Monitoring System. Capital flight strategies are restricted, and outbound payments for royalties or dividends require proof of genuine commercial transactions. The convertibility is thus full for current account but restricted for capital account, imposing meaningful compliance hurdles for repatriating funds back to the HK parent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC foreign exchange regulations (SAFE Circular 2019 and revised rules), all export proceeds must be repatriated into China within a statutory deadline of 5 business days of receipt. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero—enterprises are allowed to retain foreign currency balances in their 'Export Goods Settlement Account' subject to aggregate monitoring. However, all trade receipts must be matched against the customs declaration data under the Goods Trade Measurement System. If the bank deems the forex risk excessive, it may require conversion to RMB. The entity must file a 'Cross-Border Income/Payment Declaration Form' within 5 working days after receipt.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

PayPal Fit: High

Not viable in mainland China. Fintech EMIs (e.g., Revolut, Wise Business, or PayPal Business) are not licensed by SAFE/PBOC to provide FX accounts or domiciliary settlement services for onshore PRC entities. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: HK$7.5 million for intercompany financing / intangible transactions and HK$300 million for tangible sales / services; Master File triggered when the multinational group has consolidated revenue of HK$750 million or more.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have separate ESR, but the IRD applies the territorial source principle and substance-over-form doctrine. To sustain an offshore claim or avoid transfer pricing adjustments, the JV must perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: strategic procurement, pricing decisions, product design ownership and risk absorption. A Hong Kong Chinese JV that merely routes contracts and invoices without local staff / offices will fail the CIGA analysis and be taxed on a net basis with penalty surcharges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: Breach of IRO Sections 80(3) and 82: criminal fines up to HKD 50,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, plus additional tax equal to 3 times the tax evaded under Section 82A.

Director personal liability: The same sanction applies personally to directors who sign or negligently permit incorrect returns; the IRD may assess directors personally and seek asset restraint / travel restriction (blacklisting).
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Adjustment under IRO Section 50AA and Section 50J: the IRD makes an arm's length adjustment, imposes additional interest, and, where the inaccuracy is attributable to carelessness, a penalty up to 200% of the tax difference; failure to produce documents under Section 51C incurs a fine of HKD 50,000 plus daily penalty of HKD 500 per day.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for fines / imprisonment where a knowingly false transfer pricing declaration is submitted; they may also be subject to disqualification and the penalty may be recovered from their personal assets if the company is used as a vehicle for tax evasion.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the JV's separate legal existence under the substance-over-form rule and assess profits directly; the Companies Ordinance allows the Companies Registry to strike off the company if it is deregistered for inactivity; penalties under IRO Section 82A (additional tax up to 3x) apply for incorrect returns induced by the shell arrangement.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to act as a shell with no real operations may be fined up to HKD 300,000 under the Companies Ordinance; where fraudulent evasion of tax is established, directors face criminal prosecution with imprisonment and personal restitution orders, plus travel bans and exit-refusal notices.
