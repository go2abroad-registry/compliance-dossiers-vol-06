Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Hong Kong has no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a private company limited by shares. The company may adopt an authorised share capital, allot shares at par or at such premium as the board determines, and issue partly paid shares if the Articles permit. No blocked escrow account or frozen deposit arrangement is required under Hong Kong law; the subscription consideration may be paid directly into the corporate bank account after incorporation.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certification under the Fire Services Ordinance Cap. 95 and Fire Safety Buildings Ordinance Cap. 572, including automatic alarm, emergency lighting, smoke extraction, extinguishers and means of escape; a registered structural engineer must certify floor loading capacity for storage racking in the warehousing portion under the Buildings Ordinance Cap. 123; the Labour Department must accept the Industrial Undertaking Registration and verify workplace ventilation, noise control and dust exposure limits; and an Employees Compensation insurance policy must be in force before any worker enters the site.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, accessible via the eTAX electronic portal (https://www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Hong Kong private limited company (the JV entity), the Companies Registry automatically notifies the IRD of incorporation. Within 7 to 14 days, the IRD issues a Business Registration (BR) Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). This BR Certificate constitutes the entity's Tax Reference Number (TRN) for all Hong Kong tax purposes. Foreign non-resident entities without a Hong Kong legal presence that commence business in Hong Kong must apply for BR within 30 days of commencement using the prescribed application form (available on the eTAX portal or at the IRD's Business Registration Office). Required documents include: certified copy of the foreign entity's certificate of incorporation or equivalent, a Hong Kong registered office address or business address, proof of identity for authorized representatives (e.g., passport), and a completed application form. Once approved, the BR Certificate is issued and must be renewed annually (if HKD 2,000 to HKD 7,250 depending on type). There is no separate TIN or VAT number; the BR Number serves as the universal tax identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Nil (Hong Kong does not impose any Value Added Tax, Goods and Services Tax, or Sales and Use Tax; only limited customs and excise duties on a narrow band of merchandise such as alcohol, tobacco, or petroleum, under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109; plastic toy manufacturing and exports are outside this scope)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax exists. A Business Registration (BR) is required for all entities carrying on business in Hong Kong, regardless of turnover. For export declarations, a licence is not required except for controlled goods; however, exporters of plastic toys need only submit electronic Export Declaration forms to the Census and Statistics Department when the consignment exceeds HKD 45,000 (per shipment).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, therefore the concept of zero-rating for exports does not arise. Goods exported from Hong Kong are entirely outside the scope of any consumption tax. Instead, the relevant tax treatment concerns profits tax territoriality: profits derived from goods manufactured and sold from Hong Kong are taxable in Hong Kong unless the profit sourcing can be shown to be entirely outside Hong Kong (offshore claim). For a pure re-export trade or manufacturing where the essential profit-creating operations (contract negotiation, sale, and delivery) occur overseas, the profits may be treated as offshore and not subject to Hong Kong profits tax. For customs purposes, export duty is zero on plastic action figure model kits; no excise stamping or clearance is required.

  • Electronic Export Declaration (Form 1) submitted through the Government's Trade Single Window, containing the shipment value, commodity code (HS 9503.00 or related), and destination country – mandatory for all consignments exceeding HKD 45,000
  • Commercial Invoice (rendered in English or Chinese, showing seller, buyer, detailed description of goods, unit price, total amount, and Incoterms)
  • Packing List (details of cartons, weights, and volumes)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (transport document evidencing export)
  • Bank Credit Confirmation / Telegraphic Transfer Voucher or SWIFT Note evidencing actual cross-border payment from the overseas buyer – retained to substantiate the true commercial nature and to support any offshore profit claim
  • Email correspondence / signed purchase order (to evidence contractual negotiations undertaken outside Hong Kong, if relying on offshore treatment)
  • Copy of shipping instruction and insurance certificate (if any)
  • Manufacturer's certificate of origin (optional, for preferential trade treatment, though not a tax requirement)

None – Hong Kong has no statutory mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping requirement. Business invoices (paper or electronic) are issued by commercial prerogative, and the IRD accepts them as long as they satisfy record-keeping requirements under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For customs, the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations require an electronic export declaration (Trade Single Window – TSW) to be lodged before goods are exported; this is a trade statistics/customs compliance, not a fiscal clearance.

Given the absence of VAT, there is no VAT clawback mechanism. However, for profits tax purposes, if a taxpayer claims offshore tax exemption and the IRD's audit later determines that the profits are sourced in Hong Kong, the IRD may raise additional assessments within the statutory period (up to 6 years; 10 years in case of actual fraud), charge interest, and impose penalties of up to 300% of the undercharged tax plus a fine up to HKD 50,000 (Inland Revenue Ordinance, s.82A). For failure to lodge the required Export Declaration or for late submission, the penalties under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations can reach HKD 50,000 and forfeiture of goods for repeat offenders. Proactive documentation in the form of the above bundle is critical to withstand scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial capital account convertibility. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, are fully convertible and subject to truthfulness and consistency verification through banks. In contrast, capital account items remain restricted and require case-by-case approval, prior SAFE registration, and qualified domestic or overseas institutional investor schemes. Enterprises receiving foreign direct investment (FDI) must complete FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE or its delegated banks, and then open a Renminbi or foreign currency capital contribution account to receive injection funds. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted only after annual audit, tax clearance certificates have been issued, and the bank verifies the authenticity of the underlying board resolution and distribution statement. Since 2012, China abolished compulsory export proceeds surrender; exporters may retain foreign currency in current account deposit accounts, but they must repatriate overseas proceeds to domestic designated accounts and report through the cross-border receipts and payments statement system within the statutory time frame. The SAFE verifies export proceeds through the monitoring system, and if proceeds are not repatriated or settled within 30 days for general trade, the entity may face penalties or downgraded conversion quotas. Payment for imports likewise demands a valid trade contract and customs declaration to obtain the RMB or foreign currency. Overall, while current account convertibility is liberalized, profit distribution requires compliance with tax and SAFE rules, and FDI capital can be converted into RMB only against real payment orders unless the entity qualifies for the pilot voluntary settlement of capital account receipts. These factors collectively make cross-border capital flows subject to multilayered oversight and documentary rigor.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Exporters must repatriate foreign currency earnings to China within 180 days from the date of collection of export proceeds (unless SAFE grants an extension). There is no mandatory surrender ratio; enterprises may keep proceeds in a current account foreign exchange account, but they must complete the international receipts and payments declaration and match the export customs data through the export foreign exchange monitoring system. Under the general trade model, foreign exchange earnings should be received and verified within 30 days after the date of customs declaration, with a grace period up to 180 days for exceptional circumstances. For goods subject to special regulations, surrender or settlement may be accelerated depending on the exporter's credit category.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Low feasibility. The PRC does not license foreign digital banks or fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) to provide direct corporate deposit or payment services in mainland China. Companies are legally required to maintain bank accounts with licensed domestic commercial banks for cross-border trade settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business or Airwallex cannot act as primary treasury rails for a Sino-Foreign JV, as they lack a local banking license and cannot open bank identification numbers (BIN) or connect to China's CIPS and SWIFT settlement interfaces without a correspondent local bank. They may be used as an auxiliary collection tool for international e-commerce sellers (e.g., PayPal for customer receipts), but all foreign exchange conversion and capital verification must flow through a domestic designated bank to satisfy SAFE reporting and tax authorities. Fintech solutions like PayPal and WorldFirst can serve as payment gateways for the e-commerce channel, but they are not legal substitutes for a bank account and cannot execute capital injection or profit repatriation. For low-risk standard manufacturing businesses, the prudent banking structure remains a direct domestic corporate account with a Tier-1 bank, with a separate PayPal wallet for online sales collection that periodically settles to the domestic account through approved payment institution channels.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The JV must first secure an FDI registration certificate (FIC) from SAFE, then open a foreign currency capital account at a designated tier-1 domestic bank. The overseas investor executes a SWIFT MT103 or MT202 wire to the capital account using purpose code 621901 (Direct investment – initial contribution) or 622901. After receipt, the bank verifies the inflow against the FDI quota and issues a foreign exchange declaration (table for FDI). For settlement into RMB, the JV submits a Payment Order Form for Capital Account Settlement (支付结汇表) supported by authentic commercial contracts (e.g., equipment procurement, raw material purchase, rent), and the bank credits the RMB into the operating account. Alternatively, if the entity qualifies under the pilot programme for voluntary settlement of capital account receipts (2020 expansion), the JV can convert the full capital amount to RMB and hold it in an RMB settlement account with use restricted to domestic payments under a negative-list regime. All inflows must be reported in the cross-border balance of payments statistics code (BOP) and the SAFE monitoring system within five working days.Outbound trade settlements for the JV's international disbursements (e.g., purchasing injection molding machines from Germany, resins from Japan, or trade commissions) are executed via SWIFT MT103 for high-value direct wire transfers, commercial letters of credit (LC at sight or usance) for larger and deferred payment transactions, and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for proven, low-risk counterparties. Each outbound transfer must be supported by a genuine trade contract, invoice, and customs declaration (if goods already shipped). For services or royalties, a tax withholding certificate from the State Taxation Administration is required. To comply with SAFE's authenticity rules, the bank will independently verify the transaction under the 'no real transaction, no foreign exchange' principle. The JV should pre-notify the bank of scheduled disbursements and keep a rolling set of source documents to avoid payment delays.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility. The PRC does not license foreign digital banks or fintech EMIs (Electronic Money Institutions) to provide direct corporate deposit or payment services in mainland China. Companies are legally required to maintain bank accounts with licensed domestic commercial banks for cross-border trade settlement. Offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business or Airwallex cannot act as primary treasury rails for a Sino-Foreign JV, as they lack a local banking license and cannot open bank identification numbers (BIN) or connect to China's CIPS and SWIFT settlement interfaces without a correspondent local bank. They may be used as an auxiliary collection tool for international e-commerce sellers (e.g., PayPal for customer receipts), but all foreign exchange conversion and capital verification must flow through a domestic designated bank to satisfy SAFE reporting and tax authorities. Fintech solutions like PayPal and WorldFirst can serve as payment gateways for the e-commerce channel, but they are not legal substitutes for a bank account and cannot execute capital injection or profit repatriation. For low-risk standard manufacturing businesses, the prudent banking structure remains a direct domestic corporate account with a Tier-1 bank, with a separate PayPal wallet for online sales collection that periodically settles to the domestic account through approved payment institution channels.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 20,000,000 for related-party tangible asset transactions; RMB 10,000,000 for intangible asset or financial asset transfers; RMB 40,000,000 for all other related-party transactions aggregated.

Economic substance scope: PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law prohibit arrangements lacking reasonable commercial substance. Substance-over-form is upheld in Chinese tax litigation. For Hong Kong incorporated group members, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2018 imposes Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) on relevant activities, including pure equity holding, financing, and intellectual property holding, requiring adequate employees, premises, and that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong. Furthermore, the Principal Purpose Test under the MLI may deny DTA benefits to shell companies without economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Underreporting)

Statutory sanction: Violation of Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law; administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%; criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines where the underpaid amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law if directors abuse the limited liability vehicle to evade taxes; directors can be held jointly and severally liable for resultant tax debts, subject to freezing of personal assets, exit bans or departure restrictions, and imprisonment alongside fines.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure or Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44, and 114; failure to produce documentation may trigger a recharacterization of profits with taxable adjustments, a penalty of RMB 2,000 to 10,000 for missing related-party forms, and additionally a penalty of up to RMB 100,000 for failure to provide documentation; underassessment penalties of 50% to 5x the additional tax also apply. Interest may be increased by 5 percentage points above the statutory benchmark.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or implement non-compliant intercompany arrangements may be jointly and severally liable with the company under civil remedies; repeated or wilful behavior can lead to personal fines, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for abuse of duty under the Company Law.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency (Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Mainland-HK Arrangement Article 4(5), MLI Principal Purpose Test, and PRC GAAR; retroactive recovery of underwithheld tax, plus interest and penalties; the tax authority may recast the transaction as directly taxed to the ultimate parent, resulting in a 50% to 5x unreported tax penalty.

Director personal liability: A director who architects or operates the shell to abuse the treaty is exposed to personal civil liabilities, including restitution of avoided tax; may be subjected to asset freezes, exit bans, and criminal charges for tax evasion and illegal cross-border transfers of capital, with imprisonment terms of 3 to 7 years if convicted.
