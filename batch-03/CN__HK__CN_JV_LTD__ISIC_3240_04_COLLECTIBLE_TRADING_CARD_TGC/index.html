Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) — Electronic Tax Bureau (e-tax) online portal

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) with the local branch of the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the Business License automatically contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 calendar days of issuance, the JV must complete tax registration through the STA's electronic tax bureau by filing the registration form, uploading the Business License, Articles of Association, legal representative and chief financial officer identity documents, proof of bank account opening in mainland China and the registered-office lease or title deed. The tax authority will issue the TIN, which is the USCC itself, and simultaneously record the VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale). If the foreign shareholders maintain a Hong Kong SAR branch or permanent establishment, separate Business Registration must be obtained from the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR; no additional PRC TIN is obtained by non-resident shareholders unless they derive China-source income, in which case a Non-Resident Enterprise TIN is issued by the local STA office with jurisdiction over the payer. The PRC TIN and VAT registration are mandatory prerequisites for issuing VAT special invoices and claiming input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero rating is available for the sale of goods, including collectible trading cards, if the seller is a PRC VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from mainland China customs territory (including to the Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory), the export customs declaration is filed before the sailing/aircraft departure date, and the export proceeds are received in a PRC bank account. The goods must not be classified as prohibited or restricted export commodities, and the entire export chain—from manufacturing of the security holographic cold-foil cards through final sale to an overseas buyer—must be supported by traceable customs, logistics, and payment documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (electronic export goods declaration filed with the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, including the unique customs declaration number and the ISIC 3240 / HS trade description)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual physical discharge from mainland China customs territory to the overseas destination
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate or SAFE bank export-proceeds collection advice evidencing that the export proceeds were received and credited to a mainland China foreign-exchange settlement account
  • Export sales contract, final commercial invoice and detailed packing list, each matching in quantity, unit price and total value to the customs declaration

The STA operates the Golden Tax Phase IV integrated invoice management system and the mandatory cloud-based electronic invoice clearance service. All VAT invoices, including the special VAT invoices used for export refund claims, must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoicing Platform using the full digital electronic invoice format. The platform assigns a unique digital fiscal stamp, validates the transaction against the general taxpayer VAT ledger in real time, and automatically rejects duplicate, over-threshold, or non-sequential invoice issuances. Paper invoices are no longer accepted for export zero-rating substantiation where the digital electronic invoice mandatory regime applies.

If the STA determines that the zero-rating claim was unsupported by the required customs and foreign-exchange documents, that the goods were not actually exported, or that the export proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day statutory period, the VAT refund on the related input tax is retroactively clawed back together with late-payment interest. The taxpayer is liable for an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to five times the amount of tax repaid wrongfully, and, in fraudulent or smuggled export cases, criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for tax evasion or the export-invoice fraud penalty regime. The STA cross-checks the General Administration of Customs export data, the SAFE export-proceeds collection database, and the VAT invoice declaration database to detect mismatches.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free-market, fully convertible external economy under the Linked Exchange Rate System. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible into USD, CNH, EUR, JPY and other major currencies within the statutory 7.75-7.85 per USD band managed by the HKMA. There are no surrender requirements, exchange licences, or conversion quotas for either current account or capital account transactions. Profits, dividends, royalties, and loan repayments may be freely remitted to mainland China, the United States, or other destinations provided that the corporate account is well documented and tax clearance and FATCA/CRS filings are current. The absence of FX controls means that the cross-border treasury challenge is not regulatory convertibility, but rather banking KYC and the reverse-flow of funds into mainland China. A Sino-Foreign JV in Hong Kong receiving capital from a mainland parent must satisfy SAFE's outbound investment registration, MOFCOM/NDRC approval, and bank verification of the ODI certificate before the remittance can be released. Once in Hong Kong, no further FX approval is required for deployment into trade payments, hedging, or intercompany settlement. This materially increases treasury efficiency but demands robust end-to-end compliance records to maintain bank relationships.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters may retain all foreign currency receipts in original currency, convert to HKD or any other currency, or deploy them freely for operating expenses and settlement. HKMA's AML guidance only requires transaction monitoring, EDD for trade-based money laundering red flags, and retention of trade documentation; it does not impose a collection timeline or central bank conversion obligation.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), acting as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of mainland China does not directly regulate the treasury accounts of the HK JV, but it governs mainland outbound remittances into the JV.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium for receivables collection, Low for direct trade finance and LC issuing. Hong Kong recognizes Stored Value Facilities (SVF) and, since October 2020, authorizes virtual banks under the Banking Ordinance. However, licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, and Airstar Bank are legal HKMA-regulated banks, whereas pure fintech EMIs like Airwallex and Currenxie may hold e-money licences in other jurisdictions but operate in Hong Kong only through licensed partner banks or SVF licences. An HK corporate can typically open an account at a licensed virtual bank for operational multi-currency receipts; however, these accounts do not replace Tier-1 commercial banks for issuing commercial letters of credit, obtaining foreign exchange lines, confirming documentary collections, or establishing CHATS settlement. Moreover, if the payment rails targeted by the business include Stripe and Authorize.Net, funds will be batched as card settlement via acquiring banks rather than EMI settlement; the JV should maintain a Tier-1 bank account as the master treasury account and may use a fintech EMI only as a downstream collection sub-rail, not for clearing or hedge execution.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Sino-Foreign JV in Hong Kong receiving equity capital from a mainland China corporate shareholder, the lawful routing chain is: (1) obtain NDRC/MOFCOM approval for the outbound direct investment (ODI); (2) register the ODI with SAFE and obtain the bank's Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate; (3) the mainland bank wires the approved registered amount via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's HK corporate account, using an approved remittance purpose code for overseas direct investment and the beneficiary account at an HKMA-regulated bank; (4) the HK bank files the transaction under applicable external financial claims procedures and applies standard EDD to source of funds; (5) a certified public accountant issues a capital verification/accountants' report once funds are received; (6) funds are credited to a designated capital account or retained in the general operating account depending on bank policy. No SAFE further approval is needed for deployment in HK; if funds are immediately intercompany-lent back to mainland, mainland laws may trigger foreign debt/quasi-equity filing and need pre-approval.For trade settlement in the collectible trading card business (holographic foil, preprint, printing, packaging, shipping), the JV should use: (a) SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier wire transfers up to agreed credit limits; (b) irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) for large production runs with banks negotiating under the seller's export LC; (c) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A under URC522) for recurring deliveries between the JV and foreign distributors; (d) CHATS in HKD/USD for real-time settlement of local Hong Kong payments; (e) CNY/CNH cross-border remittance through a CNY clearing bank, preferably Bank of China (Hong Kong), using CIPS rails for payments to mainland suppliers. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong imposes transfer pricing documentation obligations on entities with related-party transactions exceeding HKD 5,000,000 in aggregate for transfers of goods or provision of services, HKD 1,000,000 for financial transactions, and HKD 100,000 for intangible property transfers in a year of assessment. A full Local File and Master File must be prepared if the connected transaction exceeds HKD 50,000,000 (goods/services) or HKD 10,000,000 (financial transactions and intangibles), or if the total turnover exceeds HKD 400,000,000 and the total connected transactions exceed HKD 8,000,000. The Hong Kong entity must also prepare transfer pricing documentation for transactions with its mainland China affiliate when the aggregate amount exceeds HKD 5,000,000, regardless of whether the mainland parent prepares a PRC Local File under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has adopted a ‘territorial source’ and ‘economic substance’ doctrine in practice, requiring the JV to demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong. The Inland Revenue Ordinance and the 2018 transfer pricing rules incorporate the substance-over-form principle, and since 2018 Hong Kong’s two-tiered profit tax system and the Economic Substance Requirements in the context of the EU’s anti-tax-avoidance review mean that a Hong Kong entity dealing in collectible trading cards and intellectual property rights must maintain real presence: office premises, local staff, local board meetings, management decision-making, and contractual risk management. A central management and control test applies: a JV that executes contractual arrangements in mainland China but holds Hong Kong board meetings with no meaningful substance will be treated as a PRC-controlled foreign company, and the Hong Kong IRD may re-characterize offshore claims. The Hong Kong representative office of the mainland parent, and the JV itself, must maintain contemporaneous records of CIGA to support the Hong Kong profits tax filing and any reduced-rate or exemption claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s. 82(1): on conviction, a fine of HKD 50,000 and additional tax of three times the amount of tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for three years. IRD may also raise amended assessments without time limit where fraud or wilful evasion is established, and the District Court may issue a confiscation order for proceeds of crime.

Director personal liability: Company directors are jointly and severally liable to pay the tax undercharged if they signed the incorrect return; the IRD may blacklist the director through departure control under IRO s. 76, obtain a travel ban preventing departure from Hong Kong, and pursue civil recovery against personal assets. Directors may also be prosecuted under the Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 200) for conspiracy to defraud, punishable by 14 years’ imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRO Part 9A, s. 50A and s. 50AAP: the IRD may adjust the income or expense to the arm’s-length amount, deny deductions, and impose penalties up to 100% of the additional tax attributable to the adjustment. In egregious cases, s. 80(2) and s. 82(1) expose the JV to fines of HKD 50,000 and treble tax payment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved non-arm’s-length pricing may be held personally liable under s. 74A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance for aiding and abetting tax evasion. Personal civil liability includes restitution of the overstated expenses and underpaid tax; the IRD can issue a director disqualification order and retain a departure prevention direction if the director seeks to leave Hong Kong. Criminal exposure is a fine of HKD 50,000 and three years’ imprisonment for each false return.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s. 776 and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) impose due-diligence and record-keeping obligations; a shell company may be struck off under s. 746, and the Financial Services and Treasury Bureau may impose penalties of HKD 1,000,000 and seven years’ imprisonment for money laundering offences under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455).

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the JV to operate as a shell may be convicted of the offence of carrying on business with intent to defraud creditors under s. 36(1) of the Crimes Ordinance, punishable by imprisonment for up to 10 years. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and issue a personal asset freezing order; the IRD and Hong Kong Police Joint Financial Intelligence Unit may place the director on a financial blacklist, bar directorship of any Hong Kong company for up to 15 years, and impose a travel ban. Civil liability includes restitution of proceeds and compensation to the tax authority.
