Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under section 658 of the Companies Ordinance, a Hong Kong company must have a physical registered office in Hong Kong; a post-office box is prohibited. Virtual office providers that maintain a genuine street address and accept service of process are accepted by the Companies Registry, but the Inland Revenue Department and banks will generally reject a bare virtual office or commercial mailbox as sufficient commercial substance for a company operating payment rails such as Shopify Payments and Stripe. For this custom mechanical keyboard keycap business, the relevant tax authority and acquiring banks will expect a physical operational footprint, at least a shared office or flexible workspace with lease evidence, local business records, and the ability to conduct a site visit. A pure virtual arrangement materially increases the risk of bank account refusal, enhanced due diligence, and a central-management-and-control dispute with the Inland Revenue Department.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: E-Tax Bureau (e-tax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (EJV) incorporated in mainland China, the company must complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes. Following SAMR registration, the EJV must complete tax registration at the competent tax authority within 30 days by submitting the Tax Registration Form, Articles of Association, business license, and legal representative identification. A Value-Added Tax (VAT) general taxpayer status must be applied for if annual turnover exceeds the statutory threshold or voluntarily, enabling self-issued VAT special invoices. For foreign non-resident entities without a physical establishment in China, a tax registration for non-resident enterprises may be required for withholding purposes or for VAT registration under the Business Tax Reform for cross-border services; however, in this structure, the mainland JV is the domestic registered taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR or other jurisdictions are eligible for VAT zero-rating or export tax rebate (exemption and credit/refund of input VAT) under Article 15 of the PRC VAT Law. The exporter must be the actual seller, must have received foreign exchange or equivalent consideration, and the goods must legally exit China customs territory. Gratis or trading-sample exports not generating foreign-exchange receipts may be ineligible. For cross-border e-commerce exports, eligibility covers goods sold through recognized cross-border e-commerce platforms and exported via designated regulatory pilot zones, provided VAT invoices for purchases are retained and customs clearance is recorded as cross-border e-commerce retail exports.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Customs Declaration Form with electronic customs manifest via China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / despatch advice for cross-border logistics)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC / bank foreign exchange receipt voucher issued by SAFE-designated bank)
  • VAT special invoices for purchase of raw materials and production inputs (to support input VAT refund calculation)
  • Official Purchase and Sales Contract or cross-border e-commerce order data (transaction records, platform data) confirming the overseas buyer and export value

China operates the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice (E-Invoice) system and the nationwide 'Golden Tax Phase IV' (Jin Shui Si Qi) platform. VAT-special invoices (VAT Fapiao) must be issued through the official e-invoice platform with real-time reporting to the tax authority. Full digital invoice pilots (fully digitalized e-invoices) require system API integration with the STA for invoice issuance, verification, and red-letter cancellation. No analogue of EU VAT OSS; fiscal clearance is achieved via automated tax control numbers embedded in fiscal invoices, and the system supports blockchain-based e-invoice pilots in selected regions.

Export tax refunds are subject to rigorous scrutiny by the State Taxation Administration and Customs. False export declarations, fraudulent VAT invoices used solely to inflate refunds, or failure to repatriate eligible foreign exchange within the statutory deadline may result in: denial of the zero-rating/refund claim; clawback of any VAT refund paid; late-payment surcharges; monetary penalties of 50% to 5 times the amount of fraudulently obtained refund; downgrading of tax credit rating; suspension or revocation of export tax refund qualification; and referral to customs anti-smuggling or public-security economic-crime authorities for criminal liability if fraud exceeds criminal thresholds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong jurisdiction operates under a free and open current and capital account regime. The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible against all major currencies, with no statutory surrender requirements, no exchange control approvals, and no profit remittance moratorium. Capital injections, dividends, loan repayments, and trade settlements may flow freely through licensed banks in Hong Kong using SWIFT, CHATS, and CLS. However, the mainland Chinese shareholders and the underlying manufacturing supply chain remain subject to PRC exchange controls. China maintains controlled current-account convertibility subject to truthfulness and documentation review, while capital-account transactions, including outward direct investment into the Hong Kong joint venture, require SAFE/NDRC registration and a documented genuine trade or investment basis. Profit repatriation from the mainland to Hong Kong, if the JV operates a mainland subsidiary, would be treated as FDI dividends, requiring a capital verification audit, a SAFE statement, audited financials, and a board declaration of distributable profits. In Hong Kong itself, no export proceeds surrender rules apply, and the entity may maintain multi-currency accounts without forcing conversion. The practical hurdle is not Hong Kong exchange controls but China's SAFE paperwork and the need to demonstrate the economic substance of the cross-border transaction chain through evidence of contracts, customs declarations, and bank flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong-incorporated entity: no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds received in any currency; HKD and foreign currencies are fully fungible under the HKMA's free capital-flow regime. For mainland Chinese affiliated exporters: SAFE requires export foreign-currency proceeds to be repatriated into the mainland via a designated foreign-exchange bank within 180 days from the customs declaration date; conversion to CNY is not mandatory, but retention in a foreign-exchange account is capped at 50% of the enterprise's prior-year current-account foreign-exchange receipts on a rolling basis, with any simultaneous excess amount required to be settled with the bank. Non-resident Hong Kong shareholders are exempt from mainland surrender requirements.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the banking and currency authority for the Hong Kong SAR; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) regulate cross-border renminbi and foreign exchange activities relevant to mainland shareholders and counterparties.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech electronic-money institutions operating in Hong Kong, such as Airwallex or Currencycloud, are licensed by the HKMA either as Money Service Operators or under the Operating a Payment Processing and Stored Value Facility regime. They can provide multi-currency virtual account numbers, payment aggregation, and e-commerce settlement rails, which are useful for Shopify and Stripe receivables. However, they are not authorised deposit-taking institutions under the Banking Ordinance, so they cannot offer corporate escrow, trade finance, overdraft, or bulk FX treasury services. Mainland Chinese payment platforms would require a cross-border payment business licence and an onshore/offshore fund-flow filing to handle JV money; without that, they are legally barred. Viability rating: Low as a primary corporate banking rail; Moderate as an ancillary collection channel after a licensed bank account is fully operational.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Parts 9 and 12 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company must maintain accounting records and prepare financial statements, and must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) to audit those statements unless a valid statutory exemption applies. A private company is generally exempt from audit for a financial year only if it qualifies as a small private company and the members validly pass an audit-exemption resolution under s.359D and s.359E. A small private company must satisfy at least two of the following three conditions for that year: (a) annual revenue not exceeding HK$25,000,000; (b) gross assets not exceeding HK$75,000,000; and (c) average number of employees not exceeding 50. A company limited by guarantee is exempt only if its annual revenue does not exceed HK$5,000,000; dormant private companies may also be exempt if they have not carried on business and satisfy s.5 of the Companies Ordinance. The audit is compulsory for any private company that fails to satisfy the small-company threshold test, for any company governed by a statutory or contractual consolidation requirement, for all public companies and non-private companies, and for any company whose members holding at least 5% of voting rights object to the audit-exemption resolution. The independent auditor must be an entity qualified under the Professional Accountants Ordinance (Cap. 50) and independently appointed under s.406 of the Companies Ordinance.
