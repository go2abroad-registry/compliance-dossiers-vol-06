Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the PRC mainland JV: State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the competent local tax service bureau, with the electronic taxpayer service portal (E-Tax / National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau Platform). For the Hong Kong SAR corporate investor: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong SAR, with electronic tax services via the IRD e-Tax / GovHK portal, and registration with the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the official 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally constitutes the entity's Tax Identification Number (TIN). Step 2: Complete the post-incorporation taxpayer filing within 30 days of the business license date through the national E-Tax platform, where the USCC is automatically populated as the TIN, and confirm VAT, enterprise income tax, and payroll/social insurance registrations. Step 3: Register as a VAT general taxpayer through the E-Tax platform once annual taxable sales reach or are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or voluntarily if accounting standards are met; this is required for export VAT zero-rating and refund eligibility. Step 4: For any foreign non-resident seller supplying goods or services to China without an establishment, the Chinese buyer or customs is generally the withholding or collection agent, and the non-resident should obtain a Chinese non-resident TIN at the competent STA office if it becomes a direct taxpayer for China-sourced income or VAT registration. Step 5: For the Hong Kong parent, complete Hong Kong business registration at the IRD Business Registration Office and obtain the Hong Kong Business Registration Number, and obtain a Hong Kong Certificate of Residence when claiming treaty protection under the Mainland/Hong Kong Arrangement for withholding tax relief.

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover-based exemption for legal-person tax registration: the JV must complete VAT registration within 30 days of issue of the SAMR business license. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer turnover threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales; a taxpayer below this threshold may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer. General VAT taxpayer status is mandatory for export zero-rating and VAT refund. The Hong Kong parent has no PRC VAT registration threshold if it does not have a PRC establishment; if it merely receives offshore dividends, no PRC VAT registration arises.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the PRC mainland customs territory, including die-cast scale automotive models sold by the JV to overseas buyers, with Hong Kong SAR treated as outside the mainland VAT area, qualify for VAT zero-rating when the seller is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the sale is a genuine cross-border transfer of title and risk, the goods are declared to and released by China Customs, and the sales proceeds are collectible and repatriated in foreign exchange. The input VAT attributable to the export production is refundable under the export tax rebate exemption, credit, and refund mechanism; qualifying cross-border e-commerce export goods may be cleared under the applicable customs supervision codes for e-commerce exports while still meeting the same zero-rating substance tests.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the electronic export goods declaration submitted through the China International Trade Single Window, with customs release endorsement; for e-commerce exports, the applicable e-commerce export declaration or goods list)
  • Ocean or airway transport document evidencing actual shipment from the PRC to the overseas buyer (Bill of Lading, Master or House Air Waybill, or postal or courier receipt)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt records and SAFE-registered export proceeds collection certificate evidencing repatriation of the exported goods settlement amount
  • Signed export sales contract or overseas purchase order identifying buyer, goods, Incoterms, price, and payment terms
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list prepared in the exporter or consignor name and consistent with the customs declaration
  • If demanded by the competent STA on audit: certificate of origin, inspection certificate, insurance certificate, and the VAT general taxpayer registration and export tax rebate filing made through the E-Tax platform
  • Supporting e-commerce platform and sales-channel records when payments are received through PayPal or SWIFT wire, including payment reconciliations showing buyer identity, order ID, and settlement currency

PRC-mainland taxpayers must issue VAT invoices under the STA's Golden Tax IV digital fiscal platform. The national roll-out of the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice requires issuance, real-time upload, encryption, dynamic QR-code anti-counterfeiting, and automatic fiscal clearance through the STA's E-Tax or National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau Platform; approved enterprises may integrate issuance through approved APIs and ERPs. Legacy block-tax-control invoices are being migrated to the Digital E-Invoice system. Export sales are documented through export declarations rather than domestic VAT special input invoices, and export data is matched electronically between China Customs, the STA, and SAFE.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are provisional and subject to post-clearance audit by the STA in coordination with China Customs and SAFE. If export declarations are false, goods are not actually exported, payment is not collected and repatriated within the statutory period, documentation is forged, or value is overstated, the STA will disallow and clawback the VAT refund and input credits, impose statutory late-payment surcharges on the tax due, and assess an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Tax Collection. Serious or fraudulent claims will be referred to criminal investigation for fraudulent export tax rebates under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with fines, confiscation, imprisonment up to life, loss of export tax rebate qualification, and inclusion in the tax non-compliance blacklist. No Hong Kong offshore status or intermediary structure can shield the PRC legal entity or its responsible persons from these clawback and penalty rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB remains convertible on current-account items for genuine goods and service transactions, while capital-account flows such as inbound equity injections and cross-border loans require registration or approval by SAFE. Hong Kong SAR is a separate customs territory with no foreign-exchange controls; the HKD is freely convertible under the Linked Exchange Rate System maintained by the HKMA within the 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar band. For the mainland joint venture, export proceeds from die-cast automotive model sales are current-account receipts: they must be collected through a designated foreign-exchange bank and may be retained in foreign currency or voluntarily settled into RMB, with no mandatory surrender to the central bank. Inbound capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be wired into a SAFE-registered FX capital account, verified by a Chinese certified public accountant, and only then converted and deployed in accordance with the national negative list. Profit remittance to the overseas partner is a lawful current-account payment once annual statutory audit, final tax clearance, and board resolution for dividend distribution are completed; banks then perform an authenticity review and process the remittance without an annual quota. In practical terms, documentation completeness, corporate governance transparency, and anti-money-laundering clearance are the true hurdles for profit repatriation, not an absolute prohibition on conversion.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Goods Trade Administration Notice No. 1 (2012), goods export proceeds must be matched to customs export declarations and repatriated through a domestic designated foreign-exchange bank by the contractual payment maturity; where no maturity is stated, collection must be completed no later than 90 days from the export declaration date unless an extension is registered in the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; up to 100% of export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency settlement account or converted voluntarily at the market rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the delegated central-bank and currency-board authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Legal feasibility for fintech EMIs and digital payment platforms such as PayPal, Airwallex, and Payoneer is Low for the primary treasury rails. Mainland China's foreign-exchange control system requires goods export proceeds and inbound FDI equity to be received through designated commercial banks that report to SAFE; no fintech EMI is licensed as a designated bank for FDI capital verification or customs-linked export collection in China. Hong Kong permits SVF and payment licenses, but those entities do not grant full corporate deposit-taking or trade-finance infrastructure and cannot issue compliant SAFE transaction codes. EMIs remain useful only for auxiliary low-value e-commerce receipts from collector marketplaces, and must be swept to the licensed bank treasury after satisfying supporting documentation.

The Hong Kong shareholder should adopt the following compliant sequence: execute the Sino-foreign joint venture contract and board resolutions, complete SAFE foreign investment registration, obtain the onshore JV's business license, open a designated FDI foreign-exchange capital account at the licensed domestic bank, and remit equity from the Hong Kong corporate account by SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE cross-border receipt/payment purpose code for direct investment equity injection into a foreign-invested enterprise. The remitter must be the registered HK investing entity and not an affiliated third party. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legal feasibility for fintech EMIs and digital payment platforms such as PayPal, Airwallex, and Payoneer is Low for the primary treasury rails. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (standard profits tax rate under Section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap.112; two-tiered concessionary rate of 8.25% applies to the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits for one eligible entity per group)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by a Hong Kong company to non-resident shareholders (Section 26 exempts dividends from profits tax); however, dividends paid from the PRC operating subsidiary to the HK JV are subject to PRC withholding tax at the statutory 10% rate, reduced to 5% under the China-HK Double Taxation Arrangement where the HK JV beneficially owns at least 25% of the PRC subsidiary's capital

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$5,000,000 aggregate annual value per category of controlled transactions triggering mandatory Local File preparation under Section 50AAH and DIPN 59 (Revised 2022); a Master File is required where the total controlled transactions equal or exceed HK$2,500,000,000 OR total assets equal or exceed HK$1,280,000,000 and the average number of employees equals or exceeds 500; the HK JV manufacturing trading operation with export sales to PRC-related distributors and royalty payments to the PRC parent must monitor each category (purchase of raw materials, sale of finished die-cast models, payment of management fees, payment of royalties, interest on intercompany loans) separately

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong operates on the territorial source principle rather than a codified Economic Substance Regulation regime (unlike BVI or Cayman); however, DIPN 48 requires the HK JV to demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong to support an offshore profit claim; for a Sino-Foreign JV owning a PRC die-casting plant, the typical structure designates the HK entity as the principal manufacturer selling to global customers and the PRC entity as a contract processor; the IRD will examine: negotiation and conclusion of sales contracts, purchase of raw materials, storage and inventory custody, invoicing and payment processing, and board decision-making; if the CIGA are performed in the PRC by the subsidiary, then 100% of the HK JV's profit is deemed Hong Kong-sourced and taxable because the profit arises from the principal's operations; conversely, if the HK JV is a paper shell while the PRC subsidiary performs all manufacturing and sales, the economic substance doctrine in both jurisdictions will recharacterize the entire profit as PRC-sourced and impose 25% PRC enterprise income tax plus 10% dividend withholding, with the HK entity relegated to a disregarded intermediary

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Fraudulent Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: Section 82A(1) IRO: on conviction on indictment, a fine of HK$50,000 and treble the amount of tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for 3 years; Section 80 IRO: on summary conviction, a fine of HK$10,000 and treble the tax undercharged; the IRD may additionally raise an estimated assessment at 100% of gross sales proceeds where records are falsified or not produced; the PRC-side sanction under the Tax Administration Law Article 63 (偷税) is a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid plus daily overdue interest at 0.05%

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause to be signed a false profits tax return are personally liable under Section 82A(6) IRO where the offense is committed with their consent, connivance or attributable to their neglect; each director faces the same fine and imprisonment as the corporation; the IRD may apply to the District Court under Section 76 IRO for a departure prevention order (travel ban) prohibiting the director from leaving Hong Kong until all tax, penalties, and security are paid; personal civil liability extends to restitution of the tax undercharged and the director's personal assets may be subjected to a garnishee order; on the PRC side, the legal representative of the JV can be placed on the tax blacklist (联合惩戒) blocking exit visas, high-speed rail and air travel, and disqualifying the individual from serving as a director for up to 7 years
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Stateless Income Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under Section 50AAH IRO and Part 8A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (effective for basis periods commencing on or after 1 April 2018), the IRD will make a transfer pricing adjustment substituting arm's length consideration; where no documentation exists, the Commissioner may draw adverse inferences and assess tax on a deemed 15% profit margin under Section 60/61A-61B GAAR; penalties on the resulting undercharge apply under Section 82A (treble tax plus HK$50,000); the PRC counterpart can be subjected to a corresponding adjustment only if the HK profit is genuine; a failure to submit contemporaneous documentation also triggers an additional 5% surcharge under Section 82B and disallowance of the foreign tax credit

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the intercompany pricing without obtaining an independent TP benchmarking study are deemed negligent; the IRD can pierce the corporate veil under Section 82A(6) and hold the finance director and managing director jointly liable for the treble tax penalty; civil recovery actions permit the IRD to seize the directors' bank accounts up to the amount of the tax shortfall; where the TP adjustment exposes a pattern of deliberate suppression of taxable profits, the Department of Justice may commence criminal fraud proceedings under the Theft Ordinance and the Companies Ordinance Section 377 (fraudulent trading) carrying a fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 15 years
Shell Company DTA Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance (Treaty Shopping)

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Articles 47 and 120 and the PRC General Anti-Abuse Rules (State Taxation Administration Announcement 2018 No.70), the PRC tax authority may deny the DTA benefits, recharacterize the HK JV as a domestic Chinese resident company, and assess 25% enterprise income tax on all repatriated profits plus 10% dividend withholding tax; penalties under the Tax Administration Law Article 63 reach 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid, plus daily interest; in Hong Kong, Section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance voids the tax avoidance transaction and the IRD will impose profits tax on the full amount as Hong Kong-sourced; the HK JV's bank account may be frozen, and the Inland Revenue Department may issue a Section 76 departure prevention order against the China-based directors who fly into Hong Kong for account signings, effectively blacklisting them at the immigration boundary

Director personal liability: Under Section 61A(2) and Section 82A(6) IRO, directors must prove that the tax avoidance motive did not exist; failure leads to personal assessment on the director as the person who materially contributed to the arrangement; under Companies Ordinance Cap.622 Section 377, where the company is wound up and the shell was used to defraud the IRD and creditors, the court may order the director to personally contribute to the company's assets for the full amount of unpaid taxes plus penalties; criminal liability: a director found guilty of fraudulent trading faces imprisonment of up to 15 years and a fine of HK$5,000,000; on the Mainland, the Public Security Bureau may initiate criminal investigation under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion, with a maximum prison term of 7 years for taxpayers evading substantial amounts, and the individual will be subject to an exit ban and asset confiscation; the corporate veil is systematically pierced in both jurisdictions because the shell company is used as a sham to defeat mandatory tax laws, rendering the limited liability shield entirely ineffective against the responsible directors
