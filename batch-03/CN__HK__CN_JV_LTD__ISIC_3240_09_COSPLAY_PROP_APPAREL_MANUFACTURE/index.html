Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn; export customs authority is the General Administration of Customs of China via the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: Within 30 days of the business license date, perform tax registration with the competent local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau and file the statutory tax registration information to create the entity's tax registration record. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box election. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is mandatorily taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; foreign investors cannot elect pass-through treatment. Hybrid mismatch attenuation is achieved through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, controlled foreign corporation rules, thin capitalization, and anti-conduit measures, none of which is an elective mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding Renminbi (CNY) 5,000,000 trigger mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; exporters may voluntarily register below this threshold to preserve zero-rating and refund rights.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated export VAT applies to the cosplay costume, prop apparel and synthetic wig goods only when the goods are physically exported from the PRC Customs territory, the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer, the sale is made to an overseas buyer, the customs export declaration is recorded, and the foreign-currency export proceeds are repatriated and documented. Sales into the Hong Kong SAR customs territory are treated as exports. Qualifying cross-border services provided to an overseas recipient are zero-rated under the cross-border service rules only if the service is consumed entirely outside China; for a manufacturing business the goods export regime is the principal path.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration with export tax-refund special linkage filed through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical cross-border carriage of the goods
  • Foreign Exchange Collection Record issued by the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System (the PRC functional equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Input VAT documentation proving the VAT borne on purchased raw materials and production inputs

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Fully-Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform. VAT-registered enterprises must use the STA e-invoice platform to issue e-invoices carrying an electronic digital signature, anti-counterfeiting stamp, unique invoice number and QR code; invoice data are transmitted to the STA in near real time through standardized API interfaces, with no separate third-party fiscal clearance API. Customs and tax data for exports are synchronized through the China International Trade Single Window and the STA export tax-refund database.

The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly profile export refund claims under Golden Tax Phase IV. If zero-rating is disallowed due to false export declarations, incomplete documentation, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the 180-day window, the exporter must repay all refunded input VAT plus late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Fraudulent export refund claims are governed by the export tax-refund fraud provisions of the Tax Collection Administration Law, which require repayment of the falsely claimed refund plus a penalty of 100% to 500% of the refund amount, and permit unlimited retrospective clawback. False customs declarations can also trigger Customs administrative penalties and criminal liability for smuggling or tax evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a fully free capital account regime with no foreign exchange controls. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and trades within a defined band of 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar under the linked exchange rate system administered by the HKMA. There are no restrictions on inward or outward remittances, no prior approval requirements for capital account transactions, and profit repatriation from a Hong Kong entity is unrestricted. Export proceeds are not subject to any surrender requirement. However, because the JV is a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the sovereign parent being mainland China, cross-border flows between the Hong Kong entity and its mainland Chinese counterparties remain within SAFE's regulatory perimeter. For mainland Chinese parties, current account transactions such as trade settlements are convertible upon legitimate documentation, while capital account transactions such as equity injections into the Hong Kong entity or repatriation of dividends to a mainland shareholder require SAFE registration, a qualified external debt filing if applicable, and the use of designated foreign exchange or RMB accounts. The HKMA does not restrict the currency of invoicing or settlement, and the JV may maintain multi-currency operating accounts, including CNH offshore RMB accounts, to facilitate frictionless trade with mainland suppliers. The practical convertibility bottleneck is therefore not the Hong Kong entity but the mainland partner's compliance with SAFE's capital account infrastructure, including the foreign exchange monitoring system and the requirement to match payment purpose codes with underlying trade contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No surrender or mandatory conversion rules apply to export proceeds in Hong Kong SAR. The HKMA imposes zero statutory timeline or conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. If any mainland China affiliate of the JV receives export proceeds in foreign currency, SAFE requires such proceeds to be repatriated into the mainland within the standard goods trade foreign exchange supervision period, currently operating under a receipt-and-verification framework where exporters must bring export proceeds back to China and settle them into RMB or retain them in a foreign exchange receipt account within the general 30-day window, subject to SAFE's guidance for goods trade foreign exchange settlement.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for Hong Kong SAR; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) govern mainland China cross-border RMB and capital account matters

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only banks that do not hold a banking license under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance cannot legally accept deposits or provide full corporate banking services in Hong Kong. The HKMA authorizes virtual banks under a special license, and locally licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank, Mox Bank, and Airstar Bank are viable for low-risk retail-oriented entities, including cosplay costume manufacturing. However, offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex operate on partnership banking rails and typically do not provide native Hong Kong FPS (Faster Payment System) or CHATS clearing membership. Their use is feasible for collection-side integrations (Stripe, Shopify Payments) and limited foreign exchange execution, but their legal classification is as money service operators or stored value facility issuers rather than deposit-taking institutions. For a JV that needs robust treasury management, multi-currency clearing, trade finance instruments (LC and documentary collections), and seamless RMB settlement with mainland China, reliance on unlicensed offshore EMIs is rated low viability. A hybrid approach using a licensed Hong Kong tier-1 bank as the anchor treasury account and a regulated EMI for payment gateway settlement is the maximum permissible architecture, provided the EMI is in compliance with the HKMA's AML/CTF and sanctions requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound equity injection from the mainland Chinese shareholder into the Hong Kong JV must follow SAFE's overseas direct investment (ODI) compliance path. The mainland partner must first obtain a SAFE approval or filing for outward FDI, and the remittance should be transmitted from its domestic bank via SWIFT MT103 or the international payment instruction with a clear foreign exchange purpose code such as 622 (overseas investment) or 621 (working capital) depending on the nature of the transfer. The Hong Kong JV should receive funds through a designated capital escrow account at its tier-1 Hong Kong bank, where the bank will require a capital verification report issued by a certified public accountant to confirm the receipt and allocation of the funds. If the injection is from a non-mainland foreign shareholder, no SAFE approval is required, but the Hong Kong bank will still need the shareholder loan agreement or the resolution for equity issuance, corporate instrument of transfer, and proof of source of funds. All inflows must be coded with the appropriate purpose in the HK bank's transaction system and the remittance advice must state the nature of the inflow as 'equity injection' or 'shareholder loan' to prevent misclassification as trade proceeds. The JV's treasury should maintain a ledger connecting each capital injection to its registration documents to support future dividend repatriation or capital reduction.For outbound trade payments to mainland Chinese suppliers, the preferred clearing rail is an SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer in CNY (offshore CNH or onshore CNY) or USD via the Hong Kong bank's RMB real-time gross settlement (RTGS) infrastructure for immediate value. If a commercial letter of credit is used, the Hong Kong issuing bank transmits an SWIFT MT700 to the beneficiary's bank in mainland China, which is then negotiated under the UCP600 rules. Documentary collections may be executed under an SWIFT MT400 series with documents drawn against payment at sight or acceptance. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only banks that do not hold a banking license under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance cannot legally accept deposits or provide full corporate banking services in Hong Kong. The HKMA authorizes virtual banks under a special license, and locally licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank, Mox Bank, and Airstar Bank are viable for low-risk retail-oriented entities, including cosplay costume manufacturing. However, offshore EMIs such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Airwallex operate on partnership banking rails and typically do not provide native Hong Kong FPS (Faster Payment System) or CHATS clearing membership. Their use is feasible for collection-side integrations (Stripe, Shopify Payments) and limited foreign exchange execution, but their legal classification is as money service operators or stored value facility issuers rather than deposit-taking institutions. For a JV that needs robust treasury management, multi-currency clearing, trade finance instruments (LC and documentary collections), and seamless RMB settlement with mainland China, reliance on unlicensed offshore EMIs is rated low viability. A hybrid approach using a licensed Hong Kong tier-1 bank as the anchor treasury account and a regulated EMI for payment gateway settlement is the maximum permissible architecture, provided the EMI is in compliance with the HKMA's AML/CTF and sanctions requirements.
