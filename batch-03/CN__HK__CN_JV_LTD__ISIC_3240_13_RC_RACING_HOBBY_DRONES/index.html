Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; a Hong Kong private company limited by shares is a separate legal and taxable person, and is not a pass-through entity. Profits tax is chargeable under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong, applying the territorial source principle.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no transparent entity election for companies, and no hybrid mismatch election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. A Hong Kong company is taxed at entity level; no pass-through status is available to a company limited by shares.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax. Hong Kong levies indirect taxes only on a narrow class of dutiable commodities, principally alcoholic liquors, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol, under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109). There is no general retail sales tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold. Every business, including a newly incorporated company, must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business, regardless of turnover. A Dutiable Commodities Permit is required only for excisable goods and is not applicable to the carbon-fiber milling, brushless motor integration, or FPV racing drone retail scope under ISIC 3240.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST system and therefore no export zero-rating rate or VAT refund claim mechanism. Exports of FPV racing drones and related carbon-fiber or brushless motor components from Hong Kong are outside the scope of any indirect tax; no VAT return, export VAT certificate, or zero-rating substantiation is required. For profits tax purposes, the territorial source principle applies separately, and export profits are taxable only if sourced in Hong Kong; the entity should maintain evidence of where contracts were negotiated and concluded if it intends to claim that profits are outside the charge to Hong Kong profits tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls administered by SAFE. Current account transactions are substantially convertible: bona fide trade in goods and services may be settled through designated foreign exchange banks with documentary authenticity verification only, in line with the Foreign Trade and Exchange Administration Reform. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment equity injections, shareholder loans, and dividend repatriation, require SAFE registration, dedicated capital escrow accounts, and post-event verification. Since the 2012 reform, export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory physical surrender; however, all foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a Chinese designated bank within 180 days of the customs export declaration and either retained in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily converted into RMB at the prevailing central parity rate. Profit remittance by a Sino-foreign equity joint venture is permitted only after completion of the annual statutory audit, filing of the corporate income tax return, and submission to the designated bank of the tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, board dividend resolution, the SAFE foreign investment registration certificate, and the foreign exchange payment application. Retained earnings may be remitted in the original currency or converted into RMB, but the dividend distribution must be sourced from after-tax profits as evidenced by the audited accounts. Non-trade current account items such as royalties, technical fees, and service payments are scrutinized through the tax and SAFE joint monitoring system.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a Chinese designated foreign exchange bank within 180 days of the customs export declaration date, extendable to 360 days for capital goods under special SAFE approval. Since the 2012 reform, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain the full amount in a foreign currency settlement account within its SAFE-approved quota or voluntarily settle into RMB at the PBOC central parity rate. All export receipts are matched against the goods trade monitoring system using customs declaration data, and any discrepancy exceeding the tolerance threshold triggers verification and a documentary audit.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low and legally non-viable as a primary treasury rail. Foreign fintech electronic money institutions such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud are not licensed banking entities in China and are barred by SAFE and PBOC from servicing onshore RMB and foreign currency settlement for a domestic Sino-foreign joint venture. These EMIs cannot open capital escrow accounts, cannot perform SAFE registration, and cannot execute tax-cleared dividend remittances. They may only support auxiliary cross-border e-commerce collection flows (e.g., PayPal, Stripe) if the JV routes those flows through a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution such as LianLian Pay or PingPong, which in turn settles through a designated foreign exchange bank. All statutory treasury functions—capital injection, current account settlement, trade verification, and profit repatriation—must be executed through a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank under PBOC and SAFE supervision. The Hong Kong holding vehicle may use an EMI for its offshore working capital, but the mainland JV remains entirely dependent on the onshore banking system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder remits the registered capital in USD or HKD via SWIFT MT103 to the JV FDI Capital Escrow Account at the designated bank, using China International Balance of Payments code 622011 for foreign direct investment equity capital. The remitting bank must include the JV full legal name, the SAFE FDI registration certificate number, and the description Registered capital injection per JV contract. Upon receipt, the designated bank performs verification in the SAFE capital project information system, matches the amount against the approved registered capital quota, and issues a transaction confirmation. The capital may be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB under the payment-first verification-second principle; utilization of the funds is restricted to approved operational purposes and cannot be applied to securities investment, entrusted loans, or real estate without express regulatory approval. The JV must complete capital verification through a licensed Chinese CPA firm and file the capital verification report with the State Administration for Market Regulation.For settlement of overseas suppliers of carbon-fiber raw materials, brushless motors, and electronic components, the JV may execute SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with trade purpose code 121010 for export receipts or 122010 for import payments, subject to presentation of the underlying customs declaration, commercial invoice, and purchase contract to the designated bank for authenticity verification under the goods trade electronic monitoring system. For bulk orders, the JV may issue an irrevocable letter of credit through the designated bank using SWIFT MT700, or use documentary collections on a documents against payment or documents against acceptance basis. RMB-denominated trade settlements with Hong Kong counterparties may be cleared through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System on the offshore leg and the China National Advanced Payment System on the onshore leg. All outbound payments must satisfy the SAFE current account authenticity review, and the enterprise must reconcile each payment with the corresponding customs record within the statutory time window.Onshore CNY deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts with a designated forex bank, supported by genuine current account trade exposure and documented future cash flows, with maximum tenor typically twelve months extendable upon SAFE approval.Cross-currency swaps for hedging foreign-currency shareholder loans or capital obligations, subject to the real demand principle and a strict prohibition on speculative or naked positions.Foreign exchange option strategies, including purchased calls and puts or option combinations, for downside protection on export receipts and import costs, permitted under SAFE rules requiring a real transaction background.Non-deliverable forwards are not permissible for onshore Chinese entities; the JV is restricted to the onshore deliverable forward market, while the Hong Kong parent may access the offshore NDF market independently but cannot net-settle or transfer the hedge to the mainland entity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally non-viable as a primary treasury rail. Foreign fintech electronic money institutions such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currencycloud are not licensed banking entities in China and are barred by SAFE and PBOC from servicing onshore RMB and foreign currency settlement for a domestic Sino-foreign joint venture. These EMIs cannot open capital escrow accounts, cannot perform SAFE registration, and cannot execute tax-cleared dividend remittances. They may only support auxiliary cross-border e-commerce collection flows (e.g., PayPal, Stripe) if the JV routes those flows through a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution such as LianLian Pay or PingPong, which in turn settles through a designated foreign exchange bank. All statutory treasury functions—capital injection, current account settlement, trade verification, and profit repatriation—must be executed through a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank under PBOC and SAFE supervision. The Hong Kong holding vehicle may use an EMI for its offshore working capital, but the mainland JV remains entirely dependent on the onshore banking system.
