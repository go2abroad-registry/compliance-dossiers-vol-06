Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: China Electronic Taxation Bureau / 电子税务局 (e-Tax Bureau) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; for Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) at https://www.ird.gov.hk

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established in Mainland China, the enterprise must first complete the establishment registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), after which the unified social credit code (18-digit USCC) is automatically issued and serves as the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN). The joint venture must then complete tax registration with the competent local branch of the State Taxation Administration within 30 days of the issuance of the business license, submitting the business license, articles of association, joint venture contract, legal representative identity documents, and proof of registered address. The STA will activate tax registration for corporate income tax, value-added tax, and other applicable taxes; in the Hong Kong SAR, if the JV is also registered for Hong Kong tax purposes, the entity must apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance, and if taxable profits are anticipated, apply for a separate Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) via the IRD's taxpayer registration process, subject to submission of the company incorporation certificate, registration of foreign company in HK where applicable, business address proof, and director/shareholder details. For VAT purposes under domestic PRC law applicable to the mainland entity, the procedure is the same as the unified social credit code registration, and the VAT registration is part of the STA multi-tax registration; no separate VAT ID number is issued beyond the USCC, although the STA maintains a VAT taxpayer qualification file.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For PRC VAT purposes, all enterprises established in China are generally required to register for VAT upon establishment. For foreign non-resident sellers of goods or services into China, the threshold for VAT registration is a periodic taxable sales amount of RMB 5,000,000 (annual equivalent) for enterprises; however, foreign entities selling goods into China through cross-border e-commerce can use the cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot zone market registration or use a mainland agent for customs and VAT registration, and are subject to the import VAT collection at customs instead of routine domestic registration unless they establish a fixed place of business. For Hong Kong SAR, there is no VAT/GST system and no registration threshold; the relevant indirect tax is Hong Kong's duties on certain goods, but the Hong Kong IRD does not impose VAT or GST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC Regulations on the Implementation of the VAT Law, goods exported by a registered VAT general taxpayer qualify for the zero-rated (VAT exemption with credit for input VAT) treatment, subject to the exporter being the actual seller of the goods, export declaration to a PRC customs clearance port, and receipt of export sales proceeds in foreign exchange within the prescribed repatriation period. The exported goods must be declared through China Customs with the correct export commodity classification (HS code) and must be shipped out of China. For dental ceramic blocks (ISIC 3250) with a HS code in Chapter 68 or Chapter 90, the export must meet all customs and tax refund filing requirements. Zero-rating is available only to VAT general taxpayers; small-scale taxpayers may qualify for VAT exemption on exports in some circumstances but generally use a simplified collection rate. The export enterprise must also complete the export tax refund/tax-exempt filing with the STA, usually through the China Electronic Taxation Bureau export tax refund module, within the statutory filing period. Qualifying offshore digital services may also be zero-rated under the pilot policy for cross-border taxable services, provided the service recipients are wholly offshore and the services fall within the positive list of cross-border taxable services.

Mainland China operates the China Electronic Invoice system under the State Taxation Administration's VAT invoice management system (增值税发票管理系统). The mandate requires registered VAT general taxpayers to issue digital VAT invoices through the e-Tax Bureau electronic invoice platform, using the national unified invoice service platform for ordinary and special VAT invoices. Real-time fiscal clearance is implemented through the STA's full digital electronic invoice pilot (数电票) system, under which invoices are issued with a 20-digit electronic invoice number, an anti-fraud watermark, and verified in real-time by the STA blockchain-based invoice service. Certain high-risk enterprises may be selected for the comprehensive cloud-based invoice monitoring system requiring automated data submission through the application programming interface (API) of the e-Tax Bureau. No physical fiscal device stamping is required for most digital invoices, but the system includes anti-fraud QR code and digital stamping through the national electronic invoice service platform. For Hong Kong SAR, no electronic invoicing mandate exists; the IRD requires only records sufficient to support profits tax returns, and no VAT clearance or fiscal stamping mandate applies.

Under Article 63 of the PRC Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and Tax Collection and Administration, if an exporter obtains export tax refunds or zero-rated treatment by fraudulent means such as false export declarations, overstatement of export goods, repeated refund claims, or use of false customs declaration documents, the STA shall recover the tax refunded, impose a penalty of 50% up to five times the amount of the fraudulently obtained refund, and suspend the export tax refund eligibility for a specified period. If the exporter fails to present adequate documentation within the statutory retention period, the export tax exemption is disallowed and VAT at the standard rate becomes payable, including late payment surcharge calculated at 0.05% per day (annualized daily surcharge) for overdue, and if non-compliance amounts to criminality under the Criminal Law, the responsible persons may face criminal liability up to life imprisonment in severe cases. Additionally, failure to repatriate export proceeds in foreign exchange within the prescribed 180-day deadline (or the extended period if approved) results in the reversal of the zero-rating and the enterprise may be subject to penalties under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. The STA also performs post-refund audits and risk-control screening; any false or unverified declaration leads to clawback of the full refund plus statutory penalties and interest, as well as possible downgrade of the exporter's tax credit status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with distinct treatment of current and capital account items. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in the dental consumables manufacturing sector, current account transactions such as export revenue from CAD/CAM zirconia blocks, foreign supplier payments for raw materials, and allowable business expenses are fully convertible for trade-related settlements, subject to genuine documentation and reporting through the SAFE's monitoring system. Capital account transactions, including inbound equity injections from the Hong Kong partner, remain regulated under the Foreign Investment Law and the Catalogue of Encouraged Industries; capital injections must be registered with SAFE via the local branch and a dedicated foreign currency capital account must be opened at a designated bank. Profit remittances to foreign shareholders are permitted on a current account basis but require a validation of the audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificate, and bank documentation evidencing the approved distribution; in practice, banks will scrutinize the timing and source of profits to prevent disguised capital flight. Conversion of retained export proceeds is discretionary, as China abolished mandatory surrender requirements in 2012; however, all foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into the country within the statutory window of 90 days from the export customs declaration date, extendable to 180 days with documented justification, and either retained in a foreign exchange account or settled into RMB. The foreign debt ratio and net assets position of the JV will also influence the ability to access onshore FX hedging products. Overall, the regime is moderately liberalized for trade but administratively burdensome for capital movements, requiring robust compliance infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

As per SAFE's Regulations on the Administration of Foreign Exchange for Trade in Goods, export proceeds arising from the sale of dental zirconia blocks must be repatriated to China within the statutory window of 90 days from the customs export date, extendable to 180 days for trade terms requiring extended payment cycles. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the JV may choose to retain the foreign currency in a designated foreign exchange settlement account or convert it into RMB at a bank at the prevailing market rate. All conversions must be supported by valid customs declarations, commercial invoices, and the bank's electronic registration records. Failure to repatriate within the deadline may trigger administrative penalties and the reclassification of the export proceeds as foreign debt.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

The use of offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Wise Business, or Payoneer for the operation of a mainland-registered Sino-Foreign JV is legally ineffective and commercially prohibitive. These non-bank financial institutions do not hold a Chinese banking license, are not approved by the PBOC or SAFE to handle Renminbi or foreign exchange current accounts for onshore entities, and cannot serve as the designated bank for capital account registration or foreign debt contracting. The PBOC's strict licensing regime under the Administrative Measures for the Online Payment Business and the Rules for Implementation of the Foreign Exchange Administration require all private and corporate foreign exchange transactions to be routed through licensed domestic banks or approved electronic payment institutions that are themselves licensed by SAFE. Consequently, any attempt to use an unlicensed EMI is high risk, likely subject to account freezing, and will breach the SAFE filing obligations. The only viable role for digital EMIs is limited to pre-onboarding scenario analysis or salary disbursement to overseas individuals, but never for the entity's Treasury clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB

Economic substance scope: For the Hong Kong affiliate, Hong Kong does not apply a Caribbean-style statutory Economic Substance Regulation, but the PRC tax authorities require substantive economic activity to be proven before granting treaty benefits. The HK entity must demonstrate real offices, staff, decision-making authority, accounting records and substantive Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for its procurement, distribution, financing or IP holding functions. In the PRC, the general anti-avoidance rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, together with SAT Announcement No.2018 No.28 on beneficial ownership and substance-over-form, must be satisfied; a shell HK company that performs no meaningful functions, controls no digital or physical assets and bears no real risk will not receive the reduced wedge in gross withholding and may also trigger a special tax adjustment against the JV.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Abuse of DTA Treaty Benefits / Shell Company Structure

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under SAT Announcement No.9/2018 on Beneficial Ownership and Article 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law on General Anti-Avoidance; assessment of the additional 5% statutory WHT difference; daily late-payment interest at 0.05%; a penalty from 50% to 500% of the under-collected tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law; possible revocation of the HK Tax Residency Certificate by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced under the PRC Company Law where the corporate form is abused and creditor or tax rights are materially damaged; directors who signed off on the false treaty claim are jointly and severally liable for unpaid WHT, penalties and interest. The legal representative may be blacklisted for tax dishonesty, have assets frozen, and be prohibited from leaving China under Article 12 of the Exit and Entry Administration Law. If the artificial structure is intended to evade tax, criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 attaches, with fixed-term imprisonment up to 3 years for ordinary amounts and 3-7 years for especially large amounts, together with fines.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may perform a special tax adjustment under Article 41 of the PRC CIT Law and SAT Decree No.6, re-characterizing the transaction at arm's length, imposing a 5% annual interest surcharge on underpaid tax, adding a 50%-500% penalty under Article 63 in cases of deliberate mispricing, and refusing to mitigate assessment where the taxpayer fails to file required contemporaneous documentation. Where the HK affiliate performs only sales-agent functions, the over-allocated profit may be reclassified as disguised dividends subject to additional 10% WHT.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved or failed to correct fraudulent transfer pricing policies are subject to personal civil restitution liability once the company is unable to satisfy tax debts due to asset stripping to the HK affiliate. The lead director and finance director may be issued administrative blacklisting, travel restrictions and asset preservation orders. If the mispricing is coupled with false documentation and intentional under-declaration of taxable income, PRC Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205 allow imprisonment of the responsible officers for tax evasion and false invoicing, including imprisonment of 3-7 years for large amounts and fines.
False Export Invoicing, Customs Undervaluation and Cross-Border Circular Payments

Statutory sanction: Issuance of false special VAT invoices violates PRC Criminal Law Article 205 and is punishable by fines, confiscation of property and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts; the Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a 50%-500% penalty on the tax evaded and demands full restitution of the fraudulent VAT export refund; customs law permits fines up to three times the evaded duties, revocation of the AEO Customs status, and confiscation of illegally exported goods.

Director personal liability: Directors, financial controllers and legal representatives are personally liable for the refunded amounts and can lose personal assets through civil restitution proceedings. The authorities may impose exit bans and have the director blacklisted under the joint tax dishonesty mechanism. Criminal exposure includes imprisonment up to 7 years under Article 201 for tax evasion and, under Article 205 for fraudulent special VAT invoice issuance, imprisonment of up to 10 years or life in the most serious cases depending on the invoiced amount, with fines and confiscation of personal wealth; a one-person-shareholder director who mixes personal funds with corporate money faces unlimited personal liability.
