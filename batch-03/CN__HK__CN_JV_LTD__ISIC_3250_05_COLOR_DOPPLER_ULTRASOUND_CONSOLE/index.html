Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). The company may issue one or more shares with a nominal value as stated in the Memorandum. Share capital may be wholly unpaid, partly paid, or fully paid, but banking and financial counterparties may require a realistic paid-up capital commitment commensurate with the manufacturing scale. No government-mandated escrow or blocked capital account is required at incorporation; however, the Joint Venture Contract may impose a contractual paid-up capital timetable and a joint escrow arrangement with a licensed bank in Hong Kong or the People's Republic of China.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a PRC resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax on its worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China. Pass-through eligibility is not available for this corporate vehicle, and Hong Kong does not reclassify a PRC-resident JV for PRC tax purposes.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election for Chinese resident enterprises. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual aggregate taxable sales for compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration under the current unified small-scale taxpayer threshold; an entity may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer before exceeding this threshold. Hong Kong imposes no VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods from PRC customs territory, including the color Doppler ultrasound console classified under ISIC 3250, qualifies for VAT zero-rating under the PRC VAT system where the goods are actually exported, the customs export declaration is lawfully lodged, the sale is made to a non-resident buyer in Hong Kong or another overseas jurisdiction, and the export proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign-exchange bank. Qualifying exports are relieved from output VAT and the associated input VAT is refundable through the statutory export-tax exemption, credit, and refund method for manufacturing exporters.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration) bearing the General Administration of Customs declaration number and the applicable HS and CIQ classification for the color Doppler ultrasound console
  • Original or authenticated transport document set comprising Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or combined-transport waybill evidencing physical movement of the goods out of PRC customs territory to Hong Kong SAR or another export destination
  • Commercial export contract, signed commercial invoice, packing list, and any medical-device export license or free-sale certificate required under the PRC Medical Device Supervision Regulations
  • Bank-issued foreign-exchange export collection confirmation or FX settlement receipt (the PRC equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate) evidencing receipt of the offshore buyer's payment in the invoiced currency through a designated foreign-exchange bank and matched to the customs export declaration
  • Electronic export-tax-refund declaration file submitted to the STA under the exemption-credit-refund method, containing the customs declaration data, VAT invoice data, and foreign-exchange collection data required for automated cross-matching

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's all-digital electronic invoice platform or an authorized VAT invoice management system, which requires real-time API transmission of invoice data to the STA, assignment of a unique digital serial number, and STA-approved anti-counterfeit digital stamping including QR codes and digital signatures. The Golden Tax Phase IV system performs centralized fiscal clearance and automatically cross-matches invoice data against VAT returns, customs export declarations, logistics information, and bank settlement records; VAT filing is on a monthly or quarterly basis through the Enterprise Electronic Tax Bureau.

The STA, the General Administration of Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly scrutinize export zero-rating claims by matching customs export data, VAT invoice data, logistics data, and foreign-exchange receipt data. Where zero-rating is claimed without actual export, without full proceeds repatriation within the 180-day monitoring period, or with false documentation, the STA will reverse the zero rating, require repayment of any VAT refund previously paid, impose the statutory late-payment surcharge at 0.05 percent per day, and assess a penalty of one to five times the amount of the fraudulent refund under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Unverified export-receipt declarations trigger retrospective clawback of the refunded VAT. In serious cases, the exporter is referred for criminal prosecution for defrauding export tax refunds under the Criminal Law, which may result in imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a currency board system but the Hong Kong dollar is fully freely convertible and there are no exchange controls governing capital or current account transactions. All major currencies, including USD, EUR, RMB, JPY, GBP, may be held in licensed banks and repatriated without prior approval. Current account convertibility is absolute for trade-related payments, and capital account movements such as equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, and redemption of shares are also unrestricted in Hong Kong. However, when the end-capital source is mainland China, the parent company must first obtain SAFE outbound investment registration and a bank must verify that the underlying ODI or shareholder loan has been recorded. The main profit remittance hurdles for a Hong Kong JV are therefore not Hong Kong restrictions but the need to demonstrate beneficial ownership, complete audited financial statements, a valid tax resident certificate, and the absence of untaxed profits. The HKMA does not impose surrender or approval requiring exchange for dividends; only a board resolution and solvency statement are needed. In practice, bank FX compliance will require the eventual dividend remittance to match the JV's retained earnings and the underlying trade purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory surrender or conversion ratio; an entity may retain 100% of export proceeds in any permitted currency. Under mainland China's SAFE regime, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic current account within 90 days after customs declaration, and the exporter may freely settle them into RMB or hold foreign currency. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, but any conversion must be conducted through a designated Tier-1 bank and matched to the customs data.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC) as the authority governing cross-border renminbi conversion and capital flows from mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as a primary corporate treasury route. Hong Kong only permits full corporate settlement accounts, trade finance, letters of credit, and SWIFT/CHATS access for licensed Authorized Institutions under the Banking Ordinance. Pure fintech EMIs, unless licensed as licensed banks or virtual banks, cannot issue documentary credit, process capital injections, or provide reliable cross-border clearing rails. An HKMA-licensed virtual bank may be used as a secondary or disbursement account, but a Tier-1 commercial bank remains mandatory for the core treasury structure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound equity or shareholder loan remittances from mainland China, the parent must first complete SAFE ODI and overseas investment registration. The remittance is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's designated Tier-1 bank in mainland China to the JV's Hong Kong account. The payment message must include the SAFE-designated ODI capital transaction code, the purpose of remittance, and the JV's registration and certificate of incorporation. After receipt, the Hong Kong bank will hold the funds in a capital escrow account until capital verification is completed. The capital verification requires a Hong Kong CPA verification certificate or accountant's confirmation that the paid-up capital has been lawfully received, and for mainland China sourced funds, the SAFE foreign exchange registration form and the parent's bank outward remittance approval must be archived.Trade settlement will use SWIFT MT103 for direct open-account transfers, MT700 for commercial letter of credit issuance, and documentary collection instructions for D/P or D/A. For USD payments, clearing occurs via Fedwire or CHIPS through the Hong Kong bank's US correspondent. For RMB payments, use the RMB RTGS system via HKMA or the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS). For HKD payments, use the Hong Kong CHATS system. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary corporate treasury route. Hong Kong only permits full corporate settlement accounts, trade finance, letters of credit, and SWIFT/CHATS access for licensed Authorized Institutions under the Banking Ordinance. Pure fintech EMIs, unless licensed as licensed banks or virtual banks, cannot issue documentary credit, process capital injections, or provide reliable cross-border clearing rails. An HKMA-licensed virtual bank may be used as a secondary or disbursement account, but a Tier-1 commercial bank remains mandatory for the core treasury structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 100 million annual related-party transaction volume in any single covered category (tangible asset transfer, intangible asset transfer, financial asset transfer or service transaction) triggers mandatory Local File preparation; group-wide consolidated revenue of at least CNY 2 billion or the cross-border threshold under SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 triggers the Master File; contemporaneous documentation must be in place before the CIT filing deadline and submitted within 15 days of a tax authority request.

Economic substance scope: China taxes enterprises based on incorporation and place of effective management; profits must be allocated in accordance with the actual performance of Core Income-Generating Activities and assumption of significant risks under the State Administration of Taxation Special Tax Adjustment Measures. The Chinese JV engaged in manufacturing and distribution of color Doppler ultrasound consoles must retain commensurate operating profit; the Hong Kong parent cannot capture residual profit without employees, premises, managerial functions and real commercial activity in Hong Kong; the General Anti-Abuse Rule allows recharacterization of transactions lacking commercial rationality, and Hong Kong's own economic substance expectations for the FSIE regime reinforce that the HK parent must be a substantive business entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC Corporate Income Tax Law Article 41 and SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 allow the tax authority to disregard the invoiced price and re-determine taxable income under the arm's-length principle; the Law on Tax Administration and Collection Article 63 imposes a civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily late-payment interest; serious cases constitute criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 and may lead to imprisonment for up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law veil-piercing principles and tax enforcement practice, directors who knowingly execute or approve below-market invoices and diversion of profits may be jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax and penalties; personal assets may be attached, the responsible director may be placed on the cross-border travel blacklist under Tax Collection Law Article 44, and a director with criminal intent faces imprisonment plus restitution.
DTA Treaty Shopping and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the Beneficial Ownership test articulated in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the General Anti-Abuse Rule; the shortfall WHT is reassessed at the 10% statutory rate, plus daily late-payment interest and a 50% to 500% penalty for false withholding declarations; the misleading Beneficial Ownership certificate may also trigger administrative fines and criminal exposure for fraudulent tax filing.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the Dividend Resolution and the Beneficial Ownership declaration may be personally assessed for the unpaid WHT when the corporate form is used to abuse the treaty; personal civil liability extends to restitution of the tax shortfall, payment of penalties and disgorgement of unlawful dividends; criminal liability arises under Criminal Law Article 201 and can result in imprisonment and a travel ban for willful treaty fraud.
Failure to File Transfer Pricing Documentation and Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: Tax authorities may invoke Law on Tax Administration and Collection Articles 62 and 64 to impose RMB 2,000 to RMB 50,000 filing fines, apply deemed income assessment, and impose a 50% to 500% understatement penalty; SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6 also allows separate special tax adjustment documentation penalties; the tax assessment window for special tax adjustments extends up to 10 years with imputed interest accruing from each adjusted year.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for finance and tax reporting who knowingly omit related-party schedules or approve false financial statements can be held personally liable for the resulting tax loss; civil claims may compel the director to compensate the Chinese JV and the tax authority; personal exposure includes asset freezing, travel restrictions, disqualification from board service, and criminal prosecution for intentional failure to truthfully report with imprisonment up to 7 years under Criminal Law Article 201.
