Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: NAT Taxpayer Electronic Tax Filing System (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in the People's Republic of China must first obtain a Business License from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which simultaneously issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Following license issuance, the entity must complete tax registration through the NAT Taxpayer Electronic Tax Filing System by submitting the Business License, Articles of Association, valid passport or corporate registration certificate of the foreign investor, and proof of the registered address. The competent tax authority will activate the USCC as the VAT taxpayer identification number and enroll the entity in the taxpayer classification as a General VAT taxpayer or Small-Scale taxpayer. The foreign parent entity must also provide its overseas Tax Identification Number for beneficial ownership and treaty withholding purposes, and the joint venture must register for payroll and social insurance contribution codes prior to hiring staff.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Chinese Renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current account items such as cross-border trade settlements, medical device export proceeds, and service payments under the SAFE Trade in Goods framework, subject to documentary verification. Capital account convertibility, including foreign direct investment injections, shareholder loans, profit repatriation, and liquidation proceeds, remains regulated and requires stepwise SAFE registration and bank-level compliance checks. As a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the entity may convert foreign currency contributions into RMB after filing an FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) Registration with SAFE, but each capital conversion must reference the FDI Registration Code and be supported by an audited capital verification report. Profits may be distributed and remitted to foreign shareholders only after annual audit, statutory reserve appropriation, and tax clearance; the remittance is processed through a domestic bank with a 'Profit Remittance to Shareholders' application. All export proceeds must be repatriated onshore within 90 days of the export customs declaration; the JV may retain foreign currency in a designated account up to its SAFE-approved quota (typically 30%–50% of prior year current account receipts) or settle the balance into RMB. There is no legal restriction on the use of RMB for current account transactions, but large outbound remittances require a valid underlying trade contract and shipping/credibility evidence. The Hong Kong SAR itself exercises no exchange controls; the Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible, although the Linked Exchange Rate System pegs HKD to USD. Consequently, the treasury blueprint leverages Hong Kong as the natural liquidity hub for settling USD, EUR, and RMB transactions without capital controls, while ensuring all onshore RMB flows comply with SAFE's convertibility and verification rules.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE Circular 2012 and the Regulations on Foreign Exchange Control of the People's Republic of China, the mandatory surrender system has been abolished. For the export of goods such as surgical laparoscopy towers, 100% of export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated onshore within 90 days of the export customs clearance date. The JV may retain up to its annual Foreign Exchange Settlement Account Quota, usually 30%–50% of the previous year's verified current account earnings, in a foreign currency account at a designated bank. Any amount above this retention limit must be settled into RMB within the bank's reporting cycle, typically five banking days. A practical conversion ratio of 30%–50% is applied at the bank's discretion for retained balances; the residual is automatically converted into RMB under the current account 'Paid-in Verification and Settlement' framework. Companies that fail to meet the 90-day repatriation deadline may incur a SAFE penalty of 0.5%–1.0% of the amount and face downgrading of their Foreign Exchange Collection Verification rating.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore Chinese Equity Joint Venture; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR treasury hub

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Neat, and Currencycloud are legally barred from directly servicing the onshore PRC EJV because they lack SAFE licenses to open RMB capital accounts, process FDI capital injection, or settle export proceeds on behalf of a Chinese legal entity. The PBOC restricts non-bank payment institutions from handling cross-border goods trade settlement unless they hold a dedicated Cross-Border Renminbi Clearing License, which most global EMIs do not possess. Therefore, the onshore JV must rely on licensed Chinese banks for all RMB and FX operations. For the Hong Kong treasury shell, if separately incorporated, a licensed HKMA Major Payment Institution can provide multi-currency virtual accounts for receipts from Western distributors under a sandbox regime, but cannot process SAFE-registered capital flows. Viability rating: Low for onshore operations; Medium for offshore ancillary receipts only, subject to a separate HK entity and a commercial bank partner holding the funds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The foreign investor, likely a Hong Kong shareholder, shall remit USD or HKD via SWIFT MT103 from its Hong Kong bank account to the PRC JV's Capital Escrow Account (FDI Capital Account) at ICBC. The wire must include the SAFE FDI Registration Code (referred as 'FDI Entity Operation Information' code) and the Remittance Purpose Code 'Foreign Direct Investment - Capital Injection' (code 602, or 'FDI - Share Capital'). Prior to submission, the JV must have completed the SAFE FDI Registration and obtained a bank-issued 'Foreign Exchange Registration Confirmation' from the local SAFE office. The receiving bank will validate the capital amount against the JV's registered paid-in capital schedule, perform a capital verification in cooperation with an auditor, and convert the funds to RMB using the prevailing spot rate upon receiving a Payment Order (Form 206) from the JV. A separate 'FDI Payment Confirmation Form' is required if the funds are used for machinery purchase; this form is filed with the bank under the 'Settlement Verification' route. Any over-contribution above the regulatory limit for an EJV, which is 15% above registered capital only under special approval, must flow back to the remitter.Trade settlements for global distribution of laparoscopy towers will be cleared through the Hong Kong treasury hub using SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers (T/T) initiated by buyers under payment-in-advance or open account terms. For larger distributors, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) at sight is issued by the buyer's bank and confirmed by HSBC Hong Kong; once the JV ships and presents the compliant shipping documents including bill of lading, invoice, packing list, and NMPA export certificate, the bank pays through the documentary credit channel. Imported components such as optical lenses and stainless steel trocars will use documentary collections (D/P or D/A) through Bank of China onshore branches, with the export remittance proceeds settled via the 'Registered Export Proceeds' system. All outbound wire instructions must state the SAFE Trade in Goods verification number and the purpose code 'Export Goods' (code 101) when the RMB proceeds are settled in the domestic account. For RMB settled transactions, CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) is the preferred rail for direct clearing with PBOC members, ensuring zero screening time and lower correspondent costs.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts with a tenor of up to 12 months, executing through a licensed bank, based on a physical underlying export order or import order (current account exposure), capped at 150% of actual exposure as verified by commercial invoices.FX options on a zero-cost collar (up-and-out put/up-and-in call) for hedging short-term receivables in EUR/USD, permitted for current account transactions under CBIRC Hedge Principle with no speculation allowed.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for the term of the FDI loan, matched with the JV's long-term machinery financing; the swap must be fully collateralized by the capital account loan and approved by the local SAFE branch via the 'transferable documentation' route.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only for the Hong Kong treasury shell to hedge CNY trade receivables of third parties, but not for the onshore JV because PRC SAFE prohibits NDF speculation by domestic entities; therefore, all onshore hedging uses deliverable instruments.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Neat, and Currencycloud are legally barred from directly servicing the onshore PRC EJV because they lack SAFE licenses to open RMB capital accounts, process FDI capital injection, or settle export proceeds on behalf of a Chinese legal entity. The PBOC restricts non-bank payment institutions from handling cross-border goods trade settlement unless they hold a dedicated Cross-Border Renminbi Clearing License, which most global EMIs do not possess. Therefore, the onshore JV must rely on licensed Chinese banks for all RMB and FX operations. For the Hong Kong treasury shell, if separately incorporated, a licensed HKMA Major Payment Institution can provide multi-currency virtual accounts for receipts from Western distributors under a sandbox regime, but cannot process SAFE-registered capital flows. Viability rating: Low for onshore operations; Medium for offshore ancillary receipts only, subject to a separate HK entity and a commercial bank partner holding the funds.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million or more in controlled tangible-asset transactions, or RMB 100 million or more in controlled intangible-property, financial-transaction, or service-transaction volumes is the statutory threshold triggering Local File preparation; a Master File is required where the overseas group-wide revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion. Contemporaneous documentation must be prepared before the filing date and submitted within 20 days of a tax bureau request.

      
Economic substance scope: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 may disregard arrangements lacking commercial substance. The Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to companies carrying on 'relevant activities' (e.g. holding entities, financing, leasing, distribution, service centres) and require a Hong Kong presence with employees, premises and board decision-making to satisfy the 'Core Income-Generating Activities' (CIGA) test. For this medical-device JV, the manufacturing activities must be physically and operationally anchored in mainland China (production floor, engineering staff, supply-chain decisions), while the Hong Kong shareholder must prove real investment management and substance in Hong Kong to retain DTA benefits; otherwise the tax authority may deny treaty relief and recharacterise the transaction as a sham.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Falsified Intercompany Documentation
        
Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63, the tax authority may assess the underpaid EIT plus a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount evaded, daily late interest at 0.05%, and, if the taxpayer fails to produce TP documentation, may impose a Special Tax Adjustment under STA Announcement 2016 No. 42. In Hong Kong, failure to file correct returns attracts a penalty under Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 82A of up to 200% of the tax evaded.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute or approve the manipulated TP pricing may be jointly and severally liable for the evasion. The court may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors personally liable for the underpaid tax and civil restitution. In serious cases, a travel ban (exit basis) is imposed by the PRC tax authority, the legal representative is blacklisted, and criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 may lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines.

      

      
      

        Shell Company Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation
        
Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may disregard the conduit personality under EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), deny the treaty benefit, recharacterise the distribution as a direct payment to the ultimate non-resident, and apply the statutory 10% WHT plus penalties. If the underpayment is wilful, a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid WHT is imposed under Tax Collection Law Article 63. Hong Kong IRD may also react under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and raise additional profits tax and penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Directors of both the JV and the HK holding company who sign the false beneficial-ownership declaration are personally subject to criminal liability for tax evasion in China (imprisonment up to 7 years), asset freezing and confiscation. They may also be disqualified as directors in Hong Kong under the Companies Ordinance, and the PRC People's Court may enforce a travel blacklisting order against non-compliant legal representatives.

      

      
      

        Illegal Fapiao / False VAT and EIT Deduction Fraud
        
Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 205 criminalises the issuance or purchase of false VAT special invoices, with penalties of up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property, or 10 years-plus imprisonment for high-value fraud. Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a civil tax penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax, and the enterprise will be downgraded to tax credit class D, triggering stricter export refund controls.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve or instruct the use of false fapiao are personally liable for the evaded tax and face criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205, carrying imprisonment of up to 10 years or more, severe fines and forfeiture of assets. The court may order personal restitution of the full evaded amount, and the directors may be barred from leaving China (exit control), from serving as directors in any PRC company, and placed on the national tax default list.
