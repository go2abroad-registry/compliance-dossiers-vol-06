Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, accessed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) portal and the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform; if a separate Hong Kong wrapper vehicle is used for offshore sales or treasury functions, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is the competent authority for that wrapper, but the subject PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture obtains its tax identity in mainland China.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which is the PRC taxpayer identification number and VAT taxpayer number. Step 2: Through the STA e-Tax Bureau portal, file tax registration particulars including constitutional documents, investor identity, registered address, legal representative, specimen seals, and business scope. Step 3: Elect VAT taxpayer status after turnover assessment; if expected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory, otherwise small-scale taxpayer status can apply. Step 4: Apply for VAT invoice issuance entitlement, special VAT invoice quota, tax-control equipment enrollment, and fully digitalized electronic invoice activation. Step 5: Register payroll source withholding, social insurance-related reporting, and any required local surcharges. For a foreign non-resident enterprise that itself derives China-source income but is not a resident joint venture, the foreign enterprise or its PRC withholding agent must apply for a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number at the responsible STA service office, supplying notarized and legalized foreign registration documents translated into Chinese, proof of responsible person, a China place of service, and any binding tax treaty/Hong Kong resident certificate evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Corporate Income Tax law does not provide a check-the-box election; a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated in mainland China is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise taxable on worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available, and any hybrid mismatch distortions with the Hong Kong wrapper are addressed through China's general anti-avoidance rule, substance-over-form doctrine, and controlled foreign corporation rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 generally triggers mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; turnover below this threshold permits small-scale taxpayer treatment with applicable reduced collection rates and temporary monthly exemption thresholds for small-scale taxpayers where monthly taxable sales do not exceed RMB 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of physical goods manufactured in China, including disposable dialysis hemofilters, are eligible for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported out of China through customs, the exporter retains valid export documentary proof, the export proceeds are collected and repatriated through licensed banking/SAFE channels, and the export VAT refund claim is matched by the STA export tax rebate system. Qualifying cross-border services and offshore digital services supplied to overseas recipients may also be zero-rated under the cross-border service rules if the recipient is outside China, the service is used wholly outside China, and the required documentation supports the non-China consumption. Goods merely moved into special customs supervision zones do not automatically attain zero-rating unless subsequently exported or otherwise deemed exported under valid VAT rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Customs Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air cargo)
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate / bank-issued export proceeds receipt (FIRC-equivalent SAFE-monitored collection record)
  • Signed export contract and sales order
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Medical device export sales certificate and product conformity/sterilization evidence (for regulated medical devices)
  • Certificate of Origin (if required for preferential customs treatment or destination-country market access)
  • Electronic export VAT refund declaration data set matched with customs, logistics, and collection records

China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoices under the State Taxation Administration's fully digitalized electronic invoice system, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the STA national platform. General VAT taxpayers must use STA-approved e-invoice platforms, integrate billing systems via open interfaces/API connections, and comply with anti-fraud digital stamping, tax-control encryption, and automatic invoice data clearance requirements. Paper special VAT invoices remain subject to the traditional special VAT invoice management and reconciliation regime, including tax-control equipment sealing.

Export VAT refund claims and zero-rating positions are subject to post-clearance STA and customs audit. Failure to substantiate physical export, falsification of customs declarations, mismatched logistics data, lack of genuine economic substance in the exporter, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable deadline will result in reversal of the 0% VAT rate, repayment of all underpaid output VAT, disallowance of any refunded input VAT, and assessment of late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection can range from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; false foreign-exchange declarations or unlawful non-repatriation can also trigger State Administration of Foreign Exchange penalties; serious fraudulent export-tax-refund schemes may constitute criminal tax fraud under the Criminal Law, with potential imprisonment and fines.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account items such as trade in goods and services, but every payment must pass a genuineness and consistency review by the designated settlement bank, using the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System and the entity special identification codes. Capital account items, including FDI equity contributions, shareholder loans, external debt, and dividend repatriation, are not freely convertible and require prior or concurrent registration with SAFE. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must complete an FDI Foreign Exchange Registration, open a Foreign Currency Capital Account and a RMB Capital Settlement Account, and obtain a Capital Verification Report from a Chinese CPA before the injected funds can be used for operating purposes. Dividend remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder are permitted only after annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax filing and payment, a board-approved profit distribution resolution, and a tax clearance verification are provided. While Hong Kong imposes no surrender or convertibility restrictions on the Hong Kong treasury layer, the mainland leg of the JV still faces strict SAFE supervision. Export proceeds must be repatriated onshore; however, mainland enterprises are no longer subject to a forced conversion quota and may retain foreign currency in a settlement account or convert to RMB on a voluntary basis at PBOC market rates. Profit remittance from mainland to Hong Kong is therefore a controlled current account plus capital account process rather than a purely free floating currency regime, and professional treasury management must preserve audit trails from source invoices to final FX settlement to satisfy PBOC, SAFE and HKMA expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The JV export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated into Mainland China within 90 days after the export customs declaration under ordinary open account terms; if extended trade credit terms apply, the extension must be disclosed to the settlement bank and reflected in the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System. No statutory percentage of export proceeds must be converted into RMB; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in an eligible current account settlement account or voluntarily convert it at market rates. Intra-group service receipts and royalties are treated as current account receipts and are subject to invoice, contract, and tax documentation requirements.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong jurisdictional layer

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the PRC capital account layer. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from opening SAFE-monitored FDI capital accounts, performing statutory capital verification, issuing import Letters of Credit, or conducting regular foreign exchange settlement and sale in Mainland China. The Hong Kong layer may engage an HKMA-licensed Stored Value Facility or EMI licensee for small-value treasury operations and vendor payments, but the primary clearing rails must remain Tier-1 commercial banks in Mainland China and licensed banks in Hong Kong.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard PRC Corporate Income Tax (CIT) under Article 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law; a 10% gross-basis withholding levy applies to non-resident enterprises deriving Chinese-source passive income without a permanent establishment

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by the Chinese JV to a non-resident Hong Kong shareholder under EIT Law Articles 3 and 27; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement only if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the dividend-paying JV and satisfies the substantive beneficial ownership requirements

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Bulletin No. 42 of 2016, the JV must prepare and maintain a Chinese Local File if annual related-party transactions exceed the relevant CNY thresholds: CNY 200 million for tangible property transfers such as dialyzer sales to the Hong Kong affiliate; CNY 100 million for intangible property transfers such as royalties for dialysis filter technology; CNY 100 million for financial asset transfers; and CNY 40 million for other related-party service transactions such as management or quality-control fees; a Master File is required where annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed CNY 1 billion or where statutory CbC reporting applies; Country-by-Country reporting is required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds CNY 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese JV must demonstrate economic substance through its own core income-generating activities, which for a disposable dialysis hemofilter manufacturer include polyethersulfone extrusion, hollow fiber potting, sterilization, quality control, final packaging, and distribution-related functions; the Hong Kong shareholder claiming treaty benefits must likewise demonstrate real Hong Kong premises, employees, business records, board decision-making, and substantive economic activities under the tax residence and beneficial ownership doctrines; China applies the substance-over-form and general anti-avoidance rule under EIT Law Article 47, and Hong Kong economic substance regulations under Cap. 613 apply separately to the Hong Kong resident if it is in the relevant jurisdictional activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: The SAT will recharacterize and adjust income under EIT Law Article 41; disallow the excessive interest and royalty deductions; impose a statutory penalty of 50% up to 5 times the under-paid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, plus a 0.05% per-day late payment surcharge; egregious cases constitute tax evasion under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approved or signed false transfer pricing schedules are jointly and severally liable for civil asset restitution under PRC Company Law abuse of legal personality principles; they are exposed to tax authority blacklisting with credit downgrading and exit visa restrictions under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and imprisonment of up to 7 years plus fines where the evaded amount is extraordinarily large.
Failure to Withhold Tax on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will order payment of the uncollected withholding tax and late surcharge, impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the unpaid tax against the withholding agent, and deny deductibility of the underlying expense; serious failures are criminalized as tax evasion, with additional administrative sanctions for obstructing tax collection.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly caused the distribution without withholding bear civil and administrative liability for the unpaid tax and can be personally ordered to compensate the JV; they face travel bans until the tax is settled, and criminal liability under the Tax Collection Administration Law and Criminal Law where fraudulent or reckless conduct causes a major revenue loss.
Abusive Treaty Claim through Hong Kong Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership doctrine, the Principal Purpose Test as implemented through Chinese anti-abuse interpretation, and the general anti-avoidance rule in EIT Law Article 47; it will reassess the dividend at the 10% statutory rate, add late payment interest and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax difference, and may impose administrative sanctions on the withholding agent for failure to satisfy due diligence obligations.

Director personal liability: Directors and the Hong Kong nominee directors who signed false Certificate of Resident Status declarations, Beneficial Ownership Statements, or board minutes are personally liable for restitution of tax losses; criminal penalties apply for fraudulent tax evasion and forgery of official tax documents, with imprisonment terms up to 7 years, asset seizure, blacklisting, and exit restriction in both mainland China and Hong Kong.
