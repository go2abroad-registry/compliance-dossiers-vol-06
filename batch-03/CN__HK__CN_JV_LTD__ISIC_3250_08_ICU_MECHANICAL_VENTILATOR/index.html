Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China - national electronic tax bureau at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. For Hong Kong SAR activities, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through eTAX at https://www.etax.gov.hk is the competent territorial tax authority.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture: (1) Register the JV with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) via the one-window business registration system; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued on the business license is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). (2) Tax registration is automatically completed through the SAMR-STA data exchange; complete the supplementary tax registration at the competent STA office within 30 days of business license issuance, covering the legal representative, finance officer, bank accounts, fiscal year, and accounting policies. (3) Complete VAT tax-type identification and, if annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, apply for VAT general taxpayer status; if turnover is below threshold, you may elect general taxpayer status only if accounting systems are sound. (4) Register with the General Administration of Customs as an exporter to obtain the customs registration code and register in the STA export tax rebate module for future zero-rated export ventilators. (5) For non-resident shareholders or foreign non-resident suppliers with no Chinese establishment, the Chinese payer or withholding agent must perform withholding registration to obtain or assign a Chinese TIN for that non-resident, and the non-resident may apply for a Chinese tax resident certificate only if it qualifies as a resident under the Enterprise Income Tax Law or under an applicable tax treaty.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no pass-through treatment. Hong Kong SAR profits tax applies on a territorial basis, but this does not alter the entity-level classification of the mainland JV.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election, partnership pass-through election for foreign investors, or hybrid mismatch tax elections; the JV is mandatorily an enterprise income taxpayer. The only relevant status election is VAT registration between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer under the VAT implementing rules, and the administrative election for preferential tax rates such as the High and New Technology Enterprise rate must be filed and approved by the STA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of taxable annual turnover in any consecutive 12 months for goods manufacturing or sales, above which VAT general taxpayer registration is mandatory; below this threshold, the taxpayer is a small-scale taxpayer under preferential levy rates and may voluntarily register as a general taxpayer if qualifying conditions are met. Hong Kong SAR: not applicable, as Hong Kong imposes no VAT or GST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC VAT general taxpayer exporting goods from the PRC customs territory to a non-mainland buyer, including Hong Kong SAR, may claim zero-rated VAT with refund for the export of ICU mechanical ventilators, provided the goods are physically exported under a Customs export declaration, the exporter has title to the export transaction and receives foreign exchange or approved cross-border RMB settlement from the overseas buyer through banking channels in the statutory period, and the export tax rebate filing is made in the STA electronic system. Qualifying cross-border services are separately zero-rated or exempt only when the service is supplied to an overseas party and consumed outside the PRC; the ventilator manufacturing scope is goods export, not offshore services.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (electronic release record) issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC.
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic sea waybill), air waybill, or cross-border land or rail customs cargo receipt showing physical export from the PRC.
  • Commercial export invoice, packing list, and sales contract or purchase order matching the Customs declared HS classification.
  • Bank remittance receipt or SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System record evidencing receipt of foreign exchange or approved cross-border RMB settlement from the overseas buyer.
  • Export tax rebate application data submitted through the STA e-tax export rebate module, cross-matching the VAT invoice, Customs declaration, and logistics evidence.
  • Medical device export clearance certificate or NMPA export marketing authorization where the destination regulator requires it for ICU ventilators.

The PRC operates the Golden Tax-IV system with fully digital electronic invoices through the STA national electronic invoice platform; VAT invoices carry tax-control encryption, anti-counterfeit QR codes, and are transmitted in near real time to the national VAT invoice database. No physical fiscal stamping is required; the STA invoice data engine matches invoice, customs declaration, logistics, and payment records. For export zero-rating, the digital export invoice and Customs export declaration must be matched in the STA export tax rebate system before refunds are released. Hong Kong SAR has no electronic fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; eTAX is used for tax filing only.

The STA and Customs jointly match export declarations, VAT invoice issuance, transport documents, and foreign exchange collection data under Golden Tax-IV. If export zero-rating is approved and later found to be unsubstantiated or fraudulent, the tax authority will claw back all VAT refunded plus interest, impose late-payment surcharges and penalties under the Law on Tax Administration and Collection and the VAT implementing rules, suspend or revoke export tax refund qualifications and customs registration, and refer criminal liability for export duty fraud if the act constitutes fraud in obtaining export tax rebates. Incorrect Hong Kong territorial profit tax treatment can also trigger IRD audit reopening, additional profits tax assessments, and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which current-account trade transactions are generally convertible after bank verification, while capital-account movements remain controlled. For this China-Hong Kong JV, RMB convertibility is achieved only with SAFE registration, supporting underlying documentation, and a licensed PRC settlement bank. Inward paid-in equity capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be registered under the FDI regime, remitted into a foreign-exchange capital account, and settled into RMB with purpose restrictions prohibiting use for securities, real estate outside permitted scope, or unauthorized on-lending. Outbound dividends are treated as capital-account outflows and require audited financial statements, tax compliance certificates, board resolutions, and a bank authenticity review; there is no open-ended conversion right. Hong Kong, by contrast, operates an open convertibility regime with no surrender rule, which permits the treasury to concentrate liquidity, execute market-rate hedging, and route compliant remittances. Profit remittance is not prohibited but is heavily evidence-gated; any failure in documentation can trigger SAFE penalty, freezing, or clawback. Therefore the treasury must treat convertibility as a documentary privilege rather than a free-market right.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China export receipts, the statutory obligation is repatriation into a PRC domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or through a licensed direct bank. Common bank practice and SAFE monitoring require export collection to be received and verified within 90 days of the export customs declaration; delayed collection beyond 360 days requires SAFE registration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the post-2012 liberalization: 100% of export proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or sold to the bank at market rate. The phrase surrender in the old central-plan sense is abolished; the binding requirement is trade-goods foreign-exchange receipt verification via the SAFE monitoring system, supported by customs declaration data, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and the customs export proceeds receipt log.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong clearing and currency-board leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions are not viable as the primary banking rail for this JV. Mainland China has not authorized digital EMIs for corporate FDI capital accounts, SAFE regulatory filing, RMB settlement, or export proceeds collection. The legal framework requires a licensed commercial bank to perform authenticity review and route funds through CIPS or the SWIFT-based agent bank system. Offshore EMIs may support minor Hong Kong treasury disbursements, but they cannot issue documentary credits, accept export surrender compliance, or verify UBOs to PRC banking standard. Recommended rating: Not Viable for capital-account and core trade flows; Ancillary Use Only for Hong Kong operating expenses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Route the HK parent remittance through SWIFT MT103 into the JV FDI capital account at a Tier-1 PRC bank. The wire must quote the SAFE purpose code for inward direct investment paid-in capital (equity), the registered FDI file number, and the JV legal name. If the remittance is in HKD or USD, the receiving bank will execute capital-account foreign-exchange settlement into RMB after verifying the board resolution, business license, and SAFE registration; if funds arrive through CIPS in RMB, the capital-account RMB settlement rules still apply. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (Hong Kong Profits Tax, applicable to corporations)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by a Hong Kong corporation to its shareholders, whether resident or non-resident. Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on dividends. China-source dividends received by the HK JV from its China subsidiary are subject to various China dividend withholding tax rates (10% standard, potentially 5% under the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA) and the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement if the HK company holds at least 25% capital in the China subsidiary and meets beneficial ownership requirements). The HK JV does not withhold when paying dividends to its China parent.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Generally one month from the issue date of the Profits Tax Return by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD). For corporations with financial year-end between 1 April and 30 November, extended deadlines often apply under the Block Extension Scheme (e.g. payment date of 15 August of the following year for FYE 31 December). The deadline is always stated on the return; failure to file by the extended deadline triggers penalties.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong statutory transfer pricing codification (as amended by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018) does not set a monetary threshold for mandatory Local File/Master File preparation; however. Under the Transfer Pricing (Documentation) Rules (Cap. 112I), a Hong Kong resident person with a covered cross-border transaction must prepare a Master File and Local File if the aggregate amount of all covered transactions exceeds HKD 50 million (for advanced pricing requests, a lower threshold of HKD 10 million applies), and must prepare a 'CbC Report' if the group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 680 million (which mirrors the OECD action 13, with a de minimis threshold of HKD 50 million for covered-type transactions). Additionally, the Hong Kong IRD expects contemporaneous documentation for material cross-border intercompany transactions even if no statutory filing is automatically required, and imposes penalties for non-compliance with documentation requests.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies Economic Substance Requirements through the 'economic substance cornerstone' of the substantial shareholder requirements under the Inland Revenue Ordinance; however, unlike BVI/Cayman, Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations. The China-HK Double Tax Arrangement contains a principal purpose test (PPT) and 'beneficial ownership' provisions. For a manufacturing JV, the HK entity must demonstrate that it performs real economic functions — strategic procurement, inventory management, invoicing, credit and risk control, and decision-making — rather than acting as a passive shell. The concept of 'core income-generating activities' (CIGA) is applied under the OECD guidelines and in China's TP audit practice, especially qualifying for the dividends reduction. The HK company must show that it takes key business decisions, has appropriately qualified employees, and incurs operational expenses proportional to the income generated. The IRD may assess profits under section 61(1), 61A, and 20 where artificial transactions are found, and the China tax authority may adjust transfer prices if the HK thin capitalization or profit split reflects no risk-bearing capability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Artificial Arrangement with China Affiliated Manufacturer

Statutory sanction: Under Section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Transaction Not at Arm's Length), the IRD may adjust the profits to reflect the arm's length price and levy additional tax equal to the difference. Under the Transfer Pricing (Documentation) Rules, failure to prepare and keep contemporaneous documentation triggers a penalty of up to HKD 100,000 (for a first non-evasive) plus a potential additional penalty of up to 10% of the transaction amount. For false documentation, the legislative penalty is treble tax and imprisonment for 3 years under Section 82. The China tax authority may also make a downward adjustment to the JV's import costs, causing double taxation subject to MAP protocols.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly initiate or authorize the artificial pricing arrangement may be liable for the deficiency in tax as responsible officers under Section 82A (removal of limitation of liability for officers of corporations). In civil proceedings, the court may order the director to return misappropriated assets from the HK JV trust if the JV has been stripped of profits (undercapitalization). Under Section 82A, the Court of First Instance may impose an order requiring the director to pay the outstanding tax. A travel blacklisting under Section 77A (departure prevention) can be imposed. If the scheme constitutes common law conspiracy to defraud the revenue, a director faces imprisonment for up to 14 years under the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) if money transfers are misrepresented.
Shell Structure for Treaty Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) embedded in the China-HK Tax Arrangement (Article 24), the Chinese tax authority may deny treaty relief, making the 10% standard withholding apply on all China dividends paid to the HK JV as a non-resident. Under Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Ordinance, section 61A and the GAAR can re-characterize the structure to be the China parent as the proper taxpayer, resulting in an assessment of tax on the HK JV as if it had no domicile in HK (or no tax treaty residence) and a penalty of 100%-200% of additional tax. If it is found that the HK JV is a bare trustee or nominee, the entire profits are attributed to the China resident entity.

Director personal liability: Directors of the HK JV who sign declarations that the entity is the beneficial owner of the dividends while knowing that all economic substance is located in China may be prosecuted for false representation to obtain governmental benefits (tax treaty relief) under Section 82 of the IRD and under the Theft Ordinance, punishable by up to 14 years imprisonment. The directors may also be jointly and severally liable for the additional 5% withholding tax and penalties under Section 24A of the Companies Ordinance if the company is used as an instrument of fraudulent trading. A court may issue a blacklisting order for international travel if tax liabilities exceed HKD 100,000, and directors may face restriction from holding directorships for up to 15 years under Section 168 of the Companies Ordinance.
