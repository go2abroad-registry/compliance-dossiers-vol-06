Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is domiciled in mainland China and therefore sits under the PBOC/SAFE split convertibility framework. Current-account items, including medical glove export proceeds, raw-material imports, trade-related service fees and routine operating disbursements, are fully convertible at the bank-offered spot rate, subject to genuineness checks and documentary confirmation of the transaction. Capital-account items are more tightly controlled. An equity injection from the Hong Kong partner requires a valid Foreign Investment Registration Certificate issued through SAFE, a dedicated FDI capital account with a licensed domestic bank, and compliance with the actual-use principle. Capital funds cannot be diverted to securities investment, entrusted loans or non-core real-estate purchases. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permissible after the local statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, and board approval; the bank verifies the profit-distribution resolution and prior-year audited financial statements before allowing the outward remittance. In practice, conversion occurs freely for trade-related FX payments, but portfolio capital flows, cross-border guarantees and cash-pooling structures remain restricted. The role of the Hong Kong SAR is limited to an offshore financing and settlement point, not an escape from PRC exchange-control supervision.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated and received in China through a licensed FX bank within 180 days of the export customs declaration date; extension may be approved for longer open-account terms after documentary justification. There is no statutory fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE trade-in-goods supervision model; exporters may retain 100% of eligible foreign-currency receipts in an export foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntarily sell them to a designated bank for RMB. The bank must match every receipt to the China Customs declaration in the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system and verify the invoice and price reasonableness.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) oversees the offshore CNH clearing market but does not exercise exchange controls over this mainland domiciled joint venture.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must remit via SWIFT MT103 to the joint venture's dedicated FDI capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank, naming the full legal entity and FDI registration number as beneficiary and using SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code 621011 for foreign direct investment paid-in capital. The receiving bank verifies the Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, converts the receipt into RMB or holds foreign currency, and files the transaction with SAFE. Within 15 business days after receipt, a PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report. The funds may then fund operating expenditure, working capital, equipment purchases and other permitted business use, but cannot be used for securities investment, entrusted loans, non-business real estate, or round-trip reinvestment without SAFE approval.
