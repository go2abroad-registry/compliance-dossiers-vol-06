Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing is conducted through the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau portal for mainland China. The Hong Kong SAR element of the structure is separately within the purview of the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department, but the JV's national tax identity, enterprise income tax, and VAT are administered by the STA.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-foreign equity joint venture first registers with the State Administration for Market Regulation, which issues a business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code; that code is used by the STA as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for enterprise income tax, VAT, and all other PRC taxes. Tax registration is then completed at the competent local STA office within thirty days of business license issuance. If the foreign non-resident Hong Kong partner derives China-sourced income without a permanent establishment, it must complete a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the STA office in charge of the withholding agent and obtain a separate non-resident enterprise tax identification number. The JV must also register as a VAT general taxpayer when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 or when it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status to access input VAT credits and export zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The PRC-resident Sino-foreign equity joint venture is a separate taxable person subject to enterprise income tax at the statutory 25% rate on its worldwide income; it is not pass-through, and the Hong Kong SAR territorial profits tax framework does not alter this PRC resident classification.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity classification election. Available relief mechanisms are limited to treaty benefits, foreign tax credit elections, and the voluntary VAT general taxpayer election; none change the entity-level taxation classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

A VAT general taxpayer registration is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below that threshold the small-scale taxpayer regime applies, with VAT collection rates of 1% or 3% depending on prevailing transitional policy. Non-resident suppliers without an establishment in mainland China are not required to register for VAT; the Chinese recipient may be obliged to apply reverse-charge withholding when importing cross-border services or intangibles, while physical exported goods are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The intraocular acrylic lenses qualify for the 0% VAT export rate under the PRC VAT export exemption and refund regime only when the goods physically leave mainland China customs territory, the JV is the exporter of record, the export is covered by a lawful customs export declaration, and the transaction is a genuine sale to an overseas or Hong Kong SAR buyer. The JV must be a VAT general taxpayer, the export must be supported by proper freight and insurance terms, and the export proceeds must be repatriated and verified. Regulatory export licensing by the National Medical Products Administration (NMPA) for the medical device, together with the destination country's registration requirements, is also required before zero-rating is sustained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) bearing the customs release seal and export date
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document (air waybill, seaway bill, or multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-generated foreign exchange receipt with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) export proceeds verification record
  • Commercial invoice, export packing list, and the VAT special invoice or fully digitalized e-invoice issued for the export sale
  • Export sales contract and purchase order with Incoterms, payment terms, SWIFT wire evidence, and documentary letter of credit confirmation

General VAT taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices through the national VAT invoice management system using tax-control equipment; invoices carry a tax-control cipher and invoice data are uploaded to the STA. Fully digitalized e-invoices are issued through the STA's national electronic invoice platform and contain a unique invoice number and anti-fraud digital stamp. API-based real-time clearance is available for system-to-system issuance of fully digitalized e-invoices, and every VAT invoice must be reconciled to the VAT return before input-credit or export-refund treatment is accepted.

The STA and customs authorities conduct post-export document checks, invoice cross-validation, and on-site audits. A zero-rated export refund or input tax credit is clawed back if the customs declaration, transport documents, export invoice, or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is missing, false, or inconsistent; the exporter must repay the tax plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Fraudulent export refund claims trigger penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax amount under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, suspension from export rebate processing, tax blacklisting, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebates under PRC criminal law, including imprisonment and confiscation of unlawfully obtained refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Despite Hong Kong's freely convertible HKD and the absence of exchange controls on the Hong Kong side, the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operating onshore in mainland China remains subject to the PRC's bifurcated convertibility framework. Current account items, including trade receipts, import payments, freight and service charges, are generally permitted once the bank has verified genuine underlying documents; settlement can occur at market-based rates through a designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account items, such as foreign equity injection, shareholder loans, dividends and liquidation proceeds, are not freely convertible and require SAFE registration, approval or participation in a specific pilot scheme. Export proceeds are subject to mandatory repatriation to a SAFE-monitored account, but after repatriation there is no fixed statutory surrender ratio; the JV may retain foreign currency or convert to RMB. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder faces practical hurdles: audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificates, retained-earnings calculation, SAFE dividend payment registration and bank-level anti-money-laundering review must all align with the exact corporate ownership chain. Mis-declared purpose codes, offshore freight payments outside licensed banks, or failure to update the FDI registration create bank refusals, penalty exposure and eventual prohibition from the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Within 90 calendar days from the customs export declaration date, the JV must repatriate all export foreign currency receipts to an onshore SAFE-designated foreign exchange account. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; no export proceeds need to be forcibly converted into RMB. The JV may hold the proceeds in a foreign currency account, but each inflow must be matched to a customs declaration in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. If the proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory window, the JV must provide a written explanation, underlying export contract, bill of lading and customs declaration to the bank and SAFE; overseas retention requires prior SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore sovereign FX regime; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR clearing and banking license structure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMIs cannot offer the statutory account types needed by an onshore Sino-Foreign JV. The PRC Foreign Exchange Control Regulations require all cross-border receipts and payments, export proceeds repatriation, and trade settlements to be handled by banks licensed by PBOC/SAFE. EMIs based in Singapore, the UK or the EU may be used only as supplementary offshore collection wallets for a separate Hong Kong treasury vehicle, provided they are not routed through the onshore entity. They cannot open RMB accounts, perform SAFE FDI registration, issue capital verification evidence, or serve as the issuing bank for commercial L/Cs and documentary collections. Legal and operational constraints therefore require primary banking relationships with Tier-1 licensed commercial banks; an EMI solution is viable only in non-China trade corridors and never for statutory capital account flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (aggregate). Master File preparation is required when total related-party turnover exceeds RMB 300 million in a year. The JV must also prepare a Local File and a Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation file if its related-party transactions exceed these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and the judicial substance-over-form principle apply to disregard tax-motivated structures without commercial substance. The PRC tax authorities require that a resident enterprise actually perform its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Mainland China, such as manufacturing, R&D, and strategic management of intraocular lens production. A Hong Kong shareholder that is a mere letterbox company with no employees, office, or decision-taking functions will be denied treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) of Article 17 of the DTA. The JV's own economic substance is evidenced by its factory premises, employees, intellectual property registration, and the performance of R&D and manufacturing functions in China; if these activities are shifted to a low-tax jurisdiction without real operations, the arrangement is vulnerable to a CFC pierce or CIT re-characterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing (虚开发票)

Statutory sanction: Under Article 205 of the PRC Criminal Law, issuing false special VAT invoices is punishable by a fine and up to life imprisonment for the most serious cases; the tax authority administratively imposes penalties ranging from 50% to 5 times the evaded tax under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or participate in the fraudulent invoicing are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest; the corporate veil is pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, allowing creditors and the tax authorities to attach the directors' personal assets; directors face a 'Limit Exit Order' (exit ban) and may be criminally sentenced for tax evasion (Article 201) to 3–7 years' imprisonment, with property confiscation in aggravated cases.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authorities may deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) of Article 17 of the Mainland-Hong Kong Arrangement and re-characterize the dividend at the 10% statutory rate, plus interest and a penalty of up to 3 times the under-withheld tax under Article 16 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. The JV as withholding agent may also be held liable for negligence penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create or maintain the Hong Kong shell can be held personally liable for the WHT shortfall under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil (Article 20 of the PRC Company Law); personal assets may be frozen or seized; travel bans are issued; criminal exposure for a tax fraud offense under Article 201 may result in imprisonment of up to 7 years, and the court may order personal restitution of the full tax and penalties.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Violation

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, the People's Bank of China may impose a fine of up to RMB 10 million on the institution and suspend or revoke its business license; criminal liability for money laundering under Article 191 of the Criminal Law includes imprisonment from 5 to 10 years, plus monetary penalties and forfeiture of the proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who knowingly permit such transactions or fail to implement required AML controls are personally liable for administrative fines of RMB 50,000 to RMB 500,000 and may be disqualified from serving as directors for up to 5 years; if criminal money laundering is proved, directors face imprisonment of 5–10 years and the confiscation of their personal assets.
