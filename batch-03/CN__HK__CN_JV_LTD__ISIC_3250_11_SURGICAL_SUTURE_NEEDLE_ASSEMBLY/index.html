Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Hong Kong law. The company must have at least one issued share, but the JV agreement may stipulate a higher capital contribution. No escrow or blocked account requirement exists; capital may be contributed in cash or in-kind as per the JV contract.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Fire Services Department (FSD) – the facility must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate (Form FS 251) after inspection. Structural load inspections are required from the Buildings Department to ensure the premises can support the manufacturing equipment. Occupational safety approval is required from the Labour Department under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509), including a risk assessment and safety training for workers handling sharp instruments and chemicals.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital under Hong Kong law. The company must have at least one issued share, but the JV agreement may stipulate a higher capital contribution. No escrow or blocked account requirement exists; capital may be contributed in cash or in-kind as per the JV contract.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Mainland China: State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, online portal being the STA Electronic Tax Bureau. Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, online portal being eTAX.

E-government filing portal

For the Mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the enterprise's statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number. Within 30 calendar days after business registration, the entity must complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau by linking the USCC and submitting the registered address, actual production and office premises, legal representative, board composition, bank account details, accounting system information, expected VAT taxpayer status, and the business scope covering surgical suture and needle assembly manufacturing. The competent STA tax service bureau then creates the taxpayer file and confirms the applicable tax types, including Enterprise Income Tax, Value-Added Tax, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp duty, and property-related local taxes. For foreign non-resident entities such as a Hong Kong parent or service provider without a permanent establishment in Mainland China, the China payer must complete a withholding registration by presenting the contract, the non-resident entity's Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and tax residence certificate, and the payer's taxpayer identification details; the STA then assigns non-resident tax records for Enterprise Income Tax and VAT withholding purposes. If a Hong Kong presence is formed, the Hong Kong entity must apply to the Companies Registry for incorporation and to the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance; the Business Registration Certificate number and separate Profits Tax file number constitute the Hong Kong tax registration infrastructure.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income; VAT treated as a general taxpayer for mainstream manufacturing activity; the Hong Kong SAR component follows Territorial_Scope for Profits Tax, taxing only Hong Kong-sourced profits, and no VAT/GST applies in Hong Kong). — For the Mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the enterprise's statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income; VAT treated as a general taxpayer for mainstream manufacturing activity; the Hong Kong SAR component follows Territorial_Scope for Profits Tax, taxing only Hong Kong-sourced profits, and no VAT/GST applies in Hong Kong).

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box election for Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Ventures, and no hybrid mismatch election is available in Mainland China. The entity is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a corporation. In Hong Kong, no elective check-the-box or hybrid mismatch regime is available either.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In Mainland China, annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 mandate VAT general taxpayer registration; a taxpayer below this threshold may elect small-scale taxpayer status unless otherwise required by the STA. In Hong Kong SAR, there is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax is imposed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Mainland China qualify for the 0% VAT export rate only when the exporter is an STA-registered VAT general taxpayer; the surgical suture needles and assemblies are physically exported from the PRC customs territory to a destination outside Mainland China, including Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory; the goods are declared to the General Administration of Customs under an official export customs declaration; the sale is a genuine commercial transaction with an overseas buyer; and the corresponding foreign currency export proceeds are received and repatriated through the banking system. The goods must not be prohibited or restricted from export, and the entity must hold the applicable medical device production and export eligibility approvals. Qualifying offshore digital services are not within this manufacturing scope; Hong Kong does not impose VAT and therefore provides no separate Hong Kong zero-rating refund.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (export bill of entry / export declaration number issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, with HS classification and exported quantity and value)
  • Bill of Lading issued by the shipping line, or air waybill for air cargo, evidencing the goods physically departed from Mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the customs declaration data
  • Foreign sales contract or purchase order between the joint venture and the overseas buyer
  • Bank export proceeds receipt voucher, including bank credit advice, export collection verification record, or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by the receiving bank
  • VAT special export invoice or fully digitalized digital VAT invoice recorded in the STA e-invoice system
  • Customs release or export inspection/gate-out records for the exported batch
  • Medical device export eligibility documentation, including the applicable NMPA manufacturing and export license for surgical suture products
  • Cargo insurance certificate, certificate of origin, or other transport evidence required by customs or the tax authority

Mainland China mandates VAT invoice issuance through the STA-approved electronic invoice system. All fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices are uploaded to the STA Electronic Tax Bureau in real time, carry unique nationwide invoice numbers, and are subject to anti-fraud matching against VAT returns, customs export declarations, and bank foreign exchange receipt data. The special VAT invoice system is the statutory fiscal clearance and anti-counterfeiting control; no private fiscal stamp or manual clearance is recognized. Hong Kong has no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate, though records must satisfy the Inland Revenue Ordinance bookkeeping requirements.

The STA cross-checks export VAT refund claims against customs export data, bank foreign exchange receipt data, transport and insurance documents, invoice data, and the general ledger. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine export or full foreign exchange repatriation, the tax authority will deny or claw back the VAT refund, require repayment of output VAT, impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and levy an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refunded. False export declarations, forged bills of lading, circular trade transactions, or counterfeit purchase invoices may lead to blacklisting as an abnormal tax credit entity, suspension or revocation of export VAT refund qualification, customs penalties, confiscation of goods or proceeds, and criminal prosecution for export tax fraud or smuggling under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. In cases involving concealment or fraud, the tax authority may apply an unlimited retrospective recovery period.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under PBOC/SAFE. Current-account items, including cross-border trade in surgical sutures, needle-assembly machinery, medical-grade raw materials and logistics services, are generally convertible subject to customs, tax and documentation verification. Export receipts may be settled into onshore RMB or retained in foreign currency, while import payments require genuine trade invoices, contracts and customs declarations. Capital-account transactions, however, remain highly controlled. For the sino-foreign equity JV, every foreign capital injection must be separately registered with SAFE under the foreign-direct-investment (FDI) framework; the funds are credited to a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account and may be converted into RMB only after an authenticity and purpose review. Dividend remittances are permitted only after the annual audit, corporate income-tax settlement, withholding-tax clearance and SAFE remittance filing. The Hong Kong SAR has no foreign-exchange controls: the HKD is fully convertible under the linked exchange-rate system and the offshore CNH market is free, but the mainland JV cannot use offshore CNH to settle onshore obligations or inject capital without a duly registered onshore transaction. Therefore, treasury planning must strictly separate the mainland onshore RMB/FX pool from the Hong Kong CNH/HKD pool, with all cross-border flows routed via licensed banks and SAFE reporting lines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from surgical-suture and needle exports must be repatriated to the PRC through a designated domestic bank within the SAFE-prescribed export-collection period, generally 180 days from customs declaration or the contractual payment due date as monitored in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitor System. There is no mandatory conversion or surrender ratio: the JV may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert any portion into RMB at the onshore rate. Every inflow must be matched against customs declarations, invoices, logistics documents and the bank's cross-border receipt/payment filing obligations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the supreme monetary authority of the PRC, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the statutory foreign-exchange regulator for all onshore FX transactions; for the Hong Kong SAR treasury lane, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acts as the currency board authority and regulator of the clearing and settlement system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the mainland sino-foreign equity JV. PBOC and SAFE regulations require all onshore cross-border receipts and payments for a Chinese foreign-invested enterprise to be routed through a licensed PRC bank with direct participation in the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Offshore fintech EMIs and neobanks cannot maintain onshore RMB clearing accounts, cannot perform SAFE registration reporting, and do not hold PBOC deposit-taking licences. For the Hong Kong treasury vehicle, licensed virtual banks may support basic CNH/HKD current-account activity, but they are not recommended for Stage 4 because the required trade clearing, LC issuance, FX hedging and capital-account settlement need the balance-sheet depth and correspondent routing of Tier-1 banks. Overall feasibility rating: Void for the mainland JV; Supplementary-Low for the HK treasury vehicle only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity funds from the Hong Kong shareholder must travel via SWIFT MT103 (single customer credit transfer) or MT202 (bank-to-bank cover) into the JV's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 PRC bank. Before transfer, the JV must complete SAFE FDI basic registration through the bank and obtain the Foreign-Invested Enterprise code. The remittance must carry the applicable SAFE purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise equity injection, typically the capital-account transaction code for investment in a non-listed enterprise, and the receiving bank must identify the funds as paid-in capital. The local bank branch verifies the contribution against the joint venture contract, and a PRC CPA issues a formal capital-verification report after the funds are credited. If the contribution is made in RMB converted in Hong Kong using offshore CNH, it must be received in a special RMB settlement account for foreign direct investment and be separately annotated in the SAFE registration. No portion of the injected capital may be used for equity investment, entrusted loans, off-licence real estate or repayment of non-approved financing without separate SAFE approval.For raw-material imports and equipment purchases, the JV should use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers against a validated commercial invoice, packing list, customs declaration and purchase contract. For high-value or contract-heavy purchases, a documentary letter of credit issued by a PRC bank under UCP600 via SWIFT MT700/MT707 is the preferred clearing rail. For low-risk repeat suppliers and consignment shipments, documentary collections (DP/DA) may be used to reduce cost. All outbound payments must be mirrored in SAFE's cross-border receipt/payment reporting system: the bank requires the trade document code, customs record and import-tax/VAT reconciliation before debiting the FX account. Where the counterparty is the Hong Kong trading vehicle, invoices must evidence arm's-length pricing and physical-goods movement to avoid circular-payment, transfer-pricing and AML review friction. The Hong Kong treasury account of the holding vehicle can serve as a settlement node, but the final underlying trade flow must be traceable from the mainland JV to the end buyer or supplier.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: annual related-party tangible-asset transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; related-party financial-asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; related-party intangible-asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File threshold: annual aggregate related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion. The contemporaneous documentation set must be prepared by 31 May of the following year and submitted to the tax authority within 15 days of request.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies substance-over-form against shell structures under the General Anti-Abuse Rule (EIT Law Art. 47) and the Beneficial Ownership requirement under SAT Ann. 35/2015. For the Hong Kong parent, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and the Economic Substance Regulations require real establishment, staff, and investment decision-making; a pure letter-box holding company with no core income-generating activities in Hong Kong will be denied treaty benefits and its profit shall be re-attributed to the Chinese manufacturing functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Artificial Transfer Pricing on Royalty and Management Fees

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Art. 41 authorises a transfer-pricing adjustment; Art. 47 applies GAAR recharacterisation. Under Tax Administration and Collection Law Art. 63, the underpaid CIT is penalised at 50% to 5 times the unpaid tax, plus daily surcharge; under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201, tax evasion exceeding the criminal threshold may result in imprisonment up to 7 years and an additional fine for the entity's direct responsible personnel.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false TP agreements are jointly and severally liable for the repatriated tax, interest, and penalties; they are placed on the tax trust-breaker blacklist and subject to an exit/visa ban preventing departure from mainland China; their personal bank accounts may be frozen; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Art. 201 can lead to a 6-month to 7-year prison sentence.
Dividend Repatriation WHT Evasion - Mischaracterisation of Profit Distributions

Statutory sanction: Under TAL Art. 69, failure to withhold is penalised with a fine of 50% to 100% of the unwithheld tax. Fraudulent concealment triggers TAL Art. 63 (0.5x-5x penalty), and the China-HK DTA competent authorities may deny treaty benefits and reinstate the 10% statutory rate with annual interest. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 applies to the responsible executives.

Director personal liability: Directors who instructed treasury staff to mislabel dividend flows bear personal civil liability for the unpaid WHT and penalties; they are subject to exit bans, asset freezes, travel restrictions, and director-disqualification orders under PRC Company Law; if the misconduct is proven as intentional tax evasion, they face imprisonment up to 7 years and a separate fine.
Shell Conduit and AML Non-Compliance - Abuse of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: PRC GAAR (EIT Law Art. 47) and the Beneficial Ownership test deny all treaty benefits; the JV must withhold at the statutory rate plus interest. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance s.61A and s.61B can void the tax-avoidance arrangement; under the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations, failure to demonstrate economic substance attracts fines up to HK$50,000 for an initial offence, HK$100,000 for a repeat offence, and daily penalties for continuing non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The Chinese ultimate controller and the Hong Kong nominee directors are jointly and severally liable for the uncollected Chinese tax; they face a travel ban from mainland China, social-credit blacklisting, revocation of PRC residency and work permits, and personal asset restitution orders. In a cross-border criminal investigation, the directors may be indicted under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax fraud) or Art. 175-1 for reckless operational dishonesty, resulting in imprisonment; Hong Kong courts may impose disqualification and compensation orders under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).
