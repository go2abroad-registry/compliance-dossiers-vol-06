Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The People's Republic of China imposes no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the current Company Law. The shareholders must subscribe the registered capital in full in the Articles of Association and contribute within the statutory five-year period from establishment or within the shorter period stated in the JV Contract. No blocked escrow account is mandatory; capital injections are to be made into the basic deposit account opened after registration, subject to bank due diligence.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of taxable sales in any 12-month rolling period for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below that threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer but must still register for tax and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code/TIN. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer that manufactures and exports tangible goods, including dental clear-aligner sheets, from the PRC customs territory may apply the 0% export VAT rate and obtain refund of input VAT if: (i) the goods are sold to an overseas buyer and actually leave the PRC customs territory under a customs export declaration; (ii) the enterprise is a VAT general taxpayer; (iii) the export sale is supported by a contract, commercial invoice, transport document and customs release; and (iv) the export proceeds in foreign exchange are repatriated to the PRC bank account within 180 days of the export declaration date. Qualifying offshore services may also be zero-rated only if separately listed in the export VAT zero-rating measures; physical goods are subject to customs-based substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration of the People's Republic of China generated through the China International Trade Single Window, with customs release and outward voyage confirmation
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or international express/courier waybill proving physical departure of the aligner sheets from the PRC customs territory to the overseas destination
  • Bank-issued export proceeds collection record and inward remittance reconciliation report showing receipt of the foreign-currency export proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, export sales contract and packing list consistent with the customs declared value and invoiced sales amount
  • Original input VAT special e-invoice or import VAT payment certificate supporting the refundable input VAT amount

All VAT invoices must be issued as fully digital e-invoices on the STA national all-electronic invoice platform, with real-time encrypted transmission and tax-control seal clearance to the State Taxation Administration. High-volume issuers are required to use the official API connection to the e-Taxation/invoice platform. The Golden Tax Phase IV data-control system automatically reconciles digital invoice data with VAT returns, customs export declarations, inventory flows, payment rails and bank/FX settlement data. No paper-only or locally issued invoice can support input VAT credits or export refund.

The STA export-tax-review system cross-checks each zero-rated export claim against the customs export declaration, the transport/departure track, the input VAT e-invoice data, the banking FX receipt records and the VAT refund return. If the zero-rating claim is not substantiated, the tax authority will claw back the refunded VAT, charge unpaid-tax surcharge at 0.05% per day, and impose a fine of one to five times the defrauded tax under Article 66 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export refunds can also trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, suspension or revocation of export-tax-refund qualifications, and demand for full restoration of prior refunds through retrospective reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR maintains no foreign-exchange control regime. The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible on both current and capital accounts, no surrender requirements apply, no central-bank approval is required for ordinary corporate dividends or loan repayments, and capital may be moved freely across HKD, USD, EUR, CNH and other major currency accounts. The HKMA relies on the Linked Exchange Rate System, not on quantitative FX controls, to preserve monetary stability. However, the linkage to Mainland China introduces real convertibility frictions: China still administers RMB convertibility under current-account rules while capital-account items such as outward direct investment, equity injections, and profit repatriation require SAFE registration, designated-bank settlement, and in many cases tax clearance. For a cross-border joint venture operating across both regimes, profit remittance from a Mainland operating subsidiary to its Hong Kong shareholder will generally require audited financial statements, a non-resident withholding-tax certification, a formal profit-distribution resolution, and a SAFE-validated remittance instruction. Hong Kong itself imposes no remittance restriction, so the treasury should treat Hong Kong as the free-flowing clearing hub while managing Mainland SAFE filings as the critical regulatory bottleneck.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio on export foreign-currency receipts; 0% of export proceeds must be surrendered, and retained proceeds may be converted at market rates without a minimum holding period. For any Mainland-related exporting entity within the corporate group, export receipts must be collected through designated banks in China and recorded in the SAFE current-account foreign-exchange monitoring system; there is no current mandatory conversion ratio, but the enterprise must observe the bank's export-collection status and settlement/retention monitoring requirements.

Central bank supervision: The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), operating as Hong Kong's de facto central bank under the Banking Ordinance and the Currency Ordinance; for all cross-border flows touching Mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China is the relevant exchange-control authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms have only a Low-to-Medium feasibility rating for this structure, and the rating falls to Low when used for capital-escrow, equity-injection or capital-verification functions. In Hong Kong, an EMI is not a distinct depository-taking licence; a fintech platform that offers accounts must be an authorised institution under the Banking Ordinance, licensed as an SVF for stored-value products, or registered as a Money Service Operator under the Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance if it conducts remittance. Unlicensed offshore EMIs are legally barred from providing regulated corporate settlement accounts and from clearing inbound shareholder equity flows on behalf of Hong Kong entities. Therefore, use digital financial platforms only for low-value operating payments after confirming their licence status; route cross-border capital, trade disbursements and FX hedging through licensed tier-1 banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For a Hong Kong-incorporated JV receiving an upward equity injection from a Mainland shareholder, the compliant route is a direct SWIFT MT103 remittance to the designated capital account in Hong Kong, using the exact beneficiary corporate name and account number and referencing in field 70 'Equity subscription under board resolution and share-subscription agreement'. The Mainland remitting bank must process the outbound remittance under SAFE's overseas direct investment recordal, applying the statutory ODI foreign-exchange purpose code, attaching the SAFE registration reference, and debiting the funds only after licensed-rate conversion. In Hong Kong, the receiving bank will credit the inward remittance and request the share-subscription agreement, certificate of incorporation, directors' resolution and, if required, an HK-certified accountant's capital-verification report. If the Hong Kong entity instead injects capital downward into a Mainland Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, routing must use a RMB or foreign-currency capital account opened with a designated PRC bank, file SAFE FDI registration, provide the joint-venture contract and capital-deposit notice, and obtain a CICPA capital-verification certificate before the funds are made available for factory expenditure or raw-material procurement.For trade disbursements from the Hong Kong entity to suppliers of dental-aligner sheets or thermoforming equipment, low-value payments should use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 instructions through a licensed bank, with commercial invoices and purchase orders preserved in the treasury file. For high-value or medium-tenor transactions, a Commercial Letter of Credit is recommended, with the beneficiary presenting clean bills of lading, commercial invoices, insurance certificates, packing lists and quality-inspection certificates against the L/C terms. Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable where the buyer-supplier relationship is well established; the exporter's bank forwards the documents through the Hong Kong bank to the importer's bank, and the importer must pay on sight or accept the draft before document release. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for annual related-party tangible property transfers requiring a Local File; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfer or financial asset transfer; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is mandatory where aggregate annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 2 billion. Transfer pricing contemporaneous documentation must be prepared before the CIT filing deadline and submitted to the tax authority upon audit request within 30 days.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply separately named Economic Substance Regulations, but the State Taxation Administration applies beneficial ownership and substance-over-form principles, including the six-factor economic substance test, when assessing treaty entitlement of a Hong Kong shareholder. The Core Income-Generating Activities of the JV must occur in mainland China; the Hong Kong shareholder must itself demonstrate real staff, office premises, decision-making and payroll records; a shell Hong Kong entity with no relevant business activity will be denied treaty benefits. For IP-centric dental aligner activities, the Hong Kong shareholder must document the development, enhancement, maintenance, protection and exploitation of the intangibles in a manner consistent with the actual allocation of risk and capital.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Under-Invoiced Export Sales

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and court-recoverable unpaid tax with daily late payment surcharges of 0.05%. Customs false export declaration penalties apply separately under the Customs Law. Criminal Law Article 201 authorises imprisonment of up to 7 years where the evaded amount is large, and Article 203 imposes personal penal liability where concealable export revenue is diverted.

Director personal liability: If the director personally designed or signed the false invoices and pricing documents, the corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20, exposing personal assets to civil restitution for creditor losses. The director may be placed on the tax enforcement blacklist, banned from leaving mainland China, and may face criminal detention or imprisonment together with confiscation of proceeds. Directors participating in export evasion also risk lifetime disqualification from serving as legal representatives of PRC enterprises.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Base Erosion Payments

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 44 authorise the tax authority to disregard the abusive arrangements, reattribute income and tax the adjusted amount at 25%. Article 114 of the Implementation Regulation allows deemed profit measurement where books and records are deficient; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation triggers a penalty under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 62 and a potential recharacterised deemed distribution subject to 10% withholding tax. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 permits a 10-year look-back period and interest on the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approved the sham intercompany agreements are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and surcharges to the extent they personally benefited from the diverted profits. The director of the Chinese JV faces an exit restriction as an individual with clear legal responsibility for unpaid tax, and the Hong Kong director may be subject to disqualification proceedings under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance for taking dividends without economic substance. Serious deliberate falsification of contemporaneous documentation exposes individuals to criminal liability for tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 201.
Shell Company Treaty Shopping and Anti-Money Laundering Breach

Statutory sanction: The tax authorities will deny treaty benefits under STA Bulletin No. 9 of 2018 and apply Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 to impose tax at 25% computed as if the Hong Kong shell did not exist; the dividends are then treated as paid to ultimate recipients, triggering 10% withholding tax. If the shell is used to launder funds from unlawful activities, PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law penalties apply to the entity and individuals, including fines of up to RMB 5 million. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance may also impose penalties for false tax residence declarations and the shell may be struck off the Companies Registry.

Director personal liability: The directors of both the PRC JV and the Hong Kong shell are personally exposed for false treaty declarations; civil recovery of the improperly reduced withholding tax may be claimed from individuals who caused the misrepresentation. Travel bans will be issued to prevent departure from mainland China and Hong Kong for tax-related investigations. Directors who knowingly structured the shell to conceal beneficial ownership can be prosecuted for money laundering, and if the veil is pierced, they face unlimited personal liability for unpaid taxes plus up to 10 years of imprisonment for egregious cross-border financial fraud.
