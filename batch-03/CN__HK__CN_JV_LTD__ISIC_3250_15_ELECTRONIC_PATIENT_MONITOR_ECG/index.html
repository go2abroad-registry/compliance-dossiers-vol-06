PRC VAT: mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable turnover exceeds CNY 5,000,000; below the threshold, the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or elect general taxpayer status. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC VAT zero-rating is available for goods that are actually exported from PRC customs territory, with the export declared to the General Administration of Customs, the JV acting as the exporter and VAT registered entity, the sale paid by a foreign non-resident buyer, and the export proceeds repatriated in accordance with SAFE trade-in-goods foreign exchange controls. The ECG patient monitors must move physically outbound on the basis of the export customs declaration and sales contract. Qualifying offshore services supplied to non-residents may also be zero-rated or exempt under STA cross-border service rules. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST exists, so exports from Hong Kong are simply outside the scope of indirect tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system in which the RMB is convertible for current-account items but remains highly controlled for capital-account items. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture registered in mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the People's Bank of China (PBOC) supervise all cross-border remittances. Current-account trade in goods and services, including import and export payments for electronic patient monitors and component procurement, can be settled freely at designated banks, but each remittance is subject to verification of underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax filings. Capital-account inflows, such as equity contributions from the Hong Kong shareholder, require an SAFE FDI registration, a foreign-exchange capital account, and post-settlement use that is consistent with the registered business scope. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permissible only after the annual statutory audit, tax clearance, and completion of a SAFE dividend remittance filing, with proof that the profits were generated by the JV's own operations and not from onshore speculative use. There are no strict surrender requirements for current-account receipts, but all export proceeds must be repatriated to China. The Hong Kong parent enjoys full convertibility under HKMA, but funds moving from Hong Kong into the mainland remain subject to PRC capital-control checkpoints.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated into the PRC through a designated foreign-exchange bank within 180 calendar days from the date of the export declaration. There is no statutorily mandated conversion ratio: the JV may retain up to 100% of the repatriated foreign currency in a Foreign Exchange Current Account or convert any portion into RMB at the prevailing onshore market rate. If the JV fails to repatriate within the 180-day window, SAFE may issue an administrative penalty and the trade verification status may be downgraded, requiring a written explanation and documentary evidence of exceptional commercial terms such as open-account credit with the importer.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign-exchange administration arm of the People's Bank of China (PBOC), together with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong parent treasury leg

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW for the onshore mainland entity and LIMITED for the offshore Hong Kong shareholder. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, and Revolut Business are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to maintain CNY settlement accounts, process export proceeds, or act as the principal clearing rail for a PRC-resident entity. Routing JV trade payments through a non-designated offshore EMI would violate SAFE authenticity verification and PBOC reporting obligations and could trigger account freezing or administrative penalties. The Hong Kong parent may use a licensed Hong Kong Money Service Operator or Stored Value Facility issuer for its own treasury purposes, but such an account cannot serve as the JV's primary collection or disbursement rail. The compliant architecture is a Tier-1 PRC bank as the owner bank plus a corresponding Hong Kong clearing account for cross-border sweep, with fintech platforms used only for ERP connectivity or secondary reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder transfers HKD or USD via SWIFT MT103 to the mainland JV's Foreign Exchange Capital Injection Settlement Account at Bank of China, using the capital-account remittance purpose code for FDI capital contribution and referencing the SAFE FDI registration number. On receipt, the bank verifies the SWIFT sender against the registered foreign investor, completes the capital-account receipt confirmation, and notifies the JV. An onshore CPA then issues a capital verification report confirming that the injection matches the registered capital schedule. When RMB settlement of the capital proceeds is required, the bank converts at the onshore market rate into an RMB capital settlement account and monitors each disbursement against permitted business purposes such as rent, salaries, and raw material purchases. Any subsequent return of capital, reduction of registered capital, or onward outbound remittance of investment proceeds requires SAFE approval and a cross-border outbound remittance with the appropriate repatriation-of-investment code.For outbound settlements, use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers to component suppliers and logistics partners up to established tolerance thresholds. For high-value ECG monitor component purchases, issue irrevocable documentary letters of credit via SWIFT MT700 with confirmed payment terms. Use documentary collections D/P or D/A only for open-account transactions with highly vetted, recurring counterparties. For RMB-denominated trade with Hong Kong or other participating banks, route through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System CIPS instead of SWIFT, using the proper goods-trade or service-trade purpose code. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's contemporaneous Local File must be prepared on an annual basis if related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 400 million, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million; the Master File is mandatory where aggregate annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or where the taxpayer fails to meet safe-harbour criteria.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations do not apply directly to a mainland China JV, but China's General Anti-Avoidance Rules under Articles 47 of the EIT Law and Articles 120-123 of the Implementation Regulations require substance over form for every cross-border arrangement; the JV's core income-generating activities (CIGA) for design, assembly, calibration, and risk control over ECG patient monitors must be performed in China, while the Hong Kong shareholder must possess staff, offices, decision-making authority, and real risk-bearing to claim treaty benefits and to avoid being re-characterised as a technology-holding shell; failure to establish substance will trigger a GAAR adjustment, denial of DTA relief, and deemed dividend reclassification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Fraud / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, and 47; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: tax authority shall reassess taxable income using TNMM or profit split, recover underpaid tax, impose daily late-interest surcharge at 0.05%, and levy an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid EIT; further TP documentation failure may lead to a separate RMB 10,000 penalty plus adverse inference adjustments.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 23 and Criminal Law Article 201, a director who knowingly signs false financial statements or instructs the CFO to prepare fraudulent transfer pricing files is personally jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and related civil damages; personal bank accounts and real estate may be frozen; exit-control measures including court-ordered travel bans and tax blacklisting apply; criminal conviction for tax evasion carries up to three years' imprisonment where the amount is moderately large, and three to seven years plus fines where the amount is exceptionally large and the false reporting proportion is high.
Unlawful Outbound Profit Repatriation With WHT Failure

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 and Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37 and 38: the JV as withholding agent is ordered to remit the unpaid 10% statutory WHT (or 5% treaty rate if properly substantiated); if the order is ignored, a fine of 50% to 300% of the unremitted WHT is imposed; unpaid WHT itself continues to accrue the daily 0.05% overdue charge, and the disguised fee payment is treated as non-deductible plus a deemed dividend subject to secondary assessment.

Director personal liability: The company directors and financial superviser who caused the JV to divert cash to the HK shareholder without WHT are personally liable to restore the withheld amount to the tax authority; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the directors used the JV as an instrument of fraud, subjecting their personal assets to restitution; the director may be placed on the State Administration for Taxation joint-discipline blacklist, triggering denial of exit visas, high-consumption limits, and public stigma; criminal liability may arise under Criminal Law Article 201 for evading tax by concealment, with maximum imprisonment of seven years for high-amount evasion.
Shell Vehicle / Abuse of Tax Treaty and AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) authorises the tax authority to disregard the HK shell, re-characterise the dividend recipient as the substantive owner, deny the 5% treaty benefit, and impose the full 10% statutory WHT on the difference, plus late interest at 0.05% daily and a 50% to 300% penalty; under the Joint Punishment Memorandum for Dishonest Taxpayers, the entity and its responsible director are listed publicly and subjected to regulatory blacklisting, procurement bans, and restriction on high consumption; Hong Kong law separately enforces filing penalties and may strike off the shell for lack of reasonable economic substance under the Companies Ordinance and Economic Substance Requirements.

Director personal liability: Directors who consciously used the HK shell to abuse the double taxation arrangement are exposed to veil piercing: Chinese courts can impose joint and several personal liability for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, and penalties; the director's Hong Kong and mainland assets are subject to freezing orders; a tax blacklisting travel ban prevents departure from China until restitution is made; if fraudulent documents were submitted to claim treaty relief, the director faces criminal sanctions under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion and Article 280 for forgery of official documents, with imprisonment up to seven years and monetary fines, as well as disqualification from holding corporate directorships.
