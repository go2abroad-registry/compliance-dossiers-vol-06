None. Under Hong Kong law, no minimum paid-up capital is required for a private company limited by shares. Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

The composite CN/HK structure requires a two-track registration. Track 1 - PRC mainland: the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) is a PRC resident enterprise; upon issue of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for corporate income tax and VAT, and the STA registers the entity through the data-sharing mechanism. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC: all entities that carry on taxable sales must obtain the USCC/TIN upon incorporation; no turnover exemption from tax registration applies. Mandatory VAT general-taxpayer registration is required when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that amount the entity is a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless general taxpayer status is elected. Non-resident suppliers without an establishment in the PRC generally have VAT withheld by the purchaser; a direct non-resident tax registration is necessary only when taxable supplies are made through an establishment in the PRC. Hong Kong SAR: no indirect tax registration threshold; only the Business Registration Certificate requirement applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC export tax rebate regime, exported physical goods are zero-rated for VAT and the exporter may claim a refund of input VAT. Eligibility requires: (i) the exporter is a PRC VAT general taxpayer; (ii) the goods are genuinely sold and transported out of the PRC customs territory; (iii) the goods are supported by a valid VAT special input invoice or customs VAT payment document; (iv) an accepted Customs Export Declaration exists under an applicable export customs procedure; (v) export proceeds are collected through a PRC bank and reported in the SAFE trade-in-goods foreign exchange monitoring system; and (vi) the export sale is documented by a commercial invoice, packing list and marine bill of lading. The Hong Kong SAR free-port leg does not levy VAT on export sales, but it does not by itself establish PRC zero-rating; the PRC export documentation must be complete.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the customs-accepted electronic export goods declaration) evidencing physical export from the PRC customs territory
  • Marine Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport document) showing the PRC loading port and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the bales of ginned raw cotton
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt record issued by a PRC bank for the export proceeds (statutory evidence in the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system; functionally the relevant PRC counterpart to an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • VAT Special Invoice for the upstream purchase of the ginned raw cotton, or the small-scale taxpayer settlement or payment voucher where the VAT rebate calculation requires it
  • Export tax rebate and zero-rating declaration data filed through the STA e-Tax portal, including export sales data and customs clearance reference numbers

PRC: VAT invoices must be issued through the STA national fully digitalized electronic invoice system and integrated electronic tax-control platform. Special VAT invoices are subject to the Golden Tax System anti-fraud environment; each invoice receives a unique tax-identification number, invoice code and QR verification code, and issuance data is transmitted to the STA in near real time. Taxpayers use the digital taxpayer account on the e-Tax portal to obtain invoice numbers, verify counterparty invoices, and file monthly VAT returns and export rebate declarations. Bulk issuance by trading companies requires the approved e-invoice API or tax-control UKey integration. Hong Kong SAR: no fiscal clearance or electronic-invoicing mandate exists; commercial invoices are accepted and no digital stamping is required.

All PRC export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to risk-based audit by the STA and the General Administration of Customs using the Golden Tax System cross-matching of invoices, customs declarations, and FX receipts. In a clawback event, the STA will disallow zero-rating, require reversal of the input VAT refund, impose late-payment interest, and may assess a penalty under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection of the People's Republic of China of 50% to 5 times the tax involved; criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds may apply under the PRC Criminal Law, including long-term imprisonment up to life imprisonment for the most serious amounts. In Hong Kong SAR, there is no export VAT to claw back, but false or unsupported territorial-source offshore claims may be reassessed with additional profits tax and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as an offshore special territory with no foreign-exchange controls: the HKD/USD link, managed under the HKMA currency-board framework, guarantees free convertibility for trade, dividends, and capital movements through licensed banks. The PRC, by contrast, runs a managed current-account-convertible but capital-account-controlled regime supervised by SAFE. The CN-HK joint venture can be viewed at two layers. At the Hong Kong JV layer, HKD, USD, RMB (offshore CNH), and third currencies may be converted freely onshore without surrender requirements but transactions must be genuine and supported by proper corporate authorizations. At the PRC parent layer, any remittance of equity injection into the HK entity is an overseas direct investment (ODI) requiring MOFCOM/NDRC approval and SAFE registration; the PRC bank will require the ODI filing certificate before execution. Profit remittance from a PRC operating subsidiary to the HK shareholder would require an annual statutory audit, PRC corporate income tax settlement clearance, board approval, and SAFE-verified dividend remittance via the bank, while dividends from the HK JV to a PRC shareholder are generally freely distributable after HK audited accounts but may raise PRC tax on the mainland investor. No blanket surrender percentage exists for export proceeds today, but all PRC export receipts must be repatriated into the designated foreign-exchange account and reconciled with customs and SAFE trade-monitoring data.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a PRC export entity, foreign-currency receipts from export sales of raw cotton bales must be repatriated into the PRC entity's domestic current-account FX receipt accounts through a designated SAFE-licensed bank no later than the statutory maturity date, generally within 90 days from the customs export declaration date, unless an extended deferred-receipt registration has been recorded in SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring system. Advance export receipts must be registered if they exceed the prescribed threshold/maturity threshold (commonly 30 days) under the advance-receipt reporting regime. There is no mandatory percentage sale to the central bank: the exporter may retain 100% of the export FX in an account or may settle it into RMB at the bank upon presentation of the underlying customs declaration, invoice, B/L and warehouse receipts. For the Hong Kong JV layer, no surrender rule applies; export proceeds may remain in USD, HKD, or CNH and be used freely for operating and distribution purposes.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR, acting in coordination with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for all cross-border RMB and PRC current/capital account flows

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally constrained. Hong Kong does not have an electronic money institution license class; companies that hold or transfer customer funds require an MSO license from the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department. A PRC-side entity is prohibited by SAFE from using unlicensed offshore EMI accounts to perform current-account trade settlements, ODI equity injections, or FDI capital inflows. Therefore, neobanks and virtual EMIs may serve only as secondary, low-value expense cards for the Hong Kong entity after primary licensed-bank accounts are live, and must never be the routing channel for capital, LC, or SAFE-monitored export proceeds. Use HKMA-licensed banks for all treasury-critical traffic.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Where the capital is injected from the PRC shareholder into the HK JV, the PRC parent must first complete ODI approvals/filings with MOFCOM, NDRC (where applicable) and SAFE bank-level registration; the designated PRC settlement bank will convert RMB to USD/HKD and send an SWIFT MT103 or CIPS/CNH remittance under the SAFE-recognized overseas investment category, with the remittance narration clearly reading 'equity injection into Hong Kong JV for raw cotton export trading'. The HK receiving bank will credit the share-capital escrow account and require the HK company's share allotment forms, board minutes, and registered capital returns to verify the consideration. If instead the structure is a PRC-incorporated Sino-Foreign EJV receiving HK shareholder capital, the HK remitter must credit the PRC entity's SAFE-registered FDI capital account, the PRC bank will verify the FDI registration certificate and the use of the funds, and the capital can be converted into RMB only for approved current expenditures after authenticity review. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low and legally constrained. Hong Kong does not have an electronic money institution license class; companies that hold or transfer customer funds require an MSO license from the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department. A PRC-side entity is prohibited by SAFE from using unlicensed offshore EMI accounts to perform current-account trade settlements, ODI equity injections, or FDI capital inflows. Therefore, neobanks and virtual EMIs may serve only as secondary, low-value expense cards for the Hong Kong entity after primary licensed-bank accounts are live, and must never be the routing channel for capital, LC, or SAFE-monitored export proceeds. Use HKMA-licensed banks for all treasury-critical traffic.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: No fixed statutory threshold in IRO; IRD's DIPN 59 administrative benchmark expects contemporaneous Local File when aggregate controlled transactions exceed HKD 10,000,000 and Master File for groups above HKD 1,000,000,000 turnover; CbC reporting is mandatory when group consolidated revenue reaches HKD 6,800,000,000

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain a formal Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the territorial source doctrine under IRO s.14 and anti-avoidance rules under s.61A/61B require real substance: Hong Kong board decisions, local staff, office, bank accounts, risk management, and Core Income-Generating Activities must support the cotton brokerage; an invoice-only conduit whose substantive selling, negotiation, and logistics decisions occur in mainland China will be treated as a shell and its income reattributed to the PRC parent

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(2) and s.82(1): penal tax of three times the undercharged tax, fine up to HKD 100,000, daily default surcharge up to HKD 2,000 per day, and imprisonment up to 3 years on indictment

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced for directors who sign or cause false returns: civil asset-recovery and restitution orders, travel restriction and blacklisting through tax investigation procedures, director disqualification up to 15 years, and criminal imprisonment between 6 months and 3 years
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61A and s.61B empower the IRD to disregard or recharacterize the sham arrangement, recompute arm's length profits, assess additional tax, and impose penalties of three times the tax undercharged under s.82A

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for assets moved through the shell; courts may order restitution, surrender of travel documents or blacklisting, disqualification orders, and conspiracy-to-defraud sentences up to 14 years; simultaneous enforcement in mainland China substantially increases personal exposure
Transfer Pricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AA(10) requires the IRD to substitute the arm's length result; penalties under s.80(2) of three times the additional tax; record-keeping penalties of HKD 10,000 plus HKD 2,000 per day; and extra tax up to 100% for neglect under s.82A

Director personal liability: Directors can be jointly and severally liable for neglected TP tax; personal civil restitution may target bank accounts in Hong Kong and Shanghai; disqualification under the Companies Ordinance s.168, IRD travel bans, and criminal prosecution for aiding false returns expose directors to imprisonment up to 3 years
