Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated under Mainland China VAT if: the seller is a VAT general taxpayer; the goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory; an export Customs Declaration for Exported Goods is lodged; the export sale is evidenced by contract and commercial invoice; and the sales proceeds are repatriated in foreign currency through licensed banks within the statutory period. For agricultural commodities such as soybeans and yellow corn, the zero-rate applies to the export supply; the export VAT rebate is substantiated through the STA export tax rebate system and customs electronic clearance records. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST regime, therefore no export zero-rating is required.

  • Customs Declaration for Exported Goods submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and released by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing actual shipment from PRC customs territory to the overseas consignee
  • Export sales contract or sale confirmation evidencing the cross-border bulk commodity transaction
  • Commercial invoice and packing list/weight list aligned with the customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt voucher, including the Cross-Border Receipts/Payment Declaration record, confirming repatriation of export proceeds
  • Electronic customs clearance/release record from the customs system
  • Phytosanitary certificate, certificate of origin, and cargo inspection certificates where required by the importing jurisdiction
  • VAT special invoice or export invoice as required by the competent STA for export rebate matching

Mainland China requires electronic invoicing through STA-controlled systems under the Golden Tax Phase IV digital fiscal infrastructure. VAT general taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices using tax-UKey or fully digitalized electronic invoices, with one-invoice-one-code encryption and real-time transmission to the tax authority; private offline invoice templates are not permitted. Hong Kong SAR: no electronic invoicing clearance or digital stamping mandate; ordinary business records and invoices are accepted by IRD.

Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating will trigger retrospective clawback of claimed VAT zero-rating/export rebate, late payment surcharges at 0.05% per day, administrative penalties from 50% up to 500% of the tax evaded, and criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law for false export declarations and fraudulent export tax refunds. China Customs may impose fines for misdeclaration up to 30% of the customs value, with smuggling liability where intent is proven. In Hong Kong SAR, incorrect offshore profit or tax treatment claims expose the taxpayer to additional assessments, penalties up to 200% of tax undercharged under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and prosecution in cases of fraud or gross negligence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility under Articles VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, while the capital account remains heavily restricted. Legal entities are permitted to convert RMB for trade in goods and services, including agricultural commodity imports and exports, provided the transaction is supported by underlying commercial contracts and customs declarations. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and can be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at the enterprise's discretion, subject to foreign exchange receipt verification through the SAFE digital platform. Profit repatriation by a Sino-foreign equity joint venture to its Hong Kong or overseas investors requires a board resolution, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and proof of compliance with the joint venture contract. Dividends and liquidation proceeds are generally eligible for remittance after completion of annual audit and statutory tax filings. However, capital account transactions, including equity injection, shareholder loans, or cross-border guarantees, require prior SAFE registration and a real transaction background; any structure involving offshore derivatives or non-deliverable forwards inside China is prohibited unless the underlying trade exposure can be substantiated. The overall convertibility is therefore asymmetric: current account items are largely liberalised, while capital account movements are subject to stringent quotas, approvals, and post-event reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC regulations, export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 calendar days of the customs export declaration date unless an extension is granted by SAFE. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively zero—an exporter may keep all foreign currency in a retained account or voluntarily convert to RMB via the banking system. However, every such receipt must be matched against the Electronic Port Data system and reported through the ASONE platform; any surplus beyond the declared contract value triggers an automatic mismatch alert and may require a written explanation and supporting bills of lading, commercial invoices, and customs manifests.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the forex regulatory arm of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is rated LOW to NIL for the onshore PRC portion. Foreign non-bank electronic money institutions such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not licensed to hold deposits or conduct foreign exchange clearing for a domestic Chinese entity. Only SAFE-designated commercial banks may act as designated foreign exchange banks. An offshore Hong Kong EMI may provide ancillary payment collection for non-resident accounts, but it cannot replace the requirement for a domestic CNY settlement account or handle RMB convertibility. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should restrict EMI usage to pre-import overseas logistics and small-ticket third-party platform settlements, never as principal clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign currency equity injection into the CNY joint venture must follow the foreign direct investment route: remittance from the overseas shareholder via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code FDI-equity contribution is received into a Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a SAFE-designated bank. The remittance must state the investment purpose as capital contribution to the Sino-foreign JV for bulk grain trading, and the total amount must not exceed the amount registered on the Foreign-invested Enterprise certificate. After conversion, the bank submits the payment-settlement-foreign-currency information to SAFE within the T+1 reporting window. A capital verification appraisal from a PRC-registered certified public accountant must be issued within 90 days of the injection and filed with the market regulation authority.Trade payments for soybean and corn imports should clear through SWIFT MT103 for direct wire settlements or irrevocable transferable letters of credit issued by the Chinese buyer's bank in favour of the overseas seller, with negotiation via the London or Hong Kong clearing branch. The LC must be opened under China's International Trade Single Window and matched with an approved foreign exchange payment application. For documentary collections, the importer's bank forwards the trade documents against acceptance or payment; settlement proceeds are released only after customs declaration matching and tax payment. Advance payment remittances require bank approval under SAFE's Advance Payment Quota System and shall not exceed 12 months before the expected shipment date.Onshore CNY deliverable forwards and swaps with a genuine trade background, transacted only with a SAFE-designated domestic bank and backed by the underlying soybean or corn purchase/sales contractCross-currency swaps for foreign currency debt service tied to the equity investment and supported by SAFE forex registrationOffshore non-deliverable forwards in Hong Kong permitted for hedging CNY/USD exposure of the HK subsidiary, strictly prohibited if executed within the PRC domestic interbank marketCurrency options and structured forwards allowed only for risk-managed positions up to the amount of outstanding trade receivables or payables, with no naked speculative positions

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI cross-border viability is rated LOW to NIL for the onshore PRC portion. Foreign non-bank electronic money institutions such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Payoneer are not licensed to hold deposits or conduct foreign exchange clearing for a domestic Chinese entity. Only SAFE-designated commercial banks may act as designated foreign exchange banks. An offshore Hong Kong EMI may provide ancillary payment collection for non-resident accounts, but it cannot replace the requirement for a domestic CNY settlement account or handle RMB convertibility. Any attempt to route systematic trade payments through an unauthorised EMI would expose the joint venture to administrative penalties, frozen liquidity, and KYC de-risking. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should restrict EMI usage to pre-import overseas logistics and small-ticket third-party platform settlements, never as principal clearing rail.
