Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow account is required under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A Hong Kong company may be incorporated with one fully or nil-paid share. However, to support trade finance, SWIFT wire capability, and documentary letter-of-credit facilities for green coffee bean imports, the board should determine an appropriate initial paid-up capital, and shareholders must evidence the contribution of that capital into the company bank account prior to the issuance of share certificates.

Funding & unlock path: The Company must obtain a Fire Services Department inspection and, where the warehouse is classified as an industrial or storage undertaking, a fire safety certificate confirming compliance with escape route provisions, emergency lighting, fire extinguishers, sprinkler system ratings, fire alarm coverage, and annual service by a registered fire service installation contractor. The structural safety of the warehouse must be certified by an Authorized Person before racking installation, particularly for palletized and containerized green coffee bean loads. The Labour Department must approve the workplace safety statement for forklift operations, competent operator certification, manual handling training, personal protective equipment, and emergency response drills. No dangerous goods licence is required for green coffee beans provided they are stored dry, not self-heating, and separated from oxidizers and heat sources. If future industrial activities such as coffee roasting or grinding are contemplated, the Company must separately obtain environmental and fire safety approvals for those processes.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

The foreign-invested equity joint venture must first complete business registration with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) to obtain a Business License bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 days after issuance of the Business License, the enterprise must complete consolidated national tax registration at its local STA office via the China Electronic Tax Bureau portal. Required submissions include the AMR Business License, articles of association, feasibility study report, capital contribution verification (if applicable), proof of registered operating address, board resolutions, and appointment of a licensed tax agent for non-resident shareholders. The USCC is activated as the VAT registration number automatically upon tax registration. The company must also register with Customs for import/export operations and with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for foreign exchange settlement, using the same USCC.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China's Enterprise Income Tax Law imposes compulsory entity-level taxation without any check-the-box or pass-through election. There is no hybrid mismatch election. Under VAT, the joint venture may elect to be a general VAT taxpayer (standard rate) or remain a small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable sales are equal to or below CNY 5,000,000. For corporate reorganizations, the 'Special Tax Treatment' rules under the Enterprise Income Tax Law (Article 75) may be elected to defer tax recognition, subject to strict continuity-of-interest and business-purpose conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for registration; every legal entity conducting business in China must register for VAT upon incorporation. However, the simplified small-scale taxpayer status is permitted for annual taxable sales not exceeding CNY 5,000,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Administrative Measures for Export Tax Refund (Exempt) of Goods and Services, exported green coffee beans qualify for a 0% VAT rate when the goods are physically sold to an overseas buyer and leave the Customs Territory of China through a designated border port. The seller must be a registered general VAT taxpayer, hold a valid Customs registration, and have the export documented on a formal customs declaration. The transaction must be settled in foreign exchange (or reported via SAFE in cross-border RMB) and must be filed for export rebate within the statutory declaration period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean or Multimodal Transport Document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Proceeds Collection Verification Record
  • Export Sales Invoice (Commercial Invoice)
  • Export Contract (Sales Agreement) and Packing List

All VAT invoices (paper and electronic) must be issued through the Golden Tax System Phase IV and transmitted in real time to the STA's National Unified Electronic Invoice Platform. Each invoice carries a mandatory digital anti-counterfeiting stamp and 20-digit invoice code. Issuers must integrate their enterprise resource planning (ERP) systems with tax authorities via approved application programming interfaces (APIs). For exports, the customs declaration and e-invoice data must be digitally matched before an export VAT refund is processed.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs declarations, VAT invoice data, and foreign exchange receipts. Any discrepancy, missing document, or fraudulent claim triggers immediate denial or cancellation of the export refund. If an unverified or fraudulent zero-rating is discovered, the taxpayer must retroactively repay the refunded input VAT, pay a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax (calculated from the day of refund), and may incur an administrative fine ranging from 50% to five times the defrauded tax amount. Where the amount is substantial or the act is intentional, criminal liability for tax evasion or export fraud under the Criminal Law may be pursued, carrying custodial penalties.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland Chinese Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operates under a managed floating RMB regime. Current-account activity is fully convertible for genuine trade and service transactions, including coffee import settlement, freight, insurance, inspection fees, and post-tax profit remittance, provided the contractual, invoicing, customs, and tax documentation is presented to the designated bank. Capital-account transactions, however, remain strictly controlled: FDI equity injection from the Hong Kong parent must be registered with SAFE, deposited into a dedicated FDI Capital Settlement Account, supported by statutory capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant, and used only within the approved business scope. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds; the JV may retain foreign currency in an onshore current-account FX quota or sell it to the bank at the market reference rate. Profit repatriation to the HK parent is permissible only after annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and a bank-level review confirming the remittance does not exceed audited distributable profits. The Hong Kong offshore parent enjoys free currency convertibility under HKMA and has access to CNH, USD, and global clearing rails, but the onshore JV cannot legally access offshore derivatives or fintech rails without violating SAFE's real-demand and capital-control rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio is currently imposed on PRC exporters after the 2012 foreign exchange settlement reform. Export proceeds from green coffee shipments must nevertheless be repatriated to mainland China within 180 days of the export customs declaration date and reconciled through the SAFE Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring Platform. The JV may choose to retain 100 percent of the foreign currency in an onshore foreign currency current account, sell the full amount to the designated bank in RMB, or use a combination of both, subject to the bank's verification of the customs entry and the exporter's vessel/container tracking data. Where the retention quota is exceeded, the surplus must be settled into RMB or moved into a separate SAFE-monitored account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the dedicated foreign exchange administration authority; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the offshore clearing and monetary authority for the Hong Kong treasury hub.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low and legally unviable for the PRC statutory treasury. Offshore digital EMIs, Hong Kong virtual banks, and non-bank payment service providers are legally barred from opening a PBOC-regulated RMB basic settlement account for the mainland JV and cannot act as clearing agents for SAFE-monitored FDI or trade payments. Under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Non-Bank Payment Institutions and SAFE cross-border payment regulations, only licensed PRC banks with SAFE-cross-border payment capability and CIPS participation can settle the JV's statutory treasury flows. An offshore EMI may serve only as an auxiliary collection or invoicing channel for the Hong Kong parent, never as the regulated clearing rail for the mainland JV's capital injections, export proceeds, import disbursements, or profit repatriation.

Root cause: Economic substance failure: the JV appears to be a shell company with no warehouse, staff, coffee inventory, or commodity flow to support cross-border payments and FX hedging.

Defense evidence: Legal lease or ownership documentation for the warehousing/office premises; payroll records and social insurance contribution filings for JV employees; inventory register with in-bound/out-bound green coffee stock and container numbers; supplier and buyer master list; specialty coffee certification contracts; board meeting minutes and cash-flow projection; customs entry/exit summary records; and recent warehouse photographs or sworn commodity inspector reports.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first execute a board-resolution authorizing the FDI equity injection. On the mainland, the JV obtains SAFE's FDI Basic Information Registration Form and opens a dedicated Capital Settlement Account at a Tier-1 designated PRC bank, preferably Bank of China. The HK parent's licensed remitting bank then sends a SWIFT MT103 wire quoting the JV full legal name, the FDI capital account number, the SAFE registration code, and the payment purpose 'FDI EQUITY INJECTION / CAPITAL SUBSCRIPTION' with the required SAFE/BOP capital-account purpose code. On receipt, the PRC bank records the funds in the frozen capital account, issues a bank credit certificate, and the JV's certified public accountant performs statutory capital verification. The funds may subsequently be settled into RMB only through the designated bank, and any payment out of the capital account must follow a board-approved use plan with underlying green coffee trade documentation and SAFE reporting.For import settlement, the JV submits to its designated PRC bank a payment application, original commercial contract, invoice, bill of lading, packing list, phytosanitary certificate, and certificate of origin. The bank verifies trade authenticity under SAFE total-flow monitoring and then routes the payment via one of three clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct commodity wire settlements; a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the PRC bank in favor of the overseas coffee exporter with advising/confirming bank and tenor matched to the shipment; or Documentary Collections under D/P or D/A terms for established coffee counterparties. Cross-border RMB trade payments may also be settled through the CIPS network, avoiding FX conversion still subject to bank compliance. Export receivables from coffee sales are collected into the JV's onshore foreign currency current account, reconciled with customs data, then retained or settled into RMB at the JV's election.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD and CNY/EUR forward foreign exchange contracts with a licensed PRC bank, limited to the JV's real green coffee trade exposureFX swaps combining a spot and forward leg, with tenors aligned to letter-of-credit settlement and documentary collection datesPlain-vanilla currency options and option collars approved under SAFE's hedging-only end-user frameworkCross-currency interest-rate swap (CCIRS) for matched USD/RMB funding on approved FDI working capital loans, registered with SAFE where requiredOffshore CNH deliverable forwards may be used only by the Hong Kong parent; non-deliverable forward (NDF) execution is not permissible for the onshore JV under SAFE real-demand rules

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low and legally unviable for the PRC statutory treasury. Offshore digital EMIs, Hong Kong virtual banks, and non-bank payment service providers are legally barred from opening a PBOC-regulated RMB basic settlement account for the mainland JV and cannot act as clearing agents for SAFE-monitored FDI or trade payments. Under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Non-Bank Payment Institutions and SAFE cross-border payment regulations, only licensed PRC banks with SAFE-cross-border payment capability and CIPS participation can settle the JV's statutory treasury flows. An offshore EMI may serve only as an auxiliary collection or invoicing channel for the Hong Kong parent, never as the regulated clearing rail for the mainland JV's capital injections, export proceeds, import disbursements, or profit repatriation.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company is statutorily obliged to appoint an auditor and to prepare financial statements with an independent auditor's report unless it qualifies as a small private company. A small private company is exempt from audit only if it satisfies at least two of the following three conditions for the relevant financial year: (a) total annual revenue not exceeding HK$50,000,000; (b) total assets not exceeding HK$50,000,000; and (c) not more than 50 employees. For a member of a group, the thresholds are applied on a consolidated group basis. Even if the small-company thresholds are satisfied, an audit remains compulsory where: (i) any member holding at least 5% of the voting rights gives written notice requiring an audit; (ii) the company is a holding company of a group that is not a small group; (iii) the company carries on a business subject to a statutory license under the Banking Ordinance, Insurance Ordinance, Securities and Futures Ordinance, or the Money Service Operators Ordinance; or (iv) the company's articles of association require an audit. For the subject specialty green coffee distribution JV, the practical audit trigger is therefore the loss of small-company eligibility, a shareholder audit requisition, or a contractual obligation under the Sino-Foreign JV agreement or Chinese parent consolidation pack.
