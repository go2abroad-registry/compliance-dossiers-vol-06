VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 (RMB five million) in annual VAT taxable sales, which is the threshold for mandatory registration as a VAT General Taxpayer; entities below the threshold are automatically classified as small-scale VAT taxpayers, and non-resident sellers with no establishment in China must also register for VAT when they make taxable supplies within the Chinese customs territory, subject to the applicable threshold and cross-border e-commerce regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals qualifies for the 0% VAT export zero-rate and associated export tax rebate when the goods are physically exported from the Chinese customs territory under a lawful customs declaration, the seller is the Chinese JV as exporter of record, the overseas buyer and sales contract are genuine, the goods are listed in the export commodity tax rebate catalogue, the required phytosanitary/product compliance certificates are obtained, and the export proceeds are collected in foreign currency within the statutory collection period through a Chinese bank under SAFE rules. Qualifying offshore digital or cross-border services require separate analysis and are not covered by the goods zero-rating route.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration submitted electronically through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal/air waybill showing physical exportation from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and the zero-rated VAT export invoice issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform
  • Packing list with weight, quantity, product identifiers, and shipping marks
  • Sales contract or purchase order naming the overseas buyer and confirming the foreign currency sale
  • Bank foreign exchange collection receipt/remittance confirmation issued under the SAFE goods trade foreign exchange monitoring system, together with the cross-border receipts and payments reporting form
  • Export tax rebate application data file and supporting customs/quarantine documentation, including the phytosanitary certificate for dried goji berries/herbal botanicals when demanded by customs or the destination country

China enforces the Golden Tax Phase IV (Jinsui Phase IV) fully digitalized electronic invoicing mandate. All VAT invoices, including zero-rated export invoices, must be issued through the STA-approved Electronic Invoice Service Platform, which performs real-time fiscal data clearance, assigns unique invoice numbers/anti-counterfeit digital identifiers, and transmits the invoice data to the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau. Physical stamping is replaced by digital authentication; the system automatically cross-matches invoice data with customs Single Window declarations and banking/SAFE foreign exchange receipts. Failure to use the approved platform or offline issuance triggers tax risk control alerts and penalties.

The STA, China Customs, and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating through automatic data matching. If foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated and substantiated within 180 days from the customs declaration date, or if the export documentation is false or incomplete, the STA will claw back the refunded VAT, charge overdue interest, and impose an administrative fine of 50% to 5 times the fraudulent rebate amount under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. If the zero-rating is obtained through fictitious exports, fake invoices, or fraudulent customs declarations, the case will be referred for criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export tax refund, which can lead to imprisonment up to life plus property confiscation; the Hong Kong non-resident shareholder and any related intermediary may be jointly penalized for structuring or abetting the fraudulent refund.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates under a genuinely free capital account with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no differentiation between resident and non-resident convertibility for current or capital account transactions. The HKD is fully convertible and is linked to the USD under the currency board mechanism, providing a highly predictable liquidity framework for the joint venture's international wholesale operations. However, because the underlying trade corridor involves PRC-origin dried goji berries, herbal botanicals, and a Sino-foreign equity joint venture structure, the entity must navigate an operational overlap between Hong Kong's liberal convertibility and PRC SAFE regulations applicable to trade settlements and foreign direct investment capital injections. For PRC-nexus flows, current account items such as trade payments and services are generally freely convertible upon submission of authentic commercial documents, while capital account items including equity injects, cross-border loans, and profit remittances from a mainland subsidiary require registration, verification, and in some cases waiting periods with SAFE. Profit remittance from a Chinese operating branch or subsidiary is permissible only after posting audited financial statements, completing corporate income tax obligations, and obtaining a bank verification of the tax payment status; a 10% withholding tax may apply unless reduced by treaty. The HK entity itself cannot be subjected to Chinese capital controls, but any remittance of Chinese-sourced funds to the Hong Kong parent must be routed through a designated PRC bank with appropriate BOP reporting and purpose codes. Overall, the convertibility risk is low for Hong Kong, but medium for the mainland-linked leg, necessitating disciplined documentary handling and pre-agreed banking channels to avoid trapped cash or delayed profit repatriation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong SAR entity, there are no statutory surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no compulsory repatriation timelines for export foreign currency receipts. Export proceeds may be retained in HKD, USD, RMB, or any freely convertible currency in an HKMA-authorised bank. For the PRC-controlled leg of the cross-border supply chain, SAFE requires that export receipts generated by a domestically registered Sino-foreign joint venture be brought back through the banking system and reported in the foreign exchange receipt and payment platform. Under the current SAFE harmony of voluntary settlement rules, the exporter may keep the proceeds in a foreign currency account or convert them into RMB at its own discretion; there is no fixed percentage conversion mandate. The key statutory timeline is that after customs declaration of the export, the goods' foreign exchange receipts should ordinarily be received and booked within 90 days in the associated trade monitoring system, unless deferred payment extensions are approved or the enterprise is designated a trade facilitation pilot. All receipts must be matched to the customs declaration through the electronic port system, and any orphaned proceeds must be reclassified for capital account examination to avoid fines.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for HKD; the People's Bank of China (PBC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the controlling monetary and FX administrative authorities for all cross-border RMB flows, capital registrations, and mainland-linked joint venture funding that touches the People's Republic of China (PRC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Cross-border digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut Business are not legally barred in Hong Kong because they either hold an SVF licence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or are licensed as authorised institutions by the HKMA. Their viability for this joint venture is medium-to-low for the core treasury function due to three binding constraints: first, they generally do not provide a formal capital escrow account suitable for SAFE-recognised foreign direct investment or equity verification; second, they lack the ability to issue commercial letters of credit and handle documentary collections for large bulk herbal wholesale transactions; and third, their transaction limits and treasury counterparty concentration risk are not suited to a corporate client that must demonstrate rigorous nexus between invoicing, shipping, and regulatory reporting. They can be used as a minor supplementary channel for small-value high-frequency operational payments, but the primary clearing backbone must remain with tier-1 domestic banks that can integrate with CIPS, SWIFT, and trade finance ecosystems.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 100,000,000 threshold for Business Transactions (transactions with non-resident associates) before a full transfer pricing study must be maintained. Transactions below HKD 100,000,000 need only a brief Contemporaneous Documentation Summary. Additionally, Master File is to be prepared if aggregate connected transactions exceed HKD 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply when the Hong Kong entity functions as a pure equity holding or IP holding company. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance requires all companies to have adequate staff, premises, strategic management and CIGA judgments to justify trading profits; the IRD may disregard artificial offshore arrangements where the 'totality of facts' indicates that a Hong Kong company is a shell with no real commercial substance. For a China-parented JV trading dried goji berries, the entity must demonstrate procurement management, inventory risk, logistics coordination, credit function, and strategic decision-making in Hong Kong, or those profits may be re-attributed to mainland Chinese permanent establishment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) Sections 80 and 82: A fine of HKD 200,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years on summary conviction; on indictment, imprisonment up to 10 years, plus potential treble tax penalty. Further, the IRD may add a 10% negligence penalty or 100% fraud penalty on the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause to be filed false returns are personally liable: the corporate veil is pierced under common law fraud principles and IRO Section 82A. Directors may face personal civil liability for restitution of tax evaded, asset freezes via injunction, travel restriction or exit blacklisting by the Director of Immigration upon IRD application, and a personal criminal sentence of imprisonment for up to 10 years. Additional disqualification as a director under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) Section 52 may apply.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Breach

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) Section 61 and Section 61A: Arm's length re-characterization by Commissioner, with assessments on nil-profit basis. The IRD will determine actual profits as if the transaction had been conducted by a commercially rational operator. Penalty can be 10% of the tax undercharged; for deliberate false statements under Section 82, a fine of HKD 200,000 and imprisonment for 10 years. Additionally, the IRD may approach the Companies Registry for compulsory strike-off of the company under the Companies Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell JV are treated as partners or sole proprietors under Section 82A when the company is struck off. They may be jointly and severally liable for all unpaid corporate taxes, suffer personal bankruptcies, receive travel bans (blacklisting) preventing exit from Hong Kong, and face disqualification orders under Section 152 of the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years.
AML / Cross-Border Capital Irregularities

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) Sections 5 and 7: For a money service operator or financial institution reporting breach, a fine of HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for 7 years. For individuals dealing with property known to represent proceeds of crime under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) Section 25: a fine of HKD 500,000 and imprisonment for 14 years. Customs authorities can seize the entire proceeds of trade.

Director personal liability: A director who authorizes or acquiesces to the irregular financial flows is criminally liable; the corporate veil does not protect passive owners. Directors face personal confiscation orders on assets derived from suspicious transactions, travel ban and exit control, imprisonment for up to 14 years under Cap. 455, and permanent criminal record barring them from serving as directors in Hong Kong, mainland China, or other common law jurisdictions.
