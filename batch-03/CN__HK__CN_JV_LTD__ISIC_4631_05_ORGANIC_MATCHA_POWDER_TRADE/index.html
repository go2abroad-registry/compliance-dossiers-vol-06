E-government filing portal

Step 1: incorporate the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the legal taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes. Step 2: complete tax registration with the competent local STA office within 30 days after issuance of the business license, filing the JV capital structure, legal representative, business scope, bank accounts, accounting system and shareholder information. Step 3: determine VAT taxpayer status by electing general VAT taxpayer treatment or small-scale taxpayer treatment where lawful, and register for VAT invoice issuance through the electronic tax bureau. Step 4: complete export tax rebate registration with the STA export rebate system, including customs export registration, foreign-exchange settlement bank details and the designated export rebate bank account. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a PRC resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law; there is no pass-through eligibility, no territorial-scope election, and no general tax-exempt zone status for this wholesale trading entity).

Available election forms

None for entity classification (the PRC does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime; entity-level taxation is mandatory). Administrative elections that remain available include election of small-scale versus general VAT taxpayer status, election of special tax treatment for qualified corporate restructuring transactions under STA notices, and revenue-recognition/accounting elections only where expressly permitted by PRC tax law. For a Hong Kong shareholder or related Hong Kong vehicle, a territorial-source profits tax claim may be available before the IRD, but this does not alter the PRC JV's entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (VAT-exclusive turnover) from wholesale supplies of goods triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration for the JV; below this threshold the JV may remain a small-scale taxpayer under the simplified collection method. All newly established PRC entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance, and export rebate registration is separately required before claiming export zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of organic culinary and ceremonial grade matcha powder from PRC customs territory qualifies for the 0% VAT export rate and corresponding export rebate (exemption and refund) treatment, provided the JV is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from the PRC, the export customs declaration and transport documents are authentic and consistent, and export proceeds are collected and repatriated in accordance with foreign-exchange control rules. The zero-rating applies to the export sale; input VAT on domestic procurement of the matcha powder inventory is refundable through the export rebate system only after documentary matching with customs, invoicing and banking records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration/customs bill of entry) showing the matcha powder HS classification, declared customs value, destination country and customs release data
  • Bill of Lading, multimodal transport document or airway bill evidencing physical carriage of the goods from PRC customs territory to the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration quantity, unit price and total invoice value
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank export foreign-exchange settlement/collection statement confirming receipt of export proceeds from outside the PRC
  • Input VAT special invoice(s) (VAT Fapiao) for the domestic procurement of the matcha powder inventory, required for VAT export rebate computation

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved electronic invoicing and tax-control infrastructure, either via full-digital electronic invoices or through National Tax Control equipment under Golden Tax Phase IV. Every VAT special invoice carries an anti-counterfeit invoice code, tax-control cipher, and verification mechanism, and invoice data is transmitted electronically to the STA in near real-time; buyers must verify invoices through the national electronic invoice verification system, and no off-platform statutory VAT special invoice may be issued for input credit or export rebate purposes.

The STA, customs administration and banking monitoring systems cross-match export declarations, VAT invoices, transport data and export proceeds. If export zero-rating is claimed on false, unverified or non-repatriated documentation, the claimed refund or zero-rated benefit is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest; under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, a fine from 50% to 5 times the evaded tax may be imposed. False customs declarations are separately penalized under the PRC Customs Law, and fraudulent export VAT rebate claims may constitute criminal fraud under the PRC Criminal Law, giving rise to imprisonment and fines. Failure to receive export proceeds within the 180-day repatriation deadline generally converts the zero-rated/refunded position into a taxable and clawed-back obligation unless an STA-recognized exceptional circumstance is properly documented and approved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The treasury mandate spans two distinct legal environments. Hong Kong SAR maintains full currency convertibility and has no foreign exchange controls, capital account restrictions, or surrender requirements; HKD and CNY are freely deployable and the HKMA does not intervene in market-based clearing. In contrast, the Mainland Chinese component operates under SAFE's current account and capital account administration. Current account transactions (goods trade, services, remittances) are generally convertible, provided a full evidentiary trail (contracts, invoices, customs declarations, tax vouchers) is presented to a designated licensed bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, require separate approval or SAFE registration, and the outflow side—especially profit remittances to the non-resident partner—is restricted until the entity holds audited financial statements, has paid enterprise income tax, and obtains a tax clearance certificate. The JV must open a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, file FDI registration, and ensure that each cross-border capital movement has a genuine and verifiable commercial rationale. Profit remittance hurdles are heightened by the requirement to demonstrate the availability of distributable after-tax reserves and to present a board resolution clarifying dividend allocation. Practically, the RMB is not freely convertible outside the limited current account and permitted capital account channels; offshore CNY in Hong Kong can be traded through CNH markets, but the movement of funds between the onshore and offshore pools must be routed through RMB clearing banks with proper trade documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For Mainland China under SAFE regulations, all export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless SAFE has granted an extended deadline. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain the foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB, subject to the bank's post-settlement authenticity review. For the Hong Kong leg, there is no statutory surrender requirement, and export proceeds may be held indefinitely in any currency, though an internal treasury policy should avoid triggering any unintended mainland income recognition or economic substance concerns.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the Mainland China component; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR component.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low for Mainland China clearance. Fintech EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut, Wise Business) cannot issue a local bank account for a mainland-incorporated JV unless the EMI holds a PRC payment institution license, which currently does not cover commercial bank-grade current accounts. Mainland regulators mandate direct supervision by PBOC/SAFE on all domestic RMB and foreign currency clearing, so a non-bank intermediary is restricted to e-commerce agency collection under a specific certificate, not cross-border corporate treasury. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The foreign partner's capital injection must first obtain SAFE/FDI registration at the subject bank, then transfer via SWIFT MT103 with the BPM6 purpose code for 'Equity capital and investment fund shares' to the JV's foreign exchange capital account. On arrival, the bank performs post-registration verification against the foreign investment negative list, the JV's business license, and the executed JV contract. For the HK treasury arm, capital can be funneled via a discretionary investment holding account, but if the HK entity receives payment for the inbound equity, the wiring reference must state 'Registered Capital Contribution under JV Agreement' to avoid a reserve-only classification, and the China side must complete the SAFE FDIC registration form within 15 days.For trade settlements, use SWIFT MT103 for all low-risk, low-value payments such as packaging materials, organic certification fees, and logistics, using the ISIC 4631 documentation template. For large purchase orders for ceremonial-grade matcha, use a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) at sight or a usance LC of up to 90 days, issued by the HK bank and advised through an ICBC correspondent branch to the supplier's bank in Japan or China. Documentary collections (DP/DA) are suitable for medium-value existing counterparties where a dedicated relationship already exists, but for new cross-border suppliers always prefer a confirmed, irrevocable LC. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for Mainland China clearance. Fintech EMI platforms (e.g., Airwallex, Revolut, Wise Business) cannot issue a local bank account for a mainland-incorporated JV unless the EMI holds a PRC payment institution license, which currently does not cover commercial bank-grade current accounts. Mainland regulators mandate direct supervision by PBOC/SAFE on all domestic RMB and foreign currency clearing, so a non-bank intermediary is restricted to e-commerce agency collection under a specific certificate, not cross-border corporate treasury. In Hong Kong, licensed virtual banks and EMIs can provide multi-currency wallets and MT103-like settlements, but they are not a substitute for a full-service corporate bank when the entity requires documentary credits, import/export financing, and SAFE-linked services. The optimal structure is a domestic bank for all onshore flows and an EMI only as a supplementary receivable collection tool in Hong Kong, with daily sweeping to the HSBC or BOCHK accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB thresholds for related-party transaction documentation under PRC CIT rules: annual tangible-asset transfers exceeding RMB 200 million; annual intangible-asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; annual financial-asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; and other related-party transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. A Master File is mandatory when aggregate annual related-party turnover exceeds RMB 1 billion; contemporaneous Local File and special adjustment documents must accompany the annual filing when thresholds are met.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese JV must demonstrate real business presence, management, staff, warehouse capacity and distribution activity inside mainland China. The Hong Kong shareholder claiming treaty benefits must maintain substantive economic substance in Hong Kong, including active decision-making functions and core income-generating activities (CIGA), and cannot be a passive shell; although mainland China does not apply the Hong Kong-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 47 of the CIT Law and the anti-abuse provisions of the Mainland/HK tax arrangement are used to deny treaty relief to shell companies and artificial arrangements.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion from Domestic Revenue Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law: the tax authority will order rectification, recover unpaid tax plus a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge, and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; serious cases are prosecuted under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, carrying criminal fines and imprisonment up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false accounting and used the limited-liability vehicle to conceal profits may have the corporate veil pierced under Article 20(3) of the PRC Company Law and be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution to the company and unexhausted tax liabilities; the legal representative is also placed on the SAT serious-violation blacklist, which triggers an exit-control/travel ban from China until the tax is settled, while criminal imprisonment up to seven years may be imposed for aggravated fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Arm's Length Remuneration

Statutory sanction: Articles 41, 44, and 47 of the PRC CIT Law, together with SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42 and the Special Tax Adjustments Implementation Measures, authorize the tax authority to re-determine taxable income using the arm's length standard; the resulting underpayment is subject to special tax-adjustment interest at the benchmark lending rate plus two percentage points; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation can trigger penalties up to RMB 50,000 under Article 70 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, in addition to full recovery of the adjusted tax and late surcharge.

Director personal liability: If the transfer pricing scheme is used to divert JV profits into the Hong Kong shareholder and is coupled with informal benefit conversion, directors can be held personally liable for asset restitution under the corporate-veil-piercing rule; the legal representative may be blacklisted and travel-banned by the tax authority's joint punishment mechanism, and directors who knowingly execute circular sham transactions risk civil forfeiture and, where fraud is proven, criminal liability for tax-related offenses.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership Abuse and Withholding Shortfall

Statutory sanction: The Mainland/HK tax arrangement and the Article 47 general anti-avoidance rule of the PRC CIT Law operate to deny treaty benefits; the Chinese payer is ordered to withhold the statutory 10% dividend tax or 10% passive-income tax, recover daily late surcharges, and pay a penalty under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law of 50% to 500% of the unpaid withholding tax; the tax authority may also impose blacklisting under the serious-violation joint punishment framework.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly arrange a sham Hong Kong beneficial owner and dividend channel can have the corporate veil pierced, becoming personally liable for the under-withheld tax and restitution of corporate assets; the legal representative is blacklisted and prohibited from leaving China until liabilities are cleared; where the arrangement is used to conceal tax from onward transfer, criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law may result in imprisonment up to seven years.
