Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the PRC manufacturing and exporting entity: State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the E-Tax Bureau online tax portal and the International Trade Single Window for customs data. For the Hong Kong SAR holding or trading arm: Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR (IRD), administered through IRD e-Services.

E-government filing portal

On incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the PRC Foreign Investment Law and Company Law, the State Administration for Market Regulation issues a business license containing the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for PRC enterprise income tax and VAT. The JV must thereafter complete tax registration through the STA E-Tax Bureau, authenticate the digital tax certificate, and file the tax registration within 30 days of obtaining the business license. The Hong Kong shareholder company, as a non-resident investor, must maintain a Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate with the IRD; it does not require a PRC TIN unless it has a permanent establishment in China, in which case it must separately register through the STA Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration channel and obtain a non-resident tax identification code. The PRC JV's USCC is the central identifier for all export VAT refund and customs procedures.

Default tax classification

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not maintain check-the-box or elective entity classification regimes. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

The PRC VAT system requires every taxpayer supplying goods to register for VAT. Once annual taxable sales of goods exceed RMB 5,000,000, the entity is legally required to register as a VAT general taxpayer; below that threshold it may remain a small-scale taxpayer, but export operations and VAT zero-rating claims normally require general taxpayer status to obtain VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The PRC JV may apply the 0% VAT rate to exported Chinese baijiu as long as: (a) the goods are lawfully produced and licensed under PRC alcohol management rules; (b) the buyer is a non-PRC resident and title/risk passes after the goods physically leave PRC customs territory, including shipments to Hong Kong SAR; (c) the export is declared through the Customs International Trade Single Window under an eligible HS classification; (d) the export proceeds are received in an acceptable foreign currency through a licensed PRC bank; (e) all proof of actual export and foreign exchange repatriation is retained and reconciled with the tax return. The VAT consumption tax on baijiu is assessed at the production stage; the VAT zero-rating is substantiated through the export goods tax rebate and refund process rather than being exempt at supply point.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry/Export Goods Customs Declaration generated by the Customs International Trade Single Window)
  • Original Bill of Lading or electronic sea/air waybill evidencing departure from PRC customs territory
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Bank Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation issued by the PRC bank for the exported baijiu
  • Commercial invoice and packing list corresponding to the customs declaration
  • Export license or liquor wholesale qualification certificate for the shipped baijiu

The PRC applies mandatory fully digitalized electronic invoices (Fully Digitalized E-Invoices) through the STA E-Invoice Service Platform. VAT special invoices for export sales must be issued digitally and transmitted to the tax authority's centralized clearing system in real time; each invoice carries a digital encryption code/tax-control stamp issued by the STA. For exports, the invoice data is reconciled with the Customs International Trade Single Window export declaration and the exporter's VAT return; no physical tax seal or paper invoice is accepted for zero-rated export refund claims.

The STA and Customs may conduct post-export audits comparing customs declarations, warehouse/manufacturing records, bank FX receipts, and shipping documents. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 180-day deadline or submission of false/documenting inflated declarations results in reversal of the 0% VAT zero-rating, recovery of any refunded VAT, late payment surcharges of 0.05% per day, civil penalties ranging from 50% to five times the underpaid or fraudulently refunded tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, and referral for criminal prosecution for export tax fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. Additionally, export compliance violations under the PRC Export Control and customs regulations may lead to suspension of export rights and loss of the JV's AEO/trusted exporter status.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with substantive current-account convertibility but strict capital-account controls. The PBOC and SAFE require all trade-related foreign-exchange receipts and payments to be routed through a designated domestic bank and reconciled with customs and contract data. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, ordinary export sales, service fees, commissions, and profit distributions are generally settled freely once a bank has verified supporting documentation. Capital transactions, including the foreign investor's equity injection, foreign-currency loans, offshore guarantees, and cross-border treasury sweeps, require prior SAFE registration and the use of dedicated capital accounts. Dividend remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder must be supported by audited financial statements, the shareholder/board distribution resolution, tax filing records, and the bank's conformity check; if the JV has outstanding foreign debt or unsettled tax liabilities, remittance may be blocked. RMB is therefore convertible for genuine operating/trade flows, while speculative conversion, offshore balance-sheet expansion, and unfettered capital outflows remain prohibited. Additionally, funds drawn down under capital accounts must be used in accordance with the Foreign Investment Negative List and cannot be recycled into securities or entrusted loans unless explicitly permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds from the baijiu wholesale business must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign-exchange bank in China and reconciled through the SAFE Goods-Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. The statutory timeline for an export receipt to be credited and verified is closely tied to the contractual payment date and the export customs declaration; in standard practice, an exporter must be able to demonstrate that proceeds are received within the agreed trade term, and any overdue receivable beyond 90 days from the export date must be reported and explained to the bank without prejudice. Although the legal framework does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio, the effective obligation is 100% repatriation: 0% mandatory sale to the banking system is required, and the JV may retain up to 100% of eligible export foreign currency in a foreign-exchange current account or may convert it to RMB at market rate. The full amount must nevertheless remain visible to SAFE, and a failure to repatriate and verify within the monitoring window can result in downgrade to a restricted trade verification category, compulsory batch settlement, and higher documentary scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the banking supervisor for the Hong Kong treasury layer but imposes no foreign-exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability is Nil-to-Low for the mainland-incorporated JV. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from servicing a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture for RMB current-account, foreign-exchange settlement, or SAFE trade verification purposes; only licensed Chinese commercial banks may provide these services and obtain the necessary trade-monitoring records. A separately incorporated Hong Kong treasury or distribution entity could validly use an HKMA-licensed digital EMI for ancillary offshore collections, but that EMI cannot be used as the settlement bank for mainland export receipts, customs reconciliation, export VAT rebates, or FDI capital injections. Any attempt to route the JV's export proceeds through an offshore EMI would breach the SAFE goods-trade administration framework and create a critical KYC blocker for future domestic banking relationships.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder executes the subscription agreement and board resolution for capital injection into the mainland JV. The JV must first complete the SAFE foreign-investment registration through its designated domestic bank, open a dedicated FDI capital account, and obtain the SAFE registration code or certificate. The injection is then transmitted from the HK bank to the JV's capital account via SWIFT MT103 or, for RMB, via CIPS, using the appropriate SAFE remittance purpose code for foreign-direct-equity investment. The beneficiary account name and number must exactly match the SAFE registration, and the bank must not accept settlement into a general operational account. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the funds against the registration, the accounting firm issues a capital verification report, and the JV may then convert the foreign-currency proceeds to RMB under the capital-account settlement rules for permitted operating expenditures, trade payments, staff costs, and other items outside the prohibited negative list.Export trade settlements from international buyers should be collected by SWIFT MT103 or Commercial Letters of Credit into the JV's dedicated export-proceeds retention account, with the bank matching each credit against the customs declaration and SAFE goods-trade file. For the JV's own outbound supplier payments, logistics costs, and overseas-agent commissions, the preferred rails are SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency disbursements, irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit governed by UCP600 for large raw-material or equipment purchases, and Documentary Collections governed by URC522 for established trading counterparties where a moderate level of settlement risk is acceptable. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 0: Hong Kong does not prescribe a statutory monetary threshold that automatically requires Local File or Master File. However, under the Inland Revenue (Advance Pricing Arrangement) Rules and the IRD's Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes No. 59, enterprises with intercompany transactions exceeding HKD 44,000,000 for property transfers or HKD 22,000,000 for financial transactions, or controlled services transactions exceeding HKD 11,000,000, are expected to maintain contemporaneous documentation. In all cases, the HK JV must prepare and retain transfer pricing documentation if its related-party transactions exceed these materiality thresholds or otherwise make a claim to offshore profits or an arm's length return.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies economic substance principles through the territorial source doctrine, general anti-avoidance rules, and transfer pricing rules. The Hong Kong JV will be expected to demonstrate that decision-making, contract execution, inventory risk management, and distribution functions occur in Hong Kong. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) specifically apply to jurisdictions in the EU/OECD list, and Hong Kong is not currently on that list; however, offshore profit claims and treaty access require the performance of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. The entity must keep board minutes, employment contracts, office leases, and evidence of logistics, invoicing, and treasury management in Hong Kong to support substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Offshore Profits Claim

Statutory sanction: Sections 82 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance impose a fine of up to HKD 50,000 upon summary conviction plus a further penalty of three times the amount of tax evaded, and a fine of HKD 50,000 plus three times the tax evaded on indictment. The IRD may also raise an estimated assessment and apply a 150% surcharge on undercharged tax where gross negligence or wilful evasion is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false Profits Tax Returns or aid in the preparation of false accounts may be personally liable for the civil restitution of the tax lost and may be blacklisted by the IRD, preventing departure from Hong Kong through a departure clearance order. Criminal conviction carries imprisonment for up to three years, and a director may be disqualified from acting as a company director for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance.
Abusive Transfer Pricing and Shell Company Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under sections 50AAJ, 50AAP, and Schedule 17 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the IRD may make transfer pricing adjustments that recompute assessable profits at the arm's length amount, disallow the deduction of the inflated purchase price or commission, and impose a penalty of 100% of the additional tax due. Failure to maintain transfer pricing documentation can shift the burden of proof to the taxpayer and expose the company to a default penalty of up to HKD 200,000 in proceedings before the Board of Review.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally assessed for penalties in civil recovery proceedings, subjected to travel restrictions through IRD departure prohibitions, found liable for wrongful trading under section 740 of the Companies Ordinance, and, where the arrangement amounts to conspiracy to defraud the revenue, criminally prosecuted, with imprisonment of up to 14 years under the Crimes Ordinance.
Ineffective Export License Compliance and Cross-Border AML / Customs Violations

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Customs Law and the Export Control Law, unlicensed export of regulated goods may lead to confiscation of goods, a fine of up to 300% of the value of the goods, revocation of customs registration, and a category B customs penalty. In Hong Kong, carrying on the business of a licensed liquor importer or wholesaler without a license under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance is punishable with a fine of up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for two years; AML violations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance carry fines up to HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors may have the corporate veil pierced where the JV is used as a facade to circumvent customs or licensing law; the director's personal assets may be subject to confiscation and orders for the disgorgement of profits. A civil blacklist by customs and the Companies Registry may bar the director from leaving the jurisdiction, and criminal conviction for an ML/TF offence exposes the director to a maximum penalty of imprisonment for 14 years, a lifetime disqualification from managing corporations, and personal liability for the company's unpaid penalties, duties, and excise taxes.
