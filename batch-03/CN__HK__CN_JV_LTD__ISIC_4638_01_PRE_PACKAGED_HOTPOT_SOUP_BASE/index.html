Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports from China of pre-packaged hotpot soup base bricks by CN_JV_LTD qualify for the 0% Value-Added Tax rate, subject to: (i) physical departure of goods from mainland China customs territory, including exports to Hong Kong SAR; (ii) a valid China Customs export declaration filed in the exporting entity's name; (iii) foreign exchange proceeds received in China through approved banking channels; (iv) the exporter being the VAT general taxpayer holding valid export tax rebate registration; and (v) complete documentation matching the customs, invoicing, and foreign exchange settlement datasets across the tax, customs, and SAFE systems.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (electronic clearance record)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract
  • Foreign exchange income receipt / settlement declaration showing inward remittance

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's VAT invoice management system. Under the nationwide Golden Tax System Phase IV, fully digitalized electronic invoices must be transmitted in real time to the tax authority via the e-Tax application programming interface (API), and each invoice carries an encrypted anti-fraud digital stamp before issuance. Issuance outside this statutory channel is legally condemned as unsubstantiated, exposing the issuee to penalties and disallowance of input tax credits.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to audit cross-matching of customs declarations, VAT invoices, transportation documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If unverified or fraudulent, the tax authority retroactively claws back any VAT refund or exemption granted, charges overdue interest, and imposes an administrative fine of up to five times the tax amount involved. Deliberate export-rebate fraud constitutes a criminal offence under the Criminal Law, carrying penalties of up to life imprisonment in aggravated cases, concurrent with customs fines for false declaration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The onshore JV operates in a controlled convertibility regime. RMB is fully convertible for legitimate current-account transactions, including the export sale of pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot base bricks, but every receipt must be reconciled against the customs declaration through the China International Trade Single Window and a designated bank. Capital-account convertibility is restricted: foreign direct investment, the foreign shareholder's equity injection, intra-group loans, and dividend/profit repatriation require SAFE registration or prior approval, capital verification by a Chinese CPA, and tax clearance. The Hong Kong SAR treasury hub sits inside a currency board with full HKD/USD convertibility and no surrender rules, so liquidity can be staged in HKD or USD offshore and only the net required amount is injected onshore. In practice, the JV should maintain a domestic RMB basic deposit account for local operations, a foreign-currency current-account to receive USD/HKD trade collections, and a capital account for FDI injections. Surplus export proceeds in USD may be either settled into RMB or kept in the foreign-currency account up to the bank-monitored trade volume ceiling, but any capital transfer such as the eventual dividend to Hong Kong will remain blocked unless audited annual financial statements and a SAFE FDI dividend remittance filing are submitted. Profit remittance hurdles also include a 5% or 10% withholding tax on dividends under the mainland China-Hong Kong tax arrangement, depending on the JV's actual shareholding structure and substantive operation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods-trade FX administration rules, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China within 180 days of the goods being released by Chinese customs, unless a formal extension is registered for force majeure or genuine trade delay. There is no current statutory requirement to surrender a fixed percentage; the JV may either convert all export proceeds into RMB at the spot rate or retain them in a current-account foreign-currency account. The retained balance must be consistent with the customs-declared export value, and each invoice must be matched to a Monetary Declaration (Bank Receipt) before the bank will credit the funds. Any mismatch, late receipt, or split collection from an undisclosed intermediary will trigger an abnormal foreign exchange receipts review and can suspend the JV's trade facilitation status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore Sino-Foreign Joint Venture; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR treasury hub

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-Medium. In mainland China, a fintech EMI cannot lawfully handle the JV's export proceeds or capital account settlement because only SAFE-designated banks are permitted to process trade settlements, RMB conversion, and FDI verification. Hong Kong does not have an EMI licensing category; companies may use a licensed Money Service Operator or Stored Value Facility for small-value incidental payments, but these entities do not provide trade finance, letters of credit, or clearing through the RMB Monetary Declaration system. The JV's primary banking rails must therefore run through tier-1 banks in China and a full-bank license in Hong Kong. A fintech EMI can be used only as a non-core operating wallet for minor procurement or staff reimbursements, and the JV must ensure that no export invoicing flows through it, otherwise the China tax and SAFE audit trail will be breached.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first obtain a SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) Registration from the bank in the name of the Chinese JV. After registration, the HK parent remits HKD, USD or RMB via SWIFT MT103/MT202 using a remittance purpose that specifically references the FDI equity injection and the SAFE business registration code. The receiving bank will place the funds in the JV's dedicated FDI Capital Account, not the operating account. If the injection is in a foreign currency, the JV may settle it into RMB but must file the foreign-currency to RMB capital conversion with SAFE and use it only for the approved business scope. A Chinese CPA must perform capital verification and issue a capital verification report within one month of receipt; the JV must then register the paid-in capital with the State Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE. Tax Evasion Through Fraudulent Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Article 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorizes tax authorities to make a special tax adjustment and require interest; Article 52 of the Implements Regulation; Article 123 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes an additional 0.05% daily interest and a 5-year statute of limitations for undocumented transfer pricing adjustments; failure to keep documents triggers a 1% daily penalty under State Taxation Administration Announcement 2016 No. 42, and understatement of tax may lead to a 50%-500% administrative penalty under the Tax Collection and Administration Law

Illegal Profit Repatriation Without Withholding and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 37 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law requires withholding at source; failure to withhold triggers Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law: a penalty of 50%-300% of the amount not withheld; the JV as the payer is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax; treating the offshore conduit as the dividend recipient without substance violates Article 46 and the Special Tax Adjustment rules; the DTA relief is denied under the Principal Purpose Test, leading to a retroactive 10% statutory rate and interest
