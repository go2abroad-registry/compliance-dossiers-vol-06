Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; Business Registration Office (BRO) under the IRD; electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk). Company registration for non-Hong Kong companies is with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that carries on business in Hong Kong must first register as a non-Hong Kong company with the Companies Registry under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) if it is not incorporated in Hong Kong, filing the prescribed registration documents and a Hong Kong principal place of business address. Within one month of commencing business, the entity must apply to the IRD's Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate using the prescribed application form on eTAX or at the BRO, submitting the Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company (or Certificate of Incorporation for a Hong Kong company), registered office/principal business address, director and company-secretary particulars, and a description of the business (ISIC 4641/NAICS 424320 off-price finished-garments liquidation trading). The Business Registration Number issued by the BRO is the statutory tax identification number for Hong Kong profits tax, salaries tax, and business registration; no separate VAT/GST registration or VAT ID exists. A non-resident entity not carrying on business in Hong Kong and deriving no Hong Kong-source profits is not required to register for profits tax, but must notify the IRD under section 51(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) upon commencing any Hong Kong-source taxable activity.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Hong Kong profits tax applies to corporations as separate taxpayers on territorial-source profits; the two-tier corporate profits tax rate is 8.25% on the first HKD 2 million assessable profits and 16.5% on the remainder; no pass-through treatment for limited companies).

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); foreign tax credit relief is available only where a bilateral tax treaty or unilateral credit rule applies.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No_General_Indirect_Tax (Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax; the only indirect duties are selective excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, none of which apply to finished garments)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration regime or turnover threshold in Hong Kong; a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is required for any person carrying on business in Hong Kong, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, so there is no statutory 0% export VAT rate to claim and no VAT refund mechanism for exports. For Hong Kong profits tax purposes, export-sales profits are taxable only if the profits arise in or are derived from Hong Kong; profits from qualifying offshore export trading may be treated as outside the territorial scope of profits tax, but this is an income-tax offshore claim, not an indirect-tax zero-rating.

  • Export declaration lodged with the Trade and Industry Department through the Trade Single Window under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E)
  • Full set of commercial invoices and packing lists
  • Bill(s) of lading or air waybill(s) evidencing the export movement from Hong Kong
  • Hong Kong Certificate of Origin (or supplier's certificate of origin) where preferential tariff or buyer documentation is required
  • Bank evidence of foreign-exchange repatriation, such as SWIFT MT103, bank inward remittance advice, or bank credit advice
  • Customs and Excise Department import/export declaration acknowledgements or other movement records maintained under Cap. 60E

No real-time fiscal clearance, mandatory e-invoicing, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime exists for VAT/GST in Hong Kong. Taxpayers must issue and retain sufficient invoices and business records for at least seven years under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and the Business Registration Ordinance; eTAX is used for profits tax, salaries tax, property tax, and business registration filings, not for indirect tax clearance.

Because no VAT/GST zero-rating exists in Hong Kong, there is no VAT clawback or VAT export-refund penalty regime. However, the IRD may audit an offshore-profits claim; if false or incomplete documentation is provided, the taxpayer may be liable under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) to penalties of up to treble the tax undercharged and, on conviction, fines and imprisonment for tax evasion. In addition, false export declarations or misrepresentations to the Customs and Excise Department or Trade and Industry Department are criminal offenses under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E), with fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime wherein current account transactions for trade-in-goods are broadly freely convertible once underlying commercial documents are verified, but capital account movements face strict licensing and registration. The off-price garment JV must maintain all foreign exchange receipts through designated domestic commercial banks, and any cross-border payment or collection must be matched against customs declarations or contractual documents. For export proceeds, China imposes a repatriation obligation that the foreign currency is to be received into the domestic settlement account within 90 days of shipment; once arrived, the enterprise is allowed to keep the proceeds in a foreign currency account or settle into RMB at the prevailing onshore rate without a fixed statutory conversion ratio, provided that the retained funds are declared in the bank's direct reporting system. Dividend and profit remittance from the JV to its Hong Kong shareholder is considered a capital account outflow and can only be executed after tax verification, audited financial statements are lodged, and the bank has reviewed the profit distribution resolution. Non-trade FX transactions such as intercompany loans, guarantees, and offshore hedging require additional SAFE/QFII-style structures. All hedging instruments onshore must be linked to a genuine exposure with a realistic trade or investment background.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be repatriated to the domestic designated account within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, subject to any extended period approved by SAFE due to exceptional circumstances. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; the JV may retain the foreign currency in an approved export-retention account or voluntarily convert it into RMB at the onshore market rate. Every transaction must be recorded in the bank's currency monitoring system, and monthly cumulative reports are required to substantiate the trade-in-goods nature.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the relevant supervisor for the Hong Kong financing arm

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to negative. Offshore fintech EMIs offer only ancillary treasury visualisation and cannot function as the principal clearing rail. They are not licensed by SAFE for onshore CNY clearing, cannot execute valid current/capital account FX registrations, and may not service a domestic JV for export proceeds with legal effect. Hong Kong virtual banks can service the HK parent's multi-currency accounts but any final RMB or trade settlement must happen via a mainland tier-1 bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI from the HK parent must be routed via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's foreign currency capital account at a tier-1 domestic bank. The JV must first complete SAFE's Voluntary Reporting of Foreign Investment to obtain an FDI code, and the remitter must supply a board resolution, corporate identification, and a subscription agreement. The receiving bank uses payment purpose code 6210 (Foreign Direct Investment) or 6213 (Capital Injection for Equity Participation) to verify and to issue settlement instructions; the bank will convert USD/HKD into RMB under the payment-by-verification principle after confirming the actual operating need. No conversion into RMB is possible for speculative use, and the funds cannot be deployed for entrusted loans, securities investment, or real estate except to the extent authorised by the Foreign Investment Negative List. A Chinese CPA is then required to issue a capital verification report to the local market regulator, finalising the equity registration.For global suppliers, the JV uses SWIFT MT103 transfers for plain settlements, with the purpose code corresponding to imported materials or general trade in goods. For big-ticket bulk purchases, an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit at sight or usance, advised by a Chinese bank and confirmed by a Hong Kong correspondent bank, is recommended as it links the opening bank, advising bank and beneficiary under documentary compliance. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are available for lower-value shipments when the JV is confident in the counterparty. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated company must be audited by a certified public accountant unless it qualifies as a small private company under section 359 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A company is a small private company if it satisfies at least two of the following criteria for the financial year: (i) annual revenue does not exceed HK$10,000,000; (ii) total assets do not exceed HK$10,000,000; (iii) average number of employees does not exceed 50. The audit exemption is unavailable if any member objects, if the company is a member of a group that fails the small-company test, or if the company is required to include its accounts in a larger consolidated statutory audit. Dormant private companies may also qualify for exemption under the Companies (Exemption of Companies from Disclosure) Regulation. The audit, where required, must comply with HKSA standards and be performed by a certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance. For this off-price apparel liquidation and wholesale trading entity, no industry-specific audit threshold applies beyond the Companies Ordinance test.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% standard Hong Kong profits tax rate; reduced to 8.25% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits under the two-tiered profits tax system.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0%. Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong company to its non-resident shareholder, including the PRC parent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and IRD DIPN 59, a taxpayer must prepare and retain a Master File and Local File where annual gross revenue exceeds HK$1,000,000,000 and the aggregate amount of controlled transactions with associates exceeds the following thresholds: HK$50,000,000 for goods transfers; HK$10,000,000 for financial transactions and intangible property; and HK$10,000,000 for other transactions. MNE groups with consolidated global revenue of at least HK$680 million must also comply with Country-by-Country reporting obligations.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate an acronymic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the same manner as offshore hubs, but the IRD applies the territorial source test and the general anti-avoidance rules in s.61 and s.61A to attack hollow nominee structures. To recognize a Hong Kong taxable presence and to uphold an offshore exclusion, the JV must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — sales negotiation, supply chain management, invoicing, logistics assignment, treasury, and final decision-making — are performed by its own employees and officers in Hong Kong. If the Chinese parent performs the CIGA and the Hong Kong JV merely passes invoices, profits will be re-allocated to China and the Hong Kong entity will be treated as a shell.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion — Fraudulent Offshore-Source Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance, Cap.112, s.82(1) and s.82A. A person who evades tax is liable to a fine of up to HK$50,000 and three times the amount of tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for up to 3 years; if aggravated by maintaining false books or destroying records, the fine rises to HK$200,000 and imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, connive, or knowingly benefit from the false offshore claim can be prosecuted personally and subjected to civil asset restitution, court-imposed travel bans/blacklisting when leaving Hong Kong pending tax recovery, disqualification under the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years, and imprisonment for fraud, in addition to joint and several liability for the underassessed tax and penalties.
Shell Company Abuse / Fraudulent Trading / Piercing the Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: Companies Ordinance, Cap.622, s.386 (fraudulent trading). A court may declare any person knowingly party to carrying on business with intent to defraud to be personally responsible for all debts and other liabilities of the company; on criminal conviction, the sanction reaches a fine of up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced: directors are personally ordered to repay company debts and creditors' losses, disgorged of siphoned cash and profits, disqualified as directors under Cap.622 Part 4 up to 15 years, placed on the IRD and court enforcement blacklist with travel restrictions, and criminally imprisoned where fraudulent intent is proved.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Non-Arm's Length Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance, Cap.112, ss.50AA, 50AB, 80, and 82A. The IRD shall adjust the assessable profits to reflect arm's length conditions and may impose an additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged, with a maximum criminal fine of HK$200,000 and up to 10 years' imprisonment if the manipulation is aggravated and committed with intent to evade tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize or cause the false invoicing, circular pricing, or concealment of related-party contracts are personally liable for the adjusted tax and penalty amounts under a piercing theory, subject to civil recovery of dissipated assets, disqualified from directorship, blacklisted with travel restrictions, and exposed to imprisonment for aiding and abetting tax evasion where active dishonesty is proven.
