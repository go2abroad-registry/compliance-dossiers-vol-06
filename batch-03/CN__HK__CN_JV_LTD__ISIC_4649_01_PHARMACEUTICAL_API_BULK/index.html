Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

The Hong Kong Companies Registry generally permits the registered office to be a service address, but a mere virtual office or commercial mailbox is not regarded as an adequate place of business for the purposes of the Companies Ordinance unless it is staffed during normal business hours by a licensed Hong Kong trust or company service provider (TCSP) holding a valid license under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance. For the pharmaceutical API wholesale JV, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) will scrutinize the Hong Kong business substance as a resident entity; a virtual office that does not maintain a physical place of business, substantive records, or primary operational management in Hong Kong materially increases the risk of a non-resident tax assessment, denial of treaty benefits, and the HKMA/anti-money-laundering suspicion of a shell entity. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the PRC-resident JV there is no monetary exemption from tax registration; all enterprises with a Business License must complete VAT registration. Under the PRC VAT framework, annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 is the mandatory threshold for general taxpayer classification; an entity below that threshold may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer using the simplified VAT method, while an entity at or above that threshold must register as a VAT general taxpayer and apply the standard-rate VAT on taxable goods. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such indirect tax is imposed.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, current account transactions are functionally convertible after legitimate verification by a designated foreign exchange bank. RMB convertibility for trade in goods, services, ordinary profit remittance and transfer of current items is granted provided genuine underlying contracts, invoices, tax payment records and customs evidence are presented. Capital account transactions, however, remain highly regulated. FDI equity contributions into the joint venture must be registered with SAFE, remitted through a designated FDI capital settlement account opened with a domestically licensed commercial bank, and verified by a PRC-registered CPA before being deployed. Dividend distribution and after-tax profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder are permissible under current account rules, but the bank will demand a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and proof that distributable earnings are real and free of disguised capital flight. Export proceeds from API sales must be repatriated into China under the 180-day repatriation rule, although enterprises may retain such funds in foreign exchange accounts or convert them based on documented trade flows. In practice, strict and granular documentary checks create a de facto capital account restrictiveness; any non-trade or speculative flow is effectively non-convertible. PBOC and SAFE may impose enhanced scrutiny for pharmaceutical API cross-border flows, especially where end-use, pricing, or counterparty risk is opaque.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exported from mainland China, all export proceeds must be repatriated into the exporter's domestic foreign exchange account or settled into RMB through a designated bank no later than 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. There is no blanket mandatory conversion ratio under current SAFE rules; the full amount must be either retained in a permitted foreign exchange current account or converted, and in both cases it must match the customs declaration data in the SAFE verification system. For high-risk or newly established enterprises, SAFE may impose a 100% settlement or acceleration requirement pending a compliance review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign-exchange administration body, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore fintech EMI or neobank accounts cannot satisfy the statutory requirement for a mainland onshore corporate account. PBOC licensing does not recognise overseas EMIs as deposit-taking institutions in China, and SAFE requires FDI capital to be routed exclusively through a licensed domestic bank as the receiving bank. Using an EMI as an intermediate interception account would break the capital verification chain and expose the joint venture to enforcement. The entity may engage a licensed payment institution as a sub-processor for low-value logistics payments, but the core treasury and trade settlement rails must remain with Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong investor must transfer the equity contribution via SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary FDI capital settlement account at the chosen Tier-1 bank, referencing the SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration Number and the entity's unified social credit code. The remittance must be classified under SAFE's international transactions code for direct investment and marked as FDI equity injection. After the transfer, the PRC bank will issue a foreign exchange receipt certificate, and a local CPA must complete capital verification within 30 days. Withdrawal or settlement of capital funds is only allowed up to the verified amount and must be supported by corresponding contracts, expenses, and customs documents; any change in capital amount requires a SAFE amendment or new capital verification.Import settlements for raw materials and APIs can be executed by SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer after bank validation of the import contract, invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, customs import declaration and, where needed, import drug licence. For larger or repeat counterparties, open an irrevocable commercial letter of credit via SWIFT MT700 routed through the beneficiary bank, with documents negotiated under UCP 600. Documentary collections (DP or DA) are acceptable for established counterparties; the remitting bank sends the documents under INCOTERMS with title retention clauses. Any foreign currency purchase for these payments must be completed at the Tier-1 bank with a valid trade purpose code and matched to SAFE records.Deliverable at-risk FX forward contracts between USD and CNY, EUR and CNY, or HKD and CNY for hedging underlying trade receivables or payables with a maturity not exceeding 12 months, subject to bank-imposed credit limits and genuine exposure documentation.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between USD and RMB for medium-term loan interest and principal exposure, executed under an ISDA or Chinese NAFMII master agreement after SAFE registration of the underlying foreign debt or capital item.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in CNH are permissible only through an offshore Hong Kong treasury vehicle or licensed offshore bank; the mainland JV may use them indirectly for macro hedge purposes, but onshore NDFs are not offered because of RMB controlled convertibility.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore fintech EMI or neobank accounts cannot satisfy the statutory requirement for a mainland onshore corporate account. PBOC licensing does not recognise overseas EMIs as deposit-taking institutions in China, and SAFE requires FDI capital to be routed exclusively through a licensed domestic bank as the receiving bank. Using an EMI as an intermediate interception account would break the capital verification chain and expose the joint venture to enforcement. The entity may engage a licensed payment institution as a sub-processor for low-value logistics payments, but the core treasury and trade settlement rails must remain with Tier-1 commercial banks.
