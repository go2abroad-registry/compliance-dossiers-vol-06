Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in semiconductors, are generally convertible and may be settled in CNY or foreign currency once genuine trade documentation is verified by the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, however, require SAFE registration, approval, or filing depending on the structure. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture formed under the Foreign Investment Law, the initial capital contribution from the Hong Kong shareholder must be injected under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting rules and deposited into a foreign currency capital account (FDI) or RMB capital account, with a SAFE capital account registration notice and payment confirmation required before the funds may be converted or used. Retained earnings and legal profits may be remitted abroad only after annual audit by a Chinese certified public accountant, corporate income tax settlement, and verification of prior capital injection compliance. Dividend remittance must be made through the bank with the Foreign Exchange Payment and Receipt Application Form and tax clearance certificates such as the Certificate of Tax Payment and, where applicable, a withholding tax certificate. Surplus funds from current account receipts, including export proceeds from sales of SSD drives and server processors, are subject to a mandatory surrender regime only for a portion of trade-related foreign exchange retained by banks; in practice, enterprises must sell or retain foreign exchange within a statutory timeframe and under a genuine trade background. For wholesale trading companies with high cross-border invoice flows, the Foreign Exchange Administration requires the trade in goods data and customs declaration numbers to match the inbound SWIFT credits to prevent fake trade or round-tripping. Overall, profit repatriation is achievable but the administrative burden is high and hinges on maintaining a complete audit trail from purchase orders to customs declarations to bank receipts and supporting notices.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Enterprises must settle or surrender export foreign exchange receipts within the statutory period normally capped at 90 days from the date of collection unless registered for a longer deferred payment period under SAFE. For current account export receipts in goods, the mandatory conversion ratio is generally zero for outright surrender, but banks must confirm that the collected foreign currency is either sold into CNY voluntarily or retained in a foreign currency settlement account within the allowed retention cap. The SAFE monitoring system requires that the export collection amount, customs declaration value, and invoice value reconcile; any discrepancy exceeding the tolerances triggers verification and possible suspension of the enterprise's trade in goods foreign exchange business.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability in mainland China. Fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, PingPong, or Currenxie can provide ancillary multi-currency wallets for trade collection, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot serve as the primary statutory bank for the Chinese joint venture's FDI capital registration, SAFE filing, export surrender monitoring, or corporate income tax connected clearing. The JV will still need a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank relationship to anchor the SAFE capital account and to satisfy the physical presence and tax registration requirements. Offshore EMIs may be used only as a secondary receivables collection channel for cross-border e-commerce and open-account trade, provided inbound funds are ultimately routed to the domestic settlement bank and the trade background documentation is available to the domestic bank for reconciliation.

Fintech electronic money institutions and offshore digital EMI providers such as Airwallex, PingPong, or Currenxie can provide ancillary multi-currency wallets for trade collection, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot serve as the primary statutory bank for the Chinese joint venture's FDI capital registration, SAFE filing, export surrender monitoring, or corporate income tax connected clearing.
