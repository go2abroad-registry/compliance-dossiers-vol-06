Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, electronic portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax); for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, Inland Revenue Department (IRD), electronic portal: eTax via GovHK.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident investor creating a PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the tax identity is procured as follows: (1) complete any required foreign investment filing or negative-list clearance under the PRC Foreign Investment Law; (2) obtain the business license from the local Administration for Market Regulation, which bears the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) used as the PRC Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN); (3) complete establishment tax registration at the competent STA office or through the e-Tax portal within 30 days of the business license date, including Enterprise Income Tax registration and VAT taxpayer registration; (4) apply for general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover will exceed RMB 5,000,000 or if voluntary general taxpayer status is needed for input VAT credit; (5) register for export tax rebate eligibility and export-qualification status in the STA export tax refund system when direct exports are planned; (6) open a foreign currency settlement account at a designated bank and complete State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) registration for export proceeds collection. For a Hong Kong entity, the tax ID is the Business Registration Number obtained by applying to the Inland Revenue Department within one month after incorporation with the Companies Registry; the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate, and no separate VAT ID is required because Hong Kong does not impose VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — The PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income and is not a pass-through entity. If a separate Hong Kong company is formed, it is also an entity-level taxpayer under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, subject to Profits Tax on assessable profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong, with offshore profits claimable outside the territorial scope where fully substantiated.

Available election forms

None — no check-the-box election, no pass-through election, and no entity classification election is available under PRC Enterprise Income Tax law or the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance. The Hong Kong beneficial owner may elect to apply the Arrangement between the Mainland of China and the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income to reduce PRC withholding on dividends, interest, and royalties; PRC and Hong Kong anti-hybrid rules may adjust hybrid mismatch outcomes, but no general elective regime exists.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5,000,000) annual taxable turnover for mandatory PRC general VAT taxpayer registration; all PRC VAT-liable taxpayers must complete VAT registration regardless of turnover. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, exported tangible goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate and refund of input VAT where: the seller is a VAT-registered PRC exporter; the goods physically leave the PRC customs territory; the export sale is made to a non-resident buyer; export proceeds are repatriated from abroad; the goods are not prohibited or restricted from export tax rebate treatment; and the transaction is reported in the STA export tax rebate declaration. Qualifying cross-border services, including offshore digital services, may be zero-rated only if supplied to non-resident customers, consumed wholly outside China, and listed in the STA zero-rating catalogue. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on domestic or export transactions, so no separate zero-rating claim or input VAT refund mechanism applies in Hong Kong.

  • Customs Export Declaration generated by China Customs through the general trade or cross-border e-commerce customs clearance system
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical export from the PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice / export invoice issued by the exporting entity
  • Export sales contract or purchase order entered into with the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • Bank foreign exchange collection receipt or export proceeds settlement voucher issued by a designated bank, evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from abroad
  • PRC export tax rebate declaration/application schedule filed through the STA e-Tax export rebate module
  • Certificate of origin, export inspection report, or export licence where applicable to the specific electronic component classifications

The PRC State Taxation Administration operates a Full Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) mandate. VAT invoices are issued through the STA national e-invoice service platform, with invoice data transmitted to the STA in near-real-time. Each invoice carries a unique invoice code and digital encryption, and the platform automatically reconciles invoice data against China Customs export declaration records and bank foreign exchange receipts. The legacy tax-control device model is being replaced by cloud-based issuance; no separate local fiscalisation stamp is required because the e-invoice platform performs central fiscal clearance. Hong Kong has no e-invoice clearance or digital stamping obligation, but businesses must retain their bookkeeping and transaction records for at least seven years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

PRC tax authorities cross-match the STA VAT invoice database, China Customs export declaration records, and bank foreign exchange collection data to substantiate every 0% export VAT claim. Missing, false, or inconsistent documentation will result in rejection of zero-rating, reversal to taxable VAT, recovery of any refunded input VAT, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and administrative penalties from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims are subject to criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, including fines and imprisonment. In Hong Kong, no VAT clawback exists, but false or misleading statements in Profits Tax returns are subject to additional assessments, penalties, and prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and there are no restrictions on current or capital account transactions under the Currency Ordinance and HKMA's currency board mechanism. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operating as a treasury and trading platform in Hong Kong, the practical convertibility constraint arises when funds are routed between the mainland Chinese and Hong Kong legs of the corporate structure. Mainland China adopts a current account and capital account bifurcated convertibility system: current account transactions (trade, services, interest and dividends) require genuine documentation and are settled directly through designated banks, while capital account transactions (direct investment, equity injection, inter-company loans) require prior approval, registration or filing with SAFE. Profit remittance from a Chinese operating subsidiary to the Hong Kong JV requires verified audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate, and completion of the SAFE distribution form, with dividends commonly paid in CNY and converted to HKD or USD at the offshore CNH market. Surrender requirements for export proceeds in mainland China have been removed; exporters are required to repatriate receipts into domestic foreign exchange accounts within a statutory period but may hold and disburse the proceeds freely. In Hong Kong, there is no mandatory surrender of foreign exchange receipts. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must segregate liquidity into a Hong Kong multi-currency ledger and a mainland RMB/CNY ledger, with periodic cross-border transfers formalized under the inter-company loan or intra-group financing framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong: No statutory export proceeds surrender or conversion requirements. Mainland China: Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic FX account within 180 days of customs declaration; mandatory conversion to RMB was abolished in 2012, so the entity may hold USD, HKD or CNH at its discretion, subject to reporting via the bank's foreign exchange monitoring system (AS2).

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for mainland Chinese cross-border capital flows

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Limited/Medium. HKMA-licensed fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, Statrys) can provide multi-currency virtual accounts for trade disbursements, but they are not recognized as qualified deposit-taking institutions by the People's Bank of China and cannot independently settle RMB cross-border payments originating from mainland Chinese banks. For a Sino-Foreign JV, the key constraints are (1) absence of full trade finance instruments such as letters of credit and guarantees, (2) inability to receive direct ODI capital injections from mainland shareholders due to SAFE’s designated-bank rule, and (3) potential higher underlying risk for documentary compliance audits. Therefore, fintech EMIs should be deployed only as a supplementary sub-ledger for technical software payments or small-value vendor settlements, never as a primary treasury clearing rail.

Inbound categories for the HK JV: (i) direct equity injection from the PRC shareholder under ODI — this requires MOFCOM/NDRC pre-approval in mainland China, followed by SAFE registration (Form FDI/ODI), and the HK entity must open a special 'Capital Escrow Account' at a designated commercial bank in Hong Kong. (ii) Inter-company loan from PRC shareholder — must be registered with SAFE under the enterprise foreign loan quota rule, and the HK bank will require a loan agreement, board resolution, and SAFE registration certificate. Capital verification at the HK entity level is performed by an independent CPA for accounting years, and the same CPA must issue a net asset certificate to satisfy bank review. For any capital injection from a third-party foreign investor outside China, no approval is needed in HK, but the bank must receive a Declaration of Source of Wealth and sworn statutory declaration to safeguard against money laundering.For operational disbursements to mainland supplier factories and to international component producers, the primary clearing rails are (1) SWIFT MT103 straight-through transfers from the HSBC/BOCHK operational accounts to the supplier’s designated bank in mainland China or elsewhere, with the trade purpose code 'D002' for general merchandise trade and a clear narrative matching the invoice numbers; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (sight or usance) issued by the HK JV’s bank in favor of suppliers where the order value exceeds USD 250,000 and the supplier requires an LC as negotiable security; (3) Documentary Collections (D/P at sight or D/A at 30-60 days) for routine repeat shipments under the existing commercial framework; and (4) cross-border interbank payment system (CIPS) using direct participant banks for RMB-denominated settlement to mainland counterparties, converting HKD/USD into RMB at a CNH rate determined by the issuing bank. To manage just-in-time inventory, the liquidity buffer will be split between the HSBC current ledger for SWIFT wires and the BOCHK CIPS account for real-time CNY settlement.Plain vanilla outright forward contracts (USD/HKD, HKD/RMB, USD/CNH) with tenors up to 12 months, executed with HSBC, BOCHK or Standard Chartered execution desks.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore CNH vs. USD for hedging the mainland RMB exposure where physical delivery of RMB is not permissible under the onshore CNH settlement scheme.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between HKD/USD and RMB/CNH to convert the income stream from the Chinese operating arm into the treasury functional currency, with no speculative implementation beyond the underlying trade exposure.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Limited/Medium. HKMA-licensed fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Neat, Statrys) can provide multi-currency virtual accounts for trade disbursements, but they are not recognized as qualified deposit-taking institutions by the People's Bank of China and cannot independently settle RMB cross-border payments originating from mainland Chinese banks. For a Sino-Foreign JV, the key constraints are (1) absence of full trade finance instruments such as letters of credit and guarantees, (2) inability to receive direct ODI capital injections from mainland shareholders due to SAFE’s designated-bank rule, and (3) potential higher underlying risk for documentary compliance audits. Therefore, fintech EMIs should be deployed only as a supplementary sub-ledger for technical software payments or small-value vendor settlements, never as a primary treasury clearing rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Controlled transactions require documentation when the aggregate value of controlled transactions for the basis period exceeds HK$4 million for transfers of goods or property, or HK$8 million for financial transactions. A Master File is necessary when the worldwide group's consolidated revenue exceeds HK$680 million and the Hong Kong entity has controlled transactions exceeding the relevant threshold.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate a formal ESR regime comparable to the UAE, but the IRD applies an economic substance doctrine through the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis in determining whether profits have a Hong Kong source and whether treaty benefits may be accessed. The JV must have committed employees, office or facilities, and management decisions in Hong Kong where trading profit is booked. A cash box with no CIGA in Hong Kong will trigger section 61A anti-avoidance, transfer pricing adjustment, denial of the Tax Residency Certificate, and denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Offshore Profits Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112) section 82A: wilful evasion or submission of false returns attracts a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, and an additional tax of up to 100% of the tax undercharged; where the undercharge is due to fraud or wilful default, additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax may be assessed.

Director personal liability: On indictment, directors who commit, aid, or abet falsity are personally exposed to imprisonment up to 3 years; the court can order restitution of the tax, freeze assets, disqualify the director under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622), and impose a departure restriction notice requiring the director to obtain permission before leaving Hong Kong.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's Length Trade

Statutory sanction: The IRD invokes section 50AAA of the Inland Revenue Ordinance to re-determine assessable profits at arm's length, may apply section 61 to disregard the sale-purchase arrangement, and for missing documentation may impose a fine of HK$10,000 under section 80(2) plus additional tax of up to 200% of the undercharged amount. The taxpayer is also exposed to penalty surcharges and best-of-judgment assessments.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign audited accounts knowingly incorporating non-arm's-length expenses can be sued by the company or its contributories; the court can order asset restitution, a 15-year disqualification order, and a travel ban. In liquidation, wrongful trading under section 776 of the Companies Ordinance may result in personal contribution to the company's assets for the tax loss.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The arrangement is voidable under section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance as a tax-avoidance arrangement whose main purpose is obtaining a tax benefit; the IRD may withdraw the Tax Residency Certificate, disallow the offshore exemption, and assess the full Hong Kong-source profits. The PRC tax authority may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and reassess at the full 10% non-treaty dividend withholding rate, plus daily late payment surcharges and an administrative fine of 50% to 100% of the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly allow the company to be used as a shell may be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax liabilities under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; civil proceedings can lead to seizure of personal assets, blacklisting by the IRD with a travel ban, disqualification as a director for up to 15 years, and criminal prosecution with significant imprisonment terms where fraud is established.
