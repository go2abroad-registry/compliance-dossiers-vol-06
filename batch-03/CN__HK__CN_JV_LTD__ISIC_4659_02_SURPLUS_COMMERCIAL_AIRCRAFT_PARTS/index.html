Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the official electronic tax filing portal, the 'Golden Tax Phase IV' system and e-Tax (电子税务局, although outputs must be in English, the portal is commonly referenced as 'E-Tax Bureau Online')

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-foreign equity joint venture in China must first complete the business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC serves as the de facto Tax Identification Number (TIN). Subsequently, within 30 days of the business license issuance, the joint venture must register tax status with the competent local office of the State Taxation Administration via the official e-Tax platform. During tax registration, the entity must submit its foreign investment approval or filing certificate, articles of association, lease or ownership proof of the registered address in China, legal representative identification, and audited financial projections. Upon completion of the tax registration procedures, the STA issues a formal 'Tax Registration Certificate' (and maintains a digital record under the USCC) that activates all tax obligations, including Value Added Tax (VAT) registration, corporate income tax (CIT) filing, withholding tax duties, and stamp duty. The same USCC enables the entity to open a corporate bank account and to obtain export-related registrations through the customs authority under the Customs Import/Export Registration Code.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Entity-Level Mandatory Registration: A newly incorporated Sino-foreign equity joint venture is required to register for VAT regardless of turnover because it is a limited liability enterprise conducting taxable sales. There is no discretionary small-scale threshold below the statutory small-scale taxpayer threshold of RMB 5 million (500 wan) annual turnover for simplified VAT accounting; however, a company with projected annual turnover exceeding RMB 5 million must register as a general VAT taxpayer. A small-scale taxpayer with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5 million may elect simplified taxation at 3% or 1% (temporary relief), but an entity engaged in cross-border export trade must register as a general taxpayer to claim export zero-rating and input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT export zero-rating is available for certified commercial aircraft parts exported outside the customs territory of the People's Republic of China, provided the goods are actually shipped out and the ownership has passed to an overseas buyer. Qualifying conditions include possession of a valid export customs declaration (出口报关单), proof of foreign exchange receipt through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) system, a signed cross-border sales contract, a commercial invoice, and transport documents such as a bill of lading or airway bill that demonstrate the goods physically leave China. The enterprise must be a general VAT taxpayer and must have obtained the relevant export registration with the customs authority. For aircraft parts that are imported into a customs special supervision zone, export-processing zone, or comprehensive bonded zone before final re-export, specific bonded-zone customs procedures apply; physical exit from the zone still substantiates zero-rating.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (出口货物报关单) bearing the customs release stamp/electronic customs clearance code
  • Bill of Lading or Master Airway Bill evidencing physical departure from a Chinese port/airport
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Certificate or bank-seated inward remittance advice evidencing receipt of export proceeds within the statutory repatriation deadline
  • Commercial invoice and signed sales contract/order
  • Packing list with HS codes corresponding to ISIC 4659 / NAICS 423860 aircraft parts
  • Electronic export tax rebate declaration (出口退税申报表) filed via the e-Tax/VAT refund module
  • Export Single Window (中国国际贸易单一窗口) submission acceptance receipt

China operates a fully digitized VAT invoice control system under the 'Golden Tax Phase IV' platform. All registered VAT taxpayers must issue electronic special VAT invoices (eVAT special fapiao) and electronic general VAT invoices through the STA-approved e-invoice service platform. The clearance process requires real-time transmission of each invoice's unique encrypted code and invoice number to the STA central system for digital stamping, authentication, and anti-fraud verification. Business transactions must be recorded via the National VAT Invoice Service Platform with mandatory implementation of the '全电发票' fully digitalized electronic invoice (digital e-invoice) model in all pilot provinces. For export transactions, the electronic export customs declaration data must be linked to the e-invoice system through the 'One-Window' trade single-window platform. Failure to use the state-mandated e-invoicing system disqualifies input VAT credit claims and may trigger excessive scrutiny due to the automated real-time data mining that detects abnormal red flags such as invoice amounts exceeding three standard deviations or mismatches between logistics, capital flow, and invoice flow.

Export zero-rating refund claims are heavily scrutinized by the STA and customs authorities. Under PRC VAT law and the Administrative Penalties Law, any exporter that fraudulently claims export tax rebates by fabricating customs declarations, using false bills of lading, or overstating export value may be subject to confiscation of the illegally obtained refund, an administrative penalty of up to five times the amount fraudulently claimed, suspension of export tax rebate rights for up to three years, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the PRC for the crime of fraudulent export tax rebate (骗取出口退税罪), which can result in imprisonment and fines. Additionally, if the foreign exchange proceeds from a zero-rated export are not repatriated into China within 180 days from the customs clearance date, the exporter must amend its VAT filing and repay the unverified refund plus interest, while a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and potential tax-related penalties may be imposed. Failure to provide concurrent documentation that reconciles the export declaration, the logistics track, and the SAFE foreign exchange record will trigger an automated audit lock and a clawback with an additional administrative fine.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a current account convertibility regime for RMB under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, with all goods trade settlements reconciled through the SAFE Trade in Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. The capital account, however, remains highly controlled: every cross-border equity injection from mainland investors into a Hong Kong-incorporated joint venture must be pre-filed as Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) with the National Development and Reform Commission (NDRC) and registered with SAFE; foreign exchange repatriation for dividends, loan repayments, or liquidation proceeds requires underlying documentation proving genuine economic substance. The Hong Kong limb, by contrast, maintains full capital and current account convertibility under the HKMA-linked foreign exchange regime, with the HKD freely convertible and pegged to the USD under the Linked Exchange Rate System. In substance, the JV enjoys free convertibility at the HK treasury level, but the mainland parent side faces strict surrender, repatriation, and purpose-code restrictions; any profit remittance back to mainland China requires a completed SAFE profit remittance registration, prior period audited accounts, tax clearance from the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong, and must be executed via RMB FDI or USD ODI windows. Chinese domestic banks also impose a matching receipts requirement for offshore funds, requiring documentary evidence of the underlying trade before conversion and cross-border transfer.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Guidelines and the 2012 reforms to current account foreign exchange settlement, export proceeds earned by mainland exporters must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the customs export declaration date and credited to a registered domestic foreign exchange account; the mandatory surrender ratio is effectively 0 percent since the 2012 reform, but the exporter must reconcile every receipt against the SAFE trade monitoring system and may only exceed the 180-day window upon filing a documented extension with the local SAFE branch. For the Hong Kong JV limb, no surrender requirement is imposed by the HKD regime; proceeds may be freely held in HKD, USD, or RMB offshore accounts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), in coordination with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR limb of the clearing structure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal constraint: Hong Kong licensed fintech EMIs operating under Money Service Operator (MSO) licences are legally allowed to service the JV for payment collection and payout traffic, but they are not authorized institutions and cannot provide escrow custody, trade finance, or full corporate treasury services. HKMA-licensed virtual banks are legal but typically cap transaction limits and require a traditional licensed bank for high-value Escrow_Aviation rails. Feasibility rating: LIMITED — recommended only as a secondary/fringe payment gateway for low-value, high-frequency disbursements; the primary treasury mandate must sit with a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank with escrow and SWIFT clearing capabilities.
