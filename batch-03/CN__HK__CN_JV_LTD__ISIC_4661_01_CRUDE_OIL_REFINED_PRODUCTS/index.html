Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up share capital is required under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private company limited by shares; there is no blocked escrow account statutory requirement. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture may therefore be formed with a nominal or contractual share capital, and the joint venture agreement may provide for an agreed capital contribution schedule to support trade licensing and bank lines.

Funding & unlock path: Civil defense and physical licensing clearance requires a Certificate of Fire Services Installations issued by the Fire Services Department for the storage depot, a structural load inspection and certificate of compliance from the Buildings Department confirming floor loading and compartmentation for fuel tanks, and an Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) workplace safety approval. The final clearance must include an emergency response plan approved by FSD, EMSD and the Labour Department, together with proof that all operators have completed dangerous goods handling and fire safety training.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital is required under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private company limited by shares; there is no blocked escrow account statutory requirement. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture may therefore be formed with a nominal or contractual share capital, and the joint venture agreement may provide for an agreed capital contribution schedule to support trade licensing and bank lines.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency and capital account regime, with no exchange controls, no surrender obligations, and no prior approval for current or capital account transactions. The HKD is linked to the USD under the HKMA Linked Exchange Rate System in the convertibility zone of 7.75 to 7.85 per USD. Corporate entities may hold, convert, and remit USD, HKD, EUR, RMB, and other currencies without restriction. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority supervises banking liquidity and balance-of-payments statistics, but it does not restrict profit remittance or foreign equity injection. However, the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture structure brings the PRC sovereign into play: any capital contribution sourced from onshore China must be routed through SAFE-registered channels, using approved purpose codes and verified against the FDI registration. Once in Hong Kong, those funds lose onshore conversion limits. Dividends from Hong Kong face no barrier; if the ultimate beneficiary is a Chinese parent, PRC tax filing and SAFE trade/current account verification apply. Thus convertibility is full in the HK jurisdiction while constrained on the mainland leg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR imposes no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain 100% of foreign currency earnings in any bank account. For mainland China legs, the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System requires export receipts to be repatriated into the exporter's domestic foreign-currency account within the contractual collection period evidenced by customs declarations (commonly 30-90 days), with no mandatory conversion; the proceeds are reported for trade verification and can be converted at the entity's discretion under current-account FX rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for the PRC-side capital flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China retains licensing and verification authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-medium viability as a primary clearing rail. Hong Kong regulates fintech EMIs under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or virtual banks under the Banking Ordinance; they are not absolutely legally barred from servicing a high-risk fuel wholesale JV, but most cannot issue or confirm documentary letters of credit, collect time drafts under documentary collections, or provide large USD/HKD MT103 commodity settlement capacity. HKMA-induced enhanced due diligence for ISIC 4661 crude oil and refined products will trigger velocity caps and source-of-funds scrutiny, and many digital EMIs will decline the relationship. EMIs are acceptable only for low-value ancillary payroll or expense clearing; the treasury mandate must remain with a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (PRC Corporate Income Tax Law Article 4); Hong Kong profits tax of 16.5% remains relevant to the Hong Kong co-venturer/investor only on profits not economically sourced to the Chinese mainland.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% under PRC CIT Law Articles 3, 27 and 37 for dividends paid by a Chinese company to a non-resident shareholder; reduced to 5% under Article 10(2)(a) of the Mainland and Hong Kong Tax Arrangement where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the dividend-paying Chinese JV and satisfying the applicable substance and beneficial ownership conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB. China's contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 400 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 100 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is additionally required when the Chinese enterprise or the cross-border group has aggregate annual related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion or otherwise meets the thresholds in STA Public Notice 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: CIGA and Economic Substance Regulations are decisive. For this crude oil/refined products wholesale JV, the Core Income-Generating Activities are counterparty identification, commodity negotiation, price and volume execution, physical delivery control, inventory and freight risk management, financing, and final sales decisions. A Hong Kong entity in the same structure must demonstrate its own CIGA through employed traders, contracts, risk policies, bank accounts, and board meetings conducted in Hong Kong. In China, the General Anti-Abuse Rule in CIT Law Article 47, combined with the beneficial ownership rules in STA Public Notice 2018 No. 9, allows recharacterization of the entire structure where the HK entity is a paper mailbox inserted mainly to obtain the 5% dividend DTA rate or to shift commodity trading profit offshore.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion, False Bookkeeping and False VAT/Fapiao Conduct

Statutory sanction: PRC Corporate Income Tax Law Article 63: recover the underpaid tax plus a 50%-500% penalty; TCAL Article 32: daily late-surcharge of 0.05%; Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion, with imprisonment of up to 7 years plus fines for serious cases; Criminal Law Article 205: issuance and use of fraudulent VAT invoices, with the most serious cases punishable by up to life imprisonment and property confiscation.

Director personal liability: Under TCAL Article 44, the legal representative or actual responsible director of the JV may be prohibited from leaving China until all outstanding tax has been settled. If the corporate veil is abused to evade tax or debts, shareholders and directors can be jointly and severally liable under the PRC Company Law (2023) provisions on abuse of independent legal personality, with civil recovery against personal assets. Directors who personally organize false invoices or conceal income are prosecuted as directly responsible persons in a unit crime and face imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion and up to life imprisonment for serious false-invoice offences, together with fines and possible disqualification from managing any enterprise.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Refusal

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 allows the tax authority to adjust taxable income by applying the arm's length principle; CIT Law Article 44 and its implementing rules permit deemed assessment where documentation is absent; TCAL Article 62 imposes filing penalties of RMB 2,000-10,000, while the special tax adjustment adds interest on the underpaid tax calculated at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points; if concealment amounts to tax evasion, TCAL Article 63 adds a 50%-500% penalty, and criminal liability may attach under Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: A director who approves sham pricing or procures fabricated transfer pricing documentation cannot hide behind the limited liability company form. The tax authority may pierce the corporate veil when profits are diverted to related shareholders or directors, making them jointly liable to restore nearly all diverted assets plus interest and penalties. Repeated refusal to pay tax adjustments leads to court enforcement, inclusion on the dishonest judgment debtor blacklist, restrictions on high-consumption activities, property seizure, and travel/exit bans. In egregious cases involving intentional false transfer pricing documentation, directors can be criminally liable as co-conspirators or directly responsible personnel and sentenced to up to 7 years in prison.
Shell Company Treaty Abuse, Economic Substance Failure and AML/Sanctions Breach

Statutory sanction: China will deny the 5% treaty dividend rate under the beneficial ownership rules of STA Public Notice 2018 No. 9 and the PRC General Anti-Abuse Rule in CIT Law Article 47, requiring the full 10% domestic WHT plus late-payment surcharge and penalties; the corporate structure may be recharacterized and the Chinese JV treated as directly controlled by the ultimate shareholders. Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, a business failing to establish internal AML controls and customer due diligence can face fines of up to RMB 10 million, suspension of key personnel, revocation of business licenses, and civil forfeiture of proceeds. In Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Regime for relevant activities can impose fixed penalties of HKD 50,000 plus daily default penalties and refuses the Certificate of Hong Kong Resident Status needed for treaty relief.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false Hong Kong residency or beneficial ownership declarations may be personally liable to compensate the Chinese JV for the extra WHT recovered from the group, and their assets can be traced and attached in a veil-piercing action. The PRC Tax Authority may notify immigration authorities to ban them from exiting mainland China until the tax is paid, and the Chinese courts may add them to the dishonest judgment debtor list where they fail to satisfy tax judgments. If the flow of funds involves laundering proceeds connected with sanctioned oil trading or unexplained wealth, directors face criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 191 for money laundering with imprisonment of up to 10 years, plus fines and permanent disqualification from corporate management in both China and Hong Kong.
