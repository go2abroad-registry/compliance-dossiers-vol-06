Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal and the National Electronic Invoice Platform; for Hong Kong SAR tax identity matters, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) operates separately through the eTAX portal and issues Business Registration Certificates under the Business Registration Ordinance.

E-government filing portal

For the CN_JV_LTD as a PRC-registered Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the entity is incorporated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days of license issuance, the JV must complete tax registration at its competent STA tax service hall or through the e-Tax portal by submitting the Business License, the joint venture approval/certification instrument, articles of association, legal representative identity proof, registered address proof, and banking details. The STA activates the taxpayer record, confirms the 18-digit USCC TIN, assigns the enterprise income tax and VAT registration profiles, and determines whether the entity is a general VAT taxpayer or small-scale VAT taxpayer. For a foreign non-resident enterprise, including a Hong Kong parent entity, that has no PRC establishment but receives PRC-source income or supplies taxable services into China, the non-resident obtains a non-resident tax record through the e-Tax portal by filing with the withholding agent's competent tax office; the system issues a non-resident taxpayer identifier used for VAT withholding and withholding income tax returns. In Hong Kong, a separate TIN is not used: the IRD Business Registration Certificate number is the taxpayer identification for profits tax, and any foreign entity with a Hong Kong place of business or Hong Kong-source profits must register under the Business Registration Ordinance through the IRD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The JV is a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxable at entity level on worldwide income at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through or transparent status is available. For Hong Kong tax purposes, any separate Hong Kong branch or entity would be assessed under the territorial source principle of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong would be subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax.

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid mismatch elections for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. The only relevant administrative elections are: voluntary registration as a PRC general VAT taxpayer when below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, and the small-scale taxpayer simplified VAT method where permitted by the VAT Law and implementing rules. Hong Kong also does not provide corporate check-the-box or pass-through elections; a corporation is automatically taxable under the territorial Profits Tax regime.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual assessable taxable sales is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold for goods like wholesale bitumen; all PRC-registered enterprises must nonetheless complete tax registration and obtain a TIN regardless of turnover. Taxpayers below the threshold are treated as small-scale VAT taxpayers and may elect, under STA conditions, to register as general VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT, exported goods qualify for the 0% export rate and corresponding input VAT refund where: the goods are physically exported from the customs territory of China; the export customs declaration is made in the name of the JV; the buyer is a non-resident enterprise; title and risk in the containerized bitumen pass abroad; the export proceeds are received and repatriated into China within the statutory foreign-exchange collection deadline; the goods are not prohibited or restricted for export; and the JV retains full documentary proof of sale, customs clearance, transport, delivery, and payment. For a wholesale/trading company, the applicable export tax rebate method is generally the exemption-and-refund method, meaning output VAT on the export sale is exempted and input VAT on the purchased bitumen and related export logistics may be refunded through the STA export tax rebate system. This substantiation requirement does not apply to sales that occur wholly within Hong Kong because they do not involve a PRC customs export declaration.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Declaration for Export Goods) with export customs declaration number, release date, commodity code, quantity, and value
  • Export sales contract in the name of the JV with the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice issued in the JV's name for the exported bitumen
  • Packing list and container loading records
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) showing shipped-on-board status and consignee outside China
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing repatriation and settlement of export proceeds from the overseas buyer; this is the Chinese statutory equivalent to an inward remittance certificate and is recorded through the SAFE goods-trade foreign-exchange monitoring system
  • SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Revenue and Payment Monitoring System export collection declaration/report
  • Export Tax Refund declaration and e-Tax electronic refund application output
  • Input VAT special invoices or Full Digital e-Invoices for purchased bitumen, freight, and export logistics services

China operates the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (Full Digital e-Invoice) platform. VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices for domestic sales must be issued through the STA electronic invoice platform, with real-time data transmission to the Golden Tax Phase IV system and an STA digital signature stamp for anti-fraud clearance. Paper VAT special invoices, where still permitted, are issued through authorized tax-control equipment such as the tax U-key. Export sales must be recorded through the same e-invoice/export-sales system and aligned with the China International Trade Single Window customs declaration data. Hong Kong does not operate an electronic fiscal clearance/invoice-stamping regime; invoices remain commercial documents subject to records-keeping requirements for Profits Tax.

Export zero-rating is subject to cross-matching among the customs export declaration, transport documents, invoices, bank foreign-exchange receipts, and the export tax refund application. If documentation is false, incomplete, or the foreign-exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, the STA will disallow the zero rating, deem the export to be a domestic taxable sale at 13% VAT, claw back any refund already paid, assess unpaid VAT and a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the overdue tax, and may impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded for tax evasion or up to 100% of the refund fraudulently obtained under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export-refund schemes also attract criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties ranging from fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for extremely large amounts, plus downgrade of taxpayer credit rating and suspension from export tax rebate facilitation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The operating jurisdiction is Hong Kong SAR, where the HKD is fully convertible and freely circulating under the Linked Exchange Rate System to the USD. Hong Kong maintains no exchange controls, no central surrender requirements, and no restrictions on capital account flows for corporate entities. The HKD money market is supported by the HKMA’s Exchange Fund and the interbank clearing via the Hong Kong Interbank Clearing Limited (HKICL) system. However, the PRC shareholder side is subject to the PBOC/SAFE regulatory perimeter. The Chinese side allows full current-account convertibility for genuine trade in bitumen, including payments for imported feedstock and freight, provided the underlying import/export documents are submitted to the supporting bank using the proper SAFE trade-payment codes. Capital account transactions from the PRC shareholder into the Hong Kong JV require ODI approvals (NDRC, MOFCOM, SAFE), an ODI certificate, a SAFE FX registration form, and a bank-level authenticity check before remittance. The capital injection must be reported under the cross-border RMB payment system (RCPMIS/CIPS) if settled in RMB. Profit remittance back to China is unrestricted in Hong Kong but the receiving PRC bank will require an audited HK entity financial statement, tax clearance, board resolution, and a valid SAFE dividend remittance declaration. Surrender of export proceeds is not applicable in Hong Kong; for China-side export receipts, SAFE requires repatriation into China (generally within 180 days) with an option to retain foreign exchange or convert to CNY, and no mandatory conversion ratio applies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR. For the mainland PRC shareholder/affiliate, SAFE regulations require export proceeds in foreign currency to be repatriated to China within the statutory period (generally 180 days from export declaration or collection, as specified in the relevant circular), credited to a current-account foreign exchange settlement account, and may be retained in FX or converted into CNY at the enterprise’s discretion; there is no mandatory surrender percentage.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking and banking-regulatory authority for the Hong Kong SAR; for all CNY cross-border flows involving the PRC shareholder, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) impose the controlling exchange-control framework.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Medium — legally permissible for licensed SVF/MSO entities in Hong Kong, but only as an operational overlay for small current-account payments and FX execution. Digital EMI accounts cannot replace a Tier-1 bank for capital escrow, documentary LC issuance, trade finance, or PRC regulatory reporting (ODI/FDI/RCPMIS/SAFE). The HKMA does not license full-service EMIs for credit underwriting, and most fintech platforms cannot onboard the complex PRC outbound investment structure. Therefore, they should be used only after the primary Tier-1 banking relationship is established and only for non-capital, non-trade-finance disbursements below USD 500,000 per transaction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China: The Chinese JV is required to prepare and maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) if its related-party transactions in a fiscal year exceed: (i) RMB 40 million (approximately USD 5.5 million) for related-party tangible asset transfers (goods such as bitumen); (ii) RMB 20 million (approximately USD 2.7 million) for related-party intangible asset transfers or service transactions; (iii) RMB 20 million for related-party financial asset transfers. Additionally, a Master File is mandatory if the aggregate amount of related-party transactions of the entire group exceeds RMB 6 billion (approximately USD 82 million) or if the group has cross-border revenues exceeding RMB 10 billion (approximately USD 137 million) and meets other thresholds. Hong Kong: Under the Hong Kong TP documentation rules (effective from April 2020 for periods beginning on or after 1 April 2020), the Local File is required for a Hong Kong entity with related-party transactions exceeding HKD 14 million (tangible property) or HKD 4 million (intangible assets or financial transactions) per category, or HKD 6 million for services; the Master File is required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds HKD 680 million and the entity meets the local file threshold. The JV will need to consider both jurisdictions' thresholds based on its actual transaction values.

Economic substance scope: China: The Chinese JV must have real economic substance in China; it is the mainland manufacturing/procurement hub. The Hong Kong parent must not be treated as a shell under China's treaty abuse provisions (GAAR in Notice 16/2014 and Article 26 of the domestic CIT law). For Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Requirements are not codified like the Cayman/BVI ESR, but the IRD applies the 'economic substance' concept when determining treaty residence and beneficial ownership. Under the China-Hong Kong DTA, a Hong Kong company is considered a resident if it is liable to tax in Hong Kong by reason of place of management; a company that is merely incorporated in Hong Kong but without actual business management in Hong Kong may not be a resident. For intercompany services (e.g., logistics coordination, procurement, quality control), the Hong Kong entity must perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) itself—employing staff with the requisite skills, making strategic decisions, and bearing business risks—to justify the allocation of profits. If the Hong Kong entity is oversimplified (no office, no employees, no board meeting records in HK, no substantive decision-making), the Chinese tax authority could apply a substance-over-form recharacterization and attribute the income to a Chinese permanent establishment, resulting in Chinese CIT at 25% plus WHT, and Hong Kong penalties for non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fraudulent Corporate Veil

Statutory sanction: China: Under the Law of the PRC on Administration of Tax Collection (2001), Article 63 makes tax evasion a criminal offence if the taxpayer fails to report or underreports income, or refuses to pay tax; the penalty is a 50%-500% administrative fine on the underpaid tax, plus criminal liability under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law (imprisonment up to 7 years and a fine equal to 50%-100% of the evaded tax). Hong Kong: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Section 82A, a taxpayer who intentionally makes an incorrect return may be liable to a penalty equal to 3 times the tax undercharged, plus a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) imposes civil disqualification orders for directors of up to 15 years for conduct that makes them unfit to be involved in the management of a company.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced if the entity is found to be a sham or facade; directors and shadow directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid Chinese tax plus penalties. The Chinese court may extend the obligations to the Hong Kong parent's assets under Chinese Civil Law (Article 20 of the Company Law – denial of corporate personality). In Hong Kong, a court may issue a 'Mareva injunction' freezing the directors' personal assets, and if the director is found to have knowingly furnished false tax returns, imprisonment is mandatory. Additionally, the director may be blacklisted and banned from travel to mainland China, and an Interpol travel alert (Red Notice) can be issued on request of the Chinese tax authorities for criminal tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 (approximately USD 7,000).
Transfer Pricing Misstatement and False Documentation

Statutory sanction: China: Pursuant to the PRC CIT Law Article 41 and the State Taxation Administration Announcement 2017/6 (Special Tax Adjustments), the tax authority can make a comprehensive transfer pricing adjustment. The underpaid CIT is subject to interest at the benchmark lending rate plus 0-5% penalty. If the documentation is not provided within the statutory deadline, a 5% surcharge on the underpaid tax applies (Article 61 of the Tax Collection Law), and intentionally filing false documentation constitutes tax evasion under Article 63. Hong Kong: Under IRO Section 61A (anti-avoidance), the IRD can disregard the transaction and adjust the assessable profits. Section 51C requires taxpayers to keep sufficient records; failure attracts a penalty of up to HK$100,000 and potential imprisonment of up to 6 months. If fraud is proven, the penalty is tripled.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the accuracy of the tax returns signed; if they knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation, they commit an offence of 'aiding and abetting tax fraud' (China Criminal Law Article 210, HK IRO s.82A). The Chinese tax authority can freeze the director's personal bank accounts (under Article 38 of the Tax Collection Law), apply for a court order for asset compensation, and if the director tries to leave China without paying the tax, border control measures are imposed (Article 44). In Hong Kong, the director's personal assets may be used to satisfy the penalty under the Companies Ordinance and the Director Disqualification Act; the court can disqualify the director from managing any company for up to 15 years and impose imprisonment for up to 14 years if the false statement leads to the underpayment of HK$1 million or more.
Anti-Money Laundering (AML) Breaches Relating to Undeclared Offshore Public Deposits and Shell Banking

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615, AMLO), Financial Institutions and DNFBPs must report suspicious transactions under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455); the failure to report is an offence with a maximum fine of HK$500,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years. Also under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, making false or misleading statements in any document to the IRD is punishable with a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, in addition to tripled tax. China: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law of the PRC, the freezing of foreign bank accounts and failure to declare overseas income are criminal fraud. If the offshore entity is judged to be a sham, its bank accounts are treated as bank accounts of the Chinese JV, and the directors are exposed to criminal liability for illegal business operations (CRCL Article 225) and tax concealment (Article 201) with maximum imprisonment up to 10 years.

Director personal liability: Directors will be held personally liable for AML violations if they fail to conduct adequate due diligence on the foreign inclusive or if they knowingly use shell entities to circumvent tax. Under the Companies Ordinance, the director's duty to exercise reasonable care, skill, and diligence (s.465) is breached, giving rise to civil liability to the company for losses. The court may issue a confiscation order under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, enabling seizure of the director's personal property up to the value of the offence (including hidden assets). Travel bans are routinely imposed by China for directors with outstanding tax debts over RMB 10,000; Hong Kong immigration may also be notified. Criminal imprisonment terms: China – up to 10 years for serious tax fraud; Hong Kong – up to 14 years for a conviction on indictment for fraudulent evasion of tax (IRO s.82A) and 7 years for AML non-disclosure; Singapore (if accounts there) – up to 10 years for money laundering under the Corruption, Drug Trafficking and Other Serious Crimes Act.
