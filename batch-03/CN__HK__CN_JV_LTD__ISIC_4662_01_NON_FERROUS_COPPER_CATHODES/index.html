Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The copper cathode wholesaler may claim 0% VAT export zero-rating only as a registered general VAT taxpayer that physically exports goods out of mainland China to Hong Kong SAR or another overseas destination. The supply must be a genuine cross-border sale, the copper cathodes must leave PRC customs territory, the export customs declaration must be filed by the exporting entity, foreign exchange proceeds must be repatriated, and the export VAT refund application must be submitted within the statutory rebate application period from the month following export up to 30 April of the following year.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods) as released by the General Administration of Customs of China and matched via the International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical exportation of the copper cathodes from a PRC port to Hong Kong SAR or an overseas destination
  • Commercial export invoice and the underlying sales contract with the overseas buyer
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt or trade settlement voucher including the Chinese statutory equivalent of an inward remittance certificate showing repatriation of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Original special VAT invoice and export invoice recorded in the STA digital e-invoice system
  • Export VAT refund declaration data and electronic export goods declaration verification information

The STA Golden Tax IV fully digitalised e-invoice environment applies. VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be issued through the national Electronic Invoice Service platform on the e-Tax system and cleared or uploaded in real time; each invoice is secured with an STA anti-counterfeit digital tax-control code. Export clearance and export VAT refund records are matched through the China International Trade Single Window, and buyers may recognise input VAT only after STA electronic invoice verification.

The competent tax authority cross-matches export customs declarations, logistics manifests, VAT invoices, sales contracts, and bank foreign exchange receipts. Any discrepancy or unsubstantiated zero-rating triggers provisional suspension of the refund, retrospective clawback of refunded VAT with penalty interest, late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day on clawed-back amounts, administrative fines from 100% to 500% of the fraudulently obtained refund under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law, and a bar on future export tax refund processing. Falsified documentation may also be referred to customs and foreign exchange authorities for additional penalties, and criminal liability may arise for fraudulent export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates an open and fully convertible foreign exchange regime; there is no capital or current account control distinction for Hong Kong-incorporated treasury vehicles. The HKD is freely market-convertible and the HKMA maintains the Linked Exchange Rate System within the 7.75 to 7.85 HKD/USD zone. Export proceeds can be retained in USD, HKD, RMB or other trade currencies and no surrender requirement applies to the Hong Kong JV. The real convertibility friction arises where the mainland Chinese shareholder remits capital into the Hong Kong vehicle or where the JV repatriates profits or trade proceeds back to China. On the mainland side, current account trade-related FX payments are convertible against genuine invoices and customs declarations under SAFE verification; capital account inflows and outflows, such as registered equity injections, require FDI/ODI registration and approval. Profit remittance from a mainland operating entity to its Hong Kong shareholder would require audited financial statements, tax withholding at the applicable treaty rate, and a SAFE dividend remittance record; there is no similar withholding hurdle for dividends paid by the Hong Kong JV to its overseas shareholders. Overall convertibility is unrestricted within Hong Kong, but SAFE controls on PRC-origin flows create documentary and timeline costs rather than absolute convertibility barriers.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong: no statutory export surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio; the Hong Kong JV may retain export receipts indefinitely in any convertible currency. 0% mandatory conversion ratio applies inside Hong Kong. Where an onshore PRC parent exports copper cathodes to or invoices through the Hong Kong JV, SAFE export verification rules require the mainland exporter to repatriate export proceeds into a domestic designated foreign exchange bank and to submit the collection data through the export collection verification system, generally within 90 days from customs declaration unless an approved longer contracted credit period is filed. There is no fixed compulsory conversion percentage on the mainland side, but if the exporter elects RMB settlement, the conversion must be processed through the designated bank at the market rate and the full receipt amount must be reported to SAFE.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the Chinese mainland authorities that regulate cross-border RMB and foreign-exchange flows affecting the PRC shareholder side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary treasury and liquidity clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI providers are not authorised institutions under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and are legally barred from accepting corporate deposits, issuing documentary letters of credit, providing LME warrant-clearing connectivity, or acting as the principal bank for a medium-risk enhanced metals trading JV. Unless the EMI holds an HKMA Stored Value Facility licence or a Customs and Excise Department Money Service Operator licence strictly for small-value remittance, it cannot lawfully service the full cross-border treasury structure of this entity. Such platforms may be used only as an ancillary collection and expense API after the main account is opened at a Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Mainland Chinese shareholder capital injections into the Hong Kong JV must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 through a designated authorised bank in Hong Kong using the RMB, USD or HKD clearing rails. The SWIFT instruction must carry the SAFE Balance of Payments transaction purpose category for direct investment equity injection and the SAFE ODI filing reference number, with MOFCOM/NDRC approvals attached when required. The Hong Kong JV should maintain a segregated Capital Escrow Account so equity proceeds are not commingled with operating cash. Upon receipt, a licensed Hong Kong CPA should issue a capital verification report confirming paid-up capital. If the inward remittance is instead structured as shareholder debt, the JV must provide a signed loan facility agreement, interest-rate confirmation, board approval and intent-to-register documentation in the intercompany loan ledger. All FX purchase or settlement records must be retained for SAFE and HKMA audit trail purposes.Trade disbursements for refined copper cathode purchases should use SWIFT MT103 for fast open-account payments to LME-registered refiners, brokers, freight providers, inspectors and tax authorities. For high-value physical imports and large contract settlements, the JV should issue an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit at sight or deferred tenor with required documents including signed commercial invoice, full set of ocean bills of lading, packing list, independent weight and assay certificate, insurance certificate, and LME warrant transfer instruction on the LMEsword digital warrant platform. For approved repeat and lower-value counterparties, Documentary Collections under D/P or D/A terms with third-party inspection and title retention clauses are acceptable. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: LOW as the primary treasury and liquidity clearing rail. Offshore digital EMI providers are not authorised institutions under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and are legally barred from accepting corporate deposits, issuing documentary letters of credit, providing LME warrant-clearing connectivity, or acting as the principal bank for a medium-risk enhanced metals trading JV. Unless the EMI holds an HKMA Stored Value Facility licence or a Customs and Excise Department Money Service Operator licence strictly for small-value remittance, it cannot lawfully service the full cross-border treasury structure of this entity. Such platforms may be used only as an ancillary collection and expense API after the main account is opened at a Tier-1 commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 80,000,000 (aggregate of intercompany transaction turnover for covered transactions with associated persons) triggering mandatory Local File preparation; Master File required when total consolidated group revenue is at least HKD 6,000,000,000 and covered transactions exceed HKD 500,000,000, or the enterprise is in the largest taxpayer class.

Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong does not maintain a statutory Economic Substance Regime (ESR) equivalent to the UAE or BVI, the IRD applies the transfer pricing doctrine in Sections 15B-15C and DIPN 58 to require entities to demonstrate 'mind and management' functions in Hong Kong; for a Sino-Foreign JV trading metals, substance documentation must evidence core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as contract negotiation, LME warrant transfer authorization, logistics control, and risk management in Hong Kong. A Hong Kong-trading profit exemption will be denied where key activities occur in Mainland China without a permanent establishment; likewise the China side of a Sino-Foreign JV should assess Chinese permanent establishment risk under the PRC-HK arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through fictitious offshore trading claim (false declaration)

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap. 112 Section 82: willfully making a false return or statement with intent to evade tax is liable to a fine of HKD 50,000 plus treble tax under S.82A(3); on summary conviction imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment imprisonment up to 3 years; plus treble tax assessment for 6 years of defaults.

Director personal liability: The court may ignore the corporate veil (Companies Ordinance Section 339) where fraud or sham trading is established: directors are jointly liable for the JV's tax liability, treble tax, and costs; the IRD may issue a departure prohibition preventing travel under S.76 and may order the register of charges against personal residences; criminal conviction can lead to director disqualification and up to 3 years imprisonment.
Compliance failure: no transfer pricing documentation and unlawful base erosion via high-intra-group payments

Statutory sanction: IRO S.50A disallowance of non-arm's length payments; S.15B-15C penalties for failure to prepare documentation: HKD 50,000 plus HKD 500 per day of continued non-compliance; additional assessment up to 50% of the disallowed amount or 100% in case of deliberate negligence under DIPN 58; penalties under Section 82B.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed annual returns attesting to arm's length pricing may be personally assessed for 'fraudulent trading' under Companies Ordinance S.373; ring-fenced assessments may pierce to the parent if the JV is a sham and to directors for knowing assistance and for receiving distributions paid out of inflated tax benefits; travel blacklisting under S.76 IRO prevents leaving Hong Kong without paying all taxes; in the PRC context, the SAT may pursue individuals as Responsible Party under PRC tax law.
AML / Shell company structuring (lack of economic substance and money laundering predicate)

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance Cap. 615 S.5 imposes CDD duties; money laundering can be prosecuted under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance S.25: 14 years imprisonment and fine HKD 5,000,000; regulatory sanctions by the HKMA: public censure, up to HKD 10 million fine, license suspension, plus civil penalties under AMLO.

Director personal liability: The court may lift the corporate veil where the company is used as a façade; directors may be blacklisted such that bank account mandates are frozen; under Cap. 615 S.93, officers who consent or connive in an offense commit the offense personally and are subject to imprisonment for up to 7 years; travel bans and asset freezing orders under Cap. 615 Part 12 may be imposed; Chinese authorities may impose individual international sanctions under the PRC AML regime for serving as the designated representative of a shell entity.
