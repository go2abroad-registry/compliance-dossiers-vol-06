VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC VAT taxpayers must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance. Compulsory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; taxpayers with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 are classified as small-scale taxpayers. A foreign or Hong Kong non-resident seller that supplies goods or taxable services into the PRC may be subject to PRC VAT withholding and must register through the payer/withholding agent if it has no permanent establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT law and implementing rules, exported battery-grade lithium carbonate powder qualifies for the 0% VAT export rate when the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically declared to and released by PRC Customs for export from a PRC port, the sale is made to a non-resident buyer, payment is received through banking channels such as SWIFT documentary credit or wire transfer and repatriated to China, and the exporter holds the customs declaration, transport documents, and foreign-exchange receipt evidence. The corresponding input VAT may be refunded through the export tax refund system. Hong Kong has no VAT, so no separate Hong Kong zero-rating claim is required for the Hong Kong transshipment or procurement hub; only profits-tax documentation and customs trade declarations apply.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC evidencing the outbound shipment and commodity classification of battery-grade lithium carbonate powder
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing physical export from a PRC port to the declared overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt/settlement slip evidencing that the export consideration has been repatriated to China through a licensed bank
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract or purchase order matching the customs declaration value, quantity, and buyer identity
  • Customs release/verification notice showing customs clearance and export permission for the underlying consignment
  • VAT special invoice inventory and export tax refund declaration records reconciling the zero-rated export sales to the tax authority

The PRC requires mandatory electronic invoice issuance through the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice platform (Golden Tax Phase IV environment). VAT general taxpayers must issue all Special VAT Invoices and ordinary invoices through the tax-control/information system, upload invoice data to the STA in real time, and obtain automatic tax-authority clearance of the invoice serial numbers before legal issuance. The mandate includes the unified digital invoice file format, real-time API reporting, anti-fraud digital stamping/tax-control encryption, and strict pre-issuance authorization for Special VAT Invoices. Manual or paper-only invoicing without platform clearance is not permitted for general VAT taxpayers.

If the zero-rating claim is not fully substantiated by the customs declaration, logistics documents, and foreign-exchange repatriation evidence, the tax authority may reclassify the export as a domestic taxable sale, impose standard 13% VAT on the sales amount, deny the export VAT refund, and claw back any input VAT already refunded together with daily late-payment surcharge. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the PRC, fraudulent export-tax-refund or false self-declaration cases may carry penalties up to five times the underpaid tax and suspension of export-tax-refund qualification; the customs authority may separately impose penalties under the Customs Law for false export declarations. Serious cases are subject to criminal liability for tax fraud, export-tax-refund fraud, or foreign-exchange fraud under the Criminal Law of the PRC. The Hong Kong IRD may also review the profits tax position of a Hong Kong entity if the transaction structure lacks economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the HKD pegged to the USD in a band of 7.75 to 7.85. The HKD, USD, and offshore RMB (CNH) are freely convertible in Hong Kong, and there are no surrender requirements, no prior FX licensing, and no restrictions on profit remittance or retention of foreign-currency earnings. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are open for corporate treasury purposes, subject only to statutory anti-money laundering and sanctions screening by licensed banks. However, because the legal ownership chain includes Mainland China, the PRC exchange-control regime is relevant: in China, current-account items are generally convertible after banks verify the underlying transaction, while capital-account items such as outbound direct investment require SAFE registration or approval. A Mainland shareholder's equity injection into the Hong Kong JV must be supported by SAFE overseas direct investment registration and a compliant cross-border capital-account remittance; dividends and profit distributions returning to Mainland China from Hong Kong must be routed through an onshore bank with appropriate tax residence proof and withholding compliance. The overall assessment is therefore full convertibility at the Hong Kong treasury level, with a controlled and regulated capital-account interface at the Mainland China level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

In Hong Kong, there is no statutory export-proceeds surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio. A licensed corporation may retain export receipts indefinitely in USD, HKD, RMB, or any other currency. For any Mainland China onshore affiliate, SAFE's goods trade foreign exchange administration regime requires export proceeds to be repatriated into a domestic foreign-currency account and matched against customs declaration records in the bank's trade monitoring system; a payment that remains unreconciled for more than 90 days after the declared export date must be reported to the bank and is subject to enhanced documentary review. Even onshore, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may keep the proceeds as a foreign-currency balance or convert them to RMB at the market rate.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), the de facto central bank and currency board authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; for Mainland China cross-border flows, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the competent authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the core treasury function. Hong Kong does not issue the UK-style electronic money institution licence. Offshore fintech EMI providers are not legally permitted to hold corporate client funds on a deposit-taking basis, cannot underwrite commercial letters of credit, and are not recognised as primary clearing rails for import settlement in Hong Kong unless they hold a full banking licence or a narrowly licenced Money Service Operator / Stored Value Facility licence. Virtual banks with a full Hong Kong banking licence can support routine operational payments, but for the capital escrow, L/C trade finance, and FX hedging required by a battery-metals arbitrage vehicle, a Tier-1 commercial bank with HKICL, SWIFT, and trade services infrastructure is mandatory.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection into the Hong Kong JV should be structured as paid-up share capital or a shareholder subordinated loan and routed via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 to the capital escrow account at a Tier-1 licensed bank. For a Mainland China shareholder, the remittance must be preceded by SAFE overseas direct investment registration at the Mainland bank and cleared through the onshore bank's capital-account system before delivery outside China. The Hong Kong receiving bank will require the director's resolution, Form NSC1 or equivalent share allotment filing, written confirmation of purpose, and, where the bank's compliance committee so requires, a capital verification statement from a Hong Kong CPA or the JV's auditor. FX conversion from USD to HKD or CNH is executed at the receiving bank's spot rate; payment instructions must state the full remitter and beneficiary names, account numbers, and the purpose code 'Capital Contribution - Paid-up Share Capital' or 'Shareholder Subordinated Loan'. For smaller, recurring operational payments, SWIFT MT103 remittances with purpose code 'Trading - Import/Export Settlement' are acceptable. Documentary collections D/P and D/A represent a lower-cost alternative for proven counterparties. Onshore Hong Kong payments in HKD and RMB should be cleared through the HKICL CHATS system; USD payments may be cleared in the United States via CHIPS/Fedwire or through US dollar CHATS in Hong Kong. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the core treasury function. Hong Kong does not issue the UK-style electronic money institution licence. Offshore fintech EMI providers are not legally permitted to hold corporate client funds on a deposit-taking basis, cannot underwrite commercial letters of credit, and are not recognised as primary clearing rails for import settlement in Hong Kong unless they hold a full banking licence or a narrowly licenced Money Service Operator / Stored Value Facility licence. Virtual banks with a full Hong Kong banking licence can support routine operational payments, but for the capital escrow, L/C trade finance, and FX hedging required by a battery-metals arbitrage vehicle, a Tier-1 commercial bank with HKICL, SWIFT, and trade services infrastructure is mandatory.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 200 million for annual related-party tangible asset transactions; CNY 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transactions; CNY 40 million for related-party service transactions; Master File is mandatory when total related-party transactions exceed CNY 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese JV must satisfy the substance-over-form principle by maintaining real procurement, logistics, warehousing, order processing, bank account control, and invoicing functions in mainland China. A pure shelf enterprise with no operational substance will be recharacterised under the China GAAR (CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Articles 120-123). The Hong Kong parent claiming treaty relief must also demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - namely negotiation, decision-making, risk assumption, and execution of the lithium carbonate offtake agreements - actually take place in Hong Kong. Lack of such substance will result in denial of treaty benefits and reversion to the 10% statutory WHT, with associated penalties and interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profiteering / Related-Party Under-Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Special Tax Adjustment under TCAL Articles 36 and 54, and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (Guoshuifa [2009] 2), allowing 5-year retroactive recharacterization, assessed additional tax, daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge, and in repeated cases a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under TCAL Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's length pricing will face civil liability for the company's unpaid tax, penalties, and surcharges under TCAL Article 49 and PRC Company Law Article 20. The corporate veil is pierced, and directors may be jointly and severally liable. In egregious cases, the responsible individuals risk personal travel bans (blacklisting and exit restriction) under TCAL Article 44 and Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years for tax fraud.
Fraudulent Tax Evasion (False Invoicing and Customs Value Manipulation)

Statutory sanction: VAT invoice fraud is punishable under TCAL Article 63 and Criminal Law Article 205. Issuance of false special VAT invoices carries sentences from 3 years to life imprisonment depending on the amount involved. Export fraud under Criminal Law Article 204 carries up to life imprisonment and confiscation of assets. Financial penalties range from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'directly responsible persons' and will be personally indicted. They face imprisonment, unlimited fines, asset seizure, and post-conviction entry restrictions for foreign executives. The corporate facade is disregarded, and shareholders may have their liability extended to personal estates. The tax authority can blacklist the director, freeze visa processing, and prevent departure from China.
Shell Company Structure and DTA Abuse (Conduit Hong Kong Parent)

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied for lack of beneficial ownership, triggering the full 10% dividend WHT plus interest and penalties. The GAAR (CIT Law Article 47) allows the tax authority to recharacterise the structure, impose additional tax, and enforce a special tax adjustment interest rate (prime rate plus 5 percentage points) under the Special Tax Adjustment Measures. Failure to file the SAFE foreign exchange registration for outbound dividend remittance may incur an administrative fine of up to 5% of the remitted amount.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate structure is a sham, judicial authorities will pierce the corporate veil and impose unlimited personal liability on the controlling shareholders and directors for all unpaid tax, interest, and penalties. Directors face travel bans, asset freezing, and criminal prosecution for international tax evasion. If the Hong Kong entity participates in the conduit scheme, its directors may also be investigated by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department for filing false offshore claims, punishable by imprisonment under Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Ordinance. In mainland China, Criminal Law Article 201 exposes the directors to up to 7 years' imprisonment.
