Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) portal; for the Hong Kong SAR component, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region via the eTAX portal

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture automatically receives a Unified Social Credit Code through the State Administration for Market Regulation; this code is the statutory Chinese Tax Identification Number and VAT registration number, and the tax registration with the State Taxation Administration is completed through the integrated one-stop government service. The joint venture must then activate its e-Tax account on the STA Electronic Tax Administration portal, bind the legal representative or authorized fiscal officer, and confirm its taxpayer classification. For a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in Mainland China, the non-resident must submit a tax registration application through the STA e-Tax portal or the competent local tax office, providing a home-country certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents or equivalent, a board resolution or power of attorney appointing a responsible person or in-country tax representative, passport copies, and evidence of the economic activity giving rise to China-source income or taxable supplies; the STA then issues a non-resident enterprise TIN. Hong Kong SAR companies obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the Inland Revenue Department; no separate VAT identification number is issued because Hong Kong imposes no VAT.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide a check-the-box election or a pass-through election for Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Ventures; the entity is taxed as a resident enterprise on worldwide income. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for basic tax/VAT registration; all resident enterprises must register upon incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below that, small-scale taxpayer treatment may apply. Hong Kong SAR: not applicable because no VAT regime exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC general VAT taxpayer may claim 0% VAT on the export sale of hydraulic cement or portland clinker where the goods are physically exported from the customs territory of Mainland China to an overseas destination or to the Hong Kong/Macau SAR customs territories, the exporter is the customs declarant holding a valid Customs Export Declaration, an ocean bill of lading evidences completed international shipment, the sales proceeds are received through legitimate banking channels on SWIFT LC or TT rail and are recorded with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, and the transaction is evidenced by commercial contracts and a zero-rated VAT e-invoice. International ocean freight for export cargo is likewise zero-rated as international transportation. Qualifying offshore digital services fall outside the goods scope but would be zero-rated where wholly consumed offshore and supported by contract and service-rendition documentation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Zero-rated export VAT e-invoice issued through the STA e-invoice platform
  • Commercial invoice, packing/weight list, and export sales contract
  • Bank or SAFE export proceeds collection record showing foreign exchange receipt

The STA mandates issuance of all VAT invoices through the fully digitalised electronic invoice (e-invoice) platform; invoice data is transmitted in real time to the national fiscal clearing system, and each e-invoice carries a unified digital tax control code, an encrypted electronic seal, and an anti-counterfeiting verification code. Taxpayers with ERP environments must integrate through approved API/e-invoice interfaces. Export sales must be supported by a zero-rated e-invoice matched to the customs export declaration. Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT e-invoicing or fiscal clearance requirement; ordinary business invoices satisfying the Business Registration Ordinance are sufficient.

Failure to substantiate the export within the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation period, or failure to produce the required customs, transport, invoicing, and proceeds documentation, triggers reclassification of the export as a domestic supply and assessment of output VAT at the standard rate together with late-payment surcharges and penalties of up to five times the underpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export declarations, forged bills of lading, false FIRCs, over-declared clinker quantities, or sham offshore counterparties result in withdrawal of the zero-rating benefit, clawback of any VAT refunded, customs penalties, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebates under the Criminal Law. Cross-agency post-clearance audits by the STA, customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange are applied routinely.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The overall regime is best characterized as full current-account convertibility for genuine trade transactions combined with managed, registration-based capital-account restrictiveness. For the Sino-foreign equity joint venture domiciled in mainland China, import/export payments and fees are processed by designated foreign-exchange banks once the bank verifies the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and logistics proof. Export proceeds must be repatriated but need not be forcibly surrendered; the JV may retain export earnings in a foreign-exchange settlement account subject to a bank-approved quota and may convert excess balances to RMB as needed. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted only after the annual statutory audit confirms realized after-tax profits, all enterprise income tax filings and clearance certificates are settled, and the bank reviews the board resolution declaring the dividend. Capital-account flows such as registered capital injection, shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds require prior SAFE foreign investment information registration, bank-of-record capital account opening, and post-receipt verification by a licensed Chinese certified public accountant. The practical hurdle is not whether the money can enter or leave, but whether the JV can prove the underlying transaction is real, the pricing is arm's-length, and the beneficial ownership chain is transparent. Banks will reject vague remittance purposes, round-dollar freight payments without bill-of-lading support, and advances on contracts lacking shipment history. The Hong Kong parent entity may face additional CNH/HKD convertibility latitude outside the mainland, but the onshore JV treasury must remain firmly inside PBOC/SAFE oversight.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods-trade foreign-exchange measures, export foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated to China through a designated bank within 180 days of the underlying export customs declaration or the contractual payment due date, whichever is applicable. China currently imposes no fixed mandatory conversion ratio, so export proceeds may be retained in the JV's Foreign Exchange Settlement Account up to the bank-approved quota and any excess above that quota must be converted into RMB. If the JV wishes to hold offshore or delay repatriation, it must obtain prior SAFE approval; failure to repatriate on time is treated as a regulatory violation subject to penalties, monetary fines, and possible downgrading of the JV's FX compliance rating.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China FX-control regime, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the corresponding currency-board authority for the Hong Kong SAR offshore clearing leg.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must execute a direct SWIFT MT103 remittance from its own licensed Hong Kong bank account to the JV's SAFE-approved Capital Account - Foreign Exchange Capital Account maintained at Bank of China Limited or another designated Tier-1 PRC bank. The remitter name must exactly match the registered shareholder; the remittance must include the SAFE Foreign Investment Information System registration number and a clear payment purpose of registered capital contribution under the foreign direct investment capital-account category. The receiving bank validates the transaction against SAFE's capital-project information system, records the specific cross-border foreign-exchange transaction code, and credits the proceeds as unavailable for speculative or unauthorized use. After receipt, the JV must engage a licensed Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, then file the report with the market regulation authority and update the SAFE registration. If the funding is denominated in HKD or USD, the JV may convert the amount to RMB through the designated bank only after capital verification; the proceeds must not be used for equity investment, entrusted loans, or repayment of domestic loans without further SAFE approval.For procurement of Portland clinker and related ocean freight, the JV should use three primary clearing rails. Open-account trade settled via SWIFT MT103 remains appropriate for vetted recurring counterparties, supported by purchase orders, commercial invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations. High-value bulk cargo should be settled through an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit opened by the JV's Chinese bank and advised/confirmed by the seller's bank under UCP600, with inspection certificates and full original shipping documents as the triggering documents. Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) may be used for mid-tier counterparties where the bank does not underwrite credit but still controls document release. All ocean-freight disbursements to the vessel owner must be executed through MT103 wire to the shipowner's bank, referencing the charter party and freight invoice, and must be coded with the correct SAFE foreign-exchange transaction code to prevent a freight-payment audit exception. If the counterparty agrees, cross-border settlement in CNY may be cleared through CIPS; otherwise USD/HKD settlement runs through the PRC bank's offshore correspondent using CHIPS/Fedwire.Permissible onshore deliverable FX forwards booked by the JV at a SAFE-licensed designated bank, supported by genuine current-account or capital-account exposure and by underlying trade documents; speculative or naked forward contracts are prohibited.Cross-currency swaps between CNY and HKD or USD are permissible only for matched balance-sheet hedging of a foreign-currency liability or receivable, limited to the approved notional amount and requiring board approval plus bank credit documentation.Plain-vanilla FX options and option-combination structures are permitted for hedging anticipated commercial flows but must have a real risk amount, a signed ISDA Credit Support Annex, and bank-marked exposure limits.Non-deliverable forwards are not permissible onshore for the PRC entity; NDF participation is reserved for offshore non-resident institutions and would be a violation of SAFE derivative regulations if booked by the onshore joint venture.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Legally unavailable for the onshore Chinese joint venture. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC as designated foreign-exchange banks and cannot open an onshore CNY account for a mainland China entity, cannot hold a SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account, and cannot perform statutory capital verification or trade authenticity checks. At best, a licensed Hong Kong money service operator may support an ancillary HKD/CNH collection pocket for the Hong Kong treasury side, but it cannot serve as the primary clearing rail for the JV's statutory capital contributions, trade settlements, tax payments, or profit remittances. All onshore financial activity must be executed through a bank holding a SAFE banking license.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds per IRD DIPN 58: intercompany transfer of goods exceeding HK$60 million; transfer of intangibles or services exceeding HK$40 million; financing arrangements exceeding HK$50 million. A Master File is required when the aggregate value of intercompany transactions for the year exceeds HK$200 million, or when related-party turnover exceeds 10% of the entity's turnover and the international group revenue exceeds HK$6 billion (CRS reporting).

Economic substance scope: Although Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in BVI/Cayman, the IRD enforces economic substance via the territorial source principle. A company claiming offshore profits must demonstrate that key commercial activities, asset management, risk-taking, and decision-making are actually located outside Hong Kong. Functional analysis and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) substance are crucial. Under the PRC/HK Arrangement, a Hong Kong company must be a bona fide resident with management and control in HK to claim treaty benefits; a shell company without staff or premises will be denied. IRO sections 61A and 61B empower the IRD to disregard artificial transactions lacking commercial substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Understatement of Offshore Profits

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s. 82A – on summary conviction: fine of HK$100,000 and 3 years' imprisonment plus three times the amount of tax evaded; on indictment: fine of up to HK$100,000 and 3 years' imprisonment plus threefold tax penalty. In addition, under s. 82B, the court may order payment of any tax underestimated.

Director personal liability: Piercing the veil: directors who signed the return or participated in preparing false computations are personally liable for the additional tax and penalties; the IRD may issue departure prevention directions (s. 77) barring travel; civil proceedings may attach personal bank accounts; directors face disqualification under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s. 786.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Inadequate Documentation

Statutory sanction: IRO s. 51C (failure to furnish information) – penalty up to HK$10,000; s. 80(3) – incorrect return – HK$10,000 plus three times undercharged tax; s. 82A for tax evasion if wilful. The IRD may also impose additional amounts per DIPN 58: penalties of up to 300% of the additional tax assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for the accounting system may be held liable for the full amount of the undercharge; the court can require restitution of diverted profits to the company; travel restrictions and asset freeze orders may be issued; in aggravated cases, criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the Revenue.
Fraudulent Trading / Sham Company (Corporate Veil Piercing)

Statutory sanction: Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s. 823 – fraudulent trading: on conviction, fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 15 years; s. 823(4) empowers the court to declare that any person knowingly a party to the fraud is personally liable for all or any of the debts and other liabilities of the company without limitation.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to restore lost assets, including non-recourse personal liability for company debts; directors face mandatory disqualification for up to 15 years; the High Court may grant a Mareva injunction freezing personal assets; criminal conviction results in imprisonment and forfeiture orders.
