Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible currency regime under the Basic Law and the linked exchange rate system administered by the HKMA. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible into foreign currencies, and there is no regulatory distinction between current account and capital account convertibility for settlement, transfer, or profit remittance. A Hong Kong entity may freely receive equity capital, shareholder loans, trade receipts, and dividends, and may remit dividends abroad without FX surrender requirements or profit repatriation restrictions, subject only to ordinary corporate tax compliance and Companies Registry filings. However, because this treasury blueprint supports a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with mainland Chinese shareholders and a recycled scrap copper supply chain involving the PRC, the foreign exchange control regime of mainland China remains critical. SAFE governs outbound investment, cross-border guarantees, trade-related settlement, and repatriation of dividends to Chinese residents. A PRC shareholder injecting capital into the Hong Kong JV must first obtain SAFE overseas investment registration and remit through a designated domestic bank; otherwise the inbound payment will be rejected by the Hong Kong receiving bank. Similarly, any dividends paid by the Hong Kong JV to the PRC parent must flow through legal banking channels and will be converted to CNY under SAFE current account rules, requiring a tax residence certificate, audited financial statements, board dividend resolution, and proof of Hong Kong profit tax withholding. Thus, while Hong Kong itself imposes zero exchange controls, the composite structure is constrained by SAFE on the mainland side.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender requirement for export foreign currency receipts in Hong Kong; a Hong Kong entity may retain, disburse, or convert export proceeds freely. For PRC entities within the same corporate structure, SAFE requires export proceeds in goods to be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless an approved overseas retention is granted. Under current SAFE current-account rules, export receipts may be credited to a foreign exchange earnings account and are not forcibly converted into CNY, but the exporter must complete the trade foreign exchange receipt verification and report the transaction in the cross-border receipts and payments platform. Mandatory conversion applies only in specific administrative circumstances, and any settlement with the bank must be at the prevailing market rate.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injection into the Hong Kong JV should be made in USD, HKD, or CNY from the PRC shareholder's designated foreign exchange bank after the shareholder completes SAFE overseas investment registration and the outbound remittance bank validates sanctions and OFAC screening. The payment is transmitted via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code corresponding to overseas direct investment equity subscription, quoting the Hong Kong company's share capital reference and the receiving bank account. The Hong Kong receiving bank should place the subscription funds into a capital escrow account until the share allotment is filed with the Hong Kong Companies Registry. If the structure includes shareholder loans, the loan agreement must be registered with SAFE, the interest rate must be arm's length, and the Hong Kong JV must maintain a complete transactional trail for future interest and dividend repatriation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The PRC transfer pricing documentation thresholds under the State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 42 of 2016 require the preparation of a Local File if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers or financial asset transfers; and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required where the annual revenue from related-party transactions exceeds RMB 1 billion, or where the assets or revenue of the affiliated group exceed specified thresholds. In addition, a contemporaneous benchmarking study (TP report) must be prepared by the filing deadline.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not maintain a separate statutory 'Economic Substance Regulations' ordinance; however, the enterprise income tax law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and its Implementation Regulations apply a substance doctrine. A PRC-resident JV must demonstrate that it has substantial business operations, actual employees, physical premises, and real decision-making functions in China. For Hong Kong intermediate entities claiming treaty benefits, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department and the STA will conduct a Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis to determine whether the Hong Kong company actually performs critical functions (negotiation, contracting, logistics management, inventory ownership, and risk assumption) in Hong Kong. If the HK entity lacks these functions, the treaty benefits are denied, and the income is re-attributed to the Chinese source, triggering additional CIT, withholding tax, late payment interest, and penalties. The same substance analysis applies to the JV itself to prevent it from being disregarded as a shell or sham for tax avoidance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Declarations

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, the tax authority will recover the underpaid tax and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the amount evaded. Where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes a criminal offence under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, the company may be subject to criminal fines, and the directly responsible personnel (directors, CFO, tax manager) may be prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who approve or direct the fraudulent reporting will have the corporate veil pierced and may be held personally liable for the unpaid taxes and statutory fines. The tax authority may freeze personal bank accounts and initiate civil asset recovery proceedings. A travel ban (blacklisting) will be imposed, and the individual is prohibited from leaving the PRC until the tax is settled. Criminal convictions carry imprisonment of up to 7 years, plus personal criminal fines.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Abuse of Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: The tax authority, under Article 123 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Articles 111-115 of the Implementation Regulations, may make a transfer pricing adjustment, re-determine the taxable income, and impose a special interest surcharge (5% per year on the underpaid tax for the period of delay) under Article 48 of the Tax Collection Law. If the taxpayer fails to provide documentation required under Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a penalty of up to RMB 10,000 may be imposed, and the adjustment may be made without further evidence in favor of the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: If the transfer pricing scheme is so egregious that it evidences a fraudulent purpose, the directors may be held personally responsible for the additional taxes and interest. The STA may refer the case to customs and criminal authorities for smuggling or tax evasion; directors face travel restrictions, asset seizure, and if found guilty of tax evasion, imprisonment of up to 7 years. For fraudulent cross-border pricing involving money laundering, a separate conviction under the Anti-Money Laundering Law may result in imprisonment of up to 10 years.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance / Beneficial Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the EIT Law (GAAR) and the so-called 'beneficial ownership' regulations (Notice of the State Administration of Taxation on the Recognition of Beneficial Owners under Tax Treaties, Bulletin No. 9 of 2018), the tax authority will disregard the Hong Kong SPV, deny treaty benefits, and re-characterize the income as directly paid to the ultimate Chinese resident. The JV will be required to withhold the full 10% withholding rate (or 10% on interest/royalties if not reduced) plus late payment interest. A penalty of up to 150% of the underpaid tax may be imposed. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue may also apply the Economic Substance Requirement (for relevant activities under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance) and impose fixed penalties for non-compliance.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in creating and operating the offshore shell may lose the protection of the corporate entity in both PRC and Hong Kong courts. They may be held personally liable for the unpaid corporate tax and for the tortious misrepresentation to the tax authority. The STA will block the director's exit from China, may confiscate passports, and may initiate criminal proceedings for aiding and abetting tax evasion. In Hong Kong, directors may be disqualified under the Companies Ordinance for fraudulent trading, ordered to compensate the company’s creditors, and criminally prosecuted where the acts involve money laundering; imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering under the Hong Kong Organised and Serious Crimes Ordinance is possible.
