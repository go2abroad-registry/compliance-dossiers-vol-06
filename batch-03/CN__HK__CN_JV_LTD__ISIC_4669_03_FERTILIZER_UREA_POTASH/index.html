Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement exists under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). The company must issue at least one share to a subscriber, and the share capital may be paid in whole or part in cash or non-cash consideration. No blocking escrow account is required under Hong Kong law for the incorporation of a private company limited by shares.

Funding & unlock path: For office-only wholesale commodity trading, no Civil Defense fire permit is required, but the premises must comply with means of escape and structural fire safety requirements under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123); the landlord or building management must confirm the fire safety inspection certificate. If the company later adds warehousing or physical blending, it will need a Fire Services Department fire safety certificate, a building license, and an occupational safety clearance from the Labour Department for storage of fertilizer products, together with a dangerous goods classification review for any dry granular fertilizer that may present a dust explosion hazard in bulk storage.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong; indirect taxation in Hong Kong is limited to duties on specific controlled commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No VAT/GST registration threshold applies because Hong Kong has no value-added tax system. Every person carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310 within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover; there is no indirect tax turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong because Hong Kong imposes no VAT or GST to zero-rate. For exports of prilled urea and potassium chloride from mainland China by the qualified PRC Sino-Foreign JV, China VAT export zero-rating applies where the goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory, the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, and the export proceeds are repatriated to China. The Hong Kong entity is the offshore buyer rather than the VAT registrant; export trading profits of the Hong Kong entity may qualify as offshore profits for Hong Kong profits tax if purchase and sale contracts and substantive commercial activities occur outside Hong Kong, but that is a territorial profits tax claim, not an indirect tax zero-rating mechanism.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the current Chinese legal framework, the Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividend remittances, and interest payments, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and appropriately documented. Capital account convertibility, however, remains controlled and requires prior approval or registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the initial capital injection from the Hong Kong shareholder is classified as foreign direct investment (FDI) and must be registered with SAFE via the local bank; the funds are then held in a foreign exchange capital account before conversion to RMB for operational use. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong parent is permitted only after the JV has completed annual audits, paid corporate income tax, obtained a tax clearance certificate, and submitted a board resolution confirming distributable profits. The enterprise must also comply with the quarterly profit remittance certification and the bank's enhanced due diligence. Although the mandatory surrender of export proceeds was abolished in 2012, exporters are required to repatriate all export proceeds into China within 90 days after customs clearance, after which the enterprise may choose to settle in RMB or retain foreign currency. In practice, this means the JV must demonstrate a clear paper trail for every cross-border transaction, with detailed invoices, bills of lading, and customs declarations. This regulatory backdrop significantly increases the operational burden for treasury functions, but does not constitute an absolute barrier to cross-border liquidity management.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE requires that export proceeds from goods be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless prior extension is granted for special trade financing arrangements. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporting entity may retain up to 100% of the foreign exchange receipts in its designated foreign exchange account, or convert to RMB at the bank's market rate. The receipt must be matched against the customs declaration electronic data in the trade monitoring system before the bank can credit the account. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers an administrative penalty and possible downgrade in the enterprise's trade classification.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) via the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability in mainland China is essentially low for primary corporate banking functions. Entities providing banking services to PRC companies require a banking license from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA). Digital EMIs such as Airwallex or Currenxie are not permitted to hold client capital in onshore accounts or provide foreign exchange settlement for capital account transactions; they may only support payment collection as facilitated agents of a licensed bank, subject to strict outsourcing rules. For a Sino-Foreign Equity JV, all capital injections, profit repatriations, and trade settlements must flow through the regulated banking system. The risk of using an unauthorised EMI includes account freezing, SAFE penalties, and inability to issue compliant trade documents. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on licensed Tier-1 commercial banks in mainland China, with any offshore EMIs limited to the Hong Kong parent for its own (non-China) operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Hong Kong investor must be wired to the JV's foreign exchange capital account at a licensed bank in mainland China, using SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code identifying FDI equity injection (SAFE purpose code 621011). The remitter must be the registered Hong Kong shareholder; no third-party advance payments are permitted. Upon receipt, the bank will clear the payment in the SAFE Capital Project Information System, after which the funds can be settled into RMB for the JV's operating expenses. Within payment processing, the JV must submit a capital verification report from a Chinese certified public accountant within 30 days after the injection, confirming the paid-in capital amount and its consistency with the registered capital schedule. All inbound wires must carry the full legal name of the entity and a clear invoice/contract reference to enable SAFE matching.For imports of potash and other raw materials, the JV may utilise SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for pre-paid or sight settlement against bills of lading and invoices. For larger shipments with established suppliers, an irrevocable commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) issued by the JV's bank will reduce counterparty risk; the LC must be opened under documentary compliance and the issuing bank will confirm underlying trade documents (invoice, packing list, bill of lading, certificate of origin, phytosanitary certificate if applicable). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMI cross-border viability in mainland China is essentially low for primary corporate banking functions. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on licensed Tier-1 commercial banks in mainland China, with any offshore EMIs limited to the Hong Kong parent for its own (non-China) operations.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% under the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China (CIT Law) Article 4; the Hong Kong SAR parent entity remains separately subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax at the territorial rate of 16.5% under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap 112 Section 14 on any Hong Kong-source profits (excluding the PRC-source dividends which are exempt from HK tax).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by the Chinese JV to its non-resident Hong Kong parent under CIT Law Articles 3(3) and 37; the rate falls to 5% where the HK parent is the beneficial owner and holds directly at least 25% of the JV's paid-in capital throughout the 365 days preceding the distribution (China-Hong Kong DTA Article 10(2)(a)).
