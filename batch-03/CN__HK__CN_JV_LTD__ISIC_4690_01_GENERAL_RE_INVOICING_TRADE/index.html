Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region – eTAX portal (https://www.e-tax.ird.gov.hk) for electronic filing and business registration

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong presence: (1) Incorporate or register the entity with the Companies Registry and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation (for local companies) or a Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company (for branch); (2) Apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the IRD within 1 month of commencement of business, under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the BRC bears the Business Registration Number (BRN), which serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all IRD tax records; (3) For a company, the IRD automatically assigns a Director/Shareholder identification; (4) If the entity has a permanent establishment in Hong Kong, it must file Profits Tax returns with the IRD, using the BRN for tax reference; (5) Optionally, obtain a Certificate of Resident Status for tax treaty benefits. For the specific case of a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (JV) that is incorporated in mainland China but carries on business in Hong Kong as a non-Hong Kong company, the branch must register under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance and obtain the BRN; the mainland parent's Chinese TIN (Social Credit Code) is also maintained for cross-border compliance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Territorial_Scope – Hong Kong profits tax is levied on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong; foreign-sourced income is not taxable unless remitted to Hong Kong and received by a person carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong. The JV branch will be taxed at entity level (16.5% profits tax) on Hong Kong-source profits only.

Available election forms

None – Hong Kong does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election. However, a taxpayer may elect to be taxed under the two-tiered profits tax rates regime (8.25% on first HKD 2,000,000 assessable profits for one entity, subject to connected entity rules). Also, the 'offshore claim' is a statutory mechanism to exclude non-Hong Kong sourced profits from assessment, where the taxpayer applies under Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.14 and submits a claim with supporting documentation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong. A nominal duty applies to certain commodities (e.g., liquors, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable – Hong Kong does not impose any indirect tax registration threshold. The Business Registration Ordinance requires all businesses (including import/export trading companies) to obtain a BRC regardless of turnover, with no turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST on exports or importations. There is no 'zero rating' concept. Instead, exports of goods are 'out of scope' of any indirect tax. For profits tax purposes, export trading profits may be treated as offshore income if the benefits of the contracts and all essential operations (e.g., negotiation, execution, delivery) occur outside Hong Kong, and thus not subject to profits tax.

  • Bill of Lading (B/L) or Airway Bill (AWB) evidencing shipment
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Full set of original shipping documents (certificate of origin, insurance policy) where applicable

  • Bank credit advice or inward remittance records evidencing receipt of sale proceeds (for audit substantiation of the offshore claim)

None – Hong Kong has no mandatory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime for general trade. Voluntary e-invoicing may be used via eTAX for tax filings; commercial invoices are regulated under private contract law. For customs, import/export declarations are filed electronically via the Electronic Data Interchange (EDI) system of the Trade and Industry Department (TID) through approved service providers.

No indirect tax clawback applies because there is no VAT/GST. However, for the Profits Tax offshore claim, the IRD will scrutinize the substance of operations; if the claim is rejected due to lack of evidence, the taxpayer faces assessment on full profits at the standard rate (currently 8.25%/16.5%) plus potential penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) – e.g., Section 80 and 82A – for incorrect returns without reasonable excuse (up to treble the tax undercharged, and conviction liable to a fine of HKD 10,000 and imprisonment for 6 months). Additionally, for customs declarations, inaccurate or fraudulent declarations may lead to prosecution under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) with fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict segregation between the current account and capital account. For the mainland Chinese parent and manufacturing leg of the JV, current account transactions such as trade in goods, services, and profit remittances are generally convertible upon presentation of authentic supporting documents, but are subject to SAFE verification and the Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate held by the bank. Capital account items including foreign direct investment injections, cross-border equity transfers, and shareholder loans remain heavily controlled and must be registered with SAFE or filed with the relevant local branch before funds can be moved. The Hong Kong SAR operates as a separate monetary and legal jurisdiction with no exchange controls, full free convertibility of the Hong Kong dollar, and no surrender requirements; however, any renminbi clearing conducted between the Hong Kong entity and mainland counterparties must comply with the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) rules and RMB trade settlement guidelines issued by the HKMA and PBOC. The principal profit remittance hurdles are documentary: the mainland joint venture must evidence prior tax payment, audited financial statements, a resolution of the board of directors approving dividends, and a valid Foreign Exchange Business Registration; in addition, the bank must verify that the remittance purpose code and underlying contracts are consistent. For the Hong Kong re-invoicing hub, no such hurdles exist, but bank account opening and ongoing transaction monitoring require a clear economic substance rationale, a traceable back-to-back trade documentation chain, and demonstrable arm's length pricing to avoid being classified as a shell treasury conduit under HKMA's AML/CFT guidelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export transactions booked in mainland China, the exporter must collect foreign exchange proceeds into a domestic designated foreign exchange account and complete receipt verification at the bank by lodging the customs declaration, invoice, contract, and logistics documents. SAFE generally requires export receivables to be repatriated and settled or retained in foreign currency within 90 days from the date of export unless extended under approved trade financing structures. Certain eligible proceeds may be retained offshore under overseas account schemes but must still be reported; mandatory full conversion has been relaxed under current account convertibility, yet the bank must confirm the proportion to be converted into RMB against the actual trade volume. For the Hong Kong entity, there are no statutory surrender or conversion obligations for trade proceeds, and all hard-currency receivables may be held, netted, or remitted freely through licensed banks, provided that suspicious transaction reporting and records of underlying trade documentation are maintained.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

The feasibility rating for offshore fintech EMI services is Low for the mainland Chinese operating leg and Low-to-Medium for the Hong Kong re-invoicing entity. Digital banks and electronic money institutions such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, and neobank platforms are not authorized by SAFE and the PBOC to act as primary settlement banks for mainland domestic foreign exchange receipts or for the capital verification of a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. They cannot issue or accept export proceeds surrender instructions, cannot access the mainland's cross-border trade verification system, and cannot complete the statutory FDI announcement and SAFE registration interfaces. For the Hong Kong entity, licensed virtual banks and e-money licensees supervised by the HKMA may open accounts and provide MT103 and cross-border collection services, but they generally lack full trade finance infrastructure such as confirmed Letters of Credit, receivables discounting, and sweeping custody facilities required for a re-invoicing hub and medium-risk enhanced due diligence profile. The blueprint therefore recommends one Tier-1 Hong Kong licensed bank as the anchor treasury and clearing bank, with fintech EMIs used only as complementary collection rail for low-value trade receivables from approved jurisdictions, never as the sole account for equity capital or dividend repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial registered capital for the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must be contributed in accordance with the approved articles of association and the foreign investor shall remit funds through a licensed bank from Hong Kong to the mainland JV's foreign exchange capital account. The Hong Kong entity must execute an FX wire using SWIFT MT103 with the purpose code reflecting 'foreign direct investment capital injection' and must include the SAFE-assigned Investment Enterprise Code in the remittance narrative. Upon receipt, the mainland domestic bank must complete the FDI registration, verify the funds through the SAFE information system, and obtain a Capital Verification Report prepared by a licensed accounting firm certifying the paid-in percentage. Inward remittances may be denominated in USD, HKD, or RMB depending on the settlement agreement; if RMB is used, the transfer must clear via CIPS or a correspondent account with proper trade and capital flow tagging. The JV may not convert or disburse such capital until the capital verification is completed. Sweeping surplus operating liquidity from the mainland JV to the Hong Kong hub is restricted to current account items such as trade proceeds, service fees, or dividends after tax, and must be supported by customs declarations, invoices, contracts, and a board resolution. The Hong Kong entity shall maintain clear segregation of the equity capital line, operational hedging accounts, and trade collections to allow rapid audit trails for both SAFE and HKMA reviewers.For settlement of approved purchase orders to mainland suppliers, use SWIFT MT103 in USD, HKD, or RMB through a licensed clearing bank. Where the mainland supplier requires a confirmed payment undertaking, the Hong Kong JV may open an irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit through Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited or HSBC with the mainland sister bank as advising or confirming bank; such LC must state the Incoterms, maritime or air waybill details, product description, and must comply with UCP 600. For open-account trade with established counterparties, Documentary Collections under ICC Uniform Rules for Collections (URC 522) are acceptable but carry higher settlement risk; therefore, treasury policy should apply them only for low-value repetitive transactions with a clear credit limit. For payments to third-country vendors outside mainland China, the standard rail is a SWIFT MT103 with full compliance narrative including the underlying purchase order reference, invoice number, and the Hong Kong entity's transaction reference; payments must be made strictly from the operational account and each disbursement must be matched against an approved goods-in-transit or immediately expected receivables event to prevent capital being stranded. The HKMA's transaction monitoring expectations require that no outgoing payment is released without a corresponding electronic record of the underlying purchase contract and logistic contract, to be stored for a minimum of five years. The blueprint therefore recommends one Tier-1 Hong Kong licensed bank as the anchor treasury and clearing bank, with fintech EMIs used only as complementary collection rail for low-value trade receivables from approved jurisdictions, never as the sole account for equity capital or dividend repatriation.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Cap. 112; two-tiered at 8.25% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits and 16.5% on the remainder

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a Hong Kong company. The Hong Kong payer has no statutory duty to withhold any amount from dividends distributed to a PRC corporate shareholder, and the PRC parent receiving such dividends will be subject to China Corporate Income Tax at 25% on the foreign-sourced dividend income, subject to foreign tax credit and the China-Hong Kong Tax Arrangement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: required when the consolidated annual group turnover exceeds HK$200 million; Local File: required when the aggregate value of related-party property-transactions exceeds HK$20 million, or when the aggregate value of related-party financial, intangible, management services or other transactions exceeds HK$10 million, in accordance with IRD's DIPN 59.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a European-style ESR statute, but the IRD applies an economic substance doctrine through territorial-source assessments, transfer pricing rules, the FSIE regime, and DTA beneficial ownership tests. For an ISIC 4690 re-invoicing intermediary, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are the negotiation of purchase contracts, the negotiation of resale contracts, credit and currency risk management, logistics control, invoicing, and treasury settlement. If the Hong Kong JV merely issues documents while the PRC parent performs CIGA, the IRD may recharacterize the entity as a shell or nominee. The IRD will examine whether the entity has local staff, office premises, director meetings, authority limits, independent judgment, and a realistic balance-sheet exposure to supplier and customer credit risk. In a joint venture context, the shareholders' agreement and board minutes must demonstrate that the Hong Kong JV has real decision-making power over the re-invoicing margin, credit terms, delivery terms and settlement rails; otherwise profits may be re-sourced to Hong Kong, denied treaty benefits, or adjusted on an arm's length basis.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Deliberate Offshore Profit Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.82(1), willfully evading tax by making a false statement or declaration is a criminal offence punishable on conviction on indictment with a fine of up to HK$100,000 and a further penalty equal to three times the amount of tax undercharged, plus imprisonment of up to three years. In addition, under s.80 and s.82(1B), the IRD may levy additional assessments of up to 200% of the tax undercharged and charge statutory interest from the due date until settlement.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the Hong Kong JV is a sham or nominee of the PRC parent: the director is personally treated as carrying on the trade and is jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall. The IRD may issue a departure prohibition order under IRO s.77 to prevent the director from leaving Hong Kong until the tax is paid or security is furnished. Personal civil asset restitution is available through the Court, and upon criminal conviction the director may be disqualified from managing Hong Kong companies for up to 15 years and subjected to confiscation proceedings.
Shell Company / Artificial Re-Invoicing with No Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.61 and s.61A, the IRD may disregard the artificial transaction and assess the PRC parent as the person deriving the affected income, or confirm a transfer pricing adjustment under Part 9A of the IRO. The IRD may impose a negligent or fraudulent return additional assessment of up to three times the tax undercharged under s.80(2A). In a liquidation, the director is exposed to fraudulent trading liability under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.377: the court may require the director to make personal contribution to the company's assets and impose criminal liability with an unlimited fine and imprisonment of up to 15 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who know that the Hong Kong entity is a mere shell and still approve invoices, transfer pricing rebates and upstream payments are personally liable for civil restitution of the mischaracterized profits. The court can order asset recovery, freezing orders, and disqualification orders under Cap. 622 s.168; the IRD may also join the director as a taxpayer for resulting profits tax. The director's personal bank accounts and directors' fees may be subject to garnishment, and a directorial disqualification order prevents the individual from acting as a director of any Hong Kong company without leave of the Court.
Trade-Based Money Laundering / AML Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25, a person who deals with property knowing or believing it to represent the proceeds of indictable crime commits an offence punishable on conviction on indictment with a fine of up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 14 years. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), directors and responsible officers of licensed institutions facing breaches may face disciplinary fines, revocation of licensing, and adverse regulatory findings. Financial sanctions and asset freezing may be imposed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority or the Drug Trafficking and Organized Crime authorities.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in, authorize, or recklessly ignore the trade-based laundering scheme are personally prosecutable under Cap. 455 and may face asset confiscation orders under the proceeds-of-crime legislation. The court may order civil restitution of all amounts laundered, with compound interest, and may bar the director from holding a management position in any regulated entity. The director may be placed on a financial intelligence blacklist, subjected to a travel ban pending criminal investigation, and, upon conviction, serve a significant custodial sentence. The corporate veil will be pierced to recover hidden assets from the director's spouse or connected companies where transfers were made without beneficial ownership change.
