Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a Hong Kong company must maintain a registered office at a physical address in Hong Kong; a pure virtual office, postal box, or commercial mail-forwarding address is not a lawful registered office. The Companies Registry and the Inland Revenue Department will reject a non-physical address for business registration. For a grocery retail JV that requires bank account opening, local POS/Interac settlement, and credit card merchant services, all HKMA-authorized banks will reject a mere virtual mailbox and will require a lease, ownership document, utility bill, and confirmation that corporate records and board minutes are accessible at the physical office. A fully serviced office with a dedicated room and permanent staff may pass, but a mail-drop only arrangement will not.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Ordinance does not impose minimum paid-up capital, no blocked escrow account exists, and foreign shareholders may hold 100% of the issued capital. The proposed Sino-foreign joint venture is therefore structured as a Hong Kong private company limited by shares with an agreed shareholders' agreement; the initial issued capital may be as low as HKD 1.00, but shareholders may set a higher subscribed amount in Form NNC1 and the statement of capital. Any bank account opening will follow standard commercial due diligence rather than government capital blockage rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the PRC JV is a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income, while any Hong Kong branch or Hong Kong-sourced profits of the JV are assessed on a territorial basis by the IRD.

Available election forms

None. Neither the PRC nor the Hong Kong SAR provides a US-style check-the-box election. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is statutorily a resident enterprise; a Hong Kong-incorporated company is statutorily a separate legal entity subject to Hong Kong profits tax. The Mainland and Hong Kong Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation may be invoked through an IRD Certificate of Resident Status, and BEPS hybrid mismatch rules remain relevant, but no elective entity classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC VAT registration applies from establishment regardless of turnover; the annual taxable sales threshold of RMB 5,000,000 governs compulsory General VAT Taxpayer registration (above) versus Small-Scale Taxpayer status (below). No VAT registration threshold exists in Hong Kong SAR.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT regime, export of goods by a general VAT taxpayer is zero-rated with credit/refund if the goods are physically exported from China customs territory, covered by a GACC customs export declaration, sold to an overseas buyer, and the export proceeds are repatriated through a bank to China. Qualifying cross-border services supplied wholly to offshore consumers can also be zero-rated or exempt under the PRC cross-border taxable services zero-rating catalogue. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT, so no zero-rating claim exists; goods imported into Hong Kong are not subject to HK VAT or customs duty unless they fall under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

  • GACC Customs Export Declaration (export bill of entry) produced through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international rail/road consignment note evidencing physical export from mainland China
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export sales contract matching the customs declaration value
  • Bank receipt / SWIFT MT103 / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt voucher evidencing payment from the overseas buyer
  • State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-in-goods foreign exchange receipt verification record or electronic declaration evidencing that export proceeds have been repatriated within the prescribed period
  • Hong Kong import/export declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations where goods are channelled through Hong Kong

PRC VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices and fully digitalised electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA's national E-Invoice Service Platform; invoice data are transmitted to the STA in near real time, with UKey/digital signature anti-counterfeit controls and, in pilot areas, blockchain-verified fully digital e-invoices. Hong Kong SAR has no real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate; taxpayers retain ordinary invoices/receipts and electronic records and file through the IRD eTax portal.

PRC tax and customs authorities will disallow zero-rating and claw back the refunded or credited VAT, with daily late-payment interest (0.05% per day), if the exporter cannot produce the export declaration, transport documents, bank receipt/proceeds verification, or if a mismatch or overstatement is found. Fraudulent export refunds are subject to penalties under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Customs Law, including fines of 50% up to 5 times the tax undercharged or refund falsely claimed, confiscation of goods, suspension or revocation of export tax rebate status, and criminal referral for false export or tax fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. In Hong Kong, because there is no VAT, no export zero-rating clawback would arise; IRD penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) apply to any misleading profits tax filings linked to the arrangement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a controlled but modernised foreign exchange regime. On the current account, the RMB is broadly convertible, enabling trade settlement for imported groceries, freight, insurance, and routine operational expenses through authorised banks under transaction authenticity checks. Capital account convertibility, however, remains strictly licence-driven, so the JV's paid-in capital from Hong Kong, shareholder loans, disposal proceeds, and profit dividends are subject to SAFE registration, restriction on currency conversion for non-trade purposes, and an active national regulatory framework designed to prevent capital flight. The corporate structural tension between sovereign mainland China and the special offshore jurisdiction of Hong Kong SAR requires two distinct clearing rationales: inbound equity injections must be recorded through the Foreign Investment Information System (FIMS), and remittance of dividends needs submission of audited financials, tax payment certificates, business authenticity, and a board resolution confirming distributable profits. There is no direct RMB convertibility on capital account, and NDRC or MOFCOM approval might be required depending on investment size. Surrender requirements are residual; no mandatory percentage conversion into RMB is currently enforced, but export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange designated account. The practical effect for a supermarket retail JV is that import payments settle smoothly, while any outbound remittance to the Hong Kong parent triggers compliance hurdles. The bank must verify each transaction against underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and internal control records, and any mismatch can trigger a suspension and escalation to SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Forex Administration, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated foreign exchange account promptly after customs declaration; the standard statutory timeframe is 90 days for service exports and 180 days for goods exports, unless a separately approved extension is granted. The enterprise may retain 100% of the receipts without any forced surrender ratio, but all amounts in excess of a reasonable holding buffer must be either converted into RMB through an onshore bank or reported for balance verification. In practice, for a grocery JV with occasional exports, the bank will enforce the 180-day goods collection period through the Trade FX Collection Verification System, and late repatriation without prior application can lead to the JV being placed on an FX downgrade watchlist.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central monetary authority; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) has jurisdiction for offshore clearing and the free-floating Hong Kong dollar.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions such as Revolut, Wise, or AirWallex are legally barred from serving as primary operating accounts for a mainland China-domiciled foreign-invested commercial enterprise. They do not hold branch licences from the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission and cannot access the CNY, CIPS, or CNAPS clearing rails. Their only legitimate role in this structure is as an offshore treasury ancillary in Hong Kong SAR for a small float in HKD or USD, never for the mainland JV's payroll, supplier payments, statutory tax settlements, or VAT refunds. A local Tier-1 bank account is mandatory for the capital escrow and monthly clearing, and the JV must maintain a separate PRC-censused domicile address and valid UBO records for PBOC and SAFE reporting. Using an unlicensed upstream EMI to aggregate inbound capital injections into the capital account is a prohibited pathway that will cause automatic transaction hold, posting to PBOC's suspicious transaction index, and possible seizure of corporate seal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech electronic money institutions such as Revolut, Wise, or AirWallex are legally barred from serving as primary operating accounts for a mainland China-domiciled foreign-invested commercial enterprise. They do not hold branch licences from the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission and cannot access the CNY, CIPS, or CNAPS clearing rails. Their only legitimate role in this structure is as an offshore treasury ancillary in Hong Kong SAR for a small float in HKD or USD, never for the mainland JV's payroll, supplier payments, statutory tax settlements, or VAT refunds. A local Tier-1 bank account is mandatory for the capital escrow and monthly clearing, and the JV must maintain a separate PRC-censused domicile address and valid UBO records for PBOC and SAFE reporting. Using an unlicensed upstream EMI to aggregate inbound capital injections into the capital account is a prohibited pathway that will cause automatic transaction hold, posting to PBOC's suspicious transaction index, and possible seizure of corporate seal.
