Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Value-Added Tax Law of the People's Republic of China and its implementing rules, exported goods and qualifying exported services are eligible for the zero percent export VAT rate. The exporter must be a VAT general taxpayer, hold customs export registration, sell the goods in its own name, submit the export declaration through the China International Trade Single Window, maintain a complete electronic chain of order, payment, logistics, and customs declaration data, and repatriate the export foreign exchange proceeds to a bank in China within the required time limit under the SAFE trade foreign exchange administration regime. For cross-border e-commerce FBA exports, customs supervision modes applicable to cross-border e-commerce export (including 9610, 9710, and 9810) must be declared accurately with matching e-commerce platform order and logistics data.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Customs Declaration of Export Goods) generated through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading for ocean and multimodal transport or Air Waybill for air export evidencing cross-border movement of the goods
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export contract, or order evidencing the underlying cross-border sale
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt or Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation evidencing receipt of the foreign currency purchase price
  • Matching cross-border e-commerce order, payment, and logistics electronic data with transaction numbers linked to the customs declaration
  • VAT export invoice and export tax rebate application documentation as required under the export tax refund administration regime

Mainland China: the fully digitalized electronic invoice regime is administered through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the State Taxation Administration, anti-counterfeit QR code digital stamping, unified invoice numbers, and authorized application programming interface (API) integration for enterprise sales and accounting systems, satisfying the fiscal clearance and anti-fraud digital stamping mandate. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate, and no anti-fraud digital stamping regime.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs jointly audit export declarations, logistics records, platform transaction data, foreign exchange receipt records, invoices, and rebate claims. A taxpayer that claims zero percent export VAT treatment without a genuine export or with incomplete documentation faces disallowance of the zero-rating, compulsory repayment of any VAT refund received, daily late-payment surcharges, and penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection of the PRC of not less than 50 percent and not more than five times the tax amount involved. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may also be prosecuted under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties including fixed-term imprisonment up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property. Hong Kong SAR has no export zero-rating mechanism because it imposes no VAT/GST; export profits are outside the charge to Hong Kong profits tax only if the profit source is outside Hong Kong, subject to the IRD territorial-source audit.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland legal framework maintains strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account transactions are freely convertible after bona fide trade verification; however, capital account inflows, including foreign direct investment capital contributions, require full registration with SAFE and conversion is allowed only through designated banks under the pay-as-you-go principle. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, all foreign exchange receipts from export sales must be repatriated into a domestic settlement account and are subject to bank scrutiny of underlying trade documents. Profit remittance is permitted, but requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and evidence of compliance with the joint venture charter, which may create operational friction. The RMB exchange rate is managed without a fully convertible capital account; enterprises must also disclose ultimate beneficial owners and maintain transaction records for five years. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer a mandatory conversion ratio, but the obligation to bring funds onshore and to settle through licensed intermediaries creates an effective liquidity control rail. The convertibility of RMB for cross-border e-commerce is enhanced by the pilot clearing arrangements, yet micro-transactions still receive multiple layers of AML scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China within a maximum of 90 days after the customs declaration date and deposited into an export settlement account at a designated bank. There is no legal requirement to sell a fixed percentage of the foreign currency to the state; the enterprise may retain the FX or convert it to RMB as market conditions dictate, subject to documentary verification of the underlying export transaction and compliance with SAFE's export proceeds monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), operating through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under Hong Kong’s transfer pricing regime and DIPN 59/60, a Local File must be prepared where the aggregate value of cross-border controlled transactions in a category exceeds HK$10 million; a Master File is required when the MNE group has consolidated annual revenue of at least HK$7.5 billion and cross-border controlled transactions exceed HK$100 million.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations registration regime similar to BVI or Cayman, but the IRD applies source-of-profits principles, beneficial ownership requirements, and general anti-avoidance rules under IRO ss.61 and 61A. For Amazon FBA and e-commerce operations, core income-generating activities (CIGA) must be genuinely performed in Hong Kong—strategic management, procurement decisions, seller-account control, inventory planning, and payment treasury functions—to support both treaty claims and offshore profit treatment. A shell office with no employees or board substance will be disregarded.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Offshore Profit Attribution

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82C provides that fraudulent evasion of profits tax is an indictable offence carrying a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment of up to 3 years, with an additional penalty of three times the tax undercharged. IRO s.82A permits additional tax of up to 100% for negligent or non-compliant understatements, and s.59(3) allows the IRD to issue best-of-judgment assessments where records are not produced.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false BIR51 returns, approve sham contractual arrangements, or participate in the concealment of Hong Kong-source profits may be criminally prosecuted personally. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the JV is used as a mere façade; directors may face civil restitution of the tax evaded, asset freezes, departure prohibition orders, travel blacklisting, disqualification from serving as directors for up to 15 years, and imprisonment up to 3 years on indictment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Inadequate Cross-Border Documentation

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AA codifies the arm’s-length principle and empowers the IRD to adjust profits where associated persons do not deal at arm’s length. Failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation may lead to additional assessments of undercharged profits tax, additional tax under s.82A of up to 100%, and, in cases involving false documentation, criminal penalties under s.82C. IRD can impose daily penalties for failure to produce records under s.80.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve inflated service fees, circular financing, or non-arm’s-length royalty payments with no valid commercial purpose face personal liability for participating in fraudulent trading. The corporate veil may be pierced if the JV is an instrumentality used to strip taxable profits from Hong Kong; directors may be ordered to restore diverted assets and are exposed to criminal prosecution, personal penalties up to three times the tax undercharged, travel bans, and disqualification orders.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61 empowers the IRD to disregard artificial or fictitious transactions; s.61A voids backdated or tax-avoidance transactions. DTA relief may be denied for lack of beneficial ownership and economic substance, causing the full statutory profits tax and mainland WHT exposure to restart. Penalties include additional tax under s.82A of up to 100% of the undercharged tax, criminal fines under s.82C up to HK$100,000 with imprisonment up to 3 years, and refusal by IRD to issue Tax Residency Certificates.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell JV may be personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622) s.528, with civil liability for the entire loss caused to the tax authority and creditors. The corporate veil will be pierced where the shell is used to abuse the legal personality of the company; directors face personal asset restitution, departure prohibition and blacklisting, disqualification, and criminal imprisonment terms of up to 3 years for tax fraud, and additional sanctions for falsifying records.
