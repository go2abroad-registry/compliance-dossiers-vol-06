Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 (RMB five million) in annual taxable sales for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; all PRC-established enterprises must complete taxpayer registration from incorporation regardless of turnover, with simplified small-scale VAT treatment below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC VAT general taxpayer may apply the 0% VAT rate to exported goods, including retail sales from the Shopify DTC brand, if the goods are physically exported from mainland China through a customs-supervised channel and sold to an overseas buyer. The export must be supported by a valid customs export declaration and the export proceeds must be collected and repatriated through SAFE-approved channels. Qualifying offshore cross-border services or digital services supplied by the PRC entity to non-resident recipients may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt under the STA's cross-border VAT treatment, provided the place of supply is outside mainland China and documentary evidence is preserved.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration for Exported Goods) with customs release endorsement
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or courier despatch record evidencing physical export
  • Export sales invoice, packing list, and export contract
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt/collection record evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • E-commerce order, Shopify payment settlement, and logistics tracking data showing the DTC cross-border sale
  • VAT zero-rated export declaration and export tax refund application filed with the STA e-Tax system

The PRC requires the use of the State Taxation Administration's National Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the VAT invoice management system for issuance of VAT special invoices. Fully digitalized e-invoices are transmitted to the tax authority in near real time through official e-Tax APIs or tax-authority-certified service providers, and carry a unified national invoice number, encrypted tax-control code, digital signature, and anti-fraud stamp. The Golden Tax Phase IV system performs automated cross-matching of invoice data with sales, payment, logistics, bank, and customs data. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST e-invoicing or real-time clearance mandate.

The STA, customs authorities, and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating by matching customs export release data, bank foreign exchange receipts, e-invoices, and logistics records. Where export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, or where zero-rated export declarations are unverified or fraudulent, the tax authority may claw back the zero-rated VAT benefit and any refunded input VAT, assess interest and late-payment surcharges, and impose administrative fines up to five times the tax evaded or fraudulently refunded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export tax refunds may also constitute the crime of fraudulent export tax refund under the PRC Criminal Law, exposing responsible persons to criminal penalties, imprisonment, and confiscation of assets.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, including cross-border e-commerce export payments and trade-related service fees, subject to genuine transaction verification and tax declaration. Capital account items remain strictly controlled. A Sino-foreign equity joint venture's registered capital injection from Hong Kong must be routed through a licensed onshore bank after obtaining the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration, opening a capital account, and complying with the special use rules for foreign exchange capital. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted under a regulatory 'current account' remittance review: the JV must submit the audited PRC financial statements, corporate income tax final settlement and tax clearances, board resolution on profit distribution, along with the SAFE foreign exchange registration and payment application; banks then verify that the distributed profits are derived from real accumulated earnings, not from capital gains, loans, or unreconciled transactions. The assessment is therefore: current account open, capital account conditional, and treasury operations must embed SAFE reporting, custodial account segregation, and post-verification documentary retention.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds generated by the DTC e-commerce business must be collected into a licensed onshore bank within the statutory trade-receivables monitoring window, generally within 90 days from the customs declaration date where physical goods are exported; if payment is delayed, the JV must register the outstanding balance in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System before expiry. There is currently no mandatory conversion ratio or forced 'surrender' requirement; an eligible exporter may retain the foreign currency in its current-account foreign exchange account or settle it to RMB with the bank at the market rate. However, every receipt must be repatriated into China, matched to export/goods/service customs data, and truthfully reported in SAFE's trade counter. The surrender rule is thus: full repatriation, zero mandatory conversion, but 100% reconciliation with underlying export documentation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low. PRC licensing and FX administration rules prohibit an offshore EMI from acting as a primary domestic bank or capital-account custodian for a PRC JV. Offshore digital platforms (Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe) may only be used as downstream collection aggregators for the DTC webstore; the settlement funds must be swept into a PRC-licensed account via lawful payment institutions or direct banking arrangements. Any attempt to keep export proceeds in an offshore EMI wallet before repatriation creates a high regulatory risk under SAFE's trade counter requirements and the PBOC's non-bank payment institution measures. Because a Sino-foreign JV must maintain its statutory capital account and operating accounts in a PRC commercial bank, the viable structure is: domestic Tier-1 bank as treasury core; Shopify/PayPal/Stripe as e-commerce collection rails only; no EMI as primary account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent shall remit equity capital to the JV's SAFE-registered capital account in the name of the JV at a PRC Tier-1 commercial bank. The wire must be sent via SWIFT MT103 with a purpose code corresponding to 'foreign direct investment equity injection' and a reference line containing the SAFE FDI registration number. The receiving bank verifies the counterparty, the capital verification report prepared by a PRC certified public accountant, and the balance within the approved investment quota. Once credited, the JV may settle the FX into RMB for permitted operating expenses and e-commerce procurement, but it cannot transfer the funds to a third-party loan or enter the securities market unless specifically permitted by SAFE. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under DIPN 58, a Hong Kong entity must prepare a Local File when its annual connected transactions exceed HK$44 million for transfer of goods, or HK$11 million for intangibles, services or financing transactions, or HK$4 million for real property transactions. A Master File is required if the MNE group's consolidated turnover exceeds HK$750 million and the relevant Hong Kong entity also exceeds the above thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code akin to the Cayman Islands, but the IRD applies the source doctrine and, under the general anti-abuse rule (IRO s 61A), will disregard artificial transactions lacking commercial rationale. A Hong Kong company seeking to claim offshore exemption or treaty benefits must prove that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as contract execution, administrative management, and decision-making are actually performed in Hong Kong; offshore services performed digitally outside Hong Kong may be insufficient. The IRD looks through shell arrangements and can assert substance over form.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Fraudulent Sales Omission

Statutory sanction: Section 80(2) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112): on conviction, fine up to HK$100,000, imprisonment up to 3 years, and a further penalty of 3 times the tax undercharged. For persistent evasion, s 82A may apply with additional penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the false return are personally exposed to criminal conviction; the court may issue a Mareva injunction freezing personal assets and travel restrictions (immigration hold), and directors may be disqualified for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622).
Shell Company – False Offshore Profits Claim

Statutory sanction: The IRD will invoke s 61 and s 61A to nullify the arrangement; additional tax of 100% can be assessed under s 82A, and criminal penalties under s 80(2) apply. A common law charge of conspiracy to defraud may also be prosecuted.

Director personal liability: Directors who set up the shell structure may be jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax if the court pierces the corporate veil; they face a travel ban via the Immigration Department, asset repatriation/dissolution orders, and imprisonment of up to 14 years for common law fraud.
Transfer Pricing Non-Documentation and Mis-invoicing

Statutory sanction: Failure to keep records is an offence under IRO s 51C: fine up to HK$100,000 and daily penalty of HK$1,000; the IRD may adjust the pricing to arm's length, increasing assessable profit and imposing an additional tax of up to 100% (s 82A) for deliberate undercharging.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize non-arm's length transfer prices and fail to maintain documentation are personally liable for penalties if the company cannot pay; civil action may be brought for breach of fiduciary duty, and criminal indictment is possible where false records are tendered to the IRD.
