Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau / Taxpayer Service Platform; for Hong Kong SAR matters, Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via eTAX.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign investors execute the joint venture contract and articles of association, then apply to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) for the foreign-invested enterprise business license. Step 2: Within 30 days of business license issuance, complete tax registration through the STA electronic tax bureau using the business license, legal representative identity proof, registered address, capital contribution schedule, and foreign investor documentation. Step 3: The STA assigns the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the enterprise tax ID and VAT ID. Step 4: Evaluate expected annual taxable turnover; apply for general VAT taxpayer status or remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. A mandatory general VAT taxpayer application is required once expected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or if export zero-rating with input VAT refund is intended. Step 5: If the foreign non-resident investor has no permanent establishment in China, register a non-resident taxpayer identity profile with the competent tax office so China-sourced payments are subject to correct withholding and treaty documentation. Step 6: If a separate Hong Kong vehicle or branch is used, apply to the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance; Hong Kong does not issue a VAT ID.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime nor a hybrid mismatch election for a Sino-foreign equity joint venture; the JV is taxed at entity level on its worldwide income. For indirect tax purposes, the only relevant election is the VAT small-scale taxpayer versus general VAT taxpayer status election, subject to the statutory turnover threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all Chinese-established JV entities are required to complete tax and VAT registration within 30 days of business license issuance without a low-value registration exemption. Hong Kong has no VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim the 0% export VAT rate for goods, the enterprise must be a Chinese VAT-registered general taxpayer; the goods must be physically exported from mainland China customs territory under a valid customs export declaration; the sale must be made to a genuine overseas buyer; export proceeds must be received in foreign currency through a licensed banking channel; and the exporter must maintain the statutory documentation bundle evidencing the cross-border transaction. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports using customs supervision codes 9610, 9710, or 9810 may use the simplified e-commerce retail export declaration list to qualify for VAT zero-rating or export exemption-and-refund treatment. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to overseas recipients and wholly consumed outside China may also qualify for zero-rating or exemption under the STA cross-border services VAT rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry equivalent; export customs declaration for the goods)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt and settlement record
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and Export Sales Contract
  • Cross-border e-commerce retail export declaration list (customs supervision codes 9610/9710/9810)
  • Fully digitalized electronic VAT invoice and TikTok Shop order/logistics transaction data

The JV must use the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) platform for all VAT invoices. Invoice data is transmitted to STA in real time through the national electronic invoice service platform, with a digital anti-fraud stamp and QR verification code. Cross-border e-commerce retail export declarations are submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and matched electronically with the STA export tax refund and zero-rating review system. E-commerce platform transaction data for TikTok Shop sales must be retained and made available to the tax authority on request.

If export zero-rating is claimed fraudulently or the required customs, logistics, foreign exchange, and invoice documentation is not substantiated, the tax authority will retroactively claw back the refunded VAT, assess late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. The exporter may be suspended or permanently disqualified from export tax refund treatment; customs smuggling penalties and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund may also apply under the Criminal Law and the Customs Law. All export substantiation records must be retained for statutory audit review.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF but imposes stringent capital-account controls. The subject Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, conducting social commerce via TikTok Shop, must treat foreign exchange receipts from export sales as current-account trade proceeds rather than freely transferable capital. The JV must open a dedicated foreign exchange settlement account at a designated onshore commercial bank, receive all overseas proceeds into that account, and complete the SAFE 'trade in goods' monitoring system declaration. Capital injection from Hong Kong requires a separate capital account with SAFE registration, a Foreign Investment (FDI) business registration, and a verified capital payment before RMB conversion can occur. Current-account proceeds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at the bank's middle rate, subject to documentary verification of customs, invoice, and logistics data. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is only permissible after annual audit, corporate income tax clearance, and withholding tax on dividends, evidenced by a tax payment receipt. Non-trade current payments, including management fees and royalties, must also be documented to satisfy SAFE's 'truthfulness and legitimacy' review. The overall regime significantly constrains offshore cash pooling and requires every cross-border transaction to be supported by underlying commercial documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Statutory repatriation deadline: within 180 days after export customs declaration or the due date for the underlying trade, unless SAFE grants an extension for justifiable reasons. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% statutory surrender for export receipts; the JV must repatriate 100% of the export foreign currency proceeds into a designated onshore foreign exchange settlement account, after which it may retain the funds in foreign currency or convert them into RMB at the prevailing bank rate. Each receipt must be matched with customs declaration data, invoices, logistics documents, and the SAFE Trade Monitoring System record before credit is released.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

In-depth feasibility rating: LOW. Offshore digital EMI providers, including Wise B2B, are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic settlement accounts, clear RMB, or act as treasury providers for PRC-resident JVs. The JV must channel export proceeds through a domestic bank participating in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System; using an EMI as a receiving bank would violate repatriation rules and create an unresolved foreign exchange reconciliation item. EMIs may only be considered as an ancillary information layer and cannot hold JV transactional balances or perform FX conversion.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company must appoint an auditor and have its financial statements audited, except for (1) a dormant private company that has delivered a dormant status notification to the Companies Registry and had no significant accounting transactions; and (2) a private company that qualifies as a small private company and validly passes an audit-exemption resolution. A small private company must satisfy at least two of the following three quantitative criteria for the financial year: annual revenue not exceeding HKD 25 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 20 million, and average number of employees not exceeding 50. The audit exemption is not available to public companies, listed companies, or companies that are members of a larger group that is not a small private group; a member holding 5% or more of voting rights can object and require a statutory audit. Non-exempt companies must engage a certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance and deliver an independent auditor's report on the financial statements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million of annual related-party transactions triggers Local File; RMB 1 billion triggers Master File; consolidated group revenue of RMB 5.5 billion requires Country-by-Country Reporting.

Economic substance scope: At the PRC JV level, economic substance is demonstrated by physical offices, warehouse facilities, employees and operational logistics in mainland China. For the HK shareholder, to enjoy DTA benefits and avoid being classified as a pure shell, it must satisfy Hong Kong's economic substance requirements for relevant activities, maintain adequate staff and premises in Hong Kong, and ensure core income-generating activities (CIGA) are not artificially shifted to mainland management. China's general anti-avoidance rule (CIT Law Art. 47) disregards arrangements lacking reasonable commercial substance and re-characterizes profits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing - Related-Party Documentation Failure and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Art. 56; SAT Announcement 42/2016. Consequences: transfer-pricing adjustment with interest at 5-percentage-point benchmark premium; administrative fine from RMB 10,000 to 50,000 for documentation non-conformance; underpayment penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall.

Director personal liability: Directors are jointly and severally liable in civil recovery for concealed taxes; they will be added to the Dishonest Taxpayer Blacklist, imposing travel bans, aviation and high-speed rail restrictions. Criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201: up to 3 years imprisonment for small-amount evasion and 3-7 years for large amounts.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Income Tax on Outbound Dividends, Interest and Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Art. 69: a fine of between 50% and 300% of the un-withheld tax; late payment interest on the unpaid amount; the obligation to withhold survives even if the non-resident does not file a return.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer and actual decision-makers may face personal fines; the tax authority may freeze personal assets and restrict overseas travel (Blacklisting). If the concealment was deliberate and exceeds criminal thresholds, the director faces criminal tax evasion charges with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Treaty Benefit Abuse - Shell Structure and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 47 (GAAR), Art. 40 and Art. 41; Tax Administration Law Art. 110; denial of treaty relief, additional tax at 10% (or 25% on attributed profit), together with 50%-500% fraudulent understatement penalty and public blacklisting.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers are personally liable for tax restitution and administrative penalties; the court may issue exit bans (blacklisting) and freeze personal bank accounts. Under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 and Art. 191, directors who engage in abusive scheme with money laundering may be sentenced to up to 7 years imprisonment, plus fines and forfeiture of proceeds.
