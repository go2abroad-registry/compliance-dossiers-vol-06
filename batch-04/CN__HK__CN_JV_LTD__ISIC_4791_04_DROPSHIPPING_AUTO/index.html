Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope. The Hong Kong joint venture is a separate legal entity and is not a pass-through entity. Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not have a check-the-box or generic entity classification election. Elective reliefs such as group loss relief under section 19C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance or personal assessment do not alter the entity's tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong). Hong Kong uses indirect taxes only in specific areas: stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance, duties on dutiable commodities (liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol) under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, and betting duty under the Betting Duty Ordinance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, there is no turnover threshold for VAT registration. However, a person carrying on a business in Hong Kong (including automated dropshipping) must register for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance within one month of commencement, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to Hong Kong because no VAT or GST exists; there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim. Cross-border sales of goods from Hong Kong are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect taxation. For profits tax purposes, export profits qualify for offshore treatment only if the operations that generate the profit (e.g., contracts, negotiation, and substantial operational steps) are performed outside Hong Kong. For goods supplied from mainland China in this CN-HK dropshipping structure, the mainland exporter/VAT-registered entity may claim PRC export VAT zero-rating/exemption only if it is a registered PRC VAT exporter, the goods are actually exported and cleared by China Customs, invoicing is made in the export trade, payment is received and foreign exchange repatriated within the statutory period, and the PRC e-commerce export regulatory mode (e.g., 9610, 9710 or 9810) is properly followed. Qualifying offshore digital services rendered through the Hong Kong JV are not subject to Hong Kong taxation if the services are performed and enjoyed outside Hong Kong, subject to source rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime in which current account transactions are generally convertible but capital account transactions remain stringently controlled. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the inbound equity capital injection from Hong Kong is a capital account item requiring SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration before the foreign currency capital can be received and converted into renminbi. Once the registered capital has been settled through the bank's capital settlement mechanism, compliance with the negative list and approved business scope determines whether the RMB can be used for operational disbursements. Current account payments such as supplier settlements, logistics costs, and remittances related to dropshipping are freely convertible if genuine and supported by commercial documents, tax certificates, customs declarations, and bank trade verification. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted but requires the JV to demonstrate accumulated statutory reserves, absence of prior-year tax losses, payment of corporate income tax, completion of tax filing and audit, and consent of both board and shareholders; the bank will scrutinize the remittance as a dividend distribution under foreign exchange rules. Surplus funds in foreign currency from exports can be retained in a designated current-account foreign exchange account or converted at will; there is no fixed surrender obligation, but all export receipts must be repatriated into China and reconciled with customs data. Capital account convertibility for investing abroad, external loans, and offshore guarantees remains subject to SAFE registration, quotas, and documentary proof of commercial need. The Hong Kong layer provides offshore liquidity with full convertibility and no surrender rules, but funds cannot move into the PRC JV without passing through the onshore FDI and current-account verification framework. Overall, the JV is fully bankable only through a licensed PRC bank with SAFE connectivity; direct fintech or offshore EMI accounts cannot substitute for the mandatory onshore FDI and capital verification rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China's post-2010 framework imposes no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the statutory mandatory surrender requirement has been abolished and 0% of export foreign currency receipts must be force-converted into RMB. However, export proceeds must be repatriated to the exporting JV's foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days after the date of export customs declaration, unless an approved extension or deferred collection filing has been made with SAFE. The receipts must be matched against customs declarations and logistics evidence through the bank's goods-trade foreign exchange verification system, and the JV may either retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert them at the prevailing market rate. For cross-border e-commerce and automated dropshipping collection through payment gateways, the settlement must be supported by platform order data, payment institution settlement files, and customs electronic records; SAFE may require dynamic reporting where orders are collected before shipment.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the PRC onshore JV; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the offshore Hong Kong treasury and licensing layer, which operates under a fully convertible currency regime without SAFE-style exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks have low viability as the primary banking chassis for the mainland Chinese JV. Under PRC law and SAFE administration, deposit-taking corporate banking, foreign exchange settlement, capital account handling, and trade verification must be performed by a licensed onshore commercial bank; offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut are not authorized to open domestic current or capital accounts for a PRC-registered entity. Stripe and PayPal are payment collection rails only and cannot act as a bank or SAFE reporting authority; they can collect consumer card payments from end buyers but the JV must then settle the proceeds into its licensed onshore bank account via an approved cross-border payment institution or direct acquiring arrangement. The Hong Kong treasury subsidiary may use a licensed Hong Kong EMI or bank for offshore working capital, but any equity injection or trade settlement flowing into the PRC JV must ultimately pass through a Tier-1 PRC bank with valid SAFE registration and transaction purpose codes. A domestic PRC third-party payment institution licensed by PBOC can support cross-border e-commerce collections, but it cannot replace the commercial bank for capital verification, export proceeds repatriation, or compliance with FX control requirements.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent will originate a SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfer from its Hong Kong bank to the PRC JV's foreign exchange capital account at the selected Tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance must carry a clear purpose description such as 'EQUITY CONTRIBUTION FOR SINO-FOREIGN EQUITY JOINT VENTURE' and the appropriate SAFE transaction purpose code as determined by the receiving bank for inbound direct investment capital injection. Before the payment, the JV must complete SAFE FDI registration with the local SAFE office or under the delegated bank registration pilot, and the receiving bank must confirm the credit against the SAFE FDI registry. After the funds arrive, they are held in the foreign exchange capital account and may be settled into RMB through the bank's capital settlement system; the settled RMB must be kept in a special pending-use RMB account and used only within the lawful business scope, negative-list restrictions, and applicable investment purpose rules. The JV cannot lend, invest in securities, purchase wealth management products, or prepay unrelated parties with the settled capital without SAFE-permitted use. Once the bank has confirmed the receipt and the JV has updated its registration, a PRC certified public accountant issues the capital verification report, which becomes the basis for the JV's business scope, regulatory licenses, and later dividend remittance calculations. Any routing through Hong Kong must not obscure the end-to-end ownership trail; the HK parent's bank must also perform its own China sanctions and counterparty screening before release.For operating payments to dropshipping suppliers, logistics providers, and fulfillment centers, the JV will primarily use domestic RMB transfers via PBOC's payment and clearing system for onshore merchants, or SWIFT MT103 for genuine cross-border supplier remittances supported by a trade contract, commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order, logistics evidence, and customs declaration records. For high-value inventory purchases or payment terms requiring documentary security, the JV can use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (L/C) with a Hong Kong or overseas supplier bank, or a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) where the supplier needs bank-mediated document transfer without full L/C cost. Cross-border remittances of service fees, platform commissions, and software subscriptions must be filed as service trade and supported by the Service Trade and Foreign Exchange Payment Tax Certificate issued by the PRC tax authority; otherwise the bank will reject the payment. For automated dropshipping where goods are shipped directly to end customers in multiple jurisdictions, the JV must maintain a precise clearing trail linking customer collections, supplier payouts, and customs declarations; using PayPal or Stripe balances to settle overseas suppliers without passing through the PRC banking system is not permitted because it bypasses SAFE trade verification. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs and offshore digital banks have low viability as the primary banking chassis for the mainland Chinese JV. Under PRC law and SAFE administration, deposit-taking corporate banking, foreign exchange settlement, capital account handling, and trade verification must be performed by a licensed onshore commercial bank; offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut are not authorized to open domestic current or capital accounts for a PRC-registered entity. Stripe and PayPal are payment collection rails only and cannot act as a bank or SAFE reporting authority; they can collect consumer card payments from end buyers but the JV must then settle the proceeds into its licensed onshore bank account via an approved cross-border payment institution or direct acquiring arrangement. The Hong Kong treasury subsidiary may use a licensed Hong Kong EMI or bank for offshore working capital, but any equity injection or trade settlement flowing into the PRC JV must ultimately pass through a Tier-1 PRC bank with valid SAFE registration and transaction purpose codes. A domestic PRC third-party payment institution licensed by PBOC can support cross-border e-commerce collections, but it cannot replace the commercial bank for capital verification, export proceeds repatriation, or compliance with FX control requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when related-party transactions in a year exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial or intangible transfers; or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is required where the group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and cross-border transactions are present.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a separate ESR regime, but the substance-over-form principle and the General Anti-Abuse Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law may deny treaty or preferential benefits absent real economic substance. For the Hong Kong affiliate, the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) apply to any relevant activity (including pure holding, intra-group services, financing, IP management), requiring adequate staff, premises, and local management; annual notification and a substantive economic test must be satisfied. Failure risks penalties (HKD 50,000 plus daily fines) and automatic exchange of information.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Intentional Under-reporting

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes recovery of underpaid tax plus late-payment surcharge and a fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Under Criminal Law Article 201, evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and representing more than 10% of taxable income is a criminal offence, with penalties up to 7 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for civil restitution; their personal assets can be attached; an exit ban may be imposed by frontier authorities; criminal imprisonment up to 7 years can be imposed, and the corporate veil may be pierced if the company is used as a mere alter ego.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation & Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 and 44 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, tax authorities may adjust taxable income and impose deemed income based on best available data; penalties under Article 64 of the Tax Collection Law reach 50%-500% of underpaid tax, plus interest on the adjusted amount at the benchmark loan rate plus five percentage points.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly enforce sham documentation may face joint and several liability; administrative fines; and criminal exposure for false tax declarations under Criminal Law Article 204, carrying up to 7 years' imprisonment and asset confiscation.
AML Violations / Shell Disguise for Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, institutions may be fined up to RMB 5 million and responsible individuals up to RMB 500,000; SAFE can impose penalties up to 30% of the illegal remittance and freeze bank accounts; Criminal Law Article 191 provides for imprisonment up to 10 years and confiscation of proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors may be criminally prosecuted for wilful blindness; personal exit bans and blacklisting can be applied; the corporate veil may be pierced to reach personal assets; imprisonment for money laundering ranges from 5 to 10 years for serious offences.
