Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) does not impose any minimum paid-up capital for a private company limited by shares. There is no statutory requirement to deposit capital into a blocked escrow account at the Companies Registry. The Sino-foreign joint venture agreement may stipulate a contractual schedule of capital contribution, but any escrow arrangement is a private banking matter and not a statutory incorporation condition.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: GovHK eTAX - Electronic Services for Tax operated by the IRD, including Business Registration e-Services and Profits Tax e-Filing.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The procedure is: (1) incorporate a Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) or register a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of Cap. 622; (2) submit the business registration application to the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencement of business; (3) receive the Business Registration Certificate containing the 8-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the Hong Kong tax identification number; (4) if chargeable to profits tax, file the Profits Tax Return under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) when issued. No separate VAT/GST registration exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The procedure is: (1) incorporate a Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) or register a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of Cap. 622; (2) submit the business registration application to the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencement of business; (3) receive the Business Registration Certificate containing the 8-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which functions as the Hong Kong tax identification number; (4) if chargeable to profits tax, file the Profits Tax Return under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) when issued. No separate VAT/GST registration exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, fiscal unity regime, or hybrid mismatch election. A Hong Kong limited company is automatically a separate taxable person under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and is taxed on territorial-source profits. The two-tiered profits tax rate concession (8.25% on the first HKD 2 million of assessable profits, and 16.5% on the remainder) is a rate concession, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; limited excise duties exist under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT or GST registration threshold in Hong Kong. Every person carrying on business must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under Cap. 310, irrespective of turnover. Excise registration applies only to importers or producers of dutiable commodities under Cap. 109.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for VAT because Hong Kong does not levy VAT or GST. For Hong Kong profits tax purposes, an entity may claim that profits from cross-border mail-order retail sales are offshore and therefore outside the territorial scope of Hong Kong profits tax. Substantiation must show that the negotiation, execution, and performance of purchase and sale contracts occur outside Hong Kong, supporting the offshore-source profits claim. This is not an export VAT zero-rating claim.

  • Electronic Hong Kong Customs Export Declaration submitted under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) with the assigned declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement outside Hong Kong
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and order documentation supporting the Cash on Delivery (COD) transaction
  • Carrier COD manifest or logistics provider settlement report evidencing collection and remittance of sales proceeds
  • Bank inbound remittance credit advice, SWIFT MT103, or Airwallex payment provider statement evidencing foreign exchange repatriation and final receipt of export proceeds
  • Proof of overseas contract formation and performance, if relied upon as evidence of offshore profits (e.g., overseas client agreements, marketplace/agent records)

None. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time fiscal clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, or a B2B e-invoicing API. Tax invoices are not regulated for VAT. For cross-border goods movements, exporters must submit electronic Customs export declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) through the Government Electronic Trading Services (GETS) EDI platform. Books and business records must be retained under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

No VAT clawback applies in Hong Kong. However, false or misleading import/export declarations are an offence under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) and may attract fines and imprisonment. Incorrect profits tax returns with intent to evade tax may attract additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), plus fines and prosecution. Failure to retain sufficient records to substantiate an offshore profits claim may cause the IRD to assess profits as taxable on a source-in-Hong Kong basis with applicable penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime and maintains a bifurcated convertibility system. The RMB is fully convertible on the current account for genuine trade, services, and dividend remittance, provided that underlying transactions are routed through a designated foreign-exchange bank and supported by commercial documents. Capital-account items, including FDI injections, foreign-currency borrowing, overseas investment, and cross-border guarantees, require SAFE registration or approval before conversion; settlement is admitted only for bona fide business purposes. Foreign-invested enterprises in the e-commerce and mail-order trade sector must demonstrate real business substance, maintain an onshore capital account with a licensed bank, and comply with the PRC negative list for foreign investment. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is current-account in nature but is subject to withholding tax clearance, audited financial statements, a board resolution, and evidence of distributable post-tax reserves. The JV must also ensure capital-account funds are not used for prohibited outbound lending, repayment of unrelated onshore RMB loans, or offshore equity purchases unless separately approved under SAFE's real-use rules. Although Hong Kong has no exchange controls, any activity touching the mainland entity is pulled through PRC FX law and the interbank forex market governed by PBOC and SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated through a designated onshore bank and are governed by the trade contract settlement terms, with a regulatory default monitoring window of 90 days from customs clearance for open-account exports and 180 days for documentary/letter-of-credit transactions; any extension requires SAFE exception reporting. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; SAFE permits full retention in a foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntary conversion to RMB at the enterprise's discretion.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low as a primary rail; conditional ancillary use only. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex possess e-money or licensed payment capabilities in Hong Kong and other markets, but do not hold a deposit-taking licence from PBOC/SAFE and cannot open the China JV's capital escrow account, receive FDI on behalf of the onshore entity for SAFE statutory purposes, or legally operate in the onshore FX settlement boundary. They may be used at the group treasury level for HK-side consolidation, multi-currency cards, and cross-border logistics remittances, but the JV's inbound capital, export proceeds, and SAFE-regulated payments must clear through licensed domestic Tier-1 banks. Only a PBOC-licensed payment institution with a cross-border payment qualification may support ancillary collection and disbursement in partnership with a domestic bank; reliance on an unlicensed offshore EMI as the sole banking rail is a regulatory and KYC failure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Registered capital from the HK parent must follow the PRC FDI flow: (1) obtain the China business licence and complete SAFE foreign investment information registration at the receiving bank; (2) originate the remittance from the HK shareholder's own bank account via SWIFT MT103 into the JV's onshore capital escrow account, quoting the SAFE transaction-purpose code for inbound direct investment capital under the balance-of-payments statistics taxonomy; (3) the receiving Tier-1 bank performs the spot RMB conversion if required and credits the operational RMB or FX settlement account; (4) a PRC certified public accountant issues the capital verification report after receipt; (5) downstream capital-account payments must be supported by contracts/invoices and must comply with the negative list and real-use rules; prohibited uses include security investment, onshore equity over-investment, unlicensed lending, and repayment of unrelated RMB loans; (6) any decrease/refund of registered capital requires SAFE registration and the bank's compliance sign-off.Use SWIFT MT103 for direct telegraphic transfers to freight forwarders, overseas suppliers, and service providers where speed is essential; use commercial letters of credit under UCP600 for high-value or first-goods imports with balanced risk; and use documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for mid-risk trade settlements requiring bank-mediated documents. For mainland RMB disbursements to local logistics or COD distributors, route via CNAPS/Chinese bank corporate e-payment rails, and for cross-border ecommerce payouts use licensed HK/onshore payment aggregators only as collection/disbursement engines beneath a domestic bank clearing backbone.Onshore deliverable RMB/foreign-currency forward contracts with SAFE-designated banks, supported by a genuine current-account or approved capital-account exposure and a self-certified non-speculative hedging purpose.Plain-vanilla FX swaps and cross-currency swaps matching the maturity, principal, and cash-flow profile of approved foreign-currency loans or trade obligations, subject to SAFE registration where the underlying exposure arises from foreign debt.Offshore non-deliverable forwards are permissible only for the Hong Kong licensed parent or other offshore group entities, and are not legally accessible to the China-resident JV for onshore settlement; any onshore NDF attempt would constitute unlawful FX dealing under PRC regulations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For the PRC JV, contemporaneous Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible property transactions exceed RMB 100 million, or when any one category of intangible property transfers, financial asset transfers, or service transactions exceeds RMB 10 million. The Master File is mandatory when the aggregate annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not use the Caribbean or Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations label, but Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and State Taxation Administration Announcement 2009 No. 2 impose a general anti-avoidance rule requiring economic substance, business purpose and the absence of artificial arrangements. The JV must perform its Core Income-Generating Activities in mainland China: order taking, warehousing, COD delivery logistics, courier settlement, cash collection reconciliation and accounting. The Hong Kong shareholder must also have real offices, employees, decision-making authority and bank substance in Hong Kong; otherwise treaty benefits are denied and the arrangement may be recharacterised, leaving the statutory 10% withholding and underpayment penalties to apply.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting of COD Cash Receipts

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of between 50% and five times the underpaid tax and a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05% under Article 32. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalises fraudulent tax returns and concealment, providing graduated penalties including imprisonment of up to seven years for especially large amounts.

Director personal liability: A director who directs or knows of the diversion of COD cash can be treated as a joint perpetrator of tax evasion. Under Company Law Article 20, courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold the director personally and jointly liable for the unpaid tax and civil restitution where corporate personality is abused. The director may also be placed on the serious dishonesty blacklist and blocked from exiting mainland China through the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 exit-control mechanism until tax, surcharges and penalties are secured.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Inadequate TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 requires the tax authority to adjust intercompany transactions to arm's length; Article 44 permits deemed taxable income if documentation is absent. State Taxation Administration Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes documentation thresholds and penalties for non-compliance. Under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the underpayment caused by false TP documentation is subject to a fine of 50% to five times the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute and approve sham intercompany agreements without commercial substance can be held jointly liable if the JV is dissolved or stripped of assets before tax is paid. The director's personal assets may be attached under civil enforcement; exit restrictions and the serious dishonest taxpayer blacklist also apply. Where the TP arrangement is used deliberately to evade tax, the director may face criminal investigation and imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.
Treaty Abuse and Shell HK Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 allows the tax authority to recharacterise an arrangement lacking business purpose and substance; State Taxation Administration Announcement 2015 No. 60 denies treaty benefits to non-beneficial owners and imposes withdrawal of the reduced rate, restoration of 10% statutory withholding, late interest and penalties. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 applies to fraudulent claims, with fines from 50% to five times the tax underpaid.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign Hong Kong Tax Residency Certificate applications, beneficial ownership declarations or treaty claim forms knowing them to be false are exposed to criminal fraud and tax evasion liability under Criminal Law Article 201. Company Law Article 20 permits courts to disregard the corporate personality and hold the directors personally liable for the tax and restitution to the revenue authority. Directors may also be placed on the blacklist and have their foreign travel restricted until the tax obligation is resolved.
