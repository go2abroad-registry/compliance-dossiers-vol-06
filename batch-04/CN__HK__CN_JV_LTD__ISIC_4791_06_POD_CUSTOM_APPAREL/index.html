13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY) annual taxable turnover triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer status; below this threshold taxpayers may remain small-scale VAT taxpayers. All entities must register for tax within 30 days of incorporation regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of POD custom apparel are eligible for the 0% VAT rate when the seller is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from mainland China under customs supervision, title and risk have passed to the overseas buyer, the sale is made in foreign currency, and export proceeds are repatriated and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to overseas consumers are zero-rated if the recipient is outside China, the service is consumed outside China, and the contract and payment records clearly evidence the offshore nature.

  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Export Commercial Invoice
  • Packing List
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher reported to SAFE's Trade Monitoring System

China mandates real-time fiscal clearance through the Golden Tax System Phase IV (Jinshui IV) and the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Taxpayers must issue invoices through the E-Tax Bureau using an STA-issued UKey; all fully digital electronic invoices (FDEI) are transmitted in real time to the STA with cryptographic anti-fraud stamping. Hong Kong SAR has no e-invoicing clearance mandate.

Unverified or fraudulent export zero-rating claims attract retrospective clawbacks of claimed input VAT and unpaid output VAT, plus daily surcharges of 0.05% and administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax amount involved under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Serious fraud is criminal under the PRC Criminal Law, with penalties including imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property. The STA may audit historical export rebate claims for up to five years. In Hong Kong, incorrect offshore claims attract IRD assessing action and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current account for genuine goods and services transactions, but only after a licensed bank completes a real-transaction-background review. Capital account convertibility remains controlled. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must register inward direct investment with SAFE or the delegated custodian bank, open a designated foreign currency capital account, receive the Hong Kong shareholder's equity wires into that account, and settle into RMB only through a Capital Project Settlement Pending Payment Account. Once settled, the RMB cannot be used for securities, entrusted loans, or non-operating investments unless a specific SAFE pilot permits. Export income is treated as current account proceeds and, for compliant A-class exporters, may be retained in foreign currency or settled at the enterprise's discretion; however, the receipts must be repatriated to China and reconciled with customs declarations. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is a current-account investment income item, but it is not unrestricted: the JV must prove distributable profits with audited financial statements, a board-approved dividend resolution, tax withholding clearance, and a current SAFE/FDI registration. Banks, acting as SAFE gatekeepers, will reject remittances where dividends exceed audited retained earnings, where prior registered capital was not paid in, or where offshore structures create opaque beneficiary chains. Hong Kong itself applies no exchange controls; the HKMA's linked exchange rate system provides free conversion between HKD, USD and other currencies, so an HK treasury entity can serve as a lawful offshore liquidity hub provided the mainland funding and repatriation steps remain fully documented under PRC rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE goods-trade rules currently require export receipts to be repatriated to China through a designated bank and matched against customs declaration data; remittance must be completed within the contracted payment period, with any collection period exceeding 90 days from the export customs declaration reported through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and, for B/C-class enterprises, registered with or pre-approved by SAFE. For A-class enterprises, there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; 0% of export proceeds must be surrendered and the enterprise may retain the foreign currency in a current account or convert it to RMB voluntarily. For C-class enterprises under strict supervision, SAFE and bank controls impose a de facto mandatory conversion of 100% of export proceeds into RMB upon receipt, with each collection requiring case-by-case review; B-class enterprises face a reduced operating window and may be subject to enhanced documentation and timing restrictions but no fixed industry-wide conversion ratio. All exemptions require current valid export collection records and no overdue foreign exchange reconciliation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the Hong Kong SAR currency and banking authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a primary corporate treasury rail; Medium only as a final-mile collection aggregator. In mainland China, an offshore fintech EMI cannot lawfully open a demand deposit or settlement account for a PRC-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; only licensed banks can provide corporate settlement and FX clearing. SAFE-licensed non-bank payment institutions holding PBOC cross-border payment licenses may facilitate small-value POD marketplace collections from Stripe or PayPal, but the PSP must hold the funds under a segregated regulated account and route the ultimate settlement into the JV's mainland bank account with full transaction-level data. In Hong Kong, licensed SVFs and MSOs may provide e-wallet, remittance and trade-payment initiation services to the HK treasury vehicle, but they cannot offer commercial lending, LC issuance or forex lines, and they are not acceptable as the sole clearing venue. Therefore, use an onshore bank account as the settlement spine and integrate licensed PSPs only as customer-payment gateways; never use an unlicensed offshore EMI as the JV's primary operational account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong treasury vehicle must remit equity capital via SWIFT MT103 to the PRC JV's designated FDI Capital Foreign Exchange Account at a SAFE-approved mainland bank. Before the wire, the JV must complete FDI information registration or bank-delegated SAFE registration and obtain a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate with the unique FDI code. The remittance instruction must clearly state the purpose as Foreign Direct Investment Equity Capital Injection, include the SAFE FDI registration number, the balance-of-payments code for inward direct investment equity capital (BOP 622010), and reference the joint venture contract and capital contribution schedule. The receiving bank will perform authenticity review, convert the foreign currency to RMB through a Capital Project Settlement Pending Payment Account if requested, and keep the funds within the approved scope. A licensed PRC certified public accountant must then issue a Capital Verification Report confirming the paid-in equity capital; the report is filed with the market supervision authority and made available to the bank. Do not split the injection across non-group or unrelated third-party accounts; all funds must come from the HK holding entity's own audited account to maintain clean source-of-funds and avoid AML multiparty-mixing red flags.All outbound payments from the JV should use bank-owned rails. For direct supplier pre-payments and POD inventory purchases, use SWIFT MT103 with the applicable SAFE goods-trade purpose code, supported by purchase order, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill and, where required, the customs declaration or e-commerce transaction record. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low as a primary corporate treasury rail; Medium only as a final-mile collection aggregator. In mainland China, an offshore fintech EMI cannot lawfully open a demand deposit or settlement account for a PRC-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; only licensed banks can provide corporate settlement and FX clearing. SAFE-licensed non-bank payment institutions holding PBOC cross-border payment licenses may facilitate small-value POD marketplace collections from Stripe or PayPal, but the PSP must hold the funds under a segregated regulated account and route the ultimate settlement into the JV's mainland bank account with full transaction-level data. In Hong Kong, licensed SVFs and MSOs may provide e-wallet, remittance and trade-payment initiation services to the HK treasury vehicle, but they cannot offer commercial lending, LC issuance or forex lines, and they are not acceptable as the sole clearing venue. Therefore, use an onshore bank account as the settlement spine and integrate licensed PSPs only as customer-payment gateways; never use an unlicensed offshore EMI as the JV's primary operational account.
