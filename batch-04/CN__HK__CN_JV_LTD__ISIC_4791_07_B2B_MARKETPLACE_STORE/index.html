Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; administered locally through provincial, municipal and county tax bureaus. The online taxpayer portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) operated by the STA, used for registration confirmation, tax filing, invoice issuance, and export VAT refund applications; the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) supplies the underlying business registration data that populates the tax registration system.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD), tax registration is integrated with business registration. The 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license constitutes the enterprise's Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identity. Within 30 days of issuance of the business license, the company must complete tax registration information confirmation with the in-charge tax authority through the Electronic Tax Bureau, activate its tax digital account, and bind the VAT electronic invoice service platform. For a non-resident foreign enterprise (including a Hong Kong parent without a permanent establishment in China), no PRC TIN is automatically issued; if it derives China-source income or performs VAT-taxable activities in China, it must apply for non-resident enterprise tax registration at the in-charge tax authority, providing its home-country tax identification number and contract information. Where a Chinese buyer withholds VAT on imported services or royalties, the withholding filing uses the non-resident's tax ID and contract/remittance details as the non-resident tax registration record.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover exemption from VAT registration for newly incorporated Chinese entities; VAT registration is completed with tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance. The statutory economic turnover threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales in a rolling 12-month period: an entity meeting or exceeding this threshold must become a general VAT taxpayer, with headline 13% output VAT on taxable goods sales and credit for input VAT; below RMB 5,000,000 the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to simplified collection at 3% with no input VAT credit. Separately chargeable e-commerce platform, commission, brokerage, and modern services are taxable at the 6% VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer qualifies for VAT zero-rating (0% with refund of input VAT) on exported goods when the goods are physically exported from China to an overseas buyer, the sale is bona fide and at arm's length, the exporter has obtained a customs export declaration using the applicable customs supervision mode (for B2B cross-border e-commerce, mode 9710 for direct B2B export and 9810 for overseas-warehouse export), the sales proceeds are collected from a non-resident counterparty, and foreign exchange is repatriated to China within the statutory period. Cross-border taxable services wholly consumed outside China and listed in the positive list for cross-border zero-rating, such as information technology and software services supplied to offshore B2B customers, may also be zero-rated provided the beneficiary is outside China and payment is received in foreign exchange. Small-scale VAT taxpayers exporting goods are VAT-exempt but not entitled to input VAT refunds unless they voluntarily register as general VAT taxpayers.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Declaration Form) bearing the customs declaration number and applicable customs supervision code (e.g., 9710, 9810 or 0110)
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical departure of the goods from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange collection receipt evidencing receipt of export sales proceeds from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract, and export VAT electronic invoice
  • China International Trade Single Window release data / electronic customs clearance printout

China mandates the issuance of State Taxation Administration fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) through the Electronic Tax Bureau. Invoice data must be transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV (Jin Sui IV) supervision system, which performs automated data reconciliation and anti-fraud risk screening. Taxpayers must use a tax digital account to issue, receive, and verify e-invoices; each invoice receives a unique 20-digit invoice number and tax-control encryption. No commercial or privately generated export invoice is valid unless it is transmitted to and accepted by the STA digital invoice platform. Export customs declarations must be filed electronically through the China International Trade Single Window, and the goods must be physically released by Customs before export zero-rating can be substantiated.

The STA and Customs conduct cross-system matching of export declarations, logistics data, foreign-exchange entries, and VAT invoice data under Golden Tax IV. Any zero-rating claim not supported by the required customs, transport, commercial, and foreign-exchange documentation will be rejected, the VAT refund clawed back with daily late-payment surcharge, and penalties imposed under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export refund claims are subject to refund clawback plus a monetary penalty of one to five times the refunded amount, suspension of export-tax-refund processing for a statutory disqualification period, inclusion in the tax dishonesty blacklist, and criminal prosecution where the fraud amount reaches the criminal threshold.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a separate customs and monetary territory under the linked exchange rate system. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and both current and capital account items can be remitted without HKMA exchange-control approval. There is no obligation to surrender export proceeds, no waiting period for dividend or profit remittances, and no restriction on corporate accounts denominated in USD, RMB, EUR or other currencies. The HKMA regulates authorised institutions under the Banking Ordinance but does not impose foreign exchange rationing. Mainland China, by contrast, applies managed convertibility. Trade-related current-account payments are convertibly processed after banks verify invoices, contracts and customs declarations under SAFE rules. Capital-account items, including outward direct investment, inbound equity, shareholder loans, and repatriation, remain permit-oriented: a PRC Chinese partner injecting equity into the HK JV must complete Overseas Direct Investment (ODI) filing with NDRC/MOFCOM and SAFE registration, and every payment must be made through a designated bank with a proper purpose code. Profit remittance from mainland entities to foreign shareholders is permissible after annual audit, tax settlement and submission of the statutory remittance form. The treasury function must therefore differentiate Hong Kong's open pool from mainland China's regulated bridge and maintain separate KYC dossiers for both.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong: no statutory surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may hold export receipts in any currency and repatriate discretionarily. Mainland China: current trade-export proceeds are no longer subject to a fixed mandatory surrender ratio; exporters may retain foreign currency in an export verification account or convert to RMB through a designated bank. Any conversion or retention must be supported by genuine customs declarations, invoices, and shipping documents, and banks must report the transaction to SAFE under the trade in goods and settlement authenticity review framework. There is no fixed statutory window for conversion, but the flow should occur within the ordinary trade settlement cycle agreed in the underlying sales or logistics contract; abnormal delays trigger enhanced monitoring by the bank.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); and for mainland China-related capital and trade flows, State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs in Hong Kong are legally distinct from authorised banks. Some providers such as Airwallex or Currenxie can offer multi-currency operating wallets under an MSO or SVF licence, but they are not licensed deposit-taking institutions and do not provide the same regulatory protection as commercial banks. For this CN-HK JV B2B platform, I rate EMIs as LOW viability for capital escrow and for any treasury position that requires credit or regulatory capital; their viability is only MEDIUM as an ancillary front-end collection tool under de minimis transaction thresholds if the EMI holds Hong Kong licensing and provides audit reporting, segregated account details, and sanctions screening. Mainland China law effectively bars unlicensed offshore EMIs from performing domestic RMB settlement and capital-account transactions; cross-border RMB and FX flows must be routed through licensed banks or designated clearing institutions. Therefore, do not place statutory capital or corporate operating reserves in an offshore EMI; use it only for marginal operational payment facilitation after bank approval.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under section 360 of the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a private company is exempt from the statutory audit requirement only if it qualifies as a small private company in the relevant financial year. Qualification requires satisfying at least two of the following three conditions in both the current and the immediately preceding financial year: (i) total annual revenue does not exceed HK$20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,000); (ii) total assets do not exceed HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,000); and (iii) the number of employees does not exceed 50. If the company fails any two of these thresholds, an independent certified public accountant (practising) must audit the financial statements. In addition, an audit is mandatory if any shareholder or group of shareholders holding at least 5% of the voting rights requests it, or if the company's articles of association require an audit. The company must always prepare financial statements giving a true and fair view, regardless of audit exemption.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory trigger under the Hong Kong Transfer Pricing Rules (Sections 50AAM, 50AAN IRO) and IRD DIPN 59: a taxpayer must prepare a Master File if total intra-group revenue exceeds HKD 7,600,000,000 (or revenue of at least HKD 560,000,000 and related-party loans exceeding HKD 560,000,000); a Local File is required if the aggregate value of all controlled transactions in the tax year exceeds HKD 5,000,000, subject to category-specific thresholds: HKD 10,000,000 for financing or royalty transactions and HKD 50,000,000 for goods/service transactions. For small taxpayers, no contemporaneous documentation is required if total revenues are below HKD 20,000,000 and controlled transactions below HKD 5,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong is not a pure zero-tax jurisdiction and does not have the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, economic substance is a vital element of the source-of-profits doctrine: the IRD will reject offshore income claims unless substantive business activities (strategy decisions, employment, office premises, and operational governance) are actually performed in Hong Kong. Under the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) framework and the Pillar Two Global Minimum Tax, a Hong Kong JV that is part of a multinational enterprise group with consolidated revenue above EUR 750 million will be subject to a Domestic Top-Up Tax at 15% effective on excess profits in Hong Kong from 2025. Such groups must also satisfy the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the Hong Kong-China DTA and OECD Multilateral Instrument to obtain treaty benefits. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is used to determine whether a non-resident claimant has adequate substance to access DTA protection on cross-border royalty, interest, and dividend flows.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under Section 80(2) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a person who knowingly and wilfully makes a false or incorrect return is liable on summary conviction to a fine of HKD 10,000 plus 3 times the amount of tax undercharged. Under Section 82A(2) IRO, tax evasion committed with intent to evade tax is punishable on indictment with a fine of HKD 100,000, imprisonment for up to 5 years, and an additional fine of 3 times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also impose a civil penalty of 300% of the tax undercharged in lieu of criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise, sign, or cause the fraudulent return are personally liable. Courts may pierce the corporate veil if the company is shown to be a sham instrument. Directors face joint and several personal liability to pay the unpaid tax, civil penalties, and interest; the IRD may seek a travel ban (restriction order) and blacklisting against directors for outstanding assessments under Section 76 IRO. Criminal conviction results in a permanent criminal record, vacation of directorship disqualification under the Companies Ordinance, and imprisonment up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Sections 50AAM and 50AAN IRO impose transfer pricing penalties of up to 100% of the tax undercharged where a transaction is adjusted and the taxpayer has not kept contemporaneous documentation. Failure to produce a document required by the IRD may result in a penalty of HKD 50,000 and a daily default penalty of HKD 500. The IRD can also disallow deductions and impute interest under Section 60A. In egregious cases, the IRD may treat the failure as a misstatement under Section 82A and pursue criminal prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length pricing without substantive analysis may be personally considered taxpayers for penalty purposes under Section 49A IRO. Individual directors can be fined up to HKD 100,000 and imprisoned for up to 12 months for obstructing the IRD or making false statements in a documentation request. The court may order asset restitution and issue a travel ban against directors with outstanding tax liabilities. In flagrant ocean of deliberate profit shifting, the IRD will ascertain and hold directors jointly and severally liable for the adjusted corporate tax, interest, and penalties.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse and DTA Benefit Denial

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke Section 61A and Section 61B IRO to disregard any artificial or fictitious transaction, recompute the profits as fully taxable in Hong Kong, and impose an additional assessment. Under the OECD Principal Purpose Test (Art. 29 of the MLI, applied through the China-Hong Kong DTA 2019 protocol), a claim for DTA benefits may be denied if obtaining the treaty benefit was one of the principal purposes of the arrangement. The penalty for an incorrect claim is 300% of the tax undercharged plus deficiency interest. Civil and criminal anti-fraud provisions under Section 82A apply where a director certifies substance that does not exist.

Director personal liability: A court may lift the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the company's tax liabilities if the company is regarded as an agent or sham benefiting the directors personally. Directors are exposed to criminal prosecution for making false declarations of business substance, carrying a prison term of up to 7 years. The IRD can obtain a court order to charge the director's personal assets, impose a travel ban/restriction order to prevent departure from Hong Kong, and place the director on the IRD's publicly published blacklist of serious tax defaulters. The director may additionally be required to repay all dividends and repatriated profits as civil restitution, with interest at commercial rates.
