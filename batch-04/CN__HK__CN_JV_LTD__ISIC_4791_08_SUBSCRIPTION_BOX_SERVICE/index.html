Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

Hong Kong has no statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement. A company limited by shares may be incorporated with a nominal issued capital of one Hong Kong dollar or one share of any currency. There is no blocked escrow account or deposit-to-public-fund rule in the Companies Ordinance. If the shares are to be paid in cash, the subscriber must pay the full subscription amount before issue of share certificates; if paid in kind, the first board resolution must specify the non-cash consideration and confirm that the asset has been transferred to the Company before the allotment is entered in the register of members. Any higher capital commitment in the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture contract remains a purely contractual obligation and is not a condition precedent to incorporation.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), the national tax authority of China, with online filing through the national Unified E-Tax Bureau electronic filing system of the STA; for Hong Kong SAR tax matters of the non-resident Hong Kong investor, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR (IRD) and its eTAX electronic filing portal.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD): 1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Business License; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the Business License is automatically the statutory tax identification number (TIN). 2) Tax registration is completed through the one-time integrated registration procedure; within 30 days of issuance of the Business License the JV must complete VAT and enterprise tax registration details with the local STA through the Unified E-Tax Bureau. 3) If annual taxable sales are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, register as a VAT general taxpayer; below that threshold it may elect small-scale taxpayer status. 4) For exporting physical subscription boxes, register in the China International Trade Single Window for customs declarations and, if required, in the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Receipt and Payment monitoring system. 5) A foreign non-resident shareholder (the Hong Kong investor) is not required to obtain a mainland Chinese TIN for mere equity holdings; if the non-resident enterprise carries on business in China through a permanent establishment or has China-source taxable income requiring self-assessment or withholding, it must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration with the local STA and obtain a Chinese TIN. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the China JV is a resident enterprise of China taxed on worldwide income at the statutory 25% Enterprise Income Tax rate; no pass-through eligibility; the non-resident Hong Kong shareholder remains subject to territorial profits tax in Hong Kong only on profits sourced in Hong Kong; the structure is not within a tax-exempt zone).

Available election forms

None (China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity-classification election). The only relevant elections are administrative: small-scale VAT taxpayer status versus general VAT taxpayer status, and simplified VAT calculation methods for small-scale taxpayers; these do not alter entity-level taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All China-registered businesses must complete tax registration with the STA upon incorporation. The compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies and voluntary general taxpayer election is available. No VAT registration exemption exists for an active trading entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Chinese VAT export regime, exported physical goods (including recurring subscription-box merchandise) are zero-rated when: (a) the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer; (b) the goods have actually departed China and are covered by a valid customs export declaration; (c) the export sale is supported by genuine overseas customer contracts and export invoices; (d) the sales proceeds are receivable and collected in foreign currency through approved banking channels and reported to SAFE within statutory time limits; (e) the goods are within the export tax rebate and zero-rating catalogue and are not prohibited or restricted exports; and (f) the exporter has filed the export tax refund/exemption data in the STA electronic export refund system. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports of subscription boxes may use the simplified customs export logistics codes and retain eligibility for VAT exemption and refund if the same documentary and foreign-exchange repatriation conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form from the China International Trade Single Window (electronic export customs clearance data)
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or cross-border express consignment note evidencing physical export movement out of China
  • Export sales contract or overseas purchase order
  • Bank receipt or credit advice, or SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System report, evidencing receipt of foreign exchange proceeds
  • Export VAT e-invoice or ordinary export invoice issued in the course of export sales
  • Electronic export tax refund/exemption application schedule submitted through the STA electronic export refund system

China operates real-time electronic invoice clearance. The STA fully digitalised electronic invoice system requires VAT taxpayers to issue e-invoices through the STA electronic invoice service platform using an approved API or the e-Tax portal. Each invoice is allocated a unique tax-control code, signed with tax-authority cryptographic algorithms, and transmitted to STA in real time at issuance, with a dynamic QR code enabling buyer verification through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform. Physical fiscal-device stamping has been replaced by this digital tax clearance for fully digitalised e-invoices; export sales must link the export e-invoice or export invoice to the customs export declaration through the China International Trade Single Window.

STA and China Customs cross-match customs export declarations, logistics records, invoice data, export refund schedules, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data. If an export zero-rating claim is unsubstantiated, the zero rate is disallowed and the export sale is treated as a domestic taxable sale; the exporter must pay output VAT at the standard domestic goods rate of 13% on the export invoice value, repay any VAT refund already received, and pay a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the underpaid tax. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, an administrative fine between 50% and 500% of the evaded or underpaid tax may be imposed. Fraudulent customs declarations, false export documentation, or false VAT invoices may also constitute smuggling or export tax fraud offences under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with criminal liability, revocation of export tax refund qualification, and possible prohibition on customs clearance and export registration.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as an offshore special territory with no sovereign exchange control law. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible under the Exchange Fund Ordinance, and the linked exchange rate system is operationally managed by the HKMA but imposes no surrender requirements, no prior approval for current account payments, and no restriction on capital account movements such as dividends, intercompany loans, or equity capital repatriation. Current account convertibility in Hong Kong is absolute; any commercial bank licensed by the HKMA may execute lawful payment instructions in HKD, USD, EUR, CNY, and other currencies without foreign exchange permission. Profit remittance from a Hong Kong JV to its Chinese shareholder is unrestricted from a Hong Kong perspective, subject only to China-side withholding tax under the China-Hong Kong double taxation arrangement and SAFE registration for dividend remittance. Capital account convertibility, however, is asymmetrically constrained by the People's Republic of China's capital controls: any injection of RMB or foreign exchange from the onshore Chinese parent must be supported by SAFE-approved Foreign Direct Investment registration and verified purpose, and the HK entity cannot rely on mainland convertibility for a CNY repayment stream without documentary evidence of the underlying cross-border transaction. Thus, the convertibility risk is not in Hong Kong but at the China-Hong Kong boundary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong statutory framework imposes zero surrender requirements and zero mandatory conversion ratio for any export proceeds; the HKMA permits exporters to keep earnings in any freely convertible currency and does not require conversion into HKD. For the Chinese parent or affiliate exporting goods to the Hong Kong JV, China's SAFE rules require export proceeds of PRC resident entities to be received within 180 days for general goods and 90 days for services, with the domestic receiving bank verifying the trade background and reporting under SAFE's cross-border receipts and payment system. No fixed mandatory conversion rate applies; exporters may retain foreign exchange or settle in RMB under China's trade settlement policy. A Hong Kong JV receiving subscription-box merchandise from mainland China must provide purchase contracts, customs declarations, and shipping evidence to the Chinese bank to permit payment onward; HK-side surrender is not applicable.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBC) administers onshore China-side approvals and cross-border capital movement authorisation for a Chinese parent investing into a Hong Kong joint venture.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail. An offshore digital EMI that lacks a Hong Kong banking licence or a stored value facility licence issued by the HKMA cannot lawfully maintain a corporate account that performs deposit-taking or multi-currency clearing for a Hong Kong incorporated joint venture. Pure fintech EMIs from the United Kingdom or United States are outside the HKMA supervisory perimeter and are legally barred from servicing this entity as a settlement bank. The JV may use an HKMA-licensed SVF or a bank-partnered EMI only for low-value e-commerce collections, but all inbound capital injections, export proceeds, trade settlement, and FX hedging must be routed through a tier-1 licensed commercial bank. Any proposed EMI contract must be reviewed against the HKMA licence registry and the bank's approved vendor programme before deployment.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity from the Chinese parent must be routed through an authorised bank under SAFE's cross-border direct investment framework. The Chinese parent must first complete overseas direct investment registration, obtain the applicable SAFE registration certificate, and secure any required overseas investment approvals. The remittance is executed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's onshore corporate account to the Hong Kong JV's capital account at one of the designated tier-1 banks. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: Hong Kong entity annual turnover exceeding HK$200,000,000 together with controlled transactions with connected persons exceeding HK$220,000,000 for goods or HK$110,000,000 for services, financial transactions or intangibles; Master File threshold: MNE group consolidated revenue exceeding HK$680,000,000; documentation must be contemporaneous and submitted within statutory timelines when demanded by the IRD

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no Economic Substance Regulations comparable to offshore registration territories, but the IRD and treaty partners require the Hong Kong JV to demonstrate real economic substance where it claims treaty benefits or offshore profits; the company must perform core income-generating activities and control functions, assets, and risks in Hong Kong; for subscription-box commerce, logistics outsourcing to the PRC affiliate does not exclude Hong Kong management and contracting activities from taxability; a shell company with no employees, premises or decision-making function fails the CIGA/DEMPE standard and is exposed to anti-avoidance recharacterization under IRO ss.61A/61B.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82(1): upon conviction, fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for three years; IRO s.82A: additional tax of up to treble the amount of tax undercharged; the IRD also has power to issue estimated assessments and impose daily default penalties for non-filing.

Director personal liability: A director who signs or causes the filing of a false return is exposed to criminal prosecution and may be imprisoned; the corporate veil is pierced where fraud is shown, leaving the director personally liable for the company's underpaid tax and costs; civil asset restitution, director disqualification, travel bans, and blacklisting are common enforcement measures while the investigation is pending.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61B and the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 authorize the IRD to adjust taxable profits to the arm's length amount; IRO s.61A permits the cancellation of the whole tax avoidance scheme; the IRD may impose treble tax under s.82A where the mispricing is made wilfully, and fixed fines plus daily default penalties may be imposed for failure to maintain or produce transfer pricing documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the issue of false or sham intercompany invoices are personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Ordinance; courts will disregard the separate legal personality of the JV if the entity is used as a mere façade; directors may be ordered to make civil restitution of the tax evaded, disqualified for up to 15 years, placed on restrictions against leaving Hong Kong, and imprisoned where criminal dishonesty is proved.
DTA Abuse and Shell Company Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The Principal Purpose Test embedded in the DTA and the OECD MLI denies treaty benefits where a main purpose of the arrangement was to obtain treaty access; IRD invokes IRO s.61A to deny the tax advantage and recharacterizes the income as taxable in Hong Kong at the 16.5% rate; false statements in the TRC application are offences under IRO s.82; the Companies Registry may strike off the shell company under the Companies Ordinance for failure to carry on genuine business.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false resident status or beneficial ownership are personally liable for the unpaid tax and may be convicted of criminal fraud; courts can pierce the veil if the company is an instrument of tax abuse, exposing directors to civil restitution, travel blacklisting, disqualification orders, and imprisonment; a director of the PRC parent who controls the shell can also be pursued in the PRC under its general anti-abuse rules.
