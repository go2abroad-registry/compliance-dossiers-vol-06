VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no de minimis economic turnover threshold for tax registration of a PRC incorporated enterprise: every business must obtain a Unified Social Credit Code and complete VAT registration upon incorporation. For VAT status, taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 per rolling 12-month period mandates general VAT taxpayer registration; taxable sales not exceeding CNY 5,000,000 can remain small-scale VAT taxpayers with a 3% statutory levy (currently reduced by preferential policy to 1%). A non-resident e-commerce seller without a permanent establishment in China is not registered under this threshold but its mainland buyer or platform is required to withhold VAT at the source.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A goods export sale qualifies for the 0% VAT rate and the corresponding input VAT refund where the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory under the exporter's own customs declaration, the exporter has documented title transfer to an overseas customer, the sale has been paid for from outside mainland China in a traceable foreign-currency settlement, and the input VAT was evidenced by a PRC VAT special invoice or customs import payment certificate. For the online consignment and resale of vintage luxury items through marketplaces, the entity may claim export zero-rating when acting as principal; when acting as commissionaire, the cross-border platform service is zero-rated or exempt if the payment is received from abroad and the service is consumed overseas. Secondhand goods acquired from private consumers without VAT invoices are exported at zero output VAT but do not generate any input VAT refund, and the exporter must not include such purchases in an export rebate computation.

  • Commercial invoice encompassing the exact vintage luxury item description, sale value, currency, and Incoterms
  • Packing list and weight manifest

  • Marketplace settlement statement from payment rails such as Stripe or Escrow Pay showing the gross amount, platform fees, and final recipient bank account

The PRC mandates digital fiscal clearance under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration Electronic Invoice Service Platform as fully digitalized electronic invoices; the platform assigns a unique invoice code and security stamp and transmits every invoice to the central tax data warehouse in real time before issuance to the counterparty. Business records and customs declaration data are reconciled using the China International Trade Single Window. Any issuance outside the platform, retroactive date-stamping, or mismatch with customs or foreign-exchange settlement triggers automated anti-fraud screening and audit referral.

The STA, GACC and SAFE coordinate substantive checks of export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs declarations, invoices, logistics waybills and foreign-exchange receipt records. An unsubstantiated claim is classified as a false export VAT refund. The responsible exporter must repay the refunded VAT plus statutory late-payment interest; the tax authority will also disallow related input VAT and claw back any refund received within the retrospective assessment period. Administrative penalties range from 50% to 500% of the refunded or evaded amount, and the exporter may be barred from export-rebate treatment for up to three years. Where the quantum exceeds CNY 50,000, criminal liability for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law arises, with imprisonment up to life imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property. SAFE separately penalizes non-repatriation and forged receipt records.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong has no exchange controls and maintains free convertibility for the HKD under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with HKMA managing a USD/HKD narrow band; current account and capital account transactions for the HK entity are freely convertible, subject only to AML/KYC documentary requirements at banks. By contrast, the People's Republic of China operates a managed float with substantial controls on the RMB. The RMB is fully convertible on current account for trade and service transactions, but capital account transactions remain controlled and require SAFE registration, validation of real transaction background, and bank-level supervision. Export proceeds are repatriable into CNY but cannot be arbitrarily converted into offshore foreign exchange without a compliant current account basis. Dividends and profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are treated as FDI-related current account remittances requiring an audited profit statement, tax clearance/residence certificate, and proof of completing foreign exchange registration; in practice, these must be converted through designated FX banks and are subject to anti-round-tripping checks. The JV must document each remittance against a verified underlying contract and can access direct FX hedging only for genuine exposures. HK is the natural clearing gateway; however the mainland JV cannot legally fund offshore liquidity or pay third-country suppliers without meeting SAFE current/capital account codes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE requires all export proceeds realized by the mainland JV for vintage luxury goods to be repatriated to China within 180 days of the export customs declaration. Upon receipt by the designated foreign exchange bank, the exporter must settle the foreign currency into RMB or deposit it in a SAFE-approved current account foreign exchange settlement account up to the account's approved quota. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio for the entire balance; however, any balance exceeding the SAFE-approved settlement account quota must be converted into RMB immediately. For deferred-payment export trade exceeding 180 days, the enterprise must complete deferred-payment registration with SAFE through the bank and obtain a payment period approval before the bank can collect the proceeds. Banks verify electronic customs declarations, logistics documents, invoices, and the trade goods foreign exchange settlement/receipt confirmation form before processing.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the monetary authority for the Hong Kong SAR.

Low - foreign EMIs cannot serve as the primary treasury account for the mainland JV because they are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE for SAFE-controlled FX settlement. Hong Kong licensed virtual banks may support basic business collection and e-commerce settlement, but they do not provide the required commercial LC, capital escrow, and SAFE reporting infrastructure needed for this cross-border JV. Stripe and Escrow_Pay may be used only as customer-facing payment collection rails in the HK e-commerce platform, with final funds settled into the licensed Tier 1 corporate bank account; they cannot clear FDI capital, trade payments, or FX hedging flows.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 1,000,000 for intercompany transactions with related parties in Hong Kong; for cross-border transactions, HKD 10,000,000 for tangible goods transactions and HKD 5,000,000 for financing transactions; HKD 1,000,000 for intangible property transactions and HKD 1,000,000 for management, administrative, and technical service transactions. Under Hong Kong's transfer pricing legislation (Inland Revenue Ordinance Part 8A), taxpayers must prepare contemporaneous documentation if the amount of covered transactions exceeds these thresholds. The threshold is stated in HKD as the local currency.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no formal Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) similar to the EU blacklist rules, but the IRD applies the territorial source doctrine and the General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) under Section 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. A pure shell entity with no office, employees, or decision-making capability in Hong Kong may be disregarded for tax purposes, and profits may be reallocated to the mainland Chinese parent. For the luxury resale e-commerce business, the core income-generating activities (CIGA) include procurement of vintage luxury goods, authentication, inventory management, marketing, and after-sales support. If these activities are performed in China while the Hong Kong entity merely holds title or invoices, the Hong Kong entity may fail the economic substance test and its profits may be taxed in China as a China-controlled foreign corporation or under permanent establishment rules. Maintain an adequate Hong Kong office, employ local staff, make strategic decisions in Hong Kong, and maintain board meeting minutes and employment records.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 61A, the IRD may disregard the artificial transaction or recharacterize the structure. The entity may be assessed on its full profits without the benefit of offshore claim, plus a 100% penalty under Section 82A for incorrect returns. Under the Mainland China-Hong Kong DTA, China may assert that the Hong Kong entity is not the beneficial owner of the income and deny treaty benefits.

Director personal liability: Directors may have the corporate veil pierced under the Companies Ordinance Sections 353-356 for fraudulent trading, rendering them personally liable for the company's debts. Directors could also face a travel ban (blacklisting) under the Inland Revenue Ordinance for outstanding tax liabilities. Criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years may be imposed for tax evasion; if fraudulent trading is proven, directors may be liable for imprisonment of up to 15 years and a fine of HKD 5,000,000.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 50A and the Hong Kong Transfer Pricing Rules (Schedule 17I), the IRD may adjust the consideration to an arm's length amount, disallow the deduction, and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax liability under Section 82A. For severe cases of intentional understatement, a fine of HKD 10,000 and imprisonment for up to 6 months may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign incorrect tax returns may be charged with tax evasion under Section 82, punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years. Where transfer pricing manipulation leads to the company's insolvency, directors may have personal liability for wrongful trading under the Companies Ordinance, requiring them to compensate creditors and potentially face disqualification as directors.
Money Laundering / AML Breach in Online Luxury Resale

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO), a company failing to comply with CDD requirements may be liable to a fine up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for 2 years. For negligent money laundering under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, the maximum fine is HKD 5,000,000 and imprisonment for 15 years. The Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department may also seize counterfeit goods and prosecute trademark offences under the Trade Descriptions Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Under AMLO, directors and officers responsible for the company's AML compliance can be prosecuted personally if they consented or connived in the offence or if the offence is attributable to their neglect. Personal criminal liability includes imprisonment up to 7 years and fines up to HKD 5,000,000. Directors may also be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and disqualified from managing companies, and their personal bank accounts and assets may be frozen under court order.
