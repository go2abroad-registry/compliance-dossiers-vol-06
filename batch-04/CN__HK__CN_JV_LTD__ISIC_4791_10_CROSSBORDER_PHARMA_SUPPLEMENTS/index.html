Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating the Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) online portal and the one-stop business-plus-tax registration service coordinated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR).

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, tax registration is completed automatically as part of the SAMR one-stop incorporation process. The STA assigns a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as both the legal entity identifier and the enterprise Tax Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID). The legal representative and supervisor must complete real-name verification through the Electronic Tax Bureau; the entity is required to file its prescribed tax information, select the general taxation method, and register bank account details. A Hong Kong or other non-resident parent entity without a Chinese establishment is separately registered in the non-resident taxpayer segment of the Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting its home-jurisdiction certificate of incorporation, board resolution appointing a PRC tax representative, and evidence of the cross-border transaction, to obtain a non-resident TIN for withholding and VAT collection obligations. All subsequent filings, payments, and invoice controls are administered through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales of goods, at or above which the taxpayer is required to register as a General VAT Taxpayer; below this amount the entity may be registered as a Small-Scale Taxpayer with simplified tax treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A General VAT Taxpayer that exports physical dietary supplement goods out of the customs territory of China (including exports to Hong Kong SAR under the relevant arrangements) can apply the 0% export VAT rate, subject to: (1) the goods are actually exported under a customs export declaration filed by the exporter; (2) the export transaction is supported by a genuine sales contract and commercial invoice; (3) the sales proceeds are repatriated into a Chinese bank account and verified through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) export collection process; and (4) the exporter submits the required export declaration and documentation in the periodic VAT return to substantiate zero-rating. The zero rate is not available for goods sold within China or where any link in the export chain is fictitious.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form / Bill of Entry) containing the customs-assigned declaration number
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill evidencing the goods left China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank- and SAFE-verified export collection record showing repatriation from the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Export packing list and customs release or inspection documentation

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-certified Electronic Invoice Service Platform under the Golden Tax Project Phase IV. Full Digital Electronic Invoices (DEI) are cleared centrally by the STA in real time; each invoice receives a unique electronic invoice number, a digital audit trail, and an anti-tamper QR code or digital signature upon issuance. Taxpayers and trading partners use the STA-published API specifications for automated issuance, red-letter reversal, query, and archiving. Offline legacy tax-control equipment is being phased out, and any invoice failing real-time fiscal validation is blocked from lawful use.

The STA, General Administration of Customs (GACC), and SAFE may jointly audit the export documentation underlying any zero-rated VAT claim. If the goods were not in fact exported, the proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period, or any document is false or inconsistent, the STA will claw back the zero-rated treatment and assess VAT at the standard rate (currently 13%) with a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Where fraudulent or unverified claims are found, a monetary penalty of between 50% and 500% of the tax evaded may be imposed, and serious cases will be transferred for criminal prosecution for tax evasion and export fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current account items, including trade-related service fees, royalties, and legitimate profit remittances, are generally fully convertible once the underlying transaction is verified through a designated foreign exchange bank and reported to SAFE. Capital account items, such as equity injections, foreign debt, and cross-border guarantees, remain heavily regulated and require case-by-case approval, filing, or quota registration. The Hong Kong side enjoys unrestricted convertibility of the Hong Kong dollar under the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate System, but any capital that ultimately flows into mainland China as registered capital or shareholder loans will be trapped inside the PRC foreign-exchange administrative framework. Profit repatriation from a mainland subsidiary to the HK parent requires an annual audit, tax clearance certificate, and board resolution confirming distributable reserves, and the remittance must be booked with a SAFE purpose code for profit distribution. Surrender requirements apply to certain export proceeds, although the current regulatory trend has relaxed mandatory conversion for many current account receipts. The practical hurdles are documentary; banks must be satisfied on the underlying authenticity, amount, and transaction chain before executing conversion and outward remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in goods trade must be repatriated into China through a designated foreign exchange account within 90 days after the date of customs declaration, unless the exporter has obtained an offshore retention quota or is expressly exempted. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: under current SAFE rules, enterprises may keep export receipts in foreign currency or settle into CNY at their own election, but the receipts must enter the monitored goods-trade verification system. The bank will require the export contract, customs declaration, shipping documents, and a written settlement instruction; any outstanding proceeds older than 90 days without a valid foreign-exchange special deferral will trigger verification classification downgrade and stricter documentary examination.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its administrative arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), exercise statutory foreign exchange controls on the mainland China side. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) functions as the de facto central banking and regulatory authority for Hong Kong SAR, administering the Linked Exchange Rate System without applying exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW as a primary clearing rail, MODERATE only as a supplementary collection channel. In mainland China, foreign fintech EMIs are not authorised to open corporate CNY settlement accounts; cross-border payments must flow through a SAFE-licensed domestic payment institution or a designated bank. In Hong Kong, non-bank EMIs may operate as licensed Money Service Operators under the Customs and Excise Department or as Stored Value Facility licensees under the HKMA, but the vast majority of such platforms impose restrictive risk appetites that exclude nutraceuticals and other high-risk special license e-commerce verticals. Card networks also classify dietary supplements as a high-chargeback merchant category, requiring rolling reserves and three-year processing history. Consequently, EMIs cannot provide capital escrow, documentary credits, or SAFE-compliant profit remittance functionality, and should be capped at API-driven payouts for low-value customer refunds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Routing: the investor executes a SWIFT MT103 from a mainland designated bank to BOCHK or HSBC in Hong Kong with the beneficiary being the CN_JV_LTD HK JV. The MT103 field 72 must quote the SAFE Overseas Direct Investment Filing Certificate and the exact transaction purpose code printed on that certificate, typically 621011 for outbound direct investment equity contribution; any mislabelled purpose code such as consultancy, travel, or advance against sales will be rejected or flagged as trade-based money laundering. After receipt, the HK JV presents the inward MT103 to a certified public accountant for statutory capital verification and issues share certificates only after verification. If the JV requires Renminbi funding, use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or the HKMA RMB RTGS system with the same ODI reference; cash smuggling and informal CNY/HKD exchange channels are absolutely prohibited.Trade disbursements from the HK JV to mainland suppliers or third-country manufacturers should use HK bank clearing rails. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For Hong Kong, the Documentation Threshold under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 and Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (DIPN 59 & 60) is HKD 100 million in total related-party transactions of any type; however, where the entity is a large enterprise with turnover exceeding HKD 200 million and related-party transaction amounts exceed HKD 10 million, enhanced documentation is expected. The statutory local file and master file thresholds are: (1) if total related-party receipts and payments do not exceed HKD 5 million and related-party property transfers do not exceed HKD 10 million, the taxpayer may be excused from full documentation but remains obliged to substantiate arm's length pricing; (2) for purposes of the Economic Substance Requirement under the FSIE regime, where offshore income is claimed, the entity must demonstrate adequate employees, premises, and decision-making activities. A Cross-Border JV engaged in online sales of nutraceuticals with mainland China must prepare a Hong Kong Profits Tax return together with a Transfer Pricing Ledger if intercompany purchases from the mainland parent exceed HK$50 million per year.

Economic substance scope: For Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Doctrine is primarily embodied in the Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime effective January 1, 2023. Under the FSIE regime, a Hong Kong entity claiming exemption for foreign-sourced dividends, interest, disposal gains, or income from intellectual property must satisfy the 'economic substance requirement' in Hong Kong. The relevant test examines whether the taxpayer conducts Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including: (1) holding and managing the investment or intangible assets; (2) making strategic decisions on the acquisition and disposal of the assets; (3) managing and bearing principal risks; and (4) employing a sufficient number of qualified employees and incurring adequate operating expenditure in Hong Kong. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department follows the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) Action 5 framework, including the nexus approach for intellectual property income. A JV that merely holds invoices and contracts while the mainland parent performs all sales, logistics, and procurement operations will fail the economic substance test, lose the offshore claim, and be assessed at the full 16.5% rate. Additionally, the mandatory 'transfer pricing documentation' rule under DIPN 60 requires contemporaneous TP reports and annual TP disclosure forms (IR Form IR51B/46) where connected transactions exceed the threshold.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Fraudulent Offshore Profit Claim and Tax Evasion (Tax Evasion)

Statutory sanction: Sections 82(1) and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112, IRO): upon summary conviction, a fine of HKD 50,000 plus a penalty of three times the amount of tax undercharged and imprisonment for 3 years; upon conviction on indictment, a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for 7 years. Under Section 80(2), failure to file a return without reasonable excuse carries a fine of HKD 10,000 and threefold undercharged tax. The IRD may also issue a tax default notice and impose additional tax up to 200% of the tax undercharged where the error is due to fraud or wilful evasion. The offence is not compoundable by payment of penalty alone.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or authorise the false tax return are personally liable to criminal prosecution under Section 82(1) IRO, even if the company is the taxpayer. Upon conviction, personal imprisonment of up to 7 years applies. The IRD may issue a departure prohibition order under Section 76A, prohibiting the director from leaving Hong Kong without paying outstanding tax. The court may order the director to pay restitution equal to the tax evaded through confiscation proceedings under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455), and civil recovery may pierce the corporate veil where the company was used as a sham to evade tax, allowing the Inland Revenue Department to seize personal assets of the director including real estate and bank accounts. A director may also be disqualified from being a company director under section 168E of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for up to 15 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion (Tax Evasion / Transfer Pricing)

Statutory sanction: Under Section 50AAK of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (TP rules), the Commissioner may adjust the taxpayer's assessable profits to reflect the arm's length condition and impose additional tax not exceeding 100% of the undercharged tax under Section 82A. Where the company files incorrect returns without reasonable excuse, a fine of HKD 10,000 plus threefold the tax undercharged applies. For wilful intent to evade tax, Section 82(1) carries imprisonment of up to 7 years and a fine of HKD 100,000. Failure to maintain contemporaneous TP documentation does not itself attract a fixed penalty, but the burden of proof shifts to the taxpayer, and the IRD may draw adverse inferences and apply penalty rates up to 200% of the tax differential.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly involved in the preparation or approval of pricing arrangements that produce an artificial reduction of Hong Kong taxable profits may be personally liable under the common law tort of wrongful trading and under Sections 90 and 124 of the Companies Ordinance for making false statements. The IRD may pierce the corporate veil and seek civil recovery against directors where the entity is a mere façade for the mainland parent's business. Directors face personal civil liability for the underpaid tax plus interest under Section 82A, and the Inland Revenue Ordinance permits the Commissioner to issue a certificate of uncollectible tax against a responsible person, allowing the IRD to pursue personal bankruptcy proceedings. On criminal conviction, a director may be imprisoned for up to 7 years, fined HKD 100,000, and disqualified from acting as a director for 15 years. Additionally, if the mainland parent is listed in Hong Kong or on a US exchange, false intercompany documentation may trigger Securities and Futures Commission (SFC) enforcement, and directors may be banned from the securities industry.
Illicit Pharmaceutical Marketing and AML-Compliance Failure (Pharmaceutical Regulatory / AML / Shell Company)

Statutory sanction: Under the Chinese Medicine Ordinance (Cap. 549), selling unregistered proprietary Chinese medicine or unlicensed retail of Chinese medicine is punishable by a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for 2 years per offence; continuing offences incur a daily fine. Under the Undesirable Medical Advertisements Ordinance (Cap. 231), advertising any drug or supplement with claims to treat specified diseases is punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for 2 years. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) Section 9, failure to conduct customer due diligence or file a Suspicious Transaction Report (STR) for a non-financial business or professional is liable to a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment of 3 months on summary conviction; for wilful fraud, the maximum is a fine of HKD 1 million and imprisonment of 2 years. If the entity is deemed a shell company without economic substance, the Registrar of Companies may strike it off under Section 746 of the Companies Ordinance, and the IRD will disallow all claimed expenses and assess profits on a deemed basis under Section 39E.

The Companies Court may additionally require the director to make personal contribution to the company's assets under Section 101 (wrongful trading).
