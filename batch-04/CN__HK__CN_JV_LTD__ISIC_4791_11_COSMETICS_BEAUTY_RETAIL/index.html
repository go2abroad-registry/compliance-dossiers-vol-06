Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Hong Kong company must have a registered office at a physical street address in Hong Kong; a mere mail-forwarding or commercial mailbox is not acceptable as the sole face of the business. For an ISIC 4791 / NAICS 456120 online beauty DTC retail structure, the Inland Revenue Department and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority will reject a virtual-office-only arrangement if the claimed management and control sit outside Hong Kong. A licensed Trust or Company Service Provider can supply a lawful virtual registered office with street presence for the Companies Registry and corporate secretary obligations, but banks and tax authorities will regard it as insufficient commercial substance unless supplemented by local management decisions, staff, business records, board minutes signed in Hong Kong, and a genuine physical presence from which the e-commerce activities are directed. Therefore, for Stage 1, a virtual office may be used temporarily, but the final due-diligence opinion must condition bank onboarding and tax-residency filings on the early establishment of a physical Hong Kong office and local operational substance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer. Below this threshold, the entity may remain a small-scale taxpayer (simplified VAT collection), although voluntary general registration is possible.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of cosmetics and personal care products from China to overseas territories qualify for VAT zero-rating if the seller is a registered VAT general taxpayer (or holds an export enterprise registration), the goods are actually declared and cleared by Chinese customs for export, ownership passes to an overseas buyer, and the export sales proceeds are repatriated to China in freely convertible foreign currency within the statutory time frame. The exporter must issue an ordinary commercial invoice and retain the full export document package; input VAT on goods exported is generally rebatable but the refund must be substantiated with the same export evidence.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration form with customs clearance endorsement and release seal)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of the goods out of China
  • Foreign Exchange Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank statement/SAFE cross-border income declaration evidencing receipt of export proceeds from overseas
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the overseas buyer
  • Packing List
  • Export Contract or Sales Agreement

All VAT invoices, including electronic invoices, must be issued through the STA's National VAT Invoice System. Invoices are transmitted in real time using tax-control equipment or the e-invoice service platform, which assigns a unique verification code and anti-counterfeiting stamp. General VAT taxpayers must use special VAT invoices to claim input VAT credits; the STA monitors, validates and reconciles invoice data through an online clearance mechanism.

Claimed export zero-rating is subject to documentary audit by the tax and customs authorities. If a zero-rated export claim is unsupported or fraudulent, the exporter must repay the VAT that would otherwise have been due, plus daily late-payment surcharges, and is liable for administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the understated VAT under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Cases of deliberate export-tax-refund fraud fall under the Criminal Law (Article 204) and can lead to imprisonment, forfeiture of wrongfully obtained refunds, and revocation of export tax refund qualifications.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating RMB regime with current-account convertibility and capital-account restrictions. The PBOC and SAFE exercise strict rules over capital flows; a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operating under a Hong Kong treasury structure must distinguish onshore RMB settlement from offshore HKD/USD movements. Current-account items, including trade remittances, refunds, and service fees, are generally convertible provided real transactional evidence is presented. Capital-account items, including registered capital injection, cross-border loans, equity transfers, and dividend distributions, require SAFE registration and designated bank approvals. Profit remittance from the mainland JV to its Hong Kong parent is permissible but subject to annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, withholding tax under the applicable double-taxation arrangement, and SAFE verification of no unreconciled foreign exchange liabilities. The joint venture may also hold offshore accounts in Hong Kong only with prior approval where required. Consumer refunds and chargebacks flowing through Shopify Payments and PayPal are treated as current-account/service-related receivables and must settle through onshore banks with valid purpose codes, matching the original export declaration to avoid trade mismatch penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current account administration, export proceeds from cosmetics retail must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration, or within 180 days from the date of overseas receipt where no customs declaration applies. No mandatory conversion ratio is applied: the JV may retain 100% of export proceeds in USD/HKD/EUR or voluntarily convert into RMB at the onshore market rate. If converted, the onshore bank will complete a Bank Foreign Exchange Settlement Form and verify the trade declaration number. A discrepancy between the customs-declared value and actual remittance exceeding 20% will trigger SAFE investigation; therefore all commercial invoices, logistics bills, payment gateway statements, and customs records must be retained for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the People's Republic of China, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the banking and clearing authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For the HK parent injecting equity into the mainland JV, the parties must first complete SAFE FDI registration and open a designated capital-account foreign exchange settlement bank in mainland China. The HK treasury vehicle will remit HKD/USD via SWIFT MT103 with an appropriate investment purpose code, typically 'investment in an equity joint venture', citing the SAFE registration number and the PRC entity name in the remittance message. Upon receipt, the onshore bank validates the capital-account business card, converts the foreign currency into RMB if required, and deposits into the RMB capital account. Funds in the capital account may be used only for operating expenses approved by SAFE; any change requires further filing. A certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report confirming that registered capital has been paid in full; this report is required for the company's annual filing and subsequent dividend remittance.Suppliers of cosmetics are paid via SWIFT MT103 for direct USD/HKD settlement, commercial letters of credit at sight for larger raw-material orders, and documentary collections for recurring small-lot purchases. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Moderate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 100,000,000 aggregate amount of controlled transactions in each category (property transfers, financial transactions, intangible property transfers, and other transactions) for Local File; Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue is at least HKD 6,800,000,000 and the Hong Kong entity enters into controlled transactions above the sector-specific threshold.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the territorial source principle and anti-avoidance provisions (IRO Sections 61 and 61A) require economic substance for: (i) claiming non-taxation of offshore profits; (ii) establishing Hong Kong tax residence for treaty benefits; (iii) defeating artificial or sham transactions. The IRD applies a Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test to determine the actual place of business operations. For e-commerce, CIGA includes where sales contracts are executed, where inventory is held, where marketing and customer support are performed, and where key management decisions are taken. A pure shell company without office, staff, or CIGA in Hong Kong will have its offshore claims and treaty benefits denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Section 82: on summary conviction - fine up to HKD 10,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months; on indictment - fine up to HKD 50,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged and imprisonment up to 3 years. Section 80 imposes penalties for failure to furnish returns or incorrect returns without reasonable excuse: fine up to HKD 10,000 and treble the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false return are personally liable as principals. The IRD may assess the director personally for the undercharged tax if the director knowingly participated. Civil proceedings may include asset freezes, departure prohibition orders preventing travel outside Hong Kong, and restitution orders for the unpaid tax. Criminal conviction leads to imprisonment and automatic disqualification from being a director under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for up to 5 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: IRO Section 58C: failure to maintain TP documentation attracts a fixed penalty of up to HKD 50,000 per offence. If incorrect returns are filed and documentation is not provided, Section 82A allows additional tax of up to 10% of the undercharged tax. The IRD may adjust profits under Section 50AAP (arm's length pricing) and impose interest on overdue tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who are knowingly involved in creating or implementing a non-arm's-length scheme may be personally liable for the additional tax and interest. Courts can make personal payment orders and may issue a prohibition order against a director leaving Hong Kong if he fails to pay. If fraud is proven, imprisonment up to 3 years and disqualification as a director may be imposed; the director's own assets may be seized to satisfy the tax debt.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The China-Hong Kong DTA (Article 27) may deny treaty benefits where the principal purpose of the arrangement is to obtain a treaty advantage. The IRD can disregard the whole structure under Section 61A and assess profits as if the business were carried on in Hong Kong. Mainland China may impose a 10% withholding tax (instead of 5% dividends or royalties), plus late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and a penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the tax shortfall under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. In Hong Kong, making a false statement to the IRD attracts a fine of up to HKD 50,000 and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify a false residency claim or sign an offshore declaration are jointly and severally liable for the full tax avoided. In China, directors may face a travel ban (exit restriction) and be listed in the 'Serious Dishonesty List' (shixin). Criminal exposure includes fraud under the PRC Criminal Law (up to 7 years imprisonment for tax evasion) and under Hong Kong's Theft Ordinance or Crimes Ordinance (up to 14 years for obtaining a pecuniary advantage by deception). Directors' personal assets can be frozen in both jurisdictions.
