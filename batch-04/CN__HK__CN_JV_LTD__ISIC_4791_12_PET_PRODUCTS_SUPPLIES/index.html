Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC VAT zero-rating applies to exports of goods when the goods are genuinely discharged from the customs territory of the People's Republic of China, the exporting taxpayer has obtained the export customs declaration, retains valid VAT invoices evidencing input tax, and the sales proceeds are repatriated into China through foreign exchange channels within the statutory period. Qualifying offshore digital services may be eligible for VAT zero-rating or exemption if the service is performed outside China and the recipient is outside China, subject to STA cross-border service rules. Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, so no zero-rating claim is necessary for the Hong Kong entity's offshore sales.

  • Export Sales Contract
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher issued by a designated bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds

Mainland China requires electronic VAT invoicing through the STA electronic invoice platform and the Golden Tax System (Phase IV), with invoice data transmitted in near real-time using approved tax control equipment or electronic invoice solutions; export e-commerce invoices must reconcile with the China Customs cross-border e-commerce retail export declaration data. Hong Kong imposes no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate.

The STA and Customs conduct risk-based matching of export declarations, logistics bills, payment data, and foreign exchange receipts. If any document is false, incomplete, or inconsistent, the export zero-rating or refund will be denied and previously refunded VAT may be clawed back. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent export tax refunds attract a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax involved, daily interest on unpaid tax, suspension of export tax refund qualification, and referral for criminal prosecution in serious cases. Hong Kong may impose penalties for false statements under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) where Hong Kong entity tax filings are affected.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. For the current account, the RMB is convertible for bona fide trade and service transactions under the unified Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures, subject to genuine transaction background verification and filing through the SAFE system. However, the capital account is not freely convertible; cross-border equity injections into a mainland China sino-foreign equity joint venture, shareholder loans, repatriation of capital, and foreign exchange hedging transactions require prior SAFE registration, specialized accounts, and verification of the actual transaction. Export proceeds from the JV's e-commerce sales of pet products and care devices into mainland China must be repatriated into a domestic foreign currency account, and the JV must complete export proceeds collection verification within the statutory time limit. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder entails an audited financial statement, tax clearance proof, and SAFE verification of the dividend resolution, and any contemporary RMB cross-border settlement under the Cross-Border RMB Settlement Pilot can reduce conversion risk only when the underlying trade and service invoices are fully compliant. The Hong Kong SAR maintains full currency convertibility for the HKD under Article 112 of the Basic Law, with the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate System pegging HKD to USD within the band of 7.75 to 7.85. The treasury obstacle is therefore not in Hong Kong but in mainland China: the JV must use SAFE-authorized banks for capital injection and profit repatriation, maintain strict purpose codes for each cross-border flow, and cannot rely on an offshore EMI for mainland-side legal tender conversion. Nevertheless, the JV can maintain a treasury in Hong Kong in USD and HKD with no surrender requirements whatsoever, while the mainland operating entity must comply with surrender rules and capital verification duties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Regulations on Foreign Exchange Administration of Export Proceeds in Trade, export foreign exchange receipts from pet product e-commerce sales must be repatriated to a domestic foreign currency account within the statutory timeframe (generally within 90 days from the date of export or as otherwise stipulated by SAFE Customs Reconciliation). For current account receipts deemed to have genuine trade background, the enterprise is required to complete verification and may retain the proceeds in foreign currency; however, where the enterprise opts to settle into RMB, the conversion must be processed through a designated bank under the matching purpose code. Compulsory surrender of export proceeds to the central bank was abolished under China's foreign exchange reforms, yet the JV must satisfy the payment-versus-payment matching rule, ensure the reported export value reconciles with customs data and logistics invoices, and remit any surplus or overdue verification items within the SAFE surveillance deadlines.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the corresponding authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low feasibility for the mainland China operating entity and moderate viability only for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle. Mainland China's foreign exchange administration framework bars unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs from operating domestic currency accounts, accessing the CIPS/SAFE reporting rails, or performing capital verification for a sino-foreign equity joint venture. An offshore EMI such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Payoneer can legally hold HKD/USD for the Hong Kong entity and process payouts to the mainland JV only through a licensed settlement bank or the JV's own SAFE-registered foreign currency account. The JV itself cannot use the EMI to receive export proceeds from mainland e-commerce sales because collection must be settled directly into a domestic designated foreign exchange account and verified by SAFE. Therefore the cross-border treasury stack should be built on Tier-1 licensed bank accounts; EMIs are relegated to ancillary expense cards and merchant settlement acceleration for the Hong Kong sales entity, never as the primary custody or clearing rail for mainland regulatory compliance purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection from the Hong Kong shareholder into the mainland JV must be routed through a SAFE-designated domestic bank account of the joint venture using SWIFT MT103 with a maximum transparency of the remitter and beneficiary. The remitting instruction must cite the Foreign Exchange Code for Capital Account Transactions, typically purpose code 622 (Injection of Foreign Currency Capital by a Foreign Investor) or the appropriate code for the equity joint venture registered capital, alongside the enterprise's Foreign Currency Registration Certificate issued by SAFE. Before the injection, the JV must complete the FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) foreign exchange registration with the local SAFE branch or the bank-holding SAFE authority, open a Capital Account (capital injection foreign exchange settlement account) in the joint venture name, and, after receipt of funds, apply for capital verification through an appointed domestic capital verification accountant. The Hong Kong remitter will complete the corporate transfer by SWIFT, capturing the invoice number and entry name in the beneficiary information or "purpose of payment" field, ensuring the remitting account is in the name of the legal shareholder. For the Hong Kong entity's operational USD or HKD funding to pay suppliers in mainland China or third-party merchants, the parent company may make a payment under the goods trade settlement current account using purpose code 101 (Trade in Goods) or a services code for commissions or logistics, but it must be accompanied by the underlying invoice and logistics contract if the mainland JV is the collecting entity. No escrow service, PPU card net settlement, or crypto route may be used for injected equity capital because the SAFE designated bank will only accept physical wire transfers from the shareholder's licensed bank account.The JV should use the following clearing hierarchy for outbound trade disbursements: (1) for direct supplier payments in USD, EUR, or RMB to manufacturer accounts, standard SWIFT MT103 with full field 59 beneficiary identification, field 70 purpose of payment, and reference to the purchase contract, preferably executed through the Hong Kong treasury company in order to benefit from immediate HKD/USD clearing in the HKMA's interbank system; (2) for bulk and repetitive inventory procurement from established pet product manufacturers, an irrevocable confirmed commercial letter of credit under UCP600 issued by the Hong Kong bank in favor of the supplier, with the Hong Kong entity as applicant and the mainland JV as consignee, to reduce counterparty risk and provide financing against the bill of lading; (3) for smaller open-account transactions, documentary collections (D/P and D/A) based on the pet product purchase invoice, packing list, and transport document, which are lower-cost than LCs though present a higher payment default risk and require the HK company to maintain adequate transaction proof; and (4) for marketplace payout settlement from Stripe and PayPal, the treasury must consolidate funds through the Hong Kong operational account and make any onward transfer to the mainland JV under a genuine service or goods settlement agreement using the required purpose code, rather than treating the PSP as a clearing bank.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts with a SAFE-authorized Tier-1 bank in mainland China, available to the JV based on genuine trade background and closed to pure speculationCross-currency swaps between RMB and USD or HKD and USD for the joint venture's capex and shareholder loans, subject to SAFE registration and underlying debt approvalNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in offshore Hong Kong referencing USD/CNY for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle when the mainland JV cannot access onshore hedging or to hedge residual conversion risk on non-capped flowsHKMA-regulated FX spot and forward transactions through licensed Hong Kong banks; no options or exotic crypto derivatives are permissible under the entity's current treasury policy

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low feasibility for the mainland China operating entity and moderate viability only for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle. Mainland China's foreign exchange administration framework bars unlicensed offshore fintech EMIs from operating domestic currency accounts, accessing the CIPS/SAFE reporting rails, or performing capital verification for a sino-foreign equity joint venture. An offshore EMI such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Payoneer can legally hold HKD/USD for the Hong Kong entity and process payouts to the mainland JV only through a licensed settlement bank or the JV's own SAFE-registered foreign currency account. The JV itself cannot use the EMI to receive export proceeds from mainland e-commerce sales because collection must be settled directly into a domestic designated foreign exchange account and verified by SAFE. Therefore the cross-border treasury stack should be built on Tier-1 licensed bank accounts; EMIs are relegated to ancillary expense cards and merchant settlement acceleration for the Hong Kong sales entity, never as the primary custody or clearing rail for mainland regulatory compliance purposes.
