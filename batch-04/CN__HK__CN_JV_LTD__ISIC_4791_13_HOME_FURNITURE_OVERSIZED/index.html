E-government filing portal

The CN JV itself is incorporated as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the PRC Foreign Investment Law. Its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, an 18-digit taxpayer identification number) is automatically obtained along with the business license from the Administration for Market Regulation through the one-stop multi-certificate integration process. The JV must complete tax registration at the competent local STA office via the Electronic Taxpayer Service System, activating corporate income tax, VAT, and payroll withholding profiles. For the Hong Kong non-resident investor, the procedure requires submission to the local STA office controlling the JV of: notarised Hong Kong incorporation certificates and certificate of current status, board resolutions identifying the PRC payer and income types, and the prescribed non-resident enterprise tax registration statement. This yields a separate non-resident TIN used for withholding on dividends, interest, royalties, and any service income made in China. Where the Hong Kong investor is eligible and not engaged in a permanent establishment, a status certificate must be obtained from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department for treaty relief.

Entity_Level_Taxation for the Mainland China JV; the Hong Kong partner remains on Territorial_Scope for Hong Kong profits tax if its central management and control is not in China.

Available election forms

None. The PRC system does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity classification elections. Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for compulsory VAT general taxpayer registration in Mainland China; below that a small-scale taxpayer regime may apply, but the JV should register as a general taxpayer because of cross-border export rebate needs. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The JV may apply the 0% VAT export rate to oversized furniture and home decor that are sold to non-resident buyers, physically exported from Mainland China, declared to and cleared by China Customs, and where the sales consideration is received from outside the PRC through approved foreign exchange channels. Qualifying cross-border B2C e-commerce retail exports using the comprehensive pilot policy can claim zero-rating or exemption with simplified customs declaration if the goods are within catalogue limits and the order, payment, logistics and customs data are fully synchronised. Offshore digital services are not in scope for this furniture business.

  • China Customs Export Declaration Form (equivalent to an export bill of entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from Mainland China
  • Commercial invoice issued by the CN JV to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list for oversized/custom freight
  • Export VAT invoice issued at 0% through the fully digitalised electronic invoice platform
  • Export Proceeds Collection Record in the SAFE digital administration system or Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) equivalent
  • Signed sales contract or purchase order with incoterms
  • Third-party logistics / handover receipt from the cross-border e-commerce or freight-forwarder operating the oversized freight channel

All VAT invoices issued by the CN JV must be generated through the State Taxation Administration's Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice Service Platform and transmitted in real time to the STA's Golden Tax System Phase IV for digital validation. The platform issues a unique invoice number, encrypted tax-control digital signature and QR code for anti-fraud verification. No separate physical fiscal stamp is required; tax authorities are automatically notified of invoice issuance and can reject transactions that fail structural validation. Cross-border e-commerce retail export orders must additionally be reported in the Customs cross-border e-commerce system so that the zero-rated export invoices reconcile with customs and forex data.

The STA, SAFE and China Customs jointly screen export zero-rating claims. If the documentation bundle is incomplete or the export proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the STA may retrospectively disallow the 0% rate, re-assess the sale at 13% VAT, issue a clawback of any refund paid and charge late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Under the PRC Law on Administration of Tax Levy, under-declared VAT can attract a penalty between 50% and 500% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulently obtaining an export tax refund with false documentation or concealed shipment data is punishable by full recovery of the refund, a penalty of between one and five times the refund obtained and criminal referral for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under the PRC Criminal Law, including imprisonment for responsible officers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates an open capital and current account with no exchange controls. The HKD is freely convertible and, under the Linked Exchange Rate System, is managed by the HKMA within a range of 7.75 to 7.85 HKD/USD. The territory has no general prohibition on executing cross-border payments, no requirement to surrender export proceeds, and no prior exchange-control permission or central bank approval for dividends, operating profits, or loan repayments to non-resident shareholders. In practice, the JV's ability to remit profit depends on solvency and prudential tests under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance and on corporate governance authorization, not on FX convertibility. The critical friction arises from the mainland China leg: a Chinese shareholder's initial capital contribution into Hong Kong must be routed through SAFE-approved banking channels and, if required, registered as an overseas direct investment, and subsequent dividends repatriated to the mainland parent require current-account documentation and proof of distributable profits. For the Hong Kong entity itself, funds may be held in HKD, USD, RMB, or any freely convertible currency; there are no surrender requirements. Convertibility is therefore full in Hong Kong, with only administrative and documentary obstacles in the PRC leg.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR: there is no statutory timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign-currency receipts. Export proceeds may be retained freely in any convertible currency. If the mainland parent exports and repatriates proceeds through the PRC banking system, SAFE goods-trade verification rules, including reconciliation against customs declarations within the applicable period, will apply to the PRC entity rather than to the Hong Kong JV.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking institution for Hong Kong SAR; for mainland China cross-border capital outflows, the People's Bank of China through State Administration of Foreign Exchange (PBoC/SAFE) is the controlling authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for unlicensed offshore digital EMIs: an overseas EMI cannot legally take deposits or provide banking services in Hong Kong without being an authorized institution under the Banking Ordinance, and non-bank payment firms may only be stored-value facility licensees with narrower remittance functions. Medium for HKMA-licensed virtual banks for basic deposit or collection accounts, but such virtual banks are generally not suitable for cross-border escrow, trade finance, and high-value furniture settlement. The conservative treasury route is to use licensed Hong Kong Tier-1 commercial banks with corresponding PRC and global banking relationships.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A compliant inbound equity injection from the mainland shareholder must use SWIFT MT103 from a SAFE-licensed PRC bank, showing in field 70 the exact reference to 'equity subscription under JV agreement' and the SAFE outward-investment registration code. The funds should land only into a dedicated Capital Escrow Account at a Tier-1 Hong Kong bank such as Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited, not into the operating account. The bank will hold the funds pending a licensed CPA's capital-contribution attestation; after attestation, the funds are transferred to the operating account as paid-up capital. Hong Kong has no statutory minimum capital or mandatory capital-verification certificate, but the JV bank will require a payment-purpose declaration, resolution of the board of directors, JV contract article on capital schedule, and copies of the mainland shareholder's SAFE or ODI certificate. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$20,000,000 aggregate covered intercompany transactions in the accounting period, with separate lower thresholds for financing and intangible property transfers, as specified in the IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Note No. 58 and IRO section 50AAB; MNE groups with consolidated revenue above HK$6,800,000,000 must also prepare Master File and Country-by-Country Report.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations statute, but the territorial-source doctrine, IRO sections 61/61A anti-avoidance law, and the China-HK DTA beneficial ownership framework require genuine economic substance. For oversized-furniture online retail, Core Income-Generating Activities include the negotiation and acceptance of customer orders, inventory management, price-setting, logistics and freight control for oversized goods, handling of returns, warranty obligations, and merchant payment agreements with Stripe and Affirm. If these functions are performed by the Chinese principals while the Hong Kong company receives the residual profit, the IRD may reallocate profits to the true economic owner and deny treaty or offshore-source benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Artificial Treaty Claim

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO sections 61 and 61A to disregard the shell transaction, re-source the profit to mainland China, deny the territorial-source exemption, and assess full 16.5% profits tax together with a penalty under IRO section 80(2) of 3 times the tax undercharged; civil recovery of the underpaid tax may be pursued as a debt.

Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to piercing of the corporate veil if the Hong Kong entity is used as a sham. They may be held personally liable for restitution of the evaded corporate tax, subject to a departure-prevention direction/blacklist order prohibiting travel outside Hong Kong pending tax settlement, and face up to 3 years' imprisonment for conspiracy to defraud the Inland Revenue Ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Cross-Border Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRO section 50AAB permits the IRD to make transfer pricing adjustments to arm's-length consideration and recover additional profits tax; failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation triggers adverse inference and higher penalties under IRO section 80(2), including treble tax penalties; group companies may also be subject to DTA claim denial and PRC special tax adjustment.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false profits tax returns as to the arm's-length nature of related-party transactions may be personally assessed for the impost and disqualified as directors under the Companies Ordinance. The courts can issue asset-freezing and travel-restraining orders preventing the directors from leaving Hong Kong while the tax is outstanding, and a conviction for fraudulent evasion under IRO section 82A carries imprisonment for up to 3 years.
AML / False Accounting and Revenue Suppression

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) provides for fines of up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years for non-compliance with customer due diligence and record-keeping; false accounting and fraudulent tax returns attract a 3x tax penalty plus a fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment under IRO section 82A.

Director personal liability: Directors will be disqualified and the corporate veil pierced to reach their personal assets where they knowingly convert or conceal turnover; they may be placed on the IRD departure blacklist, their passports refused on leaving Hong Kong during tax investigation, and they face criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years under the AMLO or 3 years under tax evasion law, together with joint and several civil liability for third-party payment losses.
