Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital for a private company limited by shares and no statutory blocked escrow account or capital verification requirement. The company may be incorporated with nil paid-up capital. If the shareholders choose to subscribe for paid-up capital, any contribution must be settled in the company's bank account and recorded in the financial statements in accordance with Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China has an administered, managed-float foreign exchange system. The Renminbi (RMB/CNY) is fully convertible on current account for genuine trade and service items, but capital account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, licensing, and supervisory approval. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture ('CN_JV_LTD'), each inward capital contribution in USD, HKD or CNH must be routed through a Foreign Exchange Capital Account opened at a SAFE-designated bank. The foreign exchange receipts of the joint venture must be paid into an onshore Foreign Exchange Settlement Account or an approved cross-border payment channel, verified against customs export declarations, and then retained or settled at the exporter's choice. Profit remittance to the foreign shareholder is current-account convertible after annual audit, tax settlement, retained-earnings declaration and bank verification, but it requires the submission of a complete tax clearance certificate and audited financials. Capital-account outflows such as foreign debt repayment, equity redemption, cross-border financing and capital injection refunds require separate SAFE filings, macro-prudential limits and bank-level post-settlement monitoring. The Hong Kong leg is separate: the HKD is fully convertible and linked to the USD through the HKMA's Currency Board system, while CNH (offshore Renminbi) trades freely in Hong Kong. Nevertheless, CNH cannot be repatriated to the onshore CNY system without a genuine underlying current or capital transaction. In practice, the JV cannot rely on fintech EMI balances to satisfy PRC FX controls because the onshore bank is the sole regulated clearing interface for SAFE purposes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC law no longer imposes mandatory surrender of export proceeds or a mandatory conversion ratio; the statutory conversion ratio is 0%. Export receipts must be collected from overseas buyers through the onshore foreign exchange settlement account of the exporting entity, an approved agent, or a licensed cross-border e-commerce payment institution and be connected to customs export declaration data. The generally accepted statutory filing/repatriation framework requires proceeds to be returned to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless the bank approves a longer period for trade-financing reasons. Once received, the JV may hold the foreign currency indefinitely or convert it into CNY; each conversion must be supported by the underlying trade declaration and recorded in SAFE's monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and Hong Kong's Linked Exchange Rate system governing the offshore Hong Kong dollar clearing leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs and online payment facilitators such as Stripe, PayPal, Airwallex, and Revolut Business have Low onshore viability as primary bank relationships for CN_JV_LTD. They are not licensed banks and are legally barred from opening the SAFE-regulated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, Basic Deposit Account, or export proceeds settlement account for a mainland Chinese entity. They may act only as front-end payment gateways/aggregators for online card payments and must settle the net proceeds into the JV's designated onshore bank account through a licensed cross-border payment institution with recognised customs/tax/FX data. The JV still carries all statutory compliance responsibilities for import/export declaration, SAFE reporting, corporate income tax, and invoice reconciliation. On the Hong Kong side, an EMI can support the offshore treasury vehicle's current-account expenses, but it cannot serve as the capital-verification bank for the China JV nor execute SAFE-compliant capital repatriation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound registered-capital injections from the foreign shareholder must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the JV's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the selected onshore bank, with the payment message clearly quoting the SAFE FDI registration number and the standard BOP transaction code for foreign-invested enterprise paid-in capital. The remitting bank and receiving bank must match the beneficiary name, FX account number, and purpose-of-payment text; the receiving bank will update the SAFE foreign investment system and complete a post-arrival verification. After the funds are credited, the JV can apply for foreign-exchange settlement into CNY for operating purposes, but every post-settlement payment requires the underlying voucher to be uploaded to the bank's system and must comply with the negative list for foreign-invested enterprises (no transfer to prohibited sectors, no speculative wealth-management products outside the permitted scope). The formal capital verification is then completed by an independent PRC accounting firm, which issues a capital verification report against bank statements, the board's equity subscription agreement, and SAFE records; this report supports the market supervision authority's paid-in-capital filing and the Customs/Tax bureau updates.For settlement of overseas supplier invoices and cross-border trade expenses, the JV will primarily use SWIFT MT103 for open-account small-value payments, an irrevocable documentary commercial letter of credit (LC) issued via the bank's trade desk for large recurring inventory purchases, and documentary collections (Documents against Payment / Documents against Acceptance) for trusted counterparties. Every outbound remittance must be supported by the underlying trade contract, commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or air waybill, customs export/import declaration, and Chinese tax-compliant VAT documentation. E-commerce refunds and chargebacks to customers should be processed back through the original Stripe/PayPal settlement pipeline or the bank's refund channel using the original transaction reference, and must be reconciled monthly so that SAFE trade statistics remain accurate and no residue is left in non-bank payment wallets. 10% dividend withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a PRC enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-HK Double Tax Arrangement where the beneficial owner is an HK company directly holding at least 25% of the JV's capital throughout the 365 days preceding the dividend payment.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions with the HK affiliate exceed RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for intra-group services; Master File obligations follow the group-wide transaction thresholds under the same regime.

Economic substance scope: The HK-incorporated investor must demonstrate real economic substance and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong to obtain treaty benefits and to avoid PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule recharacterization under EIT Law Art. 47. The PRC JV itself must carry on its principal revenue-generating activities in China through staff, physical inventory and fulfillment operations; a shell company lacking core income-generating activities will be disregarded for tax purposes, income may be attributed to the entity that actually performs the functions, and treaty relief may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent E-commerce Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 and Tax Administration Law Art. 63. Tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and exceeding 10% of tax payable is punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years; if the amount exceeds RMB 500,000 and exceeds 30% of tax payable, imprisonment is 3 to 7 years. The tax authority also recovers the evaded tax and imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Art. 20, controlling shareholders and directors who abuse the limited-liability corporate form to evade tax debts face veil piercing and joint personal liability for company tax liabilities and civil restitution. The legal representative and actual controller will be placed on the serious-tax-violators blacklist, triggering departure bans, restriction on high-consumption activities, asset freezes, enforcement against personal property and criminal imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping Through HK Affiliate

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Art. 41 and Tax Administration Law Art. 36 authorize tax authorities to adjust taxable income by applying the arm's-length principle. Late-payment interest is imposed at the PBOC benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points from the filing date to the adjustment date; penalties for failure to maintain documentation may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute fraudulent intercompany agreements or falsify transfer pricing documentation can be jointly liable for adjusted tax and civil damages. If the transfer pricing adjustment is escalated to tax evasion, the directors involved face personal asset restitution, travel bans and blacklisting, disqualification from serving as directors, and imprisonment under Criminal Law Art. 201.
Withholding Tax Default and Abusive Treaty Relief

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Art. 69 imposes a fine of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld. The JV remains liable to recover the underpaid tax from the non-resident payee; if the treaty claim is denied, the full 10% statutory WHT is payable with daily late-payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance director and responsible officers can be held personally liable for unremitted withholding tax and fines when their unlawful instructions cause the default. They face civil asset seizure, exit-control and blacklisting by tax and border authorities, debarment from leaving China until tax is settled, and criminal prosecution for deliberate failure to withhold followed by fraudulent concealment.
