E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its provincial/municipal counterpart, at which point the PRC authority issues the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which constitutes the statutory PRC Taxpayer Identification Number. Within 30 calendar days after business-license issuance, the JV must complete taxpayer registration with the STA through the Electronic Tax Bureau, providing the SAMR business license, articles of association, legal representative identity evidence, registered address proof, bank account filings, accounting-principal details, and expected VAT taxpayer classification. The STA will register the JV as a PRC resident enterprise and activate the relevant VAT invoice and filing system. A non-resident foreign enterprise without a permanent establishment in China is not issued a separate PRC TIN for passive income; instead, the JV as withholding agent must register non-resident withholding particulars through the e-Tax Bureau and file declarations using the Hong Kong investor's foreign tax identification reference. If the non-resident foreign enterprise establishes its own branch or permanent establishment in China, it must obtain a separate USCC-based tax registration through the same e-Tax Bureau procedure.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a fully convertible currency regime within the RMB offshore and global dollar clearing system. The HKD/USD exchange rate is anchored to a tight band of 7.75 to 7.85, but funds can move freely across current and capital accounts with no pre-approval from the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. There are no surrender requirements, no repatriation restrictions, and no profit remittance hurdles for a Hong Kong-incorporated joint venture. The legal entity may hold multi-currency accounts, deploy liquidity across time zones, and remit dividends or repayment of shareholders loans at will. In contrast, the mainland China component exposes the enterprise to People's Bank of China/SAFE controls: the RMB is convertible on current account for genuine trade, while capital account transactions must satisfy real-deal and real-use checks. Mainland profit remittance requires an audited financial statement, the tax clearance document, a capital account payment application, and the bank's post-verification. Therefore, the HK treasury is the unconstrained clearing core while the PRC leg is treated as a regulated gateway requiring documentary continuity. The treasury blueprint must maintain segregated ledgers to evidence trade authenticity and tax compliance for mainland correspondent banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong: No statutory export proceeds surrender rule, no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain revenue in HKD, USD, RMB, or any freely convertible currency. Mainland China: Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days of the date of customs collection, unless SAFE has approved an extended timeframe. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the reporting enterprise may elect to settle into RMB or retain foreign currency, subject to SAFE's declaration system and the authenticity of supporting trade documents.

Central bank supervision: The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the central banking authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, maintaining the Linked Exchange Rate System for the Hong Kong dollar. To the extent the Sino-Foreign Joint Venture interfaces with the People's Republic of China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the competent foreign exchange regulator governing RMB convertibility, cross-border capital flows, and any PRC-sourced export proceeds.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs generally lack a Hong Kong Monetary Authority stored value facility license and therefore cannot legally be the primary depository or settlement institution for this corporate treasury. They are not subject to HKMA corporate account onboarding, and their licences typically permit e-money issuance only, not commercial treasury deposits, lending, or documentary LC operations. As a result, their viability is low as a standing operating account and only moderate as a supplementary collection conduit connected to a licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder equity injections into the Hong Kong JV should be remitted from the holding parent via SWIFT MT103 or CHIPS into the designated HSBC, BOCHK, or Standard Chartered corporate account. The remitter field must accurately describe the sender and the beneficiary must match the exact entity name. In the purpose block, where the correspondent bank serves the mainland China leg, the creditor should cite SAFE's Foreign Exchange Payment Application code for FDI capital injection, such as code 622 for foreign-invested enterprise foreign exchange capital payment expenses, and attach the capital account registration. Once landed in Hong Kong, funds are freely deployable; if subsequently injected into a mainland WFOE/JV, the receiving bank will require the PSA Capital Registration, board resolution, and a certified public accountant's capital verification report. Turnover: There are no quantitative turnover, total-asset, or headcount thresholds that exempt an active Hong Kong company from audit. Under sections 403 to 408 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622), every active Hong Kong incorporated company must appoint an auditor who is a certified public accountant holding a Practicing Certificate issued by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA), and its financial statements must be audited annually. The only statutory dispensation is for a dormant private company that has made no relevant accounting transactions during the financial year and has resolved to be dormant; such company may be exempt from appointing an auditor only while the dormancy conditions in Cap 622 remain satisfied.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 6,800,000 in aggregate intercompany transactions for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File required if group revenue exceeds HKD 680,000,000; CbC report above HKD 6,800,000,000 consolidated revenue.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong ESR does not apply directly, but IRD requires economic substance for treaty benefits and territorial source claims. CIGA must be performed in Hong Kong: board meetings, staff offices, and sales decision-making. The JV must avoid shell status or IRD may apply anti-avoidance rules under IRO section 61A.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company Abuse / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under IRO section 61A, IRD may disregard the HK entity and reattribute profits to the Mainland parent. Section 82 imposes fines of HKD 100,000 and treble tax. Imprisonment up to 3 years. The China STA may impose 10% withholding tax on deemed dividends and impose GAAR penalties.

Director personal liability: Chinese and Hong Kong directors may be personally liable for fraudulent trading under Cap. 622 section 499. Directors face travel bans, asset freezes, and imprisonment up to 14 years if found to have conspired to defraud the IRD. Under Mainland law, directors are jointly and severally liable for taxes evaded.
False Offshore Profit Claim / Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: IRO section 82(1): HKD 100,000 fine plus treble tax and imprisonment up to 3 years. Section 80(2) civil penalty for incorrect returns. Mainland EIT Law penalty of 50% of underpaid tax plus annual interest at 5% above benchmark. Criminal tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 203: up to 7 years for amounts exceeding 30% of taxable income.

Director personal liability: The director signing the false return is personally criminally liable. Courts may lift the corporate veil to pursue personal assets. Directors may also face entry-exit bans under China's Exit-Entry Administration Law and be blacklisted by tax bureaus from leaving Mainland China.
AML / Payment Aggregation Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Hong Kong AMLO (Cap. 615), unlicensed money service operation: fine of HKD 100,000 and up to 6 months imprisonment per transaction; fraudulent conversion: up to HKD 500,000 and 7 years imprisonment. In China, foreign exchange violations attract 30% fines; serious cases constitute illegal business operation under PRC Criminal Law Article 175 with up to 10 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement unlicensed payment structures are personally liable. HKMA can blacklist directors, causing bank account closures and asset freezes. Directors are subject to asset confiscation under OSCO (Cap. 455) and travel bans. The JV's corporate veil will be pierced where payments are routed personally through directors' accounts.
