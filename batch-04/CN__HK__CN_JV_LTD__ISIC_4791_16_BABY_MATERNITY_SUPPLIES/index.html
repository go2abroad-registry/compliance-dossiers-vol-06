Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility for bona fide trade and service transactions, but remains capital-account restricted. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must channel all cross-border receipts through banks designated by SAFE and report through the ASOne system. Current account settlements (e-commerce sales, service fees, royalties) are freely convertible after verifying authenticity, including platform settlement certificates, logistics data, customs declarations, invoices and contracts. Capital account movements (FDI capital injection, shareholder loans, outward investment) require SAFE registration, actual-purpose certificates, and qualified bank handling. Profit remittance is a current account item but requires the bank to see audited annual financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, the board's dividend resolution, and a complete application to confirm the profits arise from paid-in capital actually contributed; additionally, non-resident parent shareholders are subject to a 10% withholding tax (reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong DTA in eligible shareholding structures). Proceeds from exported baby and maternity goods must be repatriated to China; they may be kept in foreign currency in a current account or converted to RMB at actual demand, but the bank must reconcile every receipt with customs export records. Capital account liberalization has not reached full convertibility, so any proposed inbound equity injection from Hong Kong must be booked through a dedicated capital account, not an ordinary operational account, and must be verified by a certified public accountant before any RMB settlement for working capital.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC SAFE rules, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the mandatory sell-off requirement was abolished in 2012. The statutory obligation is full repatriation: the China JV must bring 100% of export foreign currency receipts into the domestic foreign exchange current account (or settle into RMB) within 180 calendar days from the customs export declaration date, or such shorter period stipulated in the bank's trade verification classification. Each receipt must be matched against customs declarations in the Single Window and banks' direct reporting system. If the receipt is for cross-border e-commerce, the underlying transaction records from Stripe or PayPal, the third-party payment settlement report, and the export logistics waybill must support the declaration. The enterprise may retain approved foreign currency balance or convert to RMB voluntarily; no specified percentage of the proceeds is mandated for conversion to RMB.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary operational account provider. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer or Airwallex do not hold PRC banking licenses and cannot open RMB basic deposit accounts, cannot receive capital-account injections, and cannot issue SAFE verification codes on behalf of a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign JV. They may be used only as ancillary collection and disbursement tools outside mainland China; ultimately all funds must be repatriated through a licensed PRC bank for SAFE reconciliation. For the e-commerce segment, Stripe and PayPal act as payment facilitators collecting consumer card payments, but their settlement into the JV must be routed to a domestic bank account via a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution or qualified bank; direct net settlement from a foreign EMI to the JV is legally barred. The practical blueprint is to use Bank of China's cross-border e-commerce collection service and retain the EMI as a receivable collection engine only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must transfer equity capital via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's FDI capital proceeds escrow account at a licensed PRC bank, using the remittance narrative 'Equity investment into PRC Sino-Foreign JV' and the SAFE capital-account purpose code for foreign direct investment (BOP code 622). The bank requires the SAFE Foreign Investment Registration Certificate, the JV contract, articles of association, capital account registration notice, and source-of-funds evidence. After the funds are credited, the bank reports to PBOC/SAFE direct reporting; the JV then engages a CICPA-registered accountant to produce a capital verification report confirming the capital came from the offshore parent and was not used for prohibited items. Only then may funds be moved into the operational RMB account for procurement, salary, logistics and marketplace expenditures. Intermediate netting through personal accounts or unlicensed payment institutions is prohibited.Cross-border supplier settlements for raw materials, packaging, and logistics should use SWIFT MT103 for documentary payments and MT202 COV for bank-to-bank transfers supporting trade. For large or recurring transactions, a confirmed irrevocable letter of credit (sight or 60-day usance) or documentary collections (D/P and D/A) may be issued by the JV bank. For RMB-denominated cross-border trade with eligible counterparties, the clearing rail may switch to CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) through a direct participant bank. Domestically, CNAPS HVPS clears RMB payments to local suppliers and logistics vendors. For e-commerce refunds to overseas consumers, refunds must be processed through the original payment processor rails to preserve the transaction audit trail. Every outbound disbursement requires the bank to verify the invoice, contract, customs declaration, and SAFE purpose code; incomplete underlying trade evidence will cause the payment to be rejected.Onshore SAFE-approved forward foreign-exchange sale and settlement contracts covering 100% of actual trade receivables and payables, with delivery expiry aligned to the underlying invoice; speculative forward positions are prohibited.FX swaps between RMB and USD/HKD on the interbank market for temporary liquidity mismatches under verified current-account flows, with no rollover beyond the real exposure tenor.Cross-currency swaps are permissible only for long-term balance-sheet exposures with prior SAFE approval; NDFs are not permissible onshore and may be transacted by the Hong Kong parent outside mainland jurisdiction only.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low as a primary operational account provider. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise, Payoneer or Airwallex do not hold PRC banking licenses and cannot open RMB basic deposit accounts, cannot receive capital-account injections, and cannot issue SAFE verification codes on behalf of a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign JV. They may be used only as ancillary collection and disbursement tools outside mainland China; ultimately all funds must be repatriated through a licensed PRC bank for SAFE reconciliation. For the e-commerce segment, Stripe and PayPal act as payment facilitators collecting consumer card payments, but their settlement into the JV must be routed to a domestic bank account via a licensed Chinese cross-border payment institution or qualified bank; direct net settlement from a foreign EMI to the JV is legally barred. The practical blueprint is to use Bank of China's cross-border e-commerce collection service and retain the EMI as a receivable collection engine only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In Hong Kong, mandatory TP documentation is not triggered by a turnover threshold; instead, the IRD may require a Local File, Master File, and TP documentation for all controlled transactions, and for annual reporting the IRD Ir46 (TP forms) requires a Form 1 (notification) if turnover exceeds HKD 10 million, Form 2 where certain thresholds apply: total controlled transactions exceeding HKD 50 million for goods, HKD 20 million for services, HKD 10 million for royalties or financing, or HKD 10 million for intercompany services. Practical thresholds: if the HK JV's intercompany transactions exceed the IRD-specific safe harbor levels (e.g., HKD 50 million in goods transactions), full TP documentation must be prepared contemporaneously.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies a territorial source principle of taxation, thus an entity claiming Hong Kong residence and treaty benefits must have substantive business operations in Hong Kong. The IRD scrutinises whether a Hong Kong JV has sufficient economic substance, including local management, decision-making, staff, office premises, and active conduct of e-commerce operations. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not a statutory Hong Kong doctrine per se, but the IRD applies substance-over-form principles, and the OECD's Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) rules require alignment of profits with value creation. The HK JV selling maternity and baby products online via Stripe and PayPal must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) — marketing development, inventory management, customer contracting, and payment processing — occur in Hong Kong to retain taxable profits in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Profits Tax)

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s. 82, a person who wilfully evades tax is liable on conviction to a fine of HK$200,000 plus three times the tax undercharged, and imprisonment for 3 years. Under s. 82A, negligent or fraudulent understatement leads to a fine of HK$50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly executes the false return or omitted income may be personally criminally liable as a principal offender. The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a sham, and directors may be held personally liable for unpaid tax in civil recovery. Directors may face a travel ban / blacklisting under the IRD's departure prevention measures, asset freeze, and disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance.
Improper Transfer Pricing (Profit Shifting)

Statutory sanction: The IRO ss. 50AAM–50AAZ empower the IRD to adjust related-party transactions to an arm's length amount. For failing to comply with TP documentation requirements, penalties up to HK$50,000 plus 100% of the additional tax assessed may apply. Under the DTA, excessive payments may be recharacterised as dividends and subject to additional tax and penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or participate in non-arm's length pricing may be personally liable to tax adjustments and penalties. In civil proceedings, a court may order restoration of diverted profits; in egregious cases, directors may face criminal liability for aiding tax evasion, a travel ban, and proceedings to disqualify them from corporate management.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abusive Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The IRD may ignore the entity's existence under the anti-avoidance rules (s. 61A), and tax profits in the hands of the real operator. The company faces strike-off under the Companies Ordinance, and principles from Chinese tax law (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) can recharacterise the structure. Penalties: additional tax of up to 100% of tax avoided and monetary fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who allow the company to function as a sham and facilitate tax avoidance may face a claim for breach of fiduciary duty, personal civil liability for losses to the corporate group, and disqualification as a director for up to 15 years. In criminal prosecutions for tax evasion or fraud, imprisonment is possible.
