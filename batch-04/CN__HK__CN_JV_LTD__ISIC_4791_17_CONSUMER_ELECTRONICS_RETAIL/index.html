Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under Section 658 of the Companies Ordinance, a Hong Kong company must have a registered office that is a physical street address in HKSAR at which communications can be addressed; a P.O. Box is prohibited. A serviced-office address (virtual office) with a physical street address and staffed reception may satisfy the Companies Registry minimum, but the Inland Revenue Department and most licensed banks, especially for online consumer-electronics retail, will reject pure virtual mailboxes because they cannot verify actual business substance, storage of records, and management presence. Banks require utility bills, a lease agreement, and an on-site meeting for account opening. Recommended alternative: a co-working office with a term lease and physical signage.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: GovHK eTAX Platform (www.gov.hk/e-tax) and Companies Registry e-Services Portal for corporate registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign capital-related joint venture established as a Hong Kong private limited company: (1) incorporate the company or register a non-Hong Kong company with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation/Registration; (2) within one month of commencement of business, lodge a Business Registration Application with the Inland Revenue Department's Business Registration Office; (3) the IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN), which constitutes the entity's tax identification number in Hong Kong; (4) maintain the BRC by annual renewal and display it at the registered place of business. A VAT registration number is not applicable because no VAT/GST regime exists.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid-mismatch elections under its Inland Revenue Ordinance. All limited companies are automatically subject to entity-level Profits Tax on assessable profits sourced in Hong Kong; no elective pass-through regime is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong SAR operates a territorial direct tax regime without any broad-based consumption tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT-origin turnover threshold because no indirect tax is levied. Every business carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong must apply for Business Registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in Hong Kong SAR. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/Sales Tax and therefore no statutory 0% export rate or VAT refund mechanism. Export sales of goods from a Hong Kong entity are outside the scope of any indirect tax, so no claim needs to be substantiated. Where the underlying merchandise is supplied by a mainland China exporter in the same supply chain, that exporter may be eligible for PRC VAT zero-rating/export rebate only if it holds the exporter-of-record position and satisfies PRC Customs, tax and foreign-exchange documentation mandates.

  • Trade Single Window (TSW) export/transhipment declaration or combined import/export declaration filed to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department for shipments leaving or entering Hong Kong where prescribed thresholds apply
  • Carrier-issued bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical export movement from the point of origin to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration and sales contract
  • Sales contract or purchase order from the offshore buyer
  • Bank settlement evidence of export proceeds, including SWIFT MT103 credit advice, payment gateway payout report, or inward remittance/foreign exchange receipt confirmation

None. Hong Kong mandates no real-time fiscal clearance, no API-linked e-invoice clearance, and no anti-fraud digital stamping. Taxpayers may issue ordinary commercial invoices or receipts; electronic e-Receipt/e-Invoice usage is voluntary and not subject to pre-clearance.

Hong Kong has no VAT zero-rating to claw back. However, false particulars in customs declarations may lead to forfeiture of goods and penalties under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109). Misdeclaration in Profits Tax returns is addressed by IRD investigations with additional tax up to 100% of undercharged tax, and penalties up to 300% in evasion cases under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). If a mainland China exporter separately claims export VAT refund/zero-rating on goods linked to this entity, fabricated or non-repatriated export documentation will trigger recovery of the refunded tax, statutory interest, and administrative fines under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, with potential criminal liability for fraudulent export refunds.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The consolidated treasury structure spans the PRC mainland and Hong Kong SAR. Hong Kong maintains full current and capital account convertibility, with RMB offshore (CNH) freely tradable against HKD and USD through the interbank and retail markets. The PRC, by contrast, operates a managed float with convertibility asymmetry: current account transactions are generally convertible under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, while capital account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, designated bank intermediation, and post-verification. For the Sino-foreign equity joint venture, foreign direct investment equity injections require an FDI Foreign Exchange Business Registration and must be remitted into a dedicated capital fund account at a licenced domestic bank before conversion into RMB. Trade-related export proceeds from consumer electronics sales must be received through a SAFE-designated bank and validated against customs declarations. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted under the current account after the PRC entity has completed annual income tax settlement, generated distributable profits from audited financial statements, and provided a board resolution authorising the dividend. In practice, profit remittance hurdles include documentation rigidity, tax payment verification, and the requirement that the remittance exactly match the audited profit distribution and the enterprise income tax final assessment. For HK-side flows, no surrender or explicit convertibility restriction exists, but clearing must still satisfy HKMA requirements for beneficial ownership identification, sanctions screening, and cross-border payment record-keeping.

Export proceeds surrender rules

In the PRC, export proceeds in foreign currency from ordinary consumer-electronics goods must be repatriated and credited through a SAFE-designated bank in the cross-border receipts and payments collection system. The operational timeline requires the exporter to complete verification against customs declarations within the SAFE-prescribed window, typically by the 30th day after the bank receives confirmation of the customs export data; where full supporting trade documentation is available, the bank will process the credit without mandatory conversion. There is no fixed statutory surrender ratio for general trade export proceeds, and the entity is permitted to retain 100% of the foreign-currency receipts in a current-account foreign-exchange settlement account or to convert them freely to RMB at quote rates. For exceptional categories subject to counter-cyclical macro-prudential measures, SAFE may direct partial or full conversion of specified receipt flows, but this does not currently apply to low-risk e-commerce retail goods. Hong Kong imposes no surrender rules, no mandatory conversion ratios, and no capital repatriation timing constraints.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the PRC controlled-currency node; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR node where the Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible and no comprehensive foreign exchange controls apply.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI rails are not viable as the primary treasury bank for the PRC leg of this structure. Stripe, PayPal, Payoneer, Wise, and similar platforms are payment collection software or authorised payment institutions; they are not commercial banks and cannot perform SAFE-registered capital account settlement, hold capital escrow balances, issue MT103 payment orders on behalf of the JV, or act as the designated FX verification bank. In mainland China, cross-border RMB and foreign-currency transactions must be intermediated by a licenced domestic bank in the settlement chain. EMIs can only operate as downstream collection gateways in supported marketplaces, and all accumulated balances must be swept into the domestic corporate bank account through an approved acquiring path. For the Hong Kong treasury node, an offshore EMI may provide convenient collection of marketplace proceeds but cannot replace the licensed bank for trade settlement, hedging, or liquidity concentration. Overall rating: Low for treasury banking and capital clearing; Medium for ancillary e-commerce collection aggregation, with daily settlement into the licensed bank.
