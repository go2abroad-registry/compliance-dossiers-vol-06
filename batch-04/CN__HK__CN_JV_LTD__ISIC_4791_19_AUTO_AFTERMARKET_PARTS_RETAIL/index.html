Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertibility split between current account and capital account. The current account has achieved de facto convertibility under IMF Article VIII: legitimate trade, service, and profit remittance payments may proceed through designated banks upon presentation of authentic commercial documents and tax records. The capital account remains heavily supervised: cross-border equity injection, shareholder loans, investment income repatriation, and foreign debt require SAFE registration or approval. For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture engaged in online automotive parts retail, the following practical constraints apply: (i) imported goods must be valued at arm's length and supported by invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and inspection reports; (ii) profit distribution to the Hong Kong shareholder requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificate, and board resolution; (iii) foreign exchange derived from e-commerce sales must enter the enterprise's domestic foreign currency account and be settled through the banking system, with cross-border electronic payment platforms acting merely as collectors rather than FX counterparties; (iv) any future capital increase or share transfer will trigger additional SAFE filing processes. Non-compliance can result in fines, suspension of foreign exchange services, and adverse records in the bank's central database. The Hong Kong parent receives dividends via RMB or HKD conversion at market rates with a withholding tax consideration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under applicable PRC foreign exchange regulations (notably SAFE's Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Goods Trade), all export proceeds from the JV's e-commerce sales of automotive parts must be repatriated to China through a designated foreign exchange bank. The mandatory surrender requirement was formally replaced by a 'willing settlement' regime in 2012; therefore, there is no compulsory conversion ratio. However, the entire amount of foreign currency receipts must be settled, held, or transferred within the banking system and must be reported in the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Export receivables due from overseas buyers must be collected within the prescribed timeframe—generally, trade credit items exceeding 90 days require filing a deferral report. If the JV retains foreign currency, it must maintain funds in an approved foreign currency current account; no offshore retention is permitted without SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility: Low for primary treasury management. Under PRC law, non-bank financial institutions and foreign electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed to operate domestic bank accounts or to perform foreign exchange conversion and cross-border settlement for a Chinese resident entity. While payment facilitators such as PayPal China (via a licensed payment entity) may be used to collect online consumer card payments, funds must be repatriated to the JV's local RMB or foreign exchange bank account after clearing. Stripe is not directly licensed in mainland China for domestic merchant acquiring; it would typically require a cross-border payment aggregator or a Hong Kong subsidiary structure. Thus, a tier-1 Chinese commercial bank remains mandatory as the core liquidity clearing spine; EMIs may only serve as peripheral collection gateways and cannot replace the SAFE-supervised bank settlement network.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Initial registered capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 from an account in the shareholder's name to the JV's FDI Capital Verification Account. Before receipt, the JV must complete FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE through the bank, obtain the FDI prospective code, and open a capital account. The wire must carry the appropriate FX purpose code (e.g., code for investment in foreign-funded enterprise / shareholder equity injection), and the bank will require a capital verification report from a PRC CPA after inflow. For amounts exceeding prescribed thresholds, a commercial contract for the joint venture and the Chinese business license are required. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's Local File threshold is triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property, RMB 100 million for intangible property, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for related-party services. A Master File is required when total related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Reporting applies when the consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under CIT Law Article 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment chapters require that related-party arrangements reflect actual functions, assets, and risks. The Hong Kong shareholder must satisfy the beneficial ownership test and the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) doctrine: substance must be demonstrated through active offices, resident directors, real employees, and genuine business decision-making in Hong Kong. A shell company that routes dividends or royalties without independent economic substance will be denied treaty benefits and may be attacked by both the PRC tax authorities and Hong Kong's tax-residence review process.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Offshore Receipt Diversion

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law: recovery of the underpaid tax, late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. The legal representative and responsible directors may be placed on the tax authority's public blacklist and subjected to travel restrictions under Article 44.

Director personal liability: If directors deliberately sign false financial statements, divert receivables, or cause the corporate entity to be stripped of assets, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law principles. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax. Criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 carries imprisonment of up to 3 years for tax evasion involving relatively large amounts, and 3 to 7 years for huge amounts, together with fines and confiscation of unlawful gains.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Related-Party Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41, 42, and 44 empower the tax authority to recharacterize transactions, re-determine taxable income, and apply the best transfer pricing method. The PRC tax authority may impose special tax adjustment interest at the benchmark RMB lending rate plus 5 percentage points. Failure to furnish related-party transaction forms may trigger fines under the Tax Collection and Administration Law, and fraudulent transfer pricing documentation may be prosecuted as tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize artificial markups, false pricing, or shell intermediaries may be required to reimburse the company for the resulting tax liabilities under fiduciary breach principles. Civil asset restitution can attach to personal bank accounts and shares. A departure ban or travel blacklisting may be issued under Article 44 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Involvement in intentional false documentation creates criminal exposure for directors as accomplices to tax evasion, with imprisonment terms of up to 7 years in serious cross-border cases.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse and Dividend Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authority will deny treaty benefits under SAT Announcement 2019 No. 35 and the beneficial ownership test. The full statutory 10% dividend withholding tax becomes payable, with late payment surcharge. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under CIT Law Article 47 permits recharacterization of the structure, imposition of interest, and penalties up to 5 times the shortfall where fraudulent misrepresentation exists.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Hong Kong shell and the mainland JV who knowingly certify false beneficial ownership declarations face personal liability for the under-withheld tax. A court may pierce the corporate veil to reach personal assets for civil restitution. The responsible persons may be blacklisted, banned from departing China, and subjected to criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 as tax evasion accomplices, with imprisonment of 3 to 7 years for large-scale treaty abuse and laundering of dividend income.
