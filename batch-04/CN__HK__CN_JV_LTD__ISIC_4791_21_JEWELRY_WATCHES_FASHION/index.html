Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and registration portal: GovHK eTAX (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/ese/); Business Registration Office operates under the IRD for issuance of Business Registration Certificates.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity participating in a Hong Kong joint venture must register as a taxpayer only if it carries on a business in Hong Kong. The standard procedure for a Hong Kong-incorporated limited company is: (1) Incorporate via the Companies Registry and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation. (2) Apply for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The application is submitted to the IRD (via eTAX or paper application) and results in a Business Registration Certificate bearing the Business Registration Number (BRN). The BRN is the statutory tax identification number for all Hong Kong tax purposes. (3) For non-Hong Kong companies deriving profits sourced in Hong Kong, the IRD may require notification under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); such entities should consult a tax advisor and apply for a BRN if a Hong Kong permanent establishment exists. (4) The same BRN is used for Profits Tax, Property Tax, and any other IRD correspondence; no separate VAT registration exists in Hong Kong. A mandatory electronic filing system does not apply to initial registration, but subsequent Profits Tax Returns may be filed online via eTAX.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not Applicable – Hong Kong has no value-added tax (VAT), goods and services tax (GST), or broad-based sales tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable – Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, and thus there is no zero-rating or export refund mechanism. For Profits Tax purposes, export income may be classified as offshore income if the essential operations of the sale occur outside Hong Kong; this is a territorial-source claim, not a VAT zero-rating. The claim must be substantiated by demonstrating that the contract, negotiation, and execution of the sale took place outside Hong Kong.

  • Not applicable for VAT; for general customs and offshore income substantiation, the following are recommended: Import/Export Declaration filed through the Trade Single Window (TSDW) or the ECCS system of the Census and Statistics Department
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods
  • Commercial Invoice, packing list, and purchase orders
  • Correspondence and records establishing that the sale was concluded and executed outside Hong Kong

No statutory electronic invoicing, real-time fiscal clearance, or digital stamping regime applies. Hong Kong does not require pre-clearance of invoices for tax purposes. Businesses are only obliged to keep adequate accounting records (including invoices) for six years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Voluntary electronic invoicing is permitted but not mandated by the IRD.

Not applicable for VAT. However, for offshore income claims, the IRD may scrutinise the entire transaction chain and can impose penalties under Sections 80 to 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance for incorrect returns, including treble the amount of tax under-assessed and a maximum fine of HK$50,000. Additionally, false or inaccurate trade declarations to the Census and Statistics Department are subject to fines under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under PRC controls, current account items for bona fide trade in jewellery, watches and fashion accessories are convertible through banks under the actual-permitting principle established by the 2019 Foreign Investment Law and SAFE rules, while capital account flows require separate registration, approval or administrative filing. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture receiving inbound capital from Hong Kong shareholders must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration and open a capital-account settlement bank account. Direct foreign exchange settlement is allowed for permitted business operations, but funds cannot be used for securities, entrusted loans, non-self-use real estate, or other restricted areas without additional approval. Profit remittance to Hong Kong requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax completion certificates, full PRC tax filing, and compliance with anti-money laundering checks; there is no automatic convertibility if tax liabilities remain outstanding. The Hong Kong side operates under the linked exchange rate system with no capital controls, meaning the primary friction lies in the PRC-HK capital path. Repatriation of export proceeds from the mainland JV to the Hong Kong sales entity is treated as current account transfer only if supported by genuine services or goods contracts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into mainland China through a designated bank within 180 days from customs declaration, unless an approved offshore retention quota is in place. Receipts may be retained in foreign currency in an export proceeds settlement account or converted into RMB at market rate; no mandatory conversion ratio currently applies. For the Hong Kong entity, export receipts earned in HKD, CNH or USD are not subject to PRC surrender rules and may remain in the Hong Kong treasury account subject to HKMA anti-money laundering supervision.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong dollar and offshore renminbi clearing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-Moderate. Mainland China does not license offshore digital EMI providers as legal clearing counterparties; only licensed banks may settle RMB or foreign currency through CIPS and SWIFT. Hong Kong's HKMA stored value facility (SVF) regime permits authorised e-money wallets, but SVFs generally cannot provide full corporate treasury accounts with clearing codes necessary for issuance of commercial letters of credit, documentary collections, or capital injection routing. Stripe and PayPal are acceptable only as customer sales collection rails and cannot serve as the treasury concentration or regulatory capital clearing layer. The compliant structure is a licensed Hong Kong commercial bank as master treasury node, with Stripe and PayPal capturing retail card payments that are swept daily into the bank operating account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong shareholder to the PRC JV, the correct routing is a SWIFT MT103 wire to the JV's SAFE-registered capital-account settlement bank. The remittance instruction must state the purpose as 'equity capital contribution under the joint venture contract' and use the SAFE-applicable foreign direct investment payment purpose code such as 62201 or 62202, as classified by the receiving bank. The bank must verify the FDI foreign exchange registration, the joint venture contract, the board resolution and the paid-in capital schedule. After funds are credited, a PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report to be filed with SAFE, and the funds may be used only for permitted operational purposes. For an e-commerce jewellery and watch retailer, the source of profit depends on where contracts are negotiated and concluded, and where the profit-generating activities are performed. Box 4 Total assessable profit before loss set-off and deductions. This box reflects assessable profit computed after adding back non-deductible expenditure. A JV claiming full offshore exemption should present an offshore profit computation in an accompanying statement. Any apportionment of expenses must be supported by a time-in-country analysis and a transaction-by-transaction source worksheet. Failure to provide this may cause the IRD to reject the offshore claim and tax all profits at 16.5%. Late filing penalties range from HK$1,200 to HK$3,000 for failing to file after an estimated demand. Box 5 Net assessable profit after deduction of approved allowances and loss set-offs. The JV may claim capital allowances for plant and machinery, industrial buildings, or intellectual property used in producing assessable profit. Losses under the territorial tax system may be carried forward and set off against future Hong Kong-source profits. A non-resident investor must ensure that losses claimed are exclusively attributable to Hong Kong-source trade. Off-shore losses may not be deducted. Late filing penalties range from HK$1,200 to HK$3,000 for failing to file after an estimated demand. Incorrect two-tiered claims attract penalties. Late filing penalties range from HK$1,200 to HK$3,000 for failing to file after an estimated demand. The JV must obtain a detailed work diary for each non-resident director to substantiate days spent in Hong Kong. Under-reporting director remuneration is a criminal offence. Late filing penalties of HK$1,200 to HK$3,000; more severe penalties apply where the failure is due to an intent to evade tax. Box 2 Stock options and other equity-based incentives. Any equity-settled awards granted to overseas employees for Hong Kong services must be reported. Non-resident employees are chargeable to Hong Kong salaries tax on the value attributable to services performed in Hong Kong. The JV must track vesting and exercise dates and provide data on grant country and services location. Late filing penalties of HK$1,200 to HK$3,000; more severe penalties apply where the failure is due to an intent to evade tax. Failure to report benefits may lead to a penalty equal to the undercharged tax. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$0 statutory threshold; however, the IRD requires contemporaneous local file and master file documentation if the total amount of cross-border related-party transactions is HK$5,000,000 or above, or if the net profits of the Hong Kong entity exceed HK$5,000,000 in a year. For PRC resident related parties, a higher threshold may apply under the PRC SAT rules.

Economic substance scope: The Hong Kong IRD adopts the economic substance guidance under DIPN 58 and the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines. The JV must demonstrate that its income-generating activities, including procurement, inventory management, customer contracting, and risk management, are performed by locally based employees with actual authority. A Chinese-owned Hong Kong company with only a registered address and no personnel will not satisfy the economic substance test for offshore claims or treaty benefits. CIGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) should be aligned with Hong Kong operations and documented through board minutes, employment contracts, and operating systems.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Offshore Income Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Under section 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the JV is liable to a fine of HK$10,000 and three times the total tax undercharged; on indictment, imprisonment of up to 3 years and a fine of HK$100,000. The IRD may also raise estimated assessments up to 10 years retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors responsible for false declarations may be personally prosecuted. Upon proof of willful neglect, directors are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Civil asset recovery may be pursued; directors may be subject to travel restrictions and, upon conviction, imprisonment of up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The IRD may assess the JV on an arm's-length basis under section 50AAB of the IRO, with penalties under section 82A up to 100% of the tax undercharged. The PRC SAT may also reassess the parent under PRC transfer pricing rules and impose a 10% late payment surcharge plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly execute non-arm's length purchases may be held personally liable for the tax deficiency under the fraudulent trading rules in the Companies Ordinance. They may be disqualified as directors, subjected to personal contribution orders in insolvency proceedings, and face criminal liability if the transfer pricing arrangement is intended to evade tax.
Shell Entity / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the entity for tax purposes under the anti-avoidance provisions and tax the beneficial owner. The PRC SAT may recapture the income under circular 37 and impose 10% withholding tax plus penalty. The Economic Substance requirements under DIPN 58 may lead to a striking-off of the Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance.

Director personal liability: Nominee directors may be criminally prosecuted for abetting tax evasion and may be disqualified for up to 15 years. They may be held personally liable for the tax debt under the trust doctrine, and may be subject to cross-border enforcement of tax judgments through mutual assistance treaties, including asset seizures and travel bans.
