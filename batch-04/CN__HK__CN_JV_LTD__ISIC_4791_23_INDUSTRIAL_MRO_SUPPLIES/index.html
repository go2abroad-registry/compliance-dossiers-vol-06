Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under section 69 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), there is no minimum authorised or paid-up share capital for a private company limited by shares. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessed through the National Integrated Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-tax)

E-government filing portal

For CN_JV_LTD, a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, tax registration begins with issuance of a business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the entity must then complete tax registration with the STA within 30 days and obtain its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number for all tax payments, VAT invoices, customs declarations and export rebates. For foreign non-resident enterprises, such as the Hong Kong parent, that have a deemed offshore supply or China-source income but no registered Chinese entity, the non-resident person must be registered with the STA through the domestic withholding agent, or directly at the competent tax office, and must obtain the Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number before filing or reporting withholding obligations. The JV must also complete VAT taxpayer registration at the same competent tax office and, if it expects to export, file for export tax rebate qualification in the export tax refund system.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border export sales of tangible MRO industrial supplies from China are entitled to 0% VAT under the export goods exemption-and-refund mechanism when the seller is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported through Chinese customs, the transaction is at arm's length to an overseas customer, title and risk pass outside China, export customs clearance is completed in the exporter's name, the export declaration matches the commercial invoice and payment flow, and foreign exchange consideration is repatriated into China within the statutory period. Qualifying exported digital services, if any, require a separate zero-rated offshore service supply filing; goods under ISIC 4791 are treated as export zero-rated only upon customs exit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form with export rebate endorsement if claimed)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / regular international transport document)
  • Export Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation (bank issued receipt evidencing repatriation of export proceeds)

China mandates electronic fiscal invoicing through the Golden Tax Phase IV integrated invoicing and clearance system. Taxable supplies must be invoiced using STA-approved VAT invoice software, with Special VAT Invoices transmitted to the STA tax-control platform in near real time. Full Digital Electronic Invoices carry a national unified invoice number, tax control seal and encrypted QR code; invoice data must match customs, banking, and VAT refund records. Auto-validation and risk-control matching are performed by the STA before the buyer can verify or use the invoice.

Export VAT refunds are scrutinized through STA-GACC-Customs-SAFE data matching. If export documents are unverified, falsely matched, or funds are not repatriated within 180 days, the exporter must reverse the zero-rated treatment, re-issue taxable output VAT (13%), repay any refunded input VAT, and pay late surcharge and interest. Administrative penalties under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Taxation Administration may reach 50% of the unduly refunded amount and an additional late fee is imposed per day; fraudulent export tax refund claims constitute criminal tax fraud and are punishable by confiscation of property and imprisonment under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland China side operates a tightly managed exchange rate regime with a closed capital account. Current account convertibility is available for genuine trade and service transactions after SAFE truthfulness verification, and export proceeds and supplier payments can pass through an onshore bank account in RMB or eligible foreign currencies. By contrast, capital account transactions—inbound equity investment, offshore loans, and profit repatriation—require SAFE registration, an approved capital project, and a bank-administered use and monitoring system. The JV may receive an HKD/USD equity injection into a capital escrow account, convert to RMB in accordance with the actual business purpose, and apply the proceeds to approved working capital or fixed asset purchases; it may not use those funds for securities investment or onward lending unless separately permitted. Dividend remittances are permitted only after audited financials, tax clearance, SAFE verification, and board approval. Hong Kong SAR, as the counterpart treasury hub, maintains a fully convertible Hong Kong dollar pegged to the US dollar at 7.75 to 7.85, with no surrender, exchange control, or conversion approval requirements, allowing the group to centralize liquidity clearing, vendor payments, and client collections in Hong Kong before deploying capital into Mainland operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the mainland operating JV, export proceeds from MRO catalog sales must be repatriated to China through a SAFE-designated bank and are reportable in the trade monitoring system; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, so the enterprise may retain proceeds in a current-account foreign exchange settlement account or convert to RMB as needed. Receipts should generally be collected within 180 days from the customs declaration date, with an extension filing required for exception cases. Hong Kong SAR imposes no surrender, conversion, or repatriation timeline on foreign currency receipts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the onshore Chinese JV. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE and cannot support SAFE capital account registration, capital verification, trade monitoring, or foreign exchange hedging on behalf of a mainland entity. For the Hong Kong treasury vehicle, a licensed HKMA stored-value facility or digital bank may be used as a supplementary low-value card/acquirer rail, but it cannot replace tier-1 banks for LC issuance, SWIFT MT103 clearing, SAFE purpose-code reporting, or onshore FX derivatives. Overall viability: low as the primary banking rail; acceptable only as a secondary payment tool for small B2B MRO transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder should remit the equity injection to the mainland JV through an SWIFT MT103 wire using the SAFE capital-account purpose code for foreign direct investment equity injection (commonly reported as 622011). The bank will require the Foreign Investment Information Report filed with the Administration for Market Regulation, the JV's business license, board resolution approving the capital call, and proof of legal source of funds. The first deposit must be routed into the foreign exchange capital account or a capital escrow account before conversion to RMB; after conversion, the funds may only be used for the approved business purposes such as MRO inventory, warehouse buildup, and working capital. A registered Chinese CPA must then issue a capital verification report, and the company files the updated paid-in capital with the market regulator. Any downward adjustment, transfer, or dividend distribution from that capital must follow SAFE approval or filing procedures.For B2B MRO procurement and vendor settlements, use SWIFT MT103 open-account wires for standardized bulk payments; MT700/MT707 commercial letters of credit for high-value or first-time counterparties; and documentary collections (DP/DA) for moderate-risk transactions. Cross-border RMB outbound clearing should be executed through CIPS with SWIFT-compatible message conversion. Card rail settlements for catalog e-commerce should flow through an acquiring bank and card network, with settlement in USD or HKD into the Hong Kong treasury account and subsequent rebalancing into mainland accounts only on genuine trade documentation to avoid creating unapproved FX conversion. All outbound disbursements must be reported with the appropriate SAFE trade purpose code and supported by invoices, contracts, and logistics evidence.Onshore deliverable CNY/USD forex forward contracts with licensed tier-1 banks, backed by genuine MRO trade receivables or payables and settled against actual underlying exposure.Cross-currency swaps between HKD and CNY funding legs for intercompany loans and working capital rebalancing, subject to SAFE registration of the underlying debt and proof of real treasury requirement.FX options and option combinations such as collars, call spreads, and put spreads to protect bid-ask margins on B2B MRO payments, capped at the value of actual forecast trade flows.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permissible only in the Hong Kong treasury entity and cannot be used inside the mainland JV; onshore NDFs are not permitted under SAFE rules.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation thresholds (effective for years of assessment commencing 1 April 2023 under IRO sections 58A-58P and the Inland Revenue (Transfer Pricing Documentation) Notice): a Local File is mandatory where any covered related-party transaction exceeds: HKD 150 million for tangible property transfers; HKD 100 million for intangible property transfers or financial asset transfers; HKD 10 million for provision of services; or HKD 10 million for financing transactions. A Master File is required where the MNE group's total consolidated revenue is at least HKD 6.8 billion. In addition, a CbCR report is required for MNE groups with consolidated group revenue of at least HKD 6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR); however, the territorial source principle, the central management and control test under case law, and the beneficial ownership requirement under the Mainland-HK DTA serve as economic substance doctrines. A Hong Kong entity arranging MRO procurement and distribution must carry out Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong (e.g., strategic sourcing decisions, contract negotiation, inventory management, invoicing, and risk-bearing treasury functions) for income to be considered either onshore profitable or for the entity to qualify as a DTA resident. If the JV exists solely to take advantage of the 5% dividend rate or the 7% royalties/interest rate while all substantive functions are in mainland China, the IRD or mainland tax authority may disregard the Hong Kong shell and pierce it.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) section 82 - knowingly furnishing incorrect information or documents with intent to evade tax: a fine of HKD 50,000 and additional tax of three times the tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for 3 years. Section 80 further imposes a fine of HKD 10,000 and three times the undercharged tax for an incorrect return without reasonable excuse.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind the corporate veil for tax fraud. Under IRO section 82A, directors may be personally assessed for the corporation's tax liability if assets have been stripped. The court may order civil restitution of the unpaid tax and penalties; directors are placed under a departure prevention notice under IRO section 81 (travel ban), and the Immigration Department will deny re-entry if there is any intention to leave Hong Kong with unsatisfied assessed tax. Criminal conviction leads to disqualification as a director for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: IRO section 58B & 80: failure to make inquiries and keep contemporaneous documentation attracts a penalty of HKD 10,000 up to HKD 50,000, a 50% supplemental tax charge on the TP adjustment, and if the return is knowingly incorrect, three times the additional tax plus imprisonment. The Commissioner may disregard the non-arm's-length structure and recompute profits at market price.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify inflated intercompany cost rates are personally liable as joint tortfeasors for breach of statutory duties. Civil proceedings under IRO section 61 (transactions intended to defeat tax) allow the court to restore the tax advantage, with personal asset disgorgement. The director's personal bank accounts may be frozen and a travel ban imposed pending payment of the TP deficiency; in egregious cases, criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud carries up to 14 years in prison.
Shell Company Abuse of the Mainland-HK Double Tax Arrangement (Beneficial Ownership Failure)

Statutory sanction: The Hong Kong IRD may issue a negative residence certificate refusing treaty benefits; under IRO section 61, the arrangement is void as a tax-avoidance transaction. The mainland tax authority will impose a 10% domestic withholding tax plus late-payment penalties equal to 0.05% per day and a 50% additional charge for unreasonable avoidance. In Hong Kong, false statements on the residence certificate application are an offence under IRO section 80, punishable by a HKD 50,000 fine and 3 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell JV face personal liability for civil restitution of all avoided dividend / royalty withholding taxes, plus compound interest. The court can pierce the veil because the Hong Kong entity has no substantial economic activity, making the directors agents of the mainland ultimate owners. Directors may also face a criminal investigation for theft under section 16A of the Theft Ordinance (Cap. 210) where they misappropriate treaty benefits, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years. The IRD may also pursue departure prevention orders, passport seizure, and disqualification orders preventing the director from acting as a promoter of any Hong Kong company for up to 3 years.
