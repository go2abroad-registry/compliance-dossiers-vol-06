Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, supported by the Business Registration Office (BRO) and the Companies Registry; online portals: GovHK (www.gov.hk) and IRD eTAX (www.etax.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: For a Hong Kong-domiciled Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (formed as a company limited by shares under Hong Kong law), incorporate with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap.622) and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation. Step 2: Within one month after commencement of business, apply to the Business Registration Office (BRO) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap.310) for a Business Registration Certificate; the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the primary tax identifier. Step 3: Non-resident foreign corporations carrying on business in Hong Kong without a place of business are also required to make the same BRO application within one month of commencing business, and may be required by the IRD to appoint an authorised tax representative resident in Hong Kong. Step 4: The IRD assigns an Inland Revenue file/tax reference number for Profits Tax purposes and issues Profits Tax returns when the entity is put onto the chargeability register. Step 5: Register for electronic correspondence through IRD eTAX using the BRN and tax file reference. No separate VAT TIN/VAT ID exists because Hong Kong has no general VAT or GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to the U.S. check-the-box election; Hong Kong has no hybrid mismatch classification election. The JV is taxed at entity level under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112) at two-tier Profits Tax rates (8.25% on the first HKD 2,000,000 of assessable profits and 16.5% on the remainder where the concession applies). The territorial source principle operates as an evidentiary claim rather than an election: the JV may claim that profits are wholly or partly offshore and therefore not chargeable, supported by IRD DIPN 21 (Offshore Profits) and DIPN 46 (Electronic Commerce). Group loss relief is available by election under s.19C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance where eligibility is met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; limited excise duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap.109); statutory import/export trade declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap.60E)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for VAT/GST because no VAT/GST regime exists; the Business Registration requirement under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap.310) applies to every corporation carrying on business in Hong Kong within one month of commencement, irrespective of turnover, and therefore has no HKD turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in a VAT sense because Hong Kong levies no VAT/GST on exports and there is no VAT zero-rating or VAT refund system. For Profits Tax purposes, export sales of solar portable power stations and solar panels to non-resident customers may be classified as offshore-sourced profits and excluded from Hong Kong Profits Tax if the essential operations giving rise to the profits, such as contract negotiation, contract execution, purchasing decisions, and sales decisions, are performed and concluded outside Hong Kong; IRD DIPN 21 and DIPN 46 provide the administrative source-test framework. Hong Kong has no statutory foreign-exchange repatriation deadline; the 180-day field below is the documentary evidentiary window used for export substantiation alignment rather than a statutory tax deadline.

  • Statutory Export/Trade Declaration filed with the Trade and Industry Department (TID) via an approved e-filing service provider (e.g., Digital Trade and Transport Network (DTTN)) under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap.60E)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing physical delivery of the portable generator/solar panel goods outside Hong Kong
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods, quantities, and export sale terms
  • Executed sales contract, purchase order, or electronic correspondence evidencing negotiation and contract conclusion outside Hong Kong
  • Bank credit advice or inward remittance confirmation from a Hong Kong bank showing receipt of payment from the overseas buyer, serving as the Hong Kong equivalent of a Mainland China-style Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), which is not issued in Hong Kong
  • Delivery and logistics evidence such as port FOB/CIF records, courier proof of export, and, if applicable, certificate of origin

No real-time fiscal e-invoicing clearance, API clearance mandate, or anti-fraud digital stamping regime applies for VAT purposes. Commercial invoices are issued at the parties' discretion and retained as supporting records. IRD eTAX is the statutory electronic tax filing channel for Profits Tax returns and correspondence. Trade declarations under Cap.60E must be lodged electronically through approved service providers (e.g., the Digital Trade and Transport Network (DTTN)) for prescribed exports and imports.

The IRD may audit offshore profit claims and require full supporting documentation. If the claim is rejected, Profits Tax becomes payable with interest and the IRD may impose additional tax under Part XI of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112), including up to three times the tax undercharged for incorrect returns made without reasonable excuse, and criminal prosecution may follow for fraudulent or negligent statements. Assessments may be reopened within six years for ordinary cases and without time limit for fraud or wilful evasion. There is no VAT refund clawback because no VAT is charged, but the JV remains subject to record-keeping obligations for at least seven years under Cap.112.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible on the current account once genuine trade and services documentation is verified, but it is not freely convertible on the capital account; inbound and outbound FDI, offshore loans, reinvestment, and repatriation must be supported by SAFE registrations, onshore bank account structures, and post-event verification. Hong Kong itself maintains no foreign-exchange controls; the HKD operates under a currency board and is freely convertible, and there is no surrender requirement for export proceeds retained by a Hong Kong entity. The core convertibility friction for this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture comes from the mainland nexus: any equity injection from a Chinese parent into the Hong Kong vehicle requires outbound direct investment registration with SAFE plus MOFCOM and NDRC approval where applicable, while dividend repatriation from mainland operations to the HK shareholder requires audited financial statements, a SAFE dividend remittance filing, proof of tax clearance, and applicable withholding tax payment. Current-account e-commerce receipts can be settled onshore only if the goods are backed by customs declarations and the trade background is genuine, whereas capital-account contributions routed through Hong Kong must be supported by a capital verification report prepared by a certified public accountant and clear evidence of source of funds. Profit remittance from the onshore JV to the HK shareholder must follow the annual judicially audited accounting cycle and pass through a designated settlement bank; no ad hoc SAFE approval is required for legitimate dividends after the tax certificate and verified financials are lodged, but the bank will independently examine the underlying trade contract, invoice pattern, and payment beneficiaries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland goods trade, export foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated and verified through the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within 180 days from the date of customs declaration; there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio, so the enterprise may retain the proceeds in a foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntarily sell them for RMB. The Hong Kong JV entity is not subject to surrender requirements; export proceeds may be held in HKD/USD/RMB accounts freely, subject only to relevant accounting and tax filing obligations.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland Chinese capital controls, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the currency-board monetary authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for principal account and capital-flow purposes. Hong Kong law does not prohibit digital EMI licenses, and HKMA-licensed SVF operators can process ancillary merchant settlements; however, a fintech EMI cannot satisfy SAFE capital account registration, capital verification, or Chinese foreign-exchange purpose-code controls, and it is not acceptable as a receiving bank for FDI or profit-remittance flows. The relationship structure should place all regulated cross-border treasury activity at a tier-1 commercial bank and use the EMI only for limited non-mainland B2C acquiring if the commercial bank approves the exception in writing.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity capital injections from the Chinese shareholder to the Hong Kong JV must be routed through a tier-1 bank with SAFE-approved cross-border rails. The remitting bank in mainland China must transmit via SWIFT MT103 using an equity-investment purpose code tied to the registered ODI and FDI files, while the Hong Kong receiving bank executes the corresponding capital receipt entry and performs independent source-of-funds verification. The preferred route is RMB through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for onshore conversion or HKD/USD through the Hong Kong CHATS system where the underlying joint-venture contract denominates capital in USD/HKD. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: In Hong Kong, every active private limited company is required to prepare audited financial statements unless it is dormant or qualifies for a narrow statutory exemption. The relevant quantitative criteria used for small-company reporting are: annual total revenue not exceeding HK$10,000,000; total balance sheet assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000; and not more than 50 employees; a company is generally considered small when it meets at least two of those three tests. However, an active online retail entity carrying out trade in portable solar power equipment under ISIC 4791 cannot rely on dormancy, and the Companies Ordinance and Inland Revenue Department require a full independent audit by a Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant (Practising) regardless of small-company turnover or asset size where the company engages in revenue-generating e-commerce transactions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million annual related-party tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million annual related-party intangible property transfers; RMB 100 million annual related-party financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million annual related-party service transactions; Master File is required where group revenue reaches RMB 2 billion or where the group has cross-border related-party transactions meeting composite thresholds

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under CIT Law Article 47 and Tax Administration Law Article 36; for treaty purposes, a Hong Kong entity claiming reduced WHT rates must demonstrate economic substance in Hong Kong, including premises, employees, decision-making and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA); a shell or mailbox Hong Kong vehicle will likely be denied beneficial ownership and DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63: confiscation of illegal gains, recovery of unpaid tax, daily 0.05% interest on arrears, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; PRC Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion fines and imprisonment up to 3 years, or 3 to 7 years for large amounts and aggravating circumstances.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law to hold directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties; directors may face civil asset restitution, nationwide court blacklisting and travel bans restricting departure, and criminal imprisonment up to 7 years for aggravated tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing and Sham Intercompany Invoicing

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 46 permit tax authorities to make primary and secondary transfer pricing adjustments; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation can trigger fines and interest surcharges under Special Tax Adjustment rules, and a civil fraud penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax if mispricing is found to be tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fabricated intercompany agreements or false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable through veil piercing, with asset freezing, travel bans, blacklisting for enforcement, and criminal exposure for forgery or tax evasion, including imprisonment up to 7 years.
Treaty Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Shell in Hong Kong

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits with reversion to 10% statutory WHT on dividends, interest and royalties; assessment of additional tax, interest and penalties under GAAR and Tax Administration Law; withholding agent exposed to a fine of 50% to 300% of the unrecovered withholding tax under Article 69.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers of both the Chinese JV and the Hong Kong shell may be liable for civil restitution, placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist with exit-restriction travel bans, and prosecuted criminally for tax evasion and false invoicing, with imprisonment up to 7 years and personal asset confiscation.
