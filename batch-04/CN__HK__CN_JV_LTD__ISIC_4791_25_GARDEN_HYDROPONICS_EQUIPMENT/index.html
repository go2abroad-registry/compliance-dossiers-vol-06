Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong's Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) does not mandate a minimum paid-up share capital for a private company limited by shares, and there is no blocked escrow or capital-deposit requirement. The subscriber shares may be issued at a par value of HK$1 or any nominal amount stated in Form NNC1; however, for banking and creditor confidence, the joint venture parties may voluntarily subscribe and pay up HK$10,000 or such other amount as the board determines in the corporate bank account.

Funding & unlock path: No civil defense fire permit is applicable to pure online retail with no industrial undertaking. However, if the company occupies a physical office or fulfillment warehouse in Hong Kong, the premises must hold a valid fire safety certificate or letter of compliance from the Fire Services Department under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), certifying adequate means of escape and fire service installations. Structural load safety must be verified under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123), and the company must complete an OSH risk assessment, provide employee training, and maintain employer's liability insurance under the Employees' Compensation Ordinance (Cap. 282); no factory registration or industrial safety approval is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Office (自然人电子税务局/新电子税务局) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; for foreign non-resident enterprises, also the tax service hall of the competent tax authority where the place of business or withholding agent is located.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in the Hong Kong SAR but with mainland China supply-chain nexus: (1) Determine whether the entity has a permanent establishment or establishment/place of business in mainland China; if yes, register with the competent local STA office within 30 days of commencing business and obtain a unified social credit code (USCC) which serves as the taxpayer identification number. (2) If no establishment in mainland China but deriving China-sourced income, apply for a taxpayer identification number (TIN) via the withholding agent using the 'Contract Registration' procedure and obtain a 'Foreign Enterprise Tax Registration Number' through the electronic tax office. (3) For Hong Kong SAR tax registration, apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the IRD will issue a Business Registration Number (BRN) which functions as the tax identification number; for profits tax, the IRD may issue a separate tax file number. (4) For VAT registration in mainland China as a small-scale or general taxpayer, submit the 'Tax Registration Form' and related identification documents through the electronic tax office; after approval, obtain the tax registration number and VAT taxpayer qualification. (5) For Hong Kong SAR, there is no VAT/GST; registration for the BRC and profits tax is sufficient. (6) Maintain all registration certificates and update any changes within 15 days.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland China: RMB 5,000,000 annual sales turnover (standard threshold for compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration; small-scale taxpayers may register voluntarily below threshold). Hong Kong SAR: No VAT/GST/sales tax; no registration threshold applicable.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from mainland China qualifies for 0% VAT rate (exemption with credit/refund) under the 'Export Tax Rebate (VAT Export Rebate) regime' when the goods are actually exported out of the customs territory and the export sales are properly declared; qualifying general VAT taxpayers may apply for refund of input VAT on exported goods. Cross-border sales of goods through e-commerce channels (including B2C and B2B exports) may also qualify under the customs declaration method '9610', '9810', '9710' or general trade '0110' where goods are actually shipped and customs export declarations are filed. Services rendered by mainland China entities to overseas consumers may qualify for zero-rating for cross-border services under specific categories, but for tangible goods the physical export condition controls. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST; exports are outside the charging scope of any indirect tax, and no zero-rating refund mechanism exists.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued by the customs authority of the People's Republic of China (出口货物报关单/出口报关单)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement from mainland China port
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by designated foreign exchange bank or via the Foreign Exchange Account System (跨境收付汇证明/出口收汇核销单) evidencing receipt of foreign currency export proceeds
  • Export VAT invoice or ordinary commercial invoice matched to the customs declaration
  • Sales contract or purchase order with overseas buyer
  • Customs declaration agent authorization or customs clearance record from China International Trade Single Window
  • Proof of payment of export logistics costs and insurance (where required)

Mainland China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoices (fully digitalized electronic invoices, 数电票) under the State Taxation Administration's nationwide electronic invoice service platform; taxpayers must use the official e-invoice service to issue VAT invoices with real-time transmission to STA systems, with anti-fraud digital stamping and unified invoice codes. Certain high-risk industries require the 'VAT Invoice Management System' with real-time verification; comprehensive 'digitalization of tax controls' requires API integration with the taxpayer's accounting/ERP systems for invoice data reporting, and block-chain based electronic invoice pilots apply. All invoices must be issued via the STA-approved platform or authorized third-party platforms connected through standard interfaces; physical stamping is replaced by digital control. Hong Kong SAR: no electronic invoicing mandate for VAT purposes; e-invoices are compliant with the Inland Revenue Ordinance and may be used for record-keeping, but no government clearance system.

Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating claims trigger retrospective clawback of all VAT refunded, levy a surcharge for late payment, and impose administrative fines under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection; the STA and customs authorities may jointly investigate export tax fraud; penalties may range from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax (three times to five times in serious cases), plus prosecution for criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebates under Article 204 of the Criminal Law where the amount is relatively large; export rebate eligibility is denied where foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline unless an extension is granted for force majeure.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free-floating or pro-market currency regime in which the Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible against all major currencies, with no surrender requirements, no prior administrative approval for ordinary trade or investment remittances, and no exchange-control licensing for corporate accounts. The mainland Chinese renminbi remains subject to a managed current account convertibility framework and stringent capital account controls administered by SAFE/PBOC. For the HK-incorporated JV, all operating income, e-commerce collections, trade settlements, and dividend distributions can be moved offshore without central bank conversion approval. The real regulatory friction is at the Chinese shareholder layer: any equity injection, shareholder loan, or inter-company payment from mainland China into Hong Kong must be classified under a lawful current/capital item, filed with MOFCOM/NDRC/SAFE as applicable, and routed through a designated onshore bank before becoming freely deployable in Hong Kong. Profit remittances from the HK entity to mainland shareholders are likewise constrained by PRC capital account requirements and require board approval, audited accounts, and tax clearance. Retained HK profits destined for re-investment face no HK exchange-control hurdle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender rule applies to the Hong Kong entity; all export proceeds in HKD, USD, or CNH may be retained in any authorized bank account. For mainland China-connected export receipts, SAFE requires entities to register in the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and repatriate export proceeds into onshore China within the contracted trade cycle, with overdue collection beyond 90 days requiring written explanation; conversion to RMB is permitted but not mandatory.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central banking authority for the Hong Kong SAR; for mainland China-linked capital flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) exercises regulatory authority over renminbi convertibility and cross-border capital movement.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for pure offshore digital EMIs without a Hong Kong banking or stored value facility license, because the HKMA Banking Ordinance and VF/PSDV regulatory regime require authorization before an institution can take deposits or provide payment services to Hong Kong corporations; unlicensed offshore EMIs cannot independently provide an eligible corporate account and would expose the entity to regulatory non-compliance and frozen funds. HKMA-authorized virtual banks and licensed money service operators may be used as ancillary liquidity rails after primary tier-1 bank onboarding, but the JV should not restructure its treasury around fintech EMIs. Given the mainland China shareholder dimension, offshore EMIs are also legally barred from serving as the ultimate clearing route for RMB/CNH unless they hold a CNH clearing license or partner with an authorized clearing bank.

Turnover: Hong Kong has no balance sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption for private companies limited by shares. Every company incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) must appoint a certified public accountant holding a practising certificate and must have its annual financial statements audited, irrespective of revenue, assets, or number of employees. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 5,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions for the mandatory Local File; the Master File and Country-by-Country Report threshold is consolidated group revenue of HKD 6,800,000,000 or approximately EUR 750 million, as set out in IRD DIPN 59.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but substance remains essential for treaty entitlement. The IRD and Chinese tax authorities will assess whether core income-generating activities are performed in Hong Kong; a company lacking local employees, office premises, bank accounts, independent management, and decision-making substance may be treated as a conduit. The IRD can apply IRO ss.61A and 61B to strike down artificial arrangements, and treaty partners can deny DTA benefits under the principal purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Profit Misdeclaration

Statutory sanction: Prosecution under IRO ss.82(1) and 82A: a fine of up to HKD 50,000, penalty tax of up to treble the undercharged tax, and imprisonment up to 3 years; the IRD may also issue estimated assessments with a 10% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns may be prosecuted and, under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.465, the court can pierce the corporate veil for fraudulent trading and require directors to contribute personally to the tax debt; the IRD may issue a departure prevention notice under s.77B, blacklist the director, and bar departure from Hong Kong until the liabilities are settled.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO s.50AOA to adjust profits to an arm's length level, deny deductions, and impose penalty tax under s.82A of up to treble the additional tax charged; failure to maintain TP documentation is separately punishable and may attract default penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false intercompany invoices and knowingly cause the transfer pricing records to misstate profits can be personally liable for restitution; they may be disqualified as directors and face criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the Revenue, with potential terms of imprisonment.
Shell Company, Treaty Abuse, and Inadequate Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the arrangement under IRO ss.61A and 61B and recompute tax as if the scheme had not existed; mainland Chinese tax authorities may deny treaty benefits and reapply the statutory 10% withholding tax rates on dividends and royalties; penalty tax up to treble the undercharged tax is assessable.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false substance evidence or submit sham treaty relief applications can be personally liable for the uncollected withholding tax, ordered to restore dissipated assets, disqualified under the Companies Ordinance, placed on travel-restriction/blacklisting lists, and imprisoned for conspiracy to cheat the public revenue.
