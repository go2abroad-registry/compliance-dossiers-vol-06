VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong SAR; Hong Kong is a separate customs territory and imposes no general indirect tax on goods or services. Specific duties exist only on alcoholic liquor, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil, and methyl alcohol under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), and stamp duty applies to specified instruments.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold because no general VAT/GST/sales tax is imposed. A business must register under the Business Registration Ordinance upon commencing business irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under Hong Kong law because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST and therefore has no 0% export VAT rate, no input VAT refund, and no zero-rating claim for a Hong Kong taxpayer. Hong Kong profits tax follows the territorial source principle: an ocean Ro-Ro carrier's income from international voyages is chargeable to profits tax only if it arises in or is derived from Hong Kong; income sourced outside Hong Kong is generally outside the charge. If the underlying vehicle export originates in mainland China, the PRC VAT zero-rating for international export transport may apply to the mainland China taxpayer/operator under the State Taxation Administration administrative rules, requiring the PRC export documentation bundle.

  • Hong Kong context: no VAT export zero-rating documentation is required; for customs purposes, retain the electronic import/export cargo declaration, the Cargo Manifest, the Bill of Lading or Seaway Bill for Ro-Ro cargo, Mate's Receipt, commercial invoice, and packing list.
  • PRC export leg (where the Ro-Ro cargo departs from a mainland China port): China Customs export declaration record, the Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill for Ro-Ro vehicle cargo), the commercial invoice and packing list, the export transport or sales contract, and the bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign-exchange receipt/collection statement evidencing export proceeds repatriation to the PRC entity claiming the 0% VAT rate.
  • Where the Hong Kong JV is the carrier, the Hong Kong operator must also retain the voyage employment/berth charter confirmation, the Ro-Ro vessel cargo manifest, and the freight invoice to evidence the offshore-sourced nature of the shipping profit for profits tax purposes.

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no statutory real-time electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API stamping, or anti-fraud digital-stamping mandate for commercial invoices. The IRD eTAX portal supports electronic filing and payment. Invoices may be paper or electronic under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance (Cap. 553), and customs declarations are made electronically through the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department's electronic cargo declaration system; no government pre-clearance of invoices is required.

In Hong Kong, there is no VAT clawback regime because no VAT/GST is levied. For profits tax, failure to maintain adequate business records or making false declarations to the IRD under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) may be prosecuted and can result in fines, imprisonment, and additional tax up to three times the amount of tax undercharged. For customs, false or inaccurate export declarations can lead to penalties under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and related Hong Kong legislation. For any PRC mainland export zero-rating claim, audit failure to substantiate the customs declaration and foreign-exchange repatriation within the statutory period (generally 180 days) triggers reversal of the zero-rating, recovery of unpaid VAT, daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05%, administrative penalties from 50% to 500% of the amount of tax, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent export-tax-refund claims under PRC law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a free and open capital account under the Linked Exchange Rate System. HKD and USD are fully convertible, and there are no surrender requirements, exchange approval gates, or profit repatriation restrictions for corporate entities licensed in Hong Kong. The Hong Kong SAR does not impose mandatory conversion ratios; dividends, capital redemptions, and proceeds from shipping operations may be remitted freely in the original currency through HKMA-authorised banks. The true convertibility constraint arises on the Mainland China side: RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account, and any injection of capital from the PRC parent into the Hong Kong venture requires SAFE registration, an ODI approval certificate, and a designated domestic bank to effect remittance. Current-account trade items such as charter hire and vehicle exports are generally more liberal, subject to authenticity review under SAFE, but the use of offshore funds to repay PRC shareholders or to fund parent operations may be treated as quasi-capital flows and require prior registration. For the Hong Kong entity, therefore, convertibility risk is a function of the PRC parent's compliance, not of local HK controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to the Hong Kong SAR entity. Hong Kong has no export surrender regime; export proceeds may be held, converted, or remitted freely. For any export receipts booked to the PRC parent or a Mainland exporter, SAFE currently requires export foreign-currency proceeds to be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days of the export customs declaration and to be subject to an authenticity review; there is no mandatory surrender/conversion percentage, so the exporter may retain the foreign currency or sell it to a bank at its own discretion.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China for cross-border RMB and capital movement involving Mainland China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cautious Medium-Low. Hong Kong's regulatory framework permits licensed stored value facilities and money service operators to provide digital wallets and cross-border payment collection and disbursement; however, an unlicensed offshore EMI is legally barred from accepting deposits, issuing commercial letters of credit, or providing escrow-grade account infrastructure to a corporate entity. For a deep-sea RoRo operator requiring commercial LC issuance, documentary collections, large USD tonnage payments, and capital verification support, a non-bank EMI cannot replace a tier-1 licensed bank. EMIs may be used only as a secondary collection rail for small freight charges or crew expenses after full KYC, and only if the EMI holds a valid HKMA SVF licence or an equivalent e-money licence accepted by counterparties. The primary treasury relationship must remain with an HKMA-authorised commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong JV should open a capital escrow account at a tier-1 bank and receive the PRC parent injection only after SAFE has approved the overseas direct investment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10,000,000 (approximately) or HKD 1,000,000 (approx.) for cross-border intercompany transactions requiring contemporaneous documentation; the IRD's TP legislation (section 58C and Schedule 17A) applies based on prescribed transaction thresholds: for transactions with a related non-resident enterprise, documentation is required if the aggregate amount exceeds HKD 10,000,000 for property transactions or HKD 10,000,000 for financial transactions, and HKD 5,000,000 for certain others; in practice, the IRD may impose a formal documentation schedule via DIPN 59 and 60, and the safe harbor for lack of documentation is loss of the penalty relief rather than automatic adjustment. For a shipping JV, prepare a Master File if global group turnover exceeds HKD 1 billion, and a Local File for HK intercompany transactions exceeding HKD 10 million; a Country-by-Country Report threshold applies if consolidated group revenue of the ultimate parent is at least EUR 750 million or HKD 6.8 billion (approximately).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has adopted the OECD Base Erosion and Profit Shifting (BEPS) package and imposes economic substance requirements through the transfer pricing rules and the Anti-Abuse Rule in section 61A. The Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are not a Hong Kong domestic statute but are relevant if the JV has a subsidiary or branch in the UAE, BVI, or other ESR jurisdictions; in Hong Kong the equivalent doctrine is the 'central management and control' test for residency and the requirement that a Hong Kong entity earns profits from the carrying on of a trade, business, or profession in Hong Kong. For a Ro-Ro vessel operator the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are: the negotiation and conclusion of charter contracts, the decision to deploy vessels, the management of freight revenue and invoicing, the coordination of port calls, the management of bunkering procurement, the maintenance of vessel registers and certification, and the strategic management of routes and capacity. To withstand an IRD audit, the HK JV should hold board meetings in Hong Kong, employ commercial and operational staff with substantive functions in Hong Kong, keep major bank accounts and treasury decision-making in Hong Kong, execute charter party agreements in Hong Kong, and maintain voyage, maintenance, and safety records in Hong Kong; failure to do so could re-classify profits as China-source under the PRC CFC rules or under the HK-source rules, or trigger PRC CFC attribution on the mainland parent if the HK JV is a 'non-genuine' offshore enterprise.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under section 82(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a person who willfully evades taxes by omitting income or making false declarations is liable on conviction to imprisonment for 3 years and a fine of HKD 100,000, together with additional tax not exceeding 3 times the amount of tax undercharged; under section 80(2), any person who knowingly makes a false return is liable to a fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for 3 years. The IRD may also levy additional tax under section 82A of not more than 100% of the tax under-assessed.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where a director signed the fraudulent return or directed the concealment: the director is personally liable to pay the additional tax and penalties to the extent of the undercharge; criminal prosecution may result in immediate imprisonment for 9 months to 3 years depending on the evasion amount and degree of premeditation; a travel restriction order can be issued by the District Court preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong pending the tax investigation; the director's personal bank accounts and assets can be garnished under the corruption and proceeds of crime framework; and the director may be disqualified as a director for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance.
Shell Company

Statutory sanction: The IRD will disregard the JV under the general anti-avoidance rule in section 61, 61A, and section 61B of the Inland Revenue Ordinance; the assessable profits will be recomputed as if the JV had not been interposed, subjecting the China parent to Hong Kong profits tax at 16.5% computed on all freight income booked in the JV, plus 10% tax surcharge under the IRD's penalty practice; if the China parent has a permanent establishment in Hong Kong, the PRC legal person will also be liable for Hong Kong profits tax on the HK-source income. Under PRC law, the China parent will be subject to the Controlled Foreign Corporation (CFC) rules in Article 45 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, requiring the untaxed profits of the 'actual management' and non-compliant JV to be included in the mainland parent's taxable income; and the Hong Kong IRD may issue an assessment on the JV for the entire global profit if the central management and control is not in Hong Kong.

Director personal liability: Directors of the JV and the China parent who sign the returns as a sham are jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax under assessments. If the shell is used to launder the proceeds of a fraudulent go-around of customs duties, the directors may be charged with money laundering under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, with maximum fine of HKD 5 million and imprisonment for 14 years; a civil claim may be brought by the Inland Revenue Department to set aside the charge or claim restitution of the tax difference; directors face personal asset tracing, freezing orders (Mareva injunction), and overseas arrest red notices (Interpol Red Notice) when the unpaid tax exceeds HKD 1 million and there is evidence of flight risk.
AML

Statutory sanction: The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and the Companies Registry can impose a financial penalty of up to HKD 10 million per contravention, or double the benefit gained from the contravention; the Financial Services and the Treasury Bureau may revoke the JV's Money Service Operator license and creditor may petition for winding-up; directors committing the act may be imprisoned for 10 years if the illicit financial transaction is connected with drug trafficking or 14 years for serious crime proceeds; the vessel itself can be arrested or confiscated under the Dangerous Drugs (Confiscation of Property) Ordinance, and the shipping license can be suspended by the Marine Department.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently supervise the AML-deficient JV owe a personal duty of care; the HKMA can ban the director from directorship in any authorized financial institution for life, fine the director up to HKD 1 million, and issue a personal restitution order to compensate the shipping partners and government for losses; criminal conviction for money laundering carries an unlimited fine and imprisonment up to 14 years, plus a court-ordered confiscation of the director's assets located in Hong Kong, including family-held assets where they are proven to be the proceeds of crime; an international travel ban and disgorgement of all professional fees may be imposed.
