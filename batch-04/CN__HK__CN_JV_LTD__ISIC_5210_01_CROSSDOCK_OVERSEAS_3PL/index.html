VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT registration in China; businesses below the threshold may register as small-scale VAT taxpayers or remain simple-rate taxpayers. Non-resident suppliers have no turnover registration threshold and must register for VAT withholding on the first taxable supply made in China. The Hong Kong SAR has no VAT, GST, or sales tax and therefore imposes no indirect-tax registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC Value-Added Tax Law and its implementing rules, exports of goods physically leaving China under the GACC customs declaration procedures are zero-rated, with entitlements to input VAT refund or exemption under the export tax rebate system. Qualifying cross-border e-commerce goods shipped through overseas-warehouse fulfilment hubs are zero-rated when the seller supplies the goods to a non-resident purchaser and retains export-proceeds collection evidence. Cross-border logistics and warehousing services supplied to non-resident enterprises are zero-rated if the place-of-supply rule places the consumption wholly outside China. Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, so no zero-rating election is required; export-based profits fall outside the territorial source of the Hong Kong profits tax if sourced outside Hong Kong.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) submitted to the General Administration of Customs
  • Ocean or airway Bill of Lading / consignment note
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange collection verification statement
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice
  • Packing list and export customs release/clearance record

China's Golden Tax Phase IV and the fully digitalised electronic invoice system require all VAT taxpayers to issue invoices through the PRC Electronic Tax Administration platform; invoice records are transmitted to the State Taxation Administration in real time for fiscal clearance, and each invoice receives an anti-fraud digital verification/QR code and unique invoice serial number. High-volume taxpayers must establish approved API-based system-to-system integration with the tax administration, and export transactions must be matched electronically with the General Administration of Customs clearance database. Hong Kong has no electronic invoicing fiscal-clearance mandate; the IRD operates e-filing for tax returns only.

The State Taxation Administration and General Administration of Customs jointly cross-match export declaration data, VAT e-invoice data, foreign-exchange collection data, and logistics records. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating claims are penalised under the Law on Tax Collection and Administration and the Criminal Law of the PRC: recovery of the refund, late-payment interest, a fine of one to five times the refund obtained, suspension of export-rebate qualification, and criminal liability where the amount defrauded reaches the statutory threshold. The normal limitation period does not bar recovery in fraud cases, permitting retrospective clawback for all misdeclared periods. In the Hong Kong SAR, knowingly making false statements or omissions in tax returns to evade tax attracts additional tax up to 300% of the undercharged amount plus fines under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under which the RMB is convertible on current account transactions for trade and service settlements, including cross-border e-commerce logistics and warehousing, provided that genuine underlying economic activity is verified through the SAFE trade and payment system. Capital account items remain strictly controlled and require item-by-item approval, filing, or registration. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in mainland China, foreign direct investment inflows are permitted only after the foreign investor obtains the Foreign Investment Certificate / Foreign-Invested Enterprise Filing (FDI registration) and opens a designated foreign exchange capital account. Funds entering through the capital account must be used within the approved business scope, and the conversion of foreign currency into RMB is not automatic; it requires a bank to verify the use of funds against the payment and contract documents. Profit remittances and investor dividends to the Hong Kong parent are permitted on a current account basis once audited financial statements prove the distributable reserve and the corporate income tax has been settled; however, the bank must conduct a non-resident profit remittance review and will require a tax clearance certificate or proof of withholding tax settlement. Export proceeds from the cross-dock overseas warehouse and 3PL fulfillment services are subject to mandatory repatriation and a surrender/currency conversion requirement: the JV must collect all offshore receipts in foreign currency, effect export declaration or service transaction registration, and through the bank convert the receipts into RMB or maintain a foreign exchange account within the permissible settlement rules. Practical hurdles include delayed SAFE registration for capital injections, proving the end-use of converted funds, and the need for real underlying service invoices to avoid fictitious trading. There is no freely floating full convertibility; forward cover is permissible only on a real economic exposure basis, and offshore NDFs in RMB are prohibited for onshore entities. Thus the treasury must keep dual-currency accounts, secure SAFE filings in advance, and route receipts through licensed Chinese banks to avoid penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export and cross-border service receipts, including logistics fee and warehousing revenue from offshore clients, must be repatriated to mainland China within the statutory time limit; SAFE currently requires that the funds be credited to a designated foreign exchange settlement account and then, unless an approved exemption applies, the JV must promptly surrender (sell to the bank) the foreign exchange into RMB. In line with the SAFE Administration of Settlement, Sale and Payment of Foreign Exchange and the 2019 optimal trade arrangements, the surrender timeline is effectively tied to the bank's receipt of the relevant export declaration or service trade certificate, with a mandatory conversion ratio of 100% for current account receipts unless the JV holds an approved requirement for foreign exchange at settlement and maintains a forex account within the allowed quota. Failure to repatriate or convert within the stipulated window is treated as illegal parking of overseas capital and subjects the JV to SAFE administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore financial technology EMIs, such as Airwallex and global virtual account providers, are not permitted to act as the primary bank or custodian for regulatory and foreign exchange flows of a mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. The PBOC and SAFE regulatory framework recognizes licensed Chinese banks as the mandatory settlement and clearing channels for FDI capital injection, export proceeds surrender, and cross-border trade/service payments. Fintech platforms can only play a complementary auxiliary role in supporting Chinese entities through licensed domestic partner banks, and they are not eligible to provide SAFE-registered capital account services. Therefore the viability of an offshore EMI for direct onshore account opening and FX conversion is very low. They may be used by the Hong Kong parent side or by offshore warehouse subsidiaries for convenience in collecting overseas orders, and later remitting lump-sum proceeds to the mainland JV through a licensed bank; but direct substitution of the domestic banking rail is illegal and operationally unsafe.

The PBOC and SAFE regulatory framework recognizes licensed Chinese banks as the mandatory settlement and clearing channels for FDI capital injection, export proceeds surrender, and cross-border trade/service payments. Fintech platforms can only play a complementary auxiliary role in supporting Chinese entities through licensed domestic partner banks, and they are not eligible to provide SAFE-registered capital account services. Therefore the viability of an offshore EMI for direct onshore account opening and FX conversion is very low. They may be used by the Hong Kong parent side or by offshore warehouse subsidiaries for convenience in collecting overseas orders, and later remitting lump-sum proceeds to the mainland JV through a licensed bank; but direct substitution of the domestic banking rail is illegal and operationally unsafe.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File triggered when previous-year related-party tangible asset transactions exceed RMB 400 million, intangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, financial transactions exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 50 million; Master File triggered when total related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese JV must demonstrate substantial business presence in China: local employees, office/warehouse leases, purchasing invoices, logistics records, and operational risk-taking. The HK shareholder claiming DTA benefits must also possess core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, e.g., strategic procurement, treasury, and board decision-making; a mail-box shell will be denied treaty relief under China's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Art. 47) and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Understatement

Statutory sanction: Article 63, Law on the Administration of Tax Collection: comprehensive fine of 50%-500% of underpaid tax, plus daily 0.05% late surcharge; Criminal Law Article 201: up to 7 years imprisonment where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of taxable income.

Director personal liability: The court may disregard the corporate veil under Civil Code Art. 83 and hold directors who instructed or participated in the evasion personally liable to the full unpaid tax and civil restitution. Tax authorities may issue exit restriction notices, blacklist the representative, forbid director re-registration, and, for criminal cases, the responsible director/CFO can be sentenced to imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 41 allows the SAT to adjust income by reference to arm's length principles; Art. 47/48 permit special tax adjustments with interest plus 5% penalty interest; Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law imposes RMB 5,000-50,000 fines for related-party disclosure failures; for deliberate misstatement, Article 63 penalties up to 5 times the underpaid tax apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who approved fake intercompany contracts are considered controlling parties and may be jointly liable for the adjusted tax deficiency; they face commercial blacklisting, freezing of personal assets, and exit-ban measures until paid. If the arrangement constitutes criminal tax evasion, the executives signing false TP documentation face imprisonment up to 7 years.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company (Beneficial Ownership Denial)

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Art. 47 (GAAR and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9) empowers the tax authority to re-characterize the transaction, deny the treaty claim, and apply the statutory 10% withholding tax on the entire payment; the shortfall is recovered including late interest and penalties under Article 63 (50%-500% of the evaded WHT).

Director personal liability: If the director of the Chinese JV facilitated the shell scheme, the corporate veil may be pierced under Civil Code Art. 83 and directors/shareholders jointly liable for the unpaid tax. The legal representative may be blacklisted by the State Taxation Administration and exit-restricted; criminal fraud charges can be brought where forged Hong Kong tax residency certificates are used.
