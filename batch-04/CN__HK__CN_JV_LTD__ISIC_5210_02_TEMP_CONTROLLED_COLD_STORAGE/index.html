Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR); electronic portal: the IRD eTAX portal (accessible through GovHK) for Profits Tax filing and correspondence; Business Registration certificates are issued by the IRD Business Registration Office. The HKSAR is a separate customs territory and separate tax jurisdiction of the People's Republic of China for this dossier.

E-government filing portal

A Hong Kong-incorporated entity must first be incorporated with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Within one month after commencing business, the entity must apply for Business Registration with the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using the prescribed Business Registration application for a local company. The IRD then issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) and the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the entity's tax reference number. A foreign non-resident entity carrying on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong must register under Cap. 310 within one month of commencement; if it has a place of business in Hong Kong, it must also be registered with the Companies Registry as a Non-Hong Kong Company under Part 16 of Cap. 622 and provide a certified copy of its home-jurisdiction certificate of incorporation. No minimum capital is required and non-resident ownership is permitted. The entity must appoint a local authorized representative and maintain a registered office or principal place of business in Hong Kong. Once registered, the IRD enters the entity on the profits tax roll and issues prescribed Profits Tax Returns for the entity in the normal filing cycle.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not offer check-the-box elections, hybrid mismatch elections, or comparable entity-classification elections. Profits Tax is imposed at the entity level under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) on the territorial source principle; taxpayers may advance an offshore profits claim in the Profits Tax Return but this is a substantive claim supported by facts, not a formal election. The IRD may provide advance rulings in certain circumstances, but they are discretionary and do not create a broad election mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT, no GST, and no general sales and use tax. Hong Kong's only indirect duties are those under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, which are inapplicable to refrigerated cold-chain warehousing and perishable pharmaceuticals in standard operation.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST/general sales tax registration threshold in Hong Kong because no such indirect tax operates. A separate Business Registration under Cap. 310 is required for any trade or business regardless of turnover, unless a specific exemption in the Business Registration Regulations applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT or GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate, no zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism for exports. Profits from the export of goods from Hong Kong may fall outside the scope of Hong Kong Profits Tax under the territorial source principle if the selling operations and relevant commercial activities are performed outside Hong Kong; that is an income tax treatment, not an indirect tax zero-rating. Hong Kong also has no foreign exchange control regime requiring proof of export-proceeds repatriation for tax zero-rating.

  • Not applicable for VAT/GST zero-rating, because Hong Kong levies no VAT/GST. For customs statistical compliance, import/export declarations must be lodged with the Trade and Industry Department through Government Electronic Trading Services within 14 days after the relevant date of import or export; supporting documents include commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading or airway bills, and cargo manifests.
  • For the substantiation of an offshore profits claim, the taxpayer should retain the complete commercial file: signed sale and purchase contracts, invoices, correspondence, shipping instructions, insurance certificates, delivery receipts, bank deposit slips, and evidence that the procurement and sale operations were concluded outside Hong Kong.

None. Hong Kong does not impose a real-time e-invoicing clearance, fiscal API filing, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate for commercial invoices. Paper and electronic invoices are both acceptable for commercial record-keeping and tax substantiation. IRD eTAX is the electronic channel for tax returns and correspondence only and does not act as an invoice clearance platform.

There is no VAT/GST clawback because no VAT/GST is imposed. For income tax, if an offshore profits claim is rejected after audit, the IRD may assess additional Profits Tax and impose penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Incorrect returns are subject to fines and additional tax up to treble the undercharged tax, with more severe penalties for fraud. Export and supporting records must be retained for not less than seven years under Cap. 112; failure to keep such records is a separate offence.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a managed floating RMB regime with strict current-account convertibility and heavily monitored capital-account convertibility. Current account receipts and payments for trade in goods and services may be settled only after truthfulness verification performed by designated banks; capital account FDI injections require SAFE registration and the use of dedicated foreign exchange capital accounts. There is no statutory fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds after the 2012 reform; however, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days of the export customs declaration unless the enterprise obtains SAFE approval for overseas retention. Profit and dividend remittances from a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture require the receiving bank to verify audited financial statements, corporate income tax finalization certificates, board resolutions, and applicable capital account procedures. Hong Kong SAR, by contrast, imposes no exchange controls; HKD is freely convertible under the Linked Exchange Rate System (7.75-7.85 HKD per USD), and there are no surrender, repatriation, or profit remittance approvals beyond standard Companies Ordinance and Inland Revenue filing duties. The treasury must therefore maintain a dual-documentation protocol: SAFE registrations for any RMB capital crossing the China border, and HKMA-compliant banking for the Hong Kong operating entity. Convertibility risk is concentrated on mainland outbound remittances, not on HKD current account activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland Chinese export entities, under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures, export FX proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic settlement bank within 180 days after customs export declaration; the current regime does not impose a compulsory fixed conversion ratio, so after repatriation the exporter may retain 100% of the foreign currency in a goods trade FX account or may convert 100% to RMB at the official market rate. Enterprises classified as B or C face more restrictive document-by-document review, mandatory payment flow matching, and in-quota conversion ceilings. For the Hong Kong SAR component, there is no statutory export surrender or conversion ratio; all export receivables may be retained freely in any convertible currency.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); for Hong Kong SAR, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acts as the currency board authority and banking supervisor, although Hong Kong does not impose exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI viability for this China/HK treasury structure is LOW for core capital account and FX hedging flows, and only MEDIUM for ancillary low-value operational payments. Mainland regulation prohibits fintech EMIs from opening foreign exchange capital accounts, performing SAFE-regulated FX settlement, receiving export proceeds surrender, or issuing commercial documentary letters of credit; any such activity requires a licensed mainland commercial bank. In Hong Kong, a fintech offering payment services is ordinarily licensed under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or as an authorised institution, but it is not a commercial bank for corporate treasury purposes and cannot underwrite capital escrow, term loans, trade guarantees, or FX derivatives. The legally compliant route is to use Tier-1 licensed banks as the sole treasury front-end and to restrict EMIs to non-regulated petty cash and travel/procurement cards with low transaction thresholds.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound route is determined by which leg is being funded. For foreign contribution to the mainland JV, wire to the SAFE-designated dedicated foreign exchange capital account of the mainland JV. For funding from the PRC shareholder to the Hong Kong JV, the PRC bank must first complete SAFE overseas direct investment (ODI) registration and then issue the MT103 with the corresponding outward direct investment purpose code; the HK recipient may receive without exchange control approval because HK sits on the free-use side of the arrangement. In all cases, the SWIFT MT103 must cite the SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration number, the foreign investment contract reference, and the monetary authority purpose code for foreign direct investment capital contribution. The receiving bank will require the board resolution, capital contribution schedule, CPA capital verification report, and a Beneficiary Statement of Source and Use. After receipt, the entity must file the foreign exchange registration confirmation and settle funds into RMB only for permitted working capital uses within the scope of the approved business licence. No same-day refunds, split wires, or third-party netting may be used to avoid SAFE monitoring.The operating treasury should use SWIFT MT103 for standard supplier payments, with the remittance purpose field containing the underlying trade/receipt code and the invoice number. In Hong Kong, a fintech offering payment services is ordinarily licensed under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance or as an authorised institution, but it is not a commercial bank for corporate treasury purposes and cannot underwrite capital escrow, term loans, trade guarantees, or FX derivatives. The legally compliant route is to use Tier-1 licensed banks as the sole treasury front-end and to restrict EMIs to non-regulated petty cash and travel/procurement cards with low transaction thresholds.
