Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxed on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate before available preferential incentives. It is not a pass-through entity and no partner or shareholder is directly taxed on the JV's income. The Hong Kong SAR may assert profits tax only on profits with a Hong Kong source; if central management and control are exercised in Hong Kong, territorial-source and treaty-residence analysis must be undertaken, but the default classification is entity-level taxation in mainland China.

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election; a corporate equity joint venture cannot elect to be treated as a partnership or disregarded entity. Hong Kong SAR law likewise has no check-the-box regime; companies are assessed under the Inland Revenue Ordinance on a territorial-source basis. There is no hybrid mismatch election available under either system; China applies general anti-avoidance and controlled-foreign-company rules to recharacterise unusual financing or distribution arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable turnover. Any VAT taxpayer with annual taxable sales above CNY 5,000,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the business may be classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless voluntary general registration is chosen. Because the bonded-warehouse operator is a customs-registered enterprise that will engage in storage, logistics and settlement services, it must complete VAT taxpayer registration from commencement regardless of turnover and is expected to obtain general taxpayer status to access input VAT credits and export VAT refunds. Bonded warehouse services are taxable modern services, generally at the 6% VAT rate, while the headline goods rate is 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from mainland China, including goods sold from a bonded depot or customs free trade zone to an overseas buyer and physically exported under customs supervision, qualifies for VAT zero-rating and export refund when: (a) the supplier is the VAT taxpayer registered with customs; (b) the export sale is evidenced by a customs export declaration; (c) the goods have been loaded and transported out of the customs territory; (d) the overseas buyer has paid and the foreign-exchange proceeds are repatriated into a mainland Chinese bank account in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules; and (e) the commercial invoice and customs declaration values are consistent. Certain cross-border services performed wholly outside mainland China, including qualifying bonded logistics services supplied to a non-resident enterprise and consumed outside the PRC, may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt under the cross-border taxable services rules; however, storage services physically rendered inside the PRC bonded zone remain standard-rated unless the statutory cross-border requirements are met.

  • Customs release or bonded transfer order showing departure from the free trade zone or bonded warehouse and entry into export customs supervision
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill or rail waybill evidencing physical export movement

China requires issuance of electronic fiscal invoices through the State Taxation Administration's nationwide fully digitalised electronic invoice system. All invoice data is transmitted to the Golden Tax IV platform in real time; taxpayers must integrate their accounting or ERP systems with the official electronic invoice APIs for batch issuance and status reporting. Anti-fraud controls include an encrypted tax-control seal, QR code verification, public invoice verification, and automatic cross-matching of invoice amounts with customs export declarations, foreign-exchange receipt records and VAT returns. A taxable transaction that fails the real-time fiscal clearance validation may be blocked before invoice issuance; forged or duplicate invoices are detected through the centralised digital stamping mechanism.

The State Taxation Administration, General Administration of Customs and State Administration of Foreign Exchange may jointly audit export VAT zero-rating claims by cross-matching the export customs declaration, e-invoice, logistics documents and foreign-exchange receipt record. If the statutory documentation is incomplete, the refund is denied; if already paid, the tax authority will claw back the full VAT refund with a late-payment surcharge and may impose monetary penalties, downgrade the exporter's tax credit rating, suspend the right to export refunds, and refer the case for customs penalties under the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export refund claims expose the entity to criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including imprisonment and confiscation of goods, together with blacklisting of the entity and responsible officers in tax dishonesty and customs credit records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's FX regulatory framework, current account items are largely convertible for bona fide trade and service transactions, while capital account items remain strictly regulated. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, as a foreign-invested enterprise, must hold a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account to receive equity injections from the Hong Kong shareholder, and each inbound remittance must cite the FDI confirmation and be applied only to approved purposes. Export proceeds collected by the bonded depot must be repatriated into a goods-trade FX account at a licensed bank; conversion to RMB is at market rate after matching customs declarations and commercial documents, and there is no general mandatory surrender ratio. Profit remittance to the overseas parent is current-account accessible after the entity submits audited financial statements, tax settlement certificates, board-approved distribution resolution, and proof that the underlying income was generated from permitted operations. However, remittance of registered capital, shareholder loans, or liquidation proceeds is a capital-account transaction requiring SAFE approval or registration. A bank acting as agent must screen purpose codes, ensure the FX receipts are consistent with HS codes and bonded warehousing declarations, and freeze or delay any payment where the compliance file is incomplete, creating a de facto profit-remittance hurdle requiring a clean treasury and tax record.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from bonded warehouse outbound sales must be repatriated to the entity's designated goods-trade foreign exchange account at a licensed domestic bank. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the JV may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency or convert them to RMB at market rate. The statutory monitoring timeline is aligned with the SAFE Goods Trade FX Reporting framework under which proceeds are expected to be received and matched to the customs export declaration within the reported payment term; in standard practice banks treat 90 days as the benchmark for deferred settlement, and any receivable outstanding beyond that window must be pre-registered or validated under the enterprise's trade credit schedule. All proceeds must be matched with customs declarations, bills of lading, invoices, and the bonded warehouse entry/exit records before the bank clears the transaction in the CNAPS/CIPS clearing environment.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the designated foreign exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low. Offshore digital EMI accounts and virtual IBAN providers are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE and are legally barred from opening RMB deposit accounts, handling SAFE filings, or acting as the primary FX clearing counterparty for a Chinese-domiciled JV. A licensed non-bank payment institution may support collection of e-commerce proceeds, but it cannot replace Tier-1 banks for FDI capital injection, export surrender, customs tax payment, or trade finance. Any use of an HK-based EMI should be confined to pre-trip expense cards or incidental offshore disbursements, not statutory treasury rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI capital must be routed through a licensed onshore Tier-1 bank using the HK parent's corporate account as remitter and the JV's SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account as beneficiary. The wire must carry the SAFE FDI filing reference and correct remittance purpose category for equity injection in the payment narrative. The bank will perform a multi-layer screening of the remitter and beneficiary, confirm the registered capital amount has not been exceeded, verify the source of funds, and apply a pre-approval exchange rate if RMB conversion is required. After credit confirmation, the JV's certified public accountant issues the statutory capital verification report that evidences the capital contribution and permits further operating disbursements.Outbound settlement for bonded warehouse trade, foreign supplier payments, and ancillary logistics costs should use SWIFT MT103 for straight-through cross-border wires, commercial letters of credit via MT700/MT707 for documentary trade where the payee requires bank payment undertaking, and documentary collections on DP/DA terms for reliable open-account flows. RMB cross-border payments may also be cleared via CIPS to Chinese and participating overseas banks, while domestic CNY transactions will clear through CNAPS. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Low. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed by the Chinese resident JV to its non-resident Hong Kong corporate shareholder, under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3 and 37. Under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement Article 10, the dividend withholding tax is reduced to 5% if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the Chinese dividend-paying company for the required period.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is triggered when related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible-property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million in a single tax year. Master File preparation is required where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transaction reporting schedule must be filed with the CIT return by May 31. All thresholds are in RMB/CNY.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a Gulf-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures impose substance-over-form, beneficial ownership, and core income-generating activities analysis. The bonded warehouse JV must maintain real commercial substance in China: actual employees, leased or owned warehouse premises, customs bonded inventory records, logistics equipment, insurance, operational decision-making, and risk control. A Hong Kong affiliate that lacks real personnel and functions, or whose core income-generating activities are actually performed in China, will be disregarded for treaty purposes, denied treaty benefits, and its income may be reattributed to the Chinese JV with interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Substance Evasion

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust taxable income under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44 and 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, impose interest at the statutory benchmark plus an additional 5 percentage points, apply a six-year special tax adjustment assessment period, and impose penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or approve the conduit arrangement may have the corporate veil pierced under the PRC Company Law, be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, face civil asset restitution, be placed on the tax blacklist with travel bans and exit restrictions, and, as persons directly responsible, face criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to seven years.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax and DTA Fraud

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 37 to 39 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 for 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld, plus late-payment surcharges and collection of the unpaid tax from the non-resident payer's assets. Fraudulent treaty claims using false residency certificates or forged documents also trigger criminal liability.

Director personal liability: Directors exposing the company to this liability face piercing of the corporate veil for civil restitution of unpaid tax, personal fines, asset freezes, travel bans and tax blacklisting. Where deliberate non-withholding or forgery is proven, the officers are personally liable under criminal law with imprisonment up to seven years.
Bonded Warehouse Customs and Tax Fraud

Statutory sanction: Customs authorities may confiscate the smuggled or diverted goods, impose fines up to five times the taxable value of the goods, and revoke the bonded warehouse operating license. Tax authorities may disallow false deductions and impose 50% to 500% penalties. Criminal prosecution may follow under Criminal Law Articles 153, 204 and 205 for smuggling, fraudulent export tax refund, and false VAT invoices, with severe cases punishable by lengthy imprisonment up to life imprisonment where the amount is enormous.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are at highest risk: the corporate veil will be pierced for restitution of unpaid customs duties and VAT refunds; personal bank accounts and assets may be frozen; exit bans and blacklisting will be imposed; responsible directors can be sentenced to years of imprisonment, and in aggravated economic crime cases personal property may be confiscated.
