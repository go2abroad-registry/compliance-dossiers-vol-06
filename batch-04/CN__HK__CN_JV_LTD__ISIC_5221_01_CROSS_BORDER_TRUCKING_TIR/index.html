Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online electronic portal: GovHK e-Tax/e-Business (https://www.gov.hk/en/residents/taxes/efiling/efiling_languages/), Companies Registry e-Registry (https://www.eregistry.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity (e.g., a Mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture) establishing a Hong Kong branch: 1. Register the non-Hong Kong company with the Companies Registry under Part 11 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) using Form NNC3; submit certified copies of the parent entity's incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, certificate of good standing, and the address of the registered office in Hong Kong. 2. Within 1 month of commencement of business in Hong Kong, apply to the Inland Revenue Department for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using Form IRBR1 (available via e-Tax or at IRD offices); attach the Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company and proof of Hong Kong principal place of business. 3. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) containing the Business Registration Number (BRN) which constitutes the Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the branch. 4. For Profits Tax purposes, the branch must notify the IRD of chargeability to Profits Tax using Form IRD 612E or via a letter if no tax return is automatically issued, within 4 months after the end of the basis period. 5. No separate VAT ID is obtainable because Hong Kong does not operate a VAT/GST system; the BRN serves as the sole tax identification number.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax; certain dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance are subject to specific duties)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable — there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in Hong Kong; all businesses (including branches of foreign companies) are mandatorily required to register for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) at zero monetary threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable — Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, so there is no export zero-rating claim mechanism. For goods exported or placed in transit, no indirect tax is chargeable; under the TIR Convention, goods transported under TIR Carnets may move under sealed customs transit without payment of duties or taxes provided they remain under customs control and are exported within the prescribed transit period. Service exports (e.g., international trucking services) from Hong Kong are generally outside the scope of Hong Kong Profits Tax if the source of the profit is determined to be outside Hong Kong, but this is a direct tax territorial issue, not a VAT zero-rating rule.

  • Electronic Cargo Manifest submitted to the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department
  • Bill of Lading / Combined Transport Document (or equivalent road transport document, e.g., CMR consignment note)

No real-time fiscal clearance, electronic invoice clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in Hong Kong. Taxpayers must, however, retain sufficient business records under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance; electronic records are accepted. For cross-border cargo movements, the Customs and Excise Department requires electronic cargo manifest submissions via the Digital Customs System, but this is a customs manifest procedure, not a fiscal e-invoicing clearance mechanism.

No VAT export zero-rating clawback exists in Hong Kong. However, customs transit offences under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) are strictly penalised: making a false declaration or failing to submit a true cargo manifest may attract a fine of up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment of up to 2 years. In the context of TIR operations, failure to comply with discharge requirements renders the carrier jointly and severally liable for all unpaid import/export duties and tax under the TIR guarantee chain, and the IRD may also impose additional tax of up to 200% of the undercharged Profits Tax plus penalties for incorrect tax returns; assessments may be reopened within 6 years (or 10 years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion).

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains full current and capital account convertibility for HKD, USD, offshore CNY (CNH), EUR and other major currencies. The HKD operates under the Linked Exchange Rate System with the HKMA guaranteeing conversion within the 7.75-7.85 HKD/USD band. There are no surrender requirements for export proceeds, no mandatory conversion of foreign currency receipts into HKD, no advance approval for dividend distribution, and no withholding tax on outbound dividends from a Hong Kong operating subsidiary. Profit remittance is therefore constrained primarily by commercial bank KYC, source-of-funds verification, and sanctions screening rather than by statutory FX controls. On the Mainland China side, current account items are generally convertible through SAFE-licensed banks, but capital account movements such as outbound equity investment into the Hong Kong JV require ODI registration, capital verification evidence, and proper filing of cross-border capital flows. These PRC controls do not restrict the HK JV's day-to-day treasury operations but create documentary, timing, and approval friction for cross-border capital injections and dividend repatriation to the Mainland shareholder.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong SAR entity: none. Export proceeds may be held in any permitted currency, converted at market rates, or remitted freely through licensed banks. For any PRC parent entity earning export receipts under the same TIR corridor, SAFE regulations require foreign exchange export proceeds to be repatriated and settled through a licensed bank within the statutory time limit unless an approved offshore retention account has been authorized; this rule does not apply to the Hong Kong JV itself.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the monetary and banking authority for the Hong Kong SAR entity; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) govern the Mainland China shareholders' outbound capital movement and PRC-side FX approvals.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

HIGH VIABILITY as a payment execution layer for fuel card top-ups, driver reimbursements, and low-value cross-border CNH/HKD transfers; LOW VIABILITY as the primary treasury banking relationship. Hong Kong licensed stored value facilities, money service operators, and digital banks can provide customer payment accounts and faster payment rail connectivity, but they are not licensed to underwrite documentary credits, issue bank guarantees for TIR bonds, or hold treasury deposits under the same legal protections as an authorized institution. EMIs are also not covered by the Hong Kong Deposit Protection Scheme in the same manner as licensed banks. Therefore the HK JV should use a Tier-1 bank as the core cash and capital account hub and may integrate an EMI only for marginal operational disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The PRC shareholder must first complete SAFE ODI registration and initiate the remittance through its SAFE-registered bank. The payment is sent via SWIFT MT103 to the HK JV's designated capital contribution account, with the ordering customer named exactly as the PRC shareholder and the beneficiary exactly as CN_JV_LTD. The wire instruction must include the SAFE ODI registration reference number, the share subscription agreement number, and the SAFE BOP transaction code for overseas direct investment equity injection, or the equivalent Cross-Border RMB Information Management System code if the remittance is made in CNY/CNH. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Every Hong Kong company, including a private company limited by shares under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), is required to prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view and undergo an independent statutory audit by a certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance (Cap. 50). There is no statutory exemption based on turnover, total assets, or headcount for private companies incorporated in Hong Kong; all companies are subject to audit, including dormant companies unless they qualify for the abbreviated audit/audit exemption under Sections 449 to 451 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and the Companies (Revision of Financial Statements and Reports) Regulation. For a Chinese-foreign equity joint venture operating in Hong Kong, the mandatory audit is inescapable regardless of size, revenue, or employee count. The audit must be conducted in accordance with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing issued by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions triggers Local File preparation; RMB 1 billion cross-border related-party transactions triggers Master File preparation.

Economic substance scope: The PRC entity must demonstrate that it performs core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as transportation dispatch, risk management, and commercial decisions in the PRC. The arrangement with any Hong Kong affiliate must have commercial substance; under EIT Law Art. 47 and GTAC Circular 2, tax authorities may reconstruct transactions lacking bona fide business purpose. The Hong Kong shareholder must also establish economic substance in Hong Kong to support its tax residency and treaty claim; otherwise treaty relief may be denied and the 10% statutory WHT applied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Underreporting Cross-Border Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law (TCAL) Art. 63: the taxpayer shall be ordered to pay tax underpayments and late-payment interest, plus a penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the tax evaded. Article 64: false reporting results in penalties. Criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201: tax evasion of more than RMB 50,000 and over 10% of total taxes payable leads to imprisonment of 3 years or less; amounts over RMB 250,000 or over 30% lead to imprisonment of 3-7 years, with fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in fraudulent accounts are personally liable to make restitution of diverted funds. Pending tax enforcement, legal representatives may be placed on exit ban (出境限制) and their properties may be seized. Criminal convictions result in imprisonment and fines; the corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a façade.
Transfer Pricing Abuse / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Art. 41 and Art. 122, the SFA may make an arm's length adjustment, increase taxable income and charge interest plus 5 percentage points above the rate prescribed. If the transfer pricing adjustment results from deliberate evasion, penalties of 0.5 to 5 times underpaid tax apply (TCAL Art. 63). Failure to provide documentation triggers documentation penalties of up to RMB 10,000 and may reverse burden of proof; fictitious invoices engage Criminal Law Art. 205.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false transfer pricing contracts and invoices can be personally assessed up to the diverted amounts; they face travel bans, asset freezing, and potential criminal exposure. For criminal falsification of VAT invoices, individuals may be imprisoned for up to life in aggravated cases. The corporate veil may be pierced if the related entity is a pure conduit.
Dividend Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Art. 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable for a penalty of 0.5 to 3 times the unwithheld tax and the taxpayer must pay the tax. Treaty benefits will be denied under the principal purpose test (GAAR, EIT Law Art. 47), and the full 10% WHT will be collected with interest and penalties. In Hong Kong, the Inland Revenue Ordinance s. 61A may strike down artificial schemes and impose additional taxes.

Director personal liability: Directors and legal representatives are personally liable for the uncollected withholding amounts if they knowingly approved the dividend without compliance; they can be subject to exit bans, blacklisting, and seizure of personal assets. Criminal liability for tax evasion applies to individuals involved, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines. Civil asset restitution may be ordered to recover diverted tax.
