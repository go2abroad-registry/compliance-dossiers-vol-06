Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC VAT zero-rating is available to VAT general taxpayers for goods physically exported outside Mainland China under customs supervision and for qualifying cross-border taxable services supplied to non-resident customers where the service is consumed wholly or principally outside Mainland China. Offshore supply-vessel leasing qualifies only if the charter is a genuine cross-border supply, the vessel is used outside the PRC tax territory, the contract and invoicing evidence establish the offshore destination, and the consideration is received from outside Mainland China in foreign exchange. Hong Kong, in the absence of VAT, does not require zero-rating substantiation for profits tax.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (export clearance copy)
  • Charter Party Agreement and export sales contract
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or ocean transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank SWIFT/telegraphic transfer credit advice evidencing foreign exchange repatriation
  • Export VAT special invoice marked zero-rated
  • Vessel flag-state registration and IMO identification document demonstrating offshore deployment outside Mainland China

PRC requires issuance and near-real-time reporting of VAT invoices through the STA Golden Tax Phase IV and the national e-invoice service platform; fully digitalised e-invoices are assigned a nationwide invoice identification and are subject to automated verification, comparison, red-letter reversal, and reconciliation through the STA e-invoice system. No independent third-party fiscal stamping is permitted. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time electronic clearance; records must be kept for seven years.

The STA and Customs jointly audit export zero-rating files. Unsupported, unverified, or fraudulent zero-rating is reversed retrospectively; refunded input VAT is clawed back with late payment interest; under the Tax Administration Law and Customs Law the taxpayer faces penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax undercharged, suspension or loss of export tax refund qualification, inclusion in the serious tax violation blacklist, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law where export tax refund fraud is established. In Hong Kong, incorrect profits tax filings or invoices can give rise to additional tax assessments of up to 300% and standard IRD penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which RMB is currently and legally convertible for genuine current-account transactions (trade in goods, trade in services, and ordinary remittance of treaty dividends) but remains strictly controlled on the capital account. A Sino-foreign equity joint venture operating in China must register all cross-border flows through SAFE-approved banks. Export proceeds are no longer subject to antiquated forced surrender after the 2012 SAFE trade-in-goods reform; however, under the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, export-foreign-currency receipts must be genuinely repatriated and credited to a domestic FX current-account within a period consistent with the declared trade terms and in any event within 180 days of export declaration unless an extension is filed. Capital-account inflows, including the foreign partner's equity injection, must be booked through the FDI capital-injection account and verified by a PRC CPA capital-verification report. Profit distribution to the Hong Kong parent is a current-account transaction only after the JV has paid Enterprise Income Tax, completed its annual statutory audit, passed examination of accumulated prior-year losses, and furnished the bank with the approved board resolution, audited financial statements, and China-Hong Kong Dual Taxation Agreement-based tax residency or withholding certificates. For remittances to Hong Kong, the bank will require the JV to demonstrate that dividends are paid exclusively out of after-tax distributable profits and that a 5% or 10% withholding tax, as applicable under the China-HK DTA and domestic law, has been declared and cleared. Capital-account convertibility remains selective for offshore RMB participation, debt financing, or foreign-exchange re-export arrangements, and all such transactions require prior SAFE or MOFCOM registration, documentary authentication, and post-remittance verification. In practice, JV treasurers should treat every material cross-border payment as a compliance event rather than a routine settlement, with full documentation mapping to the registration or verification code of the underlying transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory conversion ratio applies to general export proceeds today; the JV may retain up to 100% of export foreign-currency receipts in a current-account FX settlement account or convert them to RMB at the prevailing spot or forward rate at its discretion. Statutory repatriation requires that export proceeds arising from physical export shipments be received into a mainland designated bank account within a maximum of 180 days after the customs export declaration, unless the trade contract specifies deferred settlement and an extension or exception is recorded in the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System; failure to reconcile the proceeds against customs declarations will block future receipts or payments and may trigger SAFE administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low/Restricted for the mainland JV; ancillary-only for the HK holding company. Offshore fintech electronic-money institutions such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Neat are not licensed as PRC deposit-taking institutions and are legally barred from maintaining the domestic RMB FX settlement, FDI capital escrow, or import/export verification accounts that the Chinese JV treasury requires. China's FX administration requires all current-account and capital-account transactions of a resident enterprise to be executed by or through a SAFE-designated bank under the PBOC's customer due-diligence and declaration framework. The JV may nevertheless use a licensed Hong Kong MPI or EMI account for the HK side's collection of charter payments from non-China counterparties before repatriation, but no offshore digital EMI can perform statutory capital verification, issue a Chinese bank FX receipt declaration, or discharge SAFE monitoring obligations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign investors domiciled in Hong Kong must remit equity funds via SWIFT or CHATS to the JV's designated FDI capital-injection bank in mainland, using the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code for foreign-invested enterprise equity capital contribution (generally declared under code 621011 as the direct-investment equity injection category) and a remittance reference clearly stating the JV name, registered capital amount, and equity ratio. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% standard Hong Kong profits tax rate for corporations, with a two-tiered rate of 8.25% on the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits; the balance above HK$2,000,000 is taxed at 16.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% under Hong Kong domestic law. Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends, including dividends paid by a Hong Kong company to a PRC resident shareholder, a Hong Kong resident, or any non-resident shareholder. The distribution is outside the scope of profits tax because dividends are not assessable profits of the recipient in Hong Kong. If the payment is made in the reverse direction, namely a PRC subsidiary paying dividends to this HK JV, the PRC statutory withholding rate is 10%, reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement where the HK beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the PRC payer for a continuous twelve-month period and meets all substance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation obligation applies where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions by the taxpayer in a basis period exceeds HK$10,000,000 (or where the IRD serves a notice requiring transfer pricing documentation). A Master File may be required where the multinational group exceeds the consolidated revenue threshold under the Hong Kong Country-by-Country Reporting rules, and a Local File must be prepared for the Hong Kong entity if the local controlled transaction value exceeds the threshold. The HK JV must maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for vessel charter hire, management fees, marine operation services, bareboat leasing, interest on shareholder loans, and any transactions with the Chinese parent or other related parties, including voyage-laden cost allocations and risk-bearing analysis.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have an explicit UAE-style Economic Substance Regulation, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance and the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement incorporate functional and substance requirements through the beneficial ownership principle, the source rule, transfer pricing adjustments under IRO section 61B, and general anti-avoidance rules under IRO sections 61 and 61A. The offshore supply vessel leasing JV must demonstrate that it performs Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: board and management decisions regarding vessel acquisition and charter strategies, treasury and financing activity, negotiation and execution of charter party agreements, invoicing and receivables collection, operational risk management, maintenance budgeting, employment of marine administrative staff, and physical office presence. If those activities are instead performed in mainland China or through PRC-based brokers and dispatchers, the IRD or PRC tax authorities may disregard the Hong Kong entity, reattribute charter profits to a permanent establishment in China, deny treaty relief, or apply transfer pricing recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Incorrect Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under section 82(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, engaging in fraud, wilful evasion, or assisting another to evade profits tax is punishable by a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for three years. Under section 82A, the IRD may assess an additional tax of up to three times the amount of tax undercharged. A knowingly incorrect return is also an offence under section 80, incurring penalties up to HK$50,000 and additional tax of 300% of the undercharge. The IRD may also issue an estimated assessment and impose late payment surcharge of 5% for late payment and 5% after six months.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced where the director personally participated in or authorized the fraudulent return. The director may be jointly and severally liable for the company's underpaid tax, penalized personally, and in egregious cases prosecuted criminally, resulting in imprisonment. The IRD can blacklist the director's Hong Kong tax and business records, and the Companies Registry may disqualify the director under the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years for fraudulent trading under section 465. A travel ban or departure prevention order may also be sought in serious cases where the director is assessed to owe substantial tax and attempts to leave Hong Kong before payment.
Transfer Pricing Avoidance / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the transfer pricing arrangement under section 61 (tax avoidance) and section 61B (arm's length pricing adjustment), reallocate profits to the PRC parent or to the Hong Kong entity as appropriately assessed, and issue additional assessments for up to six years; if fraud or wilful default is shown, the additional assessment period extends to ten years. Failure to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation may result in penalties in the range of HK$10,000 to HK$50,000 plus higher compliance costs, and the IRD may shift the burden of proof onto the taxpayer to demonstrate the arm's length result. The PRC tax authority may also invoke General Anti-Avoidance Rules under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and recharacterize the HK JV as a conduit, imposing 10% withholding tax on outbound payments and interest penalties at up to 0.05% per day for underpaid tax, in addition to late payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement a sham offshore structure can be held personally liable for fraudulent or wrongful trading under section 465 of the Companies Ordinance; the court may order them to contribute to the company's assets to satisfy tax debts and creditor claims. The IRD can issue personal assessment or recovery proceedings where funds have been extracted as dividend or loan to the director rather than retained for tax liabilities. Persistent non-compliance can trigger director disqualification orders, seizure of travel documents, and criminal prosecution for aiding tax evasion, with imprisonment up to three years and civil restitution for the underpaid tax.
DTA Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation / AML and Maritime Sanctions

Statutory sanction: The PRC and Hong Kong tax authorities can deny treaty benefits entirely and reinstate the full domestic withholding rates, with interest and penalties. The Hong Kong Court may impose cumulative sanctions under the Companies Ordinance for failure to maintain the Significant Controllers Register: a fine of HK$25,000 plus a daily default fine of HK$700; under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), unlicensed money service activities, failing to perform customer due diligence, and dealing with proceeds of crime attract fines and imprisonment up to seven years for individuals. Violations of UN sanctions and maritime embargoes can be prosecuted under the United Nations Sanctions Ordinance and the Weapons of Mass Destruction (Control of Provision of Services) Ordinance, with unlimited fines and imprisonment up to 14 years for directors found to have knowingly facilitated sanctionable charter operations. Tax benefits under the DTA will be cancelled and the PRC withholding agent may be charged a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day.

Director personal liability: A director who signs a false DTA claim, submits forged TRC documentation, or knowingly uses the JV to launder maritime charter proceeds will incur civil liability for restitution of all avoided taxes and treasure, be disqualified as a director, and be criminally prosecuted. The court can pierce the corporate veil where the JV is used as an instrument of fraud or as an agent of the Chinese controlling persons, making the directors personally liable to creditors and tax authorities. Under the Companies Ordinance, fraudulent trading liability supports an order for personal contribution to the company's assets without any limit. Travel bans and asset freezes are available under the United Nations sanctions frameworks and the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, and the director can be blacklisted from holding directorship in Hong Kong, China, and the OECD financial system.
