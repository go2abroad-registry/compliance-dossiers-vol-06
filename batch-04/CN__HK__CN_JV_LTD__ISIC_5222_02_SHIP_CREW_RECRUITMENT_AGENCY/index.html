A virtual office or commercial mailbox is not permissible for the statutory registered office under section 658 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election; the territorial source doctrine is applied on facts under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). A taxpayer may present evidence of offshore profits, but this is not an elective regime. Mainland China likewise does not allow a pass-through tax election for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; the entity is taxed under the Enterprise Income Tax Law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general Value Added Tax (VAT), Goods and Services Tax (GST), or Sales and Use Tax is imposed by the Hong Kong SAR; only limited excise duties exist under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST exists. The relevant statutory trigger is the commencement of a business: any person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate within one month under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). For mainland China VAT purposes, registration as a general taxpayer becomes mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5 million; below that amount a small-scale taxpayer simplified mechanism applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to Hong Kong because there is no VAT/GST system; therefore there is no zero-rating regime, no export VAT relief to claim, and no input VAT refund mechanism. For the mainland China export/cross-border services dimension, a VAT general taxpayer may be eligible for zero-rating or an exemption on qualifying cross-border services, including certain crew-management and recruitment-related services supplied to overseas recipients, under the prevailing cross-border services VAT rules published by the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration, provided the services qualify under those rules and supporting documentary evidence is retained.

  • For Hong Kong: no statutory VAT export document package is required because no VAT zero-rating claim exists; trade documentation obligations for controlled goods are limited to Import/Export Declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E).
  • For mainland China exports: PRC Customs export declaration and customs release record from the China Customs electronic port.
  • Bill of lading or air waybill evidencing physical export movement.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and underlying export sales contract between the PRC exporting entity and the non-resident buyer.
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt record or cross-border settlement proof from a designated bank evidencing repatriation of export proceeds.

No real-time fiscal clearance, API e-invoicing, or anti-fraud digital stamping requirement exists in Hong Kong; the IRD does not mandate a prescribed electronic invoice format. Business records must be kept for at least seven years under Section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). If the mainland China VAT nexus is engaged, the STA requires VAT invoices to be issued through the e-invoicing controls and general taxpayers to use the fully digitalised electronic invoice platform with tax-control and invoice-data reporting to the electronic tax bureau.

Hong Kong: no VAT clawback exists because there is no indirect tax. False offshore profit claims are subject to reassessment and penalties; Sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) allow the IRD to disregard avoidance arrangements, and Section 82A imposes additional tax of up to 300% of the tax undercharged as well as fines for fraudulent or wilful tax evasion. Assessments are time-barred after six years except in cases of fraud or wilful default. Mainland China: false export refund or false zero-rating claims are handled under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection; the tax authority may require repayment of VAT refunded, charge late-payment surcharge, impose a penalty of 50% to five times the amount of tax underpaid, and refer criminal liability where the amount is material. The 180-day repatriation deadline is retained as an evidentiary control point for export proceeds substantiation rather than as a Hong Kong statutory deadline.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current account items such as trade in goods, services, and profit remittances, subject to genuine transaction verification and SAFE filing requirements. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled, requiring case-by-case approval, quota-based schemes (e.g., QFII, RQFII), or specific administrative registration for cross-border loans, equity injections, and return of investment capital. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (EJV) incorporated in mainland China, foreign currency capital injections must pass through a capital account settlement system under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration regime, with mandatory reporting to SAFE via the bank. Profit remittances are permissible after annual statutory audit and tax clearance, but require submission of audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificate, and a board resolution on dividend distribution. Disposal of the entity's registered capital or foreign investor exit remittance triggers a separate capital verification and SAFE exit registration procedure. Export proceeds from crew recruitment and vessel management services (cross-border transaction scope) must be repatriated to the domestic bank account of the EJV and are subject to the current surrender/retention rules set by SAFE. There is no blanket item-by-item approval for services export fees, but the bank must perform authenticity checks under the Principles of Truthfulness and Compliance for Foreign Exchange Payments and Receipts. Given the operational nexus in Hong Kong (disbursement hub) and mainland China (contracting entity), intercompany flows must be ring-fenced by contractual documentation, tax residency certificates, and transfer pricing records to withstand SAFE and tax authority scrutiny. Financial derivatives accessible to the EJV for hedging include currency forwards and swaps between RMB and HKD/USD, provided the underlying exposure is real and documented; NDFs are not permitted for onshore corporate clients because RMB deliverable forwards are available. Overall, the regime is globally restrictive but functionally navigable for a licensed maritime crew agency that maintains clean trade documentation and a qualified local banking relationship.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency earned by the EJV must be repatriated to its domestic foreign exchange account or RMB settlement account within the statutory time limit of 90 days from the date of invoice or receipt, unless an extension is granted by SAFE for legitimate commercial reasons. Under the current Circular on Further Facilitation of Trade and Investment, the mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%: the entity may retain receipts in foreign currency or convert to RMB according to business needs; however, payment and settlement in China for domestic operational expenses normally requires conversion through the bank, and the bank must register each collection under the digital SAFE system using the correct transaction code for 'Other business services – Maritime crew services' (typically code 228022 or similar services code). For amounts exceeding the equivalent of USD 500,000 per transaction, a customs declaration or service trade declaration and relevant underlying contracts must be submitted. The surrender rule previously mandating a fixed percentage of export earnings to be exchanged into RMB has been abolished; the current regime focuses on timely repatriation and authenticity review, not forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Payoneer are not viable as the primary banking channel for this mainland China EJV. Under Chinese law, only banks licensed by the PBOC and operating inside the country with a registered presence can open RMB current accounts and receive FDI capital injections. Foreign EMIs cannot act as the depository bank for capital verification, nor can they interface with the SAFE FDI registration and capital settlement system. An offshore EMI may be used only as a payment facilitator for HKD-denominated crew payroll disbursements from the Hong Kong settlement hub, but even that must be backed by a licensed bank in Hong Kong to execute the actual payment wire. The legal constraint is absolute: the EJV's statutory books, corporate seals, and cash flows must reside in a permitted onshore clearing bank. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File and Master File thresholds: HKD 10 million for all controlled transactions other than intra-group services and royalties; HKD 4 million for intra-group services; HKD 1 million for royalty or intangible-related transactions; CbC report threshold: consolidated group revenue of HKD 6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD enforces OECD/BEPS substance principles through the general anti-avoidance provisions in IRO s.61A. A cross-border crew recruitment agency must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities occur in Hong Kong: negotiating crewing contracts, vetting and hiring seafarers, maintaining payroll records, issuing employment contracts, managing vessel staffing operations, holding bank signatory control, and making management decisions from a physical Hong Kong office. Absence of such substance will result in refusal of Tax Residency Certificates and treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(1) (fraudulent evasion of tax): upon conviction, maximum fine HKD 200,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, plus payment of 3 times the tax evaded; IRO s.80(2) (inaccurate return) allows a fine up to HKD 10,000 and additional tax up to 3 times the undercharged amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and verify the tax return are exposed to criminal prosecution and piercing of the corporate veil. Courts may order personal restitution of unpaid tax and interest, freeze personal assets, issue departure prohibition orders (travel bans/blacklisting) under IRO s.77, and impose custodial sentences.
Shell Company - No Economic Substance / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRO s.61 (artificial or fictitious transactions) and s.61A (general anti-avoidance rule) permit the IRD to disregard the shell entity, recharacterize the arrangement, and assess tax on the true economic owner; IRD will refuse Tax Residency Certificates, and mainland tax authorities may impose 25% enterprise income tax plus additional withholding and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who falsely certify tax residence or treaty benefit claims may be prosecuted for furnishing false information. The corporate veil can be pierced for sham transactions, exposing directors to joint and several civil liability for all avoided taxes, asset seizure, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: IRO Part 9A (s.50AAA et seq.) requires arm's-length pricing; IRD may make transfer pricing adjustments, reassess the Hong Kong entity at 16.5%, and impose penalties under IRO s.80(2) up to HKD 10,000 plus 3 times the tax undercharged. Director personal liability: Directors responsible for financial reporting and transfer pricing documentation may be held personally liable for reckless or intentional incorrect returns. Civil recovery can reach personal assets, and if the mispricing is part of a deliberate scheme, directors face criminal prosecution, disqualification under the Companies Ordinance, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 3 years.
