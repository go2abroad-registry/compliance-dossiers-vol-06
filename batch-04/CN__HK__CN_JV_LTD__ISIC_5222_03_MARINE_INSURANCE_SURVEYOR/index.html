Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked capital account rule under Hong Kong law. The first subscribers may fix the issued capital at any lawful amount, including HKD 1 or one share, and the allotment consideration need not be fully paid before incorporation. Any unpaid share consideration must be reflected in the statutory share capital records and called for payment by board resolution. A Chinese shareholder in the Sino-foreign JV should separately complete any PRC outbound investment filing with MOFCOM and NDRC, but that obligation does not impose a paid-up capital floor on the Hong Kong entity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, www.chinatax.gov.cn, and the mainland China Electronic Taxpayer portal for all PRC tax filings; for any Hong Kong SAR registered branch or Hong Kong taxable presence, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, www.ird.gov.hk, under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

After incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the JV is automatically assigned an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number for VAT and corporate income tax under the one-license-one-code system. The JV must then complete supplementary tax registration in the China Electronic Taxpayer portal, select VAT taxpayer status, register legal representative and finance officer identity, submit bank account records and company chop/seal specimens, and activate electronic invoice entitlement. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China, tax registration is ordinarily effected through a PRC withholding agent for China-source income using the non-resident enterprise identifier; the mainland JV remains the primary tax-registered entity. If a Hong Kong branch is established, the branch must also obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. A PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a mainland-resident limited liability entity taxed at entity level; no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election is available. Under PRC VAT, if annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000 the JV may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer using the simplified collection method; above that threshold it must register as a general VAT taxpayer. For Hong Kong profits tax, the territorial-source principle applies and no general tax-residence election exists under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Marine cargo damage survey services are eligible for PRC VAT zero-rating or exemption only if the service qualifies as a cross-border taxable service under the catalogues annexed to Cai Shui [2016] No. 36, is supplied to a non-resident client, is paid in foreign currency, is not deemed to be consumed within mainland China, and is supported by contract filing and documentary evidence accepted by the STA. Where physical hull or cargo inspection is performed in mainland China for an overseas underwriter, the tax authorities may regard the service as performed and consumed in China, making the standard 6% VAT rate applicable unless an accepted zero-rating or exemption filing is obtained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (e-Customs export declaration with bill number) for goods accompanying the survey or for any separately exported cargo samples
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill evidencing international movement of surveyed cargo or vessel components
  • Signed international marine survey service contract or underwriter appointment letter
  • Commercial invoice and VAT electronic invoice issued through the STA digital e-invoice platform
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt certificate or SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System record evidencing repatriation of the offshore payment

All PRC VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved VAT invoice systems, including the fully digital electronic invoice platform; special VAT invoices carry an approved quota and are transmitted to the STA for near-real-time monitoring. Paper invoices require tax-control equipment and tax UKey signature; large taxpayers may use API integration from ERP systems for direct e-invoice issuance. Export-related invoices are digitally cross-checked by the STA against customs export declarations and foreign exchange receipt data, with anti-fraud digital signatures and tax-control serial numbers replacing physical fiscal stamps.

The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly monitor export declarations, VAT invoices, and foreign exchange collection records. If zero-rating is unsubstantiated, the JV must pay the unpaid VAT plus late-payment interest at 0.05% per day and may face administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, with clawback of any export VAT refund already received. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may additionally trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, suspension of general taxpayer status, and enhanced permanent audit monitoring.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully open capital and current account regime. The HKD is freely convertible and is supported by the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the HKMA intervening between HKD 7.75 and HKD 7.85 per USD. There are no surrender requirements, no exchange licensing restrictions, no repatriation moratoriums, and no approval gates for current-account payments such as marine surveyor fees, insurance inspection income, or subcontracted disbursements. Dividends, profits, interest, and liquidation proceeds can be remitted freely to any shareholder, including a Mainland Chinese corporate parent, without withholding tax in Hong Kong. The practical friction sits on the Mainland side: any inbound capital injection from the PRC parent into the HK JV requires prior approval or filing with NDRC, MOFCOM, and SAFE, and the remitting PRC bank will enforce strict FX purpose codes and documentary scrutiny. For trade-in-services income retained in HK, no conversion mandate exists. A marine claims surveyor can therefore maintain multi-currency accounts in HKD, USD, RMB, EUR, and JPY; however, if funds are later sent back to China, the PBOC/SAFE anti-money-laundering and foreign-exchange verification regime will apply. Profit remittance to Mainland China is feasible through a declared dividend or service fee, but the PRC receiving bank will require audited financials, tax residence certificates, and a genuine commercial contract to clear the payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable in Hong Kong. Hong Kong has no statutory export-proceeds surrender, mandatory conversion ratio, or repatriation deadline for foreign currency earnings. The PRC parent, if exporting from Mainland China, must repatriate export proceeds through a designated SAFE-listed bank and may either retain them in a current-account foreign currency account or settle them to RMB in line with prevailing SAFE trade verification and risk account management rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and banking supervisor of Hong Kong SAR, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China administering any Mainland China-originated capital and trade flows affecting the JV.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise is the only nominated fintech rail in the payload and is viable as a secondary, low-value disbursement channel for survey fee refunds, subcontractor reimbursement, and small ancillary port payments. Wise holds a Hong Kong Money Service Operator licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) and is not an authorized banking institution; it cannot issue capital verification documents, operate escrow, provide documentary collections, or participate in HKMA settlement systems such as CHATS. Under Wise's own restricted-business policy, insurance claims disbursements and payments involving regulated insurance underwriters may be rejected or paused without a full source-of-funds file. Therefore the corporate treasury blueprint should treat Wise as an auxiliary rail with transaction caps, not as the primary clearing channel. Feasibility rating: Medium for ancillary disbursements and Low for treasury-grade settlement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party tangible asset transactions exceed RMB 200 million; intangible asset or financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country Report filing is triggered where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue in the preceding fiscal year exceeded RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The PRC tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 50 of the CIT Law. The JV itself must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities, management decision-making, operational staff, survey equipment, risk control, and accounting function are physically and substantively located in mainland China. The Hong Kong shareholder must satisfy the beneficial ownership and main purpose test under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA and the MLI; a Hong Kong company staffed only with mailbox offices and agents will be denied treaty benefits and may be treated as a shell or conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, a penalty of 50% to 500% of the amount of tax evaded; daily late-payment interest of 0.05%; under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, imprisonment of up to seven years and fines for criminal tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved or knowingly participated in the false filing are jointly and severally liable for restitution of the unpaid tax. Courts may pierce the corporate veil by proving abuse of the limited liability structure, imposing unlimited civil liability on the directors and the Hong Kong parent; the legal representative may face an exit ban and restriction on high-consumption activities, and in severe cases imprisonment for tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may adjust taxable income, impose interest at the central bank benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points, and levy a penalty under TAL Article 44 and SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42. Excessive interest may be recharacterized as dividends and disallowed as deductions.

Director personal liability: Directors executing non-arm's length arrangements may be held personally liable for the corporate loss in derivative civil actions; the corporate veil may be pierced where the JV is used as a conduit for tax abuse. Criminal tax evasion penalties apply if the transfer pricing distortion is found to be fraudulent and wilful.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Evasion and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, the withholding agent is liable for the tax not withheld and a penalty of 50% to 300% of that amount. Under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, fraudulently evading withholding tax may result in imprisonment and fines.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial officer who caused unlawful distributions or issued false treaty documentation may be jointly and severally liable to the tax authority. Courts may blacklist the directors, impose exit restrictions, and order civil restitution. Forgery of official residency certificates is additionally punishable under Criminal Law Article 280, with imprisonment and criminal penalties.
