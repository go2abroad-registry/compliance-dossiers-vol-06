Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the RMB is convertible for current-account trade, services, and remittances after bank authenticity review, but remains strictly controlled on the capital account. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture formed in mainland China with a Hong Kong corporate shareholder must complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration, achieve business filing at the local market regulator, and open a dedicated capital foreign-exchange settlement account before any inbound equity capital can be received. Capital account receipts require a valid purpose code, an underlying contract, and a bank compliance check; funds cannot be freely converted without documented working capital, fixed asset, or permitted M&A use. Profit remittances to Hong Kong are permissible after statutory audit, tax clearance, and board approval, and are settled through the bank after deducting applicable withholding taxes and reserve contributions. Because marine towage and salvage are cross-border services, there is no customs declaration; SAFE therefore validates payment purpose via invoices, towage/salvage agreements, classification society certificates, vessel tracking evidence, and insurance documents. Liquidity for ordinary operations is not blocked, but foreign debt, cross-border guarantees, and FDI injections above the registered amount require separate SAFE approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-exchange receipts from towage and salvage services must be repatriated into the JV's designated onshore account within 180 days of the service completion date or the contractual collection date, whichever is later. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under SAFE's current framework; the enterprise may retain the foreign currency in its settlement account or convert into RMB at the prevailing market rate after the bank verifies the authenticity of the underlying transaction.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) exercising authority over Hong Kong-dollar treasury activities and offshore clearing desks for the Hong Kong parent jurisdiction.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low and legally impermissible for the mainland-domiciled JV. Offshore financial technology EMI providers such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Revolut are not licensed by the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission or SAFE to open onshore settlement, lien, or foreign-exchange accounts for resident foreign-invested enterprises. They cannot be used for SAFE-registered inbound equity capital, export receipt repatriation, tax payment, or dividend remittance. An HKMA-licensed EMI may serve only the Hong Kong treasury shell or the offshore group cash pool, and even then only outside the mainland JV's primary clearing chain and tax treasury boundary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must remit the registered capital via SWIFT MT103 from its fully verified corporate Bank of Hong Kong account to the JV's designated onshore capital foreign-exchange settlement account. The wire must state the beneficiary as the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, quote the FDI project code, and use the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose category for inbound direct investment equity capital. The recipient bank will verify the business registration certificate, the final FDI certificate or filing record, the board capital call resolution, and the HK shareholder's identity documentation before crediting funds. Upon receipt, a licensed PRC accounting firm must issue a capital verification report to the local SAFE branch; for operating liquidity, the JV may then apply for RMB conversion by submitting the intended payment purpose, supply contracts, payroll schedule, and tax declarations. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% in Hong Kong on dividends paid out by a Hong Kong corporation. Where the underlying Chinese JV (if incorporated in China) pays dividends to the Hong Kong parent, China's statutory WHT is 10%, reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong DTA if the Hong Kong shareholder is a beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the Chinese entity.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Profits Tax Return is generally issued on the first working day of April and is due within one month, with extension to 15 August (paper) or 15 September (e-filing) for 31 December year-end corporations. The Income Tax Return on Enterprise Income Tax in China is annual, filed by 31 May following the calendar year; quarterly prepayments are due within 15 days after each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong TP documentation thresholds: Master File required where turnover exceeds HK$400 million and cross-border related-party transactions exceed HK$110 million; Local File required where property transactions exceed HK$5 million, financing transactions exceed HK$5 million, intangible transactions exceed HK$1.1 million, and total related-party turnover exceeds HK$10 million. Schedule 17 / TP disclosure form must be filed if related-party transactions exceed HK$25 million. China PRC thresholds: Local File for related-party transactions involving tangible property in excess of RMB 200 million; intangible property transactions in excess of RMB 100 million; financial asset transactions in excess of RMB 100 million; and controlled transactions exceeding RMB 40 million; Master File required where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies the general anti-avoidance rules (IRO s.61A) and the TP Rules which require economic substance when granting DTA benefits and when assessing intercompany transactions. For towage/salvage, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include route planning, vessel operating, crew management, salvage co-ordination and contract negotiation. The HK entity must demonstrate through physical premises, employees, and operating risk that it controls CIGA. If the JV is a Chinese domestic company, the PRC's GAAR and Special Tax Adjustment measures disregard arrangements lacking commercial purpose. A mere mailbox in Hong Kong with no towage operation will trigger recharacterization and treaty shopping denial under the DTA's Principal Purpose Test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent offshore Sourcing Claim

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong IRO s.82A: fine up to HK$200,000 and imprisonment up to 3 years, plus treble tax undercharged. China Criminal Law Art. 201: 3-7 years imprisonment if the evaded amount is huge (over RMB 500,000), plus fines. China CIT Law Art. 48: 15% annual interest under Special Tax Adjustment; TCAL Art. 63: administrative fine of 50%-500% of underpaid tax. IRD can issue estimated assessment to the fullest extent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve false filings can be criminally prosecuted personally; the corporate veil is pierced for tax fraud. Directors may be jointly liable for unpaid taxes, receive travel bans (exit control lists in China, IRD s.76 leave prevention in HK), and face disqualification from managing companies for up to 15 years. Imprisonment terms of up to 10 years for forgery/fraud under Hong Kong Crimes Ordinance and up to 7 years in mainland China for criminal tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC SAT may adjust taxable income to arm's length, add 15% annual interest, and impose penalties under TCAL Art. 63 of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. IRO TP rules may de-disallow deductions and additional tax; s.61A may nullify the tax advantage and impose treble penalties. Failure to provide TP documentation carries a specific penalty under PRC Filing Rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the flawed pricing can be held personally liable for the resulting unpaid corporate tax when the veil is pierced for evasion; may be blacklisted by PRC tax and immigration authorities. For intentional TP fraud, directors face criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201, fines up to double the evaded tax, and imprisonment. Hong Kong directors may face disqualification, and civil recovery of loss from minority shareholders and joint venture partners.
AML and Sanctions Evasion / Shell Company Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong AMLO Cap. 615: fine up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for failing to conduct due diligence. UN Sanctions Ordinance Cap. 537: fine up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 7 years for dealing with designated persons. PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and Criminal Law; SAFE penalties for cross-border forex fraud from 5% to 30% of illicit amount. For a shell company misrepresentation, IRD and Chinese tax authority will deny DTA and impose full withholding.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for sanctions violations if they knew or suspected the prohibited activity; personal criminal fines and 7-year imprisonment. Civil asset restitution may be obtained by tracing and freezing personal bank assets. Directors are blacklisted from leaving China and may be placed on Hong Kong border control; they face lifetime disqualification from acting as directors in serious cases. For egregious violations, imprisonment may reach 14 years under the Crimes Ordinance for conspiracy, and in China life imprisonment for money laundering involving especially serious amounts.
