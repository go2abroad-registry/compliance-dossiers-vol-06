Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online services via the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) Portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

Under PRC law, the Sino-foreign equity joint venture is a resident enterprise. The JV must first obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) as its legal identity by registering with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and issuing a Business License. The USCC automatically becomes the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes. Within 30 days of business license issuance, the JV must complete enterprise income tax, VAT, and other tax registrations through the local tax bureau under the STA, either in person or via the e-Tax portal. Non-resident foreign shareholders receiving PRC-source income must be registered for tax recordal, with the JV acting as withholding agent; if no PRC permanent establishment exists, non-resident enterprise income tax applies at 10% or a reduced treaty rate, and no separate USCC/VAT ID is required for the foreign shareholder. For VAT, the JV acquires general taxpayer status when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or by voluntary election, and the VAT taxpayer identifier is directly linked to the USCC.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT reform document Caishui [2016] No. 36, zero-rated VAT treatment is available for (i) exports of goods by registered VAT taxpayers and (ii) international transport services supplied by PRC VAT taxpayers where the service is performed in cross-border trade. For this bonded road feeder truck fleet, zero-rating is sustainable only where the service is directly connected with i) the export carriage of goods from a Chinese customs-bonded airport or port to a foreign destination, or ii) the inbound leg into China under a bonded customs regime, evidenced by authorized customs export declarations, GPS-sealed truck cabin records, customs transit documentation, and international waybills. Purely domestic bonded-road movements within China between bonded logistics zones remain taxable at the applicable domestic transport VAT rate (9%).

  • Customs export declaration form (electronic export bill of entry released by China customs computer system)
  • Original or certified truck manifest / CMR consignment note with GPS sealing record
  • Multimodal bill of lading, sea bill of lading, or air waybill covering the international transport leg
  • Customs transit documentation for bonded goods moving through the bonded logistics park or airport customs-controlled zone
  • Commercial invoice, transport contract, and packing list for the cross-border transaction
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher (the PRC modern equivalent of FIRC), issued by an authorized foreign-exchange bank, confirming repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory deadline

China mandates a nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) system operated by the STA. All VAT general taxpayers must issue e-invoices through the official e-invoice service platform. Each invoice is digitally signed, encrypted, and transmitted to the tax authority in real time at the moment of issuance; the system generates an anti-fraud verification code and applies an electronic tax-control stamp. The legacy golden-tax controlled invoice software and physical tax-control devices are being phased out in favor of e-invoice digital logging, and non-compliance can block invoice issuance and VAT input-credit claims.

Upon audit, if the tax authority determines that zero-rating was incorrectly applied due to insufficient documentation, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the statutory deadline, artificial routing, or fraudulent declarations, the VAT refund or exemption is clawed back and a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% (5/10,000) accrues on the underpaid tax from the date the tax became due. Administrative penalties range from 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Serious export-tax-fraud cases are prosecuted under the PRC Criminal Law as export tax fraud, carrying criminal fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment. Concurrently, Customs Law sanctions may apply for false or inaccurate declarations in the bonded transit and export process.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The jurisdiction for this entity formation is Hong Kong SAR, which operates as an offshore special territory with full current and capital account convertibility. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) administers the Linked Exchange Rate System, where the Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the US dollar within a band of 7.75 to 7.85. There are no surrender requirements on export proceeds in Hong Kong, and no statutory foreign exchange controls restrict profit remittance. However, the ultimate parent entity in mainland China is subject to SAFE regulation. For the Chinese shareholder injecting capital into the Hong Kong JV, the outward remittance must be routed through SAFE-approved channels under the foreign direct investment (ODI) framework. Capital account items in mainland China, including overseas investment subscriptions and guarantees, require registration, approval, and verification with SAFE and the relevant provincial branch. Current account items such as trade-related payments are generally freely convertible, but require a valid underlying commercial document. For the Hong Kong treasury centre, conversion between HKD and USD is unrestricted. Conversion between CNY and HKD or USD is permissible in Hong Kong through the offshore CNH market; nevertheless, prudential limits and reporting obligations apply to large currency conversions. The relationship between the mainland JV operations and the Hong Kong holding/treasury entity must be documented through a genuine cross-border service and payment scheme to avoid the risk of re-characterisation as disguised capital flight. Profit remittance from Hong Kong to mainland shareholders is subject to Chinese withholding tax under the double tax agreement, not to FX controls. The principal liquidity hurdle is not convertibility but the compliance burden of moving funds across the border in a way that satisfies both HKMA anti-money laundering expectations and SAFE regulatory reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

In mainland China, SAFE mandates that export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into the domestic receiving bank account via a domestic foreign currency account or a special settlement account. The statutory surrender timeline is not a fixed hourly limit under the modern registration system; exporters must complete receipt and settlement or retention within the regulatory time frame, usually within a reasonable period aligned with the collection term of the export contract. SAFE allows enterprises to keep foreign exchange in their domestic retention accounts subject to a cap determined by their prior operating scale, currently set at a dynamic ratio which has varied between 5% and 30% of the trade volume base; however, for a bonded transportation company, actual surrender and settlement are to be conducted under the enterprise's trade-investment facilitation ledger. The practical rule is that all export proceeds must be repatriated to a SAFE-registered domestic bank account within 180 days of customs declaration, with mandatory conversion to CNY only if the enterprise chooses to settle or if the retention cap is exceeded. In Hong Kong, no surrender requirement exists for the treasury subsidiary, which may hold HKD, USD, CNH or other freely convertible currencies in its operating accounts without forced conversion.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers, including neobanks and electronic money institutions licensed in the EEA or Singapore and operating under the Hong Kong stored value facility or money service operator regime, have very limited viability for this treasury mandate. A customs bonded truck fleet engaged in airport feeder services must execute high-value B2B cross-border payments, support documentary collection and commercial letters of credit, and receive customs-bonded settlements that require a clear legal chain to a regulated bank. Hong Kong authorized institutions do permit some fintech-led corporate accounts, but almost all EMIs are not licensed to take deposits or issue trade finance instruments. The legal constraint is that a deposit-taking institution license is required for any entity to accept client funds in Hong Kong. Most EMI licences are stored value facility licences which cannot support trade finance, FX forwards or large-value settlement. Moreover, mainland Chinese shareholder obligations under SAFE require the receiving bank to be an authorised institution with a direct SWIFT relationship and the capacity to issue capital verification reports. EMI platforms cannot issue such reports. Therefore, the recommended structure is to use a tier-1 licensed bank for all regulated accounts and to permit an EMI only as a named beneficiary pay-out channel in low-value operational streams, not as the primary account provider.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The mainland parent must route the equity injection through a designated SAFE-registered bank in China, using the foreign exchange purpose code for overseas direct investment and filing the ODI remittance under the SAFE digital platform. The bank will debit the parent's RMB account, purchase HKD or USD at the onshore rate, and transfer via SWIFT to the Hong Kong JV's capital account at a licensed bank in Hong Kong. The Hong Kong bank will treat the payment as a capital inward remittance and record it as share capital after receiving a certificate of inward remittance. The JV must then engage a Hong Kong certified public accountant to conduct a capital verification and issue a capital verification report. No funds may be transferred to the operating account until the escrow account has been fully funded and the Companies Registry annual return confirms the issued share capital. For the bonded trucking operation, any subsequent capital injections must follow the same route with updated ODI filings. Trade-related current account payments from the mainland shareholder to the Hong Kong JV for management fees or equipment leases must be supported by underlying contracts and the standard SAFE current account transactional form. The receiving Hong Kong bank must file the inward remittance with the HKMA if the transaction exceeds HKD 800,000, and the remittance must include the proper purpose code for international transport services.Trade settlements for the customs bonded truck fleet operation will use three main clearing rails. First, SWIFT MT103 for direct credit transfers when paying overseas subcontractors, airport handling agents, fuel suppliers, and insurance providers; the MT103 must show the complete remitter/beneficiary chain, invoice numbers and the transport service descriptor ISIC 5222. Second, commercial letters of credit (L/Cs) when purchasing vehicles or sophisticated GPS-sealed telematics systems from overseas suppliers. The L/Cs must be issued by the Hong Kong operating bank through the SWIFT network as MT700 messages, with the joint venture as applicant and the overseas supplier as beneficiary, and must incorporate the Incoterms and delivery milestones under the bonded logistics contract. Third, documentary collections can support smaller equipment import settlements. For outbound payments to the mainland partner or mainland subcontractors, the Hong Kong entity must make cross-border RMB payments through the clearing platform at the bank or use USD remittances with the correct inward remittance code; the mainland receiving bank will process the payment as a service import under SAFE's current account rules. The bond and customs duty obligations will be settled through a dedicated customs payment account that is linked to the electronic customs payment system, allowing immediate payment of duties, taxes and GPS bond fees without breaking the foreign exchange compliance chain.Deliverable forward contracts with licensed Hong Kong banks for HKD-USD exposures arising from the Linked Exchange Rate System, available without restriction but within HKMA standard market conduct requirements.Offshore CNY deliverable forwards (CNH forwards) between the Hong Kong JV and a licensed Chinese bank subsidiary in Hong Kong to hedge operational income in RMB against HKD payment obligations.Currency swaps between HKD and USD or HKD and CNY for term funding and capital inflows, permitted only with a licensed authorized institution in Hong Kong and subject to HKMA classification as over-the-counter derivatives requiring contractual documentation under ISDA.NDFs are not permissible as a primary hedging instrument for this entity because onshore mainland access to NDFs is prohibited and the Hong Kong treasury entity would not require an NDF when deliverable CNH forwards are available through the offshore market.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI providers, including neobanks and electronic money institutions licensed in the EEA or Singapore and operating under the Hong Kong stored value facility or money service operator regime, have very limited viability for this treasury mandate. A customs bonded truck fleet engaged in airport feeder services must execute high-value B2B cross-border payments, support documentary collection and commercial letters of credit, and receive customs-bonded settlements that require a clear legal chain to a regulated bank. Hong Kong authorized institutions do permit some fintech-led corporate accounts, but almost all EMIs are not licensed to take deposits or issue trade finance instruments. The legal constraint is that a deposit-taking institution license is required for any entity to accept client funds in Hong Kong. Most EMI licences are stored value facility licences which cannot support trade finance, FX forwards or large-value settlement. Moreover, mainland Chinese shareholder obligations under SAFE require the receiving bank to be an authorised institution with a direct SWIFT relationship and the capacity to issue capital verification reports. EMI platforms cannot issue such reports. Therefore, the recommended structure is to use a tier-1 licensed bank for all regulated accounts and to permit an EMI only as a named beneficiary pay-out channel in low-value operational streams, not as the primary account provider.

Turnover: Under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a Hong Kong incorporated private company is exempt from the statutory auditor reporting requirement only if it qualifies as a small private company or as a member of a small private group. To qualify as a small private company, the company must satisfy at least two of the following three conditions for the financial year: (i) total annual revenue not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000); (ii) total assets at the balance sheet date not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000); and (iii) the average number of employees during the financial year not exceeding 100. The exemption is not available to public companies, listed companies, banks, deposit-taking companies, insurers, licensed corporations under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, money service operators, or trust companies. A company that does not qualify for the small private company exemption must appoint a Hong Kong certified public accountant (practising) as statutory auditor and deliver an independent auditor's report prepared in accordance with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing. In practice, for a customs-bonded truck fleet entity with significant turnover and intercompany airport feeder arrangements, compulsory independent audit will apply unless the HKD 10 million revenue threshold and both additional balance sheet and employee thresholds are all clearly satisfied. Even where audit-exempt, the company must prepare financial statements giving a true and fair view and must generally submit audited financial statements and tax computation to the Inland Revenue Department on the Profits Tax Return; the Commissioner may require audited financial statements where turnover exceeds HKD 5,000,000 or where the business involves customs bonded operations.
