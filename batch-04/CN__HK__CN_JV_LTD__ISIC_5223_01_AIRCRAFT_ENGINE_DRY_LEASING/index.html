Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital, no authorised capital minimum, and no blocked escrow account requirement exists under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 for a private company limited by shares. The JV may adopt a nominal issued capital such as 10,000 ordinary shares of HKD 1.00 each. The Companies Registry does not require capital escrow; any escrow arrangement in connection with aircraft engine leasing is a commercial matter between the JV, lessee, lessor and financing bank. In practice, banks and lessors will require a disclosed capital commitment, shareholder loan facility or parent guarantee proportionate to the lease portfolio.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable — the JV does not occupy a factory, chemical plant or warehouse and no Fire Services Department fire safety certificate, Civil Defense fire permit or structural load inspection is required for the leasing administration activity. If a leased engine is ever stored in Hong Kong, the storage operator must obtain the relevant Dangerous Goods licence, fire services approval and any Customs approval.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), administered through the provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; official online filing and compliance portal is the nationwide Electronic Taxpayer Portal (Electronic Tax Bureau).

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must first obtain a business license and Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the JV must complete initial tax registration with the competent local tax authority through the Electronic Taxpayer Portal, submitting the certificate of incorporation, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, board resolutions, shareholder and ultimate beneficial ownership information, and bank account details. The competent tax authority verifies the filing and records the USCC as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for the PRC resident entity. The VAT taxpayer profile is then activated, and the JV is classified as either a small-scale VAT taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer depending on annual taxable sales. A foreign non-resident shareholder, including a Hong Kong SAR resident company, is not automatically registered for PRC tax; before receiving China-sourced income or claiming treaty benefits, the non-resident must obtain a Non-resident Taxpayer Code through the Electronic Taxpayer Portal or the competent tax authority and provide a valid Hong Kong SAR tax residency certificate where applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the JV is a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxed at entity level on worldwide income; it is not pass-through, not territorial, and is not located in a tax-exempt zone).

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or a hybrid mismatch election. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise for Corporate Income Tax purposes; VAT general taxpayer classification follows statutory turnover thresholds and is not a treasury check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every PRC-established enterprise must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance regardless of turnover. For VAT purposes, annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (five million Renminbi) make mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the entity is generally classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer, unless it elects general taxpayer status under the statutory criteria.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available only for genuine cross-border transactions. For an exported aircraft engine, the PRC exporting JV must complete all customs export formalities, be named as the exporting seller/consignor on the customs declaration, and receive the export proceeds in foreign exchange; if those conditions are met, the export sale may be zero-rated with refund of input VAT. For a cross-border dry lease of an aircraft engine, qualifying lease rentals may be treated as exempt or zero-rated under the cross-border taxable services rules only if the leasing service is supplied to an overseas enterprise and the leased engine is consumed entirely outside China, supported by contemporaneous lease documentation, customs temporary export/import records, and foreign exchange receipts. A domestic lease of the engine to a PRC airline remains taxable at 13%. This business does not involve qualifying offshore digital services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry/Export Goods Declaration) in the name of the exporting JV
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill if the engine is transported by air) evidencing physical export from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank receipt of export proceeds in foreign exchange credited to the JV account

China operates a real-time electronic invoice clearance regime through the nationwide digitalized electronic invoice system. VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices, must be issued through STA-approved invoicing software or the Electronic Taxpayer Portal, with issuance metadata transmitted to the tax authority in near real time. High-volume issuers integrate through STA-recognized application programming interfaces (APIs). Each valid digital invoice carries a unique invoice code and number, an electronic seal, and an anti-counterfeit digital signature; fully digitalized e-invoices do not require physical fiscal stamping, but paper VAT invoices remain subject to tax-control equipment and encrypted anti-counterfeit codes.

Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, unsubstantiated or fraudulent export zero-rating and VAT refund claims are subject to retrospective clawback of the refunded VAT and input VAT reversal, plus overdue-payment interest at 0.05% per day and administrative fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded or falsely refunded. Fraudulent export refund declarations may also be referred for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law for export-refund fraud, and the taxpayer may be placed on the national tax credit blacklist, restricted from future export refund facilitation, and subjected to extended audit examination of the relevant tax periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Within mainland China, PBOC and SAFE operate a managed floating exchange rate system. Current account flows have achieved de facto full convertibility for genuine trade and service transactions; the capital account remains regulated, requiring prior approval or registration for outbound direct investment into Hong Kong. For the CN-HK JV, lease rental inflows into the Hong Kong entity are freely convertible into HKD/USD under HKMA's free convertibility framework, but remittances of dividends or management fees back to mainland shareholders require tax clearance and a treaty residence certificate. The central hurdle is the capital account conversion of the original equity injection: Chinese forex controllers require the parent to evidence overseas investment registration and to settle only through designated banks. Repatriated profits from Hong Kong must be credited through a Chinese bank with a verified service trade payment declaration or returns on investment. There are no surrender requirements for foreign currency retained in Hong Kong, while mainland export proceeds must be repatriated to domestic settlement accounts within statutory timelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE mandates repatriation of export proceeds to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration; an extension to 180 days may apply with bank approval for certain trade terms. Since 2012, no mandatory conversion ratio is enforced; exporters may freely choose to retain the proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or convert to CNY at the market rate. For cross-border leasing receipts classified as services, the proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days of invoice issuance and must be reconciled against the validated leasing agreement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), and Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the currency board regulator for Hong Kong SAR.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Group annual turnover above HKD 750 million requires Master File and Local File. Small transaction exemptions apply: intercompany transfers of real property or tangible goods up to HKD 5,000,000 per category, and other transactions up to HKD 1,000,000. Aircraft engine dry leases involve high-value transactions and will always exceed the de minimis thresholds, mandating full TP documentation.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a codified ESR but the IRD applies economic substance principles to source-of-profit tests and anti-avoidance rules. For the aircraft leasing concession, the company must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) — negotiation, approval, execution of lease agreements, asset management, and risk assessment — are performed in Hong Kong with adequate staff and premises. Failure to satisfy CIGA results in loss of the 8.25% concessionary rate and reapplication of 16.5%, with potential penalties under GAAR (IRO s.61A). The company should maintain records of board meetings, staff presence, and operational decision-making in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82(1) — maximum penalty of treble the amount of tax undercharged plus HK$100,000; for incorrect returns s.80(2) provides a fine of HK$10,000 and treble the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the financial penalties; criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment of up to 3 years. IRD may apply for travel bans (prohibition from leaving Hong Kong) and asset freezing orders under IRO s.76. Directors may also be disqualified under the Companies Ordinance for fraudulent trading.
Transfer Pricing / Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAI-50AAU empower the IRD to adjust prices and assess additional tax; non-compliance with documentation requirements carries penalties up to 100% of the additional tax (IRO s.50AAU). False statements in TP documentation may trigger criminal prosecution under s.82.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve incorrect tax returns or TP disclosures may be personally liable for penalties. In severe cases, they may be prosecuted for tax fraud, leading to imprisonment of up to 3 years and personal asset seizure. The IRD can issue a director's assessment to recover unpaid corporate taxes.
AML / Shell Company / Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) Cap.615 s.9 — on conviction, a fine of HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment of 7 years. The IRD may challenge the concession via GAAR (s.61) and recover full tax plus penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally liable for AML offences due to deficient KYC and anti-money laundering controls. Personal civil liability for tax if the veil is pierced; travel bans (blacklisting) and asset freezing orders can be imposed under AMLO; imprisonment up to 7 years. Additionally, directors may be debarred from acting as directors for up to 15 years.
