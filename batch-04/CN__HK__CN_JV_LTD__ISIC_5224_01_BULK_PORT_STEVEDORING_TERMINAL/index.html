Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private limited company; however, the Marine Department's terminal operator licensing criteria and the JV's bank transaction threshold require demonstration of an initial paid-up capital of at least HKD 10,000,000. The funds shall be credited to a restricted capital deposit account with a licensed bank under an escrow-style arrangement for no less than 60 days, and the bank-issued Capital Deposit Confirmation Notice shall be submitted with the stevedoring licence application.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department will issue a Fire Safety Certificate under section 7 of the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) confirming adequate fire escape, extinguishers, hydrants, and emergency lighting. The Buildings Department must inspect and certify structural load capacity of the quay, crane rails, hardstanding and storage piles under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123). Labour Department (Occupational Safety & Health Ordinance Cap. 509) shall approve the workplace safety manual, forklift licensing, PPE, and stevedore training. Dangerous Goods (as per Dangerous Goods Ordinance Cap. 295) must obtain separate authorisation where bunkering tanks or chemical containers are stored on site.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private limited company; however, the Marine Department's terminal operator licensing criteria and the JV's bank transaction threshold require demonstration of an initial paid-up capital of at least HKD 10,000,000. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not have a check-the-box regime or a hybrid mismatch election. The entity-level tax classification of a Hong Kong limited company is determined by the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) and the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), subject to the territorial source principle; no elective tax classification is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT / GST / Sales and Use Tax (Hong Kong does not impose any broad-based indirect tax; specific duties under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance, Cap. 109, do not apply to stevedoring services).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold because no VAT/GST is levied. However, under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), every person carrying on business in Hong Kong, including non-Hong Kong companies, must obtain a Business Registration Certificate; there is no turnover exemption from that obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not levy VAT, GST, or sales tax, and therefore there is no statutory 0% VAT export rate, no export VAT refund mechanism, and no zero-rating claim to substantiate. For profits tax purposes, stevedoring and cargo handling services physically performed within Hong Kong waters are generally Hong Kong sourced and assessable to profits tax; any offshore-source position must be supported under the territorial source principles of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

  • Where profits tax source or transfer pricing evidence is required, retain the Bill of Lading for the bulk vessel, cargo manifest and stevedoring tally sheets, terminal handling service agreement, port authority service invoices, SWIFT wire transfer confirmations, and port clearing statements.

Trade invoices are commercial documents; business records must be retained under section 51 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) to support profits tax computations. Port terminal tariffs are issued as commercial invoices and are not cleared through any government fiscal system.

No VAT clawback or false zero-rating penalty can arise in Hong Kong because no indirect tax is levied. However, if the Inland Revenue Department recharacterises a claimed offshore-source profit or applies anti-avoidance provisions under sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, additional profits tax (up to the applicable 16.5% corporate rate), interest, and additional tax of up to three times the undercharged tax may be imposed. Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under the Linked Exchange Rate System with the HKD freely convertible. Current-account and capital-account transactions are generally not subject to prior exchange-control approval, creating a materially different environment from mainland China where RMB convertibility on the current account is broad but capital-account convertibility remains controlled by SAFE. For the Hong Kong-incorporated JV, export proceeds, port stevedoring revenues, service fees, shareholder loans, and international trade settlements may be received in USD, HKD, EUR, CNY, or other currencies without mandated conversion. There is no statutory requirement to surrender export earnings, no import payment approval for genuine commercial transactions, and no FX conversion approval for ordinary operating cash flows. Profit remittance to foreign or mainland Chinese shareholders may be made freely from Hong Kong, subject to corporate formalities such as board approval, audited financial statements, and tax clearance where applicable. The practical constraint is mainland-side: if the Chinese JV partner injects RMB or receives dividends back into China, SAFE foreign-investment filing, capital-project verification, and current-account income repatriation rules must be observed. Settlement liquidity is supported by HKMA-regulated licensed banks, the interbank USD/HKD market, CHATS RTGS systems for HKD, USD, EUR, and RMB clearing, and CIPS for offshore RMB flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable under Hong Kong law. The HKMA imposes no statutory surrender timeline and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; all foreign-currency receipts, including USD, EUR, and RMB, may be retained in unrestricted multi-currency accounts. If such proceeds are subsequently distributed or upstreamed to a mainland Chinese parent or JV partner, SAFE current-account collection, repatriation, and verification requirements apply, and the relevant SAFE system filing must be completed within the reporting period prescribed for the mainland entity's foreign-exchange annual inspection and income-registration records.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for RMB transactions and capital flows between Hong Kong SAR and mainland China, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) governs mainland-side registration and verification

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue (Transfer Pricing) Rules (Cap. 112I), a corporate taxpayer must prepare Master File and Local File if its turnover exceeds HKD 200 million, related-party transactions in specified categories reach HKD 22 million, or certain thresholds apply for land, finance, and intangible transactions (e.g., total amount of covered transactions exceeding HKD 5 million for inventory and HKD 1 million for services/royalties when the taxpayer also meets the small taxpayer threshold of HKD 2 million in assessable profits). For the bulk port stevedoring JV, if the Hong Kong company transacts with its mainland Chinese parent or affiliates for vessels, cargo handling equipment leases, management fees, terminal services, or cross-border loans, documentation is required when the aggregate related-party receipts exceed the de minimis threshold; otherwise, the taxpayer must still maintain sufficient contemporaneous TP documentation (a prudent business rationale file) to avoid penalties.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not currently impose EU Code of Conduct-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for all entities, unlike the Caricom/Cayman-style ESR; however, a Hong Kong company claiming DTA benefits, treaty resident status, or exemption on offshore income must demonstrate real economic substance in Hong Kong. The IRD applies the 'central management and control' test to determine tax residence, requiring board meetings, strategic decisions, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) to be performed in Hong Kong. For the JV engaged in stevedoring at a bulk port terminal, the place of loading/unloading and provision of stevedoring services at Hong Kong waters constitutes the CIGA, meaning profits are inherently Hong Kong-source; the JV should maintain employment records, vessel logs, terminal leases, client contracts, and supervision arrangements to evidence that operational decision-making and actual port activities are performed in Hong Kong. A mere 'shell' Hong Kong entity that subcontracts all its substantive activities to mainland Chinese affiliates and lacks local office, staff, and management may be challenged under IRO s. 61 or s. 61A, and the PRC tax authority may recharacterize or deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test if the only purpose of the Hong Kong intermediary is treaty shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Willful Incorrect Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112, IRO s. 82) – conviction on indictment: fine of HKD 50,000, imprisonment for up to 3 years, and a further penalty of treble the tax undercharged. On summary conviction, the penalty may be a fine of HKD 10,000 and imprisonment for 6 months, plus treble tax. The IRD may also issue recovering assessments and impose default penalties of up to 3x additional tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or authorize a false tax return commit an offence and may be personally criminally liable, facing imprisonment for up to 3 years. The court may also order restitution of unpaid taxes from personal assets through civil recovery, and under IRO s. 82A the IRD may seek arraignment of directors for tax fraud. Directors may be disqualified from acting as company directors under company law and may have their travel restricted or be blacklisted by the Immigration Department when there is a risk of absconding (e.g., denial of departure orders under the Summary Offences Ordinance and debt recovery proceedings).
Transfer Pricing Fraud and Lack of Arm's Length Documentation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap. 112 and Inland Revenue (Transfer Pricing) Rules Cap. 112I – the IRD may adjust the taxable profits to reflect an arm's length result under IRO s. 50AAP, impose a penalty of up to 100% of the additional tax assessment (through normal IRO s. 82 anti-avoidance and penalty provisions), and in egregious cases, initiate criminal prosecution for tax evasion. The IRD may also impose late filing penalties on mandatory TP schedules and increased penalties for incomplete documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in implementing non-arm's length schemes may be assessed personally, because the tax adjustment plus penalties may exceed the company's capacity. Under IRO s. 82(3), a director who 'participates in' the preparation of a false return is criminally liable. In civil proceedings, the court may pierce the corporate veil when the company is a sham or a mere façade used for tax evasion, ordering directors to repay diverted corporate assets; directors may be issued departure prevention orders (travel bans) and face up to 3 years imprisonment if the evasion is proven.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s. 61 and s. 61A, the IRD may disregard artificial transactions and treat the tax benefit as void; the China–Hong Kong DTA's Principal Purpose Test under the MLI will deny treaty benefits. The Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) imposes fines for failure to file annual returns, and a shell company might be struck off the Companies Registry. In Mainland China, the PRC State Administration of Taxation may levy tax at full domestic rates (up to 25% corporate income tax plus 10% dividend withholding) on the basis that the Hong Kong intermediary is not the beneficial owner.

Director personal liability: Directors of a shell company may be personally liable for fraudulent trading under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622 s. 903) and the common law tort of deceit, with civil liability for the full amount of the company's liabilities if the business was carried on with intent to defraud creditors or for a fraudulent purpose. Personal asset restitution can be ordered by the court, and directors can be disqualified for up to 15 years. Immigration authorities can impose travel bans where there is a likelihood of leaving Hong Kong to escape these liabilities, and criminal imprisonment for fraudulent trading may extend to 5 years and to 7 years upon conviction on indictment.
