Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a resident enterprise of mainland China and is subject to corporate income tax at entity level on worldwide income; it has no pass-through eligibility and is not a tax-exempt zone entity. Hong Kong's territorial profits tax is separate and applies only to the non-resident shareholder on Hong Kong-source profits, not to the mainland JV.

Available election forms

None. China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime; entity classification follows the Enterprise Income Tax Law. The only available election relates to VAT status: an eligible taxpayer may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer before exceeding the RMB 5 million threshold if it meets accounting and tax-control requirements; no other elective classifications apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales (VAT-exclusive) is the mandatory threshold for classification as a general VAT taxpayer; any entity making taxable supplies must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining its business license irrespective of turnover. Below RMB 5,000,000, the JV remains a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the VAT Law where: (i) the mainland general VAT taxpayer exports tangible goods and the goods are physically declared to customs and leave mainland China; or (ii) a qualifying cross-border service is supplied to a recipient located outside mainland China and the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China. For this air-cargo charter broker, freight-forwarding and charter-brokerage commissions are generally classified as logistics auxiliary services and are eligible for VAT exemption rather than zero-rating, unless the JV itself acts as the international air carrier/operator of the chartered aircraft. Export goods handled by the JV, if invoiced in the JV's own name, are eligible for 0% VAT with input VAT refund subject to the documentation and 180-day foreign-exchange receipt requirements.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Export Declaration Form) with customs release endorsement and export manifest data
  • Master Air Waybill or House Air Waybill (Bill of Lading equivalent) evidencing international carriage from mainland China to a foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list issued in the JV's name
  • Export sales contract or aircraft charter agreement demonstrating the cross-border nature of the transaction
  • Full digital electronic VAT invoice or special VAT invoice for the export supply
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt/settlement certificate evidencing receipt of export proceeds from a foreign counterparty
  • Customs logistics tracking records and container/aircraft movement records supporting the physical export

All VAT invoices, including full digital electronic invoices (e-invoices), must be issued through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the Golden Tax System (Golden Tax IV). Each e-invoice receives a unique invoice code, encrypted digital stamping, and is transmitted to the STA tax-control database in near real time. General VAT taxpayers issuing special VAT invoices must apply for invoice quota and block issuance through the platform; export sales must be linked to customs export declaration data through the national electronic data interchange before zero-rating is recorded.

The STA will conduct three-way matching among customs export declarations, VAT invoice data, logistics records, and foreign-exchange receipt data. If zero-rating is claimed without a genuine physical export or if export proceeds are not repatriated through the banking system within the statutory 180-day deadline, the tax authority will claw back any refunded input VAT, assess late-payment interest from the date of refund, and impose a penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulent export declarations or fabricated documentation may be referred to the public security authorities for export tax fraud prosecution under the Criminal Law, and the JV may have its export-tax-refund qualification suspended, downgraded, or revoked with compliance disqualification for future zero-rated transactions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a fully convertible currency regime anchored to the Linked Exchange Rate System, and the HKMA does not impose exchange controls on the flow of foreign exchange into, out of, or through the SAR. Current-account payments for air cargo charter services, brokerage commissions, and operating disbursements are freely remittable; capital-account items such as shareholder loans, equity injections, and liquidation proceeds are also unrestricted at the Hong Kong level, subject only to general banking propriety and sanctions screening. The true control friction lies in the PRC leg: where the mainland joint-venture partner or parent routes funding through SAFE, the transaction must be supported by an ODI or FDI filing, a formal capital verification certificate, and a valid foreign-exchange purpose code. Profit remittance from the Hong Kong operating vehicle to a PRC shareholder is not blocked by HKMA, but the receiving mainland entity must handle the inbound foreign exchange under PRC current-account rules, including tax clearance documentation where dividends or service fees are characterized. No forced conversion or surrender requirement applies in Hong Kong.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong has no statutory surrender or mandatory conversion regime; exporters and air cargo charter brokers may retain 100% of foreign-currency receipts indefinitely and convert freely at market rates. For a PRC-domiciled entity in an underlying mainland flow, SAFE would require repatriation to a domestic foreign-currency account within the statutory settlement period and mandatory conversion into Renminbi unless the local administration grants a retention quota; however, the Hong Kong entity itself is outside that regime.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China as competent authority for any mainland-linked FX approval or capital account submission.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary treasury usage. Offshore digital EMIs that lack an HKMA-authorised banking licence are legally barred from carrying on deposit-taking or full corporate current-account business in Hong Kong. Local virtual banks licensed under the HKMA regime can offer onboarding convenience, but generally do not yet provide the documentary trade finance, multi-currency DDA structures, and air-cargo charter settlement capacity required for a medium-risk broker. Use of a licensed virtual bank as a secondary disbursement channel may be acceptable only where funds are segregated and the account does not become the sole clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from the PRC joint-venture partner should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the partner's SAFE-designated bank in mainland China to the Hong Kong capital escrow account at a tier-1 HK bank. The wire must reference the approved SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code for inbound direct investment capital or foreign debt, include the full legal entity name of the Hong Kong JV, and be accompanied by the executed joint venture agreement and board resolution. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance Cap.622, every Hong Kong incorporated company must prepare true and fair financial statements and have them audited by an independent practising Certified Public Accountant unless the small private company exemption is available. A private company qualifies as a small private company and may be exempt from statutory audit only if it satisfies at least two of the following size tests: total revenue does not exceed HKD 20 million; total assets do not exceed HKD 10 million; number of employees does not exceed 50. The exemption is not available if the company is a member of a group in which any member is not a small private company, if a shareholder or shareholders holding not less than 5% of total voting rights give written notice requiring an audit, if the company carries on banking, insurance, money service operation, or is a licensed corporation under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, or if the Inland Revenue Department requires audited accounts in support of the Profits Tax Return. In all such cases, a full statutory audit by a licensed external auditor is mandatory.
