Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal, with local in-person competent tax offices at the enterprise's registered address.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established under Chinese law, the foreign investor and the Chinese partner first register the joint venture with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Post-incorporation, the entity automatically receives a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), an 18-character legal identifier that serves as the statutory tax identification number (TIN) for income tax, VAT, and all fiscal filings. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the joint venture must complete post-issuance tax registration (seat-in-charge registration) via the e-Tax portal at the competent district tax authority. Required evidence includes the SAMR business license, articles of association, joint venture contract, identification documents of legal representative and foreign shareholders, and proof of registered office address. The system automatically issues the taxpayer file number and activates the electronic tax account. The JV then must apply for general taxpayer status (VAT general taxpayer) if estimated annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5 million, or elect small-scale taxpayer treatment if below. Non-resident foreign investors without a permanent establishment are separately registered by the domestic withholding agent under the Non-Resident Enterprise Withholding Tax filing process to obtain a non-resident tax code; this process is not required for the JV itself because the JV is a domestic resident legal entity.

Available election forms

None. The People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box or elective pass-through regime for foreign-owned equity joint ventures. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Basic VAT registration is compulsory for all legal entities at incorporation via the unified social credit system; there is no turnover exemption for entity formation. Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration applies when annual VAT taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, or at any time if estimated turnover within 12 months exceeds this threshold. Below RMB 5,000,000, the JV may remain a small-scale taxpayer with a reduced collection rate of 3% (or 1% under current presumptive relief), but can voluntarily elect general taxpayer status upon obtaining a VAT invoice system approval. For non-resident enterprises making cross-border supplies without an establishment, VAT registration is required if they have a Chinese withholding agent; otherwise they must register as a non-resident VAT payer through the e-Tax portal before invoicing a domestic customer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A customs brokerage house may zero-rate its cross-border logistics services only if the services physically start or end outside mainland China and are rendered to non-resident customers, or are directly related to exported goods in the course of customs export clearance. For tangible goods exported by or through the JV, the 0% VAT export rate applies when the goods have been physically exported from the People's Republic of China through a designated customs port, all export customs declaration formalities are completed, and the exporter has received and repatriated the foreign-currency export proceeds. Qualifying export services include export customs brokerage and freight forwarding services that are supplied to an overseas consumer and support the export movement of goods; these services must be evidenced by the corresponding export customs declaration. Pure domestic import clearance services are taxable at the standard VAT rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Customs Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill or combined transport document issued by the ocean carrier or airline
  • Export Invoice (commercial invoice) naming the overseas buyer and payment terms
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank export proceeds collection statement
  • VAT special invoice for export goods purchased (for export VAT refund substantiation)
  • Export cargo inspection / quarantine certificate if goods are subject to export inspection

China operates a mandatory real-time fiscal clearance system for special VAT invoices under the Golden Tax Project Phase IV (Golden Tax IV). All VAT invoices must be issued via the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoice platform; the new fully digitalized e-invoice (digital e-invoice) can be issued and delivered online with no physical paper requirement. The e-Tax system performs real-time invoice number validation, metadata reconciliation, and anti-fraud verification against the enterprise's VAT return data, inventory, bank receipts, and customs import/export declarations. Special VAT invoices with VAT input credit function must be uploaded to the central invoice database within 24 hours of issuance. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; approval is digital through the invoice control number and QR-code data bundle generated by the STA platform.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subjected to post-clearance fiscal audits by the State Taxation Administration. The enterprise must retain the export declaration form, B/L, FIRC, purchase VAT special invoices, and full custody records for at least 5 years. If the tax authority discovers a false export declaration, unrepatriated export proceeds, forged B/L, mismatched HS codes, or nominal circular exports, the zero-rating is retroactively clawed back and the exporter must pay the unpaid VAT at the statutory rate plus surcharge. Fraudulent export tax refund claims are punishable under the Tax Administration Law and Criminal Law: the tax authority will recover the evaded tax, impose a penalty of 50% up to 500% of the fraudulently obtained refund, suspend the enterprise's export VAT refund qualification for up to 3 years, and where criminal fraud is established, responsible persons face criminal liability including imprisonment. The assessment period is extended indefinitely for deliberate fraud and defective/incomplete documentation invalidates the export rebate waiver.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a linked exchange rate system with a free-floating capital account and full convertibility of the Hong Kong dollar. There are no foreign exchange controls, no mandatory currency surrender requirements, and no restrictions on the repatriation of earnings, dividends, capital, or liquidation proceeds for a Hong Kong-incorporated entity. Current account transactions are fully liberalized, and capital account flows such as equity injections, intra-group loans, and intercompany trade advances can be routed freely through licensed banks in Hong Kong without prior approval from the HKMA. The practical hurdles for the PRC parent are, however, asymmetrical: while Hong Kong itself imposes no exchange controls, the mainland PRC parent is subject to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) overseas direct investment (ODI) filing, foreign debt registration, and profit remittance compliance. For the Hong Kong JV, banks will assess the ultimate Chinese beneficial owner, the source of injected capital, and the existence of genuine commercial purpose. Profit remittance back to China is blocked only by mainland documentation requirements, not by Hong Kong banking law. The HKMA is responsible for supervising the banking system, monitoring the linked rate, and preserving stability, but it does not approve each cross-border payment in Hong Kong.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender or forced repatriation of export proceeds. Exporters and customs brokerage houses may retain all foreign currency earnings in any licensed bank account and may convert to HKD, CNY, USD, or other currencies freely. For the PRC parent, SAFE rules require mainland exporters to repatriate and verify export proceeds through the Trade Monitoring System within 180 days, with mandatory conversion only if the mainland entity receives foreign currency under the unified settlement and sale system, but this requirement does not bind the Hong Kong JV treasury.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) acting as the de facto central bank under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155), the Clearing and Settlement Systems Ordinance (Cap. 586), and the Exchange Fund Ordinance (Cap. 66).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating is LOW for primary corporate treasury use. Hong Kong-licensed stored value facility (SVF) operators are legally permitted to provide limited e-money and prepaid payment services but are not licensed to issue corporate IBANs, process MT103 SWIFT payments, provide trade finance, escrow custody, or foreign exchange hedging instruments. Offshore EMI fintech providers are not authorized by the HKMA to open corporate accounts for Hong Kong entities without a formal banking license, and the HKMA has publicly warned banks against entering into relationships with unlicensed EMIs. For the customs brokerage house, all inbound capital injection, outbound duty payments, and trade settlement must therefore be run through a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank. An EMI may only be used as a secondary petty-cash wallet for payment of low-value logistics incidentals, and only if the EMI is a licensed SVF in Hong Kong anda formal AML/CFT review has been completed.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million in annual related-party tangible asset transactions, RMB 100 million in annual related-party intangible or financial asset transactions, or RMB 40 million in other annual related-party transactions triggers mandatory contemporaneous Local File preparation under SAT Public Notice No. 42 (2016); a group Master File is required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds the statutory threshold and the PRC JV is a related-party transaction participant.

Economic substance scope: The PRC JV must demonstrate real economic substance in mainland China through licensed customs brokers, physical premises, management decision-making, employment contracts, operational workflows, settlement of Customs duties and retention of risks and benefits. For the Hong Kong shareholder, Hong Kong's foreign-source income exemption and the operationally relevant CIGA standard require core income-generating activities, including substantive board management, deal negotiation and risk management, to be performed in Hong Kong. If the JV is a mere letterbox, or if the HK shareholder is a nominee with no CIGA, the STA may apply Article 41 and Article 47 of the EIT Law to reattribute profits, deny treaty benefits, recharacterise debt as equity and impose special tax adjustment interest.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: Recovery of underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge under Articles 32 and 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law once the evasion amount reaches the statutory threshold.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law where the shareholder or director abuses corporate independence to evade tax debts, leading to joint and several personal liability for the unpaid tax and civil restitution. The tax authority can blacklist the JV and its legal representative and impose a travel ban preventing departure from mainland China. Criminal conviction exposes the responsible director to up to 3 years imprisonment for a relatively large evasion, and 3 to 7 years imprisonment plus confiscation for an enormous amount.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41, 46 and 47 of the PRC EIT Law and SAT Public Notice No. 42 of 2016, the STA may make an arm's length re-determination, disallow excessive interest and royalties, impose special tax adjustment interest and reassess up to 10 prior tax years; failure to file related-party transaction forms or contemporaneous documentation triggers administrative penalties and may escalate to Article 63 evasion fines where there is deliberate underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve non-arm's-length transactions may be held personally liable for the adjusted tax and associated interest through derivative civil claims and veil-piercing. If the inflated payments are routed through the HK affiliate for the purpose of fraud, directors may face asset freezing and seizure under the AML regime, travel bans, disqualification orders, and imprisonment for up to 7 years where the transfer pricing manipulation constitutes criminal tax evasion or defrauding the State.
Shell Company, Beneficial Ownership Abuse and AML Breach in Customs Brokerage

Statutory sanction: The Customs Administration Law empowers Customs to fine the broker, revoke the customs brokerage registration and recapture unpaid duties and fines where the brokerage service is exploited for false declarations or smuggling. The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines of up to RMB 5 million for failure to conduct customer due diligence, suspicious transaction reporting, or record-keeping; the tax authority may also apply Article 47 EIT Law GAAR, deny treaty benefits and order the cancellation of business registration for a shell company.

Director personal liability: Directors face personal civil restitution for damages caused to customs brokers, clients and the State; exit controls and serious-violation blacklisting apply. Conviction for money laundering under Article 191 of the Criminal Law, particularly where funds derive from smuggling, customs fraud or tax evasion, may result in imprisonment of up to 10 years, plus criminal fines and confiscation of assets; directors may also be disqualified from holding management positions for up to 3 years after conviction.
