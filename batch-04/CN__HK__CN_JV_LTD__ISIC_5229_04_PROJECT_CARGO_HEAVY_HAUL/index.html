Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under PBOC/SAFE. The current account is substantially convertible for trade in goods and services, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and declarations, invoices, contracts, and customs records match. The capital account remains tightly controlled: equity injections, cross-border loans, and profit remittances require SAFE registrations, filings, or approvals before funds move. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with a Hong Kong parent must complete Foreign Direct Investment registration and open a capital account escrow for the injection. RMB received as registered capital may be converted for operating needs within approved purposes, but proceeds from capital cannot be transferred to the offshore parent without dividend distribution formalities. Profit remittance is permitted only after audited financial statements, tax clearance, board approval, and SAFE verification; the conversion may be affected by market T+0 or T+1 USD/CNY settlement windows but is not rationed if documentation is complete. Non-compliance exposes the entity to administrative penalties, blocked remittances, and downgrading of bank compliance rating.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to China within 180 calendar days of the export customs declaration date unless SAFE grants an extension or the proceeds qualify for permitted offshore retention. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to RMB; the exporting entity may retain foreign exchange onshore in a current-account foreign currency account or voluntarily convert to RMB with the supporting customs-documentation package. The bank performs SAFE monitoring and cross-checks export collection vouchers against customs data.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable. PRC banking and FX law reserves onshore settlement, renminbi clearing, and capital-account custody for institutions licensed by PBOC/SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs, including neo-banks and money-service operators domiciled in Hong Kong or other common law jurisdictions, are not licensed to hold basic RMB accounts or process SAFE-registered capital flows for a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. Using an unlicensed EMI would create a failure of KYC chain and a free-floating capital movement outside SAFE supervision. The JV must execute treasury operations exclusively through SAFE-licensed banks.

Turnover: Under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 sections 359-360, all Hong Kong private companies must be independently audited unless they qualify as a small private company and a valid audit exemption resolution is passed. A small private company must satisfy at least two of the following three conditions: (i) total revenue not exceeding HKD 25 million (approximately USD 3.21 million); (ii) total assets not exceeding HKD 15 million (approximately USD 1.92 million); and (iii) total employees not exceeding 50. BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 / Line 1 Operating revenue: gross revenue from out-of-gauge heavy industrial project cargo logistics, heavy-haul transportation, crane rental, rigging, escort, and freight-forwarding services. Recognize revenue in accordance with PRC Accounting Standards and the EIT Law. Revenue derived from international transport or cross-border services with a Hong Kong affiliate must be priced at arm's length; understated freight revenue to shift profit to the Hong Kong shareholder is a transfer-pricing red flag. Late filing of Form A100000 by 31 May triggers a penalty under Article 62 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection of RMB 2,000, increased to RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 where the default is serious. Any unpaid CIT bears a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day on the unpaid amount.
