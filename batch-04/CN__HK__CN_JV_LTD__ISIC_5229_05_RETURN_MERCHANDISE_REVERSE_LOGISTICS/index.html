Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A Hong Kong company must maintain a registered office in Hong Kong that is capable of receiving service of legal documents and official communications during business hours. A virtual office or commercial mailbox may be used only where a physical service-provider office accepts legal-process service, maintains a current board address register, and has a Hong Kong address that is not a post office box. However, since the entity will be engaged in cross-border reverse logistics and returned goods grading under ISIC 5229 / NAICS 488510, major Hong Kong banks and the Inland Revenue Department typically reject a pure virtual mailbox for corporate account opening, company secretary verification, or business registration tax feedback unless there is an actual leased premises or a service provider with dedicated, monitored meeting rooms and records storage. The bank may require an executed tenancy agreement for a physical office or a registered office service agreement with a licensed corporate service provider, utility bill, and detailed commercial substance explanation. For low-risk reverse logistics, a commercial serviced office shared with the parent is acceptable if the company maintains its own books and records, separate telephone and email identifiers, and a substantive management presence for AML risk-triggered review. The wholly virtual mailbox-only arrangement should be rejected for this target company because of bank on-boarding requirements, FATCA/CRS self-certification, and potential re-characterization as a tax shell under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Hong Kong law. The Companies Ordinance does not impose a minimum paid-up capital requirement. A company limited by shares may be incorporated with no paid-up capital, and no blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Registry. Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China (www.chinatax.gov.cn) through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) portals; for the Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (www.ird.gov.hk) through the IRD e-Tax platform. The primary entity registration for this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is with the PRC STA.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the PRC business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for PRC tax purposes. Step 2: The STA receives the entity formation record automatically through the national data-sharing mechanism; the JV must nevertheless log on to the local Electronic Tax Bureau portal within 30 days of business license issuance to complete tax status confirmation, legal representative verification, financial officer designation, accounting policy confirmation, registered address confirmation, and bank account reporting. Step 3: The e-Tax portal activates the enterprise tax profile and determines VAT identity: annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 requires general VAT taxpayer registration; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies, but the enterprise may voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status if accounting records are sound. Step 4: Apply for the electronic invoice/tax-control device: a tax U-Key or activation of the fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform quota. Step 5: For the foreign non-resident investor that merely holds equity, no separate PRC TIN is required for holding shares; dividends are subject to PRC withholding tax by the JV. If the foreign investor establishes a permanent establishment in China, it must complete non-resident tax registration with the competent tax authority and obtain a non-resident taxpayer identifier via the e-Tax portal using its home-country TIN and corporate documents. Step 6: If a Hong Kong SAR branch or permanent establishment is created, register with the IRD for Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance and, if applicable, Profits Tax; the Hong Kong branch is not separately subject to VAT/GST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration in mainland China; all PRC-established enterprises must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers have a reduced/ simplified VAT regime, and the Hong Kong SAR has no VAT/GST registration threshold because it has no indirect tax of that type.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, exported goods are eligible for the 0% export VAT rate / export tax refund where the goods are actually sold by the Chinese entity, exported from mainland China under customs supervision, declared on an electronic customs export declaration, and supported by a valid export contract, commercial invoice, transport document, proof of exported goods leaving China, and collection of the export sales proceeds. Cross-border reverse logistics and returned-goods quality grading services may qualify for zero-rating or exemption as cross-border taxable services only if the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China, the contract and payment documents demonstrate the offshore service recipient, and the supporting evidence meets the STA's cross-border service zero-rating / exemption filing requirements. The Hong Kong SAR has no VAT/GST, so no Hong Kong zero-rating substantiation is required for exports; relevant profits remain subject to Hong Kong territorial Profits Tax rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) with electronic customs release status from the China International Trade Single Window
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical cross-border movement of the returned goods
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract / Purchase Order
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher or SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System collection record (the functional equivalent of the historic Inward Remittance Certificate / export proceeds verification form)
  • Logistics service agreement, warehouse receipt, and returned-goods grading report substantiating the cross-border reverse logistics service scope

In the PRC mainland, all VAT invoices must be issued through the STA Value-Added Tax Invoice Management System or the newer Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice Service Platform. The system applies Golden Tax Phase IV controls, real-time transmission of invoice data to the national tax big-data environment, automatic cryptographic stamping, anomaly flagging, and reconciliation of invoice, customs, and foreign-exchange data. Fully digitalized e-invoices carry a unique national invoice number and verification code and do not require a physical tax-control device; abnormal invoices can be quarantined by the tax authority before deduction/refund processing. The Hong Kong SAR does not operate a fiscal invoice clearance or e-invoice approval mandate.

The PRC STA and Customs can disallow export zero-rating and claw back refunded VAT if the physical export, the declared export value, the transport route, or the foreign-exchange receipt records are inconsistent or unverifiable. Export proceeds must normally be recovered within 180 days of export; failure to repatriate within the statutory period can lead to restoration of output VAT, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax involved. Fraudulent export declarations intended to obtain VAT export refunds constitute exporting-tax-fraud and are subject to comprehensive audit, retrospective disallowance, confiscation, fines, and criminal liability. In the Hong Kong SAR, no VAT/GST clawback applies, but false or unsubstantiated offshore profit claims are scrutinized by the IRD and may be reassessed with penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including unlimited reassessment in fraud cases.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as an offshore financial territory with no exchange controls. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible under both current and capital account transactions and is managed within the HKMA's linked exchange rate band of 7.75 to 7.85 HKD per USD. There are no surrender requirements, no foreign exchange budget approvals, and no profit remittance restriction on distributions from a Hong Kong company. However, because the parent is a mainland Chinese entity and the operating supply chain crosses into China, the effective regulatory burden is driven by mainland China's foreign exchange regime. Mainland RMB is convertible for current account trade in services and goods under SAFE documentary supervision, but capital account flows, including overseas direct investment equity injections, require ex-ante registration or approval. Offshore RMB (CNH) held by the Hong Kong joint venture is freely transferable in Hong Kong, but transfers between mainland China and Hong Kong must be supported by genuine trade or investment background. Profit and dividend remittance from the Hong Kong JV to the mainland parent is not subject to Hong Kong withholding tax, but the parent must include the income in its China annual returns and manage any deemed direct investment filings with SAFE. A reverse logistics and returned-goods grading model will be credit tested on documented product flows, return-merchandise authorisation, customs declarations, and third-party warehouse records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rule and no mandatory conversion percentage; export receipts may be retained in HKD, CNH, USD or any freely useable currency in approved corporate accounts. If the mainland China parent receives export foreign currency receipts directly, SAFE requires such receipts to be repatriated within the statutory export verification period, generally 180 days from customs declaration unless an approved extended payment term applies, registered in the cross-border foreign exchange monitoring system, and verified against customs declaration data; there is no fixed conversion ratio, and the exporter may voluntarily settle or retain the foreign currency in an approved account.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for mainland China cross-border capital and foreign exchange controls, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the competent regulatory authorities.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Viability: Medium as a payment-only rail, but low as a replacement for a corporate bank account. Hong Kong does not issue a generic importable EMI licence; stored value facilities and digital payment institutions are regulated by the HKMA under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, while non-bank providers such as Airwallex commonly hold a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence only. Therefore Airwallex and similar offshore fintech EMIs are legally permitted to support low-value collections, customer refunds, supplier payments and direct debit feeds, but they cannot accept deposit-taking business, provide capital escrow, issue trade-related bank confirmations or give deposit protection under the Hong Kong Deposit Protection Scheme. The JV should use a Tier-1 bank as the liquidity prime broker and clear outbound reverse-logistics payout flows through an EMI only as a secondary settlement gateway, with the EMI's sanctions screening, transaction limits and counterparty risk included in the annual AML testing programme.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the mainland China parent to the Hong Kong JV must be routed by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's designated SAFE-monitored bank to the JV's Hong Kong Tier-1 bank. The remitter must cite the SAFE overseas direct investment registration reference and use a direct investment foreign exchange purpose code consistent with an equity contribution to a Hong Kong subsidiary. The receiving bank will credit the capital escrow account; release of funds requires a certified capital verification report by a Hong Kong certified public accountant and the JV's business registration certificate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability: Medium as a payment-only rail, but low as a replacement for a corporate bank account. Hong Kong does not issue a generic importable EMI licence; stored value facilities and digital payment institutions are regulated by the HKMA under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, while non-bank providers such as Airwallex commonly hold a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence only. Therefore Airwallex and similar offshore fintech EMIs are legally permitted to support low-value collections, customer refunds, supplier payments and direct debit feeds, but they cannot accept deposit-taking business, provide capital escrow, issue trade-related bank confirmations or give deposit protection under the Hong Kong Deposit Protection Scheme. The JV should use a Tier-1 bank as the liquidity prime broker and clear outbound reverse-logistics payout flows through an EMI only as a secondary settlement gateway, with the EMI's sanctions screening, transaction limits and counterparty risk included in the annual AML testing programme.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$100 million for intercompany financing transactions and HK$10 million for property/goods transactions and other controlled transactions (per IRD DIPN 59 and s.50AAS IRO).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not administer offshore Economic Substance Regulations in the manner of the UAE or BVI. However, the IRD applies an economic substance requirement when determining the source of profits and when assessing DTA claims. For a reverse logistics JV, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - procurement of returned goods, quality grading, repair/repackaging, inventory storage, and local dispatch - must be physically and managementally performed in Hong Kong. An entity with no staff, premises, or board residence in HK may be regarded as a shell; any profits booked in HK will be re-sourced to Mainland China, and treaty benefits under the HK-Mainland DTA can be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Submission

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82(1) - on indictment, a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; pursuant to s.82A, the court may also order a fine of treble the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, direct, or approve the fraudulent return are treated as principals. The veil will be pierced where the company is a mere device for tax evasion; directors may be subject to personal asset restitution, a travel ban (blacklisting) by the Immigration Department, and disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.723.
Shell Company / Treaty Benefit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Sections 61 and 61A of the IRO enable the IRD to disregard artificial transactions and assess tax on the underlying substance for up to 6 years of assessment. A fine of HK$100,000 and treble tax under s.82A may be imposed, and a protective assessment can be raised.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly participates in a sham arrangement loses limited liability protection. Civil restitution of the undercharged tax, interest, and penalties can be recovered from the director personally. Criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the IRD can lead to imprisonment. Directors may be blacklisted and prohibited from managing a company for a term.
AML / Money Laundering

Statutory sanction: Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) s.25(1) - dealing with property known or believed to represent proceeds of an indictable offense is punishable on conviction to a fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years. The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing (Financial Institutions) Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes licensing penalties and regulatory censure.

Director personal liability: Directors who fail to lodge suspicious transaction reports or establish AML policies commit an indictable offense. Personal criminal liability, worldwide asset freezing, travel bans, and exclusion from the financial services sector may follow. Under Cap. 615, the court can order directors to pay restitution to victims or the state.
