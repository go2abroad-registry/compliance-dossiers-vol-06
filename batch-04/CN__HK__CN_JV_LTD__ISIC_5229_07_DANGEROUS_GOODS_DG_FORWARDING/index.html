Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. A Hong Kong private company limited by shares may be incorporated with a share capital as low as HK$1; there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no mandatory escrow of subscription moneys, and no blocked capital account requirement at the Companies Registry. The amount stated on Form NNC1 is the subscribed capital, and directors must issue shares only after receipt of the corresponding subscription moneys.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain a Fire Services Department fire safety certificate covering automatic fire detection, fire hydrant, portable extinguisher, and emergency lighting installations, together with Buildings Department structural load approval for high-density storage. Civil Defense obligations include a hazardous materials incident response plan, assembly point plan, spill drill schedule, and fire safety management registration. The Labour Department requires an Occupational Safety and Health registration and a Dangerous Goods Workplace Safety Certificate for employees handling DG, including documented medical surveillance and personal protective equipment standards.

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture CN_JV_LTD is formed in mainland China as a Chinese tax-resident enterprise. On incorporation and business licence issuance, the Administration for Market Regulation assigns an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Identifier (USCI), which is the taxpayer identification number (TIN) used for all State Taxation Administration filings. Within 30 days of licence issuance, the JV must complete tax registration with the competent in-charge tax authority using the China Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform. For a foreign non-resident entity (including the Hong Kong parent) deriving China-source income or engaging in taxable activity in China without a permanent establishment, the SOP is: (i) obtain the home jurisdiction tax certificate and tax treaty residence certificate; (ii) file a non-resident tax registration application online through the China Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform or with the competent tax authority where the Chinese payor/withholding agent is located; (iii) appoint a China tax liaison or legal service representative for service of notices; (iv) obtain the non-resident TIN and, if a permanent establishment or fixed place of business exists, apply through the standard e-tax workflow for VAT general taxpayer registration or withholding registration. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All mainland Chinese entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance. Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in a rolling 12-month period; taxpayers below that threshold remain small-scale VAT taxpayers unless voluntary general taxpayer status is approved. The standard headline VAT rate on goods is 13%, with 9% for transport and 6% for modern services such as freight forwarding and logistics support services.

China requires electronic invoicing under the national fully digital e-invoice programme and the Golden Tax Phase IV invoice-control system. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) every Hong Kong private company must appoint a certified public accountant as auditor unless it qualifies as a 'small private company' or a 'company in a small group' and has passed a special resolution to dispense with the audit. The statutory small-company test requires meeting at least two of the following three conditions in the relevant financial year: (1) annual total revenue not exceeding HKD 10,000,000; (2) total assets at the balance sheet date not exceeding HKD 10,000,000; and (3) average number of employees not exceeding 50. This hazardous-goods freight forwarding vehicle, as an ISIC 5229 licensed operator, will in practice be subject to independent audit by investor bank covenants, by the overseas parent entity's consolidation requirement, and by the Inland Revenue Department if it exercises its powers to call for audited financial statements as evidence of correct tax reporting. Furthermore, a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture holding a Hong Kong special-purpose corporate vehicle will need to file audited financial statements with the mainland Chinese parent under PRC accounting and reporting requirements, effectively making audit mandatory even if the Cap. 622 exemption could be technically available.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds: RMB 200 million for annual related-party transfers of tangible goods; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers; RMB 50 million for related-party financing; RMB 40 million for related-party services. Master File thresholds apply when aggregate related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion in a given tax year.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not apply a Caribbean-style statutory ESR regime, but the China-HK DTA's beneficial-ownership clause and the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Art. 47-48) require the Hong Kong shareholder and the JV to conduct real CIGA: the JV must carry out freight contracting, DG rate-setting, carrier selection, hazardous-materials compliance, invoicing, claims handling, and risk management in China; the HK shareholder must hold meetings, employ logistics professionals, maintain substantive bank accounts, and take business decisions in Hong Kong. Absent these pillars, tax authorities may disregard the HK entity, deny treaty rates, and reattribute profits to the ultimate PRC beneficiary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Related-Party Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Art. 41 (arm's length adjustment), Art. 46 (thin capitalization), Art. 47-48 (GAAR and special interest), TCAL Art. 63 (tax evasion penalty of 50%-500% of underpaid tax), plus 0.05% daily late payment interest, transfer-pricing documentation penalties, and possible public blacklisting under the Major Tax Violation Cases list.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and directly responsible directors may be barred from leaving China (exit ban) if assessed tax is outstanding; directors who personally devised the fictitious service contracts face criminal prosecution for tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201, with imprisonment of up to 3 years, or 3-7 years for major amounts, plus fines and personal asset seizure; company-liability veil-piercing occurs when funds are mingled with director accounts.
Abusive DTA Relief / Shell Company Conduit

Statutory sanction: Denial of China-HK DTA benefits under the beneficial-ownership rule and GAAR; reassessment at the full 10% statutory withholding rate on dividends, interest, and royalties; TCAL Art. 69 penalty of 50%-300% of the uncollected WHT; daily late interest; tax authority can disregard the HK shell, impose tax on the ultimate beneficial owner, and list the JV as a serious tax dishonest taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the HK SPV and the PRC JV are jointly exposed to civil restitution of diverted profits; PRC authorities may impose blacklisting and travel bans on the responsible directors; criminal tax-evasion charges under Criminal Law Art. 201 can carry imprisonment up to 7 years; assets held in Hong Kong or through SWIFT/L/C proceeds can be frozen and confiscated.
AML / Customs / Dangerous Goods False Declaration

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law institutional fines up to RMB 5 million and responsible-person fines up to RMB 500,000; suspension/revocation of the ISIC 5229 dangerous-goods forwarding license; Customs Law false-declaration penalties of 10%-50% of goods value; Criminal Law money-laundering provisions (Art. 191) and customs-smuggling provisions authorize confiscation, fines, and up to 10 years imprisonment; AEO certification is revoked and the entity is blacklisted from the DG air/sea market.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false DG bookings or omit AML checks can be personally fined, permanently banned from the logistics industry, prohibited from leaving China, and sentenced to imprisonment for money laundering or smuggling; civil asset restitution and joint liability attach where director bank accounts were used to receive DG freight proceeds, and any resulting illegal exports trigger personal criminal liability for endangering public safety and environmental harm.
