VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In the PRC, mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the joint venture may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Export-oriented rail freight and international freight forwarding turnover can qualify for zero-rating or exemption only after the enterprise is registered as an eligible VAT general taxpayer. Hong Kong has no VAT registration threshold because no VAT exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For PRC VAT purposes, zero-rating applies to qualifying cross-border supplies made by VAT general taxpayers, including export of goods, cross-border transportation services, and qualifying offshore digital services included in the PRC statutory positive list. For this transcontinental container block train rail freight and intermodal expediting joint venture, the international transport element must cross the PRC customs border into an overseas destination, the service or goods must be supplied to an overseas party, the consideration must be received from outside the PRC, and the enterprise must retain full supporting documents proving actual export, delivery and payment. Domestic rail freight within the PRC is taxable at the standard transportation services VAT rate and cannot be zero-rated. Hong Kong has no VAT, so no zero-rating claim arises in Hong Kong.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Form for Export Goods) with electronic customs clearance approval
  • CIM/SMGS international railway consignment note or multimodal transport bill of lading evidencing cross-border rail freight movement
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, sales or transportation service contract showing the overseas counterparty and the final overseas destination
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) evidencing the repatriation of export consideration into China

PRC VAT requires the use of statutory VAT invoices issued through the State Taxation Administration electronic tax system and the national integrated VAT invoice service platform; under the fully digitalized electronic invoice programme, VAT general taxpayers must issue, deliver and archive digital VAT invoices with STA-authenticated fiscal signatures, and the invoice data must pass real-time electronic clearance, digital identity verification and anti-fraud stamping before an input VAT credit or export refund can be claimed. Special VAT invoices must be reported to the STA with real-time invoice data matching against customs and foreign exchange records. Hong Kong SAR has no electronic invoice clearance mandate because it has no VAT/GST.

The State Taxation Administration and China Customs conduct joint data matching and audit enforcement using customs export declarations, VAT invoice systems, bank foreign exchange receipts, railway waybills and logistics manifests. Any fraudulent, overstated or unsubstantiated zero-rating or export VAT refund is subject to immediate clawback of the refunded amount, statutory late-payment interest, administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the tax involved, cancellation of zero-rated invoicing and refund eligibility, inclusion in the national tax violation and anti-money-laundering records, and criminal prosecution for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds under PRC Criminal Law. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The regulatory perimeter is a dual-regime structure: mainland China operates a managed float where RMB is convertible for genuine current account transactions but remains strictly managed on the capital account; Hong Kong SAR, although within Chinese sovereignty, operates as a separate offshore RMB clearing hub with full current and capital account convertibility. For the mainland China-incorporated CN_JV_LTD, current account receipts and payments arising from rail freight forwarding, haulage, containers, demurrage, port handling, and cross-border logistics fees are freely convertible at banks subject to authenticity checks. Capital account items, notably the equity injection from the Hong Kong parent and any shareholder loans, require SAFE-backed FDI foreign exchange registration or foreign debt registration before a bank can credit the JV's capital account and permit RMB settlement. Profit remittances of after-tax dividends to the Hong Kong shareholder are treated as investment income and are generally permitted, but the bank requires the board profit-distribution resolution, audited financial statements, tax payment certifications, and proof that the JV has satisfied statutory reserve requirements. Remittance of foreign shareholders historic capital is not unrestricted; sale or equity transfer proceeds require capital account registration and may be subject to tax clearance. Treasury must therefore separate onshore CNY operational liquidity from offshore CNH/USD liquidity and ensure that every cross-border transfer is mapped to a permissible BOP code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE no longer imposes a blanket mandatory export surrender ratio; instead, all export-generated foreign currency and service-trade income must be repatriated to a licensed domestic foreign currency account within the statutory trade monitoring window under the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration and Service Trade Foreign Exchange Guidelines. A-class enterprises must normally receive and match export or service proceeds within 30 to 90 days of the underlying invoice or customs declaration; B-class and C-class enterprises are subject to enhanced pre-verification and may not use simplified settlement facilities. Conversion into RMB is voluntary at the bank's spot rate for A-class enterprises; there is no mandatory fixed conversion percentage today. The JV must file transaction codes such as 101010 for goods-export advances or 220010 for transport service receipts, and must evidence that freight proceeds correspond to real rail and intermodal contracts.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for onshore CNY jurisdiction, with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the separate offshore central-banking authority for CNH clearing and Hong Kong banking supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

RATING: LOW / NOT PERMITTED as an onshore account or FX settlement provider. Mainland China's licensing regime restricts deposit-taking and foreign exchange settlement to designated commercial banks; offshore digital EMIs cannot connect to CNAPS, CIPS, or SAFE reporting systems, cannot provide onshore corporate current accounts or FDI capital accounts, and cannot perform capital-account conversion. Such EMIs may be used only for non-regulatory treasury cash management in Hong Kong or for the HK parent's offshore liquidity, never for the onshore CN_JV_LTD compliance perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must remit funds from its own bank account in Hong Kong by SWIFT MT103 or ISO20022 to the onshore JV's FDI foreign exchange capital account at a licensed Tier-1 Chinese bank, using BOP transaction code 621111 for a new equity investment and stating the JV's unified social credit code in the remittance narrative. Before any credit is accepted, the JV must complete the Foreign Investment Information Report and obtain an FDI foreign exchange registration form through the bank's capital account information system. Upon receipt, the bank matches the SWIFT reference to the registration and reports the transaction to SAFE through the cross-border receipt and payment platform. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where annual related-party tangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million; intangible property or financial asset transactions exceed RMB 100 million; related-party financing exceeds RMB 10 million; or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is compulsory where the consolidated group revenue in the preceding year is RMB 1 billion or more and the group has cross-border related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 CIT Law allows recharacterisation of transactions lacking reasonable commercial purpose. The Hong Kong shareholder must prove substantive operations and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong to access DTA benefits. For the Chinese joint venture, actual economic substance is demonstrated by a fixed operation centre, full-time logistics staff, strategic management decisions in China, real intermodal invoicing and risk-bearing capacity; failure to maintain these attributes may lead the tax authority to pierce the corporate form and deny treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Dual Contracting and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law, the unpaid CIT is recoverable together with a penalty of not less than 50% and not more than five times the underpaid amount, plus daily surcharge at 0.05%; Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes a fine and imprisonment of up to seven years for tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 or 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Article 20 of the Company Law where directors misuse the limited liability structure to commit tax fraud; personal assets may be seized for tax restitution. Directors face travel bans, tax blacklisting and, after criminal conviction, disqualification from management and imprisonment.
Shell Company and Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 CIT Law and SAT Public Notice 5/2015, disregarding the Hong Kong vehicle, denying treaty relief and reclassifying the dividend as subject to 10% Chinese WHT, plus interest and late-payment penalties. If the commercial purpose is fraudulent, Article 63 evasion penalties also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or operate a conduit in Hong Kong can be held jointly and severally liable for unpaid withholding taxes; Chinese courts may pierce the veil and order personal restitution. Criminal exposure arises if the sham structure is used as part of a tax evasion scheme; travel bans and entry-exit control measures are commonly imposed during investigation.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Rail Fees

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 of the CIT Law, the tax authority may make an arm's length re-pricing adjustment using CUP or TNMM; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation shifts the burden of proof and allows the authority to estimate taxable income. Penalties under Article 63 range from 50% to five times the underpaid tax plus daily surcharges; severe overstatement may trigger criminal referral.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or maintain artificial transfer pricing may be personally liable when the company becomes insolvent or the profit has been extracted to the parent; criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the Criminal Law if the transfer pricing scheme is established as intentional tax evasion, exposing directors to imprisonment, asset freezes, and permanent disqualification as corporate officers.
