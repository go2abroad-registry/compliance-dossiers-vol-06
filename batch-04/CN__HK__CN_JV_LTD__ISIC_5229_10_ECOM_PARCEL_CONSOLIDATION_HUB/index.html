Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not operate a check-the-box regime and does not permit hybrid mismatch elections. Available statutory elections include: (a) voluntary general VAT taxpayer registration when annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000; (b) election, where eligible, for preferential Enterprise Income Tax rates such as High and New Technology Enterprise status at 15%; (c) election to apply special tax treatment for qualified corporate restructurings; and (d) a non-resident enterprise may, with STA approval, use a deemed profit method when accurate accounting records are not maintained. No pass-through entity election is available for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC enterprises must complete tax and VAT registration upon establishment without a monetary threshold for registration itself. The material threshold is for general VAT taxpayer status: compulsory general taxpayer classification applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. Below this threshold the JV may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer under simplified taxation, or may elect general taxpayer status. Exported goods may be zero-rated and qualifying international freight forwarding services may be VAT-exempt.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating is available only when the goods physically leave China customs territory, a valid export customs declaration has been filed, the sale is made to an overseas party, and the export proceeds are collected and verified. For the JV's own parcel consolidation and line-haul services, direct international transportation services supplied by a PRC-licensed transport provider may qualify for zero-rating, while international freight forwarding services for exported goods are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. To defend the treatment, the JV must demonstrate that the service recipient is outside China, the service is consumed outside China, the contracted counterparty is an offshore entity, and the invoicing and payment trail are consistent with cross-border provision. Any domestic consolidation, warehousing, or last-mile handling within China remains within the scope of PRC VAT at the applicable rate.

Under the STA Full Digital Electronic Invoice reform, VAT invoices must be issued through the National Electronic Invoice Platform accessed via the Electronic Tax Bureau. Issuance is subject to STA tax-control clearance in near real time under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. All-digital electronic invoices are issued with a nationwide unified invoice number, transmitted to the STA tax-control system, and connected to the GACC Single Window for export and customs data matching. New taxpayers are automatically assigned electronic invoice quotas; no private anti-fraud digital stamping device or fiscal printer is required on the all-digital electronic invoicing route.

The GACC Customs Single Window, SAFE bank reporting, the Gold Tax Phase IV system, and the STA electronic invoice platform are cross-matched to verify that customs declarations, logistics tracks, VAT invoices, and export proceeds agree. If foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated and verified within 180 days of the export declaration, the exported VAT zero-rating or refund is disallowed and previously claimed exemption or refund is clawed back with interest and late payment surcharge. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free capital-account and current-account convertibility regime under the linked exchange rate system. The HKD is fully convertible, and there is no central bank permission required for capital inflows, outflows, dividends, or profit repatriation. The HKMA's role is limited to exchange-rate stability and management of the Exchange Fund; it does not impose surrender requirements or conversion ratios. The Sino-foreign JV in Hong Kong therefore benefits from complete flexibility in holding HKD, USD, CNH and other currencies. The only material constraint is on the Chinese parent side: mainland capital controls under SAFE require prior ODI registration, NDRC filing, and a qualified designated bank to execute the outward remittance. Once funds reach Hong Kong, they are free; however, transactions involving CNH are cleared through the HKMA's RTGS system. For treasury operations, the JV should still document the source of all incoming funds under HK banks' account-opening and CRS obligations. No profit remittance hurdles exist at the HK level, but dividend payments to the Chinese parent may be subject to mainland tax formalities and banking evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirement applies in Hong Kong. Exporters and trading companies, including parcel consolidation hubs, may retain 100% of export proceeds in any permitted currency indefinitely. There is no maximum holding period. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. If the JV receives proceeds on behalf of mainland e-commerce sellers, it must not be seen to facilitate outbound remittances without proper underlying trade documentation and must channel through licensed banks to avoid violating mainland SAFE rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for mainland-China outbound capital flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the People's Bank of China (PBOC) exercise extraterritorial jurisdiction over the Chinese shareholder

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Airwallex HK is licensed as a Money Service Operator (MSO) under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance and offers multi-currency virtual accounts, but non-bank EMIs are not authorized to provide licensed checking accounts, accept deposits, or access the HKMA's RTGS clearing as settlement members. Therefore, a fintech EMI can be used as an ancillary payment rail for receiving small parcel shipment fees and paying overseas couriers, but cannot serve as the primary treasury or escrow bank. The JV must maintain a Tier-1 licensed bank account for regulatory capital, large outbound trade settlements, and compliance with Chinese-official ODI remittance documentation. Hong Kong law does not ban EMIs, but the mainland Chinese parent's cross-border ODI remittance generally requires a licensed commercial bank to complete SAFE registration and supporting verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: IRD practice under DIPN 58 generally requires a Local File where the aggregate amount of connected-party controlled transactions exceeds HK$10,000,000, and a Master File where the ultimate parent's global group revenue exceeds HK$680,000,000; these de facto thresholds apply only where the relevant documentation requirement is triggered, and the IRD may demand contemporaneous documentation for any transaction that is high-risk, including intercompany management fees and intangible royalties regardless of size.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no statutory Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime in the offshore tax-haven sense, but the Inland Revenue Ordinance, DIPN 58 and prevailing double-taxation arrangement anti-abuse rules require that the Hong Kong entity perform the core income-generating activities (CIGAs) relevant to its claimed income in Hong Kong. For the parcel consolidation JV, CIGAs include sorting, quality inspection, linehaul procurement, billing and risk management; where these functions are performed in China rather than Hong Kong, the IRD may allocate the profit to the Chinese parent and deny treaty benefits, effectively piercing the corporate structure as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s.80(2) and s.82(1): fine up to HK$10,000 on summary conviction and up to HK$50,000 on indictment, plus treble the tax undercharged and imprisonment of up to three years for fraudulent evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the false return are exposed to personal civil restitution for the undercharged tax and penalties; the court may pierce the corporate veil where the company is used as a sham, impose director disqualification, and issue a departure prohibition under IRO s.76 preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong until the assessed liabilities are paid.
Aggressive Transfer Pricing and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAB and s.50AAC empower the IRD to apply the arm's length principle, disallow the excessive deductions, attribute additional assessable profits to the Hong Kong JV, and impose penalties up to treble the extra tax undercharged; the absence of transfer pricing documentation is an aggravating factor.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve or orchestrate non-arm's length fee structures can be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt on fraudulent trading grounds; civil asset recovery actions may attach personal assets, and criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment where false documentation is supplied to the IRD.
Conduit Shell Company Abuse and AML Banking Fraud

Statutory sanction: The IRD may disregard the corporate entity under substance-over-form and DTA anti-abuse principles, deny treaty relief, impose a fine of up to HK$10,000 plus treble the undercharged tax under IRO s.80(2), and refer the matter to law enforcement under the Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance and the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced for fraud and sham transacting; directors face civil restitution of the transferred profits, worldwide asset freezing orders, travel bans and blacklisting by the IRD, and imprisonment of up to seven years where circular payments are used to launder profits and conceal beneficial ownership.
