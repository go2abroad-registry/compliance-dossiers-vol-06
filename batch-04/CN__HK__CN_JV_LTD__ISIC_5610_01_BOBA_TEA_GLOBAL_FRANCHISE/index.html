Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the US check-the-box or hybrid mismatch sense. A Chinese limited liability equity joint venture is always treated as an opaque resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax at 25% on worldwide income; no elective entity classification is available. The only relevant election is the VAT taxpayer classification: a taxpayer whose annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000 may elect simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment, but general VAT taxpayer status is mandatory for any exporter claiming zero-rating and may be voluntarily elected by a company with sound accounting. Non-resident treaty-relief elections for dividend and royalty withholding under the PRC-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement are also available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; however, every incorporated enterprise must complete basic tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance regardless of turnover, and the Chinese JV engaged in export of syrups and food ingredients will in practice be required to register as a general VAT taxpayer from the date of first export in order to issue special VAT invoices and claim export zero-rating.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory by a registered VAT general taxpayer are zero-rated for VAT, and the exporter may claim an export tax rebate equal to the refundable input VAT on the exported syrups and ingredients. To qualify, the Chinese JV must: be the actual exporter on the customs declaration, transfer title to the goods to the overseas franchisee, prove actual loading and departure from the PRC, receive the export proceeds through banking channels from the overseas buyer, and file the export VAT refund declaration within the statutory deadline. Cross-border franchise licensing royalty income supplied to Hong Kong or other offshore franchisees is likewise zero-rated or exempt from VAT to the extent the service is consumed entirely outside China; the JV must retain a cross-border service contract, evidence that the recipient is outside China, and supporting bank receipts.

  • Customs Export Declaration in the name of the Chinese JV, commonly known in trade operations as the export customs declaration and generalised as a Bill of Entry for export, showing the export clearance status and commodity classification
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, rail waybill or road waybill evidencing that the goods left the PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list issued by the JV
  • Purchase VAT special invoices for the exported syrup raw materials, used to compute the export input VAT rebate
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher or bank foreign-exchange collection record issued by the settlement bank, being the current Chinese equivalent of the former Inward Remittance Certificate, evidencing that export proceeds were received from the overseas franchisee
  • Export contract or purchase order connecting the shipment to the foreign buyer
  • Export tax rebate declaration and supporting electronic data submitted through the STA export refund system and SAFE trade monitoring system

The PRC operates invoice issuance through the STA-controlled VAT invoice management system, with fully digitalised electronic invoices issued and cleared on the Electronic Tax Bureau invoice-service platform under Golden Tax Phase IV. Special VAT invoices must be issued using tax-control equipment or the platform in near real time; invoice data is transmitted electronically to the tax authority, and each fully digital invoice carries a unique framework agreement number, commodity-and-service tax classification code, tax rate, digital signature and tax-control code. Anti-fraud stamping is achieved through system-side authentication and machine-driven invoice matching of sales invoices, purchase invoices, customs declarations and foreign-exchange receipts; API-based integration is available to taxpayers using the electronic invoice platform for bulk issuance, but no off-system issuance is permitted.

Export zero-rating claims are automatically cross-matched by Golden Tax Phase IV against the customs export declaration, the VAT special invoice dataset, and the bank collected-proceeds record. If the export declaration is not genuine, the goods do not leave the customs territory, the proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day trade-collection monitoring period, or the tax authority determines that the refund claim was false or unsubstantiated, the STA will disallow the zero-rating, claw back all refunded input VAT, assess late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative fine of not less than 50% and up to 500% of the tax evaded or refund wrongfully obtained. Serious cases involving fraudulent export tax refunds are referred to public security authorities and prosecuted under the PRC Criminal Law as export-tax-rebate fraud, carrying criminal fines, confiscation, and imprisonment; SAFE and the General Administration of Customs may separately impose penalties for foreign-exchange violations or false customs declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB partially convertible. Under the current SAFE/PBOC framework, current account items such as trade and service payments are freely convertible after authenticity verification, whereas capital account items such as equity investment, cross-border loans, and securities trading remain subject to SAFE registration, approval, or quota control. A Sino-foreign JV holding a SAFE-registered foreign exchange account may receive shareholder capital in foreign currency and convert it to RMB through a licensed bank, but conversion must be supported by real underlying documents and the funds must be placed in a 'capital account settlement' sub-account. Export proceeds from the franchise syrup and related product trade are not subject to a forced surrender ratio, but they must be repatriated into China within 90 days of customs declaration; any retention above the account ceiling requires prompt sale or justification. Profit remittance to the HK parent is permitted, but requires annual audit, tax clearance, and dividend declaration; a 10% withholding tax may apply unless reduced by the China-HK double taxation arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE rules for goods trade, total export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days of customs export declaration. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for USD/HKD earnings; the JV may retain the foreign currency up to the bank-approved account ceiling or sell it to a licensed bank at market rates. Any amount above the ceiling must be converted or repatriated with a documented business justification, and the bank will require the customs declaration, original sales contract, and transport documents to verify that the receipt matches the underlying export.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the controlling authorities for the onshore JV; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the relevant monetary authority for the Hong Kong treasury center without FX controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Low for the onshore Chinese entity. Chinese law prohibits non-bank fintech EMIs from opening foreign exchange accounts, settling capital account items, or providing cross-border treasury services for foreign-invested enterprises; only PBOC-licensed commercial banks may perform FX settlement and sale. For a separately incorporated Hong Kong treasury subsidiary, a licensed Hong Kong virtual bank or money service operator (MSO) can act as a secondary multi-currency liquidity, but it cannot be the primary account for the mainland JV. The EMI must use a licensed correspondent bank for any RMB clearing into mainland China, making it an ancillary payment rail rather than a substitute for Tier-1 corporate banking. Thus, the blueprint mandates a Tier-1 domestic bank for statutory and tax purposes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder funds must be remitted from the Hong Kong treasury account directly into the JV's SAFE-registered capital escrow account held at a Tier-1 mainland bank. The SWIFT MT103 must quote the exact legal name of the JV as registered with SAFE, the statutory FDI purpose code 'Investment Inward Remittance' (SAFE code 621), and the remitter name matching the registered shareholder. The receiving bank will freeze the funds pending CPA capital verification; after the verification report is filed, the bank issues a capital account settlement notice, and the funds may be converted into RMB or placed in the operating account. Any conversion must be booked under the capital account settlement mechanism and cannot be used for prohibited investments such as domestic equity or property purchases.For supplier payments (tapioca pearls, fruit syrups, packaging, and compounding machinery) under the JV's global franchise model, SWIFT MT103 remains the standard clearing rail for amounts below the bank's large-value threshold and for royalties, with the supporting invoice and customs declaration attached. For high-value equipment orders, an irrevocable, confirmed commercial letter of credit issued by the JV's bank and advised through the supplier's bank is the preferred rail, with documentary collections used for open-account arrangements on medium-credit-risk counterparties. All outbound FX payments require SAFE trade-in-goods authenticity review; the payment purpose code must match the contract, and for RMB-denominated settlement the JV may use the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) for faster clearing.RMB/USD or RMB/HKD plain vanilla forward contracts with a real trade background and SAFE-approved documentation, typically up to one yearRMB cross-currency swaps matched to the JV's investment loan principal and interest obligations, subject to SAFE approvalFX call and put options used only for underlying commercial exposures, not for speculative open positionsOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permissible solely for the Hong Kong treasury center; NDFs are not permitted for the onshore JV

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is Low for the onshore Chinese entity. Chinese law prohibits non-bank fintech EMIs from opening foreign exchange accounts, settling capital account items, or providing cross-border treasury services for foreign-invested enterprises; only PBOC-licensed commercial banks may perform FX settlement and sale. For a separately incorporated Hong Kong treasury subsidiary, a licensed Hong Kong virtual bank or money service operator (MSO) can act as a secondary multi-currency liquidity, but it cannot be the primary account for the mainland JV. The EMI must use a licensed correspondent bank for any RMB clearing into mainland China, making it an ancillary payment rail rather than a substitute for Tier-1 corporate banking. Thus, the blueprint mandates a Tier-1 domestic bank for statutory and tax purposes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: required if the JV's aggregated annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1,000,000,000. Local File: required if related-party transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000 for tangible goods, RMB 100,000,000 for intangibles or financial assets, or RMB 40,000,000 for services/other related-party transactions. Annual related-party disclosure is mandatory where any related-party transaction exists.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not operate a statutory Economic Substance Regulations regime; however, the beneficial-ownership test (SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9) and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Art. 47) require the HK shareholder to perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong (management, approval of IP, actual funding decisions). The Chinese JV must itself have real staff, property, business records, and decision-making functions. A Hong Kong shell holding the franchise IP without substance and merely licensing it back to the JV is vulnerable to DTA denial, CFC/anti-abuse recharacterisation, and PE risk in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion / fraudulent understatement of income

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Art. 63: penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest (Art. 32); Criminal Law Art. 201: up to 7 years' imprisonment plus fines for tax evasion; public disclosure and enforcement blacklisting with travel restrictions.

Director personal liability: Directors who ordered or consented to false accounts face joint and several liability for the company's tax shortfall, travel bans and asset freezes, and imprisonment for criminal tax evasion. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for fraudulent distributions or concealment.
Transfer pricing non-compliance and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 41 requires arm's-length pricing; Art. 44 permits the tax authority to make deemed assessments. Tax Administration Law Art. 70 and SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 impose monetary fines for non-documentation; the tax authority may add 10-year retroactive adjustments and interest at benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for fines if they instructed the non-documentation; in double-tax agreements the JV's failure triggers mutual agreement procedure but the director could face criminal charges for tax fraud if the TP understatement is concealed. Travel bans are used to compel production of offshore records.
Treaty abuse / shell company dividend stripping

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test (OECD BEPS) and PRC GAAR (CIT Law Art. 47); the tax authority recharacterises the 5% WHT to 10% statutory WHT, imposes penalties and interest, and may reclassify the entire distribution as a taxable deemed dividend or asset transfer; SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 provides anti-beneficial-ownership features.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the artificial scheme are jointly liable for the incremental withholding tax and penalties. They may be placed on the tax blacklist (travel ban), subject to asset freezing, and prosecuted for fraudulent tax declaration where the treaty abuse is criminal. The corporate veil is pierced if the shell's capital is nominal or the company is used as a mere instrument for tax fraud.
