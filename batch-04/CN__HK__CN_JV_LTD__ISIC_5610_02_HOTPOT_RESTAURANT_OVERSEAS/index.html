Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free capital account where HKD is fully convertible under the Linked Exchange Rate system managed by the HKMA; there are no surrender requirements or exchange controls for current or capital transactions. The PRC shareholder, however, faces a distinctly controlled convertibility framework: RMB is freely convertible for current account items, such as trade and service payments, provided genuine underlying documentation is available, but capital account transactions, including outbound direct investment into the HK entity, require prior approval/registration by SAFE and possibly NDRC/MOFCOM. In practice, the Chinese parent must complete ODI filing through SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administrative Registration, then acquire FX at a domestic designated bank and remit HKD/USD to the HK company. There is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds under PRC rules, but all export FX receipts from the sale of hotpot condiments or equipment must be repatriated to China within the prescribed period (generally 180 days from customs export declaration); these receipts may be retained in a foreign exchange account or settled to RMB at the company's discretion. Profit remittance from HK to the PRC shareholder is permissible once the HK company has filed its audited financial statements, paid any tax in HK and the PRC tax on that dividend is settled; however, the dividend must be traced to registered equity capital and cannot exceed the accumulated distributable profits. The main hurdles are documentation intensity, strict underlying transaction verification, and the need to obtain SAFE registration before dividends are repatriated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC SAFE regulations, export proceeds from goods trades (including hotpot condiments and restaurant equipment) must be collected and repatriated into China within 180 calendar days from the date of customs declaration, unless an extension is approved by SAFE for bona fide commercial reasons. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the exporting JV may retain the receipts in a current-account foreign exchange settlement account at a Tier-1 Chinese bank, or sell them to the bank for RMB at the prevailing market rate. For service trade royalties and franchise fees, the collection period is generally 180 days as well but must be matched by a valid service trade contract and tax compliance documents.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation under DIPN 59 requires a taxpayer to prepare both a Master File and Local File where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds HK$10 million and the taxpayer's annual turnover exceeds HK$100 million; for smaller JV hotpot entities, simplified documentation comprising a global organization chart, transaction overview, contract identification, and functional and risk analysis is expected; intercompany transactions in the hotpot restaurant chain may include brand license fees, condiment purchases from the mainland parent, management fees, shared marketing costs, treasury loans, and profit-sharing from direct-operated branches in China

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate as a classic zero-tax shell jurisdiction, but the IRD applies the territorial source and economic substance concepts through the sourcing of profits principle, the general anti-avoidance provision in IRO Section 61A, and the OECD-aligned transfer pricing rules. A Hong Kong JV seeking reduced DTA rates or an offshore claim must demonstrate that the core income-generating activities (CIGA) – e.g., brand development, strategic management of hotpot restaurant franchises, procurement decisions, pricing of condiment supply, treasury management, and board-level control – are actually performed by personnel physically present and with decision-making authority in Hong Kong. If the Hong Kong entity is merely a paper owner of the restaurant brand or an invoice conduit, the mainland Chinese tax authority may deny the 5% dividend and royalty treaty benefits and the Hong Kong IRD may deem the profits as HK-sourced because the Hong Kong entity, through its overseas operations, is carrying on business in Hong Kong. Comprehensive substance records – employment contracts, office leases, board meeting minutes, negotiating papers, bank account statements in Hong Kong, and staff tax filings – are mandatory for any international tax structuring.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Cash Skimming and Under-Reporting of Restaurant Receipts

Statutory sanction: Under Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 80(2), willful tax evasion with intent to defraud the IRD is punishable by a fine of HK$50,000, treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment for up to three years. Under Section 82(1), assistance in the preparation of false returns may also be prosecuted. The PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid enterprise income tax plus a daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% where tax evasion is established.

Director personal liability: Where the director personally sanctions or turns a blind eye to cash suppression, the corporate veil will be pierced for criminal purposes; the director is jointly and severally liable with the company for the unpaid tax and penalties, personal bank accounts may be frozen, assets sold by court execution, and a travel restriction order may be issued prohibiting departure from Hong Kong; imprisonment of up to three years applies on conviction, and the same conduct makes the director ineligible to be a company director under the Companies Ordinance.
Shell Company Substance Abuse and Fraudulent DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke IRO Section 61A (anti-avoidance) and IRO Section 61 (artificial transactions) to disregard the offshore claim and tax all profits at 16.5% with a 100% penalty under IRO Section 82A; in China, the State Taxation Administration may apply the GAAR under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, deny the treaty benefit, impose a 5% additional withholding on dividends, and add a 0.05% daily late fee and a fine of up to five times the tax underpaid; fraudulent claims of tax residence may also lead to criminal prosecution for false statements.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly certify false offshore claims or beneficial ownership declarations are personally liable for the resulting corporate tax, interest, and penalties; the Hong Kong court may issue an order disqualifying the directors, freezing personal assets, and banning travel; if the shell arrangement is used to conceal proceeds of crime, the director may be prosecuted under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance for money laundering, with penalties including imprisonment up to 14 years and confiscation of assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation of Brand Royalties and Condiment Supply

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong's codified transfer pricing rules in IRO Section 50AAB require the IRD to make an adjustment to the taxpayer's profits where a controlled transaction is not performed on arm's length conditions; failure to keep adequate contemporaneous documentation can result in a penalty under IRO Section 80(2) of HK$50,000 plus treble the tax undercharged, and in the PRC, the tax authority may reassess the taxable income, deny the royalty deduction, impose punitive withholding tax at 10%, levy 0.05% daily late repayment interest, and impose fines under PRC Tax Administration Law Articles 44 and 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign intercompany agreements without supporting economic substance may have the corporate veil pierced if the transactions are found to be fraudulent or sham; the directors can be held personally liable for the amounts of tax assessed as a result of the transfer pricing adjustment, share in civil restitution actions brought by tax authorities, suffer immigration control or travel bans, and face criminal liability for false accounting or aiding tax evasion with imprisonment of up to three years in Hong Kong.
