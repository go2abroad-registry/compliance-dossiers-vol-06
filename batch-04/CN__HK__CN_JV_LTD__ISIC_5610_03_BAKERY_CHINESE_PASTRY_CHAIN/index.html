Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a company limited by shares has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account obligation. The shareholders may subscribe for one or more ordinary shares at a nominal value determined by the board, and any premium must be credited to the share premium account. The Hong Kong entity is not subject to PRC capital contribution escrow rules, although if the ultimate Chinese shareholders are making outbound investment filings, separate SAFE/MOFCOM reporting may apply at the PRC shareholder level.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certification under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), including approved fire service installations, emergency lighting, means of escape, and fire apparatus access. Buildings Department Occupancy Permit or Letter of Compliance confirming structural adequacy, floor load capacity, and fire-resistant construction for bakery ovens and industrial equipment. Labour Department occupational safety and health approval under the Occupational Safety and Occupational Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) and the Factories and Industrial Undertakings Ordinance (Cap. 59), covering electrical safety, ventilation, noise, machine guarding, and safe systems of work. Civil defense emergency preparedness requirements are administered through the Security Bureau and the Fire Services Department, and the operator must maintain an emergency evacuation plan and conduct periodic fire drills.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a company limited by shares has no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account obligation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operational through the National Tax and Fee Collection Digital E-Government Portal (eTax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the 12366 tax service hotline

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established under the PRC Company Law, the enterprise must first complete market entity registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) which functions as the sole statutory tax identification number. The USCC is automatically issued upon the business license approval. Within 30 days, the joint venture must complete post-license tax registration through the eTax portal or at the competent tax bureau, supplying the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity, shareholder register, and premises documentation. For a non-resident foreign enterprise without a local establishment earning PRC-source income, a separate Taxpayer Identification Number for Non-Residents is assigned by the tax authority upon filing for withholding registration. However, for CN_JV_LTD as a resident enterprise, the USCC is the definitive tax identifier and VAT taxpayer registration number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

PRC tax law provides no 'check-the-box' entity classification election or hybrid mismatch elections akin to US or EU concepts. However, the following elections exist: (a) VAT general taxpayer versus small-scale taxpayer status election (a small-scale taxpayer may elect general taxpayer status if it has fixed operating premises and sound accounting standards); (b) election to apply preferential enterprise income tax rates (e.g., High and New Technology Enterprise status) where eligible; and (c) consolidated tax payment election for cross-region branches of the same legal entity. None of these alter the entity-level C-type taxation of the joint venture.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 0 - mandatory VAT registration for all business entities from the date of business license issuance; simplified taxation / small-scale taxpayer threshold is annual turnover of RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC tax rules, VAT zero-rating (0% rate with credit/refund mechanism) applies to: (i) exported goods physically transported out of the Chinese customs territory under formal export customs procedures, (ii) certain internationally traded services and offshore services (including central kitchen supplies to overseas franchisees) listed in the cross-border zero-rated and tax-exempt service catalogue, and (iii) exported digital services consumed entirely outside China. For the bakery chain's cross-border transactions, zero-rating is available only where there is a genuine cross-border supply to a non-resident franchisee located outside mainland China, where the POS and central kitchen demonstrate transfer of ownership and risk to the overseas buyer, and where the export customs declaration is filed in the name of the JV, not the overseas franchisee. Sales to Hong Kong SAR via physical movement of goods require Hong Kong Customs processing; goods crossing to Hong Kong are treated as exports and can qualify for zero-rating, provided the goods are actually delivered outside mainland China. Domestic sales within mainland China, even to Hong Kong-invested entities, are subject to standard VAT at the applicable rate (6% for catering supplies).

  • Customs Export Declaration (PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration Form) with a unique customs entry number and electronic export bill
  • Bill of Lading / Sea Waybill or Airway Bill evidencing physical movement of goods out of the PRC customs territory, together with the packing list and commercial invoice
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt Voucher (or Export Proceeds Repatriation Receipt) derived from the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system, evidencing receipt of payment within the statutory repatriation period

Since December 1, 2023, China has fully rolled out the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice mechanism through the National Tax and Fee Collection E-Government Platform. Mechanism: (i) taxpayers issue invoices in near-real-time via the eTax portal or API-connected enterprise systems; (ii) each invoice is transmitted to the tax authority's information system for digital confirmation, assignment of a unique invoice code, and anti-fraud stamping; (iii) invoice data is securely hashed and encrypted with national cryptographic algorithms; (iv) offline paper invoices are gradually being phased out. Bakeries with POS systems may opt for the official point-of-sale invoice integration API. The mandate requires retention of digital invoice XML data for at least 30 years and real-time transmission of point-of-sale takings to the tax authority when using approved fiscal cash registers in non-digital-invoice zones, subject to local State Taxation Bureau circulars.

Export VAT zero-rating is subject to rigorous scrutiny by the STA, the General Administration of Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). At least 15% of paperless export declarations are selected for electronic risk analysis, and suspicious declarations trigger document review and on-site inspection. If a zero-rated supply is later found to be unqualified (e.g., goods returned domestically, amount inflated, false customs declaration, or failure to repatriate foreign currency within 180 days), the tax authority will: (a) claw back the full amount of VAT refunded plus exempted output VAT, (b) charge interest on the short-paid tax from the original refund date, (c) impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount fraudulently refunded under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 66, and (d) if the fraud is criminal, refer the case for prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 204 for the crime of fraudulent export tax refund, punishable by imprisonment up to life or death in very serious cases. Additionally, exporters that fail integrity checks may be suspended from export tax refund processing for up to 36 months.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating RMB regime with full but document-contingent current account convertibility for goods and services and a restricted capital account. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture can convert current-account receipts into RMB on a real-demand basis after the receiving bank has verified invoices, customs declarations, or service contracts. Capital account flows are not freely convertible: inbound registered capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be recorded with SAFE through the combined FDI registration and routed into a designated foreign exchange capital account, after which it can be settled into RMB for permitted business purposes. The HK parent enjoys full HKD convertibility under Hong Kong's linked exchange rate system, but that does not bypass PRC supervision at the entry point. Profit remittance to the HK investor is treated as a current-account dividend, yet the bank requires an audited annual financial statement, a tax clearance or withholding certificate, a board resolution, and SAFE service trade reporting. There is no compulsory conversion ratio for export or current-account foreign exchange, and any suspicious round-tripping pattern between the PRC entity and Hong Kong treasury accounts will trigger enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated to a domestic trade foreign exchange current account through the designated PRC bank within 90 days of the customs export declaration, unless the exporter has obtained a SAFE-approved deferred collection registration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain 100% of export foreign currency receipts in its settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB, provided the trade foreign exchange monitoring declaration, customs documentation, commercial invoices, and transport or logistics evidence are available on file.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the PRC onshore joint venture; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong dollar clearing and payment system layer

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary settlement channel. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed domestic deposit-taking institutions in mainland China and cannot open the SAFE-controlled capital account or receive FDI registered capital for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. They also cannot execute the required tax clearance, capital verification, or trade-monitoring filings on the PRC side. A licensed PRC payment institution can support POS settlement and low-value collection from bakery franchise stores, but the statutory treasury and FX functions must remain with a Tier-1 commercial bank. For the Hong Kong clearing leg, a licensed HK EMI or money service operator can manage operational float under the HKMA regime, but it should not replace the onshore banking relationship.
