Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None, as to entity classification. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or flow-through election for a limited-liability Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; the JV is taxable at entity level as a PRC resident enterprise. The only relevant election in the indirect-tax layer is the VAT small-scale taxpayer simplified method at a 3% collection rate while annual taxable sales remain below the mandatory general taxpayer threshold; crossing CNY 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales triggers compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration. Hybrid mismatch positions are handled under statutory anti-avoidance and controlled foreign corporation rules, not elective elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For the restaurant/catering service business the headline VAT rate is 6% for general taxpayers and the simplified collection rate is 3% for small-scale taxpayers, subject to the STA's temporary reduced 1% small-scale taxpayer policy where applicable. There is no turnover exemption from basic tax registration for incorporated enterprises; the JV must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales above CNY 5,000,000; below that threshold the JV may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, and small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales at or below CNY 100,000 (quarterly CNY 300,000) are eligible for the temporary zero-VAT ordinary-invoice exemption while that policy is in force. Packaged retail goods sold off-premises and central-kitchen wholesale supplies may be classified under the 13% goods VAT rate, requiring careful revenue-classification analysis.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer may claim 0% VAT and input-tax refund on goods that are legally exported from mainland China under a customs export declaration and for which the JV is the seller of record, the goods physically leave Chinese customs territory, the sale is made

The JV must issue Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices through the Unified E-Tax Bureau digital invoice module for B2B and B2C domestic sales and for export sales. Each digital invoice carries a unique invoice code, encrypted information and anti-counterfeit QR code and is transmitted in real time to the STA invoice management database; no private offline invoice is legally usable for VAT input credit or export rebate. VAT Special Invoices supporting input tax credits are validated against the STA invoice service system before the customer may deduct input VAT. There is no separate municipal fiscal stamping mechanism for mainland China VAT; tax clearance is effected through the real-time e-invoice upload and reconciliation of sales and export records in the e-tax database. Export goods are separately cleared through China Customs electronic customs systems, and customs clearance data are matched with the STA export-rebate module.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR operates as a fully convertible, offshore-style currency jurisdiction under the Linked Exchange Rate System administered by the HKMA; the Hong Kong dollar trades freely and there are no cap or current account conversion restrictions. The mainland China component of this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is subject to a uniquely bifurcated FX regime: current account transactions such as trade, royalties, and service fees are generally convertible once the underlying transaction is verified and supported by invoices and contracts, while capital account movements such as equity injection, shareholder loans, and return of investment require specific SAFE registrations and step-by-step approval. Under the current account convertibility framework, the JV may pay cross-border royalties or supplier invoices in foreign currency after tax authorities confirm withholding; however, profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent remains a highly scrutinized matter requiring audited financial statements, a verified underlying profitability position, and a valid foreign exchange payment application submitted through a domestic designated bank. All export proceeds generated by the Chinese production and supply side must be repatriated through a domestic settlement account; SAFE performs monitoring but does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio. The principal remittance hurdles are tax clearance certification, proof of the underlying economic substance, and consistency between the JV articles of association and the actual beneficial ownership structure, with stricter documentary scrutiny when the Hong Kong conduit is viewed as a passive treasury vehicle rather than an operating commercial partner.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods export receivables, the statutory repatriation window is generally 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless an extension is granted by SAFE with documented commercial justification; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero because SAFE abolished compulsory surrender and now requires export receipts to be credited to a designated foreign exchange account or settled into RMB on a voluntary basis, subject to trade monitoring and reconciliation with customs declarations, VAT records, and logistics documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for mainland China operations; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the currency board authority for the HKD and the Linked Exchange Rate System for the Hong Kong treasury side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for mainland China because foreign fintech EMIs and neobanks are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to maintain domestic corporate accounts, execute RMB/CAP settlement, or perform statutory FX verification for a Chinese JV; they cannot substitute for a Tier-1 domestic bank. For the Hong Kong treasury and licensing side, a Hong Kong-licensed Stored Value Facility or Money Service Operator may support ancillary merchant collections and small-value remittance, but it remains legally and practically constrained from acting as the primary cross-border banking bridge for equity injection, royalty remittance, or trade settlement. Overall viability rating: Low for the mainland entity, Moderate only for auxiliary HK treasury functions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Returns

Artificial Shell / Treaty Abuse and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: China GAAR (EIT Law Article 47, Implementation Regulations Article 120) permits the tax authority to disregard the Hong Kong vehicle and recharacterize the payment as a taxable dividend at 10% or as taxable income of the ultimate resident; anti-abuse provisions push the rate up to mainland 25% plus late penalties; State Taxation Administration may deny DTA benefits under the Principal-Purpose Test; Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance Section 61A counteracts tax avoidance and imposes additional 100% assessable tax
