Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) imposes no minimum paid-up share capital for a Hong Kong private company limited by shares, and there is no escrow, blocked-account or capital deposit requirement. The founding shareholders may fix the initial issued capital at any amount, including HKD 1.00, and the actual allotment and subscription terms will be recorded in the Articles of Association and the first board resolution. For a ghost kitchen joint venture, the board should nevertheless maintain a prudent initial working capital to satisfy FEHD licence hygiene, equipment, rent and payroll requirements.

Funding & unlock path: The Facilities must obtain an Occupation Permit from the Buildings Department confirming structural load capacity for commercial kitchen equipment. Fire safety approval must be obtained from the Fire Services Department under the Fire Safety Ordinance, including a Certificate of Completion of Fire Service Installations and Equipment, appropriate fire-resisting construction, exit routes, portable extinguishers and automatic sprinklers where required, annual fire safety inspection, and gas or electrical isolation protocols. Occupational safety clearance is required under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509), including certification of exhaust hoods, gas safety, slip-resistance, first aid and staff fire drills. The Food and Environmental Hygiene Department will inspect the premises to ensure that the physical layout, waste storage, wash-up facilities and ventilation meet the Food Factory Licence conditions before issuance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing and records portal: IRD eTax via GovHK (www.gov.hk/en/theme/bf/etax.htm) and the Trade Single Window for customs declarations; Business Registration Office under the IRD issues Business Registration Certificates; Companies Registry handles non-Hong Kong company registration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing operations in Hong Kong: (1) incorporate a Hong Kong company or register the foreign entity as a non-Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance by filing the prescribed registration form with the Companies Registry; (2) upon commencement of business in Hong Kong, apply to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate within one month, using the statutory business registration application form and paying the prescribed Business Registration Fee and Levy; (3) receive the eight-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the de facto tax identification number for all Hong Kong tax interactions; (4) if the entity has no physical presence but derives Hong Kong-source profits, file standalone Profits Tax Returns issued by the IRD and maintain a Hong Kong tax representative; (5) if the entity is registered under the Companies Ordinance, the Company Number must be quoted together with the BRN on tax filings; Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT/TIN because no value-added tax or goods and services tax exists in the jurisdiction.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign non-resident entity establishing operations in Hong Kong: (1) incorporate a Hong Kong company or register the foreign entity as a non-Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance by filing the prescribed registration form with the Companies Registry; (2) upon commencement of business in Hong Kong, apply to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate within one month, using the statutory business registration application form and paying the prescribed Business Registration Fee and Levy; (3) receive the eight-digit Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the de facto tax identification number for all Hong Kong tax interactions; (4) if the entity has no physical presence but derives Hong Kong-source profits, file standalone Profits Tax Returns issued by the IRD and maintain a Hong Kong tax representative; (5) if the entity is registered under the Companies Ordinance, the Company Number must be quoted together with the BRN on tax filings; Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT/TIN because no value-added tax or goods and services tax exists in the jurisdiction.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box pass-through election, fiscal unity, or hybrid mismatch election. Companies are taxable entities under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and profits tax liability is determined by the territorial source principle; offshore profits may be reported as non-taxable, but this is not an elective entity classification, it is a factual sourcing claim subject to IRD audit.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Not applicable -- Hong Kong imposes no VAT, GST, or general sales and use tax. Section 5610 ghost kitchen food delivery services are outside the scope of indirect taxation; only goods falling under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance may attract customs or excise duties upon importation into Hong Kong.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for indirect tax purposes. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold. The only analogous threshold is under the Business Registration Ordinance: a business with annual gross turnover above HKD 500,000 must pay the Business Registration Fee and Levy, but this is a business registration obligation, not an indirect tax filing obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable for VAT purposes because Hong Kong does not operate a VAT system and therefore cannot grant a zero-rated export VAT claim. For profits tax purposes, the substantive export relief is the territorial source principle: profits derived from the export of goods or provision of services will escape Hong Kong profits tax if the earning operations are performed and concluded outside Hong Kong. A taxpayer may report such offshore income as non-taxable in the Profits Tax Return and must substantiate the offshore nature of the profit with objective evidence of the place where contracts were negotiated, concluded, and performed, as well as the place of delivery and payment.

  • Exact Export Declaration lodged with the Census and Statistics Department through the Trade Single Window (CEDARS electronic lodgement system) for each outward shipment of physical goods
  • Commercial invoice issued by the Hong Kong operating entity describing the goods and the overseas buyer
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical movement of goods from Hong Kong to the foreign destination
  • Packing list detailing the consignment contents for customs verification
  • Bank credit advice or remittance advice from a Hong Kong authorized institution evidencing actual receipt of foreign sales proceeds from the overseas buyer, as Hong Kong does not issue a formal Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) but the bank-generated documentation performs the same evidentiary function

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no real-time fiscal clearance, no continuous transaction controls, no e-invoicing API requirement, and no anti-fraud digital stamping for invoices. Taxpayers must retain adequate business records, including invoices, receipts, and underlying transaction documents, for at least seven years under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance; electronic invoices are acceptable if accessible and legible.

Hong Kong does not impose VAT clawback because no VAT applies, but profits tax clawback and penalties apply for false, incorrect, or unsubstantiated offshore profit claims. The Inland Revenue Department may reopen assessments within six years, or within ten years in cases of fraud or neglect, and issue assessments to recover undercharged tax plus additional tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance of up to 100% of the tax undercharged. Criminal penalties for knowingly and wilfully making a false return or statement may reach HKD 50,000 plus three times the tax undercharged and imprisonment up to three years. For failure to notify chargeability to profits tax, automatic penalties and publication of taxpayer details may also apply. The IRD generally treats a claim of non-taxable offshore export income as high-risk absent contemporaneous export documentation, customs entry evidence, payment receipts, bank statements, and a coherent explanation of where the profit-earning operations occurred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China uses a managed floating FX regime. Current account items, including profit remittances and trade services, are generally convertible after verifying genuine underlying documents. Capital account items are restricted: inbound equity investment, foreign debt, cross-border guarantees, and external ownership changes require approval or registration with SAFE. The Sino-foreign JV must first secure an FDI registration, open a foreign currency capital account, and convert those funds through a licensed domestic bank; such RMB may only be used for lawful operating purposes within the negative-list framework. Dividends payable to the Hong Kong shareholder are remittable only after tax payment, statutory audit, and board approval, with current-account verification and withholding tax deduction. Export proceeds are no longer subject to a mandatory surrender ratio, but all export receipts must be repatriated and matched with customs declarations. Each conversion or outbound remittance must be classified under the relevant BOP transaction code and supported by documentary evidence; capital-account inflows cannot be netted or used to settle current-account payables. This is therefore a partially convertible jurisdiction, and banks treat every cross-border fund movement as a supervised compliance event.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 calendar days from the date stated on the export customs declaration or transport document. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%; China no longer forces the compulsory sale of export foreign exchange. Exporters may retain the foreign currency in a licensed foreign exchange settlement account or sell it to the bank at the market rate. The receiving bank must match the receipt against SAFE's Trade Monitoring System and the customs clearance record; late or unmatched receipts can trigger B-class trade supervision and stricter documentary review.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW and legally non-viable as a primary onshore treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMIs are not authorized under PRC banking law to hold corporate RMB basic accounts, foreign exchange settlement accounts, or FDI capital accounts for a Sino-foreign JV. Using an EMI to receive the inbound HK equity contribution would violate PBOC/SAFE account-gatekeeping requirements and fail KYC underwriting. The Hong Kong shareholder may use such tools outside China only for ancillary payments; the onshore JV must custody operating and capital liquidity with a licensed domestic commercial bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong lacks an explicit local currency threshold, but under the IRD's administrative practice, a local file and master file are required for MNE groups with group revenue above EUR 750 million and related-party transactions exceeding HKD 22 million (intangibles), HKD 44 million (services/financial), or HKD 220 million (goods). For lower revenue groups, a simple contemporaneous benchmark report is compulsory if an offshore claim is made.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not enact economic substance regulations similar to Cayman/BVI, but source principles require that companies claiming offshore exemption prove core income-generating activities physically located outside HK. For a ghost kitchen JV, CIGA will include order acceptance, food production, cloud kitchen management, and payment processing. If all CIGA are in HK, profits are fully taxable despite the JV being owned by an offshore parent. Shell corporate structures without physical presence or qualified staff will be disallowed and the entire return recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False Offshore Claims

Statutory sanction: Offences under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) sections 82 and 82A: fine of HKD 100,000 plus three times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to 3 years. Taxpayer must also pay interest and penalties. The IRD may assess within 6 years after the end of the year.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the JV is merely a facade. Directors are jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debt up to the amount of dividends/fees extracted; court can make restitution orders to compensate the company and impose disqualification orders under Companies Ordinance sections 168-170. Travel bans under IRO s.72A prohibit directors from leaving Hong Kong until the assessed tax is settled; prison sentence for willful evasion can be up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing – Fabricated Royalties and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Transfer Pricing Ordinance in Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018: IRD may upward adjust the JV's assessable profits by disallowing the excessive royalty. Penalties under Cap. 112 s.82A up to HKD 100,000 plus 100% of the additional tax assessed. If tax avoidance exists, the taxpayer faces a conservative assessment and no statutory defense.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved and signed the false transfer pricing documentation are in breach of fiduciary duties and can be sued by the JV's shareholders for losses. Under the Ordinance, officers are personal liable for recovery of tax as they knowingly permitted the underpayment. In serious cases, the court may order banking searches, asset freezing orders and sentence up to 2 years for conspiracy to defraud the CRA (Commissioner).
Unlicensed Food Business – Ghost Kitchen Without Statutory Licence and AML Violations

Statutory sanction: Violation of Food Business Regulation sections 2 and 31: maximum fine of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment of 6 months. Continued operation after prosecution may incur daily penalty of HKD 500. Under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO), failure to maintain record-keeping (s. 3) carries a fine of HKD 100,000.

Director personal liability: Every director and officer is liable if the offence was committed with their consent or connivance. Directors may be imprisoned for up to 6 months personally. In tax lien proceedings, the IRD may lift the corporate veil to force directors' personal assets to satisfy unpaid taxes. The court can also order a compulsory winding-up of the shell JV and ban directors from holding any public office.
