Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Hong Kong law. There is no statutory minimum issued, paid-up, or escrowed share capital for a private company limited by shares. The statement of capital filed with Form NNC1 must disclose the number of issued shares, class, and amount paid or credited as paid. No blocked escrow account or capital deposit is mandated; however, the joint venture parties should allot shares at par or premium according to the subscription letter and record allotment in the register of members.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic filing portal: eTAX (Electronic Services for Tax). Business registration is administered by the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-incorporated joint venture company: (1) apply to the Companies Registry for incorporation under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and obtain the Certificate of Incorporation; (2) apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business; the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the entity's taxpayer identification number (TIN). A foreign non-resident entity carrying on business in Hong Kong must first register as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 11 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and then obtain its Business Registration Certificate; no separate VAT/GST tax ID exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST. The BRN must be quoted on tax returns, invoices, and correspondence with the IRD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. The Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) provides no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election. Profits tax treatment is driven by the territorial source doctrine; an entity may factually assert that profits arise outside Hong Kong, but this is a source claim, not a tax classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales and use tax; selective Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) excise regime only for dutiable commodities such as alcohol, tobacco, and hydrocarbon oil.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST/sales tax registration threshold exists. A Dutiable Goods Licence is required only where the catering kitchen imports, possesses, stores, or uses dutiable commodities; the trigger is qualitative, not a monetary turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable - Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax, so there is no export VAT zero-rating claim and no VAT refund mechanism. For international airline catering delivered to outbound aircraft, the goods are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax; the legal points are customs declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) and, for profits tax, whether the profits arise in or derive from Hong Kong under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

  • Statutory export declaration lodged electronically with the Customs and Excise Department through an approved electronic service provider (Government Electronic Trading Services/Tradelink) under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60)
  • Air Waybill or Bill of Lading evidencing carriage of the catering consignment from Hong Kong to the overseas destination
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and airline catering contract or purchase order identifying the buyer, product, quantity, and destination
  • Aircraft ramp delivery docket or airline/relevant ground-handler signed receipt evidencing delivery of the meals to a departing international flight
  • Bank payment/remittance advice or foreign exchange receipt from the airline customer evidencing cross-border settlement; Hong Kong has no exchange-control repatriation certificate, but bank records substantiate the transaction flow

No real-time fiscal clearance, B2B e-invoicing clearance, API clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists. The IRD requires records to be retained under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and eTAX is used for tax filing, but invoices are not pre-cleared or stamped by the Government.

No VAT clawback is possible because no VAT is imposed. However, the Customs and Excise Department may audit export declarations and false declarations may attract penalties under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60); the Inland Revenue Department may re-characterize an offshore claim, raise additional assessments, and impose interest and additional tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) of up to treble the tax undercharged, and may prosecute for fraud or willful evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The operating entity, CN_JV_LTD, is a mainland China-registered Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, so the effective convertibility regime is the PRC managed float plus SAFE approval-based monitoring rather than full free convertibility. Current-account transactions, including genuine imports of catering ingredients, aviation packaging, freezer equipment spare parts, and payment of service fees for the airline inflight catering kitchen, are generally convertible after a commercial bank conducts an authenticity review based on contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax vouchers. Capital-account items are heavily monitored: the Hong Kong shareholder's equity contribution must be wired to a SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account, verified by a licensed Chinese CPA, and converted into RMB for the enumerated business purposes in the Joint Venture Contract and Feasibility Study. RMB converted from capital accounts cannot be applied to securities, wealth management products, entrusted loans, onshore equity investments, or interbank lending. Dividends and residual profits can be remitted to Hong Kong only when the JV has completed tax filing, the audited financial statements show distributable retained earnings, the board of directors passes a dividend resolution, and the bank obtains tax clearance and profit-remittance records. The practical result is a managed pseudo-convertibility system: current-account flows are open and routine, while capital-account flows remain restricted but executable with documentary proof.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE abolished the former mandatory export surrender mechanism. Under the currently effective goods-trade foreign-exchange administration rules, CN_JV_LTD may retain 100% of export proceeds in a domestic foreign-currency account or convert them into RMB at any designated Tier-1 bank; there is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio. The statutory monitoring timeline requires that proceeds for goods shipped or services performed be repatriated in a timely manner and, where a payment is not booked within 90 days after the customs export declaration date, the JV must file the overdue collection item through the SAFE goods-trade monitoring system and adjust the corresponding trade-information record. For bonded airport inflight catering supplies delivered to international airline customers, the JV must evidence the transaction with the airline catering agreement, flight manifests, onboard meal delivery orders, and customs bonded or export documentation; the same 90-day reporting discipline applies to any deferred airline receivable.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the governing exchange-control and balance-of-payments authority for the onshore Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulates Hong Kong SAR banks but imposes no foreign-exchange controls on the Hong Kong capital conduit.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the onshore PRC entity. Offshore digital EMI providers and multi-currency payment platforms are not licensed by the PBOC, CBIRC, or SAFE as domestic settlement banks and cannot open the required RMB basic deposit account or SAFE Foreign Exchange Capital Account. They cannot clear RMB through CNAPS/ACS, cannot file the SAFE cross-border payment statistics, and cannot host the company's statutory capital verification. Any attempt to use an unregulated EMI as the JV's principal treasury rail would fail KYC, make the capital injection invalid for SAFE, and expose the JV to money-service-business restrictions. An EMI may only be used by the Hong Kong shareholder as an ancillary remittance interface before routing final settlement through a licensed Tier-1 bank into the onshore account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder instructs its HK bank to send a SWIFT MT103 to the beneficiary account at the designated Tier-1 bank in mainland China. The receiving bank must confirm the JV's SAFE Foreign Exchange Investment (FDI) registration code, the JV business license with the unified social credit code, the board resolution approving the capital injection schedule, and the maximum outstanding amount under the Joint Venture Contract. The remittance must specify the SAFE BOP statistical declaration code for foreign direct investment equity capital formation, commonly declared as 'FDI capital injection into a foreign-invested enterprise' in the SWIFT field 70-59 remittance information, and must not be routed through an unlicensed EMI. After the funds are credited, a licensed PRC CPA completes a capital verification report, the bank updates the FDI verification record in the SAFE system, and the JV may then settle the foreign currency into RMB according to the approved use-of-proceeds plan. The entire inbound route must produce a full audit trail from HK bank SWIFT, intermediate correspondent bank, receiving bank credit advice, SAFE cross-border reporting, and CPA closure.For the inflight catering kitchen, operational outflows will travel through CNAPS/ACS for domestic RMB payroll, utilities, and airport fees; through SWIFT MT103 for overseas purchases of packaging films, cooking equipment spare parts, specialized food ingredients, and service fees; and through Commercial Letters of Credit for high-value imported galley equipment such as blast chillers, tray assembly lines, and aircraft loading vehicles. Documentary Collections are acceptable for low-risk, stable traders where the supporting invoices and bills of lading can be reconciled. All outbound MT103 instructions must carry proper SAFE BOP transaction codes consistent with the underlying import or service contract, customs declarations, transport documents, and tax payment certificates. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the onshore PRC entity. Offshore digital EMI providers and multi-currency payment platforms are not licensed by the PBOC, CBIRC, or SAFE as domestic settlement banks and cannot open the required RMB basic deposit account or SAFE Foreign Exchange Capital Account. Any attempt to use an unregulated EMI as the JV's principal treasury rail would fail KYC, make the capital injection invalid for SAFE, and expose the JV to money-service-business restrictions. An EMI may only be used by the Hong Kong shareholder as an ancillary remittance interface before routing final settlement through a licensed Tier-1 bank into the onshore account.
