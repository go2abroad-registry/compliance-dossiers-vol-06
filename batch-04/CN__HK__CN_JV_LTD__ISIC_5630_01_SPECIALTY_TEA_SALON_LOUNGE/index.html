Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (PRC STA) with the electronic tax service hall of the competent local STA branch for mainland China; and, for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), including the Business Registration Office and the IRD eTAX online platform, acting through the Companies Registry one-stop registration service.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the entity. If the entity is a Mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, apply to the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) for the business license; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the license is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for both Enterprise Income Tax and VAT, and no separate tax certificate number is issued. If the entity is or also maintains a Hong Kong incorporated private limited company, apply for incorporation through the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and, under the one-stop company registration and business registration service, automatically apply to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the resulting Business Registration number is the Hong Kong statutory tax identification number for profits tax and business records. Step 2: Complete PRC tax registration within 30 days of the AMR business license issuance by filing through the local STA electronic tax service hall or counters, submitting the Articles of Association, USCC business license, legal representative identification, premises proof, bank account certificate, and appointed financial and accounting personnel information. Step 3: If a non-resident foreign company establishes a Hong Kong branch rather than a domestic Hong Kong company, register the non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and apply for a Business Registration Certificate at the IRD Business Registration Office; appoint a Hong Kong resident authorised representative for service of documents and tax notices. Step 4: Confirm whether the China-side entity requires export registration: a mainland exporter must complete Customs Registration for foreign trade with the PRC Customs, obtain a Customs Registration declarant code, and register the export proceeds collection account with banks under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) goods trade foreign-exchange administration. Step 5: Note that Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax registration, so no Hong Kong VAT ID or threshold filing is required; maintain the Business Registration Certificate renewal and IRD tax return filings as the only recurring tax identity obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Mainland China EJV is a resident enterprise taxed at entity level; the Hong Kong limb, if maintained, is a territorial-source Profits Tax taxpayer with no pass-through or transparent treatment).

Available election forms

None. Neither the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law nor Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Ordinance provides a check-the-box pass-through election or a hybrid mismatch election for a limited liability JV. Hong Kong does not permit consolidated group filing; there is no analogous group relief election; the entity must be taxed as a standalone limited liability taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

PRC VAT applicable to supplies made in Mainland China; Hong Kong SAR has no general VAT, GST, or sales tax regime.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Hong Kong: no VAT/GST registration threshold because no such tax exists; the IRD Business Registration regime applies from commencement of business regardless of turnover. Mainland China: VAT obligations arise when the entity commences taxable sales; general taxpayers use the standard rates (13% for goods, 9% for certain goods or services, and 6% for most services), while small-scale taxpayers may use the 3% simplified collection rate, but no monetary VAT registration threshold is available for an enterprise with a business licence. The specialty tea salon's dine-in beverage service in Mainland China generally falls under life services at 6% or the simplified collection rate, while retail sale of packaged loose-leaf tea is generally a 13% goods supply; in Hong Kong no such tax is charged.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong: because no VAT/GST is imposed, goods exported from Hong Kong are outside any Hong Kong indirect tax; there is no zero-rating claim to file and no export VAT refund mechanism. Mainland China: zero-rating of exported tea products applies only where (i) the goods leave the PRC customs territory, including exports to Hong Kong; (ii) the exporter holds a valid export customs declaration; (iii) the export is not on the prohibited or suspended export list; (iv) the sales invoice records the export sale; (v) a bill of lading or equivalent transport document proves the actual shipment; and (vi) the export proceeds are repatriated to a domestic collection account and matched with the export declaration. If these criteria are not met, the VAT rate reverts to the applicable domestic rate and the exporter must self-assess output VAT. Food-exporting tea salons must also satisfy product quarantine, phytosanitary, pesticide residue, and Hong Kong import hygiene requirements before customs release; failure in food safety documentation delays but does not automatically destroy zero-rating if the actual export is later proven by the statutory bundle.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - in Mainland China, the e-Customs export declaration record; in Hong Kong, the import/export declaration submitted under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations for declarable outward shipments
  • Bill of Lading, airway bill, or equivalent contract of carriage issued by the carrier evidencing goods shipped from the PRC export customs territory to Hong Kong or an overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or, for Mainland China export proceeds, the bank-issued export foreign-exchange receipt document and the SAFE goods-trade monitoring record proving the foreign or RMB export proceeds are collected and repatriated to the exporting entity
  • Export commercial invoice, packing list, and the original VAT invoice or electronic invoice issued for the export sale
  • Certificate of origin and, for tea intended for human consumption, the inspection, quarantine, phytosanitary, and health certificates accepted by PRC Customs and the relevant Hong Kong food import authority, where applicable

Hong Kong: no real-time fiscal clearance, no mandatory continuous transaction controls, no anti-fraud digital stamping, and no mandated electronic invoicing API. IRD merely requires adequate records under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and receipts or invoices may be issued in paper or electronic form without tax authority pre-clearance. Mainland China: taxable invoices must be issued through STA-controlled invoice systems; the fully digitalised electronic VAT invoice platform has replaced traditional paper invoices for qualifying taxpayers, and VAT special invoices are cleared and reported to the STA before buyer authentication; restaurants and retailers generally issue ordinary VAT invoices, including digital e-invoices where adopted, and must retain point-of-sale transaction records. There is no requirement for a further customs digital stamp on domestic VAT invoices, but cross-border export declarations are submitted through the China International Trade Single Window or e-Customs system.

For Mainland China: the STA and PRC Customs jointly audit export declarations against logistics, invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt data; where zero-rating is improperly claimed, the exporter must repay the refund or zero-rated output VAT with late payment surcharge, may be fined up to five times the tax defrauded under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, and may have export tax refund eligibility suspended or revoked; serious VAT export fraud carries criminal liability under Chinese criminal law. For Hong Kong: since there is no export VAT, no clawback of a VAT refund is possible; however the IRD may impose penalties for failure to maintain proper business records, failure to notify chargeability, or false statements in business registration, and the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Double Taxation Arrangement enables exchange of information in fraud examinations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with the RMB convertible on the current account for trade and service payments but subject to strict capital account controls. Current account transactions (import settlement, royalties, service fees) can be executed against authentic documentation such as invoices, contracts, customs declarations and tax vouchers. Capital account inflows and outflows, including FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, repayment of offshore debt, dividends and divestment proceeds, require transactional registration with SAFE, filing with the onshore custodian bank, and compliance with the Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit remittance for the Hong Kong JV shareholder is permissible only after statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, and the bank's verification of the capital account payment under the dividend remittance purpose code; no blanket conversion or repatriation is allowed without an economic substance review. The joint venture must settle funds through a designated designated foreign exchange bank, and conversion should be on a real demand basis. Although the offshore CNH market in Hong Kong creates a natural hedging pool, onshore CNY and offshore CNH remain separate, and arbitrage-driven transfers through onshore corporate accounts are prohibited. Therefore the treasury function must treat convertibility as an operational privilege conditioned on continuous documentation rather than a free automatic right.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts denominated in foreign currency must be repatriated to a qualified domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days of customs export declaration (or, where a confirmed exception is granted by SAFE, within the approved extension window). The exporter may elect to retain receipts in foreign currency for future approved use or convert the full amount into RMB. There is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion percentage in the current regime; however, 100% proceeds must be brought onshore and banked in a designated settlement account. Conversion must be supported by authentic trade documentation and the resulting RMB funds may only be used for lawful operating purposes.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers, including Hong Kong-licensed virtual banks and Singapore/UK EMI platforms, have no direct legal standing to open local operating bank accounts for a mainland China joint venture. They cannot settle VAT, pay statutory social insurance, or issue official tax invoices through their rails, and PBOC does not recognize foreign EMI licences as a substitute for a deposit-taking or payment institution licence. The only feasible use is ancillary: an offshore HK treasury account at a licensed bank for CNH cash pooling, but the onshore entity must maintain all settlement and tax collection flows through a PRC licensed commercial bank. Therefore, the fintech EMI viability rating is Low for onshore treasury operations and the JV should not route trade collections through an unlicensed digital EMI.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10,000,000 per category of controlled transaction where the taxpayer's gross income exceeds HK$20,000,000 – this triggers Local File preparation. A Master File is required when gross income exceeds HK$200,000,000 and any controlled transaction category exceeds HK$50,000,000 (or total assets exceed HK$400,000,000). These thresholds are per Inland Revenue Ordinance Cap.112 s.50AAFJ and Schedule 17F.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction like the BVI or Cayman, but the IRD applies a source-based and substantial-activity doctrine. For a specialty tea salon lounge, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include sourcing raw tea leaves, blending proprietary recipes, operating the physical salon, staffing customer service, managing inventory, and organising cultural ceramic exhibitions. These functions must actually be performed in Hong Kong to support on-shore assessment. If the Mainland parent performs procurement or product development, a documented cost-plus service agreement must reflect the true functional profile. A Hong Kong company with no staff, no premises, and no management will be treated as an empty conduit; the IRD will recharacterise the arrangement and impose full tax with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – suppression of cash and POS sales

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112) s.82(1)(a)(i), a person who knowingly and wilfully provides false information with an intent to evade tax is liable to a fine of HK$50,000 and treble the amount of tax undercharged, plus imprisonment for up to 3 years. Under s.80(2), failure without reasonable excuse to file the BIR51 incurs a HK$10,000 fine and a daily fine of HK$200.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as 'officers' and may be criminally prosecuted as principals in the evasion. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the company is wholly controlled by the director and the evasion is performed through the company. Personal civil liability includes restitution of the underpaid tax and interest, potentially unlimited liability for company debts if s.906 of the Companies Ordinance (fraudulent trading) applies. A travel ban (departure prevention direction) can be imposed by the IRD until the tax debt is settled, and imprisonment of up to 14 years may follow if money-laundering aspects are proven.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance – no contemporaneous documentation

Statutory sanction: Although the IRD does not impose a standalone penalty for documentation failure, a notice to produce records under s.50AAFO Cap.112 may be issued; failure to comply attracts a HK$10,000 fine and a further daily fine. In practice, the IRD will make a transfer pricing adjustment and charge the resulting understated tax plus penalties up to 100% of the additional tax under s.82A, imposing a substantial civil penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly fail to commission and sign transfer pricing declarations expose themselves to joint and several liability for the company's additional tax and penalties if the corporate structure is used as a mere conduit for profit shifting. In egregious cases, directors may be disqualified from managing companies, blacklisted from leaving Hong Kong, and may face civil breach-of-fiduciary-duty claims by minority JV shareholders. Criminal liability under the Theft Ordinance may attach where the director intentionally misrepresents related-party information to the IRD.
Shell Company / Artificial Profit Shifting – no economic substance in Hong Kong

Statutory sanction: The IRD may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rules in s.61A of Cap.112 to disregard the scheme and assess tax as if the substance had been located in Hong Kong. False offshore profit claims attract penalties under Cap.112 s.82(1): up to HK$50,000 plus treble tax and imprisonment for up to 3 years. Where the arrangement involves repeated cross-border payments, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance and Drug Trafficking and Organized Crime Ordinance may also be engaged.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under s.906 Companies Ordinance (fraudulent trading): a director may be made personally liable for all of the company's debts without limit. The court may impose a disqualification order prohibiting management of any company, a travel restriction/blacklisting by the IRD, and civil asset restitution to the Government and minority shareholders. Criminal imprisonment of up to 15 years can be imposed if the scheme is deemed fraud or money-laundering, and proceeds may be confiscated.
