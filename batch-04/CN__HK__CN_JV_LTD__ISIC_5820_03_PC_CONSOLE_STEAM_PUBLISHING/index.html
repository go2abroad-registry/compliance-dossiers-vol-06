Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A pure virtual office or commercial mail-forwarding address will generally be rejected by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and AML-compliant banks for a Sino-Foreign JV digital game publishing entity. The Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 requires a Hong Kong registered office for service of process, but a non-trade company may use a serviced office. However, Steam publisher agreements and PlayStation partner agreements require a physically verifiable business presence, auditable records, and operational substance. The IRD will likely deny territorial-source profits if no staff, lease, financial records, domain/warehouse infrastructure, or board minutes are present in Hong Kong; banks will close accounts placed under virtual offices where no named lease, live management, or employee check-ins can be demonstrated. Therefore, a co-working space with a dedicated lockable suite, named on the lease, and a locally employed manager is the minimum acceptable standard for this industry.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the National Electronic Tax Bureau e-tax portal (chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

Upon issuance of the Business License by the local Administration for Market Regulation, the CN_JV_LTD is automatically registered for tax purposes under the 'one-licence, one-code' system. The STA assigns an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) which serves as the TIN for all tax filings, including VAT and Corporate Income Tax. No separate application is required for the JV itself. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (e.g., the Hong Kong corporate shareholder) that derives China-source income without a permanent establishment, the non-resident must file a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration with the competent tax authority where the withholding agent is located, submitting: (a) a copy of the foreign incorporation certificate; (b) a tax residence certificate issued by the jurisdiction of residence; (c) a board resolution appointing a Chinese tax representative; and (d) the completed registration application. The STA will then issue a TIN in the format of a foreign taxpayer code. If the foreign investor establishes a branch or permanent establishment in China, separate tax registration is required at the local tax authority.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border digital publishing services (including downloadable PC/console video games and software licenses distributed via Steam and PlayStation Store) qualify for the zero rate under the VAT reform circular (Finance and Taxation [2016] No. 36) and subsequent VAT reforms when the services are rendered to recipients outside China, the relevant intellectual property is held by the Chinese entity, and the services are consumed entirely outside the PRC. The zero rate also applies to ancillary software maintenance and hosting services provided to non-resident platform operators, provided the recipient is not registered in China and the benefit is not enjoyed within China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (only for physical goods exports; digital services do not require customs clearance)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (for physical consignments)
  • Overseas Digital Distribution Agreement executed with Valve Corporation (Steam) and Sony Interactive Entertainment (PlayStation Store)
  • Commercial Invoice issued to the non-resident platform or overseas end customer
  • Foreign Exchange Settlement Slip (bank receipt) demonstrating receipt of funds in a foreign currency or from an offshore account within the statutory repatriation period
  • Non-Resident Tax Residence Certificate or equivalent corporate documentary evidence of the recipient's offshore jurisdiction
  • Software Copyright Registration Certificate (if applicable) evidencing the Chinese entity's intellectual property ownership

Under the Golden Tax Phase IV system, all VAT invoices must be issued electronically via the STA's New Electronic Invoice (e-invoice) service. The issuer must apply for the e-invoice quota through the National Electronic Tax Bureau and transmit invoice data in real time using the Tax U-Key control device. Each invoice is authenticated with a unique 20-digit invoice code and encrypted tax control code. The system validates invoice issuance and reports to the STA automatically at the time of creation; no post-issuance clearance is required.

If the STA determines that zero-rating was claimed without the required documentary substantiation, or that services were in fact consumed in China, it will claw back the zero-rated output VAT and compel the taxpayer to output VAT at the applicable rate, plus late-payment interest. Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, fraudulent export refund or zero-rating claims may be punished with a fine of 50% up to five times the amount of tax evaded, and criminal liability for tax fraud can be pursued in severe cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong and mainland China operate as separate currency and FX-monetary regimes for this purpose. Hong Kong has no foreign exchange controls: the Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible, the offshore renminbi (CNH) market is unrestricted, and both current-account and capital-account transactions may be executed through licensed banks without SAFE-type approval. The HKMA maintains the Linked Exchange Rate System with a USD/HKD band of 7.75 to 7.85. Export proceeds, cross-border payments, royalties, and dividends may be freely repatriated and no surrender requirement exists. By contrast, the mainland China leg of the JV remains subject to PRC controls: current-account payments require genuine trade documents, while capital-account outflows such as the PRC shareholder's equity injection require MOFCOM/SAFE registration and clearing through a designated PRC bank. This asymmetry means the HK JV can rely on fast HKD/USD/CNH conversion for treasury operations, but the original PRC equity contribution must be deliberately routed through SAFE-approved ODI filings or the bank compliance team will freeze the inflow.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Hong Kong JV is not subject to any export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirement under HK law. If the JV ultimately receives proceeds through a PRC subsidiary, then SAFE current-account monitoring requires export earnings to be repatriated into China and either retained in a foreign exchange settlement account or converted into RMB at banks upon verification of the underlying trade documents; there is no fixed statutory surrender ratio and no mandatory conversion percentage.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the binding authorities for all mainland China cross-border RMB/foreign exchange capital account flows affecting the PRC joint-venture shareholder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low-to-Medium as a primary treasury rail. Hong Kong does not issue a distinct EMI banking licence; non-bank fintech payment firms generally hold a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance or a Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, neither of which authorizes full deposit-taking, capital-escrow custody, or commercial lending. A licensed Hong Kong virtual bank is a bank under HKMA supervision and can provide routine collections, but its credit capacity and onboarding limits are not suitable for holding the JV's capital injection and primary liquidity. For the mainland China leg of the equity injection, the PRC shareholder must remit through a PBOC/SAFE-designated commercial bank; fintech EMIs are legally unqualified to service that capital-account flow. Therefore the JV should use a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank as the principal treasury and may use an HKMA-licensed fintech payment service only as a secondary operating rail after full compliance due diligence.

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong operates a mandatory statutory audit regime with no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount de minimis exemption. Every active non-dormant company under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 must engage a certified public accountant (practising) to audit its financial statements. The legal trigger is the existence of any accounting transaction: a company with any turnover, any assets/liabilities movement, or any employees is required to have an independent certified CPA audit. The only statutory exemption is the dormant company exemption in section 5 of Cap. 622, where the company has had zero accounting transactions during the period and passes a dormant resolution; the exemption ceases immediately upon receipt of Steam/PlayStation store sales proceeds or any other business income. A Sino-Foreign JV limited company in Hong Kong therefore crosses the mandatory audit threshold at the first commercial transaction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$10 million of controlled transactions per category by a Hong Kong entity triggers Local File preparation; Master File required if group turnover exceeds HK$600 million and related-party transactions exceed HK$20 million; DIPN 60 compliance requires contemporaneous documentation.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no BVI/Cayman-type ESR statutory code, but the IRD applies the territorial source principle and the OECD alignment. For the Steam publishing JV, core income-generating activities (CIGA) (including contract negotiation, risk-taking, strategic marketing, and game supply decisions) must be physically performed in Hong Kong. A paper office with payments routed through HK is insufficient; IRD will deny an offshore claim, impose 16.5% profits tax, and add penalties. For China-related IP, the China-HK DTA may increase substance scrutiny, requiring a PRC tax residence certificate and proof of management and control.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82A imposes a fine of HK$200,000 and imprisonment for 3 years on conviction, plus an additional assessment of 300% of the tax evaded; IRO s.82(1) also penalises knowingly making false returns or statements with a fine and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Courts can pierce the veil where the company is a facade or sham; directors are personally liable for the civil tax loss, equitable compensation, and trust-anatomical restitution; the High Court may issue a Mareva freezing order and prohibit departure from Hong Kong pending trial; for fraudulent tax evasion, corporate officers face up to 14 years' imprisonment for theft/conspiracy to defraud.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Under IRO Part 9A and s.51C, failure to maintain adequate transfer pricing documentation is punishable by a fine of HK$10,000; if tax has been undercharged, the IRD may impose additional tax up to HK$20,000 plus 100% of the undercharge; fraud on TP data invites the higher s.82A penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign incorrect transfer pricing computations or destroy documents face personal fines and disqualification under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.168; personal liability in civil proceedings for the tax underpaid can arise from breach of fiduciary duty; the Chinese parent group may also be exposed to joint liability under anti-conduit principles.
Shell Company with False Economic Substance (AML / DARR)

Statutory sanction: The IRD may deny foreign-sourced income treatment, assess 16.5% profits tax on the full gross profit, add a 300% penalty for deliberate understatement; under Anti-Money Laundering Ordinance (Cap. 615), the JV may be reported to the Customs and Excise Department and the company struck off under Companies Ordinance s.112; for defrauding mainland tax authorities, Chinese criminal law and PRC tax administration sanctions may be triggered.

Director personal liability: Piercing veil results in personal liability for the unpaid tax and related penalties; directors may be blacklisted by the IRD and face a travel ban and asset preservation order; a Hong Kong court can order disqualification for up to 15 years; in mainland China, directors of a Hong Kong entity who orchestrate the tax loss are personally liable, and tax evasion crosses into PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 with up to 7 years' imprisonment plus unlimited fines.
