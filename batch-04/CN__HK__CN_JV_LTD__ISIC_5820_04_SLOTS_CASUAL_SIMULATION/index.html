Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: IRD eTAX (www.etax.gov.hk) for tax filings and GovHK (www.gov.hk) for Business Registration e-Services and government transactions.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate TIN or VAT ID. The statutory tax reference for an entity is the Business Registration Number (BRN) shown on the Business Registration Certificate issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). For a Hong Kong-incorporated company, incorporation under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) automatically triggers registration with the IRD Business Registration Office through the Companies Registry integrated process, and the IRD issues the BRC with the BRN plus a Profits Tax file number for the entity. A foreign non-resident shareholder does not need to obtain a Hong Kong tax ID merely to hold equity in a Hong Kong entity. If a foreign non-resident enterprise itself carries on business in Hong Kong or derives Hong-Kong-source income, it must apply for Business Registration using the statutory prescribed application under Cap. 310 through GovHK or the IRD eTAX portal before commencing business, or within one month after commencement, and must appoint an authorised tax representative for service of documents. The BRC must be displayed at the registered place of business and renewed annually by payment of the prescribed business registration fee and levy.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation / Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not offer a check-the-box election, US-style entity classification election, or hybrid mismatch election; entity classification follows the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and taxing rights follow the territorial-source principle of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None (no VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong SAR; only excise duty on specified commodities under the Excise Duty Ordinance (Cap. 109) and no general consumption tax)

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. No VAT/GST registration threshold exists in Hong Kong, and there is no HKD turnover threshold for indirect tax registration. Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is mandatory from the commencement of any business in Hong Kong, irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory export VAT zero-rating is not applicable because Hong Kong does not levy VAT/GST, so there is no output VAT to zero-rate and no VAT export refund mechanism. The relevant relief for cross-border digital software publishing is the territorial-source profits tax rule: profits derived from offshore sales are not chargeable to Hong Kong Profits Tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). For the simulated social casino and virtual slots software operation sold through Apple IAP and Google Play Billing, the entity must demonstrate that the digital services are negotiated, concluded and economically supplied from outside Hong Kong, that the app-store settlement and user-contract activities occur through the foreign platform channels, and that no profit-generating trade is carried on in Hong Kong. For physical goods exports, no VAT zero-rating certificate is required because Hong Kong has no VAT; export customs declarations remain necessary for trade statistics and restricted-goods control.

  • Electronic Export Declaration lodged with Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations made under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) via the Trade and Industry Department EDI/Tradelink channel; Hong Kong does not use a Bill of Entry for exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement (applicable only where goods are exported; digital app-store software supply will normally involve no physical shipment)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order and signed sale contract evidencing the overseas customer, destination, and transfer of title and risk
  • Contemporaneous offshore-profit file including negotiation correspondence, app-store settlement statements, Apple IAP and Google Play Billing platform reports, user-location analytics, and service-performance evidence
  • Bank inward remittance credit advice and foreign-currency collection records (the Hong Kong equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate; FIRC is not issued by Hong Kong banks)

Not applicable. Hong Kong does not impose real-time e-invoicing clearance, continuous transaction controls, anti-fraud digital stamping, or a statutory fiscal clearance API regime. Taxpayers must retain ordinary invoices, receipts, contracts, bank statements and underlying business records sufficient for Profits Tax assessment under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); e-invoicing is voluntary and not government-cleared.

Although there is no VAT export zero-rating to claw back, the IRD may reopen Profits Tax assessments within the statutory six-year assessment period, extended to ten years for fraud or wilful evasion, and may disallow an unsupported offshore claim, assess the profits as Hong-Kong-source, and impose interest plus additional tax. Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), incorrect returns, failure to disclose assessable profits, and tax evasion may attract monetary penalties, additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged, and imprisonment on prosecution. False or unverifiable export declarations to Hong Kong Customs may be prosecuted under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60), with fines and imprisonment, and the court may order forfeiture or repayment of any improperly avoided duty, levy, or fee. Because no VAT refund is granted, there is no separate VAT clawback mechanism in Hong Kong.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates with a fully convertible HKD under the Linked Exchange Rate System administered by the HKMA. The capital account is open: there are no controls on inbound or outbound exchange conversion, profit remittance, dividends, or repatriation of capital for a Hong Kong incorporated entity. Current account transactions are equally unrestricted. The operative friction arises on the mainland China side: the PRC parent remains subject to SAFE exchange controls. Renminbi is fully convertible for current account trade payments with genuine documentation, but capital account flows require pre-registration, including overseas direct investment filing by NDRC/MOFCOM, SAFE registration, and an underlying economic purpose. Profit remittance from any mainland operating entity to the Hong Kong JV generally requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax completion or clearance, foreign exchange registration for profit distribution, and a board resolution; dividend withholding tax may apply under the China-HK double tax arrangement. There is no statutory surrender of foreign currency for the Hong Kong JV. The dominant treasury risk is not HKD convertibility but the compliance pipeline through SAFE-regulated banks and the need to maintain separate capital escrow and operational accounts for the gaming/software publishing business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR: no surrender requirement. Export receipts may be held indefinitely in USD, HKD, CNY, or any convertible currency without mandatory conversion or a statutory repatriation timeline. For any PRC-parent export proceeds routed to or on behalf of the JV, SAFE requires the mainland exporting entity to repatriate the proceeds through a designated bank and to match the receipt against customs declaration data in the trade foreign exchange monitoring system; deferred receipts require registration if collection exceeds 90 days. There is currently no fixed mandatory conversion ratio, but banks apply a risk-based matching ratio and will refuse settlement for unexplained over-receipts or under-receipts. All non-trade proceeds require a supporting contract, invoice, and appropriate cross-border purpose code.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), in coordination with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China for cross-border capital flows involving the PRC parent

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low as a primary banking channel; Medium only for small ancillary disbursements if the EMI holds an HK Money Service Operator licence under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) and does not act as a deposit-taking institution. Legal constraints: deposit-taking in Hong Kong requires authorization by HKMA under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155); offshore fintech EMIs such as neo-banking wallets and quasi-banks are not authorized deposit-takers and cannot legally hold client operating funds as a bank. Furthermore, Tier-1 banks will not accept an offshore EMI as the sole treasury bank because the social-casino gaming profile requires transparent, auditable clearing rails and FATF-compliant KYC. Accordingly, this blueprint prohibits offshore EMI accounts for capital escrow, trade settlement, and treasury liquidity; any EMI ancillary account must be funded only after a Tier-1 licensed bank account is live.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The remitting mainland bank wires equity capital or shareholder loan by SWIFT MT103 using the SAFE-designated cross-border FX purpose code for overseas direct investment equity, or the SAFE foreign debt purpose code if the injection is structured as a shareholder loan. The beneficiary account at the Hong Kong Tier-1 bank must bear the exact entity legal name, HK company number, and business registration certificate; the swift reference must carry the board resolution number and purpose descriptor. Upon receipt, the HK bank verifies the credit against the KYC and source-of-funds profile. For equity contributions, the HK JV files the share allotment return (NS1) with the Companies Registry and engages a Hong Kong certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 1,000,000 for controlled transactions involving goods, property or services; HKD 5,000,000 for controlled financial transactions and intangible property transactions; contemporaneous Local File documentation becomes mandatory when these thresholds are exceeded; Master File and Country-by-Country Report obligations apply to large multinational groups with consolidated annual revenue of not less than HKD 680,000,000 or EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have the standalone Economic Substance Regulations used in some offshore centres, but the Inland Revenue Department applies the General Anti-Abuse Rule under section 61A and the Principal Purpose Test under the Double Taxation Arrangement to deny treaty benefits to shell or conduit Hong Kong JVs. For the social casino digital publishing scope, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) must actually be performed by the Hong Kong entity: negotiating software and game content licenses, directing user acquisition, controlling Apple and Google payment rails, approving virtual item pricing, managing risk and making strategic decisions. A Hong Kong JV that merely invoices royalties or holds payment settlement rights while the parent performs all decision-making in Mainland China will be treated as having failed the economic substance test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Return

Statutory sanction: Under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), a person who wilfully and with intent to evade tax makes a false return or fails to disclose income is liable to a fine of HK$50,000 and three times the amount of tax undercharged; where fraud or wilful evasion is proven, section 82 raises the maximum fine to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to three years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign a false profits tax return or procure the JV to conceal revenue are jointly and severally liable for the additional tax, penalties and costs; the court may pierce the corporate veil if the JV is a mere facade, and the IRD may apply for a departure prohibition order under section 76 to place a travel ban on the director until tax is settled; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment and disqualification as a director under the Companies Ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The IRD may adjust the taxable profits to an arm's length result under section 50A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance and impose additional assessment within the statutory period; failure to keep proper transfer pricing records can trigger penalties under section 80, and inaccurate returns that understate profit carry a penalty of three times the tax undercharged under section 82A.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve sham intercompany agreements or intentionally use non-arm's length pricing to strip Hong Kong profits may be personally liable for restitution of diverted amounts; the corporate veil is pierced where the JV is a puppet for the parent or the directors have enriched themselves through the artificial pricing; unpaid taxes can lead to asset seizure, travel ban and blacklisting, and fraudulent shifting of profits may support imprisonment of up to three years for criminal tax evasion.
AML and Payment-Rail Fraud / Shell Company Abuses

Statutory sanction: Violations are actionable under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455), which provides a maximum fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years for money laundering; the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes civil and criminal sanctions on unlicensed money service operators and failing institutions; false trade declarations may also attract customs penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who direct, assist in or knowingly benefit from laundering the JV's payment flows are personally prosecutable; the court may order restitution, asset freezing and forfeiture, issue travel bans and blacklisting, disqualify the directors, and imprison them for up to 14 years; the corporate veil will be removed where the JV is used as an instrument of the directors' or the parent's fraud.
