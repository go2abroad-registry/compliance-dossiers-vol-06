Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region - the operative tax authority for jurisdiction HK. Electronic portal: GovHK (https://www.gov.hk) and IRD e-Tax (https://www.ird.gov.hk). Hong Kong is a separate tax and customs jurisdiction within the People's Republic of China under the one-country-two-systems principle.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity operating through the Hong Kong SAR: (1) If the entity is to be incorporated in Hong Kong, apply to the Companies Registry using the prescribed incorporation form (Form NNC1 for a private company limited by shares) under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation. (2) If the entity is already incorporated outside Hong Kong and will carry on business in Hong Kong, register it as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) using Form N1 and obtain a Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company. (3) Within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong, apply to the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using Form IRBR 1 for a Business Registration Certificate. (4) The Business Registration Number is the entity's Hong Kong tax identification number for profits tax and payroll-related tax purposes; no separate VAT/GST ID exists because Hong Kong has no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general sales tax. (5) A foreign entity with no physical presence in Hong Kong but that still carries on a Hong Kong-source revenue trade or derives Hong Kong-source profits must nonetheless obtain business registration and file Profits Tax Returns when issued by the IRD. (6) Foreign non-resident persons may be assigned their own tax reference number by the IRD upon first filing or assessment, but for corporate entities the Business Registration Number is the operative TIN.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Control Regulations and SAFE rules, the onshore Sino-Foreign Equity JV is subject to strict capital account controls. Current account items, including service fees, tournament platform subscriptions, prize disbursement fees and trade in services, are convertible once the bank has performed an underlying-transaction authenticity review. Capital account items such as equity contributions from the Hong Kong shareholder, shareholder loan principal repayment, and profit remittance to Hong Kong require FDI registration, SAFE filing, and a licensed commercial bank to monitor the use of converted RMB. RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account; the inbound HKD/USD equity injection must enter the designated FDI capital foreign exchange settlement account and be converted strictly for approved operating purposes. Profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificates, bank capital verification reports, and SAFE profit remittance declarations. Export earnings are no longer subject to a mandatory surrender percentage; an A-class enterprise may retain 100% of export foreign currency in a current-account settlement account until it needs RMB. Hong Kong, by contrast, has no exchange controls and permits unrestricted convertibility, making it an efficient offshore CNH treasury hub.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a mainland A-class export entity under SAFE trade monitoring, export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated into the domestic current-account foreign exchange settlement account within 180 calendar days from the export customs declaration date or the commercial payment maturity date, whichever is earlier, and must be matched to the electronic customs declaration in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; the JV may leave 100% of proceeds in foreign currency, but any conversion to RMB requires presentation of the customs declaration, commercial invoice, and bank receipt. B-class and C-class enterprises face immediate settlement requirements and transaction-by-transaction document review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for mainland China and Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability rating for onshore mandate: 0/10. Offshore digital EMI providers are not licensed by the PBOC or the National Financial Regulatory Administration as Chinese settlement banks, are not connected to CNAPS clearing for RMB, and cannot lawfully open an FDI capital settlement account or basic deposit account for a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity JV. An offshore EMI may serve only the Hong Kong treasury side if it is licensed by the HKMA as a stored value facility or money service operator, but it must not be used as the primary corporate banking rail for inbound capital, FX conversion, or statutory capital verification. All onshore regulatory duties must be discharged through a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before any remittance, the JV must complete SAFE FDI registration at the local SAFE branch or through the bank under the FDI information system and receive the Foreign Exchange Registration number. The Hong Kong shareholder then executes a SWIFT wire transfer in HKD or USD to the JV's designated FDI capital foreign exchange settlement account at the selected domestic Tier-1 bank, using SAFE declaration purpose code 622011 for foreign equity injection into a newly established foreign-invested enterprise. The domestic bank verifies the remittance against the FDI registration, credits the capital account, and converts the funds into RMB only for the approved operating payment schedule. Within 30 days after settlement, a Chinese registered CPA must issue the capital verification report, and the JV must file it with the corporate registry and SAFE. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Every Hong Kong company must prepare audited financial statements unless it obtains a valid exemption. For private companies, the audit exemption in Cap. 622 section 359 applies only if the company qualifies as a 'small private company' or is a member of a 'small private company group' by satisfying at least two of three financial year conditions: total revenue not exceeding HKD 25 million; total assets not exceeding HKD 25 million; and employees not exceeding 50. The exemption also requires unanimous consent of all shareholders or member groups and is lost if any member with 5% or more of the voting rights objects. A dormant private company may also avoid audit under the dormant company regime if it had no significant accounting transactions. An independent statutory audit by a Certified Public Accountant (Practising) is mandatory for any company that is not small, is part of a non-small group, has a shareholder requiring an audit, is a regulated business such as a bank or licensed financial institution, or whose financial statements are intended to support third-party reliance by lenders, investors, or cross-border payment partners.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong IRD's administrative guidance suggests contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation must be kept where the aggregate value of specified cross-border related party transactions exceeds HKD 100 million. In mainland China, Master File and Local File thresholds are generally CNY 200 million (approx. HKD 220 million) for related party transactions; however, the landscape: keep documentation if intercompany transaction exceeds HKD 100 million in HK, or if the mainland parent is subject to Chinese TP filing thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply generic Economic Substance Regulations (as in UAE), but applies a territorial source doctrine. The IRD examines whether the core income-generating activities occur in Hong Kong. The China-HK DTA includes a Principal Purpose Test and limitation-on-benefits type protection, so the HK entity must demonstrate actual business premises, employees, and decision-making substance in HK, especially for e-sports platform revenue sharing and intercompany licensing.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Abusive Transfer Pricing / Offshore Profit Strip

Statutory sanction: China SAT Special Tax Adjustment and tax fraud rules: VAT/CIT leakage charged; additional corporate income tax of up to 10% of underpaid tax plus interest at benchmark lending rate + 5 percentage points. The SAT can challenge under PRC Law on Tax Administration and Article 122 of SAT Bulletin No. 6 (2023). HK side: if a tax return states offshore source falsely, the IRD can apply section 82 IRO penalties up to 200% tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors can face personal assessment as shadow directors under section 74 IRO, court-imposed liability for fraudulent trading under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622, and personal criminal fines/imprisonment for tax evasion. In mainland China, responsible persons can be subjected to 'exit denial' (出境不准) and criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law if the tax evasion amount exceeds statutory threshold.
Failing Treaty Substance Requirements / Shell Company

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA benefits; China imposes 10% statutory WHT plus interest, late fee and penalties. SAT may deem the HK entity a shell and impose enterprise income tax on the mainland payer. The HK IRD may refuse to issue a TRC or may revoke treaty benefits if the entity lacks economic substance.

Director personal liability: Directors may be disqualified under Cap. 622 for insolvency mismanagement; if the shell company is used to defeat creditors, personal fraudulent trading liability applies. The courts can pierce the corporate veil where the entity is a sham, and directors may be subject to a travel ban and restrictions on leaving Hong Kong and mainland China under cross-border criminal cooperation.
Misclassification of Income and Failure to Withhold

Statutory sanction: China: withholding agent failing to withhold is fined 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount. If a payment is reclassified from dividend to royalty/interest, the taxpayer must self-correct with a retroactive adjustment. In HK, the recipient may also be taxable on the service income if sourced in HK; otherwise penalties under the AEOI CRS rules may apply.

Director personal liability: The directors of both the mainland payer and HK recipient may be personally jointly liable for the withholding tax shortfall (in China) and subject to export-restriction orders. Under the PRC criminal regime, tax evasion by misleading treaty claims can lead to personal indictment for tax evasion under Criminal Law, with imprisonment up to 7 years in serious cases. HK directors face liability for signing false profits tax returns, leading to imprisonment and personal asset forfeiture.
