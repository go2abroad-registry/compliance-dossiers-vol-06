Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland China leg of this structure operates a hybrid convertibility regime: current-account receipts and payments are generally convertible through designated foreign exchange banks, while capital-account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, quota, and post-event verification. A Hong Kong SAR treasury vehicle is fully convertible under the linked exchange rate system, with no surrender requirements and unrestricted capital flows. For the onshore JV, foreign-currency equity deposits into the capital escrow account are not freely convertible for purposes outside the approved business scope; conversion to RMB for working capital requires a bank declaration and must match the FDI registration. Profit remittances to Hong Kong shareholders are permitted after annual audit, tax clearance, and presentation of the foreign-invested enterprise distribution resolution, but are treated as capital-account outflows and undergo a genuine-transaction check. The practical effect is that the JV cannot pool or co-mingle liquidity with its Hong Kong affiliate without SAFE filings; each leg requires separate treasury governance and separate bank account infrastructure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE trade receipts monitoring regime, export proceeds from the global distribution of micro-drama content are captured when the platform remits revenue to the JV's designated foreign exchange bank. The statutory monitoring timeline requires the JV to repatriate and settle/reconcile export proceeds within 90 days following the export declaration; in practice, banks monitor unusual delays through the export invoice verification system. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the JV may elect to retain the foreign currency in its settlement account or convert it to RMB at spot, provided the source contract and platform payout schedule are furnished. Any deviation from the 90-day window or repeated non-conversion balancing requires a written explanation and supporting documents acceptable to SAFE, and is subject to on-site inspection.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) exercising supervisory and clearing authority for Hong Kong SAR banking and foreign exchange matters.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable for the mainland JV's onshore corporate account because SAFE requires all cross-border receipts and payments to move through designated foreign exchange banks; no EMI has a licensed domestic settlement account for capital-account flows. For the Hong Kong treasury leg, an HKMA-authorised EMI or licensed digital bank is feasible for collecting Apple/Google/Stripe settlements in USD/HKD and for initiating payment runs, provided the EMI is not the sole treasury counterpart and is backed by a tier-1 bank account. Overall rating: not viable for China onshore; medium viability for Hong Kong treasury with a tier-1 bank backstop.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10 million for controlled property transactions (goods, services, or royalties) triggering a mandatory Local File; HKD 100 million for controlled financial transactions (loans, guarantees, cash pools) triggering a Local File; a Master File is required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue reaches HKD 2 billion and the HK JV's controlled transactions exceed HKD 100 million; Country-by-Country reporting begins at HKD 6.8 billion in group revenue

Economic substance scope: The Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime effective 1 January 2023 requires a HK member of an MNE group claiming exemption for passive income to demonstrate economic substance in Hong Kong: the performance of core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as strategic asset management, risk assumption, and the DEMPE (Development, Enhancement, Maintenance, Protection, and Exploitation) of the micro-drama IP, with full-time employees and adequate local premises. The Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61 and 61A also empower the IRD to disregard any tax-avoidance scheme lacking commercial substance, and a HK shell company may be re-located to Mainland China for taxation if management and control is found to sit outside Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Offshore Profit Declarations

Statutory sanction: IRO sections 80, 82 and 82A: on conviction for willful evasion, a fine of HKD 50,000 per charge and imprisonment for up to three years; the IRD may also raise the assessment to include additional tax of up to 300% of the undercharged tax and a late-payment surcharge of 5% to 10%. The false offshore claim is voidable under section 61A and the entire profit adjustment becomes immediately due.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under the fraudulent sham principle and section 77 of the IRO, which renders every principal officer, director, and manager personal liability for a corporate tax offense. Directors who knowingly authorize a false declaration may be personally assessed for the company's unpaid tax, subjected to departure prohibition orders under section 77I (blacklisting), have their assets frozen by court order, and be sentenced to three years of imprisonment; the court may also disqualify the director for up to 13 years under the Companies Ordinance.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Round-Tripping of Royalty Payments

Statutory sanction: The IRD applies the arm's-length principle under section 50AAF IRO and makes a transfer pricing adjustment to restore taxable profits; penalties under section 82A (failure to keep contemporaneous documentation) of 10% to 100% of the primary tax displacement, and if the arrangement is shown to be fictitious, a further 200% additional tax. The sham arrangement will be disregarded under section 61A, and the interest/royalty deduction will be denied under section 61(1).

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the BIR51 or authorize the intercompany payments are jointly and severally liable for the corporate deficiency under section 77 IRO. The serious fraud panel can institute civil restitution proceedings for the diverted amounts, secure asset freezes, and impose a travel ban/blacklisting; criminal prosecution for falsifying records may lead to imprisonment up to seven years under the Theft Ordinance section 24 and a fine of HKD 500,000.

Statutory sanction: The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes on the corporation a fine of HKD 5 million and a term of imprisonment for naturally responsible officers; for a conviction on a proceeding involving a money-service operator, the penalty can reach HKD 10 million; the IRD separately charges failure to keep records under section 51B IRO with a fine of HKD 100,000 and a further daily penalty.

Director personal liability: A director who acted negligently or with consent is personally liable to a fine of up to HKD 1 million and imprisonment up to 14 years under AMLO section 7; the corporate veil is pierced for equity-restitution where the JV is used as a conduit for tax evaded on the dividends, the director's property may be traced for repayment of underpaid tax, and a travel ban (blacklisting) will be issued while the investigation is pending. Directors may also face asset forfeiture and a 14-year criminal record for a single offense.
