Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal is the National Electronic Tax Administration portal (e-Tax Bureau), which is the mandatory system for VAT registration, electronic invoice issuance, tax return filing, export tax refund applications, and entity tax-status administration. For Hong Kong SAR situs activities, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong is a separate tax authority; however, the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is registered as a PRC mainland entity with the STA.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-funded Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated under PRC law, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation. The procurement procedure is as follows: (1) complete SAMR registration and obtain the business license carrying the Unified Social Credit Code; (2) within 30 days of incorporation, complete taxpayer registration with the local STA office through the National Electronic Tax Administration portal using the Unified Social Credit Code, legal representative passport/ID, articles of association, board resolutions, registered address, capital contribution schedule, and anticipated business scope under ISIC 5920; (3) obtain e-Tax login credentials and tax-control CA certificate; (4) open a designated foreign exchange RMB/FX bank settlement account and file the account details with the STA; (5) determine VAT payer status: if annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, file the general VAT taxpayer registration through the e-Tax portal; otherwise remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, with the option to elect general taxpayer status to claim input VAT credits; (6) register withholding obligations for payroll, social insurance, stamp duty, and non-resident dividends/royalties; (7) file foreign-exchange registration with SAFE through the bank for export proceeds repatriation; (8) apply for export-tax-rebate/zero-rating qualification with the STA's export tax rebate office where cross-border goods or services will be supplied. Foreign non-resident shareholders must provide notarized, legalized corporate documents and identity proof of the legal representative and financial officer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC business entities must register for VAT at incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period; below that threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, though voluntary general taxpayer registration is permitted. Domestic digital/royalty/licensing services, including online stock-music subscription and commercial soundscape licensing, are generally subject to the modern-services VAT rate band of 6%; physical audio-media goods are subject to the 13% standard VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT rules, exported physical goods are zero-rated when sold to an overseas buyer, physically shipped out of Chinese customs territory under a valid customs export declaration, supported by commercial export documentation, and paid for in foreign exchange repatriated to the PRC. For cross-border services and digital supplies, including royalty-free stock music licensing, audio sample subscriptions, and commercial soundscape licenses delivered electronically to non-resident customers, VAT zero-rating or exemption with input VAT refund is available where the services are received entirely outside China, consumed outside China, supplied to an overseas non-resident customer, supported by an international service contract, and settled through designated foreign-exchange channels with the overseas payer evidencing payment remittance from abroad. The exporter must demonstrate that the payor is not a PRC resident/procurement agent and that the digital product is not used in China. Eligibility requires complete concordance between the tax invoice, service agreement, overseas customer identity evidence (contract, IP address, beneficial-owner certificate), customs electronic records where goods are involved, and bank receipt.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Bill of Entry) filed through the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by PRC Customs
  • Ocean Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Multimodal Transport Document evidencing physical departure of goods from a PRC customs port to a foreign destination
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List on entity letterhead with Incoterms, quantity, valuation, and overseas consignee details
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order with the overseas buyer and payment terms evidencing the offshore commercial relationship
  • Designated Foreign Exchange Bank's Export Proceeds Repatriation/Settlement Voucher matching the customs declaration amount and proving collection and inward remittance from the overseas buyer
  • STA export-tax-rebate settlement application record reconciling electronic invoice data, customs declaration data, foreign-exchange receipt data, and the zero-rating claim

China operates a real-time fiscal-invoice clearance mandate under the Golden Tax Phase IV framework. VAT invoices, including fully digitalized e-invoices, must be issued exclusively through the STA e-Tax Bureau platform using the entity's Unified Social Credit Code. Invoice metadata, invoice numbers, amounts, and tax rates are transmitted to the STA in near real time, and each fully digitalized electronic invoice carries a statutory encrypted anti-fraud invoice number. No private third-party invoice stamping is permitted. Export transactions must be declared through the China International Trade Single Window for customs clearance, and the export declaration data must reconcile with electronic invoices and bank foreign-exchange receipt vouchers in the STA export-tax-rebate system.

Export zero-rating is subject to cross-agency audit scrutiny among STA, PRC Customs, and SAFE through data matching of customs declarations, electronic invoices, VAT returns, and bank foreign-exchange receipt vouchers. Any false declaration, forged transport document, unverified inward remittance, or failure to satisfy the FX repatriation deadline will trigger retrospective clawback of all VAT zero-rated refunds and input VAT credits previously allowed, plus late-payment interest. Administrative penalties under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection range from 50% to 500% of the evaded tax amount. Serious or fraudulent export-tax-rebate claims are prosecuted under PRC Criminal Law as export-tax-rebate fraud, exposing responsible directors, financial officers, and the entity to criminal fines and custodial penalties. The entity will additionally lose export-tax-rebate facilitation status, be downgraded in Customs credit classification, and may be treated as making a taxable domestic supply with VAT, customs duties, and penalties assessed retroactively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a linked exchange rate system with the US Dollar, and maintains full capital and current account convertibility. No surrender requirements exist for export proceeds; entities may retain and deploy foreign currency freely. The Hong Kong dollar (HKD) is pegged to USD within 7.75-7.85, and the RMB is freely convertible in the offshore CNH market in Hong Kong. For this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) that is incorporated or has material treasury functions in Hong Kong, there are no statutory hurdles to profit remittance or capital repatriation. However, because the shareholders include a mainland Chinese party, cross-border flows into or out of the PRC are subject to SAFE’s capital account controls. Equity injections from mainland investors require ODI (Outbound Direct Investment) registration, and any conversion of CNH to HKD or USD within Hong Kong can be done freely through licensed banks. Dividends paid to the mainland shareholder will trigger Chinese corporate income tax and VAT consequences, and the remittance must be wired through the RMB cross-border payment system (usually via BOCHK as the RMB clearing bank). In practice, the treasury should use distinct purpose codes such as 'Dividend Distribution' or 'Royalty Payment' and retain all supporting contracts and board minutes to satisfy both HKMA and SAFE audit expectations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio for export foreign currency receipts. Exporters may keep 100% of proceeds in any currency and hold them offshore indefinitely. If the JV is registered on the mainland, PRC rules would require repatriation within 180 days and forced conversion only for a portion depending on current account rules, but that is not applicable to a Hong Kong-incorporated entity or a licensed treasury centre operating from Hong Kong.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) – de facto central bank and monetary authority; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) govern CNY convertibility for mainland-involved capital flows but do not directly regulate Hong Kong's free-FX regime.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Hong Kong’s virtual banking licence framework permits fintech entities such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, and Mox Bank to provide core corporate accounts. These are technically viable for a low-risk audio licensing entity, especially for receiving Stripe and PayPal settlements in USD or EUR. However, for a JV involving mainland shareholders, traditional banks with strong RMB clearing and cross-border treasury expertise (HSBC, BOCHK, Standard Chartered) are preferred. Fintech EMIs often lack full trade finance, documentary collection, and NDF/swap capabilities. Hong Kong does not bar EMIs, but their credit assessments and account limits may be insufficient for larger capex or dividend flows. Therefore the viability rating is 'Conditional – medium risk' and recommended only as a secondary collection account, never as the primary treasury route.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from mainland shareholders must follow the ODI framework: the mainland investor obtains MOFCOM/NDRC approval and SAFE FX registration, then instructs its domestic bank to SWIFT MT103 the funds using purpose code 'ODI 221000' (Investment of overseas enterprise). The funds are settled in CNH through the offshore clearing bank (BOCHK) and credited to the JV’s capital account in Hong Kong. Upon receipt, a licensed CPA in Hong Kong issues a capital verification certificate confirming the paid-up capital. Withdrawals from the capital account on the mainland side are not available, and any subsequent remittance back to the mainland must be clearly lettered as repatriation of investment profits, supported by audited financial statements and tax withholding certificates.Royalty and licensing payments to creators and sub-licensees are best executed via SWIFT MT103, cleared through CHATS (local HKD) or CHIPS/Fedwire (USD). For high-value contractual settlements, an Irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) can be used when the counterparty demands secured payment, though digital licensing rarely requires LC. Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) may be utilised for fixed-fee master license deals, but the efficiency of e-wallet batch payouts through Stripe Connect or PayPal is acceptable for micro-royalties. All outbound wires must include the correct purpose code (e.g., 'Royalties and License Fees') and supporting invoices or settlement statements to avoid friction with correspondent banks.Outright forwards (Full Delivery) on USD/HKD and USD/CNH with HKMA-licensed banksNon-Deliverable Forwards (NDF) on USD/CNH – permitted for hedging offshore RMB exposureCross-currency swaps between USD, HKD and CNH – subject to bank counterparty limitsFX options (call/put) on USD/CNH for flexible hedging of contingent royalty streams

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Hong Kong’s virtual banking licence framework permits fintech entities such as ZA Bank, WeLab Bank, and Mox Bank to provide core corporate accounts. These are technically viable for a low-risk audio licensing entity, especially for receiving Stripe and PayPal settlements in USD or EUR. However, for a JV involving mainland shareholders, traditional banks with strong RMB clearing and cross-border treasury expertise (HSBC, BOCHK, Standard Chartered) are preferred. Fintech EMIs often lack full trade finance, documentary collection, and NDF/swap capabilities. Hong Kong does not bar EMIs, but their credit assessments and account limits may be insufficient for larger capex or dividend flows. Therefore the viability rating is 'Conditional – medium risk' and recommended only as a secondary collection account, never as the primary treasury route.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A Hong Kong-incorporated company is generally required to have its financial statements audited by a certified public accountant under Section 372 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A private company (not part of a group) may be exempt from audit for a financial year if it qualifies as a 'small private company' by satisfying at least two of the following three criteria: (1) annual turnover/revenue not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (equivalent to approximately USD 1,282,051); (2) total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,051); (3) not more than 50 employees. No audit exemption is available to public companies, companies that are members of a group unless the group as a whole qualifies as a 'small group', or if any shareholder holding at least 5% of voting rights objects to the exemption. The exemptions are set out in the Companies (Exemption of Companies and Companies of a Group from Audit) Regulation. Otherwise, full statutory audit is mandatory, and the same audit requirement applies regardless of the level of capital or the ownership structure, including Sino-foreign equity joint ventures domesticated as Hong Kong private limited companies.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where cross-border related-party transactions of a particular category (tangible property, intangible property, financial assets, financing or intra-group services) exceed RMB 100 million in a tax year; Master File documentation is additionally required where the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and the Chinese entity's cross-border controlled transactions exceed RMB 100 million. Lower category-specific thresholds and Annual Related-Party Transaction Form requirements apply under SAT Announcement 42 (2016).

Economic substance scope: China applies a general anti-avoidance rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the substance-over-form doctrine in SAT Bulletin 30/2018 on beneficial ownership. Although mainland China does not have a stand-alone Economic Substance Regulations filing regime comparable to other territories, treaty benefits are denied to HK shell companies that merely hold Chinese IP or channel dividends without having real functions, workers, offices, and decision-making presence in Hong Kong. The HK shareholder must demonstrate its own core income-generating activities and cannot rely solely on the JV's activities; otherwise SAT may recharacterise the HK company as a conduit and impose the full 10% withholding.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Declaration of Digital Sales Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the PRC: the tax authority will recover the underpaid tax, impose a 0.05% daily late surcharge, and levy a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. The taxpayer is also listed in the Serious Tax Violations public disclosure (tax blacklist), which triggers credit sanctions and long-term administrative supervision.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and actual director who participated in or approved the concealment is personally jointly and severally liable with the company for the recovered tax, surcharge and penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20 of PRC Company Law to seize personal assets, freeze bank accounts, impose travel bans and exit restrictions under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, and impose imprisonment of up to three years for relatively large evasion, rising to three to seven years for huge amounts under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Failure to Maintain Contemporaneous Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 41 and Article 114 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the SAT may make an arm's length adjustment, recalculate taxable income by reference to CUP or TNMM, impose interest at the benchmark RMB loan rate plus 5 percentage points for documentation default, and levy a fine of up to RMB 10,000 for failure to file contemporaneous documentation; where the mispricing constitutes willful under-declaration, penalties under the tax evasion provisions also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors involved in issuing false intercompany invoices or manipulating royalty bookings are jointly liable for the adjusted tax and additional interest; civil restitution can be enforced against their personal assets where corporate funds were diverted; exit restrictions and de-registration blacklisting may be imposed by SAT and Customs, and criminal liability arises if the TP documentation was forged, including imprisonment under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 162 regarding fraudulent accounting and evasion.
Shell HK Holding Company / DTA Abuse (Treaty Shopping) and Deficient Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: SAT Bulletin 30/2018 denies treaty benefits where the beneficiary lacks economic substance and real ownership of the income; the tax authority will recharacterise the HK shell as a conduit, apply the domestic 10% statutory WHT, recover the additional 5% underwithholding plus daily interest, and may impose a 50%-500% penalty if deliberate avoidance is established under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced by treating the JV directors and the actual controlling person of the HK shell as jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax, penalties and interest; the traveler/legal representative will face a court-ordered exit ban if the tax is not settled, assets of the shell company and of the individual beneficial owner can be attached, and criminal money-laundering or tax-evasion charges may be commenced if offshore bank transfers were disguised to avoid the tax authority's report, carrying imprisonment and full asset restitution.
