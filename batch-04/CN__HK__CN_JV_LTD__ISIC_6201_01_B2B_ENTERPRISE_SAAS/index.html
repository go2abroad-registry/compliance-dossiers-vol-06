Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT cross-border rules, a VAT general taxpayer may claim zero-rating (0% VAT) and related input-VAT refund for exported taxable services, including B2B enterprise SaaS and software/IT services, where the services are supplied to a non-resident enterprise, consumed entirely outside mainland China, consideration is received from outside mainland China, and the supply falls within the statutory zero-rated cross-border service categories. For physical goods, export zero-rating is available only if the goods are actually exported through declared customs procedures. The taxpayer must report zero-rated sales on the VAT return, retain the statutory export documentary bundle, and apply for the VAT refund through the electronic tax system.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic export goods declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Original commercial invoice, packing list, and export contract
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / railway consignment note evidencing export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign currency export proceeds collection confirmation
  • For SaaS/services exports in lieu of goods customs clearance: executed enterprise software agreement, offshore delivery and acceptance records, and service consumption substantiation

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the SAT Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice platform. VAT invoices are issued through the SAT electronic invoice platform, which transmits invoice data in real time to the tax authority, validates the Unified Social Credit Code, service tax classification code, VAT rate and amount, and applies encrypted tax-control stamps and anti-fraud risk controls before the invoice can be used for input VAT credit. ERP and billing systems connect through SAT-certified APIs or the Electronic Tax Administration portal. The Hong Kong SAR has no fiscal clearance or electronic invoicing mandate.

If export zero-rating is unsubstantiated, incomplete, or found fraudulent, the tax authority will claw back the claimed VAT exemption or refund with statutory interest. Under PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law Articles 63 and 64, inaccurate or fraudulent claims attract a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid, and serious export tax refund fraud triggers criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 204. In Hong Kong, false declarations in profits tax or business registration filings may be penalised under the Inland Revenue Ordinance by additional tax equal to three times the tax undercharged, monetary fines, and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC EJV is subject to China's managed floating RMB regime with strict separation between current account and capital account treatment. Current account items, including B2B SaaS subscription export receipts, software development service fees, royalty payments, and ordinary operational disbursements, are generally convertible once the underlying transaction is genuine, documented, and verified through a SAFE-designated domestic commercial bank. Capital account items are not freely convertible: inbound equity contributions from the Hong Kong investor must be registered with SAFE before remittance, and any subsequent conversion into RMB for domestic operating expenditures must comply with the payment-based settlement requirements, which require proof of underlying contracts and invoices. Outbound capital movements, including dividend remittances, repayment of offshore loans, and cross-border direct investment by the EJV, require registration, tax clearance, and purpose-of-payment declarations. Although the Hong Kong jurisdiction has a currency board, full HKD/USD convertibility, and no surrender rules, the PRC-side EJV cannot treat the HK treasury as a gateway that bypasses SAFE controls. Dividends paid to the Hong Kong Corporate shareholder are remittable after the annual audit and tax on profits are settled, subject to the applicable withholding tax concession under CEPA or the Hong Kong/China double taxation arrangement, but the remittance flow must still pass through the domestic bank's foreign exchange monitoring system. There is no mandatory conversion of export proceeds into RMB under the current framework, but all export-affiliated receipts must be repatriated into a domestic licensed bank account within the relevant trade payment term, and banks are obliged to verify the transaction against signed SaaS agreements, invoices, and service delivery evidence. The overall assessment is that operating receivables are convertible, capital inflows are controlled but executable, and profit repatriation is available only after successful tax clearance and SAFE-documentation review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory export surrender ratio has been abolished; a PRC EJV may retain 100% of export foreign currency receipts in a domestic foreign exchange account. Export goods receipts must be repatriated into a SAFE-supervised domestic bank account within the contracted settlement term, generally no later than 90 days after customs export declaration unless a bank-approved trade financing extension applies. Service export receipts for the B2B SaaS business should be collected according to the payment schedule in the software subscription agreement and entered into the trade-in-services reporting system. Advance export receipts must be reported in the foreign exchange monitoring system within one month of receipt. Any conversion of these foreign currency proceeds into RMB must be supported by E-form trade records, the corresponding sales contract, commercial invoices, and settlement records in the bank's digital SAFE portal.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the PRC-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the currency-board authority for the Hong Kong corporate shareholder and HKD clearing leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banking, including Wise and Stripe Treasury-style platforms, is not viable as the primary legal account rail for the PRC-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. These fintechs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic RMB current accounts, process FDI capital injection files, handle capital account registration, or satisfy the local account-opening physical-presence rule for a Chinese legal entity. The primary treasury, FACR, hedge execution, and RMB settlement must remain with a Tier-1 PRC commercial bank. The Hong Kong investing entity may, however, use Wise B2B and Stripe Billing as merchant collection rails for overseas SaaS subscriptions where the Hong Kong entity is the merchant of record, provided the collected funds are first reconciled, taxed, and then remitted into the mainland EJV under a transfer-pricing policy and service contract that satisfies SAFE's current account verification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound routing starts with SAFE FDI registration through the designated domestic bank, which issues a Foreign Exchange Business Registration number for the EJV. The Hong Kong investor then remits equity proceeds via SWIFT MT103 to the EJV's Capital Foreign Exchange Account using a capital-account purpose code corresponding to Foreign Direct Investment equity capital injection. The receiving bank applies the funds to the SAFE verification ledger and credits the account only after confirming that the remitter matches the registered investor. If the EJV requires RMB to fund operations, conversion is executed under the payment-based settlement regime by presenting underlying contracts, invoices, and intended-use evidence to the bank. A licensed PRC accounting firm must subsequently issue a capital verification report confirming receipt of the registered capital contribution. The funds cannot be used for equity investment in unapproved sectors, short-term lending outside the EJV, or settlement of unauthorized offshore debt.For the B2B enterprise SaaS scope, outbound flows will predominantly be trade-in-services payments rather than physical goods settlements. The standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with a service-trade purpose code, accompanied by the service agreement, commercial invoice, provider tax certification, withholding tax payment receipt, and the Service Trade Foreign Exchange declaration record. If the EJV also distributes hardware or cloud appliances, commercial letters of credit can be opened through the domestic bank using SWIFT MT700/MT701 with required shipping documents, while documentary collections are acceptable only when the underlying transport documents and marine/ocean bills of lading are presented through the correspondent banking network. All outbound disbursements must be indexed to the SAFE trade monitoring system before the bank releases funds.Onshore RMB/FX forward contracts are permissible for the EJV only where a genuine underlying current account or capital account exposure exists, documented by a signed contract, invoice, or SAFE registration record, and the tenor cannot exceed the maturity of the underlying exposure.RMB/FX swaps are permissible for short-term liquidity hedging when the EJV has matching spot and forward receipts or payments in different currencies, provided the swap is executed through a licensed onshore bank and recorded in the bank's derivative risk-management framework.Cross-currency interest rate swaps are permissible for medium and long-term offshore borrowing or intra-group financing where the EJV has a SAFE-registered foreign debt facility, but the notional amount and maturity must align with the underlying loan agreement.Plain vanilla FX options and risk-reversal combinations are permissible for current account risk hedging when the EJV proves a non-speculative hedging intent through a board-approved treasury policy and underlying trade documentation.Offshore non-deliverable forwards using CNH exchanges are not permissible inside the onshore PRC regulatory boundary for the domestic EJV; they may be accessed only by the Hong Kong treasury entity as an offshore hedge, and the EJV cannot take the derivative position directly without obtaining specific SAFE approval.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI banking, including Wise and Stripe Treasury-style platforms, is not viable as the primary legal account rail for the PRC-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. These fintechs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic RMB current accounts, process FDI capital injection files, handle capital account registration, or satisfy the local account-opening physical-presence rule for a Chinese legal entity. The primary treasury, FACR, hedge execution, and RMB settlement must remain with a Tier-1 PRC commercial bank. The Hong Kong investing entity may, however, use Wise B2B and Stripe Billing as merchant collection rails for overseas SaaS subscriptions where the Hong Kong entity is the merchant of record, provided the collected funds are first reconciled, taxed, and then remitted into the mainland EJV under a transfer-pricing policy and service contract that satisfies SAFE's current account verification.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For years of assessment commencing on or after 1 April 2023, a Hong Kong taxpayer must prepare a Local File if total related party transactions reach HKD 2 million for finance transactions, HKD 5.5 million for transfer of goods (including SaaS-provided software/intangibles), or HKD 2 million for other transactions; a Master File is required if the group's annual consolidated turnover exceeds HKD 1 billion, and the Singapore-style threshold is not used. In practice, the IRD's DIPN 59 and 60 follow OECD thresholds, and a Local File is mandatory for any multinational group with related-party revenue above these de minimis limits; the one-sided or two-sided documentation must be prepared before the filing deadline and produced within 21 days of the IRD's notice. The current per-category thresholds are HKD 5.5 million for intercompany goods, HKD 2 million for intercompany services or royalties, and HKD 2 million for finance transactions; the total threshold is HKD 11 million.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) through the Inland Revenue Act and the Final Beneficial Owner transparency regime. For a B2B SaaS joint venture, the CIGA (Core Income-Generating Activities) are the planning, decision-making, and operational execution in Hong Kong. Merely holding contracts in Hong Kong or a mailbox office is insufficient. The IRD will scrutinize whether the Hong Kong entity has real, active management, local directorship substance, adequate staff, and premises. For treaty benefits, Article 4 of the China-HK DTA incorporates the OECD PE and LOB/PPT limitations, requiring the Hong Kong company to prove it is not a shell; failure to meet substance can result in treaty denial, 16.5% tax on HK-source profits, and penalties for incorrect returns.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion: False or Misleading Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Section 80(4) Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO): upon conviction, a fine of HKD 50,000 plus treble the tax undercharged and imprisonment of up to 3 years. Section 82A IRO allows the IRD to impose a civil penalty of treble tax in lieu of prosecution, and Section 80(1) carries a first-instance fine of HKD 10,000 plus treble tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly or negligently authorize or participate in filing a false return are personally liable; Courts can order them to restore the underpaid tax plus interest and penalties. Where the entity is used as a vehicle for deliberate tax evasion, the corporate veil may be pierced; directors face criminal imprisonment up to 7 years under general fraud statutes, travel restriction notice under the IRO (s.22 Finance Act / travel limitation for tax debts over HKD 150,000), and 10-year debarment from company management under the Companies Ordinance.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency Leading to Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the China-HK DTA principal purpose test (PPT) and OECD BEPS Action 6, the IRD and PRC tax authorities may deny treaty benefits, impose the full 10% dividend / 7% royalty PRC withholding, and apply the Hong Kong profits tax plus penalties. A shell company also violates Hong Kong corporate law: s.21 Companies Ordinance requires at least one natural director; s.776(2) requires registered office; Inland Revenue may assess the entire offshore profit as Hong Kong-sourced if CIGA are found in Hong Kong, with additional penalties under s.82A of up to HK$500,000 and treble tax.

Director personal liability: Directors' personal liability arises under the Corporate Veil Doctrine: where a director signs a tax return knowing that the entity is a shell, he or she may be criminally prosecuted for aiding tax evasion. Courts may order restitution of the offshore profits, impose a travel ban preventing departure until tax debts are settled, and sentence directors to up to 3 years' imprisonment under s.80 IRO. If the shell structure is used for fraud or money laundering, directors face up to 14 years for conspiracy to defraud and asset freezing orders.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Sections 50AAM-50AAO and s.50AB IRO apply. The IRD may raise transfer pricing adjustments, disallow deductions, and impose a penalty. For a failure to provide documentation within 21 days, the maximum penalty is HKD 100,000 plus a daily default penalty of HKD 500 per day. For incorrect returns due to a TP adjustment, the penalty is treble the tax undercharged (up to HKD 50,000 imprisonment alternative under s.80), and the IRD may issue a Section 82A assessment for treble tax.

Director personal liability: Under the 'directing mind' doctrine, directors can be personally sued by the company for losses caused by negligent tax management. In tax proceedings, directors who sign inaccurate TP declarations may be subject to criminal liability and imprisonment of up to 3 years. Where a transfer pricing manipulation is accompanied by fraudulent intent to evade tax, the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance applies penalties plus 6 years' imprisonment; travel restrictions and disqualification orders can be imposed on directors found liable for serious tax defaults.
