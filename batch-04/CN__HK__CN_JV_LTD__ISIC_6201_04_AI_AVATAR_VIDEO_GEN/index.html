VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis turnover threshold; VAT registration is mandatory from the first yuan of taxable supply in China. Foreign suppliers of digital services to Chinese consumers also must register through a tax/financial representative even without a local establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) is available for exported software services, including AI video synthesis and virtual avatar generation, when such services are supplied to and fully consumed by an overseas entity (offshore consumption), consideration is received from abroad and remitted into China via banking channels, and the service is not used in domestic taxable transactions. The VAT-registered exporter must file the cross-border zero-rated service declaration on the e-Tax portal.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) — only when physical goods or media cross the border
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / Express Consignment Note — for physical media movement, if applicable
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign-exchange receipt certificate — evidencing inward remittance and foreign settlement
  • Cross-border service contract signed by both parties — identifying the overseas customer, service scope and offshore consumption terms
  • Digital service delivery evidence — server logs, delivery receipts, hash proofs, usage data, or acceptance certificates
  • Zero-rated export service declaration — as submitted on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal

China has adopted the Fully Digitised Electronic Invoice (FDEI) regime; e-invoices are issued on the Electronic Tax Bureau platform and synchronised in real time with the State Taxation Administration central database, bearing a unified national invoice number and digital stamp. Certified VAT Invoice Service Platform (tax-control UKey) is still required for traditional VAT special invoices. API-based automatic synchronisation is available for e-commerce platforms and large enterprises; anti-fraud red-flag detection and periodic reconciliation are mandatory.

The State Taxation Administration will strictly audit export zero-rating. Failure to provide the required export documentation, to repatriate export proceeds into China within the 180-day statutory deadline, or to satisfy the offshore consumption tests will trigger retrospective clawback of the 0% rate, reassessment of VAT at 6% (or higher applicable rate, e.g. 13% for any incidental goods), late-payment interest and penalties under the Tax Collection and Administration Law (Article 63) of 50%–500% of the underpaid tax. For fraudulent export refunds, the Administrative Measures on Export Refunds impose a separate penalty of 100%–500% of the falsely claimed refund and suspension or loss of zero-rating/refund eligibility; willful tax fraud carries criminal liability under the Criminal Law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar operates under a currency board system linked to the US dollar, guaranteeing full convertibility on both current and capital account items within Hong Kong. There are no surrender requirements, no prior approval for foreign exchange acquisitions, and no central bank restrictions on repatriation of dividends, trade settlements, or loan repayments for entities resident in Hong Kong. However, the Chinese parent contributing capital to the Hong Kong JV must navigate mainland China's capital account controls. The initial injection requires approval and filing with SAFE under the overseas direct investment (ODI) regime, with funds remitted only after the ODI certificate and bank verification. Once funds are in Hong Kong, they may be freely converted into any major currency. Profit distribution back to the mainland parent is technically a cross-border remittance from Hong Kong and thus unrestricted locally; the inbound transfer to China is classified as a cross-border dividend under a special SAFE current-account or capital-account item, requiring tax clearance and proof of audited profits. The practical hurdle is not Hong Kong convertibility but Chinese regulatory issuance of ODI quotas, the authenticity of the underlying AI SaaS business, and resubmission of supporting contracts and intellectual property valuations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender or repatriation timeline on export proceeds; a Hong Kong exporter may retain, convert, or deploy all foreign currency receipts at its discretion. If the mainland operating subsidiary of the JV receives exports in foreign currency, SAFE requires repatriation of goods-trade receipts into a designated foreign exchange settlement account within 90 days of the settlement date (extendable in certain sectors to 180 days), with no mandatory conversion ratio; 100% of the proceeds can be retained in foreign currency, but banks must verify the underlying trade documents and report through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the effective central bank and exchange fund manager; cross-border flows involving mainland China also require compliance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC).

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Hong Kong does not define 'EMI' as a regulated category; digital cross-border players (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie) hold Money Service Operator (MSO) or Stored Value Facility (SVF) licences from HKMA, or have acquired licensed banking subsidiaries. They can legally service Hong Kong entities for payments, FX conversion, and multi-currency accounts, but they cannot act as a primary settlement bank for cross-border capital injections from mainland China because they do not have direct access to the SAFE/ODI approval workflow and are not covered under the Chinese banks' SWIFT CPS reporting with SAFE. Their viability is therefore Medium: appropriate for low-value trade disbursements and platform payouts (Stripe, Paddle) but not for corporate treasury holding of statutory capital or dividend repatriation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Hong Kong entity in a multinational group must prepare Local File and Master File if its annual turnover (or gross income for financial institutions) exceeds HK$20 million, or HK$4 million for financial income, respectively. Once these small enterprise thresholds are breached, contemporaneous TP documentation is required for all controlled transactions with non-resident enterprises: assets, intangibles, services, and finance. Even below the thresholds, the IRD may request documentation for any controlled transaction.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong's Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime effective 1 January 2023 subjects offshore passive income (dividends, interest, royalties, and gains) received in Hong Kong by a member of a multinational group to tax unless the taxpayer demonstrates economic substance in Hong Kong: (i) adequate premises; (ii) sufficient and qualified employees; and (iii) core income-generating activities (CIGA) performed in Hong Kong (e.g. strategic planning, procurement, pricing, risk management, and approval of transactions). For IP income, a nexus-approach determines the deductible qualifying expenditure but only to the extent of your Hong Kong CIGA. The doctrine prevents shell companies from claiming tax-free offshore passive income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – False or Misleading Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82A: upon conviction, a fine up to HK$50,000 and additional tax of three times the amount undercharged. IRO s.80(2): false statement in tax return – fine up to HK$10,000, plus three times tax undercharged, and imprisonment up to 3 years. The IRD may also issue a 'severe default' assessment imposing up to 100% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign a false return face criminal liability for conspiracy to defraud the Inland Revenue, with imprisonment up to 3 years. In cases of fraud or sham, the court can pierce the corporate veil, rendering directors personally liable for unpaid tax. Travel restriction: section 77I IRO allows the Commissioner to obtain an order to prevent a person from leaving Hong Kong if tax is outstanding and departure may impede collection; bank account and asset seizure may be ordered.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Shell Company Artificial Arrangement

Statutory sanction: IRO s.58C imposes an obligation to retain sufficient TP documentation. Failure to do so is an offence under s.80A(1)(a): fine up to HK$100,000. The IRD may re-characterise or disregard the transaction under s.61 or advance the anti-abuse rule, computing profits on the arm's length basis; tax undercharged is subject to three times penalty or 100% surcharge.

Director personal liability: Officers/directors are jointly and severally liable for the HK$100,000 fine and any additional tax, if the default occurs with their knowledge. Civil liability under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s.375: if business was carried on with intent to defraud creditors, the court may order the directors to contribute all assets to the company's liabilities; fraudulent trading carries criminal liability and imprisonment up to 15 years. The District Court may also make a personal fines order against directors.
FSIE Economic Substance Failure and AML Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under the FSIE regime, the passive income is deemed Hong Kong-source and chargeable at 16.5% on the gross amount, with penalties for failure to maintain records (IRO s.51C: fine up to HK$100,000). AML contraventions under Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615): a corporation is liable on conviction to a fine of up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 months (first conviction), with up to 7 years for repeat offences. The Financial Services and Treasury Bureau may impose market-entry prohibitions.

Director personal liability: Directors who knew or consented to the lack of substance and AML controls can be prosecuted personally under Cap. 615; directors may be deemed shadow directors of the mainland shell and personally assessed for profits tax under IRO s.76(2) if they cause the company to fail to file. Civil recovery: the company's deception can be set aside, and the director's assets can be frozen under mutual legal assistance orders (Cap. 609). A travel ban/blacklisting may be imposed by the IRD or the court where the director is a flight risk, and an arrest warrant may be issued for failure to comply with a restraint order.
