Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office; electronic filing and taxpayer services portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk); company incorporation registration is administered separately by the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - For a new Hong Kong company, incorporate with the Companies Registry by filing the statutory incorporation form (NNC1 for a company limited by shares), the Articles of Association and the incorporation fee; the Companies Registry automatically notifies the Business Registration Office, which issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the Business Registration Number (BRN). Step 2 - For a foreign non-resident company that carries on business in Hong Kong, apply to the Business Registration Office within one month of commencing business using the prescribed Business Registration Ordinance application form, accompanied by a certified copy of the overseas registration/certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, particulars of directors, Hong Kong registered address, and evidence of the local principal place of business; the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate and BRN. Step 3 - The BRN is the company's Hong Kong taxpayer identification number and must be quoted on Employer Returns (IR56B), Profits Tax Returns (BIR51 for corporations) and all eTAX correspondence. Step 4 - A foreign non-resident entity that has no Hong Kong branch, no Hong Kong-sourced profits and no business in Hong Kong is not required to obtain a BRN or tax file; because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST, there is no separate VAT registration number or VAT ID to procure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (a Hong Kong company limited by shares is a separate taxable entity; profits tax is imposed at the corporate rate only on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong under the territorial source principle; no transparent or pass-through treatment).

Available election forms

None - Hong Kong does not operate an IRC check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election. A group of Hong Kong companies may elect for group loss relief under section 19C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the two-tiered Profits Tax rate regime (8.25% on qualifying first HKD 2,000,000 and 16.5% thereafter for corporations) applies statutorily without a classification election.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a People's Republic of China legal entity, and therefore its RMB capital, operating cash flow, and cross-border foreign exchange transactions are governed by the PRC foreign exchange regulatory regime administered by SAFE. The RMB is fully convertible for current account items, including trade payments, service remuneration, royalty and license fees, and dividend remittances, provided the underlying transaction is genuine, documented, and routed through a designated commercial bank. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, equity injections, cross-border loans, and repatriation of invested capital upon liquidation, remain subject to prior SAFE registration or approval. Profit remittance is permitted only after statutory annual auditing, tax clearance, and presentation of the prior-year audited financial statements, board resolution declaring dividends, and the bank's verification of foreign exchange payment purpose codes. There is no free floating of RMB against foreign currencies for speculative purposes; all settlement at commercial banks must contain genuine trade or investment background. The Hong Kong SAR side of the joint venture benefits from full capital account convertibility and free repatriation of funds, but the underlying PRC treasury function is the binding constraint. Foreign currency earnings may be retained in domestic foreign exchange settlement accounts or converted into RMB at bank-determined rates, subject to SAFE macro-prudential monitoring and the submission of trade receipt declarations through the Asynchronous Information System of the FX Administration. Companies must register all foreign debt and direct investment structures with SAFE. Therefore, all liquidity strategy must be designed around the PRC entity as the central treasury point, with HK serving as a regional collection and investor vehicle rather than an unrestricted offshore liquidity hub.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC exporters must repatriate export foreign exchange receipts onshore and deposit them into an eligible foreign exchange settlement account at a licensed bank. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio to RMB under the current SAFE framework; enterprises may freely choose to retain or settle export receipts. The collection and settlement must be declared in the trade monitoring system, with bank verification against the customs declaration. Export receipts are generally expected to be collected within 90 days after the customs-declared shipment date, unless commercial terms substantiate a longer payment cycle, in which case the bank must be notified and SAFE monitoring tolerances are applied. Failure to meet the repatriation and declaration timeline may trigger SAFE administrative review, data discrepancy flags, and impediments to future FX settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China; for the Hong Kong SAR limb, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) administers the Hong Kong dollar clearing framework and currency convertibility.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for the PRC onshore JV as the primary banking rail. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment institutions are not legal substitutes for a licensed Chinese commercial bank when opening SAFE-regulated capital accounts, RMB settlement accounts, or foreign exchange current accounts. Such EMIs cannot verify the foreign exchange purpose code, perform SAFE reporting, or hold the entity's statutory capital escrow. They may be considered only for ancillary disbursements from the Hong Kong holding vehicle to overseas app-store operators or third-party service providers, provided the Hong Kong entity separately maintains its own bank relationship. The PBOC and SAFE prohibit unlicensed payment processors from conducting large-value corporate FX settlement inside the PRC. Any online payment channel used for app store transactions must connect through a licensed bank payment interface with a valid cross-border payment business qualification.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection into the PRC JV must be routed through the SAFE-registered capital account held at a designated domestic commercial bank. The foreign investor remits equity or shareholder loan proceeds by SWIFT MT103 to the bank's receiving correspondent, attaching the correct external debt or direct investment purpose code. The bank verifies the SAFE FDI registration number, the capital account payment instructions, and the authenticity of the underlying joint venture contract. After receipt, the bank converts or credits the foreign currency into the capital escrow account. A capital verification report issued by a licensed CPA is required to evidence the injection has actually been paid into the entity's capital account and to allow the subsequent conversion to RMB and transfer into the operating account. All payments toward expenses outside of the registered business scope are expressly prohibited.For outbound trade and service disbursements, the JV shall use SWIFT MT103 for direct bank-to-bank trade payments, domestic commercial letters of credit issued through the negotiating bank, and documentary collections for lower-risk supply contracts. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate under the People's Republic of China Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law), Article 4.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident Hong Kong enterprise under EIT Law Art. 3 and 27 and Implementation Regulation Art. 91; reduced to 5% under Article 10 of the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the Chinese entity's capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a contemporaneous Local File is mandatory for controlled transactions that exceed RMB 40 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 20 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 20 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 10 million for service transactions. A Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion, or where the enterprise meets large-enterprise criteria (assets over RMB 200 million, revenue over RMB 200 million, and over 100 employees) together with the relevant individual thresholds. The Related Party Transaction Report Form (A/B) must be annexed to the annual corporate income tax filing when aggregate controlled transactions exceed the prescribed reporting threshold.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate classic Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) as in offshore centres, but substance is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR, EIT Law Art. 47) and the beneficial ownership test under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9. The Chinese entity must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in China, including app development, maintenance, user support, and product management. The Hong Kong parent must demonstrate real presence, employees, business premises, and risk-bearing capacity in Hong Kong; a shell-company structure will be disregarded for DTA purposes and the income reattributed to China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Accounting

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, underpayment by fraud attracts a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax plus daily surcharge and back-taxes. Under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law, tax evasion above RMB 50,000 and exceeding 10% of the statutory tax payable is criminal; imprisonment up to three years for a relatively large amount, and three to seven years for a huge amount, together with criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative, and financial officers who approve or sign fraudulent returns may have the corporate veil pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, making them personally liable for restitution of unpaid tax and creditors' losses. A tax dishonesty blacklisting imposes travel bans and high-consumption restrictions; passports are suspended, and criminal custodial sentences are imposed upon conviction.
Transfer Pricing and Base Erosion / Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Art. 41 and Art. 44 allow the tax authority to adjust income or apply a deemed profit rate where TP documentation is absent; SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes documentation penalties under TAL Art. 60 and 62 including fines up to RMB 10,000 for non-filing. Adjusted income attracts interest equal to central bank lending benchmark plus 5 percentage points, late surcharge, and penalties of 50% to five times the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The Chinese JV's controlling shareholders and responsible directors are jointly and severally liable for TP adjustments and interest when they abuse the corporate limited-liability structure. The legal representative may be restrained from leaving China pending payment of outstanding tax, placed on the serious tax violation blacklist, and subjected to asset seizure. Where TP abuse is accompanied by false documentation, criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 or Art. 205 can result in imprisonment.
Improper Dividend Repatriation and Treaty Relief Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under TAL Art. 69, the withholding agent is liable for the unwithheld tax; the tax authority may deny treaty relief under GAAR (EIT Law Art. 47) and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, leaving the 10% statutory WHT plus daily surcharges of 0.05% and penalties of 50% to five times the underpaid amount. Criminal sanctions under Criminal Law Art. 201 or Art. 204 apply where the abusive treaty claim is part of a fraudulent tax evasion scheme.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise or execute the dividend payment without compliance are personally liable for the unpaid WHT and penalties under Company Law Art. 20. They become subject to the social credit blacklist, travel bans, and restriction on leaving China; their personal bank accounts and assets may be frozen or seized to satisfy the tax debt. Imprisonment is possible for a conviction of intentional non-withholding or tax fraud.
