Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, accessible via the eTax electronic portal at https://www.gov.hk/etax

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the Business Registration Office under the IRD within one month of commencing business in Hong Kong. The application is submitted via Form IRBR1 (Business Registration Application) and can be filed electronically through the eTax portal. For profits tax purposes, the taxpayer will be assigned an irrevocable tax reference number upon issue of the BRC. If no BRC is required (e.g., Hong Kong-sourced income is derived without a physical presence), the non-resident must file Form IR 1301 (Profits Tax Return – Non-resident) to open a tax file and obtain a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Supporting documents include: certified copies of incorporation certificates, the company's constitutional memorandum and articles, director and shareholder details, Hong Kong agent appointment letter (if any), and audited financial statements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None - Hong Kong imposes no general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax; only excise duties on a limited list of dutiable commodities such as liquors, tobacco, hydrocarbon oils, and methyl alcohol exist.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no indirect tax registration threshold exists in Hong Kong.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong does not levy VAT or GST on exports; therefore, no zero-rating regime applies. From a profits tax perspective, exports of goods and qualifying offshore services are generally treated as offshore income and are not subject to profits tax under the territorial source principle. Eligibility requires that the goods are sold and supplied from Hong Kong to an overseas customer without a Hong Kong onshore delivery or negotiation of the contract.

  • Hong Kong Vessel or Air Cargo Export Declaration (submitted electronically via TradeSingle Window / Tradelink system)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill showing Hong Kong as the port of shipment
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Evidence of full receipt of foreign exchange consideration from the overseas buyer, such as bank credit advice or a payment confirmation

Not applicable - Hong Kong does not operate a mandatory e-invoicing or real-time fiscal clearance system. Voluntary e-invoicing is permitted.

Although no VAT clawback exists, erroneous classification of onshore income as offshore in profits tax returns may trigger IRD assessments, additional tax up to 100% of the tax undercharged, and penalties under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Fraudulent declarations to the Customs and Excise Department may lead to summary fine of HKD 50,000 and six months' imprisonment, or on indictment HKD 2,000,000 and three years' imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility and a strictly controlled capital account. The Sino-Foreign JV may freely convert RMB into foreign currency for trade and service payments, but each transaction must be supported by genuine underlying contracts and verified by a designated bank under SAFE's real-transaction principle. Exported VPN/encryption-software license fees and SaaS subscription revenue generated abroad are current-account receipts and may be retained in foreign currency accounts or settled into RMB. Capital-account movements, including the HK partner's equity injection, are subject to FDI registration and the issuance of a capital-account approval/registration certificate prior to receipt. Profit remittance to the HK shareholder is permitted after the annual audit, tax settlement, and board declaration; the bank will verify the audited financial statements, dividend tax withholding certificates, and the payment form cross-checked against prior registered profits. There are no quantitative caps on dividend conversion, but SAFE applies enhanced due diligence to VPN businesses because revenue arising from encrypted traffic can be difficult to trace to legitimate service contracts. Remittance of license royalties requires proof of technical service obligation and withholding tax clearance. The enterprise should maintain technical audit logs and a customer-list register because the collection of recurring subscription fees from foreign users may be challenged as cross-border data-usage receipts unless the actual delivery of software and network services can be clearly demonstrated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current-account reform, mandatory surrender of export proceeds has been abolished; the Sino-Foreign JV may retain export receipts in a designated foreign-exchange settlement account or convert them to CNY at the bank's prevailing price. All export receipts must be repatriated to China through a licensed domestic bank within the trade verification window, ordinarily 30 days after the date of the export Customs declaration or within 90 days when an approved trade-finance deferral is registered with SAFE. The enterprise must report trade receipts and payments in the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System and match each receipt with the relevant export Customs declaration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; however, if the foreign-exchange account balance exceeds the annual current-account income quota approved by SAFE based on prior-year actual receipts, the excess must be settled into RMB.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, exercising delegated authority from the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for the onshore mainland JV and Medium only for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle as an ancillary overseas collection rail. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from acting as the primary FX settlement account or capital-account receiving platform for the Chinese JV because SAFE requires all cross-border receipts/payments and foreign-exchange operations to pass through a domestically licensed bank with a PBOC/SAFE banking license. EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex or Revolut cannot issue a domestic CNY current account, cannot receive the HK partner's equity capital, and cannot perform SAFE capital verification. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK partner will initiate a SWIFT MT103 from an HKD/USD account at a licensed bank in Hong Kong to the JV's designated foreign-currency capital account at the onshore domestic bank, with the remittance reason stated as FDI equity injection and the SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise capital contribution. Before the funds are credited, the onshore bank must verify the SAFE/FDI registration certificate, the signed JV contract, the approval certificate, and the foreign-currency capital payment confirmation letter. After receipt, the statutory Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in contribution against the approved registered capital. Any conversion of capital into RMB for project expenditure must be processed through the bank's capital settlement mechanism, which requires the JV to present a payment intention document and an undertaking that the settled RMB will be used only for permitted operational purposes, never for equity investment or securities speculation without prior SAFE approval.For overseas supplier and trade settlements, the preferred rail is a SWIFT MT103 for service payments using SAFE service-trade purpose codes, supported by a signed contract, an invoice, a China tax withholding certificate, and a service-trade foreign-exchange payment/collection form. For hardware and equipment imports that cross customs, a sight Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) routed through the onshore bank is recommended, with shipping documents comprising the commercial invoice, bill of lading, packing list, insurance certificate, and any required encryption-hardware import licence. For smaller trade flows, documentary collections (D/P at sight or D/A) can be used, but the bank will require the same underlying trade contract and customs declaration records. Turnover: Hong Kong Companies Ordinance Cap 622 imposes a mandatory annual statutory audit on every active Hong Kong incorporated company, irrespective of turnover, total assets, or number of employees. There are no quantitative size-based audit exemptions for private companies in Hong Kong. A company must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) as auditor, and the auditor must express an opinion on whether the financial statements present a true and fair view in accordance with Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards and the Companies Ordinance. The only relevant exemption is for a dormant private company that has made no significant accounting transactions as defined in section 5 of Cap 622 and has filed the required dormant company declaration with the Companies Registry; a VPN subscription business with commercial revenue does not meet dormancy and therefore must be audited.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25 percent on taxable income of the China-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, with non-resident dividend, interest and royalty income separately taxed at 10 percent (or 5/7 percent under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement) when paid offshore.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

China levies a 10 percent dividend withholding tax on dividends paid by the China JV to its non-resident corporate shareholder, unless the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong company holding directly at least 25 percent of the JV's equity and meets the 365-day holding and substance conditions, in which case the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA reduces the rate to 5 percent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Taxpayers engaged in related-party transactions must prepare a contemporaneous Local File for transactions exceeding the following annual RMB thresholds: (i) holding or transfer of tangible assets over RMB 200 million; (ii) sale or use of intangible assets over RMB 100 million; (iii) financial asset transfers over RMB 100 million; (iv) related-party financing where average interest-bearing liabilities exceed RMB 100 million or annual interest paid/received exceeds RMB 10 million; (v) other connected transactions over RMB 40 million. A Master File is mandatory when aggregate connected transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or when the entity belongs to a group whose combined related-party transactions exceed the SAT public threshold.

Economic substance scope: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the China tax authority may recharacterize transactions lacking reasonable commercial purpose, including shell structures in Hong Kong that exist solely to obtain treaty benefits; the Hong Kong shareholder must therefore demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong - including contract negotiations, investment management, financing decisions and board leadership - and must show that its profits are not merely booked passive flows. Public Ann. 2018 No.9 and the OECD-style Limitation-on-Benefits analysis apply to reject DTA relief for a direct recipient that does not exercise substantive commercial functions; the China JV and Hong Kong parent must maintain annual substance documentation, office occupancy, payroll, bank records and board minutes to withstand both China GAAR review and Hong Kong's Economic Substance Requirements, because a mailbox parent will have its dividend WHT reinstated at 10 percent and may also lose its Hong Kong tax-residency privileges.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Concealed Digital Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, the underpaid tax is recovered with a penalty of 50 percent to 500 percent of the evaded amount; non-filing and false-filing also attract the 0.05 percent daily late surcharge under Article 32. Criminal liability arises under Article 201 of the Criminal Law where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10 percent of total tax payable, giving fines of 1-5 times the evaded tax and imprisonment up to seven years for excessively large amounts; the tax defaulter will be publicly blacklisted.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under Article 20(3) of the PRC Company Law if controlling shareholders or directors abuse the JV personality to evade tax and seriously harm creditors; directors who signed false financial statements or designed the offshore revenue routing are jointly and severally liable to restore the corporate assets and pay the unpaid tax; the legal representative is subject to exit-control travel bans, high-consumption restrictions, property preservation and criminal detention pending tax fraud investigation.
Failure to Withhold Non-Resident Dividend/Interest/Royalty WHT

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50 percent to 300 percent of the tax that the withholding agent failed to withhold and pay; Article 32 adds the daily 0.05 percent late-payment surcharge; if the JV fails to withhold and the Hong Kong shareholder does not pay, the China tax authority may directly assess the JV as withholding agent and may collect from the JV's bank accounts; intentional failure to withhold can be prosecuted as aiding tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who voted for the unaudited remittance and instructed the finance team not to withhold face joint and several civil liability to the tax authority under Article 20 of Company Law, because their instruction allowed an unlawful distribution of corporate assets; the finance director and legal representative are personally liable to repay the WHT, plus penalties; a freezing order and exit ban are issued, and if the non-withheld amount is significant and they received the overseas benefit, criminal charges for tax evasion conspiracy apply.
Shell Company Abusing DTA and Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the tax authority may make a special tax adjustment, deny the treaty benefit, recharacterize the Hong Kong parent as a conduit, and re-impose 10 percent or 25 percent tax on the diverted income; Public Ann. 2018 No.9 requires re-dissection of beneficial ownership; the underpaid tax is collected with a 50 percent-500 percent penalty under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law and daily 0.05 percent late interest; if forged supporting documents are used, the act constitutes tax evasion under Article 201 and document forgery under Article 280 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the false Tax Residency Certificate, beneficial ownership questionnaire and board minutes are personally liable for restitution of the unpaid WHT and GAAR adjustment; the personal assets of the legal representative and of the HK parent's shadow director are subject to preservation; both are placed on the tax-default blacklist, banned from leaving China through exit controls, and disqualified from serving as officers in import-export and high-tech enterprises; criminal conviction leads to a fine and imprisonment.
