Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) for mainland China; electronic registration and filing are conducted through the STA e-Tax Bureau online portal. For Hong Kong SAR, the separate tax authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTAX electronic filing platform.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in mainland China: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the Business License carrying the Uniform Social Credit Code, which is the statutory TIN for all STA filings; (2) after incorporation, complete supplementary tax registration and taxpayer status notifications in the STA e-Tax Bureau within the statutory timeline; (3) if the venture expects taxable turnover above RMB 5,000,000 in any 12 consecutive months, apply for VAT general taxpayer registration through e-Tax Bureau; otherwise remain a small-scale taxpayer until the mandatory threshold is crossed; (4) if a foreign non-resident entity has no mainland permanent establishment, it must provide its overseas TIN and tax residence certificate to the Chinese payer, and the payer registers the non-resident in the STA non-resident taxpayer repository for withholding filings. For Hong Kong SAR, foreign-incorporated entities carrying on a business must apply to the Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate; the IRD then issues the Business Registration Number and, for Profits Tax purposes, a Taxpayer Identification Number used in eTAX filings.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a resident enterprise in mainland China and is assessed on worldwide income at the entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; it is not a pass-through vehicle. A separately tax-resident Hong Kong entity would instead be assessed under Hong Kong territorial-source Profits Tax rules, but that is not the default classification for this mainland JV.

Available election forms

None — mainland China does not provide a check-the-box entity classification election or a domestic hybrid mismatch election; the JV is mandatorily treated as a corporate taxpayer. The China-Hong Kong tax arrangement and general anti-avoidance rules govern any hybrid mismatch claims; Hong Kong also has no elective check-the-box mechanism.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland PRC: RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan) of aggregate taxable supplies in any consecutive 12-month period; turnover above this threshold mandates VAT general taxpayer registration. Below the threshold the JV may operate as a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST/sales tax and therefore no registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: zero-rating applies where the goods are actually exported from mainland customs territory, the exporter is a VAT general taxpayer, the sale is at arm's length to an overseas buyer, customs export clearance is obtained, and export proceeds are repatriated to a licensed PRC bank. Qualifying offshore digital/software/ERP/CRM services: zero-rating or VAT exemption applies when the mainland VAT general taxpayer supplies software/cloud services to a non-resident recipient that has no PRC establishment, the service is delivered and consumed entirely outside mainland China, the contract substantiates the cross-border place of supply, and consideration is received from abroad and repatriated into China. Input VAT attributable to zero-rated supplies is refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued through China Customs single-window export clearance
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or international transport document evidencing physical export movement
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt/export proceeds voucher
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract and purchase order
  • Foreign exchange repatriation and settlement record from a licensed bank
  • For digital/software cloud zero-rating where no customs declaration exists: cross-border service contract, overseas recipient certificate, service delivery/log records, and bank receipt record

Mainland China mandates the use of the STA e-Tax Bureau or STA-certified electronic invoice platforms for all VAT invoices; every invoice must be issued with the national unified invoice code and anti-counterfeiting digital stamp/QR code. Golden Tax Phase IV receives invoice-level data in near-real time and cross-checks issuance with tax filings, so invoice creation, API transmission, and fiscal clearance are integrated. Any third-party invoicing software must meet STA encryption and digital-certificate API standards. Hong Kong does not impose an electronic fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate.

The STA and customs authorities cross-match VAT returns, export declarations, transport documents, contracts, and bank forex receipts. A zero-rating claim that lacks statutory substantiation will be retroactively reversed, the supply will be re-taxed at the applicable domestic VAT rate, input VAT refunds will be clawed back, daily late-payment surcharge will accrue at 0.05%, and a monetary penalty of between 50% and 500% of the underpaid VAT will be imposed under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export rebate claims are referred under Article 204 of the Criminal Law and can result in imprisonment, business license revocation, tax integrity blacklisting, and suspension of future export refund entitlements.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under PBOC/SAFE. Current-account receipts and payments, including software licensing, SaaS subscription fees, cloud deployment, and associated trade/technical services, are generally convertible once the bank verifies the underlying transaction against contracts, invoices, and the practical business purpose. Capital-account items are not freely convertible: inbound equity contributions, outward dividends, cross-border loans, and foreign currency conversions of equity capital require SAFE registration, use of a designated foreign-exchange capital account, and a compliant operational foundation. Under the Foreign Investment Law and applicable SAFE circulars, a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture may open a foreign currency capital account and convert approved capital into RMB at a designated bank for verified operating expenditure only. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder faces procedural hurdles rather than absolute prohibition: the JV must submit a board-approved profit distribution resolution, audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and a SAFE capital-account payment registration; the Hong Kong shareholder may apply for the 5% or 10% reduced withholding rate under the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement and the double tax arrangement if treaty eligibility is evidenced. Early exit, equity transfer, and liquidation proceeds are subject to strict capital-account verification and cannot be routed through current-account settlement. Export proceeds remain within SAFE trade monitoring, but current-account foreign exchange is increasingly liberalized; however, all RMB-FX transactions must be reported and cannot bypass designated bank channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current-account monitoring framework, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to the PRC financial system and reported in the enterprise's trade-in-goods monitoring records. The statutory timeline is driven by the documentary payment terms of the export contract and the bank's trade reconciliation cycle; in practice, a 90-day overdue exception will trigger scrutiny if the receivable has not been collected, and any collection tenor exceeding 180 days from the customs declaration requires documented justification and, if needed, SAFE filing. There is no fixed conversion ratio for an A-class enterprise: the JV may retain eligible export foreign currency in a designated foreign-exchange settlement account or convert it into RMB at its discretion. A B- or C-class enterprise, however, is subject to enhanced monitoring and may be directed by SAFE or the bank to settle 100% of designated export receipts through a monitored channel until its risk classification is restored.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the monetary authority for the Hong Kong SAR leg but does not override PRC capital controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the PRC regulated entity. Offshore digital EMIs, even those licensed by the FCA, HKMA (MSO or stored value facility), or other authorities, cannot open a PBOC-regulated corporate account for the PRC JV, cannot accept its export proceeds, cannot perform SAFE capital registration, and cannot provide legal CNY clearing or FX settlement. Such EMIs may only support ancillary spending or the Hong Kong holding company's US/HKD current account; they must not be used for inbound FDI, capital account, or statutory trade verification tasks. The JV must maintain its primary banking relationship with a PRC-licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The only compliant routing is a SWIFT MT103 or a CNY CNAPS/RTGS transfer (for RMB injection after SAFE approval) from the HK shareholder's licensed bank to the JV's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account. The remittance instruction must cite the SAFE FDI registration reference, the foreign exchange purpose/BOP declaration code for direct investment equity capital, and the underlying equity subscription agreement. The receiving bank will perform payment authentication, report the transaction into SAFE's capital account information system, and require the JV to obtain a PRC CPA capital verification report before the funds can be used. The JV may then convert the foreign currency into RMB at the designated bank for registered operating expenses; funds cannot be lent to affiliated entities or invested in securities without further regulatory approval.SWIFT MT103 is the default clearing rail for high-priority foreign-currency trade and service disbursements. For physical goods embedded in the software/cloud hardware bundle, a commercial letter of credit (SWIFT MT700 series) or documentary collection can be used, with documents negotiated through the PRC bank and HK advising bank. All outbound disbursements must be supported by the underlying agreement, commercial invoice, customs/tax filing evidence, and SAFE declaration codes; royalty or software fee outflows additionally require withholding tax payment or non-resident tax registration and, if treaty benefits are claimed, the relevant certificate of residence. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the PRC regulated entity. Offshore digital EMIs, even those licensed by the FCA, HKMA (MSO or stored value facility), or other authorities, cannot open a PBOC-regulated corporate account for the PRC JV, cannot accept its export proceeds, cannot perform SAFE capital registration, and cannot provide legal CNY clearing or FX settlement. Such EMIs may only support ancillary spending or the Hong Kong holding company's US/HKD current account; they must not be used for inbound FDI, capital account, or statutory trade verification tasks. The JV must maintain its primary banking relationship with a PRC-licensed commercial bank.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) rate; effective rates may be reduced to 15% for High-and-New Technology Enterprises or qualifying encouraged industries, and small low-profit enterprises may enjoy an effective rate of 5% on taxable income up to RMB 3,000,000

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid to a non-resident corporate shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Mainland and Hong Kong Special Administrative Region Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA) if the Hong Kong shareholder directly holds at least 25% of the equity capital of the Chinese paying enterprise throughout the 12 months preceding the dividend distribution

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: China's transfer pricing documentation thresholds (in RMB) trigger mandatory local file and master file preparation: Local File required if any one category of related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceeds - tangible property transfer RMB 20 million; intangible property transfer RMB 10 million; financial asset transfer RMB 10 million; provision of services RMB 40 million. Master File required if aggregate related-party turnover exceeds RMB 10 billion or the enterprise is a member of a multinational group that meets certain consolidated revenue thresholds.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form principle and a General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations. PRC tax authorities may re-characterize transactions that lack economic substance, disregard unreasonable corporate structures, and reassess income based on the substance of the activity. Although China does not apply the Offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) system used in tax havens, the concept of Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) is relevant to determining the source of income, permanent establishment attribution, and the beneficial ownership analysis under the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA. A PRC software enterprise must demonstrate real personnel, premises, and risk management functions in China to support its income allocation and to prevent a sham arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Mismatch and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 46; Implementation Regulations Articles 109-115; SAT Announcement No. 6 of 2017. Tax authority may make their own primary adjustment, impose a 10% late-payment surcharge, and issue penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax for negligence. In egregious cases, a 30% penalty on the adjusted tax amount is applied for lack of documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the deficient transfer pricing documentation face joint and several liability for the enterprise's unpaid tax and penalties to the extent they acted fraudulently or with gross negligence. The legal representative may be blacklisted for exit (travel ban) under the Administrative Measures on Arrangement for Tax-related Disputes; criminal liability for tax evasion may lead to imprisonment of 3-7 years, plus fines and confiscation of assets.
Fabrication of False VAT Invoices (虚开发票) and Falsifying Financial Records

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 (Falsely Making Out Special VAT Invoices) imposes fines up to RMB 5 million and imprisonment up to life imprisonment; Article 201 (Tax Evasion) imposes fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years. The Tax Administration Collection Law Article 63 provides the administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, financial officer, and any director who directed or knowingly benefited from the false invoicing are personally criminally liable, not shielded by the corporate veil. Courts may order asset restitution, property seizure, bans on serving as directors of any Chinese company, and exit restrictions. In cross-border cases, extradition arrangements may apply.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Non-Resident Tax on Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 - the withholding agent must pay the unwithheld tax and is fined 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount; Article 32 adds a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge. If the payment involved foreign exchange settlement, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose administrative penalties for unauthorized remittance.

Director personal liability: Directors who willfully failed to withhold or misrepresented the applicability of treaty relief are personally liable for the tax and fines. The legal representative may be subjected to a travel ban and placed on the tax collection blacklist; serious evasion or fraudulent treaty abuse may trigger a criminal investigation under Criminal Law Article 203, resulting in imprisonment, company disqualification and personal asset seizure.
