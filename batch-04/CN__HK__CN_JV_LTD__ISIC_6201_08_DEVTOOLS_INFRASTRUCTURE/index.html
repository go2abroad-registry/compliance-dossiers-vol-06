E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with Hong Kong participation: (1) incorporate the JV with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license, which carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) within 30 days of issuance of the business license, complete tax registration with the competent STA tax service bureau through the e-Tax portal or in-person service hall; (3) file the legal representative, financial, shareholding and business-scope particulars; (4) receive the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which for a corporate resident enterprise is the 18-character USCC; (5) for the Hong Kong non-resident shareholder that has no permanent establishment in China, no separate China TIN is generally required, but if the shareholder derives China-source income subject to withholding, it must register for a non-resident enterprise tax number through the e-Tax portal before filing the withholding return; (6) VAT registration is triggered by engaging in taxable sales and, for general taxpayer status, by expected annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000, and the VAT status must be recorded with the local tax bureau.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the current VAT legal framework and the cross-border taxable services policies (Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 and State Taxation Administration Announcement [2016] No. 29), export sales of goods are zero-rated, and qualifying cross-border software services, information-system services, database and API monitoring cloud services provided to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China may apply the 0% VAT rate. The JV must demonstrate that: the customer is a non-resident enterprise or individual, the service is performed and consumed entirely outside mainland China, the contractual and invoicing documents correspond to the actual service, and the consideration is received from overseas and repatriated through foreign exchange settlement within the statutory period. If any of these conditions is absent, the supply is standard-rated VAT (typically 6% for modern services) and output VAT must be collected.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical goods exported from China; for pure digital cloud and software services the contractual service delivery statement and export-service filing are used instead
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill for physical goods; for cloud services, server logs, API call records and technical delivery acceptance records evidencing overseas consumption
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE-compliant foreign exchange export collection and settlement receipt
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice (Fapiao) issued through the STA electronic invoice system and matching cross-border service contract
  • Overseas customer identity evidence, non-resident certificate or offshore purchasing entity details, and service-consumption certificate/performance reports

China operates the nationwide e-Fapiao electronic VAT invoicing system under the STA VAT Invoice Management System and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform. Invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled electronic invoice service platform or approved API integrations before delivery to customers; each invoice carries a unique digital anti-counterfeit code and tax-authority digital signature; data is transmitted to the tax authority in near real-time, enabling automatic reconciliation, billing-pattern monitoring, and anti-fraud inspection. Physical stamping is not required for FDEI, but legacy paper-based VAT special invoices still require the tax-control seal from the tax authority.

The STA, together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), will verify export zero-rating claims against customs, shipping, invoicing, contract and foreign-exchange settlement records. If documentation is missing, inconsistent or fraudulent, the tax authority may claw back the zero-rated treatment, re-apply the standard VAT rate (13% for goods or 6% for services), require repayment of input VAT credits, and impose daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05% from the original due date. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, administrative fines range from 50% to five times the underpaid tax; where export zero-rating is claimed by falsifying export documents or foreign-exchange receipts, criminal liability for fraudulent export refund or tax evasion may arise, and retrospective recovery is not time-barred in cases of fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict foreign exchange controls, administered by SAFE under the delegation of the PBoC. Current account transactions are generally convertible after documentary verification, but capital account transactions remain heavily regulated and require case-by-case approval, registration, or filing with local SAFE branches. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established in Hong Kong but with Chinese operational capital flows, the critical conversion hurdles arise when injecting equity, repatriating dividends, or settling trade-related disbursements. Surrender requirements mandate that exporters convert foreign exchange receipts into RMB within a statutory timeline unless a genuine hedging or retention exemption is granted. Profit remittance from China is permissible only after annual audit confirmation, tax clearance, and submission of a corresponding foreign exchange payment application, with further scrutiny when the distributing entity is a joint venture with offshore shareholders. The onshore entity must maintain a SAFE-designated capital account for equity injections and a separate routine foreign exchange settlement account for operational receipts. Any cross-border loan or intercompany transfer must comply with SAFE's macro-prudential parameters on foreign debt, including the 'all-round cross-border financing macro-prudential regulation' formula, which caps the external borrowing capacity based on the entity's net assets and a risk-weighted factor. Dividends are generally remittable once audited financial statements and tax payment declarations are submitted, but the joint venture charter must expressly document prior approval rights and the currency of conversion. Because Hong Kong operates a separate free-economy monetary regime under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), in practice the JV should pivot operational liquidity through HK treasury entities to avoid unnecessary onshore conversion frictions, while keeping onshore RMB balances only for local wage, rent, and tax obligations. This dual-center approach reduces surrender pressure and enables smoother clearing for cloud infrastructure subscriptions sold to global customers who pay in USD, EUR, or HKD.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated to China and settled into RMB within a statutory 90-day window from the date of customs declaration or service completion invoice unless a SAFE retention approval, netting arrangement, or cross-border pooling exemption is obtained. Mandatory conversion applies to proceeds under the current account once documentary verification is complete; the ratio is generally 100% surrender to the domestic foreign exchange settlement system, although JV entities can apply to retain balances in designated foreign exchange accounts if the funds are earmarked for documented future import payments, hedging margins, or debt service. Failure to comply generates a 'Foreign Exchange Receipt Verification' exception that blocks future remittance applications and triggers administrative penalties.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is Medium-Low. Hong Kong-licensed fintech EMIs such as Mox, WeLab Bank, and ZA Bank can provide multi-currency accounts and payment rails, but they are legally barred from performing the capital verification, SAFE registration filing, and export proceeds surrender duties that only a physical SAFE-designated onshore bank branch can complete for the Chinese leg of the transaction. The joint venture must therefore maintain at least one tier-1 Chinese commercial bank account in the Mainland for SAFE reporting and capital verification, while an HK EMI may serve only as a settlement overlay for collected cloud subscription revenues. Reliance on offshore EMI-only banking would cause the cross-border capital injection to fail at the local SAFE pre-registration checkpoint.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route is a SWIFT MT103 wire from the Hong Kong treasury account to the Mainland JV's SAFE-designated capital escrow account, with the payment narrative containing the exact SAFE foreign exchange registration number, the purpose code for 'Investment in an equity joint venture', and the unique FDI verification code. The remitting and receiving banks must both confirm that the funds are traceable and unencumbered; the onshore bank then submits the capital verification report to the local SAFE office. Any share subscription amount above the registered capital threshold triggers a separate capital increase filing. For initial registered capital, the JV must first obtain a Foreign Investment Catering Business Notification or its applicable FDI notice code, open a provisional account before the regulatory approval is fully granted, and later convert it into the permanent RMB capital account. The total inbound amount cannot exceed the verified registered equity subscription, and once verified, the funds may be swept into the general RMB expense account only for audited business purposes such as payroll, rental, and cloud infrastructure vendor payments.For recurring global cloud infrastructure subscription payments and software licensing fees, the JV should clear disbursements through Hong Kong accounts using SWIFT MT103, Commercial Letters of Credit, or Documentary Collections as required by the vendor. Because cloud infrastructure licences are intangible, the service import under RMB is not eligible for a documentary credit; therefore, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 with a service contract, invoice, and mandatory non-trade foreign exchange payment registration. Multi-currency funding should be swept from the HK treasury account through a concentration account to avoid unnecessary export surrender losses. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million; intangible property or financial asset transactions exceed RMB 100 million; service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions in the year exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting applies only if the group's consolidated revenue in the preceding year equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion (approximately EUR 750 million).

Economic substance scope: China does not apply Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but it implements an equivalent standard through the beneficial-ownership test (Announcement 2018 No. 9), the general anti-abuse rule (EIT Law Article 47), and BEPS Action 6/8-derived principles. The Chinese JV must demonstrate real operational substance through the core income-generating activities of software development, server maintenance, data security, and client support located in China. The Hong Kong parent must independently demonstrate management, staff, premises, and risk absorption to claim treaty benefits; if the HK company is a pure mail-box entity with no CIGA, the tax authority may look through it and recharacterize dividends as taxable profits of the ultimate Chinese owners.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Dividend WHT Evasion by Mischaracterizing Distribution as Intercompany Loan

Statutory sanction: Withholding agent liability under Article 69 of the PRC Tax Administration Law: the JV must pay the un-withheld 10% tax, plus a fine of 50% to 300% of the unpaid WHT. If the disguised fee was also deducted, the tax deficiency, daily surcharge, and a 50% to 500% fraud penalty under Article 63 TAL will be assessed.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved the sham loan can be held personally and severally liable under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law (corporate veil piercing) for tax liabilities arising from abuse of corporate personality. The legal representative and CFO may face criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment of up to 7 years, fines, and confiscation of assets for evasion exceeding RMB 500,000. Travel bans, asset freezes, and exit blacklisting under Article 44 TAL are routinely applied to responsible officers.
Artificial Transfer Pricing of Cloud Software Royalties

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may reallocate profits under EIT Law Article 41 and make a special tax adjustment under Article 114, imposing an extra interest rate of 5 percentage points above the base rate from the date the tax was due until the adjustment date (Announcement 2017 No. 6). If the mispricing is intentional evasion, Article 63 TAL imposes a 50% to 500% penalty on the tax deficit.

Director personal liability: The directors who signed the intercompany agreements and approved the intangible migration are exposed to piercing liability under Company Law Article 20 for harm caused to the company and creditors; the tax bureau can also issue an exit ban under TAL Article 44. In cases where the underpaid tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the legally payable tax, the direct responsible persons risk criminal charges under Criminal Law Article 201, with sentences of up to 7 years for large-scale evasion.
Failure to File Related-Party Disclosures and Shell-Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Failure to file the related-party schedule triggers a RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 fine per form under TAL Article 62, plus a special tax adjustment that may deem the transaction non-arm's length. The tax authority may impose an additional documentation penalty of RMB 20,000 to RMB 500,000 under the Special Tax Adjustment Measures and deny treaty benefits under Announcement 2018 No. 9, resulting in a full 10% WHT and interest.

Director personal liability: The directors can be held civilly liable for the unpaid taxes and surcharges under Company Law Article 20 for using the shell to evade tax, subjecting their personal assets to restitution and freezing orders. The legal representative may be placed on the border-control blacklist and prohibited from leaving China until the tax deficiency is settled. For intentional concealment of cross-border income, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 can lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years, criminal fines, and a prohibition on serving as a company director in the PRC.
