VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT-taxable turnover, which is the mandatory threshold above which general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory; a PRC-incorporated entity must complete basic tax/VAT registration upon incorporation regardless of turnover. For a small-scale taxpayer below the threshold, the applicable collection rate is 3% unless general taxpayer status is voluntarily elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under PRC VAT where the supply is a cross-border taxable service in the zero-rated service catalogue (including information system, software, and related cybersecurity SaaS services), the counterparty/beneficiary is outside mainland China, the service is delivered and consumed entirely outside China, and the consideration is received from outside China in foreign currency and repatriated through banking channels. For goods exports, zero-rating applies where the goods are physically exported and cleared by PRC Customs. If the qualifying conditions are satisfied, VAT is charged at 0% and the supplier may claim input VAT credit or refund; absent zero-rating, the cybersecurity SaaS supply would be taxable at 6% (or 3% under small-scale taxpayer status).

The STA mandates issuance of fully digitalized electronic invoices (digital e-invoices) through the official e-invoice service platform, integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV (Tax Control and Anti-Fraud Digital Stamp) system. Tax-encrypted invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time; medium and large taxpayers use API-based reconciliation with the STA platform. Zero-rated export supplies must be invoiced at 0% through the official e-invoice system, and the supporting export documentation package must be retained for real-time tax office inspection and retrospective verification.

The STA and customs authorities can retrospectively reassess any zero-rated export treatment where the statutory documentation or foreign-exchange repatriation conditions are not met. The taxpayer would be required to repay the VAT forgone at the applicable rate (6% for SaaS, 13% for goods) with late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date, plus an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is fully convertible for current-account transactions (trade in goods and services), consistent with IMF Article VIII, but capital-account transactions remain tightly administered. For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture with Hong Kong participation, a bifurcated operational reality applies. On the onshore side, RMB convertibility is processed through banks under SAFE’s Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, requiring authentic underlying documents (contracts, invoices, customs filings) for each settlement. Offshore, in Hong Kong, RMB (CNH) is freely convertible, creating a natural cross-border treasury corridor. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder require audited financial statements, tax payment proofs, and a certificate of foreign exchange registration for profit repatriation; dividends may not be remitted before the annual audit and required corporate tax filing. Although the mandatory surrender of export proceeds has been abolished, export receivables must be reported via the bank’s trade monitoring system and cannot be held indefinitely outside China without nexus verification. Capital contributions must be registered with SAFE and can be converted into RMB only for declared business purposes. Capital reductions or withdrawals require approval from the Ministry of Commerce or SAFE, and full liquidation is subject to exit audit. Therefore, the treasury function must treat current-account flows as relatively liberal but capital flows as consent-based and document-intensive.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has eliminated the mandatory surrender of export foreign exchange earnings. Exporters may retain 100% of eligible foreign currency receipts in a current-account bank account. The only statutory requirement is to deposit the receivables with a domestic designated bank within a reasonable time (normally upon receipt) and to report the transaction electronically through the bank’s foreign exchange monitoring system (usually within 5 working days). The funds can then be either left in the original currency or settled into RMB at the exporter’s discretion. For companies rated B or C by SAFE under the trade foreign exchange audit classification, stricter supervision applies, including documentary review and, for B-class, validation of customs records within 30 days of shipment, and for C-class, a shorter settlement and payment timeline. For cross-border services, the underlying service contract and delivery evidence replace customs declarations. No specified minimum conversion ratio is imposed for cyber security SaaS exports.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability for any fintech electronic money institution (EMI) to act as the primary corporate bank for this onshore Chinese joint venture. The PRC central bank and SAFE strictly allocate foreign-exchange settlement and capital-account custody functions to licensed banking institutions. Non-bank EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot lawfully provide an onshore capital escrow account or a regulated current-account settlement vehicle for a domestic JV whose principal place of business is mainland China. Even though the jurisdiction tag ‘HK’ suggests an offshore holding layer, the operational entity must open its statutory RMB and foreign-currency accounts at a licensed PRC bank. EMIs can be used only as auxiliary collection vehicles for overseas SaaS subscription payments (e.g. Stripe’s payment gateway), but the funds must ultimately be settled through a Chinese commercial bank and reported to SAFE. Therefore, any treasury blueprint that relies on EMI accounts for primary liquidity or capital custody would be non-compliant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must first ensure that the JV has completed the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) onboarding registration and obtained the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate. Once the certificate is in place, an inbound telegraphic transfer (SWIFT MT103) or bank draft can be sent to the JV’s foreign-currency capital account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. The payment narrative must clearly state ‘Investment (code 101)’ as the purpose. On receipt, the domestic bank will require the presentation of the registration certificate and the investment agreement to credit the account. The funds may be kept in HKD/USD or converted to RMB only for ordinary business purposes; the conversion must be accompanied by an underlying transaction document. Within 30 days of receipt, the company must procure a certified public accountant’s capital verification report and file it with the local SAFE office. No local FinTech EMI is allowed to touch these funds onshore.For international software subscription sales, the main settlement rail is the SWIFT MT103 message received into the JV’s current-account foreign-currency bank account. The JV should issue professional invoices (proforma, commercial, or recurring SaaS statements) to overseas clients. Each receipt must be reconciled against a contract or service order and reported to the bank under the service trade category. Payments to external overseas vendors (e.g., cloud hosting providers) can be routed via outbound SWIFT MT103 or, if the vendor opens a local RMB account, through cross-border RMB clearing (CIPS). A commercial letter of credit is not a natural fit for monthly SaaS subscriptions but can be used for one-off enterprise licence agreements with a deployment milestone. Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) are generally not suitable for cloud services and are not recommended unless a physical device is part of the deliverables.Deliverable forward FX contracts (deliverable foreign exchange settlement and sale) - permitted for hedging current-account exposures; non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited for onshore corporate treasury use.FX swaps - permissible to manage temporary mismatches between RMB liquidity and foreign-currency obligation dates, provided the underlying matches real commercial cash flows.FX options - allowed under the bank’s master derivatives agreement for capping currency risk on forecast recurring revenues; premiums and payoffs must be settled within China and reported for SAFE inspection.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for any fintech electronic money institution (EMI) to act as the primary corporate bank for this onshore Chinese joint venture. The PRC central bank and SAFE strictly allocate foreign-exchange settlement and capital-account custody functions to licensed banking institutions. Non-bank EMIs such as Wise, Payoneer, or Airwallex cannot lawfully provide an onshore capital escrow account or a regulated current-account settlement vehicle for a domestic JV whose principal place of business is mainland China. Even though the jurisdiction tag ‘HK’ suggests an offshore holding layer, the operational entity must open its statutory RMB and foreign-currency accounts at a licensed PRC bank. EMIs can be used only as auxiliary collection vehicles for overseas SaaS subscription payments (e.g. Stripe’s payment gateway), but the funds must ultimately be settled through a Chinese commercial bank and reported to SAFE. Therefore, any treasury blueprint that relies on EMI accounts for primary liquidity or capital custody would be non-compliant.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for related-party tangible property transactions; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers or financial asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions, including services, royalties, and interest. Master File is required when the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion and the Chinese entity meets the Local File preparation threshold.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code. However, under Article 47 of the EIT Law and SAT Announcement 2019 No. 35, the Hong Kong shareholder must demonstrate genuine economic substance and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong to claim China-HK DTA benefits. A shell or conduit entity without real staff, decision-making, or business activity in Hong Kong will be denied treaty relief and may be re-characterized under general anti-abuse rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Cross-Border Transfer Pricing Misreporting

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may impute arm's length profits under Article 41 of the EIT Law and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, assess supplementary CIT at 25%, and charge interest at the benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points per year under Article 48. Failing to file or complete related party returns attracts penalties under Article 44 of the Tax Administration Law of RMB 2,000 to RMB 50,000. If fraudulent misreporting constitutes tax evasion, a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax is imposed under Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who knowingly executed the false transfer pricing scheme may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of underpaid tax. The company and responsible individuals may be entered on the joint dishonesty tax payer blacklist, resulting in exit-entry control and travel bans. Criminal prosecution under Articles 200 and 201 of the Criminal Law can lead to fines and imprisonment of up to seven years for the directly responsible personnel.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company Beneficial Ownership Sham

Statutory sanction: Treaty benefits are denied under Article 4 of the China-HK Double Taxation Arrangement and SAT Announcement 2019 No. 35. The General Anti-Abuse Rule under Article 47 of the EIT Law may re-characterize the dividend; the under-withheld 5% difference between treaty and statutory rates becomes instantly payable, with daily 0.05% late surcharge and penalties of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax under Articles 32 and 63 of the Tax Administration Law. False beneficial ownership declarations also attract RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000 administrative fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified false beneficiary statements and executed dividend payments at the reduced rate are personally exposed to civil asset restitution for the underpaid withholding tax. They may be blacklisted as seriously dishonest taxpayers, triggering travel bans, credit constraints, and exit control. Where the sham is characterized as intentional tax evasion, criminal imprisonment of up to seven years may apply to the responsible board members and compliance officers.
Evasion of Dividend Withholding Through Disguised Royalty or Management Fee Payments

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will disallow the false deductions, recharacterize the payments as distributions, impose the applicable dividend withholding tax, and levy penalties. Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, the penalty is 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. If false VAT invoices are issued, additional penalties arise under the VAT invoice regulation and Criminal Law Article 205, including significant fines and potential imprisonment for revenue evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties; the corporate veil may be pierced in civil execution if company assets are diverted or hidden. Travel bans and business exit controls may be imposed under the dishonest taxpayer blacklisting mechanism. Criminal liability includes fines and imprisonment of up to seven years for the individuals who designed or signed the fraudulent payment scheme, plus personal asset confiscation or restitution orders.
