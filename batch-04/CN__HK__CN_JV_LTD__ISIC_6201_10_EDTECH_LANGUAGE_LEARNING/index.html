Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) for PRC national tax administration; primary e-filing portal is the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform. Hong Kong SAR tax matters fall under the Inland Revenue Department with the separate eTax system, but the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is predominantly a PRC resident taxpayer.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the State Administration for Market Regulation issues a unified social credit code (USCC) that automatically serves as the PRC taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax, VAT, and all other PRC taxes. The foreign non-resident shareholder (e.g., a Hong Kong investor) that receives PRC-source income without a permanent establishment must apply for a non-resident enterprise tax identification number through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau non-resident registration module or through the designated withholding agent (typically the JV). The application must include the entity formation certificate, board/shareholder resolution, apostilled or notarised incorporation documents, and, where treaty benefits are claimed under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, a current tax residency certificate issued by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods exports, zero-rated VAT applies when goods are physically shipped out of China Customs territory, filed on the China Customs Export Goods Declaration, and sold to an overseas buyer. For qualifying cross-border digital services, including EdTech language-learning and automated tutoring software supplied to non-resident enterprises and consumed entirely outside China, VAT zero-rating or exemption may apply under the cross-border taxable services catalogue established by CaiShui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement No. 5 of 2016. The supplier must demonstrate that the recipient is non-resident, the service is wholly consumed offshore, consideration is received in foreign exchange (including through Apple IAP or Google Play Billing where the underlying user is non-resident), and the records separately account for zero-rated, exempt, and taxable supplies.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (CCD) — customs electronic manifest submitted via the China International Trade Single Window, identifying the exporter, HS classification, value, destination, and export contract number
  • Original transport document evidencing physical departure from China — Ocean Bill of Lading, Master Air Waybill, or International Railway Waybill, as applicable
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list reflecting the declared export value, Incoterms, and destination
  • Foreign exchange receipt and verification record issued by the PRC designated foreign-exchange bank under the SAFE cross-border trade monitoring system, evidencing inbound remittance of export proceeds (the historical Inward Remittance Certificate is superseded by this electronic SAFE record)
  • Executed export contract or order with the overseas buyer including payment terms, delivery terms, and platform-specific distribution agreement for EdTech digital exports
  • STA export tax refund declaration package generated through the export tax refund module, reconciling the invoice, customs, and foreign exchange data

China mandates the issuance of Full Digital Electronic Invoices (Digital e-Invoices) through the STA Electronic Invoice Platform, with real-time transmission to the national sequential database and mandatory digital stamp and QR-code anti-fraud identifiers. VAT special invoices must be cleared and reconciled through the STA VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform before input tax credits can be claimed; offline manual invoice countersigning is phased out for digitised taxpayers.

A false or unsubstantiated export zero-rating claim is treated as export refund fraud: the entire reclaimed or zero-rated VAT amount is retrospectively clawed back, late payment interest accrues at 0.05% per day, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax amount is imposed under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Where forged customs declarations or false VAT invoices are involved, criminal liability for fraudulent VAT refund and export rebate fraud under the Criminal Law of the PRC applies, with penalties ranging from substantial fines to life imprisonment. Tax authorities may audit historical filings for up to the statutory limitation period, and the taxpayer's Customs credit rating will be downgraded, export refund reviews suspended, and future zero-rating claims subjected to enhanced desk or field audit verification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR operates as an offshore special territory with fully convertible HKD, zero surrender rules, and no statutory distinction between current and capital account transactions. HKD is freely floated against the US dollar within the HKMA-backed Linked Exchange Rate system (7.75-7.85 band). Profit remittance, dividend distribution, and capital repatriation from a Hong Kong entity are unrestricted and do not require central bank approval, provided corporate governance and tax filing obligations are met. However, the material exposure arises from the mainland Chinese parent and the underlying Sino-Foreign JV structures. Onshore China maintains a hierarchical current-account / capital-account regime. Current account items, such as Edenred trade payments or royalty fees, require genuine transaction verification through bank systems and SAFE 'current account verification process'. Capital account items, including inbound equity injection, shareholder loans, and outbound ODI remittances from the Chinese parent to the HK treasury vehicle, require SAFE registration, certification by auditors for capital verification, and specific purpose code declarations (e.g., 6201 for initial equity, 6202 for capital increase). Surrender requirements in China no longer mandate a fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds after 2012, but all export forex receipts must be repatriated onshore and processed through designated SAFE bank systems within a generally accepted 90-day window from customs declaration. For an EdTech business monetising through Apple IAP and Google Play, the critical treasury obstacle is the mismatch between offshore digital collection rails (USD/HKD in Hong Kong trust accounts) and the need to remit convertibly into mainland RMB for tax, payroll, and infrastructure costs. A Sino-Foreign JV must complete SAFE 'FDI registration' and maintain an audit trail showing the HKD capital inflow matched by an asset or business substance in China. Dividends from the Chinese operating JV to the HK shareholder require an annual audit, a board resolution, tax clearance from Chinese tax authorities, and a SAFE dividend remittance certificate. Without these, banks will block repatriation. The HK entity itself, being outside the PRC FX perimeter, may hold any currency and freely hedge via HKMA-approved licensed banks, but all cross-border RMB dependency introduces SAFE scrutiny. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must dual-track: maintain full HKD/USD liquidity in Hong Kong for operational flexibility, while pre-clearing each RMB movement through PBOC/SAFE-compliant bank channels with evidence-backed purpose codes and capital registration.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR: No surrender requirement and no conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in any currency without mandatory exchange. Mainland China: Export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account within 90 days of customs declaration; mandatory conversion has been abolished, but SAFE verification and bank-based settlement are required, and any retained forex is subject to false-trade scrutiny. For the Sino-Foreign JV dividend remittance, the corresponding rules require audited financials, full PRC corporate income tax settlement, and SAFE 'FDI Dividend Remittance' registration before outward wire.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for mainland China capital account controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-Medium for this specific Sino-HK JV structure. Hong Kong itself is permissive and allows licensed Stored Value Facility (SVF) operators and Money Service Operators (MSOs) to service local corporates for operational cash flows, particularly for Apple IAP and Google Play settlement netting. Fintech EMIs like Airwallex, Nium, and Currenxie hold HK MA licenses and can sponsor accounts for the HK treasury vehicle, enabling fast multi-currency collection and payout of invoice payments and marketing costs. However, those EMIs are generally not recognized by SAFE as authorized banks for FDI capital contribution verification, overseas direct investment (ODI) remittance, or settlement of Chinese current account obligations. The mainland Chinese leg and any eventual RMB conversion must be routed through a Tier-1 physical banking relationship because SAFE requires electronic product connection to the bank's Foreign Exchange Control Regulation system (SAFE 'good bank' network). Additionally, the JV's ownership structure, with mainland UBOs, triggers heightened anti-money-laundering (AML) scrutiny, and many EMIs refuse to open accounts where the ultimate beneficial owner is located in China or where funds originate from a Chinese parent without a physical mainland bank endorsement. As such, fintech EMIs should be used only for the low-risk offshore collection rails (Apple/Google payout, vendor payments in USD/EUR) and never for the critical capital injection or dividend repatriation pathways. For permanent cross-border banking, we mandate primary HSBC or BOCHK accounts and keep the EMI exposure capped below HKD 5 million monthly turnover.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from the Chinese parent to the HK treasury vehicle must follow the SAFE ODI path: (1) The Chinese parent obtains approval from SAFE via the local branch for overseas direct investment, and the HK entity is registered as the target. (2) The injection is made via SWIFT MT103 using purpose code 'Overseas Direct Investment - Equity (6201)' for capital or 'Shareholder Loan (621254)' for debt. (3) The receiving HK bank (HSBC or BOCHK) must be instructed that the transaction is a capital remittance from a PRC entity, triggering automatic cross-reference to the SAFE ODI certificate furnished in the bank mandate. (4) The HK company verifies receipt via a bank credit advice and issues a Board Resolution confirming capital acceptance. (5) For the reverse flow (HK entity investing in a mainland operating JV), the HK company must complete FDI registration with SAFE through a designated PRC bank, and the bank will issue a Foreign Exchange Business Confirmation Form (FDI F) before the HKD is converted to RMB and credited to the JV's capital account. (6) All capital verification requires a Chinese CPA issuance of a capital verification report within 20 business days of the USD/HKD transfer. For EdTech operating revenue from Apple IAP and Google Play, typical settlement arrives in USD/HKD into the HK entity's account; those inflows should be segregated into a 'Revenue Proceeds' ledger and can be freely used for HK operating expenses or further invested, but any sale of IP to mainland affiliates or royalty payments requires a separate SAFE current-account registration and a Chinese tax withholding payment certificate. We recommend entering into an inter-company cross-border service agreement with clearly documented pricing to justify the transfer of revenue from China to Hong Kong, using purpose code '922990' or 'Service Trade' on outbound MT103s.For trade settlements and operating payments, the HK treasury operation should execute the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 Urgent for all vendor, payroll, and cloud infrastructure payments above HKD 100,000, using standard MT103 fields with the beneficiary bank BIC, reference text, and purpose. For mainland third-party vendors, the payment must be denominated in USD/HKD to an offshore account when permissible, or settled directly in RMB via BOCHK or HSBC's RMB clearing channel under the same MT103 with CN01 type code. (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (LCs) are not standard for a subscription software business, but if the JV purchases hardware packs or implements physical language-learning centers, a sight LC is acceptable; the issuing bank will require a signed LC instruction and underlying supply contract. (3) Documentary Collections (D/P and D/A) may be used only for niche trade flows, recognized in the governance framework but discouraged due to manual processing. (4) For Apple/Google settlement, the treasury must set up direct payout accounts through the platforms' financial APIs; funds are credited to a standard corporate bank account with a daily reconciliation file; no third-party piggyback accounts should be allowed. (5) Cross-border disbursements of dividends from HK to Chinese shareholders must use SWIFT MT202 for beneficial transfer, but only after a formal dividend declaration, audited accounts, and PRC withholding tax clearance if the shareholder is a mainland entity. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-to-Medium for this specific Sino-HK JV structure. Hong Kong itself is permissive and allows licensed Stored Value Facility (SVF) operators and Money Service Operators (MSOs) to service local corporates for operational cash flows, particularly for Apple IAP and Google Play settlement netting. Fintech EMIs like Airwallex, Nium, and Currenxie hold HK MA licenses and can sponsor accounts for the HK treasury vehicle, enabling fast multi-currency collection and payout of invoice payments and marketing costs. However, those EMIs are generally not recognized by SAFE as authorized banks for FDI capital contribution verification, overseas direct investment (ODI) remittance, or settlement of Chinese current account obligations. The mainland Chinese leg and any eventual RMB conversion must be routed through a Tier-1 physical banking relationship because SAFE requires electronic product connection to the bank's Foreign Exchange Control Regulation system (SAFE 'good bank' network). Additionally, the JV's ownership structure, with mainland UBOs, triggers heightened anti-money-laundering (AML) scrutiny, and many EMIs refuse to open accounts where the ultimate beneficial owner is located in China or where funds originate from a Chinese parent without a physical mainland bank endorsement. As such, fintech EMIs should be used only for the low-risk offshore collection rails (Apple/Google payout, vendor payments in USD/EUR) and never for the critical capital injection or dividend repatriation pathways. For permanent cross-border banking, we mandate primary HSBC or BOCHK accounts and keep the EMI exposure capped below HKD 5 million monthly turnover.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation obligations apply when the multinational enterprise (MNE) group's consolidated revenue exceeds HKD 750,000,000. The Hong Kong entity must prepare a Local File if its controlled transactions exceed: (a) HKD 400,000,000 for goods; (b) HKD 110,000,000 for services, finance, or intangibles; (c) HKD 44,000,000 for all other transactions. Even below these thresholds, the entity must exercise reasonable efforts to comply with the arm's length principle and maintain basic records.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the territorial source concept and the foreign-sourced income exemption (FSIE) regime (effective 1 January 2023) require a Hong Kong resident company to demonstrate sufficient economic substance to maintain tax exemption on offshore passive income (dividends, interest, IP income, and disposal gains). The entity must perform Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong through employees and premises, and for IP income, apply the nexus rule. Additionally, the IRD's anti-abuse rules deny treaty benefits and offshore exemption where there is no real commercial substance. For EdTech operations, activities such as strategic management, contractual negotiation with licensors, and holder-of-risk functions should be exercised in Hong Kong; merely booking revenue through Hong Kong without local substantive activity is a red flag.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Offshore Income Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) sections 82 and 82A. On summary conviction, a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; where taxed under charged, the court may order payment of three times the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue a tax assessment with a 100% uplift for fraud or wilful evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause or permit the filing of false returns may be prosecuted personally under IRO section 82A. The court may impose a travel ban (stop-departure order) under section 77A, freezing the director's assets pending payment. Directors can be held personally liable for the company's unpaid tax under section 74 if the company is non-resident or is likely to leave Hong Kong. Civil recoveries include restitution of undercharged tax, penalties, and costs, and on criminal conviction imprisonment up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: IRO sections 50AAP, 51C, 51D, and 58. The IRD may disregard the non-arm's length transaction and recharacterise profits, leading to additional assessments up to 100% of the undercharged tax plus interest. Failure to produce documentation without reasonable excuse incurs a penalty of HKD 50,000, with a daily default penalty of HKD 500. Where negligence or wilful default is established, penalties may reach three times the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors participating in or knowingly approving non-arm's length arrangements may be jointly and severally liable for the tax attributable to the adjustment if the company becomes insolvent. Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a director who abets fraudulent trading (using the company to evade tax) is liable for the company's debts and may be disqualified from acting as director for up to 15 years. Criminal liability attaches under IRO section 82A where the transfer pricing activities constitute tax evasion, punishable by imprisonment up to 3 years.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Royalties/Interest

Statutory sanction: IRO sections 15(1)(b), 20(1), 21A, and 80. The payer is personally liable to pay the tax unpaid by the non-resident, even if the non-resident cannot be assessed. The IRD may assess the payer as agent for the non-resident. Additional penalties of up to HKD 100,000 and three times the unremitted tax may be imposed, plus daily penalties for continued failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who have actual control of the company's payment processes and knowingly fail to withhold are personally liable for the withheld tax amount. The IRD may issue a stop-departure order (section 77A) to prevent the director from leaving Hong Kong until the tax is settled. Where the omission is fraudulent, directors face criminal prosecution and imprisonment up to 3 years, and may have personal bank accounts traced and garnished for recovery.
