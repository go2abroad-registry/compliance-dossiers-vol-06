Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with electronic services through the IRD eTAX portal (GovHK eTAX) and the Companies Registry e-Registry/ICRIS for entity incorporation and non-Hong Kong company registration.

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong incorporated limited company, file incorporation with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance and apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance within one month of commencement of business; the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the statutory tax identifier used by the Inland Revenue Department. A foreign non-resident entity that establishes a place of business or begins carrying on business in Hong Kong must register under Part 11 of the Companies Ordinance as a registered non-Hong Kong company and must likewise obtain a Business Registration Certificate for its Hong Kong permanent establishment, which will be issued the BRN and assigned an Inland Revenue Department file number for Profits Tax assessment. There is no separate VAT/GST registration because Hong Kong levies no VAT/GST/sales tax.

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales tax regime in Hong Kong SAR; selective indirect taxes are limited to stamp duty and dutiable commodities duty.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable to the Hong Kong tax jurisdiction because no VAT/GST/sales tax is imposed on Hong Kong supplies or exports. If the underlying China supply chain claims export VAT zero-rating, eligibility arises only for VAT-general-taxpayer exporters whose goods are physically exported or whose specified offshore services are supplied to non-resident customers and consumed outside mainland China, with foreign-currency or offshore RMB consideration received and reported to the State Taxation Administration.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt proof

No real-time fiscal clearance, e-invoicing API, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists for indirect taxes in Hong Kong. The Inland Revenue Ordinance requires taxpayers to keep adequate books and records in English or Chinese for at least seven years; eTAX is used for tax return filing but is not a transactional e-invoicing clearance system.

Hong Kong has no VAT/GST clawback. For mainland China export VAT zero-rating, tax authorities will deny or claw back refunded VAT and charge late-payment surcharge where customs declarations, transport documents, receipts of export proceeds, or digital service delivery evidence are false or unsubstantiated; administrative fines and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law may apply, with responsible individuals subject to imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

This assessment covers the Hong Kong SAR jurisdiction and its integration with the Chinese sovereign. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible under the HKMA currency board mechanism, which maintains full convertibility for current account and capital account transactions. There are no surrender requirements, no profit remittance permits, and no quantitative limits on foreign currency holdings for entities incorporated in Hong Kong; dividends and capital returns may be freely paid to shareholders abroad. Conversely, all RMB transfers crossing the China/Hong Kong boundary are regulated by the PBOC and SAFE. On the current account, payments for app development services, IP royalties, and digital advertising must be supported by genuine transaction evidence and, where exceeding the threshold, by SAFE-approved tax payment certificates. On the capital account, the injection of Chinese onshore equity into a Hong Kong Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must be routed via the ODI / FDI registration mechanism, with an offshore capital verification report. The main profit remittance hurdle from the Chinese side is the obligation to prove commercial substance and to file withholding taxes under the China-Hong Kong double tax arrangement. Since the entity is in Hong Kong, the actual legal remittance barrier is absent; the only friction arises when funds must be converted back into onshore RMB for payment of Chinese suppliers, at which point the convertibility assessment is governed by SAFE genuine-transactions doctrine and the use of the CNH offshore market for hedging.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion. A Hong Kong exporter may retain all foreign exchange revenue indefinitely in any currency account and can deploy it without central bank approval. However, if the entity also originates export proceeds from mainland-committed activities under the parent JV, PRC SAFE rules require those proceeds to be repatriated to a Chinese-licensed bank within 30 days of collection; since the mandatory conversion ratio was set to zero in 2017, the exporter may choose to retain the foreign currency in an approved foreign exchange settlement account rather than convert to RMB, subject to filing the export collection verification form.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong dollar and the Linked Exchange Rate System; People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for cross-border RMB and China-side payment controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

In Hong Kong, fintech electronic money institutions are legally permitted to offer digital corporate wallets under the HKMA Stored Value Facility regime, but they cannot act as the sole banking rail for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture engaged in physical capital verification and cross-border equity flows. The HKMA does not bar fintech EMIs from servicing this entity, yet the use of such platforms is only feasible for low-value disbursements, rental payments, and collection from app stores. Most EMIs cannot provide the SWIFT MT103 risk-control narration required for SAFE and ODI compliance, and their account statements are not accepted by auditors for capital verification. Recommendation: adopt a tier-1 licensed bank as the primary treasury system and use an authorized fintech EMI for non-critical operational expenses.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound equity funding should be executed by wire transmission from the PRC shareholder via a SWIFT MT103 message to the HK entity escrow account at a tier-1 bank. The remitting bank in mainland China must first obtain the SAFE ODI record for the overseas investment; for USD wires, the instruction must indicate the payment purpose code related to direct investment, such as equity investment in an HK project, along with the SAFE business registration number and the tax filing certificate for cross-border payments. The receiving HK bank will request the share allotment resolution, a statutory declaration of good standing, and the stamped company formation documents. Within 15 working days of receipt, the entity must submit a capital verification report issued by a Hong Kong certified public accountant confirming that the subscription monies are reconciled with the allotment register. If the injection is in offshore RMB, the instruction should be routed through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) using the code for direct investment remittances, and the CNH funds will be recorded in the entity RMB account at BOCHK or HSBC.For outbound trade settlements, the preferred rails are SWIFT MT103 for high-value services, software royalties, and contractual compensation; commercial letters of credit (L/C) when requiring banker confirmation for capital goods or IP acquisition; and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for recurring subscription payments to or from Chinese supply-chain vendors. All disbursements must include banking purpose narratives aligned with the HKMA economic substance review, and for related-party royalty payments the entity must file transfer pricing documentation with its annual corporate tax return. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on dividends distributed by the PRC JV to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the PRC-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement where the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the JV's capital directly and satisfies beneficial ownership and substance tests.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a PRC fiscal year ending 31 December, the annual enterprise income tax return must be filed and settled no later than 31 May of the following year; quarterly prepayments are due by the 15th day of the month following each quarter.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 where annual related-party tangible property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible property or financial asset transactions exceed RMB 100 million, the aggregate of tangible, intangible and financial transactions exceeds RMB 200 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File documentation is generally required when the Chinese entity is part of a multijurisdictional group whose consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and reportable related-party transactions have occurred.

Economic substance scope: The PRC tax authorities apply the substance-over-form doctrine through Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Announcement 2015 No. 7. A Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits must establish beneficial ownership, legitimate commercial purpose, real employees, active management, operational premises, and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong. Hong Kong's foreign-source income exemption rules also require substantive economic activity for offshore passive income; if the Hong Kong owner is a mailbox company, the SAT will disregard the arrangement, deny treaty relief, and reclassify income as PRC-source taxable to the actual recipient.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent corporate income tax filing

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes 50% to 500% of the evaded tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge; PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes fines and imprisonment of up to seven years where the amount of tax evaded is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors can be prosecuted as directly responsible persons under Criminal Law Article 211, incurring personal fines and imprisonment. Under PRC Company Law principles of veil piercing, directors who abuse corporate form to evade tax and harm the State or creditors may be held personally and jointly liable for the JV's unpaid tax, penalties, and interest. Unpaid tax liabilities can trigger exit restrictions and placement on the tax credit blacklist, preventing board members from leaving China.
Treaty abuse and shell Hong Kong holding company

Statutory sanction: The SAT may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial ownership provisions of SAT Announcement 2015 No. 7 and apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, restoring 10% withholding tax, adding interest, and imposing a 50% to 500% fraud penalty under Article 63. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department may also reopen the Hong Kong profits tax position if offshore exemption is claimed without substantive economic activity.

Director personal liability: If the corporate veil is pierced, the controlling Hong Kong shareholder and directors are personally liable for the under-withheld PRC tax, interest, and penalties. Directors who knowingly execute sham agreements may be treated as principal offenders in tax evasion proceedings, resulting in criminal imprisonment, travel bans, asset freezes, and disqualification under the SAT blacklisting system.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and non-arm's length offshore royalty

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorises transfer pricing adjustments; Tax Administration Law Articles 62 and 63 impose fines, daily surcharges, and penalties ranging from 50% to 500% where mispricing constitutes evasion. The tax authority may apply deemed profit methods and refuse to accept the related-party contractual allocations if no documentation exists.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements or direct the preparation of fraudulent documentation face civil joint and several liability for the additional tax through veil-piercing principles. Criminal liability arises if forged invoices or fraudulent tax returns are used, and tax authorities may impose exit restrictions and judicial travel bans while the enterprise tax debt is unresolved, exposing directors to personal insolvency and criminal imprisonment in egregious cases.
