Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region - the competent tax authority for the Hong Kong jurisdiction in this formation, not the State Taxation Administration of Mainland China. Online electronic portal: eTAX (Electronic Services for Tax) at www.gov.hk/etax. Customs administration is separately handled by the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity or joint-venture limited company operating in Hong Kong obtains its statutory taxpayer identity through the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). After incorporating at the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), or after registering as a registered non-Hong Kong company, the entity must apply to the Business Registration Office of the IRD using the prescribed application form within one month of commencing business. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) bearing a Business Registration Number (BRN); the BRN is the Hong Kong taxpayer identification number and must be quoted in Profits Tax returns, employer returns, and customs trade declarations. Foreign non-resident entities that do not carry on business in Hong Kong and do not derive Hong Kong-source income are not obliged to obtain a BRN solely because they are foreign entities. Non-resident persons who become chargeable to Hong Kong Profits Tax may be issued an IRD tax file number for assessment purposes. Applications can be submitted through the IRD eTAX portal, by post, or in person at the Business Registration Office; the prescribed registration fee and levy must be paid. The territorial-source principle is applied separately under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), but this is a scope rule, not a registration election.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is conditionally convertible: current-account payments may be settled freely by authorized banks after documentary verification, while capital-account outbound investment requires prior ODI filing with MOFCOM/NDRC and SAFE registration. The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible under the HKMA's linked exchange rate system, with no surrender or approval requirement for ordinary corporate payments. The critical bottleneck is the PRC parent's injection of equity capital into the HK JV: funds cannot be remitted until the parent obtains its Certificate of Overseas Investment Filing from MOFCOM/NDRC and completes SAFE registration with a qualified PRC bank. Upon receipt in Hong Kong, the JV must treat the funds as shareholder capital and support the inward remittance with a board resolution, share allotment, and Companies Registry filings. Dividend repatriation from HK to the PRC parent is unrestricted at the HK side but will be characterized as Chinese tax-resident dividend income and may attract withholding tax under the arrangement between mainland China and Hong Kong; adequate economic substance and contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation must be maintained to avoid recharacterization. A separate capital escrow ledger is recommended to segregate injected capital from operating revenue.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The Hong Kong JV is outside PRC surrender rules. Any PRC trading subsidiary that holds export foreign-exchange receipts must repatriate the proceeds into a domestic foreign-exchange account within 30 days from receipt, and the mandatory conversion ratio is zero because enterprises may choose to retain the currency or sell it to a PRC bank as permitted by SAFE. The HK JV itself can keep receivables freely in HKD/USD.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for PRC-side capital account controls; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking and clearing authority for the Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Medium for low-value SaaS collections but Low as the primary banking layer. Hong Kong's Banking Ordinance restricts deposit-taking and clearing settlement to authorized institutions, so offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie) can act only as money-service operators or SVF licensees and cannot issue commercial letters of credit, provide RMB NDFs, open CHATS settlement accounts at HKMA, or serve as the custodian of injected share capital. The JV may use Stripe/Paddle as merchant-of-record rails for subscription billing, with settlement flows swept automatically to a tier-1 bank account. All capital injections, trade disbursements, and FX hedges must be executed at a licensed commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital must flow from the PRC parent's SAFE-qualified bank under the approved ODI quota using a wire with purpose code 'Overseas Direct Investment - Equity'. The remittance should be received in the HK JV's Capital Escrow Account at a designated tier-1 bank; after receipt, the bank will issue an inward remittance credit advice, and the JV should promptly file the share allotment (Form SC1) with the Companies Registry and issue shares to the parent. For amounts exceeding the HKMA large-value threshold, use CHATS or SWIFT MT103; do not split transactions to avoid declaration thresholds.
