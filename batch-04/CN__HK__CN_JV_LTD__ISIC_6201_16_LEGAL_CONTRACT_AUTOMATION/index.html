Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. A private company limited by shares under the Companies Ordinance has no statutory minimum paid-up capital. The company may be incorporated with one or more shares of nominal value HKD 1.00 each, and allotment may be fully paid, partly paid, or unpaid as specified in the Articles of Association. There is no blocked escrow account requirement in Hong Kong SAR; share subscription monies may be held in a licensed bank account subject to customer due diligence and AML requirements.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for ISIC 6201 software operations. If an office premise is used, a Fire Services Department certification confirming means of escape and fire service installations may be required for the building's occupancy certificate; this is an occupational clearance rather than an industrial operating license.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the national unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform operated by the STA. For Hong Kong SAR-specific elements, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the competent authority, but the PRC-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is registered with the STA.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); under the multi-license integration reform, the USCC is the statutory TIN and VAT registration number. Step 2: Complete tax registration on the STA e-Tax platform using the business license, articles of association, foreign investor identity and beneficial ownership documentation, registered address proof, bank account confirmation, and financial accounting policy statement; SAMR data is automatically synchronized to the STA core taxation system. Step 3: Complete the taxpayer classification profile covering enterprise type, industry code ISIC 6201/NAICS 541199, ownership structure, place of effective management, and expected cross-border transaction types. Step 4: Register for VAT status; general VAT taxpayer status is mandatory when projected annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, while smaller taxpayers may elect small-scale taxpayer status or apply for general status. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident entity with no permanent establishment in mainland China, the entity or its China withholding agent must file cross-border contract and withholding-tax registration with the STA; the non-resident uses its home-jurisdiction tax identification number or Hong Kong tax file number, and the STA may assign a temporary non-resident tax code where necessary. Step 6: Activate the electronic invoice account and digital signatures on the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, authorizing issuance of fully digitalized electronic invoices and participation in Golden Tax Phase IV controls.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold for mandatory PRC General VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold the JV may remain a Small-Scale VAT taxpayer. Every legal person with mainland China taxable activities must complete national tax registration and VAT registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, zero-rating applies when physical goods are exported from the PRC customs territory, including exports to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, evidenced by a valid customs export declaration. For qualifying offshore services, including software services, information technology services, LegalTech SaaS, and e-signature authentication, zero-rating applies under the PRC cross-border service rules when the service is supplied to an overseas recipient, is consumed entirely outside mainland China, payment is received from outside mainland China in foreign exchange or cross-border RMB, and the service has no mainland China domestic consumption. If these conditions are not satisfied, the supply is taxable at 6% VAT or may qualify for VAT exemption.

  • Original Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill (or equivalent electronic transport record)
  • Export sales invoice issued through the VAT/FDEI system with zero-rated notation
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank cross-border foreign-exchange or RMB receipt declaration evidencing payment from the overseas buyer
  • Cross-border service contract or SaaS/e-signature master agreement, including service scope, overseas consumption statement, and digital delivery and usage logs
  • VAT zero-rated declaration returns and STA e-invoice verification records for the exported supply

Mandatory issuance of Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (FDEI) through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each invoice is assigned a countrywide unique number, encrypted, uploaded synchronously at issuance, and protected by a digital tax-control verification stamp. B2B and B2C taxpayers must integrate ERP or billing systems to the STA platform through approved APIs; Golden Tax Phase IV matches invoice issuance, VAT declaration, customs, and bank receipt data in near real time for anti-fraud clearance.

STA and customs authorities will match the zero-rated claim against customs declarations, transport documents, bank receipt data, contracts, and service-consumption logs. Unverified or fraudulent zero-rating is clawed back with late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the tax underpaid, and revocation of any input VAT refund. Serious cases are referred for criminal export-tax-fraud prosecution with imprisonment exposure; the entity may also be removed from the general VAT taxpayer registry, placed on the dishonest taxpayer joint-punishment list, and barred from export-refund processing. For fraud and evasion, the statutory assessment period is unlimited and recovery can be made retrospectively.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate system, with the Renminbi trading onshore under a current account that is largely convertible for trade, services, and profit remittance, while the capital account retains material regulatory controls. The Hong Kong SAR operates under a separate monetary and legal regime, with the Hong Kong dollar fully convertible and no foreign exchange surrender requirements. For the mainland Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, capital inflows from the Hong Kong shareholder are classified as foreign direct investment and must be routed through the onshore capital settlement account, subject to SAFE registration, purpose code validation, and capital verification. Profit repatriation is permitted after the annual audit and corporate income tax settlement, provided the accumulated distributable profit is real, tax-paid, and verified by a licensed CPA. Currency conversion under current account items is made at onshore market rates via bank's settlement/sale, while capital account conversion is restricted to genuine transaction, with borrowing abroad, cross-border guarantees and offshore reinvestment requiring prior SAFE approval or registration. This duality requires treasury planning to differentiate current versus capital account paths for each US-Dollar, HKD, or RMB flow, and all foreign exchange conversion must be supported by underlying invoices, contracts, and statutory declarations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE regulations, China has abolished mandatory forced surrender of export foreign exchange. All export proceeds, including software-as-a-service and digital contract automation revenues, must be repatriated to China through a licensed bank within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration or, for pure service exports, from the date the service is completed and performance confirmed, unless the enterprise has obtained a specific offshore retention quota. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the exporting JV may retain proceeds in a foreign-currency bank account or convert them into RMB at the onshore market rate. A 100% reconciliation duty exists: each receipt must be matched with the customs declaration/contract, commercial invoice, valid SAFE purpose code, and the enterprise's foreign income collection verification record in the bank's digital FX monitoring system. Failure to repatriate or record the receipt triggers a capital repatriation penalty and downgrade of the bank's compliance classification.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the separate monetary authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Onshore viability is very low. PRC foreign exchange regulations reserve the creation and servicing of corporate cash management, foreign exchange settlement, and cross-border accounts to locally licensed banks; an offshore EMI is not a licensed deposit-taking institution for the mainland JV and cannot execute SAFE verification. For the Hong Kong hub, registered SVF or digital banking providers may be used only for ancillary collections, but primary liquidity should remain in a licensed Hong Kong bank or a branch of a Chinese Tier-1 bank. A global PSP such as Stripe can be used for low-value SaaS subscription receipts if the settlement sink is a licensed Chinese bank; however, the JV cannot rely on an EMI as its treasury clearing rail for invoice financing, capital injection, payroll, or regulatory reporting. Legal constraint: contract automation and e-signature SaaS does not change the fact that the mainland entity must hold its capital account and operational liquidity in a supervised bank, otherwise SAFE, AMRO and tax reporting are impossible to complete.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong shareholder to the mainland JV must be remitted under SWIFT MT103 with explicit remittance reference to the SAFE FDI registration ID and the official SAFE capital-project purpose code for inbound direct investment. The receiving Chinese Tier-1 bank must verify the digital SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Form, match the remitter name to the registered shareholder, and credit the Foreign Exchange Capital Account; funds cannot be drawn down for operations until capital verification is completed. After the remittance lands, a licensed PRC CPA must issue a Capital Contribution Verification Report, and the bank should complete the SAFE declaration of received foreign exchange. Once verified, a portion of the capital may be converted into RMB at the onshore market rate through the AIC settlement process and transferred to the RMB basic deposit account. No anonymous, pooled, or multi-invoice lump-sum credits are acceptable.Outbound treasury disbursements should use the following clearing rails: first, SWIFT MT103 direct remittance for recurring software licences, royalties, subcontractor fees, and operational payments, supported by contract, commercial invoice, proof of performance, and SAFE current-account purpose code; second, Commercial Letter of Credit, typically irrevocable at sight, only where the underlying transaction includes tangible deliverables, hardware modules, or contractual time-bound acceptance milestones; third, Documentary Collections only for low-risk related-party trade with standard shipping documents. For a 100% digital LegalTech service, the MT103 direct rail is preferred and must be sent from a bank with a strong Asia-Pacific clearing corridor such as Bank of China Limited, China Construction Bank, or CITIC Bank International in Hong Kong. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (8.25% concessionary rate applies to the first HK$2 million of assessable profits under the two-tiered Hong Kong profits tax regime for a qualifying corporation)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% (Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends paid by a Hong Kong resident company to its non-resident shareholders, including the PRC parent, because of the territorial source system and the absence of a domestic dividend WHT law)

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and maintained when controlled transactions with associated persons exceed HK$16 million in aggregate for services, intangibles, financial transactions, and other non-goods transactions, or HK$8 million for controlled goods transactions; Master File is required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue meets the HK$6.8 billion Country-by-Country Report threshold, as specified in IRD DIPN 59. The BIR51 also includes an online TP Overview requiring disclosure of related-party transaction volume and whether contemporaneous documentation has been prepared.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted a standalone Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code similar to the British Virgin Islands or Hong Kong shell regimes; however, the IRD applies the territorial source doctrine and IRO s.61A to disregard artificial transactions lacking commercial substance.
