The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) requires a physical street address in Hong Kong for registered office service of process; a P.O. box is void. A virtual office is accepted only if it is a serviced office with a licensed company secretary and registered office agent, not a mere mail-drop. For a PropTech SaaS business with payment rails (Stripe Billing and ACH Direct), the Inland Revenue Department will assess whether the place of management and control is genuinely in Hong Kong, and banks conducting KYC under Cap. 615 will reject a shell-like virtual-mailbox profile because it lacks substance, auditable domestic management, and presence. Thus a virtual office is not permissible as the sole location; the JV must lease physical office space, hold management meetings there, keep board minutes there, and maintain local bank accounts, payroll and AML/CFT records there.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating the electronic tax administration portal known as the 'E-Tax Bureau' (电子税务局; however the official English interface is referred to as the 'E-Tax Bureau' or 'Online Tax Payment Service' at etax.chinatax.gov.cn). For Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD), with electronic services via the 'GovHK' portal and the IRD's 'eTAX' electronic filing platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established in mainland China, the enterprise is required to complete industrial and commercial registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Following issuance of the business license, the taxpayer must, within 30 days, complete tax registration at the competent county-level or district-level STA office where the registered address is located. The STA assigns an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the enterprise's Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all tax filings, including VAT and corporate income tax. The foreign joint venture partners must provide certified copies of their home-country registration certificates, passport copies of legal representatives, and the joint venture contract and articles of association. For the Hong Kong SAR entity involved in this cross-border structure, the corporate vehicle is registered with the Companies Registry and then obtains a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) from the Inland Revenue Department. The IRD assigns a Commercial Registration Number (CR number) that functions as the Hong Kong taxpayer identification number. Where the Hong Kong entity carries on a business in Hong Kong and supplies taxable goods or services, it must register for Goods and Services Tax only if Hong Kong had implemented GST; currently Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, though customs duties and profits tax apply. If the Chinese JV engages in export activities, it must additionally complete foreign trade operator registration and customs registration with the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC), obtaining a Customs Registration Code (10-digit customs code) linked to its USCC, and must also register for the export tax rebate system with the competent tax authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Under the Measures for the Administration of Value-Added Tax Identification of Small-Scale Taxpayers, the annual VAT-registered sales threshold is RMB 5,000,000 (five million Renminbi). However, any enterprise established as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is generally required to obtain general VAT taxpayer status if its annual turnover exceeds RMB 5 million or if it has a fixed place of business and applies the general method of VAT calculation. Small-scale taxpayers with annual sales not exceeding RMB 5 million may alternatively register under the simplified levy method with a 3% collection rate, but the standard general VAT rate applicable to software-as-a-service and technology services supplied to business customers is 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods sold by a Chinese registered enterprise to overseas customers are zero-rated for VAT, provided the goods are physically exported and customs export procedures are completed. For software-as-a-service and cloud-based PropTech services, China's VAT regime zero-rates the export of software products and certain digital services where the recipient is outside mainland China and the service is supplied wholly outside China, or qualifies as exported services under Caishui [2016] No. 36. Eligible offshore services include software as a service, data processing, and information technology services provided to overseas entities and paid for in foreign currency. The zero-rating applies only where the service is rendered to a non-resident enterprise, the service is not consumed within mainland China, and the enterprise retains contemporaneous documentation proving the export nature. Under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and the STA's definitions, cross-border sales of services may be exempted or zero-rated after filing the Cross-Border Tax Declaration Schedule and submitting relevant documentary evidence to the competent tax authority for review. For the Hong Kong SAR side, Hong Kong does not levy VAT, so no export zero-rating claim arises; profits tax on export-related income follows the territorial source principle.

Mainland China operates the nationwide 'New e-Invoice' (数电票 / fully digitalized electronic invoice) system, rolled out by the STA. Fully digitalized electronic invoices use a unified electronic invoice format without physical paper, carry an anti-forgery electronic stamp encrypted with an official tax-control code, and are issued through the E-Tax Bureau platform or approved tax-UKey / ERP integration channels. The system requires real-time reporting of issued and received invoices to the STA's electronic invoice service platform through API-based or web-based clearance. Businesses must use official tax control devices or the new digital e-invoice service to generate invoices, and invoice data is automatically transmitted for comparison against VAT returns. For cross-border e-commerce exports, the customs clearance process is administered through the GACC 'single window' integrated platform, and ordinary VAT invoices must be reported by purchasers in the VAT invoice verification platform when claiming input VAT credits. Non-resident digital service suppliers without a physical presence in China are not generally required to issue Chinese VAT invoices for exports; however export transactions must be supported by formal contracts, customs declarations where goods are involved, and foreign exchange receipts.

Under the Measures for Administration of Value-Added Tax on Exported Goods and Services, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days from the date of export declaration may result in the export sale being treated as domestic sales, requiring the taxpayer to pay VAT at the applicable rate (6% for technology services) and Corporate Income Tax adjustments, plus late payment surcharges. If the exporter submits false customs declarations, uses counterfeit documentation such as forged bills of lading, or engages in fraudulent export tax rebate schemes, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating, claw back any exemptions or refunds, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Suppression of Tax Evasion and Tax Refusal, and refer the case to customs anti-smuggling authorities or police for criminal prosecution. Additional sanctions include revocation of tax registration or export tax rebate qualification, a tax trust blacklist designation, blocking of export rebate applications, and customs credit downgrade under the AEO mutual recognition framework.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a completely open capital account system with no foreign exchange controls. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible into all major currencies, and the linked exchange rate system ensures stability against the US dollar (7.75-7.85 HKD/USD). Current account transactions, including profit remittances and dividends from a Hong Kong entity, are unrestricted and require merely proper board authorization. Capital account transactions such as equity injections and loan repayments also enjoy full liberalization. However, for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with a People's Republic of China (PRC) shareholder, the Chinese side must navigate the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) capital account controls governing outbound direct investment and repatriation of dividends back to the mainland. Specifically, the PRC shareholder must obtain prior approval/filing from NDRC and MOFCOM for its foreign investment, and remittances must be channeled through a designated Chinese bank with a valid foreign exchange business registration. The use of offshore RMB (CNH) in Hong Kong is unrestricted, but conversion between CNH and onshore CNY is only permitted through licensed banks within the mainland's trade settlement framework. Overall, the Hong Kong entity enjoys pristine convertibility, while cross-border flows involving mainland China require dual regulatory compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender requirements are imposed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. Exporters may hold, convert, or remit foreign currency proceeds freely without any conversion ratio or timeline. For mainland Chinese parents with direct export operations, SAFE requires repatriation within 180 days of export declaration, but no mandatory conversion ratio applies; the broader group should observe the Chinese external debt and receipt verification rules.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit
