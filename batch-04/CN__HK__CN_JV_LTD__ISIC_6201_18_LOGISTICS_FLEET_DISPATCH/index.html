Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; electronic portal: GovHK eTAX (https://www.ird.gov.hk/eng/ese/); related agencies: Business Registration Office of the IRD, Companies Registry (Companies Ordinance, Cap. 622), and Customs and Excise Department (import/export declarations under Cap. 60E).

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong incorporated limited company, including a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established as a Hong Kong company limited by shares, the Companies Registry one-stop incorporation process integrates incorporation under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) with business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the IRD Business Registration Office issues a Business Registration Certificate displaying the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the de facto corporate tax identification number for Hong Kong. A foreign non-resident entity that does not incorporate locally but establishes a Hong Kong branch must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office in the prescribed written form under Cap. 310 within one month after commencing business in Hong Kong, and will receive a BRN for that branch. There is no separate VAT/GST identification number in Hong Kong; the BRN is used for Profits Tax correspondence, employer returns, customs declarations, electronic invoicing, and government filing. The application must state the legal form, principal business activities, registered office or principal place of business address in Hong Kong, and, for a branch registration, the ultimate parent entity and local authorized representative. The IRD assigns a separate Profits Tax file number upon first tax filing and may issue employer and tax file references for payroll and compliance purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box entity classification election, no hybrid mismatch election, no group taxation election, and no fiscal consolidation regime. A Hong Kong limited company is automatically taxable at entity level under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); its liability is governed by the territorial-source principle, under which only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are chargeable to Profits Tax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax applies in Hong Kong. The only indirect taxes are selective duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and other specific instruments such as betting duty and motor vehicle first registration tax; none of these applies to the logistics telematics SaaS business.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; there is no VAT/GST registration threshold in HKD or any other currency. Under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), a person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain business registration within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable: Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on exports or on qualifying offshore digital services, so there is no 0% export VAT rating to claim and no export VAT refund mechanism. For Profits Tax purposes, offshore-source profits are generally not chargeable to tax under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); a Hong Kong exporter of logistics SaaS should document the place of contract formation, negotiation, performance, use, and customer residence to demonstrate that profits are not sourced in Hong Kong.

  • No customs declaration is required for pure digital or logistics SaaS services; if physical goods are exported from Hong Kong, an Export Declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) must be lodged through the Trade Single Window.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical movement of goods from Hong Kong, where applicable.
  • Commercial invoice and signed SaaS or logistics service contract identifying the overseas customer, billing entity, payment currency, delivery terms, and place(s) of service performance.
  • SWIFT MT103 or bank credit advice evidencing receipt of consideration from outside Hong Kong; Hong Kong does not issue a mainland-style Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate and imposes no foreign exchange control or mandated FX repatriation deadline.
  • Business Registration Certificate showing the BRN of the exporting entity, and, where applicable, any required Certificate of Origin or preferential tariff documentation.

No real-time fiscal clearance, government API e-invoicing, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists in Hong Kong. Private-form commercial invoices are accepted, but taxpayers must retain sufficient business records in English or Chinese for at least seven years under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). For physical exports, import/export declarations must be lodged electronically through the Trade Single Window or the approved electronic declaration system under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E); these declarations support customs statistics and control, not VAT clearance.

There is no indirect-tax clawback in Hong Kong because no export VAT zero-rating is granted. However, incorrect trade declarations lodged with the Customs and Excise Department under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E) may result in fines and forfeiture; false or negligent statements that understate tax liability may lead to additional tax and prosecution under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and documents used to support an offshore-source profits position remain subject to IRD audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The entity operates as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture registered in mainland China with a Hong Kong SAR intermediate holding jurisdiction. The Chinese renminbi (CNY) regime is classified as a managed float with strict capital account controls, while current account transactions are generally convertible for bona fide trade and service payments. The JV's core SaaS-based logistics fleet telematics business generates cross-border subscription revenue and pays overseas vendors, so the key distinction is whether each transaction is classified as current account (services, software licensing, royalties, operating expenses) or capital account (equity injections, shareholder loans, dividends, offshore debt repayment). Under the Foreign Investment Law and the PBOC/SAFE consolidated rules, the JV must register its foreign investment information with SAFE through the designated bank, and the Hong Kong parent entity must ensure the capital contribution is remitted through a compliant foreign exchange settlement account. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permissible after audited financial statements and tax clearance, but SAFE requires the underlying current account transaction to be verified with contracts, invoices, and customs or service trade declarations. Surrender requirements apply to export proceeds in goods, but for services and software exports, the JV is generally required to repatriate receivables into China within the statutory period and may retain the proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account before conversion, subject to documentary verification. The practical hurdles include the matching of payment purpose codes, the need for a valid technology import or export registration for software and telematics data services, and the impossibility of converting CNY to foreign currency for speculative purposes or unverified capital account flows. In practice, dividend distributions require a board resolution, audited financial statements, and a tax withholding certificate, and the bank will escalate any payments lacking granular underlying documentation. The overall assessment is that the JV can achieve current account convertibility with disciplined documentation, capital account items require pre-registration and approval, and the Hong Kong treasury should maintain full source documents to avoid freezing or rejection of outward remittances.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts from goods must be repatriated within 90 days, and the JV must surrender or settle foreign currency proceeds through the designated bank in accordance with SAFE's Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. For service and SaaS exports, revenue may be retained in a foreign currency settlement account, but proceeds must be repatriated and either converted to CNY or documented as foreign currency deposits within the statutory timeframe; mandatory conversion ratios apply only where SAFE imposes temporary macro-prudential measures, which currently require full repatriation and do not permit offshore retention without prior approval.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the primary servicer for the mainland Chinese JV's domestic treasury because China does not license foreign fintech electronic money institutions to open RMB or foreign exchange accounts for onshore entities, and SAFE requires all capital account and foreign exchange transactions to flow through domestically licensed banks. EMIs such as Airwallex or Currencycloud can only serve as supplementary payment facilitators for cross-border invoicing and collections from overseas customers, but they must partner with a licensed Chinese bank for settlement, and they cannot execute SAFE-registered capital injections, dividend remittances, or export surrender. Therefore the blueprint mandates a tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the primary banking relationship, with a Hong Kong-licensed virtual bank or EMI used only for offshore treasury concentration of the Hong Kong holding entity, never for the onshore JV.

Turnover: A Hong Kong company is required to appoint a certified public accountant (practising) to audit its financial statements unless it qualifies for the small private company or small private company group audit exemption under the Companies Ordinance. The small entity exemption is available where the company or group satisfies at least two of the following three conditions for the financial year: (i) total revenue does not exceed HKD 20 million (USD 2.55 million); (ii) total assets at the balance sheet date do not exceed HKD 10 million (USD 1.27 million); and (iii) the average number of employees does not exceed 50. Audit exemption is not available if a member or members holding the requisite interest under the Companies Ordinance serve a notice requiring an audit. For all non-exempt companies, a statutory audit by an independent licensed CPA is mandatory, together with preparation of financial statements showing a true and fair view.
