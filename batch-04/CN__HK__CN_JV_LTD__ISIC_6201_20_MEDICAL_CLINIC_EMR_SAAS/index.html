Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: GovHK (www.gov.hk) and IRD eTax (www.ird.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

Foreign non-resident entities do not obtain a VAT ID in Hong Kong because no VAT/GST regime exists. If CN_JV_LTD carries on business in Hong Kong, it must apply for Business Registration with the Business Registration Office of the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement of business. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate; the Business Registration Number (BRN) is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all tax filings, correspondence, withholding and information-exchange purposes. A non-resident entity that is not carrying on business in Hong Kong is not required to register; if the IRD later issues a Profits Tax Return or an information notice, the IRD will assign a separate tax file/reference number. There is no separate VAT registration or TIN process.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; Territorial_Scope

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election applies to a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. The two-tiered Profits Tax rate concession applies automatically for eligible Hong Kong-sourced profits.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales Tax - Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or a general sales tax; indirect imposts are limited to Business Registration fees and Stamp Duty on specified instruments

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no turnover threshold exists because no VAT/GST regime is legislated. Business Registration is required once a business is commenced in Hong Kong, and Stamp Duty may apply to specific instruments irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong imposes no VAT on any supply. A cross-border EMR software/SaaS supply from Hong Kong to overseas customers is outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax. For Profits Tax purposes, the effective 0% outcome is achieved only through the territorial source principle, under which the IRD taxes profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong; the JV must demonstrate that the substantive business activities creating the profit occurred outside Hong Kong.

  • No mandatory customs tax bundle for VAT zero-rating exists because no VAT is chargeable in Hong Kong
  • For profits tax territorial-source substantiation: SaaS subscription agreement, commercial invoices, foreign exchange inward payment receipts or bank statements showing receipt in Hong Kong from overseas, service delivery performance logs, and board/management records showing offshore selection and decision-making
  • For any physical goods: electronic Export Declaration under the Import and Export (Registration) Ordinance (Cap. 60), commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill

No statutory real-time fiscal clearance, API-based tax reporting, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate exists. Private commercial e-invoices are acceptable for record-keeping; electronic tax filing is voluntary through IRD eTax and is not an invoice clearance requirement.

No VAT clawback or input tax recovery penalties apply because Hong Kong has no VAT. For Profits Tax, if the IRD determines that the offshore-source exclusion is unsupported or that any return is false or misleading, the Commissioner may reopen assessments, claw back previously excluded profits, impose additional tax and penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), and institute criminal prosecution in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region operates a fully convertible and liberalised foreign exchange regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with no surrender requirements and no restrictions on the free movement of capital for current or capital account transactions. The HKD is freely convertible, and the HKMA, as the monetary authority, maintains currency stability without imposing exchange controls. By contrast, the mainland Chinese leg of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is subject to the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations of the People's Republic of China. The current account—covering trade in goods, services, and routine SaaS licensing royalties—is conditionally convertible, meaning genuine underlying transactions must be verified by banks with SAFE registration, but funds can generally be remitted without prior approval. The capital account, including equity injections, dividends, and intra-group loans, remains tightly controlled. For a Hong Kong-incorporated treasury hub funding a mainland EMR SaaS JV, profit remittances from China to Hong Kong require audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, SAFE remittance filing, and the bank's due diligence on the distribution to ensure no round-tripping. Surrender of export proceeds no longer takes the form of forced conversion; instead, the JV must repatriate the foreign currency to a designated bank within the statutory period and may retain it in a foreign exchange settlement account, subject to quota verification. The dual-track system means that while the HK treasury function enjoys full convertibility, the cross-border flows into and out of China demand meticulous SAFE compliance, rigorous trade documentation, and careful allocation between current and capital account purposes. This asymmetry creates an imperative to route strategic liquidity through Hong Kong while keeping the mainland JV in a fully compliant netting position.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong treasury entity, there is no statutory timeline or mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be held indefinitely in any approved currency. For the mainland China JV, under SAFE Circular 221/2012 and the Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, and must be deposited into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account. There is no mandatory percentage of conversion to CNY; the JV may retain 100% in foreign currency or convert as needed, provided the bank verifies the export contract, customs declarations, invoices, and logistics documents to meet the 'real trade background' requirement. Failure to repatriate or to present valid documentation incurs penalties under the Measures for the Punishment of Foreign Exchange Violations.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong SAR; the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) govern the mainland China operations of the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture structure.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$100,000,000 aggregate controlled transactions for the year trigger the Local File requirement under Section 50AAF of the IRO. A Master File is required when the multinational group's global consolidated revenue exceeds HK$6,800,000,000 (roughly EUR 750 million). Contemporaneous documentation must be completed by the return filing date.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime like classic offshore jurisdictions; however, the territorial source principle and anti-abuse rules create an economic-substance requirement to claim offshore income exemption or tax treaty benefits. The IRD will examine the core income-generating activities (CIGA) to determine the source of profits: for the medical EMR SaaS business, key activities include software development, cloud hosting, contract negotiation, billing, and client support. If these activities are performed in mainland China through employees or an office, a permanent establishment may arise in China, and the HK entity may be regarded as a conduit. For treaty claims, the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) denies benefits if obtaining treaty relief was a principal purpose of the structure. Credible economic substance in Hong Kong requires an active board of directors, employees, office premises, bank accounts, and decision-making functions in Hong Kong. The IRD can challenge pure shell arrangements and re-characterise the profits as fully taxable in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Offshore Income Declaration

Statutory sanction: Contravenes Section 82(b) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) (knowingly making a false statement in a tax return). Upon conviction on indictment, maximum fine of HK$200,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged, and imprisonment up to 3 years. In addition, the IRD may disregard the artificial arrangement under Section 61 and levy an estimated assessment with a 100% additional penalty.

Director personal liability: The director(s) who signed the return and directed that all profits be booked offshore are personally and criminally liable. Courts may pierce the corporate veil and order directors to pay the unpaid tax, interest, and penalties from their personal assets. A travel restriction order or departure prevention order can be issued under the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance or the Criminal Procedure Ordinance to block directors from leaving Hong Kong pending trial. Imprisonment of up to 3 years and disqualification from acting as a director (up to 15 years) may also be imposed.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The IRD can adjust the assessable profits to an arm's length amount under Section 50A of the IRO. Failure to keep relevant TP documentation per Section 50AAF subjects the taxpayer to a penalty of up to HK$50,000. If the incorrect pricing is found to be due to wilful evasion, Section 82 applies with a fine of HK$200,000 plus treble tax and imprisonment. The IRD may also charge interest on tax underpaid and impose a surcharge of up to 10% of the tax adjusted under Section 82A.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly design or approve the profit-shifting arrangement are jointly and severally liable for the tax adjustment, regardless of corporate status. Criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the revenue (common law offence) carries a maximum of 14 years' imprisonment. The IRD may also apply to the court to issue a Mareva injunction freezing the directors' personal assets and a travel ban. In civil proceedings, directors may be liable to the company for breach of fiduciary duty in causing unlawful payments, and excess management fees must be repaid to the company.
Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Both China and Hong Kong apply anti-beating measures. Under the DTA Principal Purpose Test, treaty benefits are denied if obtaining the benefit was a principal purpose of the arrangement; the Chinese source country may then impose dividend/royalty withholding tax at the domestic statutory rate of 10% (or 20% in the case of interest? but for dividends 10% plus late payment interest and a penalty of 50% of the under-withheld tax under the Tax Collection Law). In Hong Kong, the IRD may apply the anti-avoidance provision of Section 61A to counteract the tax advantage, and any benefits from HK-source profits may be reassessed. A penalty of up to 100% of the tax avoided plus 3% monthly default interest can be levied.

Director personal liability: Directors who structured the conduit arrangement may be personally liable for all under-withheld Chinese tax, interest, and penalties. On the criminal side, the directors may be charged with offences under Section 82 of the IRO for false declarations, and under the Theft Ordinance for obtaining a pecuniary advantage by deception, resulting in imprisonment up to 10 years. The Hong Kong court may, on application by the IRD, order a worldwide asset freezing order and prohibition on leaving Hong Kong while an investigation is pending. Corporate veil-piercing may be ordered to recover the tax advantage from the directors' personal funds.
