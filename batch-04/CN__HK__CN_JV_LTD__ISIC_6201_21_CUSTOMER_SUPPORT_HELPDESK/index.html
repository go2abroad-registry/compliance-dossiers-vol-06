Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a Hong Kong company must maintain a registered office in Hong Kong. A virtual office or commercial mailbox is permissible for registered-office purposes only if it is a physical address at which legal notices can be served and a licensed local service provider is authorized to receive correspondence. However, for the target Helpdesk SaaS business, the Inland Revenue Department and most licensed banks will reject a pure mailbox or virtual office when opening corporate accounts or issuing business registration certificates, because the entity must demonstrate a genuine local nexus and administrative capacity. A virtual office with a dedicated phone line, meeting room, mail forwarding, and professional company-secretary support is likely to be accepted by the Companies Registry, but it will not satisfy a bank's physical-presence assessment unless the bank performs a site visit and confirms staff availability. A Hong Kong company secretary and a local registered office are mandatory regardless of the virtual office arrangement.

None under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A Hong Kong private company limited by shares may be incorporated with one share, e.g., one HK$1 share, and no minimum paid-up capital is required. The company must have at least one shareholder, at least one director who is a natural person, and at least one company secretary who is either an individual ordinarily resident in Hong Kong or a body corporate with its registered office in Hong Kong. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the China Electronic Tax Administration platform; where the joint venture operates a Hong Kong SAR branch or sales entity, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through eTAX is the competent filing authority.

E-government filing portal

The foreign non-resident investor incorporates the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) and receives the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes. Within 30 days after the business license is issued, the entity must complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Administration, bind the USCC to the corporate income tax and VAT profiles, and declare its VAT taxpayer status. If a Hong Kong SAR branch or subsidiary is established, that entity must separately register with the Companies Registry and apply to the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance; the Business Registration number is the Hong Kong TIN. A foreign non-resident parent without a PRC establishment may obtain a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number through the same STA e-Tax registration workflow for non-resident enterprises before claiming treaty benefits under the Arrangement between the Mainland of China and the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC VAT taxpayers must complete VAT registration. General taxpayer status becomes mandatory when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the joint venture may remain a small-scale taxpayer. Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The PRC joint venture may claim the 0% VAT export rate for cross-border helpdesk SaaS and conversational bot cloud services only when the service is provided to an overseas customer located outside Mainland China, the contract is in writing, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, and the consideration is received from the offshore customer in a foreign currency or RMB through a licensed PRC bank; the services must also fall within the statutory information technology services zero-rating catalogue for cross-border taxable services.

  • Customs Export Declaration issued by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China, used where the SaaS or bot solution is shipped as a physical appliance or together with export goods; the declaration must state the HS code, customs value, quantity, and export destination.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international transport waybill evidencing movement of any tangible goods from Mainland China to the overseas customer.
  • Bank cross-border receipts declaration and SAFE foreign exchange collection verification record showing the inward remittance from the offshore customer and the matching export invoice.
  • Signed cross-border service agreement, commercial invoice, and proof of overseas customer existence such as the Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate or equivalent foreign registry extract.

Mainland China mandates real-time fiscal clearance of invoices: VAT invoices must be generated on the STA national electronic invoice platform, receive a unique anti-counterfeit digital verification code, and be transmitted to the tax authority at the time of issuance. The fully digitalized electronic invoice is the required anti-fraud digital invoice standard for the helpdesk SaaS revenue stream. Hong Kong SAR does not maintain an electronic invoice clearance or digital stamping mandate for private service contracts.

The STA can claw back the 0% VAT rate where the customs evidence, bank receipts, foreign exchange repatriation, or overseas consumption condition is unverified; the taxpayer must then pay the 6% VAT shortfall plus late-payment interest. Administrative fines may range from 50% to five times the underpaid VAT under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. False customs declarations or forged bank receipts constitute tax evasion and can additionally trigger customs penalties, loss of export tax refund status, and denial of benefits under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland RMB is current-account convertible for genuine trade, services and remittance transactions; SAFE and PBOC enforce authenticity-based verification rather than quota-based conversion. The CN_JV_LTD is a PRC-resident entity, so all cross-border receipts and payments must flow through authorized foreign-exchange bank accounts and be declared in the SAFE Trade in Services and Trade in Goods monitoring systems. A foreign-invested JV may open an FDI capital settlement account after SAFE registration; injected equity capital must be used strictly within the approved business scope and cannot be deployed into prohibited capital-account activities such as unapproved equity investments or entrusted lending. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted after the annual statutory audit, corporate income tax final settlement and non-resident dividend withholding formalities are completed; the remitting bank will require tax payment certificates, audited financial statements and a board resolution. There is no mandatory export-surrendered currency conversion ratio today, but all export-related service income must be repatriated to mainland bank accounts and supported by contracts, invoices and performance evidence. Hong Kong's separate free-convertibility regime remains available for the treasury holding node, but the onshore JV treasury must satisfy SAFE's capital and current-account filing controls.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from Helpdesk/SaaS services must be repatriated to the JV's mainland foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of service performance or export declaration. No mandatory surrender percentage is applied: the JV may retain 100% of the foreign currency in its SAFE-monitored FX account or sell it to an authorized bank voluntarily at the prevailing onshore market rate. SAFE pre-approval is required if the JV intends to retain export proceeds offshore beyond the timeline.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the controlling authorities for the mainland-incorporated joint venture treasury; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulates the Hong Kong SAR banking and treasury node.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Low for direct onshore clearing. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex and Revolut do not hold PBOC non-bank payment institution licenses and cannot open a domestic RMB settlement account or clear through CNAPS for a Chinese-resident JV. These EMIs are legally barred from acting as the primary onshore banking rail. The compliant inbound architecture is: overseas customer or e-commerce platform collects via Stripe Billing/PayPal at the sales layer, then funds are swept to a licensed cross-border payment institution (for example LianLian Global or PayPal China) that holds a PBOC license and SAFE cross-border FX approval, and finally settled into the JV's onshore bank account. Direct payment from an offshore EMI account into the JV's SAFE-registered capital/current account is not permitted. The same limitation applies to the Hong Kong intermediate; an HK treasury account may, however, use licensed HK EMIs only for ancillary settlement before remitting to the mainland.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK investor completes SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration with the local SAFE branch or through the receiving bank's delegated FDI registry. The bank then opens a Capital Settlement Account for the JV; the remitter's SWIFT MT103 must use the purpose code for inbound direct investment equity and field 70 must recite the JV contract and 'FDI equity injection'. After funds are credited, the bank lodges the cross-border income declaration in SAFE's monitoring system and a certified public accountant issues the capital verification report. The capital account proceeds can be converted to RMB through the capital settlement procedure for operating use, but cannot be used for prohibited transactions such as unapproved securities investment, entrusted loans, or repayment of intra-group loans. The remitter name on the wire must exactly match the registered non-resident investor; any deviation will be rejected by the bank's compliance team.Outbound service disbursements should clear via SWIFT MT103 through the onshore settlement bank, supported by the underlying contract, the tax invoice, proof of service delivery and, when applicable, the withholding tax certificate. Because the business is a Helpdesk/SaaS software service, Commercial Letters of Credit (UCP600) and Documentary Collections (URC522) are not operationally relevant for software royalty or subscription flows; they are only used if the JV later imports physical hardware or settles goods-backed trade. If such instruments are required, they must be issued by the mainland bank's trade finance desk and be accompanied by transport documents, commercial invoice, insurance certificate and the SAFE trade declaration form.Onshore CNY/USD FX forwards with SAFE-authorized banks, executed only on the basis of real existing or projected trade/services receivables; no speculative positions.Onshore CNY/USD FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for matched intercompany liquidity and trade flows, subject to SAFE documentary verification and tenor-limit control.Vanilla FX options and zero-cost collars hedging the JV's service-export foreign-currency exposure, provided the hedging notional is no greater than the aggregate contracted receivables.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are permitted for the Hong Kong treasury holding vehicle and for the non-resident shareholder's hedge; the mainland JV may not book onshore NDFs unless a specific SAFE authorization is obtained.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Rating: Low for direct onshore clearing. Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe, Airwallex and Revolut do not hold PBOC non-bank payment institution licenses and cannot open a domestic RMB settlement account or clear through CNAPS for a Chinese-resident JV. These EMIs are legally barred from acting as the primary onshore banking rail. The compliant inbound architecture is: overseas customer or e-commerce platform collects via Stripe Billing/PayPal at the sales layer, then funds are swept to a licensed cross-border payment institution (for example LianLian Global or PayPal China) that holds a PBOC license and SAFE cross-border FX approval, and finally settled into the JV's onshore bank account. Direct payment from an offshore EMI account into the JV's SAFE-registered capital/current account is not permitted. The same limitation applies to the Hong Kong intermediate; an HK treasury account may, however, use licensed HK EMIs only for ancillary settlement before remitting to the mainland.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Enterprise Income Tax rate; reduced to 15% for certified High and New Technology Enterprises, or 10% for certain encouraged software enterprises)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends distributed by a PRC resident company to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Agreement (DTA) when the Hong Kong shareholder directly owns at least 25% of the PRC company’s capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A local transfer pricing file (Local File) must be prepared if, in a single fiscal year, the aggregate amount of related-party transactions reaches any of the following thresholds: (a) tangible asset transfers (excluding interest) exceeding RMB 200 million; (b) financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (c) intangible asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (d) other related-party transactions (including service fees, royalties, and interest) exceeding RMB 40 million. In addition, a Master File is required when the annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion (or where the group combined revenue meets certain global thresholds).

Economic substance scope: China does not have the Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) applicable in HT/HK territories. However, the PRC tax authorities apply general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) and economic substance requirements under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the Implementation Rules. In the context of cross-border structures, the tax authorities examine whether the Hong Kong shareholder or intermediate JV has genuine business presence, personnel, office premises, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) relevant to the income received. Where a Hong Kong entity lacks commercial substance and functions solely as a conduit to obtain treaty benefits, the tax authority may deny treaty relief, recharacterize the transaction, attribute profits to the PRC PE, and apply a 10% withholding tax with penalties. For the PRC-resident JV itself, it must maintain an economic substance profile consistent with its stated business (e.g., active employees, payroll, contracts, and decision-making in mainland China); otherwise, it risks deemed transaction adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law (TACL), the taxpayer shall be ordered to remit the overdue tax within a prescribed time limit, with a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day and a fine of between 50% and 500% of the unpaid tax. Where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 100,000 and accounts for more than 10% of the taxable income, Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law imposes criminal liability: up to 3 years of imprisonment or detention and a criminal fine; for huge amounts or aggravated circumstances, 3 to 7 years of imprisonment plus a criminal fine.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false tax returns or participate in profit concealment can be pierced through the corporate veil under the PRC General Principles of Civil Law and the Company Law. They are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, fines, and surcharges. The tax authority may impose a travel ban (blacklisting) on the legal representative and principal directors, preventing them from leaving China or acquiring new business licenses. Criminal convictions for tax evasion can result in personal imprisonment from 6 months to 7 years, and a prohibition against serving as a company director.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may reassess the taxable income under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and impose an adjustment with a compulsory interest supplement: base interest rate plus 5 percentage points. Under TACL Article 44, failure to provide transfer pricing documentation triggers a special audit adjustment and a fine of up to RMB 50,000. For intentional transfer pricing adjustment, a fine of up to 50% of the underpaid tax may also be imposed under TACL Article 61.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve inaccurate transfer pricing reports or who fail to maintain documentation can be held personally liable in a civil restitution suit by the tax authorities. They may be blacklisted from exit, and their personal assets may be frozen to cover the additional tax, interest, and penalties. In aggravated cases (where transfer pricing manipulation is treated as tax evasion), directors face criminal prosecution under Article 201, leading to imprisonment and fines.
Fraudulent Invoicing and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Issuing, obtaining, or knowingly using false VAT invoices may violate Article 205 of the PRC Criminal Law (false issuance of special VAT invoices). Punishment ranges from 3 to 10 years' imprisonment with fines, up to life imprisonment where the amount involved is huge or the circumstances are especially serious. Additionally, TACL Article 71 authorizes tax authorities to impose a fine of 1 to 5 times the illegal amount and to void all fraudulent tax offsets. The company may lose its general taxpayer status, be subject to VAT invoice controls, and have its tax registration revoked.

Director personal liability: Courts have pierced the corporate veil in fraudulent invoicing cases to hold directors personally liable for all unpaid taxes and penalties. Directors may face travel bans, asset seizures, and personal bankruptcy proceedings. Criminal penalties include imprisonment from 3 years up to life, depending on the amount of false invoices involved, and confiscation of personal or business property used in the crime. Additionally, the director may be disqualified from acting as a director of any company for a period of up to 10 years.
