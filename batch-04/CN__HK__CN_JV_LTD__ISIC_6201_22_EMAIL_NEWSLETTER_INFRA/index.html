Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not accepted by mainland China tax authorities or commercial banks for the IT/email-infrastructure industry. The Company Law requires a registered domicile with a physical address capable of receiving legal process and regulatory correspondence. The State Taxation Administration and commercial banks uniformly reject applications from entities using virtual office addresses, as they fail the 'place of actual management' test under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Therefore, a physical lease must be secured with a valid property ownership certificate and a formal lease agreement. Co-working spaces with dedicated private offices and subleases may be accepted if properly documented, but pure virtual addresses or mail-forwarding services are per se insufficient for this industry. Banks also require an on-site visit for account opening; without a physical office, the account application will be refused.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) - Electronic Taxpayer Registration Portal: 国家税务总局电子税务局 (www.etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporated in China, the process begins with obtaining the Business License from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and assigning a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the taxpayer identification number. Tax registration is automatically completed through data sharing between SAMR and the STA. The JV must then complete supplementary tax registration within 30 days via the electronic tax bureau portal, providing the Foreign Enterprise Certificate (if applicable), articles of association, investor identity documents, business license, bank account details, and information on the legal representative and tax responsible persons. The electronic system issues a taxpayer status confirmation. For foreign non-resident entities without incorporation, a non-resident taxpayer identification number is obtained by filing Form W-8 equivalents under the Domestic Tax Administration Rules with the local STA office, or by applying through the competent tax authority of the residence country under a Double Tax Agreement. The effective VAT registration for the JV is automatic upon issuance of the USCC, but activation of VAT invoice management system and electronic invoicing requires additional registration with the local tax authority.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For general VAT taxpayers: annual sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 for service-oriented enterprises. Below this threshold, registration as a small-scale taxpayer is possible with a simplified levy rate of 3% or 1% under certain preferential policies. Foreign entities engaged in taxable activities or cross-border services may be required to register regardless of threshold when they have a China presence or a withholding obligation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods by a registered VAT general taxpayer qualifies for zero-rating under Article 15 of the Provisional Regulations on VAT, provided the goods are actually exported and customs filings are genuine. For email newsletter SaaS services provided by a Chinese entity to offshore customers, the services are treated as exported services if the recipient is domiciled outside China and the service is consumed entirely outside China. Under the 'zero-rating for cross-border services' policy (Notice Caishui [2016] No. 36), qualifying offshore software services, information technology services, and platform services supplied to foreign businesses or individuals can be zero-rated, provided the contract, payment evidence, and service descriptions substantiate the offshore nature. The JV must also prove that the services do not relate to tangible property located in China and that no Chinese establishment of the foreign recipient benefits. For pure email infrastructure services, the critical condition is that the foreign recipient is not a Chinese resident and the platform has no Chinese user telecom access.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for physical goods - Electronic customs declaration form (出口货物报关单) with customs assigned registration number
  • Export contract or commercial invoice (跨境服务合同 or 商业发票) showing the foreign recipient and service description
  • Proof of cross-border foreign exchange receipt from the bank (涉外收入申报单 or Bank Receipt Notification) - equivalent to Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill for physical exports (海运提单/空运提单) or a certificate of virtual services delivery (电子服务交付凭证) for digital services
  • Service completion certificate or customer acceptance record (境外客户签收单/服务验收凭证)
  • Electronic VAT invoice issued with zero tax rate (零税率普通发票) if no special export declaration is required
  • For digital services: system logs or platform records evidencing remote service delivery and foreign IP or foreign billing address of the customer

China mandates full digital electronic VAT invoicing (全电发票) since 2023, progressively expanded nationwide. Taxpayers must issue invoices through the integrated electronic invoicing system under the STA digital platform (国家税务总局电子发票服务平台). Real-time approval is not used as in Latin America; instead, the system requires secure API connections for invoice issuance, submission of VAT return data, and compliance with the 'one-invoice-one-code' anti-fraud stamping mechanism. Certain high-risk industries require blockchain-based invoice traceability under the pilot program. For cross-border exports, the customs declaration is electronically transmitted to the tax authority for zero-rating validation.

The tax authorities may scrutinize export zero-rating claims during annual audits and special anti-fraud campaigns. Fraudulent zero-rating or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days may result in the reversal of zero-rating to export at standard VAT rates, plus late payment penalties (0.05% per day on outstanding tax), and a fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the amount of tax evaded under Article 63 of the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. In severe cases, criminal liability under smuggling or VAT fraud offense may apply with sentences up to 10 years or indefinite imprisonment. Retrospective clawback is possible for up to 10 years. Additionally, the 'false export' crime under the Customs Law can lead to loss of export tax rebate eligibility and suspension of customs credit status under the AEO program.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a managed and bifurcated convertibility framework. Current-account transactions, including SaaS subscription export revenues, operating expenses, and profit remittances, are convertible after submission of genuine contracts, invoices, and tax-related documentation to a licensed domestic bank. Capital-account transactions, including the HK parent's injection of registered capital into the Mainland Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, are not freely convertible and require a valid SAFE FDI registration, a capital-account opening confirmation from the designated bank, and use of a dedicated FDI capital escrow account. Surrender requirements are no longer a fixed percentage for ordinary current-account receipts; instead, the enterprise may retain foreign currency in a current-account FX settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB. Profit and dividend remittance to the HK parent is treated as investment income; it requires audited PRC GAAP financial statements, proof of payment of enterprise income tax and withholding tax, a board-approved distribution resolution, and confirmation that no SAFE compliance violations are outstanding. In practice, all capital injections and outbound remittances are pre-screened by the accepting bank, but there is no blanket prohibition; the regime is a risk-based and document-intensive control framework rather than full liberalization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export service proceeds must be repatriated to an onshore current account in the name of the Mainland JV within 90 days from the invoice date or service completion date. Mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent for current-account export proceeds because SAFE permits voluntary retention and voluntary settlement; however, each receipt must be matched with a customs declaration for goods or a service transaction declaration for software and email infrastructure, and the receiving bank must assign a valid foreign-exchange transaction purpose code. Failure to document the receipt can result in a frozen credit and a suspicious transaction report.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), acting as the foreign-exchange regulator; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) governs only the HKD clearing and offshore banking side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

OFFSHORE EMI RATING: NON-VIABLE for the Mainland JV. Offshore digital EMI banking providers are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open accounts for a Chinese domestic legal entity, cannot perform RMB clearing, cannot be recognized for FDI capital registration, and their use would unlawfully bypass the requirement that all foreign-currency receipts and payments pass through a licensed bank of foreign exchange. An exception may exist for the HK parent under an HKMA licensed virtual bank, but the Mainland CN_JV_LTD must not treat any EMI as its primary treasury or clearing rail. If Stripe Billing or credit-card receipts are collected through an overseas affiliate, those funds must be swept into a licensed HK bank account and then remitted to the Mainland JV as a documented current-account service fee or as a registered capital injection through a Tier-1 bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File: mandatory if the multinational group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds EUR 750 million (approx. HKD 6.8 billion). Local File: required where the HK entity's aggregate controlled transactions with overseas connected persons exceed HKD 10 million in any category in the basis period.

Economic substance scope: No standalone Economic Substance Regulation exists, but the territorial source doctrine requires the core income-generating activities (CIGA) of the email newsletter infrastructure business (e.g., contract negotiation, server hosting, data processing, customer support) to be performed in Hong Kong to tax profits as Hong Kong sourced. For offshore claims, the IRD will scrutinise the substance; a shell operation with all CIGA outside Hong Kong may be denied the offshore deduction.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion (Fraudulent Filing)

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance, Section 82: upon conviction, a fine of up to 300% of the tax undercharged and imprisonment for up to 3 years; in addition, default surcharges may be imposed.

Director personal liability: The director who signed the materially incorrect return may be prosecuted criminally; the corporate veil is pierced to recover the unpaid tax from personal assets; a travel restriction blacklisting order may be issued for non-payment.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance (FSIE Abuse)

Statutory sanction: Sections 16(1), 20(2), 61, 61A and 61B of the IRO: the IRD may disallow the deduction, reconstruct the pricing to an arm's length standard, impose additional assessments, and levy penalties up to 100% of the tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors can be personally assessed for the overdue tax if the company is unable to pay, and they may face criminal sanctions for wilfully allowing the preparation of false documentation, including imprisonment and disqualification as a director.
Offshore Claim Abuse / Shell Company (Conduit)

Statutory sanction: IRO Sections 82 and 82A: tax evasion penalties up to 300% of tax undercharged, plus interest; the IRD may issue accelerated assessments and freezing orders. Under anti-abuse rules (DTA Article 29), treaty benefits may be denied.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return or participate in the shell arrangement may be held personally liable for the tax debt and subject to criminal prosecution, personal bankruptcy, and blacklisting. Civil recovery actions can be taken against directors who accepted distributions while the company was insolvent.
