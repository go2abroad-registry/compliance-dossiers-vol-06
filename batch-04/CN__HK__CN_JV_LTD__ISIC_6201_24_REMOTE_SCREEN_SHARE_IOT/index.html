Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online filing/portal: IRD eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk) and GovHK (https://www.gov.hk)

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-incorporated entity that is commercially described as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the entity will be classified for Hong Kong tax purposes as an ordinary company limited by shares under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), unless a different Hong Kong vehicle is used. The procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate with the Companies Registry and obtain the Company Registration Number; (2) within 30 days of commencement of business, apply to the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the Business Registration number is the TIN for Hong Kong tax and is used in all correspondence with the IRD; (3) the IRD opens a Profits Tax file and issues a tax file reference when the first Profits Tax Return is issued or when a Tax Residence Certificate is requested. A foreign non-resident entity with a Hong Kong branch must complete Part 16 registration under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) with the Companies Registry and then register for Business Registration under Cap. 310 within 30 days, submitting its overseas incorporation certificate and constitutional documents. No separate VAT identification number exists because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a currency board system under the HKMA in which the Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible and freely negotiable. There are no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, no capital account approval gates, and no central bank permit needed for current account or capital account transactions executed by a Hong Kong-incorporated entity. Businesses may hold foreign currency balances in HKD, USD, CNH, EUR and other currencies, may remit dividends and capital profits abroad, and may receive subscription funds from overseas shareholders without HKMA prior approval. The pegged regime narrows the USD/HKD band to 7.75-7.85 but does not impede convertibility. For the Chinese parent involved in this cross-border JV, the People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime where current account transactions are convertible after verification but capital account outbound investments remain controlled and must be registered with SAFE. Therefore, the Hong Kong JV enjoys full convertibility, while the PRC shareholder's injection is subject to outbound direct investment registration and tax filing before the funds can clear to Hong Kong. The main routine obstacles are not HK-side but China-side: valuation verification, NDRC/MOFCOM filing, SAFE registration, and use of a compliant bank purpose code.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong entity there is no statutory surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio and no deadline for bringing export proceeds onshore; proceeds from software licence subscriptions earned in USD, EUR or CNH may be retained indefinitely in multi-currency accounts or converted freely. If the mainland Chinese parent were the exporter of services, SAFE would require the export foreign exchange proceeds to be repatriated and submitted for trade verification within 90 days after the customs or service trade declaration date, and the PRC enterprise may keep the proceeds in a foreign currency account without compulsory conversion into RMB, but the receipt must be linked to a valid underlying contract and SAFE registration.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA), the de facto central bank and currency board authority of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with additional applicability of State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for any mainland China cross-border capital flow to or from the Chinese shareholder.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Statrys, Neat and Currenxie are not prohibited from onboarding a Hong Kong JV, but they are licensed as Money Service Operators or Stored Value Facilities under the Hong Kong Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Financing of Terrorism Ordinance, not as authorised banks under the Banking Ordinance. They cannot take deposits, are not covered by the HK Deposit Protection Scheme, cannot issue a banker's certificate for capital verification, and lack direct CHATS clearing membership. They are legally viable as a secondary collection channel for low-value Stripe and Paddle software subscription receivables, but they should not be the primary treasury rail for SAFE-registered inbound capital injections, dividend repatriation, or intercompany settlement requiring audit-grade bank confirmation. The practical feasibility rating is Medium for ancillary sales collection and Low for principal cross-border treasury clearing due to the mainland Chinese ownership complexity and bank-account transparency expectations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant route for a mainland Chinese shareholder to capitalise the Hong Kong JV begins with domestic approval: the shareholder must complete the National Development and Reform Commission outbound investment filing, the Ministry of Commerce ODI certificate, and SAFE foreign exchange registration at the PRC remitting bank. The remitting bank then executes a SWIFT MT103 from the shareholder's corporate account to the Hong Kong JV's designated HKD, USD or CNH capital escrow account, quoting the SAFE ODI registration number, the PRC tax identification reference and the bank-designated ODI equity investment purpose code. On the Hong Kong side the receiving bank will credit the capital contribution to the restricted escrow account, and a Hong Kong certified public accountant should thereafter sign a capital verification report confirming the paid-up capital and share premium. No HKMA approval is required for the Hong Kong entity to receive the funds, but the bank will refuse the credit if the remitter is a third party or if the SAFE documentation is incomplete.Operational disbursements for cloud hosting, subcontractor engineering, licensing fees, sales commissions and payroll should be settled via SWIFT MT103 using CHATS for HKD/USD same-day clearing or via the CIPS/CHATS RMB bridge when paying mainland vendors in CNY. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are appropriate only for physical IoT hardware or component procurement where negotiable shipping documents are involved; for intangible software and remote-access services the industry standard is clean telegraphic transfer supported by a contract and invoice. Dividends and capital repatriation back to the Chinese parent must be made through a licensed Hong Kong bank using SWIFT MT103 after an auditable declaration of distributable profits and Hong Kong profits tax considerations, and the PRC recipient bank will apply its inbound receipt verification and tax withholding procedures.Deliverable FX forward contracts with HKMA-authorised licensed banks for USD/HKD, USD/CNH and HKD/CNH up to 12 months, documented against underlying software receivables, cloud hosting costs or known intercompany settlement exposure.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards and deliverable CNH forwards executed in the Hong Kong offshore RMB market, where the mainland parent's net settlement is converted offshore and subject to no PRC capital account restriction at the HK-side transaction level.Cross-currency interest rate swaps and plain-vanilla FX swaps with HKMA-authorised banks for matching tenor of shareholder loans or trade receivables and payables, provided the hedge has a genuine commercial underlying and the JV does not enter speculative, leveraged or crypto-based derivative instruments.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Hong Kong has no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, revenue, or headcount exemption that releases an active limited company from audit. Under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), the directors of every non-dormant Hong Kong incorporated company must prepare accounts and the company must engage a Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant as auditor to report on those accounts for each financial year. The sole statutory carve-out is a dormant private company that has formally applied to the Registrar for a declaration of dormancy; a dormant company may elect not to pass audit-only reporting obligations while dormant. For all active companies, irrespective of size or business class, the audited financial statements must accompany the Profits Tax Return (Form BIR51) filed with the IRD.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate on worldwide taxable income of the Chinese-resident Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture under Article 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law of the People's Republic of China

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% domestic withholding Corporate Income Tax on dividends paid by a Chinese-resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return and final payment must be filed no later than 5 months after the fiscal year-end, ordinarily by 31 May of the immediately following calendar year. Quarterly provisional returns are due within 15 days after each quarter close.
