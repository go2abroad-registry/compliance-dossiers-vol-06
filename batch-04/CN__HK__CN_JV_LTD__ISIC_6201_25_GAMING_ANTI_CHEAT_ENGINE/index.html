VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC: all entities must tax-register upon establishment; there is no monetary turnover threshold for VAT registration. Mandatory conversion to general VAT taxpayer status applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, while annual taxable sales at or below CNY 5,000,000 may remain small-scale taxpayer status. HK: no VAT/GST registration threshold exists because no such tax is imposed; only Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance is required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law and the Ministry of Finance/STA measures on cross-border taxable services, export sales of goods are zero-rated, and qualifying cross-border taxable services, including software middleware licensing, technical support and anti-cheat engine deployment supplied to non-resident recipients, are zero-rated or exempted provided the recipient is located outside Mainland China, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, the consideration is received in foreign exchange, and the exporter files the cross-border service zero-rating declaration on the STA electronic platform. For physical exports, the Customs export declaration and foreign exchange collection verification must be completed. Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST and therefore no zero-rating is claimed; under the territorial source principle, profits from exports are not subject to Hong Kong profits tax if the goods are sold or services are rendered and the contract was negotiated and concluded outside or ex-Hong Kong, supported by the full transactional trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form issued by the Customs of the People's Republic of China for physical exports or for software delivered on physical media
  • Export Bill of Lading or Master Air Waybill evidencing the movement of goods out of Mainland China
  • Cross-Border Taxable Service Export Contract or Software Licensing Agreement executed with the overseas customer
  • Digital delivery evidence for anti-cheat middleware, including activation keys, licensing server logs, API access timestamps, or server-side download records demonstrating overseas consumption outside Mainland China
  • Report on Receipt of Foreign Exchange under the integrated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) system showing foreign currency received and repatriated to the PRC exporting entity's bank account
  • Full Digital Electronic Invoice or Special VAT Invoice issued through the STA platform for the zero-rated export transaction
  • Acknowledgment or filing record of the cross-border taxable service zero-rating declaration accepted by the STA electronic portal

Mainland China operates a real-time fiscal electronic invoice infrastructure through the STA e-Tax platform. General VAT invoices and Special VAT invoices are issued through the VAT invoice control system, with invoice data transmitted to the STA in near real time. Full Digital Electronic Invoices are generated on the e-Tax system and carry a unique QR code and encrypted electronic seal, eliminating the need for a physical fiscal stamp. High-volume taxpayers may use STA-approved APIs for automated machine-to-machine invoice issuance and reporting. Hong Kong does not mandate an electronic invoicing clearance system or real-time fiscal stamping; ordinary commercial invoices and receipts governed by IRD record-keeping rules are acceptable.

The STA, SAFE and Customs may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If the zero-rating is unsupported or documentation is deficient, the exported transaction is recharacterized as domestic taxable supply, the VAT at the applicable rate is clawed back with statutory late payment interest, and administrative penalties from 0.5 to 5 times the underpaid tax may be imposed for fraudulent or negligent zero-rating. Criminal liability for tax evasion and smuggling penalties can arise in serious cases. In Hong Kong, inaccurate territorial-source assertions are reassessed by the IRD; onshore profits are charged at the prevailing profits tax rate of 16.5% for corporations, with the two-tier rate of 8.25% applied to the first HK$2 million assessable profits, plus interest and potential penalties up to three times the tax undercharged. Retrospective assessments are available under applicable limitation periods, with no limitation for fraudulent concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under mainland China's FX framework, the RMB is convertible on the current account for genuine trade and services transactions, but the capital account is heavily administered by SAFE. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD) must open a SAFE-registered FDI capital account for inbound equity subscriptions; RMB converted from foreign-exchange capital can be deployed only within the approved business scope and cannot be used for equity investment, securities, wealth-management products, or unauthorized on-lending. Current-account export proceeds are subject to repatriation into China but no longer to a fixed mandatory surrender ratio; the JV may retain FX in a designated settlement account or convert it to RMB at a domestic Tier-1 bank. Profit remittance to foreign shareholders is permitted but requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, a board-approved dividend resolution, and SAFE transaction reporting; dividend withholding tax applies. Hong Kong SAR has a fully convertible HKD, no foreign-exchange controls, no surrender rules, and no profit-remittance approvals, making it the natural treasury conduit. Every cross-border flow between the mainland JV and the HK treasury vehicle must nevertheless cross the PBoC/SAFE barrier using correct BOP purpose codes, underlying contracts, and bank verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, export proceeds from the JV's software and middleware sales must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or RMB account at a designated bank by the contractual payment due date; if the contract contains no due date, the bank applies a practical 90-day collection benchmark from the invoice or services-completion date, and receipts beyond that window require deferred-receivables registration or a supplementary SAFE explanation. There is no fixed statutory percentage of export receipts that must be converted into RMB; the JV may retain all collected FX or convert at its own discretion. For Hong Kong, there is no surrender requirement and no conversion mandate for HKD or any foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), for mainland China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the linked-rate central banking authority for Hong Kong SAR.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is LOW as a primary treasury rail for the mainland JV, but MEDIUM-HIGH as an ancillary collection and payout channel for the Hong Kong treasury entity. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Neat, Statrys, and Payhawk are not licensed Chinese commercial banks and are legally barred from opening the RMB basic deposit account, the FDI capital settlement account, or the foreign-exchange settlement account that PBoC/SAFE requires the mainland JV to maintain. They also cannot perform export-proceeds repatriation, capital verification, dividend tax clearance, or BOP purpose-code reporting. For the HK entity, a HKMA-authorized bank or licensed EMI may support multi-currency collections, Stripe payouts, and low-value operational cards, but all balances must be swept to the tier-1 bank account at least daily to ensure final clearing in CHATS/CIPS. The EMI must never be used for SAFE-regulated capital-account transactions involving the mainland JV.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, a contemporaneous Local File is mandatory for a mainland enterprise where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for service transactions; a Master File may also be required where the MNE group's related-party transaction scale and consolidated revenue meet the statutory thresholds.

Economic substance scope: Although the People's Republic of China does not apply a UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, the State Taxation Administration applies the OECD's principal-purpose test and the beneficial-ownership doctrine under SAT Public Notice [2018] No. 9. The Hong Kong intermediate holding and IP licensing entity must demonstrate substantive business operations, actual premises, qualified employees, significant decision-making authority, and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong. Substance is required both to claim treaty relief and to validate cross-border charges; a Hong Kong mailbox company or pass-through IP conduit will be disregarded for tax treaty purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company and DTA Treaty Shopping Abuse

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may invoke the General Anti-Abuse Rule under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice [2018] No. 9 to deny the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA benefits, reassess the full 10% statutory withholding tax or adjust the mainland JV's deductions, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose an additional penalty of 50% to three times the tax understated.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 where the director used the corporate form to abuse limited liability; the director becomes jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and civil restitution. The tax authority may blacklist the director under the major tax violation list, leading to exit restrictions and travel bans under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, and criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years for a major evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Defective Related-Party Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority makes a special tax adjustment under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41, recharacterizes or reallocates profits, disallows excessive related-party charges, collects additional tax, applies daily interest of 0.05% on the underpayment, and imposes penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law; failure to file required TP documentation may also attract a separate administrative fine under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42.

Director personal liability: If directors knowingly sign false related-party agreements, approve fictitious invoices, or cause the preparation of fraudulent TP documents, they may be treated as directly responsible managers under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 and face imprisonment of up to 7 years, fines, civil damages to the corporate treasury, and exit bans or blacklisting as seriously dishonest taxpayers.
Criminal Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Violation triggers Criminal Law Article 201 where the evaded tax amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax due, with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years; civil and administrative penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law include recovery of the unpaid tax, a daily surcharge of 0.05%, and a penalty of 50% to three times the evaded tax for the corporate taxpayer, plus additional withholding-agent penalties of 50% to three times the untaxed amount under Article 69.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the director used the company as a fraud device; directors may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of the evaded tax. The tax authority can blacklist directors, impose travel and exit bans, and the criminal court may sentence the responsible director to up to 7 years imprisonment, concurrently with monetary fines and confiscation of the proceeds of tax evasion.
