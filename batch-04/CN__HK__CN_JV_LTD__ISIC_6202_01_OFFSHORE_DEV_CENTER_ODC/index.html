Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, including the Business Registration Office; online filing and business registration are conducted through the GovHK eTAX portal (https://www.etax.gov.hk) and Business Registration Online (https://www.businessregistration.gov.hk). E-government filing portal

The statutory taxpayer identification number for the Hong Kong entity is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The procurement procedure for a foreign-owned non-resident joint venture company is: (1) incorporate the company with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), obtaining the Certificate of Incorporation and the registered office information; (2) within one month of commencement of business, submit Form IRBR 1 (Application for Business Registration) to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department, accompanied by the Certificate of Incorporation, certificate of registered office, identification documents of directors and ultimate beneficial owners, and a declaration of the principal business activity (ISIC 6202 offshore software engineering and maintenance); (3) the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the BRN, which is the tax identification number for profits tax, employer returns, and all IRD correspondence; (4) if the entity has no Hong Kong registered office, it must appoint a Hong Kong resident authorised representative and provide an address for service; (5) non-resident shareholders are permitted and no minimum capital is required under Hong Kong law; (6) no separate VAT/GST registration is required because Hong Kong imposes no general indirect tax.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election, no hybrid mismatch election, and no shareholder pass-through election for limited companies. Profits tax is imposed at the entity level under the territorial source principle in the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). The two-tiered corporate profits tax rate (8.25% on assessable profits up to HKD 2,000,000 and 16.5% on the remainder) applies automatically to qualifying corporations, subject to anti-fragmentation rules for connected entities.

For this compliance policy, a 180-day inward foreign exchange documentation window is applied to substantiate the offshore income claim, although Hong Kong does not impose a statutory export proceeds repatriation deadline. If the Inland Revenue Department audits the offshore claim and determines that the substantive profit-generating activities occurred in Hong Kong, the income will be recharacterised as Hong Kong-source and assessed to profits tax at the two-tier corporate rates. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict current account convertibility and heavily regulated capital account transactions. For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (JV) acting as an Offshore Development Center (ODC), the critical friction point is the distinction between current account items, which generally permit conversion upon submission of real trade or service documentation, and capital account items, which require SAFE approval, registration, and quota allocation. The JV's inbound capital injections from the Hong Kong parent or offshore investors must be routed through a foreign exchange capital account opened with a domestic designated bank, and each injection requires SAFE capital project registration, usually through the bank's digital SAFE system, with the actual foreign currency conversion subject to the prevailing settlement rules (payment of genuine transaction, so-called 'pay-as-you-go' principle). Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is allowed under current account treatment only if the JV has completed its annual foreign exchange inspection, provided audited financial statements, tax clearance, and board resolutions are in place; however, the bank will scrutinize the underlying software service revenue recognition to ensure the remitted amount does not exceed the verified distributable profits. Exported software and services are generally subject to a mandatory surrender rule that no longer requires full forced conversion, but the JV must repatriate all offshore receipts into China within the statutory timeline. There are no direct surrender ratio requirements for private enterprises; instead, all export proceeds in foreign currency must either be sold to a domestic bank for CNY or kept in a foreign currency settlement account within the repatriation window. The capital account remains non-convertible for speculative purposes, and any attempt to route funds through Hong Kong without underlying real service contracts would be classified as round-tripping or disguised capital flight, triggering anti-money laundering (AML) and foreign exchange violation investigations. The JV must also maintain a proper foreign debt quota if it intends to receive intercompany loans from the Hong Kong shareholder, with registration via the PBOC/SAFE foreign debt macro-prudential management system. Dividends, technical service fees, and royalty payments to non-resident parents must be supported by tax invoices (fapiao), withholding tax filings, and transfer pricing documentation to avoid beneficial ownership denial under the double tax agreement between Mainland China and Hong Kong SAR.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds of the JV for software services rendered to the Hong Kong parent or overseas clients must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of the relevant customs declaration or service completion, or within the period explicitly approved under the foreign trade contract, and must be credited to the JV's export settlement foreign exchange account at a domestic designated bank. There is no mandatory fixed statutory conversion ratio requiring sale of 100% of proceeds into CNY; the JV may retain such foreign currency in its settlement account, but every receipt beyond the threshold must be reported to SAFE through the bank's monitoring system, and conversion to CNY is permissible upon proof of genuine trade in services such as the software development contract, invoice, and payment receipt from the overseas counter-party.

Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Wise Business, Payoneer, and similar non-Chinese-licensed fintech providers have Low viability as the primary account or clearing rail for the JV's domestic treasury operations in Mainland China, because they are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions by the National Financial Regulatory Administration and do not provide local CNY bank account numbers with CNAPS routing compliance. They cannot legally hold CNY client funds, offer RMB accounts, or process SAFE-reportable trade service transactions on behalf of a Chinese-resident JV entity. They can be used only as peripheral receiving tools for small-margin overseas collections from non-China sources, subject to the JV's own Chinese bank repatriation obligations and tax reporting. The core corporate banking structure must remain with domestic tier-1 commercial banks that are SAFE-designated and able to complete foreign exchange registration, capital verification, and outward remittance filings.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The JV's Hong Kong parent or offshore shareholders must remit equity injection funds via SWIFT MT103 or Fedwire-equivalent to the JV's foreign exchange capital account at the designated Chinese commercial bank. The payment must include a correct purpose code, specifically Capital Account - Foreign Direct Investment Equity Injection, typically SAFE code 6011 for payment of capital injection under a foreign-invested enterprise. The remittance should be accompanied by an email or bank instruction referencing the JV business license number, the foreign investment registration code, and the contract. Once funds arrive, the bank will conduct capital verification with a Chinese certified public accountant, input the transaction into SAFE's Foreign Exchange Account System, and release the funds for use only after the pay-as-you-go rule is satisfied for actual operating expenses such as payroll, rent, and IT procurement.For operational disbursements from the Chinese JV to overseas contractors, vendors, or the HK parent (to the extent permitted), the primary clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for direct wire transfers with full underlying documentation; for larger equipment or software licensing acquisitions, Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight or usance) and Documentary Collections may be used when the counterparty requires bank-verified documentary assurance; all outbound remittances must be tagged with the appropriate current-account payment purpose code, for instance 121010 for software licensing or technical service fee, and must be backed by the signed contract, invoice, fapiao, withholding tax certificate if applicable, and the bank's foreign exchange payment application form.In-principle permissible CNY/USD and CNY/HKD FX forward contracts with domestic banks for genuine trade-in-services exposures under the JV's real underlying orders; the contracts require a bank-approved FX risk management file and the JV must demonstrate economic exposure from its software export contracts; speculative overlaying is prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for the Chinese JV onshore; any offshore NDF exposure must instead be converted into deliverable onshore forwards with a licensed Chinese bank, subject to the aggregate position and real demand verification rules.Cross-currency swaps are permissible only for genuine financing flows, such as converting a USD or HKD-denominated intercompany loan into CNY with a direct matching interest payment structure; all derivatives must be transacted with the JV's designated tier-1 Chinese bank and must comply with SAFE derivative registration and PBOC Macro-prudential windows.
