None. A company limited by shares incorporated in Hong Kong has no minimum paid-up capital requirement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the integrated Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) online portal; for Hong Kong SAR operations, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the eTAX electronic filing system and the IRD Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) automatically generates a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number in China under the PRC Tax Administration Law; the SAMR registration is synchronized to the STA tax file and no separate VAT registration number is issued because the USCC serves as the TIN/VAT ID. Foreign non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment but deriving PRC-source income must complete non-resident tax registration with the competent STA office or appoint the PRC payer as withholding agent in order to obtain a non-resident TIN; this is applied for through the Electronic Tax Administration portal. For a Hong Kong SAR entity, a non-resident carrying on business must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the Hong Kong TIN; a non-resident not carrying on a Hong Kong business may apply directly for an IRD Number where a tax identity is required.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident Sino-Foreign Equity JV taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through); Territorial_Scope applies to any Hong Kong SAR branch or non-resident Hong Kong taxpayer under the Inland Revenue Ordinance; Tax_Exempt_Zone status is not default and only arises with specific PRC preferential-zone approval.

Available election forms

None for U.S.-style check-the-box elections or discretionary hybrid mismatch elections; PRC and Hong Kong do not permit pass-through classification. The only relevant statutory election is the PRC VAT general taxpayer status election for taxpayers below the mandatory turnover threshold; OECD hybrid mismatch outcomes are addressed through domestic CFC and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) provisions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) of annual taxable turnover; mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed this threshold, while taxpayers below it are classified as small-scale VAT taxpayers unless they elect general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC Interim Regulations on VAT and the Measures for the Administration of Export Tax Refund (Exemption), goods physically exported from mainland China are eligible for VAT zero-rating and export refund/exemption where the goods leave PRC customs territory, are recorded in an electronic customs export declaration, transfer title and risk to an overseas buyer, and are paid for in foreign currency with export proceeds repatriated to China. Cross-border services supplied to non-resident recipients are zero-rated only where performed entirely outside mainland China and consumed wholly abroad; data-center colocation and bare-metal hosting performed and consumed at server facilities inside mainland China generally remain domestic taxable services and do not qualify for zero-rating, except that separately invoiced export sales of server hardware delivered overseas may qualify. Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT and therefore has no export zero-rating mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration) filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Customs release or export clearance record from the General Administration of Customs
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the goods from PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • VAT export invoice or ordinary VAT invoice required in the STA export tax-refund application package
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt record evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds

China mandates the national Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under Golden Tax Project Phase IV. FDEIs are issued through the Electronic Tax Administration platform or integrated enterprise systems using approved APIs; each FDEI carries a unique tax-control number, encrypted QR anti-counterfeiting stamp, and digital signature, and invoice data is transmitted to STA in near-real time. Invoice records, customs export declarations, and foreign-exchange receipts are cross-matched via the Golden Tax system and the China International Trade Single Window. Hong Kong SAR has no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate because no VAT/GST regime exists.

STA and the General Administration of Customs cross-audit export declarations, logistics data, VAT export invoices, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. A zero-rating claim that is fraudulent, unsupported, inconsistent, or missing any mandatory documentation will be disallowed, with the VAT refund/exemption clawed back and a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day imposed. Under the PRC Tax Administration Law, false or inaccurate export tax refund claims attract fines from 0.5 times to 5 times the tax evaded, and fraudulent export declarations can additionally lead to customs fines, confiscation of goods, and criminal liability for export VAT fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB current-account items are fundamentally convertible for bona fide trade in services under the PBOC/SAFE framework, meaning cross-border colocation leasing fees, managed hosting service fees, and monthly ACH receipts can be settled freely after bank-level authenticity verification and matching against the tax, contract, and customs transaction file. Capital-account transactions, including the HK investor's registered capital injection, shareholder loan disbursement, and any foreign debt financing, remain strictly managed: each flow requires prior SAFE foreign-exchange registration, a dedicated FCY capital account, and a permitted settlement purpose. The entity cannot presume free convertibility for capital transactions, and any cross-border transfer from HK must be evidenced by board resolutions, audited or provisional financial statements, and the underlying lease, building, and equipment procurement contracts. Surrender requirements are now largely repatriation rather than forced conversion: export proceeds are required to be brought back to a domestic bank and either retained in a foreign-currency account or voluntarily sold to the bank, with any conversion verified against the customs electronic data; there is no fixed percentage being forcibly converted into RMB. Profit remittance hurdles remain the most sensitive: dividends may be remitted only after annual audit, statutory tax clearance, tax payment certificate, board dividend resolution, and SAFE/ISO capital account confirmation; the remitting bank will require the latest salary and social security payment evidence to confirm ongoing operational substance. Given the low-risk IT colocation model, banks still expect a detailed service contract, transfer-pricing file, and evidence that no shell-like intra-group arrangements are being used to bypass China's FX control.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Administration of Payment, Settlement and Verification of Trade in Goods regime, export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated into a domestic designating bank account within 180 days of the export customs declaration date, or by the earlier contractual payment date if stipulated; the JV may request a documented deferral only for exceptional circumstances. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent because China abolished forced surrender of export proceeds in 2012; the JV may retain the FCY receipts in an eligible domestic foreign-currency account or sell them to the bank at the market rate. Every receipt must be matched against China Customs electronic export data and the bank's accurate declaration record, with service-related proceeds from cross-border colocation receipts treated under the current-account services ledger with the same repatriation verification but no statutory conversion percentage.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China, delegated to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange for mainland PRC foreign-exchange controls; Hong Kong Monetary Authority supervises the offshore Hong Kong dollar clearing and cross-border bank interoperability side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as global payment platforms and virtual banking wallets are legally barred from serving as the primary transaction account, clearing channel, or treasury rail for an onshore PRC Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and PBOC payment-licensing requirements, only a licensed bank can conduct foreign-exchange settlement and cross-border RMB/FX clearing on behalf of a Chinese corporate entity; an offshore EMI lacks a SAFE forex licence, cannot obtain a capital-account quota, and cannot operate statutory dividend, capital injection, or trade settlement flows. Feasibility rating: low for core treasury purposes, with only ancillary utility for HK-side employee cards or travel expenditure that does not touch the mainland JV balance sheet.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK investor must telegraph the capital contribution via SWIFT MT103 from an account holding the same legal entity name as the shareholder contract, using the SAFE investment purpose code tied to the registered capital and quoting the JV's SAFE capital-account registration reference. The wire must land in the JV's dedicated foreign-currency capital escrow account at a designated domestic tier-1 branch; upon receipt, the bank verifies the underlying investor's identity, source of funds, board resolution, and the SAFE filing record before accepting payment. For any subsequent conversion into RMB for operating expenses, the JV must submit a capital-account settlement payment instruction, real invoices or contracts, and a board-approved use case; a PRC-certified accountant must issue the capital verification report confirming the registered capital has been fully paid and filed with SAFE and the Administration for Market Regulation.Outbound trade disbursements should be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for fast settlements such as recurring interconnection fees, software licensing, maintenance contracts, and cross-border colocation partner revenue-sharing; commercial letters of credit are preferred for high-value imported server racks, cooling plants, UPS units, and battery systems requiring shipping-document protection; documentary collections are acceptable for medium-value equipment or vendor-backed transaction terms where the JV and supplier have a long trade relationship. Every outbound remittance must carry the underlying contract, invoice, tax clearance or tax payment evidence, and the approved SAFE current-account payment purpose code; if the beneficiary is the HK affiliate, an additional transfer-pricing file and VAT cross-border service treatment worksheet must be presented to the remitting bank to avoid rejection by post-payment authenticity audits.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every active Hong Kong-incorporated company, including the joint venture, must appoint an independent certified public accountant registered as a practising auditor and must have its annual financial statements audited under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). There is no de minimis turnover, balance-sheet or headcount threshold that exempts an ordinary active company from audit. A private company may be eligible to dispense with audit only if it qualifies as a small private company by satisfying, for the financial year, at least two of the following three tests on a company or consolidated group basis, as applicable: (i) total revenue not exceeding HKD 100,000,000; (ii) total assets not exceeding HKD 100,000,000; (iii) total employees not exceeding 100. The audit waiver requires a special members' resolution and is not available to banks, insurers, securities dealers, licensed money lenders, or other excluded companies; any member or the Registrar may require an audit. Dormant companies may also apply for a separate dormant-company exemption if they have triggered no accounting transactions during the relevant year.
