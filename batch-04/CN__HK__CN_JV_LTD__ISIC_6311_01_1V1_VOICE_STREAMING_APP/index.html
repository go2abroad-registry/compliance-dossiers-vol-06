Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: IRD eTAX accessible via www.ird.gov.hk and the GovHK portal

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong vehicle: (1) Incorporate a Hong Kong limited company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) with the Companies Registry, or if the entity is a non-Hong Kong company, register a place of business under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance. (2) Upon incorporation or registration, the Companies Registry transmits particulars to the IRD Business Registration Office. (3) The entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business, with local companies covered on incorporation. (4) The IRD issues a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for Hong Kong tax purposes. (5) For a non-Hong Kong company, the application to the IRD Business Registration Office must state the corporate name, registered office or principal place of business address in Hong Kong, date of commencement of business, nature of business, and particulars of responsible persons. (6) The BRN must be displayed at the place of business and quoted in all tax returns, employer returns and correspondence. No separate VAT registration exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT or GST.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. A Hong Kong limited company is a separate taxable entity; profits tax under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) section 14 is chargeable at the entity level. There is no tax-transparent pass-through. The source principle is territorial: only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are taxable, while offshore profits are outside the charge unless special statutory provisions apply.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no US-style check-the-box election, no elective entity classification for profits tax, and no separate hybrid mismatch election in the Inland Revenue Ordinance. Offshore treatment is asserted by factual presentation in the profits tax return, not by a formal election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/sales-and-use tax. Hong Kong does impose stamp duty and duties on dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), but those are not VAT, GST, or general sales taxes.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST registration threshold expressed in HKD. All persons carrying on business in Hong Kong must register under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement, regardless of turnover, and no separate indirect tax registration exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT or GST, so there is no statutory 0% export VAT rate, no export zero-rating claim, and no VAT refund mechanism for exported goods or services. For a voice-streaming and companion social application supplied to overseas users, the supply is not subject to any Hong Kong indirect tax. For profits tax, a claim that services are performed or profits sourced outside Hong Kong is a direct-tax offshore-source position, not a VAT export zero-rating.

  • For physical merchandise exports, the standard statutory declaration is the Import/Export Declaration made to the Census and Statistics Department under the Import and Export (Merchandise) Regulations (Cap. 60A), supported by a commercial invoice, packing list, and transport document such as a bill of lading or air waybill.

Taxpayers must preserve records and invoices under section 51C of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China applies managed-float exchange-rate policy, current-account convertibility, and capital-account registration. The JV's inward equity contribution must enter through a SAFE-designated foreign exchange capital account, and each drawdown is reconciled against the FDI registration certificate. Once converted to RMB, funds are freely deployable for operating expenses, but any re-conversion or outward remittance of capital requires a capital repatriation filing. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation and customs-lineage verification, but they may be retained in foreign exchange or settled to RMB at the bank's spot rate. Profit distribution to the Hong Kong shareholder is treated as a current-account dividend remittance and is legally unrestricted once the JV has completed its annual audit, settled corporate income tax, and submitted the required tax clearance and SAFE verification documents to the remitting bank. The Hong Kong SAR maintains full convertibility for HKD and offshore RMB (CNH), with no surrender obligation, no profit remittance approval, and no central bank exchange control, although anti-money-laundering screening applies. The principal latency and compliance cost arise in the PRC side from obtaining SAFE registration, collecting supporting trade documents, and matching electronic customs data with receipts and payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the PRC goods-trade side, export proceeds must be repatriated into the JV's foreign exchange current account within 90 calendar days of the export customs declaration date unless a deferred-collection period is registered with SAFE before the due date. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0%: proceeds may be retained in the original foreign currency or converted into RMB at a designated bank. Hong Kong imposes no surrender or conversion quota.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for the PRC JV, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for Hong Kong's offshore RMB and HKD clearing; Bank of China (Hong Kong) Limited acting as the offshore RMB clearing bank.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI viability is Low for the PRC JV and Medium only for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle. Under PBOC and National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) rules, no unlicensed EMI may open a domestic bank account or process capital-account transactions for a PRC-incorporated JV; the JV must use a SAFE-designated licensed bank for equity injection, export settlement, tax clearance, and dividend remittance. In Hong Kong, a licensed Money Service Operator or Stored Value Facility may handle incidental cross-border collections and disbursements, but it cannot serve as the sole clearing rail for equity capital, large trade settlements, or account reconciliation with the PRC bank, and the HKMA requires strong AML controls for digital payments flowing into the mainland JV. Airwallex, Currenxie and similar platforms are therefore supplemental at the offshore treasury level, not statutory substitutes for Tier-1 commercial banking.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Before remittance, the PRC JV must complete the SAFE FDI registration and open a foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank. The Hong Kong investor then sends a SWIFT MT103 as an equity contribution for the paid-in capital increase, with the FX purpose code being the SAFE-registered FDI capital injection paid-in capital code; the remittance message must name the JV and reference the FDI certificate. The receiving bank verifies the MT103 against the registration, books the funds to the capital account, and applies the bank's spot rate for conversion to RMB. A licensed Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital amount. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated company must prepare audited financial statements unless it qualifies as an audit-exempt small private company under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A small private company is one not within a group and satisfying at least two of the following three thresholds for the financial year: annual revenue not exceeding HK$20 million (about US$2.56 million); total assets not exceeding HK$10 million (about US$1.28 million); average number of employees not exceeding 50. A small company within a qualifying corporate group must satisfy the same criteria on consolidated group figures at governing level. Even when the size criteria are met, audit exemption requires member consent and is not available to banks, insurers, licensed corporations, or regulated financial intermediaries. For cross-border consolidation by the People's Republic of China joint-venture shareholders, an audit under Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS) is normally required even if the local statutory audit exemption applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by a Hong Kong resident company because Hong Kong imposes no dividend withholding tax.
