Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on goods exported from the PRC customs territory and on qualifying cross-border services supplied to an overseas customer where the service is wholly consumed outside China. For the video-matching social app, the taxpayer must evidence non-resident user status, offshore delivery and consumption of the digital service, settlement by overseas users in foreign currency, and repatriation of proceeds into China. Supplies to PRC mainland consumers remain taxable at 6% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) transmitted through the China International Trade Single Window for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual departure from the PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt voucher showing proceeds repatriation within the statutory period
  • Export contract, commercial invoice, digital service delivery logs, geolocation/IP evidence, and platform customer records for qualifying cross-border digital services

China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoicing with real-time fiscal clearance through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Taxpayers must integrate their billing systems with tax authority APIs, issue each invoice with a unique electronic invoice code and anti-counterfeiting digital stamp, transmit invoice data in near-real time, and reconcile issued invoices with declared VAT turnover. Hong Kong imposes no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance requirement.

Export zero-rating is examined by Golden Tax Phase IV data matching across customs declarations, VAT returns, export invoices, foreign exchange receipts, and platform transaction records. The tax authority may claw back zero-rated VAT amounts, deny refunds, impose late-payment interest, and apply statutory fines of 50% to 500% of the undercharged tax. Deliberate false export declarations or foreign exchange repatriation failures within the 180-day deadline invalidate zero-rating and may trigger criminal tax-fraud enforcement under PRC criminal law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration, RMB is convertible on current-account items when the underlying transaction is genuine and documented, but capital-account convertibility remains highly managed. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture may receive foreign currency from the Hong Kong shareholder as registered foreign direct investment under SAFE, yet the onward settlement of those proceeds into RMB is limited to the JV's lawful business purposes and requires a capital settlement account plus negative-list screening. Dividends and profits can be remitted offshore once the JV completes a statutory audit, files the withholding tax return, obtains tax clearance or a tax payment certificate from the in-charge tax authority, and submits the board resolution on profit distribution to its designated bank. Service-payment remittances for bandwidth, cloud, licensing, and marketing are current-account items and are generally processed against contract, invoice, and bank records of tax withholding; however, the bank must screen for round-tripping and transfer-pricing anomalies. The Hong Kong SAR has no exchange controls and its monetary base is fully backed by the US dollar under the linked exchange-rate system, so the HK node can move USD and HKD freely; the practical convertibility barrier exists entirely at the China border. The JV should therefore centralize treasury decisioning onshore but keep an HK concentration account for receivable collection from Apple IAP and Paymentwall.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE requires export and cross-border service receipts to be repatriated to an onshore account; advance payments must be brought onshore within 30 days of receipt, and credit-term proceeds must be repatriated by the contractual maturity date. If an export invoice remains unreceived 90 days beyond maturity, the bank must flag the overdue item in SAFE's monitoring system and may suspend automatic conversion. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: 100% of export foreign currency receipts may be retained in a foreign exchange export account or 100% converted to RMB at the JV's election; the only hard rule is that all proceeds must be remitted onshore unless SAFE has granted a specific offshore-receivables quota.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBoC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the authority for the Hong Kong SAR clearing node

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: not viable as a primary banking rail. Under the PRC Administrative Measures for the Payment Services of Non-Financial Institutions and PBOC real-name account rules, offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, or Paymentwall-class platforms are not licensed as deposit-taking commercial banks in China and cannot open a domestic RMB basic deposit account, hold SAFE-registered FDI proceeds, or issue an external payment verification certificate. Their ability to service the entity is limited to aggregating consumer card and app-store payments in foreign markets, such as Apple IAP and Paymentwall collection, before settlement to a licensed Tier-1 bank. If the JV books collections directly to an offshore EMI, SAFE will treat the receivable as unrepatriated export or service income; the local bank account may be frozen in cases of suspected back-to-back structure. Use EMIs only as a merchant acquiring and collection front-end and maintain clearing through Tier-1 commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound route from Hong Kong to the PRC JV must be executed as two legs: first from the HK treasury account through HSBC or Bank of China Hong Kong, and then onshore via SWIFT MT103 into the Foreign Currency Capital Account maintained at Bank of China Limited or another designated Tier-1 PRC bank. The remittance advice must carry the SAFE cross-border inbound purpose code for an FDI equity injection and reference the beneficiary's foreign exchange capital account registration. Upon receipt, the bank matches the funds against the MOFCOM foreign investment filing, joint venture contract, business license, and SAFE FDI registration QR code. Funds are booked as undisbursed capital and release into RMB only through the capital settlement account after a negative-list check on the JV's business activities. A PRC-certified CPA must then issue a capital verification report, and the local Administration for Market Regulation must be updated with the final paid-in capital position. Inbound proceeds may not be used for securities, entrusted loans, real estate, or restricted equity acquisitions without separate SAFE confirmation.Operational disbursements for cloud infrastructure, marketing, licensing, SDK fees, and localized services should be cleared with SWIFT MT103 from the onshore JV account, supported by signed contracts, invoices, tax withholding certificates, and current-account declarations. For physical equipment or media procurement, a commercial letter of credit with SWIFT MT700 or MT400 messaging, or documentary collections, provides title security and bank-level authentic evidence of trade. For recurring Apple IAP and Paymentwall merchant settlements, the JV should route collections through a licensed PRC bank cross-border e-commerce clearance node or a qualified domestic payment institution, ensuring each outbound debit to the HK concentration account maps to verified service invoices and tax records. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: As revised by the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 6) Ordinance 2018, a multinational enterprise group with annual consolidated group revenue of at least HKD 680 million is required to prepare a Master File; a Local File is required when the Hong Kong entity's related-party transactions exceed HKD 4 million for property transfers or HKD 4 million for financing transactions, or HKD 2 million for other transactions such as royalties and management fees in a year of assessment; CbC notification requirements apply with the same HKD 680 million group revenue threshold

Economic substance scope: The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance contains judicial and statutory economic substance doctrines: the source principle requires that profits are chargeable to profits tax only if they arise in or are derived from Hong Kong, which requires an assessment of where the operations that produce the relevant profits occur; the IRD applies the 'totality of facts' test and will ignore a purely formal corporate structure where the taxpayer has no real presence, staff, or decision-making capacity; the judicial anti-avoidance doctrines of sham, agency, and fiscal nullity may allow the IRD to disregard a back-to-back arrangement; and the statutory general anti-abuse rule (GAAR) under Section 61A permits the IRD to cancel tax benefits from transactions with the sole or dominant purpose of tax avoidance. While Hong Kong does not apply the six Economic Substance Regulations of the EU's Code of Conduct Group and is not on the EU non-cooperative list, the JV must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) including strategic decisions, risk management, product development, and contracting performed by adequately remunerated Hong Kong staff; a pure pass-through entity is also exposed to the revised treaty benefit rules, particularly the preferential DTA claims with mainland China, which require substantive business operations in Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Offshore claim fraud and wilful tax evasion through false source declarations

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112) Section 82: fraudulent or wilful tax evasion with intent to evade tax is punishable by a fine of HKD 50,000, treble the amount of tax undercharged, and three years' imprisonment. Section 80: incorrect returns without reasonable excuse attract a fine of HKD 10,000 and a further penalty of treble the tax undercharged. Section 82A empowers the IRD to impose a civil penalty up to 100% of the tax undercharged for failure to use due care; a higher penalty of 150% to 200% applies for intentional conduct.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the JV is a mere façade or sham; each director who signed, caused to be signed, or knowingly permitted the false return is personally liable as a principal offender for the criminal fine and imprisonment. Directors may be subjected to civil asset restitution proceedings by the IRD under the revenue debt recovery provisions, disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance, and a travel restriction / departure ban under Section 76 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance if they leave Hong Kong without paying assessed tax; personal liability extends to fraudulent trading under Section 275 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622), rendering directors personally liable for the company's debts with potential imprisonment of up to 15 years for fraudulent trading.
Transfer pricing manipulation and missing statutory documentation

Statutory sanction: Sections 50AAF-50AAJ of the Inland Revenue Ordinance empower the IRD to adjust the consideration for the controlled transaction to the arm's length amount and impose tax on the adjusted profits. Failure to prepare or retain transfer pricing documentation within the statutory timeline is an offence under Section 50AAJ(5) with a fine of HKD 50,000 plus a daily penalty of HKD 500 per day. Inaccurate documentation without reasonable cause or misstatements are subject to penalties under Section 80(2); intentional understatement may attract criminal prosecutions under Section 82 with treble tax penalties. Interest on underpaid tax is charged at the statutory rate; if the transaction is also physically structured through mainland China, the PRC tax authority may impose transfer pricing penalties of 25% plus interest on the underpayment.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign financial statements and tax returns that misrepresent the arm's length nature of related-party transactions are jointly and severally liable to criminal sanctions for false statements; the Companies Ordinance imposes civil liability for fraudulent trading, allowing liquidators and creditors to recover personal assets; the IRD may issue a notice of assessment to the director personally if the company is liquidated and the tax is unpaid, and apply for a restriction order preventing departure; all directors remain disqualified for up to 15 years from acting as directors where their conduct makes them unfit, and any external management fee paid offshore without withholding on the part of the mainland affiliate can trigger cross-border mutual collection assistance under the DTA Article 24
AML, sanctions and cross-border payment infrastructure abuse hidden through related-party settlement

Statutory sanction: Drug Trafficking and Organized Crime Ordinance (Cap 405) Section 25: dealing with property known or believed to represent proceeds of crime attracts a maximum fine of HKD 5,000,000 and 14 years' imprisonment. The Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO, Cap 615) impose custodial sentences of up to 7 years and fines of HKD 1,000,000 for failure to comply with CDD/record-keeping obligations where the company conducts a regulated financial business without a licence; the Companies Ordinance requires a significant controllers register and failure to maintain it is a fine of HKD 25,000 plus a further fine of HKD 700 per day; operating an unlicensed stored value facility or money service is an indictable offence under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, with a maximum fine of HKD 5,000,000 and 7 years' imprisonment; fraudulent concealment of assets in a winding-up is punishable under the Companies Ordinance with imprisonment of 2 to 5 years.

Director personal liability: Directors of a JV engaged in money laundering are treated as principals where they authorise or consent to the arrangement; each director is individually liable for fines of up to HKD 5,000,000 and 14 years' imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced under Sections 276-277 of the Companies Ordinance where the business is carried on with intent to defraud creditors or for a fraudulent purpose, imposing unlimited personal liability for all debts and criminal imprisonment. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority and the Police may freeze personal as well as corporate bank accounts; directors face restriction orders, travel bans, and asset seizure, and upon conviction the director's disqualification becomes automatic; extradition or cross-border mutual legal assistance to mainland China may expose the director to PRC criminal liability for the same conduct under PRC Criminal Law articles relating to illegal business operation and money laundering.
