Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China, operating through the eTAX (Electronic Tax System) portal and the GovHK digital services platform at www.ird.gov.hk.

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture Limited company, the entity is first incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) at the Companies Registry, obtaining a Certificate of Incorporation. Within one month of incorporation or commencement of business, the company must register with the IRD under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) by filing the statutory Application for Business Registration (IRBR1) with the Business Registration Office of the IRD and paying the prescribed registration fee and levy. The IRD then issues the Business Registration Certificate bearing the Business Registration Number, which functions as the taxpayer identification number for Hong Kong profits tax, property tax, salaries tax and employer obligations. For a non-resident foreign company carrying on business in Hong Kong through a branch without local incorporation, the branch must file the corresponding Business Registration application with the IRD within one month of commencing business, appoint a Hong Kong authorized representative or agent, and obtain a separate branch Business Registration Certificate. Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT identification number because no VAT/GST/sales tax is imposed.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (with Hong Kong territorial source principle)

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box election, pass-through election, or comparable entity classification election. A limited company is mandatorily taxed as a separate entity-level taxpayer under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax; Hong Kong operates no broad-based consumption tax. The only indirect excise regime is the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) covering alcohol, tobacco, hydrocarbon oil and methyl alcohol.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no turnover-based VAT/GST/sales-tax registration threshold in Hong Kong. Business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance is triggered by carrying on business, not by reaching any economic turnover threshold; no separate indirect-tax registration exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable under indirect tax law because Hong Kong does not impose VAT on exports or on cross-border digital supplies. For Hong Kong tax purposes, there is no VAT clawback because no VAT zero-rating or refund regime exists. However, the IRD may scrutinize offshore profits claims under Part XIII of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including the anti-avoidance provisions in sections 61 and 61A, and may disregard artificial transactions or re-characterize the arrangement. Penalties for negligent or fraudulent returns include a fine of up to HK$50,000 and up to three times the amount of tax undercharged, with the assessment period extended to unlimited years where fraud or wilful evasion is involved. The entity should retain the full export documentation bundle for at least seven years, with a 180-day internal deadline for collecting foreign exchange evidence to support the offshore-sourced income position.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland RMB is convertible on current-account items for genuine trade and service transactions, but capital-account items remain subject to verification-before-conversion controls. For this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the Hong Kong shareholder's capital contribution must enter through a SAFE-registered FDI foreign-currency capital account, and every subsequent use of the converted RMB is limited to the registered business scope. Dividend/profit remittance to the HK shareholder is lawful only after annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax filing and clearance, and the bank's verification of the distributable profit, underlying surplus reserve and applicable withholding tax. Export and service-currency receipts are subject to inward repatriation monitoring, typically within 180 days from the invoice or customs declaration date, but there is no mandatory surrender ratio today; an entity may retain foreign currency in a current account or settle into RMB at market rates. However, outbound payments for syndicated newsfeed licensing, API hosting and shareholder loans require banks to pre-match contracts, invoices and the valid SAFE purpose code, creating real administrative friction. The Hong Kong SAR itself imposes no FX controls, but the mainland JV cannot benefit from that absence without risking informal or circular remittance exposure. Overall, this is a partially controlled regime: routine current-account working capital is convertible, while capital-account freedom is not granted and can only be accessed through SAFE-approved structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC SAFE practice, exporters and service providers must repatriate foreign-currency proceeds into their designated onshore bank account within 180 days of the underlying invoice, customs declaration or service completion date. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent: all export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency current account or sold into RMB at the corporate's discretion, provided the bank's electronic SAFE monitoring and trade authenticity filings are completed. For the JV's financial newswire API subscriptions and syndication fees, such service exports are treated as current-account receipts subject to the same 180-day inward monitoring and an exact purpose-code match.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the central foreign-exchange regulatory authority for mainland China; for the Hong Kong SAR conduit, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central bank and sole regulator of the linked HKD/USD system.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMI viability is Low for the onshore Chinese legal entity. Offshore EMI platforms including Stripe Treasury, Currenxie, Airwallex and trusted digital banking rails are not licensed by PBOC or the former CBIRC to hold RMB current accounts, receive FDI capital, or perform SAFE capital-account filings on behalf of a mainland JV. They are legally barred from acting as the primary bank for regulated cross-border RMB flows and cannot satisfy the Foreign Exchange Administration registration mandate. Their practical use is limited to auxiliary collections of micro newswire subscription fees from overseas customers outside the SAFE banking system, routed to the Hong Kong treasury vehicle rather than the mainland JV. For the mainland entity, the core treasury plant must sit with a Tier-1 domestic bank as SAFE reporting and clearing agent; a licensed HK bank or Money Service Operator can support the HK conduit but cannot substitute for onshore compliance.
