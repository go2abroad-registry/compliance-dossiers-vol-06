None. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) does not prescribe any minimum paid-up or authorised share capital, and Hong Kong does not maintain any blocked escrow account requirement for incorporation. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; electronic administration via the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online platform accessed through the provincial, municipal or district-level competent tax authority with jurisdiction over the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture's registered address.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Obtain the enterprise business licence issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation. Step 2: Complete automatic tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance through the STA multi-document integration procedure using the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which functions as the taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number. Step 3: File initial tax registration information with the competent tax authority, elect small-scale taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000, or apply for general VAT taxpayer status where turnover exceeds that threshold. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident corporate investor, the non-resident company must register for a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identification number through its Chinese withholding agent or directly via e-Tax by filing proof of incorporation, tax residence certificate (for Hong Kong SAR companies a Hong Kong tax residency certificate issued by the Inland Revenue Department), and executed contractual documents. Step 5: Maintain the tax registration through monthly, quarterly and annual filings even where the SPV has no taxable revenue.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification; the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is mandatorily treated as a Chinese resident enterprise and there is no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch reclassification election under PRC enterprise income tax. The only election available is indirect tax status: a taxpayer may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment while annual taxable sales remain at or below RMB 5,000,000, and once that threshold is exceeded registration as a PRC general VAT taxpayer becomes mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover in any consecutive 12-month period is the mandatory threshold for registering as a General VAT Taxpayer; all newly established enterprises, including passive holding SPVs, must still complete basic tax registration with the STA and obtain the Unified Social Credit Code-based taxpayer identity immediately upon establishment even if turnover is zero.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from mainland China qualify for a 0% export VAT rate only when the seller is a registered General VAT Taxpayer, the goods are physically declared to and leave China through Customs in the name of the exporting entity, the export sale is supported by genuine commercial documentation, the export declaration is completed, and the purchase price from the overseas buyer is repatriated to China in foreign exchange within the statutory period. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas parties and consumed entirely outside China may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt under the STA cross-border taxable services categories, but passive dividends and equity holding activities do not constitute taxable VAT supplies. As this entity is a pure equity holding SPV, it will not ordinarily engage in export zero-rated supplies.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration form filed with and accepted by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China)
  • Bill of Lading or air waybill or international courier receipt proving physical export from China
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract
  • Special VAT invoice (official e-Fapiao) issued for the export sale
  • Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation / Cross-Border Receipts Declaration filed through a Chinese settlement bank under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange Goods Trade Monitoring System

China operates mandatory digital electronic invoicing through the STA e-invoicing platform, requiring real-time reporting of invoice data to the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT invoices, including the fully digital electronic invoice (all-electronic e-Fapiao), carry unique encrypted invoice codes and are subject to anti-fraud digital stamping and verification in the STA invoice checking platform. Input VAT credits are allowed only after the invoice is certified and matched in the VAT invoice verification system. Taxpayers must issue invoices through STA-approved electronic invoicing software, tax-control U-Key devices, or the e-Tax platform; no traditional physical stamping is required for valid electronic invoices.

Fraudulent or unsupported export zero-rating and export tax rebate claims are subject to mandatory recovery of the refunded VAT, daily late-payment surcharges, and an administrative fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the amount wrongfully refunded under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where the conduct constitutes export tax refund fraud, the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, Article 204, imposes criminal liability including imprisonment and confiscation. Tax and customs authorities may retrospectively claw back rebates for false declarations, and for fraudulent conduct there is no statutory limitation period for collection.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR itself maintains full current-account and capital-account convertibility under the linked exchange rate system, with no surrender requirements, no prior SAFE approval for foreign exchange transactions within Hong Kong, and free conversion between HKD, USD, CNY (CNH offshore) and other major currencies. However, whenever capital flows cross the border between the PRC mainland and the Hong Kong SPV, the strict PRC foreign exchange control regime applies through PBOC and SAFE. Mainland Chinese current-account items, such as dividends and trade receivables, are generally convertible subject to bank verification and supporting documentation. Capital-account items, including outbound direct investment, inbound equity injections from PRC residents into the HK entity, and shareholder loans, remain subject to administrative approvals, filings, quotas, or SAFE registration. Profit remittance from any PRC operating subsidiary is permissible but requires independent audited financial statements, a competent tax clearance or withholding tax settlement certificate, proof that the underlying investment was duly approved and registered with SAFE, and bank-level verification that the funds represent genuine post-tax profits. For the HK-incorporated SPV, there are no exchange-control obstacles to receiving equity or distributing dividends, but the bank will expect full substance documentation and will apply enhanced due diligence whenever shareholder funds originate from mainland jurisdictions or pass through SAFE-regulated channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not directly applicable to the passive Hong Kong equity holding SPV because it does not generate export receipts. For any underlying PRC trading subsidiaries, the current Chinese regime does not impose a mandatory conversion ratio on export proceeds; exporters may retain their foreign exchange receipts in designated current-account foreign exchange settlement accounts or sell them to banks at market-determined rates, provided the proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory 180-day period from the date of Customs export declaration, and the receipt is verified through the SAFE digital monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) are the competent authorities for all foreign exchange controls and cross-border capital flows involving the People's Republic of China; for the Hong Kong SAR treasury operations, the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the relevant de facto central bank and maintains the liquidity and clearing infrastructure for HKD and multi-currency banking.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Regulated fintech EMI service providers are legally permitted to operate in Hong Kong if licensed or registered under the HKMA's stored value facility regime or money service operator regime, and they can service corporate clients. However, for a pure equity holding SPV classified as Medium Risk Enhanced, most digital EMIs have a low risk appetite for entities without operational substance, physical office presence, or active trade flows. Their product suites generally lack trade finance instruments such as documentary letters of credit, escrow account services, cargo documents verification, and local cheque clearing. Therefore, an offshore digital EMI may be used only as a complementary payment rail for narrowly defined working-capital payments; it is not a viable substitute for a Tier-1 commercial bank in this treasury structure and may be rejected at onboarding where the entity's UBO structure is controlled from the PRC mainland.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For any equity injection into the Hong Kong SPV from a mainland Chinese shareholder, the remittance must first be approved under the PRC outbound direct investment regime, requiring a project code from NDRC, MOFCOM approval, and SAFE registration; the Chinese commercial bank will then route the foreign-currency wire via SWIFT MT103 using the applicable foreign exchange purpose code for overseas direct investment, followed by a mainland-side bank instruction and an FX settlement or purchase record. In Hong Kong, the receiving bank will credit the SPV's multi-currency account and will require the scanned documentation pack to satisfy its own authorisation, including the board resolution of the HK entity, the subscription agreement, the SAFE registration certificate, and a CPA-issued capital verification report confirming that subscribed capital has been paid. Where the injection is in RMB, it should be cleared through the offshore CNH market or via the RMB clearing bank in Hong Kong, with the payment message carrying the specific business code for cross-border RMB direct investment.Although the SPV is a pure holding company, any onward funding to operating subsidiaries, dividends to shareholders, or settlement of intercompany obligations will be cleared through standard international banking rails. Ordinary wire transfers should be sent as SWIFT MT103 using the correct reason for payment and the appropriate bank's clearing agent; for commercial trade transactions where the SPV is acting as a buying or selling intermediary, commercial letters of credit should be issued as SWIFT MT700 and settled against compliant documents at the negotiating bank, while documentary collections should be handled under the URC 522 as D/P or D/A with the presenting bank collecting trade documents against payment or acceptance. HKD transactions clear through the HKMA's CHATS real-time gross settlement system, USD and EUR through commercial bank nostro accounts with Clearstream or Fedwire, and RMB cross-border through the bank's participation in the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) or the designated RMB clearing bank under the Hong Kong Monetary Authority framework.Deliverable offshore CNY forward contracts (USD/CNH) for hedges on expected RMB cash flows from mainland subsidiaries, provided the maturity matches the contractual dividend or trade payment and the underlying exposure is demonstrable.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on onshore CNY (USD/CNY) limited to the non-convertible onshore renminbi exposure, with cash-settled differential payments and maturities confined to a maximum of one year unless exceptional SAFE approval applies.Cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) between HKD and USD or CNY for long-term intercompany shareholder loans, provided the notional amount does not exceed the outstanding principal and the swap is fully documented with an ISDA or HKMA-recognised master agreement.

Turnover: No quantitative turnover, balance-sheet, or headcount threshold exempts a Hong Kong incorporated private company from audit. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$4 million for Local File preparation, and HK$40 million for Master File preparation, measured by the aggregate value of controlled transactions in a basis period as set out in IRD DIPN 58. A pure equity holding SPV with an intercompany shareholder loan or royalty arrangement in excess of these thresholds must prepare contemporaneous documentation even if no Hong Kong-source profit is assessable.

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) do not apply to Hong Kong, but the territorial source principle and anti-abuse provisions under Part 9A IRO impose de facto substance expectations. The SPV must show that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) - the holding of subsidiary shares, collection of dividends and onward financing - are managed from outside Hong Kong. Board minutes outside Hong Kong, custody of share certificates in a foreign depository, foreign bank account authorisation and local corporate compliance performed by a Hong Kong agent for administrative convenience will support the offshore claim; a company with no office, no records and no management discretion is vulnerable to a deemed onshore reassessment at 16.5% and, if Part 9A applies, an additional 15% diverted profits tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - False Offshore Profit Claim

Statutory sanction: Sections 82(1) and 80(2) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap.112): fine of HK$10,000 plus three times the tax undercharged and imprisonment for three years. The Commissioner may also apply s.82A for careless or unreasonable conduct, adding up to three times the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: The director who signs the false return is personally liable as a directing mind for civil restitution of the tax and penalties; the IRD may obtain a court order for asset recovery and issue a departure prohibition to prevent travel abroad until the liability is discharged. Criminal enforcement allows imprisonment up to three years, and the director may be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Companies Registry as a disqualifying event.
Shell Company - Abusive Holding SPV Without Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under s.51C IRO, failure to maintain sufficient records is punishable by a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for six months. In serious cases, the IRD will recharacterise the SPV as a Hong-Kong-sourced trading company and assess profits at 16.5%; if the arrangement is designed to retain tax advantage, Part 9A IRO imposes a 15% diverted profits tax.

Director personal liability: The directors are jointly and severally liable for the restored tax and penalties because the corporate veil is pierced due to the sham nature of the entity. Civil asset restitution of all diverted dividends plus statutory interest may be ordered. The directors will be placed on an IRD/watch-list travel ban and can be prohibited from leaving Hong Kong while the matter is under investigation; upon conviction they face imprisonment for up to three years for fraudulent revenue evasion.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Intercompany Financing

Statutory sanction: Under s.50A(3) IRO, the IRD adjusts the intercompany loan and royalty to arm's length amounts, resulting in additional assessable profits at 16.5%. Because the arrangement lacks substance and artificially shifts profit, s.21(2) read with Part 9A IRO permits a 15% diverted profits tax on the excessive profit. The record keeping default attracts a fine up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for six months under s.51C IRO.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the non-arm's-length terms are personally liable for the unpaid tax differences and any penal additions. The IRD may impose a departure restriction (blacklisting) to secure collectability and seek a court order for restitution of diverted funds. Since the returns omit material related-party transactions, a wilful omission constitutes criminal tax evasion, subject to imprisonment for up to three years. If the SPV becomes insolvent, the directors face common law fraudulent trading claims for civil compensation.
