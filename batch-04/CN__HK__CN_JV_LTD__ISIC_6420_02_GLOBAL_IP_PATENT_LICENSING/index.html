A virtual office or commercial mailbox is categorically rejected as the sole registered office in Hong Kong for an intellectual-property licensing holding company. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For the Hong Kong SAR tax jurisdiction (jurisdiction_id: HK) within the People's Republic of China: Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portal: eTAX at https://www.etax.gov.hk. Companion registration authority: Companies Registry (Hong Kong) at https://www.cr.gov.hk. E-government filing portal

The statutory tax identification number in Hong Kong is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong presence: (1) determine whether the entity is 'carrying on business' in Hong Kong; if yes, within one month after commencing business, register the foreign legal person with the Companies Registry as a non-Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and obtain a Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company; (2) apply to the IRD Business Registration Office using the prescribed business registration application form, furnishing the foreign company's charter documents, registered address, Hong Kong principal place of business, and particulars of local representatives; (3) receive the Business Registration Certificate and, where the operation is carried on at more than one place, a Branch Registration Certificate; the BRN serves as the TIN for profits tax, employer tax returns, and stamp duty purposes; (4) for a locally incorporated JV limited company, use the one-stop Companies Registry incorporation and business registration service so that the Certificate of Incorporation and the Business Registration Certificate are issued together; (5) if a non-resident entity is chargeable to Hong Kong profits tax but has no fixed place of business, it must nonetheless obtain a tax reference number from the IRD by filing the appropriate taxpayer registration and attending to any assessment notices; all documents may be submitted in English or Chinese, and eTAX is the electronic filing channel for tax returns and specified communications.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No General VAT/GST/Sales Tax in Hong Kong SAR

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Business registration is mandatory from the first day of carrying on business in Hong Kong regardless of turnover. Importers and exporters of controlled or dutiable commodities may separately require licences under the Import and Export Ordinance and the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable in the VAT sense because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, and therefore no 0% export VAT rate or VAT refund mechanism exists. Goods exported from Hong Kong are outside the charge to any Hong Kong VAT/GST; customs export declarations are required for trade statistics and statutory import/export control. For cross-border intellectual property licensing, the controlling substantive relief is the territorial-source profits tax rule: royalty income derived from patents and trademarks exploited and used solely outside Hong Kong by non-resident licensees, with no Hong Kong negotiation, execution, performance, or enforcement activity, is treated as offshore-source income and is outside the charge to Hong Kong profits tax. To substantiate the offshore claim, the taxpayer must maintain the complete licence contract chain, evidence that the licensed IP is used only outside Hong Kong, absence of Hong Kong staff operational involvement, and payment from non-Hong Kong bank accounts. For the purposes of this global licensing dossier, foreign-exchange proceeds should be repatriated and documented within 180 days of invoicing; this 180-day benchmark is the stated compliance standard even though Hong Kong does not impose a general FX repatriation deadline.

  • Hong Kong Export Declaration submitted through the Government Electronic Trading Services (GETS) or an approved electronic service provider
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Shipping order or pre-shipment booking confirmation for physical exports
  • SWIFT MT103 bank payment instruction or bank inward remittance credit advice evidencing receipt of export or royalty consideration
  • For pure IP licensing: executed patent and trademark licence agreements, sub-licence chain agreements, registered IP title certificates, evidence of place of use outside Hong Kong, annual royalty utilisation statements, and board minutes evidencing offshore negotiation and decision-making

No statutory real-time electronic invoicing clearance mandate exists in Hong Kong. There is no fiscal API requirement, no mandatory anti-fraud digital stamping, no Peppol-style B2B/B2G e-invoicing obligation, and no pre-clearance of invoices by the Inland Revenue Department. Ordinary commercial invoices are accepted; taxpayers must retain sufficient business records in English or Chinese to enable profits to be readily ascertained.

For failure to substantiate offshore-source treatment of IP royalty income, the Inland Revenue Department may assess Hong Kong profits tax at the applicable corporate rate and may reopen completed assessments within six years, extended to ten years in cases of fraud or wilful evasion. Penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance may be up to 100% of the tax undercharged where erroneous returns are filed negligently without fraudulent intent, and up to 300% of the tax undercharged plus criminal sanctions where fraud or intentional evasion is established. For goods exports, false or inaccurate export declarations attract penalties under the Import and Export Ordinance and the Customs and Excise Ordinance. Because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, there is no VAT zero-rating scheme, no VAT clawback mechanism, and no VAT refund denial regime.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a free-market currency regime under the Linked Exchange Rate System, with the HKD fully convertible and no foreign exchange controls on current or capital transactions. The HKMA ensures monetary stability; cross-border movement of capital, royalties, dividends and loan repayments are unrestricted. The RMB is convertible in Hong Kong's offshore CNH market, allowing the JV to transact in CNY without onshore SAFE approval, provided the counterparty utilises CNH outside the mainland. However, any capital injection from a mainland shareholder remains onshore CNY and must first be converted and remitted under SAFE-approved ODI protocols; SAFE rules also require repatriation and reconciliation of direct investment returns. Profit remittance from the HK entity to non-HK shareholders is entirely free. For an IP HoldCo, the only material friction arises when funds enter or leave the mainland, where a dual-track CNY/CNH regime creates price differentials and documentary obligations under the FDI and ODI foreign exchange registration regime. No surrender quotas apply to HK resident companies.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios and no repatriation timelines. All export and service proceeds may be freely retained in any currency. For entities with mainland intercompany flows, SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Rules require export proceeds to be repatriated into China within 90 days from the date of export declaration; once repatriated, the enterprise may hold such foreign currency in its settlement account or convert to CNY freely—there is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) and, for mainland China-nexus flows, the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: LOW-MEDIUM as primary bank; HIGH as supplementary collection rail. Legal constraints: Licensed Money Service Operators (MSO) or Stored Value Facility (SVF) licensees in Hong Kong are neither banks nor deposit-taking institutions; they cannot issue capital escrow facilities, provide clearing settlement finality, or offer overdrafts. For an ISIC 6420 IP HoldCo carrying cross-border royalty flows, many EMIs decline onboarding due to intangible asset complexity and perceived ML risk. If used, an EMI must be licensed under the Money Service Operators Ordinance (Cap 615) and the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance (Cap 584); funds should be for disbursement only and swept daily to a Tier-1 bank. For ultimate AML defence, the board of directors must approve the EMI's CDD framework and auditable trail.
