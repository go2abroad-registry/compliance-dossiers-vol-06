VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In mainland China, VAT registration is mandatory from the first taxable supply; there is no general VAT exemption threshold. The separate compulsory threshold for classification as a general VAT taxpayer is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT/GST registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, a 0% VAT rate with refund of input VAT applies when the JV is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory to an overseas destination (including Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory), the export is evidenced by a valid customs export declaration, the transaction is not a prohibited export, and the export proceeds are received in foreign currency. For cross-border fiduciary services supplied to the offshore Hong Kong SPV/trust, zero-rating is available only if the service falls within the STA zero-rate catalogue for cross-border services and is completely consumed outside mainland China; all other cross-border services are VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated. The exporter must retain electronic customs data, logistics documentation and foreign exchange collection records to substantiate the zero-rated nature of the supply.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (export goods customs clearance declaration, including the export tax rebate electronic record/declaration copy)
  • Original Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from PRC customs territory to the overseas/Hong Kong destination
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list issued by the exporting JV
  • VAT special invoice (deduction copy) or, where applicable, the general VAT invoice supporting the export transaction
  • Export sales contract or purchase order establishing the overseas counterparty and payment terms
  • Bank foreign exchange collection receipt/settlement certificate or the electronic Export Proceeds Collection record transmitted by the bank to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and STA; a separate FIRC is not a Chinese statutory document
  • For zero-rated cross-border fiduciary services: executed service agreement, records showing performance and offshore consumption, fee invoice, and bank SWIFT wire receipt from the trust account

Mandatory use of the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System Phase IV and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) reform. VAT invoices are issued through the STA electronic invoice platform or the taxpayer's ERP via an approved API, and invoice data is transmitted in near real time to the tax authority. Each FDEI bears a unique national invoice number and a digital anti-counterfeit stamp/signature. Legacy block-style VAT special invoices must be verified online before input VAT deduction, and the deduction copy must be retained. Invoices not cleared through the tax control system are non-deductible. Hong Kong SAR has no equivalent fiscal clearance or anti-fraud stamping mandate.

The STA and Customs administration jointly audit export VAT refund applications by cross-checking the customs declaration, logistics data, invoice flow and foreign exchange collection records. Failure to substantiate zero-rating with the required export documentation, or failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days of export unless an approved extension is granted, will cause the refund to be clawed back and the corresponding output VAT imposed, with late payment surcharge accruing at 0.05% per day. Deliberately false export refund declarations trigger administrative penalties of 50% up to five times the defrauded tax, removal from low-risk export tax refund facilitation arrangements, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a full currency convertibility regime rather than a controlled foreign exchange environment. The HKMA administers the Linked Exchange Rate System through its Currency Board Account, maintaining the HKD-USD exchange rate within the 7.75-7.85 convertibility zone with practically no frictions for current account or capital account transactions. The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible into US dollars, RMB (offshore CNH), euros, sterling, Japanese yen and any recognised currency cleared through international payment systems. Current account payments, including fees, dividends, trust distributions, and fiduciary disbursements, can be made without authorisation, registration, or surrender obligations. For the mainland Chinese shareholders or settlors involved in this China-HK joint venture, capital account movements remain subject to China's cross-border regulatory framework. Inbound investment from Hong Kong to mainland China, and outbound investment from mainland China into Hong Kong, require SAFE registration, ODI or FDI filings, and the use of a designated Chinese commercial bank as the remittance gateway. Profit remittance from the Hong Kong trust vehicle to a mainland corporate beneficiary does not require Hong Kong exchange control approval, but the mainland recipient bank will request audited accounts, tax payment proofs, and underlying contract documentation to satisfy its own foreign exchange verification. No compulsory surrender of export proceeds applies to a Hong Kong entity; however, if export receipts are collected by the mainland operating entity, the mainland five-working-day repatriation rule and bank-based verification under SAFE apply.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to the Hong Kong entity; export proceeds can be retained and disbursed freely. For mainland-linked export flows, SAFE and PBOC require that export foreign-currency proceeds be repatriated into China within 5 working days from the date of collection, and the responsible commercial bank must verify the underlying trade documents before crediting the enterprise's foreign-exchange settlement account. The 2012 foreign exchange reform introduced full voluntary settlement, so the conversion ratio is zero to 100 percent at the enterprise's discretion, provided that the receipt is traceable to legitimate goods-trade background and the bank submits the transaction to the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the banking and foreign exchange authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China retaining jurisdiction over cross-border capital flows between mainland China and Hong Kong.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as the primary treasury rail. The HKMA has not authorised typical offshore fintech challengers such as Revolut, Wise, or Arival as full licensed banks in Hong Kong; they operate either as authorised money service operators or under a stored value facility licence, neither of which permits safekeeping of third-party trust funds or provision of a corporate deposit account with the same creditor priority as a licensed bank. The Hong Kong Companies Ordinance and Banking Ordinance require entities offering deposit-taking services to hold a banking licence, restricted banking licence, or deposit-taking company licence under the HKMA. Moreover, an offshore digital EMI that is not licensed in Hong Kong cannot be used for capital escrow or discretionary trust distributions because the AMLO and local customer due diligence rules require the account-holding institution to have a physical compliance presence and full AML examination files. Such EMIs may be viable only as a sub-limit operating utility for expense payments, with a maximum balance below HKD 50,000 and no trust asset custody.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound funds from the mainland Chinese shareholder into the Hong Kong trust entity must be routed as a trust capital injection, not as a loan, using SWIFT MT103. The Chinese remitting bank will require the outward remittance application and will tag the transfer with the SAFE cross-border transaction code corresponding to overseas direct investment capital, subject to the prevailing SAFE manual and the designated Chinese bank confirmation. The Hong Kong bank will credit the designated capital escrow account after verifying the SAFE registration certificate, NDRC and MOFCOM filing, board resolutions, and the capital verification report. If the injection is from the Hong Kong holding company to the Chinese operating company, the inward transfer must be declared under the inbound direct investment transaction code and the mainland bank must obtain a capital account confirmation from the local SAFE branch. The Hong Kong auditor or licensed CPA must then issue a capital verification certificate to close the loop.The Hong Kong trust entity will maintain a multi-currency settlement account with HSBC or Bank of China (Hong Kong) and use the following clearing rails: HKD, USD, RMB and CNH payments via SWIFT MT103 through CHATS, Fedwire and CIPS; irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit issued through SWIFT MT700 for goods importation where a mainland supplier requires bank confirmation; Documentary Collections presented through SWIFT MT400 for invoice-based trade; and internal swept balances for same-day local clearing. The Hong Kong Companies Ordinance and Banking Ordinance require entities offering deposit-taking services to hold a banking licence, restricted banking licence, or deposit-taking company licence under the HKMA. Moreover, an offshore digital EMI that is not licensed in Hong Kong cannot be used for capital escrow or discretionary trust distributions because the AMLO and local customer due diligence rules require the account-holding institution to have a physical compliance presence and full AML examination files. Such EMIs may be viable only as a sub-limit operating utility for expense payments, with a maximum balance below HKD 50,000 and no trust asset custody.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong does not impose a statutory threshold for preparing Local File or Master File; however, the IRD's Departmental Interpretation and Practice Notes (DIPN 58 & 59) require contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for intercompany transactions exceeding HKD 15 million (gross related-party transactions) or HKD 3 million (intercompany financing transactions) unless exemptions apply. The Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2018 codified the arm's length principle under Section 50AAH (TP adjustments) and requires documentation to be submitted within 21 days upon IRD request.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) comparable to the UAE, but the IRD applies substance requirements under judicial anti-avoidance doctrines (Ramsay principle as adapted), Section 61 (artificial or fictitious transactions), Section 61A (tax avoidance transactions), and Section 61B (arrangements with dominant purpose of obtaining tax benefits). The discretionary asset protection trust must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: locally based trustees, full legal ownership, investment oversight, risk management, compliance functions, and record-keeping. A mere mailbox or nominee arrangement with decision-making in China or another jurisdiction will be disregarded. IRD can pierce the trust or JV veil if the trust is a sham or alter ego of the settlor, invoking reassessments, penalties, and criminal liability.

Statutory sanction: Sections 80(1), 80(2), 82, and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) Cap. 112: fine of up to HK$10,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged for incorrect returns; daily penalty of HK$100 per day for non-compliance; wilful evasion under Section 82: fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years; under Criminal Fraud Ordinance (Cap. 222) fraudulent misrepresentation may incur imprisonment for up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Under Section 26 of the Companies (Winding Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance (Cap. 32) and common law, a director who signs a false tax return without reasonable excuse is personally liable for civil restitution of the underpaid tax plus penalties and surcharges. Travel restrictions (Mirs) and asset freezes can be imposed by the IRD under Section 76 (Business Preservation) or by court order.
