Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTAX (IRD's Electronic Services Portal). For People's Republic of China cross-border tax compliance: State Taxation Administration (STA) and its official Electronic Tax Bureau portal.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity must obtain its Hong Kong Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) through Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) of Hong Kong. Step 1: Appoint a Hong Kong resident authorized representative and establish a registered place of business in Hong Kong; if the entity is a foreign company, register with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Step 2: Within one month of commencing business, submit the prescribed application for business registration to the Business Registration Office of the IRD. Step 3: Provide a certified copy of the certificate of incorporation or equivalent constitutional document, the identity document of the authorized representative, and proof of the Hong Kong business address. Step 4: Upon approval, the IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate containing the Business Registration Number, which constitutes the entity's TIN for all Hong Kong tax filings; this certificate must be renewed annually. Step 5: For a private charitable foundation, simultaneously apply to the IRD for recognition as a charitable institution or trust of a public character under section 88 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), which is the statutory mechanism for exempting philanthropic endowment income and grants from Profits Tax. Filing may be done through the eTAX portal or by submitting paper applications to the Business Registration Office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong; indirect taxation is confined to the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) on selected dutiable goods and the Motor Vehicles (First Registration Tax) Ordinance. For mainland China cross-border supplies, China's Value-Added Tax (VAT) system and electronic invoice regime apply.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable for Hong Kong because there is no VAT/GST registration threshold. For mainland China VAT registration, mandatory general taxpayer registration is triggered when cumulative annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (approximately HKD 5,800,000); below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong has no VAT/GST regime and therefore no export zero-rating. For exports of goods from mainland China by the China-affiliated enterprise or the joint venture, a 0% VAT export rate is available if the exporter is registered as a VAT general taxpayer, the goods have physically exited China through a customs-controlled port, the China Customs export declaration has been accepted and released, the corresponding foreign exchange has been repatriated into China, and the export VAT refund claim is filed with the State Taxation Administration. The zero-rating may also extend to qualifying cross-border services supplied to non-resident clients, provided the service is performed outside mainland China or is specifically designated as an offshore service under STA notices.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry), including the export goods declaration form accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or Land Waybill evidencing physical exportation from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the equivalent SAFE/bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher confirming the repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export VAT refund application schedule as prescribed by the State Taxation Administration, together with the legally issued VAT special invoice or export invoice
  • Certificate of Origin or export license, where applicable, and any freight/insurance documentation supporting shipment terms

Hong Kong has no statutory real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate; invoices are governed by the record-keeping requirements of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For mainland China cross-border trade, the State Taxation Administration requires digital VAT invoices to be issued through the official e-invoicing platform, subject to API-based clearance, digital stamping, and real-time transmission to tax authority systems under the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice scheme.

China Customs and the State Taxation Administration may conduct retrospective audits of export VAT refund claims. If the export declaration, transport documentation, foreign-exchange receipt, or invoice is found to be fraudulent or insufficiently substantiated, the zero-rated refund is clawed back and VAT is assessed at the standard rate, plus a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day accruing until payment. Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, fraudulent export refunds are penalized at 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, with criminal liability for serious cases. In Hong Kong, since no VAT refund exists, false records or declarations made to the IRD may attract additional tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, together with fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong exercises full and free capital account convertibility under the Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS) managed by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA). The HKD is freely convertible within the guaranteed band of HKD 7.75-7.85 per USD, and both current and capital account transactions are unrestricted for a Hong Kong-domiciled entity. There are no surrender requirements, no prior FX approval, no central bank allocation quotas, and no mandatory repatriation timelines for export proceeds or endowment capital. Profit remittances, dividend distributions, and grant disbursements can be settled in any currency via the Hong Kong interbank clearing system or SWIFT. However, where the Sino-Foreign JV is ultimately controlled from Mainland China, cross-border RMB movements between the SAR and Mainland China remain governed by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the People's Bank of China (PBOC), and China's balance-of-payments framework. Capital injections from Mainland China to Hong Kong constitute offshore direct investment (ODI) requiring SAFE registration, while inbound funds from Hong Kong into Mainland China are subject to foreign direct investment (FDI) controls. The convertibility of CNH (offshore RMB) is market-determined, but onshore CNY remains regulated. For a private charitable endowment, this bifurcation means that ordinary offshore treasury operations are fully liberalized while any Mainland-source capital contribution must be channelled through an authorized bank with valid ODI documents and proper purpose codes. No fiscal split between current and capital accounts exists in Hong Kong; the only practical friction is the China nexus.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Not applicable to Hong Kong SAR. The statutory regime imposes no surrender ratio, no timeline for converting export foreign currency into HKD, and no mandatory repatriation. Exporters may retain all proceeds in any currency. For a Mainland Chinese parent receiving trade proceeds from Hong Kong or overseas, SAFE rules may require registration, verification, and repatriation within the statutory window; however, the HK-domiciled JV is outside the onshore SAFE surrender dossier.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China jointly regulate cross-border flows between the Hong Kong SAR and Mainland China.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Hong Kong requires stored value facility (SVF) operators to hold an HKMA SVF license under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, and banks must be licensed under the Banking Ordinance. Offshore fintech EMIs that are not licensed in Hong Kong cannot access local RTGS/CHATS clearing and are legally barred from maintaining designated bank accounts for Hong Kong corporations. A licensed virtual bank such as ZA Bank, Mox Bank, WeLab Bank or Livi Bank is viable for a charitable foundation, but only for operational treasury, not as a substitute for a traditional Tier-1 lender. Risk-based onboarding may impose shorter transaction limits and enhanced CDD for grant distributions. Therefore, foreign EMIs such as European e-money institutions have Low to Moderate viability and should be used only as supplementary payment rails, never as the sole clearing route, due to regulatory restrictions on holding legal grant transfers and their limited ability to provide notarized capital verification vouchers.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound capital injection from a Mainland Chinese parent to the Hong Kong JV must clear via a designated SAFE-registered bank under the Outbound Direct Investment (ODI) regime. The equity capital or shareholder loan should be remitted through SWIFT MT103 or HK-CHATS from the mainland gateway with the bank referencing the SAFE ODI filing number, the investment nature 'Equity Contribution', and SAFE purpose code 621011 for Overseas Direct Investment. In Hong Kong, the receiving bank credits the capital escrow account upon submission of the Hong Kong Companies Registry certificate, Board resolution, signed share subscription agreement, and CPA capital verification. For non-mainland investors, funds can be remitted directly in USD, EUR, HKD, or CNH without SAFE approval; however, the account name must match the contract party under FATF Recommendation 16. All inbound wire instructions should state 'Capital Contribution - No Trade Payment' and must not be mispriced as trade proceeds. The bank's compliance team will complete a trigger review, and the final step requires a Hong Kong certified public accountant's capital verification report confirming that the funds are credited and matched to issued shares.Grant distributions and trade settlements can be cleared via (1) SWIFT MT103 for straightforward grant remittances and purchase payments, using a structured beneficiary field with full legal name, bank identifier (BIC), and grant reference; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP 600 issued via SWIFT MT700 for procurement contracts linked to philanthropic projects, where a confirmed or unconfirmed LC protects the beneficiary; (3) Documentary Collections under URC 522 using SWIFT MT730 or MT400 for trade disbursements where buyer and seller have an established commercial relationship and want lower costs than an LC. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Hong Kong requires stored value facility (SVF) operators to hold an HKMA SVF license under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance, and banks must be licensed under the Banking Ordinance. Offshore fintech EMIs that are not licensed in Hong Kong cannot access local RTGS/CHATS clearing and are legally barred from maintaining designated bank accounts for Hong Kong corporations. A licensed virtual bank such as ZA Bank, Mox Bank, WeLab Bank or Livi Bank is viable for a charitable foundation, but only for operational treasury, not as a substitute for a traditional Tier-1 lender. Risk-based onboarding may impose shorter transaction limits and enhanced CDD for grant distributions. Therefore, foreign EMIs such as European e-money institutions have Low to Moderate viability and should be used only as supplementary payment rails, never as the sole clearing route, due to regulatory restrictions on holding legal grant transfers and their limited ability to provide notarized capital verification vouchers.
