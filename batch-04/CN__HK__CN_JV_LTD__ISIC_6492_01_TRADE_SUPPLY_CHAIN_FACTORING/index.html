Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic portal: China National Electronic Taxation Administration (e-Tax), accessible via chinatax.gov.cn. For Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and the Business Registration Office, accessed through the GovHK eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-foreign equity joint venture incorporated in Mainland China, the entity must first be established through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the primary taxpayer identification number. The JV must then complete tax registration within 30 days via the STA e-Tax portal, after which the STA assigns the definitive taxpayer identification number, VAT payer status, and tax filing obligations. A foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China is not automatically issued a USCC; instead, before claiming treaty benefits or receiving China-sourced income, it must obtain a non-resident tax identification number by completing tax registration through e-Tax or through the withholding agent, who files the required withholding tax registration. If the same group maintains a Hong Kong branch or a Hong Kong-incorporated vehicle, that entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate with the Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of incorporation or commencement of business; the certificate number is the Hong Kong taxpayer identifier for profits tax, and the IRD eTAX portal is the statutory filing channel.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation with Territorial_Scope overlay. The Mainland Chinese joint venture is automatically a PRC resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at 25% corporate income tax; no pass-through, Tax-Exempt Zone, or entity-level check-the-box election applies. For Hong Kong, profits tax follows the territorial source principle, so only profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong are taxable, while offshore profits are outside the charge to Hong Kong profits tax.

Available election forms

None for entity classification. The PRC system does not permit a check-the-box or pass-through election for a limited company. Anti-hybrid and controlled foreign corporation rules apply under the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law. For Hong Kong profits tax, there is no entity-classification election; an offshore claim may be made on a factual source-of-profit basis, but that is not a statutory election. For VAT, a below-threshold taxpayer may voluntarily elect General VAT taxpayer status even when mandatory registration is not triggered.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer treatment applies unless voluntary general registration is elected. A non-resident supplier with no Chinese establishment is not required to register for VAT solely as a matter of local registration, but the PRC purchaser is required to withhold VAT at source on cross-border taxable services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT, exported goods qualify for zero-rating where the goods are physically exported from mainland China, sold to a non-resident purchaser, covered by an accepted export customs declaration, and supported by repatriated export proceeds matched to the customs declaration. Exported services are zero-rated only if they fall within the statutory catalogue of offshore zero-rated services; cross-border financial services, including trade finance and factoring, are not zero-rated and may only qualify for exemption if the service is consumed wholly outside China. The trade-factoring entity should not treat its service fees as zero-rated export turnover unless the services independently satisfy the statutory offshore-consumption test.

  • Customs Export Declaration lodged through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original bill of lading, sea waybill, or airway bill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank export-proceeds collection receipt validated against the SAFE Foreign Exchange Monitoring System for Trade in Goods
  • Special VAT invoices and other input-VAT documentation supporting the export refund calculation
  • Customs inspection records or export verification notices where applicable

All VAT invoices are issued through the STA VAT invoice management system. General VAT taxpayers must issue Special VAT Invoices using the digital tax-control U-Key or fully digital electronic invoices through the national e-invoice platform; invoice data is transmitted in real time to the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform. Golden Tax Phase IV cross-matches invoice, customs, logistics, settlement, and foreign-exchange data to detect false invoicing and abnormal VAT refunds. Hong Kong has no e-invoicing, fiscal clearance, or anti-fraud digital stamping mandate for VAT purposes, though proper books and records must be retained for Inland Revenue Ordinance compliance.

Export VAT refund claims are audited through STA, customs, and SAFE data sharing; if zero-rating is unsubstantiated, the refund is denied or retrospectively clawed back with statutory interest. Administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Administration Law range from 0.5 to 5 times the tax unpaid or improperly refunded, and fraudulent export refund claims are punishable under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. False customs declarations attract penalties under the Customs Law. For Hong Kong profits tax, a false offshore claim may result in penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including a fine and additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

On the mainland Chinese leg, the Renminbi (CNY) is convertible for current account transactions, including goods trade, transportation, insurance, and the associated service payments, without prior SAFE approval; however, each bank payment must be supported by authentic contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and commercial documents. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment into the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, shareholder loans, equity transfer consideration, and dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent, are not freely convertible and require SAFE registration or filing, a designated domestic foreign-exchange bank, and a valid underlying transaction. The Hong Kong SAR leg is fully convertible; HKD, USD, and CNH positions can be freely held, transferred, and remitted without exchange control restrictions. For the PRC entity, current account receipts must be repatriated and reported under the SAFE trade monitoring system, and capital account receipts must be used strictly within the SAFE-approved scope. Dividend remittance from the PRC JV to the HK parent is permitted after audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement, and a board resolution, but the paying bank must verify realised distributable profits, unimpaired equity, and a valid SAFE capital account filing. This creates a legal convertibility framework layered with fact-driven bank compliance checks, particularly for the receivable factoring business.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods trade foreign exchange administration framework, export proceeds from cross-border trade must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within the baseline statutory window of 90 days from export customs clearance unless the exporter has filed an extension with SAFE. The current mandatory conversion ratio is zero; exporters may voluntarily retain qualifying export proceeds in foreign currency or convert them into CNY through a designated bank. However, SAFE and PBOC retain macro-prudential powers to impose temporary surrender requirements or countercyclical adjustment factors during disorderly market conditions. In Hong Kong, there is no surrender obligation for export proceeds, and HKD/USD/CNH funds can circulate freely.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China leg; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong SAR leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for the PRC onshore leg and Medium-High only for ancillary Hong Kong SAR payment activity. A foreign fintech EMI is not licensed by SAFE or PBOC to perform capital account opening, SAFE-registered FX settlement, export proceeds verification, or onshore CNY clearing for the mainland JV. If an unlicensed EMI is used as a settlement rail for the PRC factoring portfolio, it would create illegal cross-border exchange and trigger severe bank AML and licensing sanctions. In Hong Kong, an HKMA-licensed stored value facility can initiate low-value payments, but it is not an authorised corporation bank and cannot support the capital escrow, receivables purchase, or commercial letter of credit operations of a trade finance entity. Therefore, the full treasury structure must sit on licensed commercial banks, with the EMI layer used only as a voluntary payment initiation accessory and never as the regulatory settlement rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the HK parent into the PRC JV must be structured as a registered foreign direct investment. The JV must complete SAFE FDI registration and open a designated capital account at an eligible bank. Each inbound remittance must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the HK investor's bank to the JV's designated capital account bank. The payment must cite the SAFE balance-of-payments purpose code for FDI capital injection, commonly 622011 in the six-digit transaction classification, and the remittance information field must clearly state 'FDI Capital Injection' with the JV's unified social credit code. Funds must enter the capital account before conversion into CNY, after which the bank will complete foreign exchange settlement, the JV must report the receipt under the foreign investment information system, and an in-house or third-party Chinese CPA must issue the capital verification report before the registered capital status is updated.Outbound trade disbursements from the PRC JV to overseas beneficiaries, including the Hong Kong factoring leg, may settle through four principal rails: SWIFT MT103 for open account trade payments under the SAFE trade baseline; SWIFT MT700 and MT707 for documentary commercial letters of credit issued under UCP600; documentary collections under URC522 for D/P and D/A shipping document processing; and CIPS for direct cross-border RMB clearing with a CNH beneficiary in Hong Kong. Every outbound trade payment must match the import customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and underlying commercial contract, and must be declared under the SAFE goods trade balance-of-payments code to permit the bank to release funds within the approved FX current account framework.Onshore CNY forward FX contracts for USD/CNY, HKD/CNY, and EUR/CNY are permissible for the PRC JV only when supported by a genuine current account or capital account exposure, with a tenor not exceeding the underlying cash flow and full SAFE-compliant documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) in USD/CNH are permissible only in the Hong Kong treasury centre and are prohibited for the PRC onshore legal entity as a standalone hedge.Cross-currency swaps between CNY and USD/HKD are permissible where the JV has an approved cross-border financing, capital injection, or dividend plan, and the swap notional cannot exceed the underlying exposure, with any unused notional cancelled or terminated upon maturity.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for the PRC onshore leg and Medium-High only for ancillary Hong Kong SAR payment activity. A foreign fintech EMI is not licensed by SAFE or PBOC to perform capital account opening, SAFE-registered FX settlement, export proceeds verification, or onshore CNY clearing for the mainland JV. If an unlicensed EMI is used as a settlement rail for the PRC factoring portfolio, it would create illegal cross-border exchange and trigger severe bank AML and licensing sanctions. In Hong Kong, an HKMA-licensed stored value facility can initiate low-value payments, but it is not an authorised corporation bank and cannot support the capital escrow, receivables purchase, or commercial letter of credit operations of a trade finance entity. Therefore, the full treasury structure must sit on licensed commercial banks, with the EMI layer used only as a voluntary payment initiation accessory and never as the regulatory settlement rail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 100,000,000 aggregate value of specified intercompany controlled transactions for Local File preparation; a Master File is required where the multinational enterprise group has annual consolidated group turnover exceeding HKD 600,000,000 or total assets exceeding HKD 300,000,000 with more than 100 employees, per Hong Kong IRD transfer pricing documentation practice; the taxpayer must nevertheless prepare contemporaneous evidence for all material related party transactions including factoring receivables, financing balances, service fees and intangible royalties regardless of threshold

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain a statutory Economic Substance Regulations regime in the style of the UAE, but it applies source-based taxation and substantive anti-avoidance doctrine under IRO s.61 and s.61A. A Hong Kong entity must demonstrate genuine business activity, effective management and control, employees performing core income-generating activities (CIGA), and a real economic presence in Hong Kong to claim treaty benefits, territorial source exclusions, or DTA relief. An artificial conduit used solely to hold receivables, route factoring fees, or exploit the name of Hong Kong as a low-tax jurisdiction will be pierced, and the IRD may disregard the structure under anti-avoidance provisions and recharacterise profits as Hong Kong sourced in the hands of the non-resident beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Profits Tax Declaration

Statutory sanction: IRO s.82(1) criminal tax evasion: upon summary conviction or indictment, a fine of up to HKD 100,000, additional tax equal to treble the amount of tax sought to be evaded, and imprisonment for up to 3 years. IRO s.82A also allows the IRD to impose additional tax of up to 300% of the undercharged tax without a criminal conviction. The Comptroller of Inland Revenue may recover tax as a civil debt and the court may make asset restraint orders.

Director personal liability: Directors and tax officers who knowingly sign false returns or cause the JV to suppress income can be prosecuted personally. The corporate veil will be pierced where the company is found to be a sham or an instrumentality of the directors; directors may be personally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax and interest, disgorgement of diverted assets, disqualification as directors, asset freezing and departure prevention orders effectively creating a travel ban for the directors, and imprisonment for up to 3 years on conviction for fraudulent tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Company Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Hong Kong transfer pricing rules under IRO s.50AAF and the anti-avoidance provisions of IRO s.61 and s.61A allow the IRD to adjust the profits under the arm's-length standard, disregard the artificial arrangement and recharacterise the actual controlled transaction. The adjusted assessable profits are subject to profits tax at 16.5%; in addition, the IRD may impose s.82A additional tax up to 300% of undercharged tax. DTA benefits are denied where beneficial ownership and economic substance are absent.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot hide behind limited liability if the Hong Kong JV is used as a conduit for tax evasion or treaty abuse; the court may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally liable for the full amount of tax avoided plus any civil penalties and costs. Directors may also face personal liability for fraudulent trading under the Companies Ordinance, bank account freezes and travel restrictions, exclusion from future directorship, and personal criminal exposure where the manipulation is accompanied by false invoicing or money laundering.
Unlicensed Factoring and Cross-Border AML / Money Service Offences

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed money-lending business is an offence under the Money Lenders Ordinance Cap.163; unlicensed loans are not automatically enforceable, the offender is liable to a substantial fine, and the assets of the unlawful factoring business are subject to confiscation. The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance Cap.615 imposes registration, CDD, record-keeping and STR obligations for cross-border payment and remittance business; contraventions carry criminal fines and imprisonment, and money laundering offences carry up to 7 years imprisonment for individuals.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate unlicensed factoring transactions face personal criminal prosecution as principals or accomplices; civil asset restitution orders may require directors to repay all illegal interest and fees extracted from customers, and the directors' personal assets may be traced, frozen and confiscated. Travel bans and immigration restriction are common in cross-border AML cases where the director is assessed as a flight risk; the directors may also be disqualified, jailed for serious AML offences, and made personally liable to the liquidator where the factoring scheme operates as a phoenix operation or fraudulent trading.
