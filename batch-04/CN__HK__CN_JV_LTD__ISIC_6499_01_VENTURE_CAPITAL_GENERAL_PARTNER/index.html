Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A private company limited by shares may incorporate with issued share capital as low as HK$1, and there is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement for a Hong Kong company. However, if the entity acts as a General Partner managing a venture capital fund under the Limited Partnership Fund Ordinance (Cap. 637), the partnership deed must evidence the General Partner commitment and capital drawdown procedures. If the entity also applies for an SFC Type 9 asset management licence, the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules require minimum paid-up share capital of HK$5,000,000 and required liquid capital of HK$3,000,000, with subscribed capital actually credited to the corporate bank account before regulated activities commence.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department certification for any fire service installations installed at the registered office, including fire alarm, detection and emergency lighting systems, must be maintained in accordance with the Fire Service (Installations and Equipment) Regulations Cap. 95 sub. leg. The registered office must obtain either a fire safety compliance certificate or a declaration of no fire service installations where the property has no such systems. The Buildings Department must approve any alteration or change of use affecting structural load, and the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance Cap. 509 governs workplace safety obligations, including a safe means of access, maintenance of fire escape routes and provision of first-aid equipment. For this pure financial management entity, no Civil Defense hazardous materials or dangerous goods license is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau / Electronic Taxpayer Service System; and, for the Hong Kong SAR leg, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD) through the eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture established in Mainland China, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) upon incorporation. Under the multi-certificate integration reform, the SAMR registration is automatically transmitted to the STA and the USCC constitutes the enterprise's Taxpayer Identification Number for both Corporate Income Tax and Value-Added Tax; the JV then logs into the Electronic Tax Bureau to complete taxpayer registration, select VAT taxpayer status, and request invoice types. For the Hong Kong corporate shareholder or investor, the IRD requires a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) if it carries on business in Hong Kong; a non-resident investor not carrying on business in Hong Kong may obtain a Certificate of Resident Status (CoRS) from the IRD for treaty purposes. If a foreign non-resident enterprise provides services into China without establishing a permanent establishment, it must apply for provisional or temporary tax registration with the competent STA office at the place of the withholding agent or where the income arises, thereby obtaining a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identification number, and may then apply for treaty relief under the Arrangement between the Mainland of China and the Hong Kong SAR for the Avoidance of Double Taxation and the Prevention of Fiscal Evasion with respect to Taxes on Income.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Corporate Income Tax law taxes the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture as a resident enterprise on its worldwide income; there is no check-the-box or fiscal-transparent pass-through election. The Hong Kong investor may elect to apply treaty protection under the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, and eligible cross-border restructuring may access the special Corporate Income Tax treatment election, but neither is an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold under the PRC VAT regulations and applicable implementing measures; below that threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies, and small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 are temporarily exempt from VAT. Fund management fee income of a venture capital general partner is within the taxable services category and, when the JV is a general taxpayer, is invoiced at the 6% VAT rate applicable to direct-charge financial services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, the PRC VAT law zero-rates export sales if the goods are declared to customs, physically leave the People's Republic of China, and all export rebate documentation is in place; the exporter must be a VAT general taxpayer. For cross-border services, only services expressly listed in the cross-border taxable services zero-rating catalogue, such as international transportation, research and development, design, software, and information system services, are zero-rated. Other offshore services, including fund management services provided to a non-resident venture capital fund, may qualify for VAT exemption rather than zero-rating if the service recipient is outside China, the service is wholly consumed outside China, the fee is received from abroad, and contracts, invoices and payment receipts clearly evidence the offshore supply. The JV must also demonstrate that the services do not relate to PRC real estate, goods located in China, or services supplied to a PRC domestic entity for domestic use.

  • Customs Export Declaration for export rebate issued by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Export sales contract
  • Bank receipt or Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form evidencing collection of export proceeds from the overseas buyer

All VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System Phase IV real-time clearance and inspection framework. The JV must issue invoices through the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform using the STA-generated invoice number, QR code, and tax-control checkpoint. Special VAT invoices must be uploaded in real time for national electronic invoice verification and anti-fraud digital stamping, and input VAT credit is allowed only after the invoice passes STA verification.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs jointly monitor export declarations, VAT special invoices and foreign-exchange receipts. If the required customs, invoice or foreign-exchange documents are not obtained within the statutory period, or are found to be false, the VAT zero-rating is revoked and the already refunded VAT is clawed back together with late-payment interest. Under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection, fraudulent export VAT refund claims attract a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the refund and, where criminal intent is established, referral under the Criminal Law provisions prohibiting fraudulent export tax refunds. The same clawback and penalty framework applies where the JV fails to substantiate cross-border service exemption with offshore payment receipts or holds insufficient proof that the services were consumed wholly outside China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong maintains a fully convertible market currency under the Linked Exchange Rate System of HKD 7.75 to 7.85 per US dollar. There are no capital controls, no surrender requirements, and no distinction between current and capital account for HK-incorporated treasury operations; dividends, management fees, redemption proceeds, and investment returns can be remitted freely in HKD, USD, CNY, or any major currency through authorized banks. The real regulatory boundary comes from the PRC parent leg: if funds originate in mainland China, the outbound remittance must run through the SAFE ODI filing and registration framework, with MOFCOM certificate and NDRC overseas investment approval where applicable. Offshore CNH in Hong Kong is freely tradable, while onshore CNY remains strictly regulated. The HK JV/GP treasury should separate PRC-sourced capital from free HKD/USD liquidity. Profit remittance from Hong Kong to China faces no exchange control hurdle in Hong Kong, but the PRC parent must recognise taxable dividend or deemed income under PRC rules. Informal corridors, back-to-back lending, or nominee clearing between mainland RMB and HK CNH are illegal and will trigger enhanced AML scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no export proceeds surrender requirement and no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in any currency. For underlying PRC subsidiaries in the supply chain, SAFE requires export receipts to be repatriated to designated foreign exchange banks and export-verification procedures to be completed within the statutory window (generally 30 days from customs declaration unless deferred collection is approved). Any export FX balance above the approved current-account settlement and sale quota must be converted into CNY, effectively a 100% surrender of surplus proceeds beyond the SAFE-approved retention limit.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); for PRC-origin cross-border flows, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC) regulates the mainland side.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Limited and constrained. HK-licensed virtual banks such as ZA Bank Limited, Mox Bank Limited, and WeLab Bank Limited are legal and can provide basic corporate accounts, but they are usually not optimal as the primary clearing rail for a capital-calling venture capital GP due to transaction size limits, more conservative investment-fund KYC policies, and less developed SWIFT/RTGS treasury services. Unlicensed offshore EMI or neobank platforms without an HK banking licence are legally barred from accepting deposits or providing settlement accounts in the entity's name under the HKMA Banking Ordinance, so they are not viable for custody of capital calls, FX hedging, or MT103 clearing. On the PRC mainland side, offshore EMIs cannot onboard the Chinese JV parent or subsidiary because only PBOC-licensed non-bank payment institutions and regulated banks may handle corporate fund settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from the PRC parent must move through the approved SAFE ODI channel. The compliant sequence is: (1) complete NDRC and MOFCOM filing and obtain the SAFE foreign exchange registration at the parent's designated PRC bank; (2) instruct a SWIFT MT103 remittance using the bank's designated overseas-direct-investment purpose code and the JV shareholders' agreed capital contribution schedule; (3) receive funds in the HK entity's dedicated HKD or USD account at a Tier-1 bank, with all inward documentation retained for HKMA and bank CDD inspection; (4) appoint an HK certified public accountant to perform a capital verification report after the injection; and (5) for LP capital calls, use a segregated capital-call account and route each drawdown wire with a unique LP reference and supporting capital call notice. The HK entity must never allow personal account transfers or split invoices to disguise the true source of funds.Trade and investment disbursements must be executed from segregated operational accounts using formal clearing rails. The standard cash-clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 (and MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) through the HKMA-designated CHATS RTGS for HKD, USD, CNH, and EUR; for commercial transactions, the GP can use Commercial Letters of Credit under UCP600 and Documentary Collections under URC522, supported by invoice, bill of lading, packing list, and insurance certificates. Venture capital disbursements to portfolio companies should be made by SWIFT MT103 only after an investment committee approval and board resolution, with the target company's registered share allocation or loan agreement attached to the payment instruction. Turnover: In Hong Kong, there is no turnover, balance sheet or headcount threshold that exempts an active company from statutory audit. Every Hong Kong incorporated private company must prepare financial statements and have them audited by a certified public accountant in public practice, except a dormant company that has passed the required dormant-company resolution. For small private entities qualifying under the Companies Ordinance and the Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards framework, the quantitative thresholds commonly applied are annual revenue not exceeding HKD 50 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 50 million and not more than 100 employees; these thresholds only permit simplified financial reporting and do not remove the audit obligation. A licensed venture capital general partner entity is additionally required by SFC licence conditions to obtain audited accounts from an independent certified public accountant irrespective of size, with the auditor's report in the form required under the Securities and Futures Ordinance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance and IRD Departmental Interpretation and Practice Note No. 58, contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (local file) is generally required where the aggregate value of controlled transactions exceeds HK$4,000,000; a master file is expected where the multinational group's consolidated global revenue exceeds HK$6,800,000,000. A CbC report is separately filed if group revenue exceeds the equivalent of EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the IRD applies an economic substance doctrine to deny offshore-profit claims and to protect transfer pricing adjustments. The venture capital GP must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as investment approval, risk management, fund accounting, capital call execution, and management of portfolio companies are performed by employees and directors in Hong Kong. A shell company with no local staff, no physical office, and no decision-making power will not satisfy the economic substance requirement and will be deemed to be a conduit taxable on all profits in Hong Kong. For the carried interest concession, CIGA must be performed in Hong Kong and the investment management licensed by SFC under Part V of the Securities and Futures Ordinance, as applicable.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) section 82(1) and section 82A impose criminal liability on the company and every director for knowingly signing an incorrect return, with a maximum fine of HK$50,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus a civil penalty of 200% of the tax undercharged. Disqualification from holding directorship may also follow.

Director personal liability: Under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, directors who use the HK JV as a mere facade will be jointly and severally liable for the whole tax, interest and penalties. The IRD may seek a court order for asset restitution and may apply to the District Court to prohibit the directors from leaving Hong Kong until outstanding tax liabilities are settled. Conviction for tax fraud can additionally lead to confiscation proceeds, disqualification orders under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), and imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong IRD is authorised under IRO section 50AA to adjust the Hong Kong entity's assessable profits to an arm's length price; a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged may be imposed on the mispricing. Simultaneously, the PRC State Taxation Administration may invoke Article 41 and Article 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law to make a special tax adjustment by imputing taxable income, adding interest measured at the base lending rate plus five percentage points, and imposing a 10% late payment penalty on the tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who participate in the structured profit-stripping arrangement can be treated as joint actors in tax nonpayment. Chinese tax authorities may issue a tax blacklist warning, freeze personal bank accounts, impose travel bans, and hold directors personally liable for unpaid enterprise income tax to the extent that the limited liability company is deemed a sham; upon criminal conviction, directors may face imprisonment of up to 7 years for fraudulent tax avoidance.
Anti-Money Laundering and Unlicensed Capital Formation

Statutory sanction: Violations of the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) (Cap. 615), specifically sections 9, 11, and 12, are punishable on conviction with a fine of up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years. Money laundering offences under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) section 25(1) carry a maximum penalty of 14 years' imprisonment and a fine of HK$5,000,000. The Securities and Futures Commission may also impose civil penalties and asset freezing orders.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly use the shell company to launder funds pierce the corporate veil and become personally liable for restitution of all investor proceeds and any profits earned. The court may order confiscation of personal assets, including Hong Kong and overseas properties, under the Proceeds of Crime Ordinance. Directors will be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Police and JFIU, subject to travel restrictions, and disqualified from serving as directors of any Hong Kong company for up to 15 years as a consequence of fraudulent and unlawful conduct.
