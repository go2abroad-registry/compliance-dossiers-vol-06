Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, operating through the GovHK electronic portal and the IRD eTAX online filing system. The State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China does not exercise direct tax administration competence in Hong Kong under the constitutional 'One Country, Two Systems' principle.

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture / special purpose acquisition company, the entity must first be incorporated with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance and obtain a Certificate of Incorporation. Within one month after incorporation, the entity must apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance. The certificate contains the Business Registration Number which is the entity's principal Hong Kong tax identifier, used for all IRD correspondence, profits tax returns, employer returns, and withholding tax lodgments. There is no separate VAT ID because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST. A foreign non-resident entity that does not carry on a trade, profession or business in Hong Kong is generally not required to register with the IRD solely because it holds shares in a Hong Kong company; dividends paid by a Hong Kong company are not subject to Hong Kong profits tax. If the foreign parent establishes a Hong Kong branch instead of a locally incorporated subsidiary, the non-resident entity must register as a Registered Non-Hong Kong Company with the Companies Registry, then obtain a Business Registration Certificate for the Hong Kong branch. The same Business Registration Number protocol applies. If the entity needs treaty relief or a residence certificate, it may apply to the IRD for a Certificate of Hong Kong Resident Status using the official application procedure; this is a certification request, not a tax registration event.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box entity classification election, fiscal unity regime, or hybrid mismatch election mechanism. A Hong Kong incorporated company is automatically treated as a separate company taxable on territorial-source profits; shareholders are not taxed at the company level through pass-through treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None. Hong Kong imposes no value-added tax, goods and services tax, or general retail sales tax. Transactional taxes are limited to stamp duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance and duties on specified commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST turnover threshold in Hong Kong because no indirect tax is levied. The only entity-level registration obligation is the Business Registration Ordinance, which requires any person carrying on a business in Hong Kong to apply for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencing business, irrespective of revenue volume. A Hong Kong incorporated company is required to obtain business registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable as a VAT concept. Because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST, all exports are outside the scope of Hong Kong indirect tax and there is no zero-rating claim, export VAT refund, or input tax recovery mechanism. For profits tax purposes, the relevant substantive test is territorial source: profits from goods sold to customers outside Hong Kong may be treated as offshore-source profits where the operations that generate the profits are carried on outside Hong Kong. Qualifying offshore services are likewise outside Hong Kong profits tax if the service is performed and the profit source is determined outside Hong Kong under established IRD source principles. A SPAC's listing, escrow, and merger income must be analysed under that territorial source framework, not under any export VAT policy.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) submitted through the Trade and Customs Declaration System to the Hong Kong Census and Statistics Department and Customs and Excise Department where the export stream is subject to declaration
  • Original or certified copy of the Bill of Lading, or Air Waybill for air cargo, evidencing physical movement of the goods from Hong Kong to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), SWIFT MT103/MT202, or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt advice confirming repatriation of export proceeds to the Hong Kong entity's bank account
  • Commercial invoice, purchase order, contract, packing list, and cargo insurance documents showing the overseas customer, delivery destination, and transfer of title and risk outside Hong Kong
  • Operational logistics file comprising carrier booking confirmations, customs broker reports, haulage receipts, and delivery proof records substantiating the offshore supply chain

No statutory electronic invoicing clearance mandate exists in Hong Kong. There is no real-time fiscal clearance, no government tax authority invoice stamping API, and no continuous transaction control system for VAT/GST purposes. The IRD eTAX portal supports electronic filing of tax returns but does not function as an invoice clearance or anti-fraud fiscal stamping system. Electronic invoicing between commercial parties is voluntary and not tax-authorised.

There is no VAT credit clawback system in Hong Kong because no VAT is chargeable. The audit risk focuses on profits tax territorial-source claims. The IRD may reopen assessments within six years in non-fraud cases and without time limit in cases of fraud or wilful neglect. Where an offshore-source claim or export documentation is found false, the IRD may disallow the treatment, assess the undercharged profits tax, and impose civil penalties up to treble the tax evaded plus interest, with prosecutions possible under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. False export declarations and fraudulent customs documents are also prosecuted by the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department under the relevant customs legislation, with penalties including fines, forfeiture of goods, and imprisonment in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is not fully freely convertible outside mainland. For current account items such as trade in goods and services, RMB is convertible under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by documentation. For capital account items, including FDI injections, SPAC trust funding, shareholder loans, cross-border repayments, and profit repatriation, advance registration, approval, or filing with SAFE is mandatory before any bank is permitted to execute the transaction. The HK-incorporated JV can act as a freely convertible liquidity hub in HKD and USD, because the HKMA currency board maintains full convertibility under Hong Kong law; however, any HKD or USD converted into CNY and remitted to a mainland operating subsidiary must pass through a licensed onshore bank with a valid FDI or foreign debt registration. Profit remittance from a Sino-foreign equity joint venture is not automatic: before dividends are paid offshore, the JV must complete an annual statutory audit, file tax returns, settle withholding tax, provide board minutes declaring the dividend, and obtain a SAFE foreign exchange registration for the distribution. The remitting bank will verify that the profit originated from real after-tax retained earnings and that no distribution is made from uncertified capital proceeds. The group should therefore treat Hong Kong as its external banking gateway and inject PRC-bound capital only through compliant FDI rails with full SAFE filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE practice, there is no mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts, so the statutory requirement for conversion into CNY is effectively 0%. Export proceeds generated by mainland operating entities must nonetheless be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange settlement account through banking channels within 180 days from the date of export declaration, unless an offshore retention arrangement is specifically approved by SAFE. After repatriation, any voluntary conversion into CNY is executed at the onshore spot rate with the bank verifying the customs declaration, invoice, transport documents, and Goods Trade Monitoring System records to prove the trade background.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for cross-border capital flows; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for HKD clearing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and unlawful for principal capital escrow. A fintech EMI holding only a stored value facility or money service operator licence cannot act as a trust bank under Hong Kong SPAC requirements or as a domestic settlement bank under SAFE's capital account regime. PBOC requires any payment institution to hold a valid payment business licence, but that licence does not authorise the acceptance of FDI injection, SPAC subscription proceeds, or corporate deposits; HKMA's deposit-taking regime restricts unlicensed EMIs from receiving customer funds in the manner required for a special purpose acquisition company. Therefore, an offshore digital EMI is legally barred from servicing the main capital and clearing rails for this entity. At most, a fintech EMI may support ancillary employee cards or visibility software, but never as the depository of trust escrow funds, inbound equity capital, or outbound trade settlement principal.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Shareholder funds entering the HK SPAC must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 to the designated licensed bank account with the remitter name, beneficiary account number, SWIFT BIC, and a clear payment purpose code such as equity contribution under the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture Agreement. If funds are converted to RMB for a mainland operating subsidiary, the group must first complete SAFE FDI registration and obtain the Foreign Investor Certificate; the money is then transferred from the HK JV account to a mainland capital account settlement account opened at a Tier-1 bank, followed by a certified public accountant capital verification report before the funds can be used. No funds may be bundled with trade payments, repaid intra-group loans, or routed through an EMI. If the acquisition target is in mainland and the deal has not yet closed, the inbound capital must remain locked in a SPAC trust escrow account until the acquisition agreement conditions are satisfied, after which disbursement is executed by the custodian under the escrow and trust deed.Use SWIFT MT103 for plain wire payments of professional fees, escrow charges, listing expenses, and small operational disbursements with full purpose codes and source of funds references. For higher-value cross-border trade settlements, issue an irrevocable commercial letter of credit from a Tier-1 bank via SWIFT MT700, with documentary presentation under UCP600 and payment against compliant shipping and title documents. For lower-risk and recurring trade flows, use documentary collections on D/P or D/A terms, routed through correspondent banks with clean title documents and INCOTERMS specified. CNY-denominated cross-border disbursements should be cleared through CIPS direct participant rails with a CNAPS/IBPS mainland leg, while HKD-denominated outflows settle through the HKMA CHATS RTGS system. A fintech EMI holding only a stored value facility or money service operator licence cannot act as a trust bank under Hong Kong SPAC requirements or as a domestic settlement bank under SAFE's capital account regime. PBOC requires any payment institution to hold a valid payment business licence, but that licence does not authorise the acceptance of FDI injection, SPAC subscription proceeds, or corporate deposits; HKMA's deposit-taking regime restricts unlicensed EMIs from receiving customer funds in the manner required for a special purpose acquisition company. Therefore, an offshore digital EMI is legally barred from servicing the main capital and clearing rails for this entity. At most, a fintech EMI may support ancillary employee cards or visibility software, but never as the depository of trust escrow funds, inbound equity capital, or outbound trade settlement principal.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory where the taxpayer's annual intercompany transaction value exceeds HK$44,000,000 for general transactions, HK$100,000,000 for financial and intangible transactions, or HK$200,000,000 for land/building transfers. Master File required if the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds HK$6,800,000,000. CbCR filing if the group total consolidated revenue exceeds HK$6.8 billion and the parent consolidates in HK.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not implement OECD ESR-style economic substance legislation (as in BVI/CAY), but imposes a substantive territorial source test under s.14 and an anti-avoidance rule under s.61A. Practically, the IRD applies a CIGA (core income-generating activities) approach to determine profit sourcing. For a SPAC, the CIGA include the negotiation, due diligence, financing, risk assessment, and board approval of the acquisition – these must take place outside Hong Kong to claim offshore exemption. A SPAC maintaining its registered office, bank accounts, directors' meetings and decision-making in Hong Kong will likely be treated as deriving HK-source profits. The SPAC must have adequate employee substance (governance, compliance officer) and business records; otherwise it will be attacked as a shell and subject to full taxation plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsifying Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Sections 82A and 80(2) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap 112): upon conviction on indictment – a fine up to HK$500,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus an additional tax not exceeding 3 times the tax undercharged. Upon summary conviction – a fine up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 6 months, plus additional tax of 3 times.

Director personal liability: A director who knowingly signs or approves a false return is personally liable to prosecution and can be imprisoned up to 3 years. The corporate veil may be pierced where the company is used as a facade for tax fraud. The court may order restitution of the underlying tax plus interest, impose a travel ban (leaving Hong Kong prohibited), and disqualify the director from managing a company for up to 15 years under s.34-35 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622).
Anti-Money Laundering Offences (SPAC Vehicles)

Statutory sanction: Sections 25, 25A of the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap 455) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615): money laundering is punishable by a fine of HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment for 14 years (on indictment). Regulatory breaches under AMLO are subject to disciplinary fines and licence revocation by the HKMA/SFC (up to HK$10,000,000).

Director personal liability: Directors who were aware or reasonably should have been aware of the laundering are criminally liable as principals. Asset forfeiture orders can reach the director's direct personal assets if they benefited from the crime; overseas travel can be restricted pending investigation. Liability may also arise for breach of fiduciary duty, requiring the director to personally restore the company's lost funds. Imprisonment up to 14 years.
Abusive Shell Company / Sham Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: Section 61 and 61A of the IRO (anti-avoidance) allows the IRD to disregard the sham and assess tax as if the arrangement had not been entered into, plus a 100% additional assessment for negligently incorrect returns. The company may be struck off under s.744 of the Companies Ordinance for non-compliance; the SFC may suspend the SPAC securities listing.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the company to engage in fraudulent trading or a sham arrangement are personally liable for the company's debts and tax liabilities under s.275 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622). Criminal penalties include an unlimited fine and imprisonment for up to 15 years. The IRD may issue a notice of personal liability and the court may impose a restitution order against the director's assets. The director can be blacklisted from entering or leaving Hong Kong if a warrant is issued.
