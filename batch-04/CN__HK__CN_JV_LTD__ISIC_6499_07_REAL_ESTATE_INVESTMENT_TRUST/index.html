Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not permit a check-the-box pass-through classification for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, and China has no domestic hybrid mismatch election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every PRC enterprise is required to register for VAT at formation; annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 determine small-scale taxpayer status, while an enterprise that expects to exceed this threshold or voluntarily meets the general taxpayer criteria must register as a general VAT taxpayer. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must file VAT returns under its Unified Social Credit Code from the date of establishment regardless of whether its first-year turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold. The Hong Kong parent has no indirect tax registration obligation because Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China qualify for the 0% VAT rate if the export has been declared to customs and the exporter is the registered seller; the export documents must show the goods left China and the proceeds must be received from outside China. Qualifying offshore services, including certain cross-border management, leasing, financial, and digital services supplied by a PRC entity to an overseas recipient, qualify for zero rating only if the services are listed in the current State Taxation Administration cross-border taxable services catalogue, are supplied to an overseas party, are performed and consumed wholly outside mainland China, and are supported by the service contract, performance evidence, and foreign exchange receipts. Domestic rental income from PRC real estate held by the REIT is taxed at 9% VAT and is not zero-rated; dividend distributions to non-resident investors are outside the scope of VAT.

The STA has implemented the fully digitalized electronic invoice system through the nationwide e-Tax platform. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the official tax digital invoice system; each invoice receives a unique digital invoice number and is transmitted to the STA tax-control system in near real time, with automatic verification and anti-fraud matching against VAT returns. Physical fiscal stamping is not used; instead the Golden Tax system performs real-time invoice comparison, red-letter invoice management, and buyer-side verification before input VAT credit is claimed.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs jointly audit all export zero-rating and refund claims. If documents are incomplete, inconsistent, or unverified, the zero rating is denied, output VAT is assessed on the supply, and any refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory interest. Under the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, taxpayers that underpay VAT through false export declaration, counterfeit documentation, or fraudulent refund claims are subject to a monetary penalty of not less than 50 percent and not more than five times the amount of tax evaded; serious cases involving forged customs documents, false export invoices, or criminal smuggling are transferred to public security authorities and can lead to criminal prosecution.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The onshore leg of the structure sits in mainland China, which operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime combined with strict current-account documentation and capital-account approval requirements. RMB is fully convertible for bona fide current-account transactions such as trade, services, and dividends once verified by a designated foreign-exchange bank, but it is not freely convertible on the capital account. All foreign direct investment into the Chinese joint venture must be registered with SAFE, and the registered capital must be injected through a capital-account foreign exchange registration certificate before the funds can be settled into CNY for property acquisition or operational use. Profit distributions to the Hong Kong REIT sponsor are treated as capital-account remittances and require a board resolution, audited Chinese GAAP financial statements, tax clearance certificates, a certified profit-distribution statement, and a completed SAFE foreign-exchange application. Property disposal proceeds, including capital gains, cannot be remitted as ordinary operating income; they must be routed through the capital account with supporting disposal contracts, valuation reports, tax payment receipts, and SAFE registration updates. The Hong Kong entity, by contrast, has no exchange controls: the HKMA operates the linked exchange-rate system, the Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible within the 7.75-7.85 USD band, and no surrender requirement applies to HKD or CNH receipts. Liquidity planning must therefore isolate China-side convertibility and remittance friction from the fully convertible Hong Kong treasury environment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the post-2012 SAFE current-account framework, there is no mandatory foreign-exchange surrender ratio for export receipts; an exporter may elect to retain foreign currency in a current-account FX settlement account or convert it into CNY at a designated bank. However, underlying trade receipts must be repatriated to a mainland China bank account within 90 days of the customs declaration date, with a possible extension to 180 days on approved capital-goods export contracts. The bank will monitor the enterprise's trade collection-to-payment ratio and require customs declarations, invoices, and bills of lading for any overdue or abnormal collection. For this REIT joint venture, the relevant receipts are service-trade rental income and property-management fees rather than goods exports; rental receivables must be collected directly into the onshore JV basic account, and any cross-border remittance of these service-trade receipts to Hong Kong requires a contract, tenant tax invoice, and the bank's SAFE monitoring record. No conversion quota applies to current-account amounts, but the bank may require transaction-by-transaction documentary proof of the underlying trade or service.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the onshore Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the Hong Kong SAR banking and currency board supervisor for the offshore REIT treasury layer

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs, including virtual banks and digital payment institutions licensed in Hong Kong, are not a compliant substitute for the onshore treasury spine of this structure. Mainland China foreign-exchange administration rules reserve the right to receive and settle RMB and foreign-exchange funds to SAFE-designated banking institutions. An offshore EMI cannot obtain a CNAPS clearing number, cannot participate in RCPMIS reporting, and will not be accepted by SAFE for capital verification or dividend-remittance filings; it is therefore legally barred from servicing the onshore JV as a primary account provider. For the Hong Kong layer, an HKMA-licensed digital bank could technically open a corporate account for the Hong Kong holding entity, but it would not provide the credit lines, commercial letter-of-credit issuance, SWIFT direct-banking infrastructure, or REIT custody services required for a real-estate treasury operation. The feasible fintech role is limited to ancillary payment execution for low-value current-account disbursements in non-RMB pairs, provided the funds are swept downstream from a primary Tier-1 bank account. The overall viability rating is Low for all regulated treasury and clearing functions and Conditional for secondary payment processing only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant inbound route is driven by SAFE FDI registration and bank-level FX processing. First, the Hong Kong REIT sponsor and the Chinese JV execute an equity subscription agreement, and the JV obtains a new or updated SAFE Foreign-Invested Enterprise Registration Certificate and an FDI foreign-exchange business registration record. The sponsor then remits the committed capital from its Hong Kong bank account via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 in USD, HKD, or CNH, with the wire narrative containing the JV SAFE registration number and the purpose code for FDI capital injection under the SAFE capital-account code set. The receiving Chinese bank credits the capital-account FX collection account and requires the JV to present the original registration certificate, the subscription agreement, and the board capital-call notice. Before the funds can be used for property acquisition or operating expenses, the JV must settle the FX into CNY under the capital-settlement procedure by submitting an RMB payment application, invoices or contracts for the intended use, and a compliance undertaking. The paid-in capital is verified by a licensed Chinese CPA, who issues a capital verification report for each tranche, and the JV reports the inflow to SAFE through the FDI quarterly monitoring return.Outbound cash from the onshore JV to the Hong Kong REIT layer is restricted to permitted categories: distributable profits, service fees, arm's-length interest on shareholder loans, and repayment of registered foreign debt. These payments are executed via SWIFT MT103 from the JV foreign-exchange settlement account to the Hong Kong beneficiary, with the SAFE payment purpose code matching the transaction and the relevant Chinese tax clearance attached. For imports of real-estate fit-out materials and technical equipment, the JV should use an irrevocable at-sight commercial letter of credit issued by its onshore Tier-1 bank, with the Hong Kong sponsor or its bank acting as advising and negotiating bank, and settlement processed by SWIFT MT700/MT760. Documentary collections are acceptable only for small-value or logistics-related trade documents where the bank presents the documents against payment or acceptance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 5,000,000 for controlled transactions involving intellectual property, and HKD 20,000,000 for controlled transactions involving goods, services, or financing. A Master File is expected where the consolidated group worldwide revenue exceeds HKD 680,000,000. These thresholds are applied per transaction or aggregated category; when thresholds are met, a Local File should be prepared in advance of filing the BIR51 and updated annually. The IRD has no statutory self-assessment TP return box, but contemporaneous documentation must be offered where transactions are above the stated thresholds. Transactions below the thresholds are still subject to the arm's-length principle and may be challenged by the IRD if they shift profits out of Hong Kong.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not impose a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) code comparable to BVI or Cayman, but the IRD and courts apply the 'central management and control' test to determine corporate residence and source of profits. For the real estate investment trust, the economic substance doctrine requires the Hong Kong JV to demonstrate, through director minutes, staff records, physical offices, and decision-making data, that the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are performed in Hong Kong for HK-source income or outside Hong Kong for an offshore claim. The CIGA for a REIT include identifying and negotiating real estate acquisitions, financing arrangements, tenant vetting and lease management, property maintenance, rent collection, and disposition strategy. A Hong Kong JV that merely appoints a nominee office, lacks local employees, and executes all commercial decisions from a PRC parent or offshore trustee will fail the economic substance analysis, and the IRD may re-characterise profits as fully Hong Kong-source on the basis that the income-generating activities are carried out at the actual management location. Permanent establishment exposure in Mainland China also arises if the Hong Kong JV habitually concludes contracts or carries on business in China through a dependent agent, which may cause double taxation and loss of treaty protection.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Incorrect Profits Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) sections 80(2), 82A, and 82B: penalties include a fine of HKD 10,000 and imprisonment of 3 years for knowingly filing an incorrect return; in addition, additional tax up to 300% of the tax undercharged may be imposed. The IRD also has power to order production of documents and to raise estimated assessments based on net worth methods.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil if directors use the corporation as a mere façade to conceal profits. The directors may be held jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax and penalties up to the amount of distributions diverted to them, their personal assets may be frozen, and the Financial Secretary may blacklist or impose a travel ban on individuals who leave Hong Kong with unpaid tax under the immigration exit control power found in the tax collection provisions. Criminal conviction may additionally lead to disqualification from acting as director for up to 5 years and imprisonment up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under sections 50A, 61, 61A, 62, and 63 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the IRD may adjust the assessable profits by disregarding sham transactions, reconstructing the transactions at arm's-length, and penalising the understatement. The monetary penalty can reach 300% of the additional tax assessed. Additionally, failure to produce transfer pricing documentation can lead to a specific penalty of up to HKD 50,000 and aggravated penalties if the IRD treats non-production as obstruction of the investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve artificial transfer pricing arrangements are personally exposed to recovery actions under the Companies (Winding-Up and Miscellaneous Provisions) Ordinance and common law breach of fiduciary duty. The IRD may issue a notice of responsibility against a director assessed under section 52 as agent of the non-resident corporation, making the director personally liable for tax unpaid by the company. In egregious cases involving fraudulent purpose, directors can be jailed for up to 3 years and subjected to global asset tracing, civil restitution orders, and blacklisting on the HK Companies Registry's disqualification record.

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), failure to maintain the register of significant controllers (section 653) is punishable by a fine of HKD 100,000 and a daily default fine of HKD 5,000.
