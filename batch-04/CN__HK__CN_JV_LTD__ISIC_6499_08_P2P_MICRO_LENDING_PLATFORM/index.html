Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The Companies Ordinance imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement for a private company limited by shares and no blocked escrow capital account is mandated; the shareholders may agree on a nominal capital. However, the Money Lenders Ordinance (Cap. 163) imposes a licensing regime under which the Licensing Court will scrutinise the applicant's financial soundness, business premises, and AML/CFT systems; a P2P micro-lending platform must maintain adequate working capital in a Hong Kong bank account, but this is a prudential or licensing condition rather than an incorporation escrow rule.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales for compulsory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold, a small-scale taxpayer status applies. All newly formed PRC JVs must complete tax registration within 30 days after SAMR business registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from mainland China are eligible for VAT zero-rating if they are physically exported and declared to Customs, the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the statutory VAT invoice is issued, export proceeds are received from foreign buyers in foreign currency and repatriated to China within the statutory FX deadline, and the goods are not within a prohibited or restricted no-zero-rating category. Qualifying cross-border services, including certain offshore financial-technology and platform services supplied to non-resident recipients and wholly consumed outside mainland China, may be zero-rated if listed under the STA's cross-border taxable services zero-rating policy and supported by export contracts, service-delivery records, foreign-currency invoicing, and repatriation evidence. Domestic P2P micro-lending platform services to PRC borrowers do not qualify for zero-rating; interest income from loans is statutorily exempt from VAT, while separately charged platform or technology fees are taxable at the applicable VAT rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) bearing the customs release/accepted date
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill or other customs-recognized transport document) proving export shipment
  • Commercial Invoice and export contract
  • Packing list and export goods manifest
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank export-collection and foreign-exchange settlement voucher evidencing receipt of export proceeds
  • Electronic export zero-rating or tax-refund declaration record generated by the STA e-Tax platform for the exported shipment

China operates a mandatory e-invoicing and fiscal-clearance regime under the STA Full-Digitalized Electronic Invoice service platform. VAT invoices must be issued through the unified tax-control platform using STA-issued invoice numbers and digital anti-counterfeit stamps. Business systems connect through the platform's API so that invoice data is transmitted, cleared, and verified in near-real time before the invoice is legally issued. Special VAT invoices must be reconciled against VAT returns. Hong Kong has no e-invoicing or fiscal stamping mandate because it does not operate a VAT/GST system.

Tax authorities carry out documentation-based and post-clearance audits of export zero-rating claims. The exporter must retain all title, customs, logistics, payment, and FX-repatriation evidence for the statutory retention period. If the zero-rating bundle cannot be substantiated or export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day FX deadline, the zero rate is denied, input VAT credits are clawed back, and the underlying output VAT is reassessed at the applicable PRC VAT rate. The PRC Tax Administration and Tax Collection Law and the export VAT-refund administrative regime authorize daily late-payment surcharges, administrative fines up to five times the tax involved, forfeiture of denied refunds, and cancellation of export-tax-refund status. Fraudulent zero-rating or false export declarations may be referred to criminal authorities under PRC Criminal Law for tax evasion or VAT-invoice fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible for current account items under the People's Republic of China's Article VIII status of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but the capital account remains subject to a negative list and strict registration and settlement controls. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (CN_JV_LTD), foreign currency equity injections from Hong Kong must be brought onshore through a designated FDI foreign exchange capital account and settled into RMB on a purpose-specific basis only after SAFE registration. The entity may retain proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account, but conversion to RMB is permitted only once the servicing bank verifies transaction genuineness against the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and board-approved expenditure plan. Profit remittance offshore requires a tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, a shareholder distribution resolution certified in accordance with the JV contract, and completion of the current account profit repatriation filing with the servicing bank. In the high-risk ISIC 6499 micro-lending fintech sector, the regulator also requires evidence of a valid financial license, borrower protection protocols, and loan collection compliance, which further constrains convertibility for early-stage proceeds before the relevant license is granted. Capital account transactions such as external debt, offshore loan extensions, and cross-border guarantees must be registered separately with SAFE and cannot be bundled with the basic operating account flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE legal framework, export goods or service receipts must be repatriated into China through a licensed bank and credited to the exporter's foreign exchange current account within 180 days of the shipment invoice date. There is no blanket mandatory percentage conversion to RMB for all export receipts; the domestic entity may decide how much to settle based on actual RMB obligations, but SAFE authenticity review requires supporting invoices, customs records, and collection statements. For foreign direct investment capital receipts, however, settlement into RMB is subject to 100% use-purpose verification through the SAFE capital account system, and any unconverted foreign currency must remain within an approved foreign exchange account.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legally non-viable as the primary onshore treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI providers operating from Hong Kong, Singapore, or the UK cannot open onshore RMB capital settlement accounts, cannot access CNAPS or CIPS directly on behalf of a mainland-incorporated JV, and cannot perform SAFE capital account registration, tax clearance, or capital verification functions. At best, an EMI can serve as a pre-funding liquidity aggregator in CNY or USD before wire transfer into the JV's licensed bank account, but every transaction must still be executed through a Tier-1 PRC deposit-taking bank. Consequently, the onboarding viability is rated Low and no offshore EMI should be contracted as the sole payment settlement provider.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company is required to prepare financial statements giving a true and fair view and must appoint independent certified public accountants registered as company auditors unless an express exemption applies. A private company is exempt from statutory audit only if it qualifies as a small private company or a dormant company. A small private company is one that satisfies at least two of the following three tests for the financial year: annual revenue not exceeding HK$10,000,000; total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000; and not more than 50 employees. The exemption is unavailable if the company is a member of a group unless all group members are small private companies, or if another enactment requires an audit. For a P2P micro-lending and licensed money-lending operation, audit exemption will rarely be appropriate or prudent because the Money Lenders Ordinance, financing agreements, investors, and high-risk licensing conditions commonly impose an independent audit regardless of nominal size.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends to non-resident enterprises; the rate is reduced to 5% under the Mainland China-Hong Kong DTA only where the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company that directly holds at least 25% of the PRC JV's capital throughout the taxable year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File and Country-by-Country reporting are triggered separately under SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 for large multinational groups, commonly aligned with cross-border group revenue thresholds of approximately RMB 5.5 billion under the OECD BEPS project.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form, general anti-avoidance rules, and the OECD-derived beneficial ownership principle. For treaty relief and transfer pricing, the Hong Kong shareholder must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities take place in Hong Kong, including real offices, resident staff, board decisions, and active investment management. A Hong Kong entity that merely holds the Chinese JV shares without employees, contracts, or independent decision-making will be treated as a conduit, the treaty advantage will be denied, and the income may be reattributed to the ultimate mainland Chinese controllers.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Unlicensed P2P Micro-Lending and Unlawful Deposit Taking

Statutory sanction: Violation of the PRC Interim Measures on the Business of Online Lending Information Intermediary Institutions and PRC Criminal Law Articles 176 and 192; regulatory authorities may revoke the platform's license, wind up the platform, confiscate unlawful gains, impose administrative fines, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution; criminal penalties include fines and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers may be held personally criminally liable for illegal absorption of public deposits and fund-raising fraud; the court may freeze or confiscate personal assets to compensate borrowers, impose travel bans through judicial blacklisting and exit controls, and sentence responsible directors to terms of imprisonment ranging from three years to life imprisonment for aggravated fraud.
Corporate Income Tax Evasion and False Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, tax evasion is punishable by 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late payment interest at 0.05% per day; criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law applies where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, carrying fines and imprisonment of up to seven years.

Director personal liability: Directors, chief financial officers, and controlling shareholders who sign false returns can be held personally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest where the corporate veil is pierced; personal bank accounts and real estate can be seized, the individual may be placed on a tax exit blacklist preventing departure from China, and imprisonment of up to seven years plus criminal fines may be imposed.
Treaty Abuse, Hong Kong Conduit Structure, and Sham Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 47, and 48 authorize special tax adjustments to disregard the conduit and recharacterize income; treaty relief will be denied, WHT will be restored to 10% or the full 25% statutory rate in extreme cases, and the taxpayer will be assessed additional tax, interest at the daily 0.05% late payment rate, and penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed the abusive structure may be jointly and severally liable for the additional corporate tax and penalties if assets were diverted or the entity was operated as an instrumentality of the controlling persons; courts may apply piercing of the corporate veil to restore distributions, issue asset freezes and travel bans, blacklist the directors from leaving China, and impose criminal penalties for evasion where the denied treaty benefit reveals fraudulent documentation, with imprisonment possible under tax fraud provisions.
