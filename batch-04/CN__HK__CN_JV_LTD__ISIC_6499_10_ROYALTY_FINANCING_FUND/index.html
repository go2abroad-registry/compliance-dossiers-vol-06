Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the Provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform operated by provincial STA offices, including the STA national electronic taxpayer service portal. Hong Kong SAR is a separate tax jurisdiction administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), with its own eTax/e-Registration portal and no VAT/GST/Sales Tax.

E-government filing portal

For CN_JV_LTD, after the State Administration for Market Regulation registers the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture and issues the Business License, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically created and is legally accepted by the STA as the entity's Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Under the national multiple-certificate integration reform, tax registration is completed at the same time as company registration. The legal representative or authorized agent must then activate the taxpayer account on the Provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform within 30 days, complete supplementary registry information, select the competent tax authority, and configure bank account and electronic invoicing services. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is a PRC tax resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxed on worldwide income as a single limited-liability entity. Pass-through treatment is not available for PRC corporate income tax purposes; dividends paid to non-resident shareholders are subject to separate PRC withholding tax at the shareholder level.

Available election forms

No PRC equivalent of a US check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch check-the-box election exists. The PRC corporate income tax system does not permit elective pass-through classification. The only relevant elections are: (a) the small-scale VAT taxpayer election for taxpayers with annual aggregate taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000, allowing simplified collection rates until they are required to register as general VAT taxpayers; and (b) the elective zero-rating or exemption treatment for cross-border services listed in the STA cross-border taxable service catalogue where the statutory export documentation is present. Hybrid mismatch elections are not recognized in mainland China.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every VAT taxpayer must complete tax registration upon issuance of the Business License. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual aggregate VAT taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million); below that threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to the simplified collection rate, but may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status. The threshold is measured on PRC VAT taxable supplies only and does not include offshore Hong Kong sales outside PRC VAT nexus.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT applies only where goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory under customs supervision and the exporter retains the full statutory export evidence set. Services: 0% VAT is available for cross-border taxable services listed in the STA Catalogue of Zero-Rated Cross-Border Taxable Services, provided the recipient is an overseas enterprise, the service is consumed wholly outside the PRC, and contemporaneous documentation proves the offshore economic use and foreign-exchange settlement. For this non-dilutive revenue-based financing fund, the actual supply must be classified before claiming zero-rating: financing interest, guarantee fees, and royalty remuneration may be subject to the 6% VAT financial services rate or a statutory cross-border financial service exemption, rather than 0% VAT. Zero-rating is defensible only if the specific service stream qualifies under an express zero-rating catalogue item, such as certain offshore consulting, software licensing, digital data processing, or royalty-type services wholly consumed by a non-resident B2B customer. The JV should obtain an advance STA tax ruling before applying 0% VAT to the royalty and financing fee streams.

China now mandates the Digital Electronic Invoice (DEI) regime, including the Full Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI). VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform via the Provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal. Each invoice is generated from the STA central fiscal ledger, digitally signed, assigned a unique authentic invoice number, and synchronized to the tax authority in near real time. Qualified taxpayers are required to integrate their billing systems through approved APIs for machine-to-machine invoice issuance and data exchange. Anti-fraud controls are embedded in the digital invoice architecture, the taxpayer enterprise digital account, and continuous reconciliation of invoice data against VAT returns, banking settlement records, and customs declarations. Traditional pre-printed paper invoices and physical fiscal stamping are no longer the primary compliance mechanism for FDEI-enabled taxpayers.

The STA, Chinese Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct integrated risk-based audits of export VAT refunds and cross-border zero-rating claims. If the documentary bundle is incomplete, stale, inconsistent, or cannot substantiate the offshore consumption and foreign-exchange repatriation, the zero-rating is denied and output VAT is reassessed at the applicable statutory rate (6% for financial services or 13% for goods), with daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% from the original filing deadline and evasion penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall. Confirmed export fraud, phantom exports, circular documentary arrangements, or inflated invoices will trigger full clawback of refund amounts with interest, suspension or revocation of the exporter's VAT refund qualification, and potential criminal liability for the entity and responsible officers under the PRC Criminal Law and Customs Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a regulated and non-fully convertible currency regime. The RMB is liberalised for current-account transactions, so goods, services, wages, and royalties may be settled and paid once the bank verifies underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and tax filings. Capital-account flows, however, remain tightly controlled. An inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong SAR financing fund into the mainland JV requires SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration, opening of a dedicated capital account at a permitted bank, and quarantine of funds until the capital verification report is issued by a Chinese CPA. The capital account funds may only be settled into RMB for permitted operating uses on the SAFE positive list; proceeds cannot be used for entrusted loans, speculative securities, or real-estate investments. Dividend/profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder faces prudence hurdles: the JV must provide audited financials, corporate income tax returns, a board resolution declaring the dividend, proof of withholding tax settlement, and a SAFE profit-remittance filing. The bank applies a real-need test before releasing funds, and the Hong Kong resident shareholder may rely on the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement for a 5% dividend withholding rate if it holds at least 25% of the JV and satisfies beneficial-ownership substance rules. The Hong Kong SAR leg itself imposes no FX controls, and HKD and CNY convertibility is free, making HK a suitable offshore treasury and liquidity bridge for the mainland operating entity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For export receipts generated by the mainland JV, SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring requires that exported goods' foreign-currency proceeds be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days of the export customs declaration date, or within the recorded contractual maturity where an approved deferred-collection arrangement exists. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current regime: the mandatory surrender ratio is 0%, but the repatriation and reporting obligation is 100%. Exporters may retain foreign exchange in their settlement accounts or voluntarily convert into CNY at market rates through their designated bank. All receipts are matched electronically by the bank against the corresponding Customs export declarations in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System; late receipt, split payments, and mismatched purpose codes trigger BOP exception reports and may freeze further settlement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and monetary authority for the Hong Kong SAR financing fund.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the mainland China JV. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks are not registered or licensed by SAFE/PBOC to open CNY or foreign-currency settlement accounts for domestic enterprises, and all cross-border payments must pass through a supervised commercial bank using SWIFT/CIPS rails. For the Hong Kong SAR financing fund, an HKMA-licensed or authorised EMI may provide basic payment services, but it cannot clear documentary trade, obtain SAFE filings, or act as the primary treasury bank for the JV's inbound capital and outbound trade flows. The operational and regulatory cost of using an EMI for this medium-risk revenue-based financing structure is disproportionate to any benefit. Recommended rating: Low; the treasury should be anchored at Tier-1 commercial banks with direct CHATS/CIPS access.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Hong Kong financing fund to the mainland JV must be wired via SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for bank-to-bank cover) from a licensed HK bank to the JV's dedicated FDI capital account at a mainland Tier-1 bank. The remittance must quote the SAFE-approved foreign-exchange purpose code for inbound direct investment/registered capital in the remittance details, and the beneficiary field must reference the JV's FDI Foreign Exchange Registration number. The receiving bank will validate the amount against the permitted registered capital and available capital account quota in the SAFE system. After validation, funds are booked to the capital account and may only be settled into CNY for operations on the permitted positive list; settlement is done via the bank's voluntary foreign-exchange settlement facility, not by the client's direct instruction. A Chinese CPA must issue the capital verification report, and the JV must update SAFE registration and the market-administration registry to reflect paid-in capital. Any shareholder loan tranche must be separately registered as foreign debt with SAFE, limited to the approved maturity and use-of-funds scope.For outbound trade disbursements, the JV should use SWIFT MT103 payments accompanied by commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, Customs declaration records, tax-payment vouchers, and the appropriate SAFE current-account purpose code for goods or services. For larger capital purchases or bulk raw-material orders, an irrevocable at-sight commercial letter of credit (L/C) issued by the mainland bank via SWIFT MT700 and advised through a HK or supplier-country bank reduces counterparty risk and provides stronger FX documentation. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are acceptable for recurring, lower-value suppliers with an established payment history. For the Hong Kong fund's outbound treasury movements, use CHATS for HKD and CNY (RTGS) and CIPS for direct cross-border CNY settlement, with SWIFT MT103/202 for USD and other major currencies. Every outward payment from the HK fund must be supported by the executed drawdown notice, financing agreement, and the portfolio company's payment instructions.Deliverable CNY foreign-exchange forward contracts and outright forwards with a domestic licensed bank, based on real underlying current-account or eligible capital-account exposure, with policy limits set by the JV board treasury committee.FX swaps and cross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) entered into by the mainland JV to hedge foreign-currency debt or shareholder loan exposure, subject to SAFE real-demand validation and prudent counterparty limits.RMB structured options (including zero-premium collars and participating forwards) purchased from an onshore licensed bank for current-account and approved capital-account exposures, without speculative option writing.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) and CNH deliverable forwards are permissible only for the Hong Kong SAR financing fund to hedge its CNH portfolio exposure; the mainland JV must use onshore deliverable CNY instruments rather than NDFs.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for the mainland China JV. Offshore fintech EMIs and digital banks are not registered or licensed by SAFE/PBOC to open CNY or foreign-currency settlement accounts for domestic enterprises, and all cross-border payments must pass through a supervised commercial bank using SWIFT/CIPS rails. For the Hong Kong SAR financing fund, an HKMA-licensed or authorised EMI may provide basic payment services, but it cannot clear documentary trade, obtain SAFE filings, or act as the primary treasury bank for the JV's inbound capital and outbound trade flows. The operational and regulatory cost of using an EMI for this medium-risk revenue-based financing structure is disproportionate to any benefit. Recommended rating: Low; the treasury should be anchored at Tier-1 commercial banks with direct CHATS/CIPS access.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on the worldwide income of a PRC resident enterprise; outbound dividends, interest, and royalties to non-residents are also subject to 10% withholding tax under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law unless a treaty rate applies.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder; under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement, the dividend withholding rate is reduced to 5% where the Hong Kong beneficial owner is a company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the PRC JV, and remains 10% in all other cases.
