Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Under the Companies Ordinance, a registered office must be a physical address in Hong Kong, not a P.O. Box. A virtual office that provides a physical address and mail screening may satisfy the statutory requirement for a simple private company, but the HKMA for an EMI/SVF license will likely reject such arrangements because the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) requires the entity to maintain a genuine place of business in Hong Kong with staff, physical records, and board meetings accessible for inspection. A virtual office or commercial mailbox is therefore not permissible for this licensed EMI vehicle. A private serviced office with a dedicated meeting room, on-site physical file storage, and a permanent Hong Kong-based AML compliance officer is acceptable, provided the address is not exclusively a mailing address and the company can demonstrate local management and business substance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: eTAX (https://www.etax.gov.hk); business registration services are also accessible through the GovHK portal and the IRD Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Hong Kong presence, the statutory procedure is: (1) incorporate a company under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) or register a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the same Ordinance; (2) within one month of commencing business, apply with the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) for a Business Registration Certificate, furnishing the prescribed application form together with certified copies of the certificate of incorporation, constitutional documents, registered office or principal place of business particulars, details of directors and shareholders, and a Hong Kong address for service; (3) the IRD issues the Business Registration Certificate and allocates the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the primary tax identity for the entity; (4) the IRD then opens a Profits Tax file and issues the statutory Profits Tax Return under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); (5) a non-resident entity that has no physical establishment in Hong Kong but derives Hong Kong-sourced profits must notify the IRD and file the required Profits Tax Return when required; the same BRN/inland revenue file number is used for all tax correspondence; (6) records must be retained for at least 7 years. No separate mainland PRC tax ID, VAT ID, or local-region tax code is applicable for a Hong Kong SAR tax person.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong does not operate a check-the-box or pass-through election system for corporate entities. All Hong Kong-incorporated companies are entity-level taxpayers under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). No hybrid mismatch elections are available. The territorial-source principle applies by statute, not by election, and the two-tiered profits tax rates are applied automatically where statutory conditions are met.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong SAR; selective excise duties are imposed on a narrow class of dutiable commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT/GST/sales tax registration threshold exists in Hong Kong. Business registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) is mandatory for all persons carrying on business, without a turnover threshold. Dutiable commodity importers/manufacturers require separate licensing or permits under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax, so there is no 0% export VAT rate to claim, no input VAT refund process, and no export VAT zero-rating conditionality. For profits tax purposes, the relevant cross-border concept is territorial sourcing: profits from exported goods or services are outside the charge to profits tax only if the source of profits is determined by the IRD to be outside Hong Kong. Documentary evidence must demonstrate that the negotiation, execution and performance of the sale or service contract occurred offshore, or that the economic activities giving rise to the profits were undertaken outside Hong Kong. This is an offshore profits claim, not an indirect tax zero-rating mechanism.

The eTAX portal is used for tax returns and registration documents, not for invoice clearance or fiscal stamping. Taxpayers must retain proper business records, invoices and vouchers for at least 7 years under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

No indirect tax clawback can arise because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax and no export zero-rating refund regime. However, any offshore profits claim is scrutinised by the IRD under statutory territorial-source principles and general anti-avoidance provisions of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). Incorrect returns, false declarations, and fraudulent evasion can result in additional tax up to three times the tax undercharged, monetary fines, and imprisonment. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a free convertibility regime for the Hong Kong dollar; there are no exchange control restrictions on the repatriation of capital, profits, or dividends for corporations incorporated in Hong Kong. The RMB, however, is not fully convertible on the capital account. China maintains a managed float with reference to a basket of currencies and imposes strict controls on cross-border capital flows. Current account transactions, including trade settlement and service payments, are generally convertible after documentary verification under SAFE rules. Capital account transactions, such as inbound FDI equity injections from the Hong Kong joint venture into mainland operating subsidiaries or the reverse remittance of capital from China to Hong Kong, require case-by-case approval, registration with SAFE, and use of designated bank channels. For a Sino-foreign joint venture operating in Hong Kong, capital flows between the Hong Kong parent and mainland Chinese operating entities must observe SAFE's capital project registration, foreign currency equity conversion, and repatriation procedures. Profit remittance from a mainland subsidiary to the Hong Kong parent requires audited financial statements, an income tax clearance certificate, and proof that the profits were derived from registered business activities. Dividend remittances are permitted after paying withholding tax at 10% under the double tax arrangement, but the foreign exchange conversion and transfer must be executed via a domestic bank with SAFE-verified purpose code for profit remittance to foreign investor. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory for Chinese exporters, but proceeds must be repatriated into mainland bank accounts within statutory timelines; conversion may be retained in foreign currency or settled at market rate depending on corporate cash management. There is no surrender requirement for the Hong Kong entity's foreign currency receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China export entities, under SAFE Provisions on Foreign Exchange Administration of Trade and Circular 10/2020, all export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a domestic account within 30 days of the payment due date. Mandatory conversion is abolished; the exporter may retain the proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account, but any conversion to RMB follows the onshore market rate. For entities in Hong Kong, no surrender requirement exists; HK companies may freely retain export receipts in any internationally accepted currency.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and banking regulator for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Digital fintech EMI providers such as Airwallex, Currenxie, and Neat are increasingly used in Hong Kong for multi-currency borderless accounts, and they are authorized by the HKMA as money service operators or licensed stored value facility entities. However, for the target entity conducting licensed EMI activities with embedded digital wallet and virtual IBAN issuance, these fintech platforms cannot act as the primary clearing bank because they do not hold a full banking license and cannot provide segregated client account coverage under the Depositors' Protection Scheme. Their risk appetite for onboarding high-risk special license fintech companies is narrow; most will reject a B2B EMI holding third-party funds due to layered KYC and flow-through risk. The practical route is to leverage a full-license domestic bank for regulated clearing rails, with fintechs used only as a supplementary white-label payment gateway where the underlying funds are always held by the licensed bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from China into the HK JV must be structured as a direct equity injection under China's Foreign Exchange Control Regulations, with the mainland shareholder completing SAFE FDI registration for the transfer of funds to a special foreign exchange account at a designated domestic bank. Remittance should use SWIFT MT103 with beneficiary as the HK JV's capital escrow account at a licensed HK bank and purpose code for outward remittance for direct investment as reported by the mainland remitter. The HK bank will open a segregated capital escrow account to receive the injection and perform capital verification; the funds are frozen until a certified public accountant issues a capital contribution verification report and the HK Companies Registry records the allotment. Where the investment is made in RMB, the mainland bank will apply China's CNAPS and cross-border RMB clearing mechanism and use the relevant RMB purpose code, but the HK receiving bank must have access to the CNH RMB clearing window at the Hong Kong Clearing House.Outbound settlements for merchant payouts, digital wallet funding, and trade-related disbursements should be executed via HKMA's Clearing and Settlement Systems for HKD, RTGS via TARGET2 for EUR, and SWIFT MT103 for multi-currency transfers. For trade-related payments, use commercial letters of credit opened by the HK entity through its main relationship bank to fund suppliers in China, or documentary collections such as D/P and D/A where the payment terms are structured under standard ICC UCP600 and URC522 rules. Their risk appetite for onboarding high-risk special license fintech companies is narrow; most will reject a B2B EMI holding third-party funds due to layered KYC and flow-through risk. The practical route is to leverage a full-license domestic bank for regulated clearing rails, with fintechs used only as a supplementary white-label payment gateway where the underlying funds are always held by the licensed bank.
