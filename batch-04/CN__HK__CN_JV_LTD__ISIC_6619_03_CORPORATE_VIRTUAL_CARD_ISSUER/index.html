Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China: exports of goods are zero-rated under the export VAT refund (export tax rebate) mechanism, provided the goods have physically departed China under customs supervision. Cross-border services, including qualifying offshore financial services supplied wholly to overseas entities and consumed entirely outside the PRC, may be zero-rated or exempt under the STA cross-border services catalogue. Hong Kong: no VAT/GST regime, hence no indirect-tax zero-rating; profits from exports are taxable only if sourced in Hong Kong.

  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bank Receipt / Foreign Exchange Settlement Notice) or equivalent proof of cross-border foreign exchange repatriation
  • Export VAT rebate declaration form and special VAT invoice or fully digital e-invoice

China: mandatory fully digitalized electronic invoices ('fully digital e-invoices') issued through the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform, integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Real-time fiscal clearance via the national invoice service platform, using unique invoice codes, QR anti-counterfeit features, and official e-invoice APIs. Hong Kong: no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate; invoices and receipts may be retained in electronic or paper form for Profits Tax purposes.

China: fraudulent or unverified export VAT zero-rating claims trigger clawback of the refunded VAT, late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount involved under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, potential criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebates under PRC Criminal Law Article 204, and tax authority blacklisting with joint cross-departmental punishment. Hong Kong: no VAT clawback applies, but false offshore profit claims or inadequate record keeping under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) may result in fines up to HK$50,000 and additional tax up to three times the amount under-assessed.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar (HKD) is fully convertible without any central bank rationing, and the HKD is linked to the US dollar under the linked exchange rate system operated by the HKMA. There are no surrender requirements, no mandatory conversion ratios, and profit repatriation from a Hong Kong company is unrestricted, subject only to corporate law solvency tests and the company's tax and filing positions. Conversely, the Mainland China dimension introduces dual domestic and cross-border constraints: the RMB is convertible for current account transactions such as trade and services, but capital account transactions, including direct investment, foreign debt, and certain profit remittances, require SAFE registration and approval. The Chinese-foreign equity joint venture shall therefore maintain a clear treasury boundary between its Hong Kong operational account and its mainland capital account. Dividend remittances from mainland operational entities to the Hong Kong holding entity must be supported by audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and SAFE foreign direct investment registration and profit remittance forms. Overall, the treasury must be structured so that liquidity flows respect Hong Kong's laissez-faire FX regime while remaining fully compliant with China's foreign exchange administrative rules.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong SAR imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender or mandatory conversion requirements; exporters may retain foreign currency indefinitely. However, if the mainland operating subsidiaries of the JV receive export proceeds under Chinese rules, SAFE requires the exporter to repatriate such proceeds into mainland China within 90 days of the export customs declaration date, and the exporter may either retain the currencies in a foreign exchange account or convert them into RMB at a designated bank, with authenticity verification. No mandatory conversion ratio applies after the 2012 deregulation.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the de facto central bank and banking regulator for the Hong Kong SAR; for Mainland China renminbi flows the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China retains extraterritorial relevance.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-to-conditional: Hong Kong does not define an 'EMI' licence class; a fintech issuing corporate virtual cards must hold a Money Service Operator (MSO) licence, a Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence under the HKMA, or partner with a licensed bank as a program manager. Offshore payment EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currencycloud) are not legally barred from servicing Hong Kong corporates, but their ability to issue Mastercard/Visa B2B cards depends on sponsorship by an authorised institution and full compliance with HKMA AML/CFT requirements. For access to Mainland China, however, no unlicensed offshore EMI may provide card issuing or payment services to mainland entities; the only route is a joint venture with a licensed mainland bank or a licensed non-bank payment institution approved by the People's Bank of China. Therefore, the blueprint recommends a bank-sponsored card program at a Tier-1 Hong Kong bank rather than reliance on a standalone EMI.

Shareholder injection should be denominated in USD or HKD via SWIFT MT103 from the foreign investor's home bank, with the reference field containing the contract number and the registered capital schedule. The Hong Kong receiving bank will acknowledge the remittance into the Capital Escrow Account, and a Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant must issue a capital verification certificate (deemed contribution). For Mainland China parent injections, the outward remittance is governed by SAFE's ODI/FDI regime: the mainland remitter must obtain SAFE registration and an Outbound Remittance Approval, and the HK recipient must be able to produce the corresponding FDI-related equity subscription agreement. The SAFE purpose code should be 621011 for outward direct investment in equity. Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The Profits Tax Return is issued by the Inland Revenue Department and must be filed within one month of the issue date; eTAX e-filing generally attracts an extension to two months from the issue date. For accounting periods ending on 31 December, IRD administrative practice may grant extended due dates. If no return is filed by the statutory deadline, the IRD may raise an estimated assessment under IRO s.59C.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HK$5,000,000 (aggregate connected transactions requiring a Local File; CbC reporting threshold for an MNE group is consolidated group revenue of at least HK$6,800,000,000)

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not apply BVI-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD applies the general anti-avoidance rule under IRO s.61A and the territorial source principle to require genuine economic substance. For a corporate virtual card issuer, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), including merchant onboarding, compliance and AML decisions, card scheme communications, treasury policy, contract execution, and board approval of card programs, must be performed in Hong Kong. If substantive management is performed elsewhere, especially by the mainland Chinese parent, the IRD may recharacterize offshore claims and impute all profits to Hong Kong.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Declarations

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) s.82(1) imposes a maximum fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus additional tax of up to three times the tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue estreated assessments and default interest under IRO s.59C and s.60.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false tax returns may be personally liable for the additional tax and treble penalty. Courts may pierce the corporate veil on grounds of fraud, order civil restitution of misappropriated corporate assets, issue asset freezing and travel restrictions preventing directors from leaving Hong Kong, and impose criminal imprisonment for up to 3 years.
Transfer Pricing Failure and Sham Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.50A, s.50B, s.50C and DIPN 58, the IRD may adjust taxable profits, disallow artificial deductions, impose default interest under s.60, and levy penalties of up to three times the tax undercharged in cases of fraud or gross negligence. Negligent transfer pricing can also trigger ratings by the IRD for avoidance and subject the structure to full audit.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate or approve sham transfer pricing may be found to have committed fraudulent trading. Personal civil liability includes asset restitution, contribution to corporate tax penalties, disqualification as a director under the Companies Ordinance, travel bans pending examination, and criminal exposure for false documentation leading to imprisonment for up to 3 years.
AML, Licensing, and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: Offences under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) Cap. 615 and the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance (PSSVFO) Cap. 584 may result in fines up to HK$1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 12 months per offence; where the activity facilitates money laundering, the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (OSCO) Cap. 455 s.25 authorizes fines up to HK$5,000,000 and imprisonment up to 14 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may be personally liable for civil restitution of laundered or evaded proceeds, disgorgement of unlawful profits, disqualification from serving as directors, blacklisting by financial regulators, travel bans, and criminal asset seizure. Courts may lift the corporate veil because the Hong Kong entity is a sham or a quasi-shelter of the mainland parent, exposing directors to imprisonment of up to 14 years for money laundering and related tax fraud.
