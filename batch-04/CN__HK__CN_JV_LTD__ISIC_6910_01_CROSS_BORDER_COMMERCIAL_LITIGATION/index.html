Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the JV is a separate juridical person and a separate taxpayer in Hong Kong; it is not a pass-through entity and no Hong Kong check-the-box transparency election exists. Hong Kong applies the territorial-source principle, so the entity is taxed at entity level on profits sourced in Hong Kong only, subject to the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Available election forms

None. There is no check-the-box, no consolidated profits-tax filing, no hybrid-mismatch election, and no statutory entity-reclassification regime under Hong Kong law. A JV may apply to IRD for a Certificate of Resident Status under the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, but that is an administrative treaty-benefit certificate and not an entity-classification election.

No VAT, GST, or Sales and Use Tax in Hong Kong. The Hong Kong indirect-tax framework comprises Stamp Duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117), Dutiable Commodities under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109), rates under the Rating Ordinance (Cap. 116), and trade declarations/import-export permits under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60).

N/A - Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold. Not applicable. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime under which current account transactions such as trade, services, and routine remittances are broadly freely convertible once declared, documented, and verified under SAFE rules, while capital account transactions such as cross-border equity investment, offshore loans, securities, and trust financing remain subject to SAFE registration, approval, or quota restrictions. Onshore RMB (CNY) is not fully convertible, whereas offshore RMB (CNH) in Hong Kong is freely convertible and HKD itself is fully convertible without exchange controls. For a Sino-Foreign equity joint venture organised in Hong Kong but with a PRC corporate parent, the main conversion friction is the PRC outbound investment and settlement architecture: capital contributions must be made through SAFE-registered ODI channels, and profits or dividends flowing from the mainland will require audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and foreign exchange payment verification. In Hong Kong, there is no surrender requirement, no central bank approval for ordinary treasury transactions, and no restriction on holding multicurrency balances, making HKD the natural settlement currency while CNY remains a carefully controlled funding currency.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For mainland China, export proceeds arising from the PRC component of the JV must be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign currency account within 180 days of the customs export declaration date or within the period stated in the SAFE trade verification system. Once repatriated and verified, the enterprise may retain the foreign currency up to its current account foreign exchange account quota; there is no mandatory conversion percentage for verified current account receipts, meaning the forced surrender ratio is effectively 0% for eligible export proceeds. Any balance exceeding the quota must be settled into CNY through a SAFE-designated bank or the quota must be adjusted. For Hong Kong, no surrender rules apply; exporters and service providers may freely retain, convert, or remit HKD and any foreign currency without mandatory conversion timelines.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

The feasibility rating for fintech EMI/cross-border digital banking is LOW for this entity's primary treasury and clearing infrastructure. Hong Kong law allows e-money issuance only through a licensed stored value facility (SVF) operator under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance; an SVF can provide ancillary payment and FX wallet services but is not a deposit-taking institution and cannot legally provide IOLTA/escrow safeguards, documentary letters of credit, capital verification reports, or professional trust segregation required for a cross-border commercial litigation chambers. In mainland China, offshore fintech EMIs are not recognised as licenced banks and generally cannot service the PRC JV's capital account or trust deposits without a PRC payment institution license from the PBOC. Accordingly, this treasury blueprint mandates Tier-1 HK commercial banks for all settlement, escrow, and capital account flows; an HKMA-licensed SVF may be used only for immaterial low-value administrative disbursements after documentation review by the MLRO.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity injection from the PRC parent to the HK JV must first complete the PRC outward direct investment (ODI) lifecycle: NDRC filing/approval, MOFCOM investment certificate, and SAFE foreign exchange registration. Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, the tax authority must collect the underpaid CIT, impose daily late interest at 0.05%, and levy an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; under Criminal Law Article 201, intentional evasion exceeding the criminal threshold carries a fine and imprisonment of up to seven years.
