Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

For Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the GovHK and eTAX electronic portals; for any mainland China taxable presence: State Taxation Administration (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau portal.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong SAR: a foreign non-resident entity incorporating a Hong Kong company first receives a Certificate of Incorporation from the Hong Kong Companies Registry, then files a Business Registration application with the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department within one month of incorporation; the IRD will issue a Business Registration Certificate and later assign a tax file number for Profits Tax purposes after filing obligations commence. Available election forms

None in Hong Kong SAR because the territorial source Profits Tax system does not offer a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election; the People's Republic of China likewise does not provide a US-style check-the-box election, but an eligible small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect the simplified 3% collection rate for qualifying turnover instead of the general 6% VAT method.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

Hong Kong SAR imposes no VAT, GST, or sales tax; a PRC VAT nexus arises only if the Hong Kong entity has a mainland China permanent establishment or separately supplies services within mainland China, where the relevant indirect tax is the People's Republic of China Value Added Tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Hong Kong SAR: there is no indirect tax and therefore no VAT registration threshold. Mainland China: a service provider with annual taxable sales of RMB 5,000,000 or more must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below RMB 5,000,000, small-scale taxpayer status using a 3% collection rate may apply, subject to applicable threshold reliefs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong SAR has no VAT/GST, so no zero-rating claim is applicable; however, profits from services performed outside Hong Kong and received outside Hong Kong are not subject to Hong Kong Profits Tax under the territorial source principle. For any mainland China export supply, the PRC VAT regime may apply a 0% rate to qualifying cross-border services provided that the recipient is an overseas entity, the service is consumed wholly outside mainland China, foreign exchange payment is received, and the required export documentation is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) or PRC Export Goods Declaration Form
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt voucher

Hong Kong SAR: no electronic invoicing clearance, API integration, or anti-fraud digital stamping is imposed. Mainland China: where a taxable supply is made, the STA requires special VAT invoices to be generated through the official VAT invoice management system, with real-time online transmission to the tax authority and the use of anti-counterfeit digital watermarks and machine-readable verification codes; there is no independent private-sector clearance stamp.

Tax authorities may retrospectively disallow a zero-rate treatment or export refund, reclassify the supply as taxable at the standard or reduced VAT rate, and assess the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment surcharges from the original due date. If fraudulent customs or foreign-exchange documents are used to claim export zero-rating, the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China provides for recovery of the full tax benefit, monetary penalties from 50 percent up to five times the underpaid tax, and criminal liability for tax fraud. In Hong Kong, any false or incorrect statement leading to underassessment of Profits Tax may result in additional tax assessments, fines, and prosecution under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the current account, the RMB is convertible for genuine trade and service transactions: export receipts may be settled or retained in foreign currency, and payment for services may be purchased from a designated bank against a validated contract, invoice, tax certificate, and SAFE reporting. The capital account, however, is strictly controlled: cross-border equity contributions, loans, and outward remittances require SAFE registration or approval, and the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture must file FDI information with the local SAFE-sanctioned bank before any capital account use. Registered capital cannot be left idle in a capital account; once converted to RMB it must be deployed only for approved operating purposes. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted only after the annual statutory audit by a Chinese CPA, full tax settlement including enterprise income tax and any dividend withholding tax, board approval, and verification of the Hong Kong beneficial owner's residence under the China-Hong Kong double tax arrangement. Additional hurdles include thin-capitalisation limits on onshore borrowing, transfer-pricing documentation for cross-border service fees, and the requirement that every outward remittance carry a direct underlying genuineness evidence file. The result is de facto full convertibility on current account for true transactions, but de jure blocking of speculative or unsupported capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current account administration, the mandatory export surrender system has been abolished; there is a 0% mandatory conversion ratio and exporters may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency current account or settle into RMB at a designated bank. Proceeds must be repatriated to China by the later of 180 days after customs declaration for goods or the contractual settlement date for services, and the receipt must be reported through the receiving bank into SAFE's Trade Monitoring System within 5 working days. Any overdue receipt must be justified with cargo, contract, or service-performance evidence or through a formal SAFE record-adjustment application.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the onshore regulators for RMB and capital account flows, with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the offshore HKD clearing authority for the Hong Kong treasury leg.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated non-dormant company must appoint an independent certified public accountant (practising) to audit its annual financial statements unless it validly qualifies for a narrow exemption. A private company may be exempt only if it qualifies as a small private company under the Companies Ordinance by meeting any two of the following three thresholds: annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000; total assets at the balance sheet date not exceeding HK$10,000,000; and headcount not exceeding 100 employees. Even a qualifying small private company loses the exemption and is legally mandated to obtain an independent certified CPA audit if members holding at least 5% of the voting rights require an audit, if it is part of a group whose parent or consolidation requires audited accounts, or if it has public-interest characteristics. All other companies, including any company exceeding one of the quantitative thresholds, are unconditionally required to file an auditor's report with the Profits Tax Return.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared and retained where the aggregate amount of controlled transactions in a category exceeds HK$10,000,000; Master File is required where the MNE group has total consolidated revenue of at least HK$680,000,000; Country-by-Country Report and notification obligations arise where the MNE group has consolidated revenue of HK$6,800,000,000 or more. For the virtual CFO services JV, intercompany service fees, management charges, software licensing and bookkeeping transactions with the PRC parent or associates should be benchmarked even below the Local File threshold to defend source claims.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not operate Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the territorial source doctrine functionally demands real economic presence. The IRD requires evidence of core income-generating activities (CIGA): where contracts are negotiated, where personnel are located, where services are physically performed, where business decisions are made, and where bank accounts and books are maintained. A Hong Kong JV that merely books invoicing while the PRC parent performs the actual CFO work will have its offshore claim rejected, and artificial or fictitious transactions may be disregarded under IRO ss 61 and 61A.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Wilful False Return

Statutory sanction: IRO s 82(1): upon conviction, a fine of up to HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, plus additional tax not exceeding 3 times the amount of tax undercharged; IRO s 82A permits the IRD to impose additional tax for incorrect returns without criminal prosecution, up to 300% of the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: A director who signs, authorises or causes the false return may be prosecuted personally; the corporate veil will be pierced where the JV is used as a sham or facade for evasion. Directors can be personally ordered to pay the additional tax, face civil asset restitution, be subject to freezing and charging orders, be prohibited from leaving Hong Kong via departure prohibition orders under IRO s 76, and suffer imprisonment of up to 3 years for wilful tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Deficient TP Documentation

Statutory sanction: IRO s 50A codifies the arm's length principle; IRD has powers to adjust profits where the condition differs from what would be made between independent persons. Failure to produce TP documentation in response to an IRD notice may attract penalties under IRO s 80(2) (up to HK$10,000 plus monthly default fines), while an incorrect return caused by unjustified intercompany pricing may trigger s 82A additional tax at up to 3 times the undercharged tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve inflated related-party invoices or fail to maintain legally required TP documentation may be held personally liable for fines and assessments on a piercing basis if they act fraudulently, use the company as a mere instrument, or engage in wrongful trading. In severe cases, directors become personally accountable for the company's tax debts in insolvency and can face disqualification orders and travel restrictions.
Shell / Conduit Arrangement and False Offshore Claim

Statutory sanction: IRO s 61 permits the IRD to disregard artificial or fictitious transactions; IRO s 61A gives the IRD broad power to set aside tax avoidance arrangements; the PRC-HK Arrangement also contains an anti-abuse Principal Purpose Test. The resulting assessment recharacterises the amounts as Hong Kong-source taxable profits, with additional tax of up to 3 times the undercharged tax and possible criminal prosecution for tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or operate a shell JV for tax avoidance can have the corporate veil pierced: the court may order personal civil restitution of diverted profits, subject assets to recovery, impose blacklisting and departure prohibition orders under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, and impose criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years where the shell arrangement is used to defraud the revenue. Directors may also face fraud charges and disqualification for failure to maintain real economic substance in Hong Kong.
