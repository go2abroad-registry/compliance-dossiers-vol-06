Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the electronic tax bureau portal (e-Tax/自然人电子税务局 and corporate e-Tax system); for Hong Kong SAR matters, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the eTAX portal

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-foreign equity joint venture in mainland China, the JV must first obtain a Unified Social Credit Code certificate (营业执照/统一社会信用代码) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). After SAMR registration and obtaining the business license, the tax registration is automatically synchronized with the State Taxation Administration. The tax authority assigns a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) which is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code used for tax filings. For foreign non-resident enterprises without a mainland establishment that earn China-source income, the withholding agent applies for a tax registration or uses the foreign entity's home-country TIN; alternatively the non-resident may register at the competent tax authority for the relevant income item. In Hong Kong SAR, a foreign company that begins business in Hong Kong must apply for Business Registration (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance and obtain a Business Registration Number from the Inland Revenue Department; if it has a representative office or place of effective management in Hong Kong, it also applies for a Profits Tax files under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Available election forms

China does not provide a generic check-the-box pass-through election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for general VAT taxpayer registration threshold (monthly RMB 100,000 for small-scale taxpayers Q&A; the general taxpayer mandatory threshold is RMB 5,000,000 per annum); Hong Kong SAR has no VAT registration threshold as no VAT/GST is imposed

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods from mainland China qualifies for VAT zero-rating and export tax rebate under the State Taxation Administration export tax refund policy. Eligible exporters must be general VAT taxpayers, have resolved the export goods in customs records, collect foreign exchange, process the goods through customs declaration, and complete the export tax refund declaration within the prescribed period. Hong Kong SAR export trade is largely outside the ambit of VAT/GST; no zero-rating certificate is required for VAT because there is no VAT, but goods exported from Hong Kong are exempt from customs duty unless the goods are dutiable goods.

  • Customs Export Declaration (报关单/出口货物报关单) accepted by China Customs
  • Bill of Lading (海运提单 or air waybill/railway waybill)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (出口收汇核销单/银行收汇凭证 or foreign exchange settlement receipt showing repatriation of export proceeds)
  • Export Invoice and sales contract
  • Processing/manufacturing proof for exported goods
  • VAT special invoice for export goods
  • Export tax rebate declaration form submitted via 出口退税申报 (or electronic export tax refund filing through the tax authority system)

China mandates the use of the nationwide electronic invoice system (全面数字化电子发票 / all-digital electronic invoice) under the State Taxation Administration's e-invoice platform. VAT taxpayers must issue specialized VAT invoices through the e-tax invoice system and upload invoice data to the tax authority for fiscal clearance; Golden Tax Phase IV real-time reporting and digital stamping applies. In Hong Kong SAR, e-tax filing through eTAX is available, but there is no continuous transaction control or national digital fiscal clearance system for sales invoices because no VAT/GST is levied.

Fraudulent or unverified export VAT zero-rating claims are subject to full recovery of the claimed VAT refund (clawback), a penalty ranging from 50% to 5 times the tax amount if the refund was obtained by deception or false documentation, late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and potential criminal liability for export fraud under Chinese law. Customs and the State Taxation Administration may conduct joint audits and require proof of physical export, foreign exchange receipt, and cargo flow; failure to substantiate renders the refund void and triggers tax collection measures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar is freely convertible under the HKMA's linked exchange rate regime; Hong Kong imposes no foreign exchange surrender obligations, no capital account licensing, and no approval requirement for current or capital transactions. The mainland Chinese legal framework, however, divides convertibility into current and capital account. The current account is broadly convertible; trade in goods, services, and profit distributions may be settled in RMB after banks have performed authenticity and reasonableness checks. The capital account remains partially restricted; foreign direct investment, offshore loans, cross-border guarantees, and portfolio investment require SAFE registration, approval, or filing. Export proceeds from mainland operations must be repatriated into a domestic bank within the prescribed trade-related time frame, and although normal A-class enterprises may retain proceeds in foreign currency, the bank will monitor the timing of collection. Profit remittance from a mainland equity joint venture to its Hong Kong parent is permitted only after the CPA audited annual financial statements, the corporate income tax final settlement, and the board resolution have been submitted; the remittance amount may not exceed the distributable profit and must not damage the registered capital. Thus a treasury model must respect the separation between Hong Kong's free clearing market and mainland China's verified fund flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PRC SAFE current trade administration, export proceeds from mainland operations must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration for an A-class company; the entity may then retain up to 100% of the foreign currency without mandatory conversion, but any RMB settlement must be executed through a licensed onshore bank. For B-class enterprises the repatriation timeline may be shortened to 90 days and a compulsory settlement order may be imposed; C-class enterprises are not eligible to retain proceeds and must sell 100% into RMB under bank supervision. Hong Kong imposes no export proceeds surrender or conversion requirement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for mainland capital controls; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the central banking authority for Hong Kong dollar convertibility and bank supervision.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. An offshore digital EMI holding no Hong Kong Banking Ordinance authorized institution license cannot take deposits, cannot provide the settlement statements SAFE accepts for capital verification, and cannot act as the clearing bank for RMB conversion or the foreign exchange capital account. The HKMA authorizes only licensed banks, restricted licence banks, and deposit-taking companies to conduct deposit-taking business; e-money institutions under the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance are not deposit-taking licensees and are legally barred from servicing the capital, trade, and foreign exchange treasury needs of this joint venture. A fintech EMI may be used only for limited low-value card payments, but it must not receive shareholder capital, trade proceeds, or FX-related disbursements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity from the Hong Kong treasury to the mainland JV must be routed through the onshore foreign exchange capital account at the chosen Tier-1 bank and matched to SAFE's FDI compliance information. The remittance must be sent as a SWIFT MT103 and the USD or RMB purpose code must be stated as 310101 for receipt into the foreign exchange capital account; if the capital is converted to RMB for expenses, the bank will apply the 310102 capital settlement procedure. Three documents must be embedded in or attached to the instruction: the SAFE FDI business registration certificate, the board capital contribution resolution, and the joint venture contract. The onshore bank freezes the remittance pending authenticity verification; after crediting, the licensed CPA issues the capital verification report used for the State Administration for Market Regulation filing. Turnover: Hong Kong has no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold that exempts an active incorporated company from audit. Every non-dormant Hong Kong company must appoint an HKICPA-certified public accountant as its auditor and file audited financial statements in support of the Profits Tax Return; the only qualitative exemption is a dormant company that has elected company-dormancy status and has had no accounting transactions other than incorporation services. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions, RMB 200 million for tangible property, RMB 100 million for intangible property, and RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; Master File: aggregate related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report: group consolidated revenue exceeding RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The China-HK structure is subject to PRC GAAR under EIT Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form rule: the Hong Kong entity must demonstrate substantive business activity, real decision-making, staff and CIGA in Hong Kong; the mainland JV must maintain actual management and operations in mainland China. If the Hong Kong entity is centrally managed and controlled in China, it risks becoming a PRC tax resident and a permanent establishment in mainland; Hong Kong IRD's source rules require genuine local activity for offshore claims.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 permits recharacterization of abusive transactions; SAT Public Notice 2015 No. 7 taxes indirect transfers of mainland assets and recharacterizes the arrangement; unpaid tax can be recovered with 0.05% daily surcharge and fines up to 50%-500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced: directors and shareholders who abuse limited liability are jointly and severally liable for the enterprise's unpaid taxes; PRC tax authorities can blacklist directors, impose exit bans under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, freeze personal bank accounts, and if tax evasion was willful directors face imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201.
Tax Evasion / False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of evaded tax, late surcharge, and a fine of 50%-500% of the unpaid amount; Criminal Law Article 205 also applies for false issuance of special VAT invoices, carrying heavy fines and imprisonment, with Article 201 punishing tax evasion by up to 7 years for large amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false invoices or fraudulent returns are treated as co-perpetrators; their personal assets may be attached for restitution, they are subject to foreign exchange/travel blacklisting, and prison terms of 3-7 years are normal for severe evasion; civil liability extends to creditors whose claims are damaged by the fraud.
AML / Treaty Abuse Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Recharacterization is ordered under EIT Law Article 47, applying the 10% dividend WHT plus late surcharge and penalties; the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law punishes payments evasion and false beneficial ownership reporting with monetary fines up to RMB 5 million for serious violations; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money-laundering activity up to 10 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors who instigate the conduit arrangement are jointly liable for the shortfall in withholding tax and can have their assets seized; travel bans and serious-dishonesty blacklisting restrict their cross-border movement; if bank statements and transfer documents are falsified, criminal liability for money laundering and tax evasion may attach, with imprisonment imposed on responsible directors.
