Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (国家税务总局) and its official electronic tax portal 'E-Tax' (电子税务局), accessible at etax.chinatax.gov.cn. For Hong Kong SAR-related cross-border matters, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the competent authority for Hong Kong profits tax, but the primary Chinese mainland tax identity is obtained through the State Taxation Administration.

E-government filing portal

For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chinese equity joint venture (JV) or otherwise deriving Chinese-source income, the entity must first complete industrial and commercial registration at the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 统一社会信用代码), which functions as the de facto Tax Identification Number (TIN). Thereafter, the JV must complete tax registration at the competent local tax authority of the State Taxation Administration within 30 days of receiving its business license, either online via the E-Tax portal or in-person at the tax service hall. A non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment that is not registered for business must instead apply for a tax registration number by filing Form 'T202' (Taxpayer Identification Number Application for Non-Resident Enterprise) with the designated tax authority, accompanied by its foreign certificate of incorporation, notarized and apostilled/legalized as required, and a Chinese translation. For VAT purposes, the JV will be assigned a VAT taxpayer identification number under the unified USCC, and must also complete registration as a general VAT taxpayer if projected annual sales exceed RMB 5 million (or voluntarily at or below that threshold).

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a Chinese equity joint venture (JV) or otherwise deriving Chinese-source income, the entity must first complete industrial and commercial registration at the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC, 统一社会信用代码), which functions as the de facto Tax Identification Number (TIN). For VAT purposes, the JV will be assigned a VAT taxpayer identification number under the unified USCC, and must also complete registration as a general VAT taxpayer if projected annual sales exceed RMB 5 million (or voluntarily at or below that threshold).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is an enterprise established under Chinese law and is taxed as a resident enterprise at the entity level on its worldwide income at 25% under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no check-the-box or pass-through election is available. However, an offshore parent or non-resident investor may elect to apply the deemed profit method (cost-plus or deemed income) for attributable profits of a non-resident enterprise with a permanent establishment, subject to the State Taxation Administration's approval, and may elect application of an applicable Double Tax Agreement (DTA) between China and its jurisdiction of residence, including Hong Kong SAR, to claim treaty benefits on dividends, interest, royalties, and capital gains by filing Form 'Non-Resident Enterprise Treaty Benefit Claim' before the dividend payment. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available under Chinese domestic law, but the entity must comply with the mixed-enterprise anti-abuse rules under the Arrangement between the Mainland of China and the Hong Kong SAR for the Avoidance of Double Taxation.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

For owners of registered status: general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory when the annual sales amount (assessed on a rolling 12-month basis) of sales of services, intangibles, or immovable property exceeds RMB 5,000,000. For goods, the threshold is also RMB 5,000,000, but a monthly sales cap of RMB 100,000 for small-scale VAT taxpayers applies for VAT exemption eligibility; the revised VAT law from 2026 continues the RMB 5,000,000 threshold. For non-resident sellers of digital services or cross-border e-commerce, registration is required when they have a Chinese collection institution, permanent establishment, or engage an agent; an offshore entity with no Chinese establishment must nonetheless register in China if it sells services to Chinese businesses or individuals through a platform with a Chinese collection facility, or if it elects to self-issue VAT invoices. There is no separate VAT registration threshold for non-resident enterprises with no establishment; they must register for each contract or supply where a Chinese registered enterprise withholds VAT at source.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For the specified market-entry consultancy services supplied by the Chinese JV to overseas clients, zero-rating is permissible only where the services are rendered entirely outside China, are consumed outside China, or are supplied to another entity in the free trade zone (such as Hong Kong SAR) for the provision of services outside China. The statutory criteria under the Circular on the VAT Policies for Cross-Border Taxable Activities (Caishui [2016] No. 36, as amended by the 2026 VAT Law implementing rules) are: (1) the service contract must expressly identify the overseas recipient and state the service is performed outside Mainland China; (2) the services must be actually performed by personnel physically present outside Mainland China (for consulting services) OR the service deliverables must be transmitted and received outside China; (3) the payment must be received from outside Mainland China in foreign exchange or RMB cleared through an offshore banking system; (4) the Chinese seller must retain a copy of the signed service agreement, a board resolution, and any applicable government approval for outbound provision; (5) the services must not be supplied to a domestic entity or for a project within mainland China; and (6) the taxpayer must have positive VAT input tax separately itemized for the directly attributable inputs. For qualifying offshore digital services (including remote consulting provided wholly by digital means to an overseas client), the place-of-supply rule deems the service to be exported if the recipient is a non-resident enterprise and the service is not physically rendered onshore; if those criteria are met, the exporter may apply for VAT zero-rating and receive a refund of residual input VAT under the 'exemption and refund' (免税退税) method, subject to online filing through the E-Tax platform's export tax refund module.

China's mandatory electronic invoice system requires VAT general taxpayers to issue all VAT special invoices (增值税专用发票) and ordinary invoices (增值税普通发票) through the State Taxation Administration's 'Golden Tax Phase IV' (金税四期) fully digitalized electronic invoice platform (全面数字化电子发票 '数电票'), using the real-time fiscal clearance and tax control console. For services, the intelligent tax classification code is selected on the platform, and the e-invoice is cleared in near-real-time with a unique invoice number and digital signature. VAT special invoices must include the Unified Social Credit Code and tax classification code; no paper invoice is permitted unless the taxpayer has special dispensation. The mandate imposes API integration between the enterprise ERP/accounting system and the E-Tax platform, through the 'Full-Digital E-Invoice' (全面数字化电子发票) system's standardized interface, for automatic upload of invoice data, purchasing data, and commodity/service descriptions. Every invoice is stamped with the tax authority's anti-fraud digital signature; invoices must be reported and reconciled no later than the 15th day of the following calendar month. Non-resident enterprises using a withholding agent must ensure the agent issues a VAT withholding certificate via the electronic platform; otherwise, they may apply to the E-Tax portal to obtain a 'Non-Resident VAT Taxpayer E-Invoice Registration' and issue invoices using the offshore reverse-charge mechanism.

Under the VAT Law and Circular 36, if an enterprise claims zero-rating or exemption without a bona fide export or where the foreign exchange receipt is not repatriated and properly recorded within 180 days, the tax authority shall assess the VAT at the standard rate (6% for consultancy) together with late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the date the VAT became due, and may impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax or an administrative fine of RMB 10,000 to 100,000 for documentation failures. Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Collection Administration Law, fraudulent export refund claims constitute tax fraud and may trigger criminal liability, including imprisonment of up to 7 years for large amounts, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded (e.g., up to RMB 5,000,000 for capital cross-border transactions), as well as blacklisting in the tax dishonesty list and restrictions on the legal representative's travel and credit. Additionally, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose fines under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations for failure to repatriate export proceeds within the deadline, at 0.1% to 5% of the foreign exchange amount or RMB 100,000 to 1,000,000. Retrospective clawback of the zero-rating refund is triggered by artificial restructuring with a main purpose to exploit the zero-rating regime, or where the offshore recipient is a related party of the mainland JV and lacks economic substance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates as a fully convertible offshore financial centre under the linked exchange rate system anchored to the US dollar. Both current-account and capital-account transactions are freely permitted; there are no surrender, restriction, or prior approval requirements on foreign exchange. The HKMA does not impose barriers to profit repatriation, dividends, or branch remittances, subject to normal corporate governance and tax filing. For the Chinese parent injecting capital into the HK JV, the relevant convertibility bottleneck lies in the PRC capital account: SAFE, MOFCOM, and National Development and Reform Commission authorisation must precede the outward remittance. By contrast, once funds are held in Hong Kong, they may be converted among HKD, USD, CNY (CNH), and other major currencies without restriction. Practical profit remittance hurdles are limited to (i) statutory audit, (ii) resolution of members, and (iii) applicable Hong Kong profits tax, while no withholding tax applies to dividends paid to non-resident shareholders, and no exchange-control permit is needed. This asymmetry requires a treasury structure that separates PRC-approval constraints from HK liquidity freedom.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export surrender regime; there is no prescribed timeline and the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. Export proceeds may be retained indefinitely in any freely convertible currency. For the PRC parent, export proceeds captured on the mainland must be repatriated through designated settlement banks and may be retained in a foreign-exchange settlement account without mandatory conversion.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) in coordination with the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for PRC-sourced RMB flows

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium-High as a supplementary payments channel; Low as the principal treasury rail. Hong Kong has no standalone EMI class; digital payment providers must hold an HKMA Stored Value Facility licence under the PSSVFA or be an Authorized Institution. Offshore EMIs licensed in the UK or EU cannot passport into Hong Kong and must partner with a licensed HK bank for settlement and CHATS access. Legal constraints: receiving money from third parties without licensing may constitute an unlicensed deposit-taking business or a money service operation under the AMLO, requiring an MSO licence. Wise_B2B can be used for small-value operational payments, but all capital injections, tax payments, and high-value disbursements must clear through a tier-1 bank.
