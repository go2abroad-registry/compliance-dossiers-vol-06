Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), The Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online portal: GovHK eTAX

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-incorporated Sino-foreign equity joint venture limited company, apply to the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622); through the one-stop company and business registration service, the Companies Registry transmits particulars to the IRD and a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) is issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The eight-digit Business Registration Number (BRN) on the BRC is the entity's Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for Hong Kong tax purposes. For a foreign non-resident entity establishing a branch or registered non-Hong Kong company, complete Part 16 registration under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and apply for business registration within one month of commencing business; the IRD assigns the BRN/TIN and opens a Profits Tax file. No VAT ID is obtained because Hong Kong SAR does not impose VAT, GST, or sales tax. A foreign non-resident entity with no Hong Kong business presence has no registration requirement.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (HKSAR) maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Hong Kong dollar (HKD) currency board, with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and free capital mobility for current and capital account transactions unless sanctioned. By contrast, the sovereign parent jurisdiction (Mainland China) applies a tightly regulated capital account, subject to the supervision of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). RMB is convertible for current account items (trade, services, profit remittance) provided the underlying economic transaction is genuine and fully documented; capital account transactions (inbound FDI, outbound ODI, portfolio investment) require ex-ante licensing/filing or the use of approved schemes such as the qualified foreign limited partnership or the HONG KONG-CNY clearing arrangement. For a Sino-foreign joint venture with a Hong Kong registry, the practical consequence is that HKD-denominated treasury operations enjoy complete freedom of convertibility, while funding flows from the mainland to the HKSAR must clear strict documentary verification for the remittance purpose code, the SAFE foreign exchange registration number of the mainland investor, and the underlying capital investment agreement. Profit repatriation from the mainland to foreign shareholders is permissible upon completion of annual audit, tax clearance, and FX proof; dividends are subject to withholding tax at the applicable treaty rate (normally 5% if the HK entity holds a domestic enterprise status certificate) and must reference the SAFE-form FII report. The absence of surrender requirements in the HKSAR allows proceeds to be held in any foreign currency; however, the mainland's monitoring regime classifies enterprises by export performance and sets trading and receipt banks within the Trade in Goods registry. Overall, the dual-currency axis comprises the fully convertible HKD/offshore CNH environment in the HKSAR and the SAFE-monitored onshore RMB environment in mainland China.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland China: Under SAFE's current account administration rules, export proceeds from goods trade must be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange account at a domestic bank within 30 days from the date of customs clearance for companies with annual export income above US$1 million; for small-scale traders the period is 45 days. There is no mandatory conversion ratio (0% surrender requirement) - the exporter may keep the entire receipt in a foreign currency account or convert to RMB at the official rate. Still, all foreign exchange receipts must be matched with an export customs declaration within the Trade in Goods Monitoring System; failure to declare within 180 days results in the bank treating the flow as capital account and imposing administrative penalties. Hong Kong SAR: no surrender rule or conversion ratio exists; export receipts may be retained indefinitely in HKD or any convertible foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the sovereign-level FX regulator for China's cross-border capital and current account transactions; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and currency board authority for the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) licensed in Hong Kong, such as those holding a Major Payment and Stored Value Facility (SVF) licence from the HKMA, are legally permitted to provide e-wallet, cross-border remittance, and payment collection/outward transfer services to corporate clients. However, they cannot take deposits, issue letters of credit, extend credit, or provide derivative products, and they are not covered by the Deposit Protection Scheme. For an entity that will execute FX forwards or hold capital escrow, a fintech EMI is not a suitable substitute for a Tier-1 authorized bank. On the mainland side, foreign EMI providers are categorically barred from onshore CNY payment business without a PBOC non-bank payment permit, and the Chinese joint-venture entity must route all RMB treasury activity through a domestic banking institution. Therefore, the recommended strategy is to use an HKMA-licensed SVF as an ancillary collection rail for low-value ESG data subscriptions, with primary corporate banking at a commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: There are no quantitative balance sheet, turnover or headcount thresholds that mandate an audit for wholly exempting small Hong Kong private companies. Every Hong Kong-incorporated non-dormant company is required by law to prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view of its financial position and to have those statements audited by an independent Certified Public Accountant (practising) holding a valid Practice Review number issued by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA). The only legal exemption is the dormant company regime under sections 372-373 of the Companies Ordinance: a company may be treated as dormant and exempt from audit if it has not entered into any accounting transaction during the financial year, except for a fee paid to the Companies Registry or the Registrar in connection with an incorporation or annual maintenance, and the board passes a special resolution not to appoint an auditor. A dormant company that resumes activity must re-enter the audit stream for that financial year.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% Hong Kong Profits Tax on corporate assessable profits; first HKD 2,000,000 of profits taxed at 8.25% under the two-tiered regime for a single qualified corporation.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Hong Kong statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 0%; no dividend withholding tax is imposed on outbound dividends paid by a Hong Kong taxpayer to any non-resident shareholder.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In HKD: a Hong Kong taxpayer must prepare contemporaneous local file documentation in a year of assessment where annual turnover exceeds HKD 100,000,000 and any single category of controlled transactions with associated enterprises exceeds HKD 10,000,000 (goods, services, financial transactions) or HKD 5,000,000 (intangible property). Master File is generally required for MNE groups with consolidated turnover at or above the prescribed HKD threshold, and CbC reporting applies to groups with consolidated revenue over EUR 750,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain a separate Economic Substance Regulations regime comparable to OFCs, but the FSIE regime requires the taxpayer to perform core income-generating activities, maintain adequate employees and premises in Hong Kong, and follow a nexus approach for IP income. A pure letterbox or shell JV claiming offshore treatment or DTA benefits will fail substance review and forfeit exemption, exposing the full profit to 16.5% profits tax plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) ss.80 and 82(1): fine up to HKD 100,000, additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax, and imprisonment for up to 3 years on criminal conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or procure the false return can be prosecuted personally. Courts may pierce the corporate veil where the JV is a sham, order personal asset restitution of the undercharged tax, and the Director of Immigration may issue a departure prevention order / travel ban on unpaid tax liabilities.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Abuse

Statutory sanction: IRD disallows the FSIE claim and assesses the entire fee income under the territorial matrix at 16.5% with profits tax surcharge; false FSIE declarations additionally expose the taxpayer to IRO s.82(1) criminal penalties and treble tax.

Director personal liability: Company directors may be blacklisted by IRD as responsible officers; personal bank mandates may be scrutinised, and directors who knowingly approved the sham offshore claim may be banned from holding board positions, have assets restrained, and face imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under IRO ss.61, 61A and the Hong Kong transfer pricing rules, the Commissioner may reconstruct the taxpayers, adjust the assessable profits, impose compensating adjustments, and apply penalties/surcharge; cross-border non-documentation can be penalised up to the amount of tax undercharged.

Director personal liability: Directors involved in the profit-shifting structure may be held jointly liable for the additional tax where they personally participated in fraudulent steps. Incorrect certificates or false DTA documentation can trigger criminal fraud prosecution, a travel ban pending tax collection, and restitution orders against directors' personal assets.
