Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which issues a Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for PRC enterprise income tax, VAT, and other national taxes. Step 2: SAMR and STA data synchronisation deems tax registration completed at incorporation, but the JV must, within 30 days, complete the supplementary tax profile in the STA e-Tax portal, including registered address, legal representative, accounting/fiscal year, bank deposit account filing, foreign investor details, expected taxable activities, VAT general taxpayer application, and invoice quota. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all PRC establishments must register for tax on incorporation irrespective of turnover and are classed as small-scale taxpayers until the threshold is exceeded (simplified levy rate 3%).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for goods is available where goods are physically exported and customs-cleared as export, the exporter retains the Customs Export Declaration, the goods are lawfully transported outside China, and export proceeds are received from abroad. Zero-rating for cross-border services is limited to the statutory enumerated offshore service categories (such as international transportation, R&D, design, software and information technology, and certain offshore outsourcing/digital services). Government affairs lobbying and foreign-stakeholder policy advisory services are not automatically zero-rated; they may qualify as exempt/zero-rated offshore services only if supplied to a foreign recipient, wholly consumed outside China, and supported by contemporaneous documentation that no benefit is derived in China. Otherwise, such services remain subject to VAT at the applicable rate (generally 6%).

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration) indicating the export entry and customs supervisory status
  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill, Air Waybill, or multimodal transport document evidencing physical export movement and foreign destination
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract/purchase order identifying the overseas buyer and export terms
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued foreign exchange receipt confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds in the required currency
  • SAFE trading-in-goods foreign exchange monitoring declaration and/or foreign exchange settlement record where applicable
  • For cross-border services: service agreement, delivery/acceptance certificate, overseas recipient tax residence certificate, and evidence that the service was performed and consumed entirely outside China

The JV must issue VAT invoices exclusively through the nationwide E-Invoice Service Platform integrated with the STA Electronic Tax Administration; invoices carry a unique invoice track number, anti-counterfeit two-dimensional code, and are transmitted in real time to the tax authority. The Golden Tax Phase IV system automatically cross-matches invoice data, customs declarations, foreign exchange receipts, and VAT returns; API/ERP integration is mandatory for high-volume taxpayers. The taxpayer must apply online for invoice allocation and quota, use digital tax-control equipment or digital certificate authentication, and issue VAT special invoices only to VAT-registered buyers whose TIN is verified in the official invoice checking database.

Unsubstantiated export zero-rating is denied by the STA; any input VAT already credited or export refund already paid is clawed back, and VAT is reassessed at the applicable rate with late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, general tax evasion/underpayment triggers a fine of 50% to five times the underpaid tax; fraudulent export refund claims trigger a specific penalty of one to five times the refund obtained and possible criminal prosecution for export-refund fraud and false invoicing. The retrospective assessment period is three years for ordinary underpayments, five years where the under-declaration exceeds 10% of the total taxable amount, and unlimited where fraud, evasion, or wilful concealment is established. Golden Tax Phase IV cross-matching of invoice, customs, logistics, and foreign-exchange data is the primary audit trigger.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong dollar (HKD) is freely convertible under the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate System (7.75-7.85 per USD), and Hong Kong imposes no exchange control, no prior licensing for ordinary corporate current account transfers, and no surrender obligation on export proceeds or advisory fee collections. The treasury blueprint must therefore treat HKD as fully current-account and capital-account convertible for CN_JV_LTD, provided the underlying government affairs advisory contracts are authentic and supported by invoices, status reports, and tax filings. However, the mainland Chinese leg of the structure is controlled by SAFE/PBOC: current account service fees, including cross-border policy analysis, lobbying, and public affairs consulting, require genuine trade documents, contract registration, tax withholding on service fees, and electronic verification before remittance. Capital account items such as equity injections from Hong Kong into a mainland Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture require SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration, capital account opening, and use of funds in line with the actual-use supervision principle. Profit repatriation from a mainland operating entity requires annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax (10% before treaty relief), and tax clearance proof. All HKD settlement in Hong Kong should use CHATS; cross-border RMB should be cleared over CIPS. No forced conversion applies in Hong Kong, but a mainland subsidiary must repatriate current account export receipts and convert them to CNY within the SAFE-regulated settlement account framework.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong: no statutory surrender or conversion ratio; export/retainer proceeds may be retained in HKD, CNY, USD, or other currencies, subject only to underlying trade documentation verification and sanctions screening. Mainland China: export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into a SAFE-designated current account settlement account; banks verify authentic transactions through the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment monitoring platform. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage, but excess over permitted current account retention limits must be settled into CNY immediately or under a bank-approved forward contract.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the banking and currency authority of Hong Kong SAR, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) governing all cross-border renminbi and mainland capital account matters arising from the China-HK dual jurisdiction treasury structure.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Hong Kong's Banking Ordinance restricts deposit-taking and corporate account facilities to licensed banks, restricted licence banks, and deposit-taking companies; an HKMA-licensed Stored Value Facility is not a full corporate electronic money institution able to hold treasury balances or provide CHATS/SWIFT clearing rails. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully open a Hong Kong domiciled corporate account for CN_JV_LTD unless they operate under a full or restricted banking licence. All substantive clearing must therefore be executed through licensed tier-1 commercial banks; fintech rails may only be used for FPS-collection of low-value retainer receipts after passing the same underlying contract and KYC controls.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

For capital injected into CN_JV_LTD from mainland China, the mainland parent must obtain SAFE overseas direct investment registration and complete MOFCOM/NDRC filing if applicable; use SWIFT MT103 or CIPS with a direct investment/outbound loan purpose code recognized by SAFE, clearly identifying CN_JV_LTD as the payee. The HK receiving bank must receive a board resolution, capital subscription agreement, share allotment form, and a declaration that funds are not structured for circumvention. If CN_JV_LTD instead funds a mainland operating joint venture, the HK vehicle must complete SAFE FDI registration through FDINet, open a capital account at the mainland bank, inject funds only for approved actual-use items, and obtain a mainland CPA capital verification report before any outbound disbursement. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Controlled transaction documentation is mandatory where the aggregate value of intercompany transactions exceeds HK$10 million for the Local File requirement, and where the multinational enterprise group has consolidated group revenue above HK$6.8 billion, a Master File and Country-by-Country Report are required. Even below these thresholds the taxpayer must maintain contemporaneous pricing evidence and apply the arm's-length standard.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies a territorial source doctrine for taxation of profits, not a formal Economic Substance Regulations regime, but complete economic substance is required in practice. The IRD will test whether the Core Income-Generating Activities of the government affairs lobbying and trade policy advisory services were performed in Hong Kong, including negotiation, execution, delivery, and management of the retainer work. An entity claiming offshore status must maintain substantive staff, office premises, board meetings, decision-making records, and service delivery capacity at the claimed location; an artificial letterbox arrangement or a purely paper booking of profits outside Hong Kong will be disregarded under section 61 or 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Wilful False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under sections 82 and 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, a person who knowingly makes a false return or statement is liable on conviction to a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for up to 3 years, in addition to a penalty of treble the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD may also issue additional assessments for up to 6 years of omitted income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign, prepare, or cause the filing of fraudulent returns can have the corporate veil pierced for civil restitution of unpaid tax and treble penalties, with their personal assets subject to restraint and confiscation. The IRD can apply for a departure prohibition order placing a travel ban on the director, and criminal conviction leads to imprisonment, cancellation of director status, and disqualification from managing any Hong Kong company under the Companies Ordinance.
Artificial Offshore Claim and Shell Company Substance Fraud

Statutory sanction: Sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance allow the IRD to disregard any artificial or fictitious transaction and recompute the assessable profits as if the substance existed in Hong Kong. The additional tax can be recovered with interest and penalty exposure up to 100% of the undercharged tax under section 82A. The IRD may further impose penalties of up to HK$10,000 for failure to notify chargeability.

Director personal liability: Courts will pierce the veil where the company is used as a mere alias for the directors' personal cross-border advisory business. Directors face civil liability for the entire avoided tax, asset tracing and restitution orders, blacklisting by the IRD and the Hong Kong Immigration Department, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for conspiracy to defraud the revenue, with imprisonment terms up to 3 years and, where the conduct extends to fraudulent breaching activities under the Theft Ordinance, more severe custodial sentences.
Unlicensed Government Lobbying, Corruption, and AML Violation

Statutory sanction: Under section 9 of the Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201), offering an advantage to a public servant with intent to influence an official act is an indictable offence carrying a maximum fine of HK$500,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years. Unlicensed lobbying activity attracts regulatory fines, revocation of the statutory licence, and disqualification from future government-facing advisory contracts. Breach of AML obligations under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance can result in fines exceeding HK$5 million and imprisonment for responsible officers.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable as principals for bribery and corruption offences where they authorised or were recklessly indifferent to illicit payments; the corporate veil is pierced to allow confiscation of personal assets under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455), with proceeds of crime subject to restraint and forfeiture. Directors face travel ban and blacklisting by the Customs and Excise Department and the Joint Financial Intelligence Unit, immediate arrest, imprisonment up to 7 to 14 years upon conviction, and permanent loss of eligibility to act as directors of regulated advisory or lobbying entities.
