Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital requirement under Hong Kong law. A company limited by shares may adopt nominal share capital of HK$1.00 or any amount determined by the subscribers. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; online official portal is GovHK and the IRD electronic tax portal eTax (ird.gov.hk). The State Taxation Administration has jurisdiction over mainland China and is not the competent tax authority for this Hong Kong SAR registration matter.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident entity that will carry on business in Hong Kong must first be registered as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and appoint an authorized representative resident in Hong Kong; alternatively, if newly incorporated in Hong Kong, the entity is incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) and obtains its certificate of incorporation from the Companies Registry. Within one month of commencing business, the entity must apply to the Business Registration Office of the Inland Revenue Department for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) using the prescribed business registration application procedure. Upon approval, the IRD issues the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for Hong Kong profits tax and business records. No separate VAT or GST identification number is issued because Hong Kong has no value-added tax or general sales tax. The BRN must be displayed at the registered place of business and shown on invoices and receipts. A foreign entity that does not carry on business in Hong Kong is not required to register solely because it is a non-resident corporation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates a fully convertible HKD under a linked exchange rate system. The HKMA currency board keeps the HKD/USD rate in a 7.75-7.85 band, with no exchange controls, no approvals, and no surrender requirement on current or capital account transactions conducted through licensed banks in Hong Kong. The CN_JV_LTD entity will therefore enjoy unrestricted hold-and-transfer rights over HKD, USD, CNY, and other major currencies in its Hong Kong accounts. This free-convertibility status, however, does not extend to the mainland China nexus. A mainland Chinese shareholder injecting capital into the Hong Kong entity must first complete outbound direct investment registration and approval with NDRC, MOFCOM, and SAFE, obtain a bank-level SAFE foreign exchange registration, and provide documentary evidence of the commercial purpose before funds can clear through CIPS, CHATS, or SWIFT. Repatriated profits, dividends, royalties, and service fees back to the mainland parent remain subject to SAFE current-account authenticity verification, tax certificates, and withholding tax considerations. Thus the standalone Hong Kong treasury layer is fully convertible, but any RMB or USD movement touching the mainland capital account triggers SAFE-controlled convertibility hurdles, making the joint venture operationally free only after compliance and documentary proof are lodged.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no export proceeds surrender rule and requires no mandatory conversion ratio; export receipts may be retained, converted, or paid out in any currency. For any mainland China consolidated export proceeds used to finance the JV, PRC SAFE rules require repatriation to a domestic nominated current-account bank and verification against the customs export declaration, generally within 30 days of the export declaration date; there is no mandatory conversion percentage, but offshore retention requires SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank and bank regulator of the Hong Kong SAR; for any mainland People's Republic of China cross-border flow, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) is the controlling authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: Medium-low as a stand-alone treasury provider. The HK-incorporated JV may lawfully use a HKMA-licensed stored value facility or money service operator for online payment aggregation, including Stripe for low-risk digital ticketing collections, but an unlicensed offshore EMI cannot take deposits, provide account screening, or act as the settlement bank. All EMIs and SVFs must settle into a segregated trust account at a licensed tier-1 bank, and acceptance of USDC requires a licensed Hong Kong virtual asset service provider under the AMLO/VASP regime. Offshore digital EMIs that lack HK licensing or an HKMA-authorized settlement bank are legally barred from servicing this entity as a treasury account provider, and therefore the blueprint directs all corporate liquidity to the four licensed banks above.
