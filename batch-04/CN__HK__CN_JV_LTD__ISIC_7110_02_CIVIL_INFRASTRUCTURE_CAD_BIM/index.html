Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (State Taxation Administration, 国家税务总局) via the national electronic tax portal etax.chinatax.gov.cn; and for Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region via the eTAX portal (gov.hk/etax).

E-government filing portal

For mainland China: A foreign non-resident entity establishing a Sino-foreign equity joint venture must complete enterprise establishment with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a Unified Social Credit Code. The tax registration step is then performed online at the official State Taxation Administration (STA) platform, where the legal representative or an authorised agent with a notarised power of attorney files Form XXX? (use factual forms such as Tax Registration Form for Overseas Enterprises and the Alternative Tax Identification Number Collection Form). The STA issues either a Special Tax Identification Number for non-resident enterprises or a unified social credit code that serves as the taxpayer identification number. For Hong Kong: A foreign corporate shareholder registering a Hong Kong company must submit IRBR1 (Application for Business Registration) to the Inland Revenue Department together with the Certificate of Incorporation, the corporate register information, and identity documents of the Hong Kong director or company secretary. IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate bearing a Business Registration Number, which is the Hong Kong TIN. The entity may also apply for a Certificate of Resident Status (IR1313A or IR1314A) for double taxation relief. All certificates not in English or Chinese must be translated by a certified translator.

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT registration as a general taxpayer; small-scale taxpayer threshold is RMB 0-5,000,000 at a 1-3% collection rate. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China: Zero-rating applies to cross-border sales of services where the service is rendered to a customer located outside mainland China and the service is fully consumed outside mainland China. For engineering consulting, CAD/BIM drafting, and offshore building information modelling under ISIC 7110, the service qualifies as a cross-border service export under Caishui 2016 No. 36 if the recipient is a non-resident enterprise and the service is used entirely outside China. Hong Kong: Zero-rating is not applicable because Hong Kong does not impose VAT/GST on any goods or services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Entry for export)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or foreign exchange receipt certificate issued by a designated bank
  • Export contract or agreement with the offshore customer
  • Commercial invoice with VAT zero-rating notation
  • Service delivery certificate or BIM progress/consumption report evidencing offshore utilisation
  • OC? (optional) Customs export manifest if goods are also physically shipped

China operates a nationwide digital electronic invoice system under the fully digitalised electronic invoice (全面数字化电子发票) reform. VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the official STA platform or approved service providers, with real-time transmission to the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Every invoice is digitally signed, carries a unique 20-digit invoice code and a tax control cipher, and must be reported via the electronic tax authority API module. Hong Kong has no electronic fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate for VAT purposes.

China: Where export zero-rating is claimed but evidence is insufficient, the tax authority claws back all input VAT previously credited, charges late payment interest of 0.05% per day, and imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the overdue tax for fraudulent claims. Criminal liability applies under the Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud. Records and FX receipts must be retained for at least five years. Hong Kong: No VAT penalty applies, but inaccurate trade declarations may be penalised under Hong Kong Customs regulations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong SAR maintains a fully convertible currency regime under the Currency Board system administered by the HKMA. The Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the United States dollar at 7.75 to 7.85 per USD. Both current account and capital account transactions, including trade settlements, service fees, loan repayments, profit repatriation and principal return, are fully convertible without central bank approval, surrender requirements or repatriation licensing. There are no statutory obstacles to paying dividends or returning capital from a Hong Kong-incorporated joint venture; the company may remit in any freely traded currency. The practical friction comes from anti-money laundering documentation and mainland China's separate foreign exchange control framework. If the PRC parent is injecting capital, the mainland remitter must complete SAFE outward investment registration and the PRC bank's capital item remittance approval before the Hong Kong beneficiary bank can clear the funds. Once credited, the Hong Kong entity can convert and disburse freely. The real hurdles are therefore KYC substance evidence and China-side SAFE compliance, not Hong Kong FX restrictions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender rules. Timeline: not applicable. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0%. Export receipts from CAD/BIM services may be retained in any foreign currency indefinitely, and the only record-keeping mandate is the Companies Ordinance requirement to retain underlying invoices, contracts and bank statements for at least 7 years.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central bank for Hong Kong SAR; on the mainland China leg of the cross-border structure, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China governs the PRC parent's outward investment remittance

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Fintech EMIs are legally permitted in Hong Kong but carry a split viability profile. Wise is a non-bank fintech payment provider, not a full commercial bank, and must hold the appropriate Money Service Operator (MSO) licence or Stored Value Facility (SVF) authorisation from the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department / HKMA. Wise is excellent for low-to-mid-value cross-border operational disbursements, staff expense reimbursements and small vendor settlement; it cannot issue commercial letters of credit, hold full fiduciary capital escrow or serve as the designated account for a capital injection. Hong Kong virtual banks licensed under the Banking Ordinance, such as ZA Bank or Mox Bank, may be used as supplementary clearing rails for digital-first liquidity but frequently impose enhanced due diligence questions on PRC-linked engineering companies. Overall viability is High for sub-USD 50,000 vendor payments and multi-currency payroll, Medium for larger operational disbursements, and Low for statutory capital escrow or project letter-of-credit work. The legal constraint is licensing; engaging an unlicensed offshore EMI creates freeze risk and may breach the Payment Systems and Stored Value Facilities Ordinance.

The Hong Kong beneficiary bank will expect the referencing to show Capital Subscription - CN_JV_LTD and may require the remitter's bank to provide a funds release confirmation. After the credit, the HK company must file a Return of Allotment Form SH1 with the Companies Registry within one month and obtain a capital verification report from a Hong Kong Certified Public Accountant. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong's transfer pricing rules (DIPN 58, 59, 60) do not set a fixed monetary threshold for Local File / Master File preparation. Instead, the Inland Revenue Department requires contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation for all controlled transactions if the aggregate amount of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 10 million for intercompany purchases or HKD 5 million for intercompany financing, and HKD 10 million for other controlled transactions (per the IRD's guidelines in DIPN 58). Specifically, an entity must prepare a Local File if the value of controlled transactions (excluding financing) exceeds HKD 10 million; a Master File is required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds HKD 680 million and the entity's controlled transactions exceed HKD 10 million; a separate 'Financing' file is required if aggregate controlled financing transactions exceed HKD 5 million. The JV must also file an annual 'Controlled Transaction Report' in its profits tax return (Schedule 17) listing intercompany transactions and confirm whether TP documentation was prepared. The IRD can impose penalties of up to 3 times the undercharged tax for failure to keep adequate records or for non-arm's length pricing adjustments.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has no specific Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) equivalent to the UAE, but the IRD applies the 'economic substance' concept through the 'beneficial ownership' doctrine for DTA claims, and through the 'general anti-abuse rules' (Sections 61 and 61A IRO). For a HK JV engaged in BIM/CAD services, to substantiate economic substance, the JV must demonstrate that it has: (1) a real business presence (registered office, physical premises, bank accounts in HK); (2) qualified employees who perform substantive decision-making and operational functions in HK (or, if claiming offshore, in the offshore location); (3) actual control over the contracts and risks assumed; (4) adequate local board meetings that are more than mere formality; and (5) proper transactional documentation. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test is relevant under the DTA context: to qualify as a HK resident, the 'place of effective management' must be in HK, meaning the highest-level policy decisions are made and business operations are conducted from HK. If the JV delegates all functions to its mainland parent (e.g., all engineers and project management in Shenzhen), the JV may be deemed a mainland tax resident, which could trigger mainland CIT on worldwide income and invalidate DTA benefits. Additionally, the HK JV must file an annual 'Employment Return' (BIR56A) for all employees, proving payroll, MPF contributions, and employment contracts. If the JV engages sub-contractors outside HK, the IRD may challenge the deduction of payments to them if no substance supports the intercompany fee.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Understatement of Offshore Profits

Statutory sanction: Under Section 82(1) of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), fraud or willful evasion of tax is an offense: upon conviction, a fine of HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus a penalty of 3 times the amount of tax undercharged (triple tax penalty). Under Section 82A, the IRD may also impose a penalty of 3 times the undercharged tax for an incorrect tax return without reasonable excuse. The IRD may further invoke Section 61A (anti-avoidance) to disregard the arrangement and re-assess the profits at 16.5%, with interest and penalties.

Director personal liability: Under the IRO, every director of a company is jointly and severally liable for penalties imposed on the company for tax evasion if the evasion was committed with the consent or connivance of, or is attributable to any neglect on the part of, the director (Section 82(4) and 82A(4) IRO). Civil liability: the IRD can issue a tax assessment on the director personally for the full underpaid tax plus penalties. Travel: the IRD can apply to the District Court for an order prohibiting a director from leaving Hong Kong if the tax debt exceeds HKD 10,000 and the director has not paid (Section 76(3) IRO) - this effectively imposes a travel ban. Criminal liability: if convicted, a director may be imprisoned for up to 3 years, and if fraud is proven, up to 10 years under Section 82. In practice, the IRD frequently prosecutes directors of shell companies used to launder funds or evade profits tax; personal bankruptcy or disqualification from being a director (under the Companies Ordinance) may also follow.
Failure to Maintain Contemporaneous Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Pricing

Statutory sanction: Sections 50AAP-50AAS of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, incorporating the OECD Transfer Pricing Guidelines, empower the IRD to adjust the assessable profits by disallowing a deduction or attributing additional profits to the Hong Kong entity. Penalties for failure to prepare documentation and for understating taxable profits: under Section 80(2) (failure to comply with notice), a fine of HKD 10,000 plus HKD 200 per day; under Section 82A (incorrect return), penalty of up to 3 times undercharged tax (capped at HKD 50,000 plus 3x tax if willful). The IRD may also charge a 7% compound annual interest on the tax undercharged. For serious cases, the IRD may apply the transfer pricing anti-avoidance provisions to reduce or disallow tax losses arising from the transaction.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participate in preparing false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable under Section 82(4) IRO for the same penalties as the company. Civil asset restitution: the IRD can sue directors for negligence or breach of fiduciary duties if the company's failure to maintain TP documentation causes a tax liability that the company cannot pay. Criminal imprisonment: up to 3 years for tax fraud; longer if the court applies the Theft Ordinance or Companies Ordinance (for making false statements in the annual return). Board members may also be prohibited from leaving Hong Kong under the tax collection provisions if the company owes tax and they are deemed 'control persons'.
Shell Corporate / Beneficial Ownership Opacity and Money Laundering (AML/CTF) and Mainland Cross-Border Customs/Foreign Exchange Violations

Statutory sanction: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), failure to keep the Significant Controllers Register (SCR) is a criminal offense: a fine of HKD 25,000 and, for continuing default, a daily fine of HKD 700. Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) and the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615), a company that deals with property known or believed to represent proceeds of crime commits a money laundering offense: imprisonment up to 14 years and a fine of HKD 5,000,000 on indictment. The Inland Revenue Department can also recharacterize the JV as a sham and assess the underlying non-resident beneficial owner under the 'look-through' provisions, treating the JV as a transparent entity, and charge the non-resident at the full 16.5% corporate rate. On the mainland side, if the JV is deemed to be a 'circular construction' to avoid foreign exchange controls, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange may impose a fine of 30%-100% of the illegal amount, and the Public Security Bureau may investigate under the Criminal Law (illegal business operation charge - Article 225) with imprisonment up to 10 years for ringleaders.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally accountable for AML failures: under Cap. 615, a director who authorizes or permits a suspicious transaction without a proper report commits an offense punishable by imprisonment up to 3 months and a fine of HKD 50,000 on summary conviction, or up to 7 years and a fine of HKD 1,000,000 on indictment. The Companies Registry may strike off the company, and directors are disqualified for up to 15 years under Section 44 of the Companies Ordinance for persistent default in filing returns and maintaining substance. If the corporate veil is pierced by the courts (e.g., under the 'sham' doctrine or the 'quasi-partnership' exception), directors' personal assets may be confiscated to satisfy the company's tax debts and civil restitution claims. Travel bans are routinely applied against directors who fail to cooperate with IRD investigations. For mainland-related cross-border payments, Chinese authorities may issue a 'no-exit' (限制出境) order against the director if the company is suspected of fraud or illegal remittance, which effectively bans the director from leaving China.
