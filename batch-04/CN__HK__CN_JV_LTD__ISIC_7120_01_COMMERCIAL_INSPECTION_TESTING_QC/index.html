Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. For Hong Kong SAR, the separate competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), with eTAX at https://www.ird.gov.hk.

E-government filing portal

For a mainland-registered Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, complete company registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation to obtain the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC); the 18-digit USCC is the statutory Chinese tax identification number and VAT ID. Then complete tax registration with the competent State Taxation Administration office within 30 days of business license issuance using the e-Tax portal; this creates an STA taxpayer file and activates VAT invoice issuance. A foreign non-resident entity without a fixed place of business in China does not routinely obtain a Chinese VAT ID unless it has Chinese-sourced taxable income or supplies; in that case the Chinese payer or withholding agent must complete non-resident withholding registration with STA and the non-resident will be assigned a non-resident taxpayer identification number. Required documents for foreign founders include the foreign incorporation certificate, constitutional documents, board resolution appointing a local tax representative, registered address proof, bank account details, and, for the Hong Kong investor, a valid Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and Certificate of Tax Residence where treaty benefits are claimed. The JV must also apply for general VAT taxpayer status (unless it remains a small-scale taxpayer below the statutory threshold) and open the STA digital invoice account with an approved UKey.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China does not permit check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections. The only available elections are VAT small-scale versus general taxpayer status, and application of PRC-Hong Kong tax arrangement benefits to dividends or royalties; neither changes the mandatory entity-level CIT classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) of VAT taxable turnover in any rolling 12-month period; once exceeded, general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory, with voluntary registration available below the threshold. The JV's inspection/testing/QC services are modern services normally subject to VAT at 6%; general goods sales attract the 13% headline rate; export goods are zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exports qualify for 0% VAT when the goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory, the JV is the exporter of record, the customs export declaration matches the commercial documents, export proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank, and the goods are not prohibited or restricted. For cross-border services, zero-rating is limited to the STA-listed offshore services catalogue such as offshore research and development, software design, data processing, business process outsourcing and international transportation; on-site factory pre-shipment inspection/QC services performed in mainland China generally do not fall under automatic zero-rating and may only be VAT-exempt where the recipient is offshore and the service is consumed outside China. The JV should obtain a written STA ruling before applying 0% VAT to inspection fees.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) showing the 18-digit declaration number and exporter of record
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing shipment from PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) evidencing foreign exchange repatriation of the export price
  • Export contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • VAT special invoice for export sales or the STA-recognized export invoice
  • Designated foreign-exchange bank export proceeds receipt or SAFE export collection verification record

The JV must issue VAT invoices through the STA fully digitalized e-invoice platform; every special VAT invoice is transmitted in real time to the STA and cleared by the Golden Tax Phase IV system using an approved UKey/digital certificate. High-volume issuers must submit invoice data sets (XML/JSON) through the STA e-invoice API; each invoice carries an anti-counterfeit digital stamp and QR signature. Manually stamped paper invoices are no longer sufficient for general taxpayer VAT credit or export refund purposes.

All zero-rating claims are audited by STA Golden Tax IV through cross-referencing customs export declarations, foreign-exchange receipts and VAT returns. Failure to substantiate the export or to repatriate proceeds within 180 days triggers suspension of zero-rating, reversal and clawback of VAT refunds and input credits, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, administrative fines of 50% to 500% of the VAT involved, downgrade of tax credit rating, and possible revocation of export tax-refund qualification. Knowingly submitting false documents or overstating export values may constitute fraudulent export tax refund under PRC law, leading to criminal liability, asset freezes, and retroactive tax assessments without limitation in fraud cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account RMB convertibility broadly liberalised, while capital-account transactions remain conditionally regulated. Under SAFE's FDI regime, the JV can receive the HK shareholder's equity injection into a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account; once the contribution is registered and verified, RMB conversion for business purposes is allowed under the negative list. Current-account receipts from overseas inspection/testing clients are freely convertible and can be sold to banks or retained in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account, subject to truthfulness of underlying documentation. Profit remittance is permitted after tax, using audited financial statements, board dividend resolution, and tax-paid certificate; a 5% withholding dividend tax may apply to qualified Hong Kong residents under the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement. Surrender of export/service proceeds no longer requires a mandatory conversion percentage; however, receipts must be repatriated to China within the period stated in the contract/SAFE monitoring, and 100% of such proceeds may be retained in FX accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory conversion ratio exists since SAFE abolished mandatory FX surrender in 2012. Export and service-trade proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic FX current account or the bank's receipt-verification channel, and may be converted to RMB at the entity's discretion or retained indefinitely for current-account purposes. The statutory timeline is contract-driven: the JV must ensure receipt no later than the expected collection date recorded in its SAFE trade declaration; a default bank standard is 90 days from the export/service invoice date, and any extension beyond that date must be justified to the bank before the due date under the trade credit category. Overdue amounts attract enhanced documentary scrutiny and forex violation penalties rather than fixed conversion deadlines.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign exchange supervision to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its provincial/municipal branches for Mainland China; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority governs the HK parent's banking counterparty but does not regulate the PRC JV's FX controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. Wise and other digital EMI providers are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open RMB settlement accounts for Mainland China corporate entities; they cannot conduct FDI capital verification, issue foreign-exchange purpose declarations, or process capital-account transactions. Such fintech EMIs are legally barred from acting as depositary institutions for onshore Chinese companies; they can only be used by the HK parent or offshore clients for incidental small-value fee remittances and are not acceptable for statutory capital injection, export collections, or tax-linked corporate liquidity management.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the HK parent's regulated bank to the JV's dedicated FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a PBOC-licensed tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance instruction must include 'FDI equity injection / capital contribution' and the SAFE FDI registration reference code, and the inbound foreign-exchange income declaration must cite the SAFE Balance-of-Payments transaction code for direct investment in China. The domestic bank verifies capital-account information and SAFE's FDI registration before converting funds into RMB; after conversion, RMB is credited to the JV's basic operational account and may be used for permitted business expenditures under the negative list.
