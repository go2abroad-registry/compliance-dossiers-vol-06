Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is incorporated through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), which automatically generates an 18-digit Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number. Foreign non-resident shareholders must complete real-name authentication in the e-Tax portal, appoint the JV legal representative, file the company's financial/accounting system and bank deposit account details, and complete tax registration. Where a foreign non-resident enterprise derives China-sourced income without a permanent establishment, it must separately complete a non-resident enterprise tax registration through a withholding agent and obtain a non-resident TIN before treaty benefit claims. The foreign investor may not elect pass-through treatment; the Chinese JV is a resident enterprise taxed at entity level. VAT classification as general taxpayer is elected when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or voluntarily by filing a general taxpayer application.

Standard VAT / GST rate

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All Chinese entities must complete tax and VAT registration at incorporation regardless of turnover. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 within any consecutive 12-month period, at which point the JV must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the JV may remain a small-scale taxpayer or voluntarily register as general taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported physical goods are zero-rated where (i) the goods are loaded and cleared through PRC customs for export, (ii) the export proceeds are received and repatriated in foreign currency, and (iii) the sale is documented with the customs export declaration, export invoice, transportation documents and bank receipt records. Qualifying cross-border testing and analysis services supplied to overseas clients and wholly consumed outside China may qualify for VAT zero-rating/exemption under the STA cross-border taxable services regime if the services are listed, the contract/purchase order specifies the offshore recipient, payment is received from outside China, and the required cross-border service declaration is filed with the tax authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Customs Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank foreign exchange repatriation certificate
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Cross-border service agreement and/or purchase order for testing services
  • Statement proving the testing service is wholly consumed outside China
  • STA e-invoice record evidencing zero-rated export supply

The JV must issue Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (FDEI) through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Each invoice receives a unique tax-control invoice number, a digital stamp and a cryptographic signature before issuance, with real-time transmission to the tax authority. ERP integration through STA-approved application programming interfaces (APIs) is required for mechanical/automated issuers; invoices issued outside this clearance framework are not valid for VAT deduction or export refund.

The STA's Golden Tax Phase IV risk system cross-matches customs declarations, e-invoices, foreign exchange receipts and taxpayer returns. If a zero-rated export claim is unsupported, if the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the mandatory 180-day deadline, or if documents are forged or inconsistent, the zero-rated VAT refund/exemption is clawed back retroactively with late-payment interest at 0.05% per day. Under Article 66 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, fraudulent export tax rebate claims are subject to an administrative penalty of 1 to 5 times the tax evaded as well as cancellation of export refund eligibility; cases involving significant amounts or criminal intent are referred under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, which carries criminal fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment for aggravated fraudulent export-rebate offences.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a regulated twin-track convertibility system under the Renminbi (RMB). Statutory current-account convertibility is established for service testing, laboratory fees, equipment imports, payroll, royalties, and other genuine trade or non-trade services, which may be settled through designated banks using transaction documentation without case-by-case SAFE approval. The capital account, however, remains subject to a substantive permission regime. Every equity injection or shareholder loan from the Hong Kong parent must be recorded in the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration system before the JV can open a foreign-exchange capital account or settle contributed funds into RMB. Once settled, RMB derived from the capital account is restricted to the registered operating purposes and cannot be diverted to equity investment, on-lending, or real-estate purchase. Dividend remittances to Hong Kong are legally convertible after tax declaration, annual audit, and tax clearance, but the bank will require proof of retained earnings, a board-approved dividend resolution, SAFE digital validation, and evidence that no transfer-pricing examination is pending. Strict surrender obligations no longer exist for current-account receipts, but all foreign-exchange proceeds from export testing and certification services must be repatriated through compliant banking channels and matched against BOP declarations; funds may be retained in an onshore foreign-exchange account or converted into RMB at the JV's discretion. Practical profit-repatriation frictions normally arise from bank-level document review, SAFE reporting cutoffs, tax clearance delays, and anti-avoidance scrutiny rather than from an absolute blockage.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since 2012, SAFE has abolished mandatory surrender of current-account foreign-exchange receipts in the PRC. Export test fees, certification income, and laboratory-service proceeds must be repatriated to a designated domestic bank account, generally within 90 days for service-trade receipts and within 180 days for merchandise export settlements if the transaction is tied to physical battery samples, unless a SAFE-approved extension arises from open-account payment terms. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the JV may retain 100 percent of qualifying current-account foreign-exchange receipts and use them for genuine operating needs, subject to bank validation, BOP reporting, and matching against customs or service-trade declarations.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Wise and other offshore digital EMI providers are legally unsuitable as the primary treasury or clearing layer for this mainland Chinese Sino-Foreign Equity JV. Under PBOC regulations on non-bank payment institutions and SAFE cross-border RMB administration, an offshore e-money institution is not authorised to open a domestic corporate settlement account, hold client RMB onshore, perform SAFE FDI registration, complete capital-account verification, or discharge tax-clearance-linked dividend remittance. Wise can act only as an ancillary payment initiator outside mainland China, and even then the receiving bank must be a licensed Tier-1 commercial bank with full compliance control. The commercially prudent classification is: Low viability for principal bank account, prohibited for capital injection, and acceptable only for low-value incidental fees if the JV obtains a prior written opinion from its Chinese relationship bank that the rail does not trigger PBOC or SAFE violations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital from the Hong Kong parent must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's SAFE-registered FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. The remittance must carry the SAFE-designated BOP purpose code for FDI equity contribution, commonly code 622011, and the bank detail must clearly state the name of the JV, the FDI registration number, and the joint-venture contract reference. The sending bank should be one with clean correspondent links to China, and the receiving bank must identify the underlying SAFE registration before accepting the credit. After receipt, the JV obtains a formal capital verification through a licensed Chinese CPA, which issues a paid-in-capital verification report evidencing that the injected amount matches the approved capital schedule. If the RMB settlement of the capital account is required, the conversion must occur through the domestic bank under the post-exchange-control capital settlement system, and the RMB must be credited to a separately supervised operating account. The JV must never use Wise, an offshore EMI, or any unlicensed payment aggregator for capital injections.Outbound treasury disbursements for equipment, consumables, subcontracting, and laboratory calibration services should be cleared through licensed domestic bank rails. Direct payments for services and routine trade should use SWIFT MT103 with full KYC-qualified beneficiary details and appropriate BOP transaction codes, supported by contract, invoice, service acceptance certificate, and where applicable customs declaration. For equipment purchases from overseas suppliers, a Commercial Letter of Credit is the preferred rail because it provides title and inspection protection while allowing the bank to verify transport documents, packing lists, and insurance certificates. Documentary Collections may be used only for low-risk repeat counterparties where no pre-shipment finance is needed and where the JV already has an established transaction history. Every outbound payment requires a SAFE current-account verification application and, for capital-account-related amortisation or repayments, a separate SAFE approval record. The commercially prudent classification is: Low viability for principal bank account, prohibited for capital injection, and acceptable only for low-value incidental fees if the JV obtains a prior written opinion from its Chinese relationship bank that the rail does not trigger PBOC or SAFE violations.
