Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic tax portal: eTAX (IRD's electronic tax filing and communication system, accessible through MyGovHK).

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong provides no separate national TIN; the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) serves as the statutory tax identification number for all Inland Revenue and profits tax purposes. A foreign non-resident entity that commences a business in Hong Kong, including via a joint venture company, must file an application for business registration with the Business Registration Office of the IRD within one month of commencement. The application must be accompanied by certified evidence of corporate existence in the home jurisdiction, certified passport or national ID copies of directors and ultimate beneficial owners, the Hong Kong registered office or care-of address, and a description of the business activities. The IRD issues a Business Registration Certificate and assigns a BRN upon approval. For non-resident entities that do not maintain a fixed place of business but nevertheless derive profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong, the IRD may open a taxpayer file and issue a Profits Tax Return; in such cases the non-resident entity must appoint a Hong Kong tax representative, usually a certified public accountant, to receive legal notices and correspond through the eTAX system. No VAT identification number is issued because Hong Kong does not levy VAT or GST.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No general VAT, GST, or sales and use tax exists in Hong Kong. Indirect taxation is limited to specific duties and fees under the Dutiable Commodities Ordinance (Cap. 109) and other special-purpose levies; there is no general consumption tax applicable to affiliate media buying or digital advertising services.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable - no VAT or GST registration threshold exists. However, all persons carrying on business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencement, irrespective of turnover; this Business Registration requirement is not an indirect tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable because Hong Kong has no VAT or GST. There is no 0% export VAT rate, no input tax refund mechanism, and no VAT export zero-rating claim within the Hong Kong SAR. If the entity moves physical goods through Hong Kong, it must comply with the non-tax customs declaration regime under the Import and Export Ordinance (Cap. 60) and the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60E). For income tax purposes, profits are assessable to Hong Kong profits tax only if they arise in or are derived from Hong Kong from a trade, business or profession carried on in Hong Kong under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); offshore export profits are not within the charge to tax when both the contract negotiation and the performance take place outside Hong Kong.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR maintains a fully convertible currency regime: HKD is freely exchangeable, no prior approval is required for current or capital account conversion, and no surrender or profit remittance restriction applies beyond standard AML/CFT verification. Mainland China, in contrast, has a managed float with strict capital account controls. Onshore current account items, including trade and services payments, are generally convertible after tax verification and authenticity review, while capital account items require NDRC/MOFCOM approval or filing plus SAFE registration. Profit remittance from a PRC entity to the HK JV or its shareholders requires audited financials, tax clearance, a board distribution resolution, and a SAFE-authorised bank FX purchase/remittance process. The HK treasury centre must therefore classify each flow as either an arm's length current account transaction with complete documentary support or a pre-approved capital account transaction. Equity injection from the PRC investor into the HK entity is an outward direct investment and must obtain SAFE ODI registration before remittance. Back-to-back payments without a genuine underlying transaction will be recharacterised as capital account flows and blocked. Thus the HK JV is freely convertible at the entity level, but cross-border RMB flows touching China remain subject to SAFE verification, registration, and taxation clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Hong Kong imposes no statutory surrender rule and no mandatory conversion ratio or timeline; export receipts and service revenue can be retained in HKD, USD, CNH, or any convertible currency. For mainland China, PRC exporters of goods must repatriate export proceeds into a designated current account foreign exchange account within 180 days of customs declaration (or 365 days with SAFE approval); the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%. The exporter may retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert voluntarily. Service export receipts and affiliate media revenue generated by the PRC parent/advertising operating company are also subject to repatriation and services-trade reporting, with no forced conversion to RMB.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as central banking authority for the Hong Kong SAR; State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for mainland China cross-border capital flows

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary low-value payout rail, Low as a primary corporate banking venue. Offshore EMIs such as Mercury, Payoneer, or Wise are not authorised institutions under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and cannot lawfully accept deposits or operate the principal settlement account of a HK JV. An EMI may provide cross-border payment services only if separately licensed as a Money Service Operator (Customs and Excise Department) or a Stored Value Facility holder (HKMA). An unlicensed offshore EMI is legally barred from servicing the entity as a bank, and using one for the main treasury account creates sanctions screening, auditability, and AML credibility issues. The correct blueprint is a Tier-1 HK bank account for clearing and liquidity, with fintech EMIs used only for small-value affiliate settlements through an HKMA-recognised licensed provider and supported by a full transaction ledger and counterparty due-diligence file.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

A capital injection from the PRC shareholder to the HK JV must follow the PRC ODI route: NDRC and MOFCOM filing/approval, SAFE overseas direct investment registration, and remittance through a SAFE-authorised PRC bank under BOP purpose code 621 (outward direct investment - equity) or 622 (capital increase). The payment narrative must reference the SAFE ODI registration number and the HK entity's identification. The HK receiving bank should be a Tier-1 institution with an active SWIFT and CHATS membership; the HK JV should open a capital escrow/multi-currency account and present the bank with the shareholders' capital contribution schedule and board resolution. Before the funds are moved to a general operating account, an accountant/auditor must issue a capital verification statement confirming the contribution. Shareholder loans are separate and must be documented in a written loan agreement; if they come from a PRC investor, they also require SAFE cross-border lending/loan registration and must respect arm's length pricing and term limits.Settlement for affiliate media buying should default to SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor telegraphic transfers. For high-value recurring media placements, a Commercial Letter of Credit or Demand Guarantee can be used where the publisher requires a bank undertaking. Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) are structurally permissible but inefficient for digital advertising and should be reserved for offline media contracts. HKD and USD clear on HKCHATS (RTGS), and FPS can be used for low-value HKD and CNH payments. RMB cross-border disbursements should be reported under the cross-border trade settlement framework with the underlying service contract and invoice retained. Every outbound payment must pass through the bank's sanctions filter and be matched to an approved counterparty KYC file before execution; a payment register should tie each MT103 reference to the relevant publisher invoice and commercial approval.Deliverable FX forwards on HKD/USD, USD/CNH, EUR/JPY, and other convertible pairs with HKMA-authorized banks under a documented commercial exposure; typical tenors 1-12 months, extendable to 24 months for contracted media campaigns.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on onshore RMB (USD/CNY) are permissible only where the HK JV has a real cross-border RMB exposure, such as anticipated dividend income from a PRC subsidiary or PRC media procurement; NDFs must be executed with a licensed bank under an ISDA Master Agreement and cannot be speculative.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) are permissible for matched debt and service contracts, converting HKD/USD liabilities into RMB exposure or fixing a cross-border funding profile; execution requires an ISDA/CSA, a licensed derivatives dealer under the Securities and Futures Ordinance, and, where the swap references PRC debt, proof that the underlying onshore loan is SAFE-registered and the swap does not create offshore deliverable CNY.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: Medium as a supplementary low-value payout rail, Low as a primary corporate banking venue. Offshore EMIs such as Mercury, Payoneer, or Wise are not authorised institutions under the Hong Kong Banking Ordinance and cannot lawfully accept deposits or operate the principal settlement account of a HK JV. An EMI may provide cross-border payment services only if separately licensed as a Money Service Operator (Customs and Excise Department) or a Stored Value Facility holder (HKMA). An unlicensed offshore EMI is legally barred from servicing the entity as a bank, and using one for the main treasury account creates sanctions screening, auditability, and AML credibility issues. The correct blueprint is a Tier-1 HK bank account for clearing and liquidity, with fintech EMIs used only for small-value affiliate settlements through an HKMA-recognised licensed provider and supported by a full transaction ledger and counterparty due-diligence file.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, the JV must prepare contemporaneous local-file documentation when related-party transactions exceed CNY 200 million for tangible asset transfers, CNY 100 million for intangible-asset transfers or financial-asset transfers, or CNY 40 million for related-party services; a master file is generally triggered for large groups with aggregate related-party transactions above approximately CNY 1 billion; annual related-party transaction reporting forms accompany the A100000

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies substance over form and GAAR under CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice [2009] No. 601; a Hong Kong shareholder claiming treaty benefits must have real business substance, not a bare corporate shell, and the core income-generating activities of the media-buying JV (ad inventory sourcing, campaign selection, pricing, risk management, lead trafficking and revenue reconciliation) must be performed by the PRC JV's employees and assets in China. Any HK affiliate receiving intercompany commissions must perform actual functions, bear real risk, and create verifiable value; otherwise treaty benefits are denied, profits are reallocated to China, and withholding tax is recalculated at the statutory rate.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Deduction through fictitious intercompany media purchases

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax; PRC Criminal Law Article 201: fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years where the evaded amount exceeds the criminal threshold and represents more than 10% of the tax due; SAT joint punishment blacklisting with travel and exit restrictions

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 and the revised Article 23 where a director or shareholder abuses the limited-liability structure through fictitious controlled-party contracts; directors who knowingly signed or authorized the false invoices may be personally and jointly and severally liable for civil asset restitution of the evaded tax, face travel bans and high-consumption blacklisting, and risk criminal imprisonment plus personal fines upon conviction for tax evasion
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Beneficial Ownership failure

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Public Notice [2009] No. 601 deny treaty benefits and require re-taxation of the dividends at the full 10% statutory WHT; late surcharges accrue from the original tax-payment date; if the structure was designed to evade payment, Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a 50% to 500% penalty; the Hong Kong corporate veil is disregarded for tax collection against the real beneficiaries

Director personal liability: Directors of both the HK conduit and the PRC JV who certified false beneficial-ownership documents may be held personally liable for the additional withholding tax, penalties and surcharges under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil; they face tax-blacklisting travel bans and high-consumption restrictions; and they may face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 for fraudulent tax avoidance or under Article 190 for unlawful round-tripping of foreign exchange
AML / Cross-Border Foreign Exchange and Withholding Failure

Statutory sanction: Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations Article 39 imposes a fine of at least 30% of the illegal foreign-exchange amount; PRC Criminal Law Article 190 criminalizes foreign-currency evasion and round-tripping with imprisonment of up to 5 years plus fines; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% of the tax that the JV failed to withhold or under-withheld

Director personal liability: Directors and senior managers who controlled the overseas payment rails are treated as directly responsible persons and may be ordered to make civil asset restitution of the unpaid taxes and FX penalties; courts may pierce the corporate veil where company funds were used to evade regulatory oversight; affected directors face exit bans, travel bans, and blacklisting; willful participants risk criminal imprisonment of up to 5 years for foreign-currency evasion and, if files and invoices are falsified, additional prison time under the tax crime provisions of the Criminal Law
