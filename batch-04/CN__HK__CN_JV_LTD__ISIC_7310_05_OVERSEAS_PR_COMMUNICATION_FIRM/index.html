Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the China Electronic Tax Bureau portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) for PRC tax registration and VAT; Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR via GovHK e-Tax portal (www.ird.gov.hk) for Hong Kong Business Registration and profits tax matters.

E-government filing portal

For the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture (EJV): the PRC Administration for Market Regulation issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) at incorporation under the one-stop integration reform; the USCC operates as the PRC Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and is automatically loaded into the STA taxpayer registration system. The EJV must then complete VAT taxpayer status determination, submit bank account and equity/legal representative particulars, and obtain its electronic taxpayer profile through the Electronic Tax Bureau in order to issue electronic VAT invoices and file VAT/income tax returns. A foreign non-resident enterprise with a PRC establishment must separately register for tax with the competent STA under the non-resident enterprise tax registration procedure and receive a PRC TIN; a foreign passive shareholder of the EJV normally does not require a separate TIN because the EJV discharges withholding through the STA system. In Hong Kong, if the same or associated group establishes a non-Hong Kong company branch, that branch must file with the Companies Registry under the Companies Ordinance and apply to the IRD Business Registration Office within one month of commencement to obtain a Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the Hong Kong tax reference number.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; no pass-through or transparent classification).

Available election forms

None for corporate income tax; PRC law does not provide check-the-box elections and Hong Kong has no equivalent election. The only relevant elective status is the VAT classification election: an EJV below the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold may elect general VAT taxpayer status instead of remaining a small-scale VAT taxpayer.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

PRC: tax/VAT registration is mandatory upon formation without any turnover threshold; the compulsory general VAT taxpayer classification threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales, below which the entity may remit VAT as a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general status. Hong Kong: not applicable because there is no VAT/GST/sales tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC cross-border service VAT regime, a 0% VAT rate may apply to eligible exported services, including certain internationally-oriented consulting, advertising, creative, and media-relations services supplied by the EJV to an offshore business recipient, provided the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the recipient's place of business is outside the PRC, the consideration is payable in foreign currency and repatriated to China, and the contract, invoice, and service-performance evidence clearly document the offshore consumption. Pure overseas PR and crisis-communication services can qualify only if they are not rendered to, or used within, the PRC and the STA confirms the service line falls within the zero-rated cross-border services catalogue. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, so no zero-rating claim is required for supplies made in or from Hong Kong.

  • PRC Customs Export Declaration (electronic goods declaration lodged through the China International Trade Single Window) for physical goods export shipments
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, together with commercial invoice and packing list, evidencing the physical export movement of goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued cross-border receipts declaration (SAFE-compliant) proving foreign-currency export proceeds were repatriated to China
  • Export service contract, global scope-of-work, deliverable evidence, and offshore-consumption declaration for zero-rated cross-border PR/communication services

PRC: electronic VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved electronic invoice service platform and transmitted in real time to the tax authority's VAT invoice management system; all-digital electronic invoices are authenticated using encrypted electronic signatures/anti-fraud controls and require no physical fiscal stamp. General VAT taxpayers must additionally authenticate special VAT invoices for input tax deduction. Hong Kong: no real-time fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate; ordinary invoices satisfy commercial law.

If the STA disallows export zero-rating due to false documentation, invoice mismatches, failure to establish offshore consumption, or failure to repatriate foreign-currency proceeds within the statutory window, the EJV is liable for the unpaid VAT at the applicable PRC rate (6% for services or 13% for goods), plus late-payment interest

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region operates as a separate monetary and customs territory under the Basic Law, with the Hong Kong Dollar freely convertible through the market. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority maintains the Linked Exchange Rate system within the 7.75 to 7.85 HKD per USD band, backed by the Exchange Fund. Both current account and capital account transactions are fully liberalised; there are no restrictions on the convertibility of HKD into foreign currencies, no surrender requirements for export proceeds, and no exchange controls on dividend, interest, royalty, or liquidation proceeds remitted to overseas shareholders. Profit repatriation from the Hong Kong to the PRC parent is straightforward at the Hong Kong clearing layer, requiring only standard bank beneficiary and purpose declarations. However, inbound capital injections from the PRC parent are treated as cross-border capital account remittances by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and must be transacted under a valid Overseas Direct Investment registration, with the documented share subscription or shareholder loan agreement. Once funds land in the Hong Kong corporate account, they are wholly outside SAFE's convertibility restrictions and may be deployed for global retainer payments and media buying. The HKD's de facto peg to the USD also gives treasury managers a stable base for hedging CNY exposure through active CNH markets, as there are no legal barriers to entering derivative contracts through licensed Hong Kong banks. Overall, the jurisdiction provides unrestricted current account convertibility, full capital account openness, and zero mandatory repatriation impediments for the JV's foreign exchange operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory export proceeds surrender rule exists in the Hong Kong SAR. Exporters and service providers are free to retain, convert, or disburse foreign currency receipts without any mandatory conversion timeline or ratio. The HKMA imposes no requirement on the percentage of export foreign exchange proceeds to be converted into Hong Kong Dollars, and there is no requirement to surrender export receipts to the Exchange Fund or any government authority. All export and retainer receipts may be settled directly through licensed banks under standard anti-money laundering declarations.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Medium. Hong Kong law does not bar a licensed corporate from using offshore digital EMI platforms for cross-border treasury operations, but these entities are not authorised banks under the Banking Ordinance and cannot hold statutory capital escrow accounts or issue capital verification documents acceptable to the HKMA and the PRC parent. An EMI must hold a valid Money Service Operator (MSO) license under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (AMLO) and must operate through Hong Kong-based settlement accounts; otherwise the cross-border flow lacks a regulated clearing channel. Offshore digital EMIs are therefore viable only as supplementary disbursement rails for low-value retainer payments and vendor settlement, not as primary inbound capital or outbound trade-finance rails. The structural recommendation is to maintain the primary treasury account with a Tier-1 licensed bank and restrict EMI usage to operational nimbleness where sanctions screening and compliance capacity have been independently validated.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from mainland China to the Hong Kong JV must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the PRC parent's designated foreign exchange bank to the beneficiary account held at a Hong Kong Tier-1 bank. The sending bank in mainland China will require the SAFE ODI registration reference and will label the remittance under the appropriate capital account foreign exchange purpose code for overseas direct investment; the Hong Kong receiving bank will request an inward remittance instruction, a copy of the capital subscription agreement or shareholder loan arrangement, and a declaration confirming the sender-beneficiary relationship. Upon crediting, the accountant must prepare a Capital Verification Report, after which the Hong Kong bank may issue a Capital Escrow Release Instruction or a Capital Injection Confirmation, enabling the funds to be swept into the operational currency account. The mainland China parent or foreign JV partner is generally not subject to PRC Corporate Income Tax on Hong Kong-source passive or active income unless effectively connected with a PRC establishment, subject to Controlled Foreign Company rules under PRC CIT)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% on dividends paid by Hong Kong to non-residents (no Hong Kong dividend withholding tax). For the PRC parent, received dividends from the Hong Kong JV are generally not subject to PRC withholding tax because the PRC parent is the taxpayer (not a payer). However, the PRC parent must include the dividend in its taxable income as foreign-source income; the underlying profits tax paid in Hong Kong (16.5% or 8.25% portion) is creditable against PRC CIT using the foreign tax credit mechanism, subject to PRC CIT law and State Taxation Administration (STA) rules. If the Hong Kong company is a 'low-taxed' non-resident entity controlled by a PRC resident enterprise, the PRC CFC rules under PRC CIT may currently be dormant but could be invoked if the profits are not distributed to a PRC resident enterprise; the Hong Kong company is generally treated as a non-CFC if it has substantive business operations and pays at least 15% effective tax. Since Hong Kong standard rate is 16.5%, the effective rate likely exceeds 15% for chargeable profits.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regime (ESR) under the unified (related) framework is not the same as the OECD 'substantial activities' requirement for no or only nominal tax jurisdictions. Since Hong Kong is a full-tax territory with a territorial tax system, it does not impose an Economic Substance Regulation similar to the UAE or BVI. However, foreign passive income (FSIE) exemption requires Hong Kong economic substance: for interest, dividends, disposal gains, and IP income, the taxpayer must carry on substantial economic activity in Hong Kong in relation to the income to claim exemption. For an overseas PR firm, the relevant core income-generating activities (CIGA) would be: (1) strategic planning and client management, (2) media relationship development, (3) crisis communication advisory, and (4) contract negotiation and invoicing. If the JV claims exemption from Hong Kong tax for offshore-source service fees (non-passive), the territorial source principle already requires the operations be outside Hong Kong; no statutory CIGA is expressly named. For any IP royalties received by the JV (if it licenses PR-related IP), the new IP tier applies, requiring nexus ratio. The JV must maintain employees, decision-making, and expenditure records in Hong Kong to meet the economic substance test if it claims FSIE. Under PRC law, the Chinese parent's investment in the Hong Kong JV is a foreign investment; the JV must have appropriate substance to avoid being characterized as a ‘shell company’ for PRC CFC or GAAR purposes.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Willful Falsification of Offshore Source Income Claim

Statutory sanction: Under section 82 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), evading profits tax by knowingly and willfully filing false returns without reasonable excuse is punishable by a fine of up to HKD 100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus a further penalty of treble the amount of tax undercharged. The IRD may additionally issue assessments under section 60 for additional tax and penalties. If the PRC parent is involved, the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid CIT and late payment surcharges.

Director personal liability: Criminal liability attaches to every director, manager, secretary or other officer of the company who knowingly and willfully authorizes, permits, or participates in the false filing. The court may pierce the corporate veil: the director’s personal assets can be used to satisfy the corporate tax penalty and restitution. A fraudulent filing will result in a conviction record, automatic disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years under the Companies Ordinance, and potential blacklisting by the Hong Kong Immigration Department for non-resident directors seeking travel permits. In serious cases, imprisonment of up to 3 years is imposed, and the PRC parent's directors may also be subject to criminal liability in mainland China under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law for failure to pay taxes or forming a tax evasion scheme.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement / Shell Company Abuse under the New FSIE Regime and OECD CFC Rules

Statutory sanction: Under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, section 61A (transaction defying commercial rationale) and section 61 (artificial or fictitious transactions) allow the IRD to disregard the arrangement, assess additional profits tax at 16.5%, and impose a penalty of up to 100% of the tax undercharged. If the transaction is deliberate tax evasion, criminal penalties under section 82A can apply. For cross-border transactions, the IRD may apply transfer pricing adjustments under Part 8A (sections 50AAA to 50AQZ) based on the arm's length principle and require recomputation; penalties are the same as for understatement. In mainland China, the State Taxation Administration may issue Special Tax Adjustment adjustments (补税) plus interest at China's benchmark rate, and a 15% penalty on underpaid tax under the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR).

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong JV who knowingly approved non-arm's length pricing or failed to maintain adequate substance can be held jointly and severally liable for the company's tax liabilities if the IRD finds that the company is merely an alter ego of the shareholder. The directors can be personally served with a 'blacklisting' order by the IRD under the Collection of Taxes provisions, which triggers travel restrictions for Hong Kong (leaving Hong Kong without paying taxes can be an offense). In mainland China, under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, the legal representative of the PRC parent can be prevented from exiting China (出境限制) by the tax authority for non-payment of taxes; criminal exposure for fraudulent export or false invoicing can lead to 7 years imprisonment for directors.
Anti-Money Laundering / Failure to Report Beneficial Ownership and Tax Leakage through Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Hong Kong: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) section 653, failure to keep beneficial ownership information is an offense with a fine of HKD 50,000 and a further daily fine of HKD 1,000 per day. Under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance, money laundering offenses carry up to 14 years imprisonment and fines of up to HKD 5 million. For tax evasion using shell intermediaries, the IRD may invoke the DTA article and the inland revenue anti-abuse doctrines. PRC: the PRC parent's failure to file the annual CIT return including foreign-source dividend income triggers a 50%-500% penalty on the underpaid CIT (25% × dividend), plus interest. If the secretary or director is aware of the sham dividend routing, they can be subject to criminal liability for the crime of tax evasion (imprisonment up to 7 years) and fraud in foreign exchange settlements (up to 10 years).

Director personal liability: Under Hong Kong company law, every director who knowingly and willfully permits the company to file false beneficial ownership declarations is personally liable to a fine and imprisonment. The corporate veil is pierced if the company is used for tax evasion: the court can order the director to repay the total tax loss from personal funds. Non-resident directors (e.g., PRC nationals) may be blacklisted by the Hong Kong Immigration Department from entering Hong Kong until the tax debt is paid. In mainland China, the director of the PRC parent may be barred from leaving mainland China under the 'Restriction on Exit' mechanism for tax debts exceeding 10,000 RMB, and face criminal detention for up to 7 years if using a foreign shell to conceal taxable income. The Hong Kong financial secretary can also apply to the High Court to disqualify directors for up to 15 years on grounds of insider dealings or breaches of the Companies Ordinance.
