Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD). Electronic registration and filing portal: IRD eTax (https://www.etax.ird.gov.hk) and GovHK (https://www.gov.hk).

        
E-government filing portal
A foreign non-resident entity commencing business in Hong Kong must apply for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) within one month of commencing business. The application is made to the IRD Business Registration Office, either through the IRD eTax portal or in written form. The Business Registration Certificate contains the Business Registration Number (BRN), which is the statutory taxpayer identifier for Hong Kong profits tax, salaries tax filing, and tax administration generally. A non-Hong Kong company must first register as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) with the Companies Registry and obtain a Certificate of Registration of Non-Hong Kong Company; it must then complete business registration with the IRD. There is no separate national TIN issuance mechanism in Hong Kong; the BRN is the operative identifier.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax and therefore no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold. Any person carrying on a business in Hong Kong must obtain a Business Registration Certificate immediately upon commencement of business under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The Hong Kong Special Administrative Region maintains a fully convertible HKD under the linked exchange rate system, with no foreign exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and no restriction on current-account or capital-account transactions for locally incorporated companies. HKMA supervises authorized institutions but does not ration foreign currency, so settlement may be freely executed in HKD, USD, EUR, RMB (CNH) or other major currencies. The principal convertibility friction lies at the boundary with mainland China: a PRC corporate shareholder injecting capital into a Hong Kong joint venture must obtain SAFE outbound direct investment (ODI) registration and approval before remittance, and any onshore RMB conversion into HKD/USD must observe SAFE purpose codes and genuine transaction documentation. Profit remittance from the Hong Kong JV to a PRC parent is a dividend distribution that requires no exchange-control approval in Hong Kong, but the receiving PRC entity must complete the original ODI filing and annual foreign exchange inspection so that the dividend can be credited through SAFE. The practical hurdle in this structure is therefore AML documentation rather than convertibility denial: banks will require economic substance evidence, source of wealth, and audited financial statements to distinguish bona fide dividends and service revenue from disguised cross-border capital movements.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Hong Kong imposes no statutory export proceeds surrender requirement, no mandatory conversion ratio, and no holding-period timeline. A Hong Kong company may retain 100% of foreign-currency export receipts in any HKMA-authorized bank and may convert them freely at prevailing market rates. Mainland Chinese subsidiaries remain separately subject to SAFE current-account administration in relation to their own export receipts.

      

      
Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) (with the People's Bank of China and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange regulating Mainland Chinese cross-border capital flows)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Stripe
            Fit: High
          

          
Wise, as a licensed Hong Kong Money Service Operator / Stored Value Facility provider, is highly feasible for secondary cross-border subscription collection and low-value disbursements, but it is not an Authorized Institution under the HKMA Banking Ordinance and cannot replace a Tier-1 bank for capital escrow, foreign exchange hedging, or professional KYC underwriting. Stripe Billing is a payment facilitator rather than an EMI, so it can collect recurring design subscription fees but must route net proceeds into a licensed bank account and does not provide the entity with a regulated settlement account or deposit protection. Neither provider is legally barred in Hong Kong, but banks will require visibility of all underlying payment rails during the AML review; therefore, the recommended treasury architecture is a Tier-1 corporate account as the primary liquidity container, with Wise and Stripe as auxiliary inbound collection rails only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: HKD 5,000,000 - A Local File must be maintained by the Hong Kong joint venture when related-party controlled transactions (goods, services, royalties, financial arrangements) exceed HKD 5 million in the basis period; Master File is required where the group's consolidated annual revenue exceeds HKD 6.8 billion; Country-by-Country Report threshold also HKD 6.8 billion (aligned with OECD minimum standard).

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has not enacted a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) law similar to offshore jurisdictions; instead, the economic-substance principle is embedded in the territorial source tax rule and the anti-abuse provisions of Sections 61A and 61B of the Inland Revenue Ordinance. A company claiming Hong Kong source profits for subscription-based UI/UX design income must demonstrate substantive Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including: (i) local contracting and subscription billing, (ii) local project management and design control, (iii) strategic decision-making by Hong Kong directors, and (iv) utilization of Hong Kong personnel for client-facing interactions. The Chinese parent's and Hong Kong JV's transfer-pricing documentation must allocate profits to the functions actually performed, with adequate payroll and office presence; otherwise, the IRD will adjust the profits and impute them to the entity bearing the economic risk.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Wilful tax evasion through false Profits Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: Under Section 82 Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112), the defendant is liable on summary conviction to a fine of HKD 50,000 plus treble the amount of tax undercharged, and to imprisonment for three years; in addition, on indictment, a further penalty of HKD 50,000 and imprisonment for three years apply.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign the return or cause its submission can be prosecuted as principal offenders. Courts will pierce the corporate veil where the JV is a façade: directors may owe restitution of the underpaid tax plus all fines, face a departure prohibition order (blacklisting) preventing travel out of Hong Kong, be subject to asset-freezing injunctions, and be disqualified under the Companies Ordinance, with criminal imprisonment recommended.

      

      
      

        Transfer pricing manipulation / aggressive profit stripping
        
Statutory sanction: Section 50AA and Section 61A of the IRO authorize the IRD to recharacterize or disregard the arrangement and impose the greater of the tax that would have been payable plus penalties. Failure to prepare timely transfer-pricing documentation can lead to an additional penalty under Section 51(5) of up to 100% of the tax adjustment; in cases of fraudulent evasion, Section 82 penalties (treble tax plus imprisonment) apply.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who instruct or approve off-market pricing are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and surcharges if they signed inaccurate group accounts. Civil restitution claims may be brought by the IRD; directors may be placed on the departure-ban blacklist where the unpaid tax exceeds HKD 100,000; and a criminal prosecution for conspiracy to cheat the revenue may carry imprisonment up to 7 years under the common law and the Theft Ordinance.

      

      
      

        Shell-company conduit without economic substance for DTA benefits
        
Statutory sanction: Under Section 61B IRO (DTA anti-abuse) and the doctrine of beneficial ownership, the IRD may deny the treaty benefit, impose profits tax at 16.5% plus a penalty of 10% if the adjustment is due to fraud or neglect, and may cancel the company's tax registration. The Chinese tax authorities may also tax the conduit at the full 10% dividend WHT in China, resulting in double taxation.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced if the JV is a mere instrumentality; directors and ultimate beneficial owners face civil liability to compensate the Revenue for tax lost, a travel ban/blacklisting under Rule 21 of the Inland Revenue (Hearing of Objections) Rules (departure prohibition), personal bankruptcy proceedings, and criminal liability for money laundering under the Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance with imprisonment up to 14 years.
