Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region; electronic portal: GovHK eTAX, https://www.etax.gov.hk; official IRD site: https://www.ird.gov.hk.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a separate VAT/TIN. The statutory tax identifier for companies is the Business Registration Number (BRN) issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). A foreign non-resident company that establishes a place of business in Hong Kong must first register as a non-Hong Kong company under Part 16 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) with the Companies Registry within one month of establishing the place of business, filing the prescribed documents including certified copy of incorporation, constitutional documents, directors and company secretary details, proposed Hong Kong registered office address, and appointed authorized representative. After the Companies Registry issues the certificate of registration, or if the entity commences business in Hong Kong through a branch, the entity must submit the prescribed business registration application to the IRD Business Registration Office, either in paper form or via eTAX, within one month of commencement of business. The IRD then issues a Business Registration Certificate displaying the BRN, which must be displayed at the principal place of business and used for Profits Tax filings, employer returns, and all IRD correspondence. A foreign non-resident entity with no physical place of business in Hong Kong but with Hong Kong-source income is not automatically required to obtain a BRN, but the IRD may open a tax file and issue an assessment/file number once the entity becomes chargeable to Profits Tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The entity operates as a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture domiciled in Hong Kong SAR with commercial substance in Mainland China under ISIC 7420. The dual-jurisdiction structure creates a differentiated convertibility regime. For Mainland China, the RMB is convertible for current account items such as trade in services and profit remittances under SAFE rules, but capital account transactions including direct investment injections, equity transfers, and external borrowing require registration, approval, or filing through the SAFE system. Export proceeds derived from stock photography licensing must be repatriated and surrendered for settlement or retained in foreign currency within the statutory time limits depending on the status of the entity as an onshore or offshore enterprise. For Hong Kong SAR, the HKMA maintains a fully liberalized capital account with no exchange controls, no surrender requirements, and free convertibility of the Hong Kong dollar and other currencies. The main hurdle for profit repatriation from the Mainland operating subsidiary to the Hong Kong JV parent is the requirement for audited financial statements, corporate income tax clearance, and SAFE registration for profit remittance to non-resident shareholders. Dividend remittances are permitted after paying the 10% withholding tax (reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong double tax arrangement if the Hong Kong shareholder qualifies as the beneficial owner). The joint venture must demonstrate economic substance in Hong Kong to benefit from treaty protection and avoid being characterized as a conduit arrangement. For the JV's online commercial stock photo platform, cross-border payment flows involving licensing fees from global customers, royalty payments to content creators, and settlement through Stripe and PayPal require careful classification under the SAFE Foreign Exchange Code for Trade in Services, specifically under code 229010 for royalty and license fees, with supporting contracts and invoices. The capital account convertibility assessment remains cautious because any incremental injection of equity capital from the Hong Kong parent to the Mainland onshore operation requires SAFE Foreign-Invested Enterprise (FIE) registration and capital verification through a designated domestic bank. Profit remittance ceilings are not absolute but are subject to substance review, tax clearance, and the absence of foreign exchange arrears. Overall, the regime is best described as partially convertible current account for China, fully convertible for Hong Kong, with compliance obligations requiring dual-banking coordination.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Mainland China export of services under the stock photo licensing model, SAFE mandates that export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of receipt unless an extended retention period is approved by the local SAFE branch. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for current account receipts as China eliminated the compulsory surrender requirement for enterprises; entities may retain 100% of export proceeds in foreign currency accounts at designated banks. However, the funds must be credited to the FIE's foreign currency settlement account and may be converted to RMB for domestic payment needs under the general current account convertibility regime. For Hong Kong SAR entities, there is no statutory surrender ratio and no repatriation timeline; residents may freely hold and dispose of foreign currency.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBC), with Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central banking authority for Hong Kong SAR

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe Treasury-based banking partners, Airwallex, and Currenxie are viable for collection side operations and cross-border payouts in Hong Kong SAR under the HKMA's Open API and virtual banking framework, provided they hold a valid Money Service Operator (MSO) license or an Authorized Institution license from the HKMA. However, for the Mainland China leg of the structured entity, these fintech EMIs are legally barred from servicing onshore corporate accounts, maintaining RMB settlement accounts, or conducting foreign exchange conversion for Chinese resident entities unless they obtain a SAFE foreign exchange business license and qualifying market entry as a foreign-funded bank or a licensed payment institution under PBOC. Since the target payment rails are Stripe and PayPal, the practical recommendation is to use the Hong Kong JV entity as the principal platform operating entity with an EMI-based multi-currency collection layer, and to route all onshore RMB settlements through the designated Chinese tier-1 bank accounts of the Mainland operating subsidiary. The fintech EMI cannot serve as the Capital Escrow Account custodian for the FIE capital injection because SAFE requires a domestic bank to verify capital receipts. The viability rating is therefore Medium for cross-border disbursement and Low for onshore Mainland banking compliance.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Hong Kong transfer pricing documentation (Local File and Master File) is required for tax years beginning on or after 1 April 2018 when total turnover exceeds HK$200 million and related-party transactions exceed the de minimis thresholds prescribed in DIPN 58/59 (generally HK$20 million for financial, intangible, and property transactions; HK$40 million for goods and services). Country-by-Country Report (CbCR) is required for multinational groups with consolidated revenue of at least HK$6.8 billion.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies the OECD substance requirements through source rules and the 'significant people functions' analysis. A company is tax resident in Hong Kong only if central management and control is exercised in Hong Kong. For DTA relief, the IRD issues a Tax Residency Certificate only after confirming economic substance (real office, local staff, board meetings, decision-making in Hong Kong). For offshore income claims, the taxpayer must demonstrate that core income-generating activities (CIGA) are performed outside Hong Kong. There is no separate ESR law as in the UAE, but shell companies without substance will be attacked by the general anti-avoidance rules (s.61 and s.61A) and the Principal Purpose Test in DTAs.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion - Fraudulent Offshore Income Declaration

Statutory sanction: Contravenes Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82(1) (fraudulent evasion of tax). The court may order restitution of tax losses, asset seizure, travel bans, and disqualification from acting as a director for up to 15 years. Statutory sanction: Under IRO s.80(2) and s.80A, failure to furnish information without reasonable excuse is an offence punishable by a fine of up to HK$50,000 and imprisonment up to 6 months, with a daily penalty of HK$600 for continuing non-compliance. Director personal liability: Directors responsible for tax and finance can be held personally liable as managing officers.
