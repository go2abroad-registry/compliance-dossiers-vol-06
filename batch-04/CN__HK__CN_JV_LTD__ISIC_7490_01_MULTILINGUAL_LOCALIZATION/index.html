None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up share capital for a private company limited by shares. The subscribers may declare HKD 1.00 nominal capital or any higher amount, and the issued shares may remain unpaid until the directors make a formal call. No blocked escrow account or capital verification certificate is required by the Companies Registry.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) for mainland China, transacting online via the Nationwide Electronic Tax Administration Platform (National e-Tax); for Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD), transacting through the eTAX portal and the Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture incorporates through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receives a Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number in China. Step 2: Under the integrated multi-certificate registration scheme, SAMR automatically feeds the USCC record to the STA, and the JV completes tax registration on the Nationwide Electronic Tax Administration Platform by real-name authentication, selection of the competent tax bureau, and creation of tax filing profiles. Step 3: The STA assigns the VAT taxpayer classification (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer) based on the annual taxable sales estimate; the JV then applies for VAT invoice issuance qualifications and electronic invoicing quotas. Step 4: For any non-resident foreign seller without a domestic establishment or business site in China, the domestic purchaser acts as VAT withholding agent; the non-resident may separately apply to the STA for a one-off tax identification number for refund or treaty benefit applications. Step 5: If the JV also maintains operations in Hong Kong SAR, the Hong Kong entity or branch must notify the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), obtain a Business Registration Certificate, and use the Business Registration Number as its Hong Kong taxpayer identifier; profits tax returns are filed through eTAX under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112).

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: the JV is a PRC-resident enterprise under Article 2 of the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law and is taxed on worldwide income at a 25% corporate income tax rate; no pass-through eligibility exists. Hong Kong territorial-source principles apply only to a separately registered Hong Kong branch or Hong Kong-incorporated affiliate: profits sourced in Hong Kong are taxed at a 16.5% profits tax rate, while offshore-sourced profits are generally outside the Hong Kong tax net.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; China does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election. The only administrative election is the VAT small-scale taxpayer simplified collection method at a 3% collection rate instead of the general VAT method, but the election is lost once annual taxable sales reach CNY 5,000,000. Hong Kong: no check-the-box election; an offshore profits claim is a factual territorial-source exemption rather than a statutory election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No de minimis threshold for a non-resident supplier; VAT registration is compulsory from the first taxable supply in China. A PRC resident JV is registered through the integrated business license/tax registration process; annual taxable sales of CNY 5,000,000 or more trigger mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. Below this amount, the JV may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer but may elect general taxpayer status if it meets accounting and invoice-control conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from mainland China qualify for VAT zero-rating and input VAT refund where the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, the goods are declared to and cleared by China Customs under an export customs declaration, and export proceeds are collected through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade-in-goods monitoring system. Cross-border services, including multilingual translation, localization, and media dubbing, qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption under Caishui 2016 No. 36 Annex 4 and the STA cross-border taxable service rules where the services are supplied to a non-resident enterprise or individual, consumed wholly outside mainland China, delivered and used offshore, and paid for from outside China, with funds repatriated to the Chinese JV's bank account.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration in the export-tax-refund electronic data link through the China International Trade Single Window and China Customs export declaration system
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract signed with the overseas customer
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international transport waybill evidencing physical movement of goods out of mainland China
  • Foreign exchange collection evidence consisting of bank inbound payment notices or SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System records evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • VAT invoice copies and corresponding purchase-side input VAT invoices for goods or services consumed in the exported supply
  • Export tax rebate declaration sheet and reconciliation package submitted paperlessly to STA
  • For cross-border services, substitute the customs bundle with the service delivery package: signed localization, translation, or dubbing order, overseas client delivery confirmation, offshore media or publication release evidence, and overseas payment receipt or cross-border RMB settlement record

China enforces electronic invoicing and real-time tax control under the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. The fully digitalized electronic invoice, known as the all-digital e-invoice, is the current standard; each invoice is validated by the STA data center with a unique 20-digit invoice number, anti-counterfeiting QR code, and a dynamic invoice issuance quota. Invoice data must be transmitted to the STA concurrently with issuance; no physical fiscal stamp is required for all-digital e-invoices, while paper VAT invoices continue to require the special invoice seal. Hong Kong imposes no electronic invoicing clearance mandate for VAT, but IRD requires the retention of underlying business records for profits tax assessment.

The STA verifies every zero-ratng claim against China Customs export data and SAFE foreign exchange receipt data. If a zero-rating claim is fraudulent, unsupported, or the export proceeds are never repatriated, the tax authority will disallow the refund, recover all VAT refunded, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the outstanding amount, and assess a penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Intentional false declaration for export tax refund constitutes a criminal offense under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, punishable by imprisonment up to life in severe cases. The clawback assessment period is generally three to ten years depending on the degree of concealment, and responsible legal representatives, financial officers, and engaged tax agents may bear joint liability.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong is a free convertibility jurisdiction: no Foreign Exchange Control Ordinance exists, and the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate System keeps the HKD within a narrow band against the USD. Current-account receipts from localization services can be freely credited to HKD or foreign-currency accounts; capital-account contributions can be injected into the JV without exchange control approval in Hong Kong. The practical constraint is upstream PRC control: if the CN parent subscribes capital or lends funds to the HK entity, the remittance must pass SAFE's ODI registration framework and the China side must use a designated corporate bank to deal in foreign exchange. Profit distributions from the HK JV to the PRC parent are not subject to Hong Kong withholding tax on dividends and can flow out freely, but the PRC parent must report the inward remittance under SAFE regulations and comply with PRC tax on foreign-sourced income. Estoppel certificates and source-of-funds declarations are expected by HK banks; however, there is no surrender obligation, no conversion cap, and no repatriation licence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Nil for Hong Kong: no statutory surrender or conversion obligation is imposed on export receipts held by an HKSAR entity. If the underlying goods are exported by the PRC parent, the PRC company must repatriate the foreign-exchange receipts recorded in the customs trade monitoring system within 90 days of the export declaration date; after repatriation the company may either retain the currency in a settlement/foreign-exchange account or settle it into RMB under SAFE's goods-trade foreign-exchange administration rules, with no fixed mandatory conversion ratio.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); with cross-border relevance of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China for capital movements between the PRC parent and the HKSAR entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low-viability as sole banking venue; High as ancillary collection rail. Under Hong Kong law, Wise and Payoneer are not authorized banking institutions; they operate as licensed Money Service Operators under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance and are outside the Deposit Protection Scheme. They cannot accept escrowed deposits, cannot provide credit lines, and cannot replace a licensed bank for capital verification or FX hedging. They may be used only to receive localization service fees from smaller overseas clients, subject to their own risk limits and counterparty due diligence. HKMA-regulated banks remain mandatory for capital injection, custody of paid-up capital, statutory corporate treasury functions, and any form of borrowing or derivative execution.

For the HK JV, shareholders remit equity by SWIFT MT103 from their corporate bank; HKD and RMB local clearing may also use CHATS (Clearing House Automated Transfer System) for intra-Hong Kong funds. Each payment must bear the reference 'JV paid-up capital', a share allotment resolution, and, if originating from the PRC parent, the SAFE ODI registration and tax identification numbers. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low-viability as sole banking venue; High as ancillary collection rail. Under Hong Kong law, Wise and Payoneer are not authorized banking institutions; they operate as licensed Money Service Operators under the Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance and are outside the Deposit Protection Scheme. They cannot accept escrowed deposits, cannot provide credit lines, and cannot replace a licensed bank for capital verification or FX hedging. They may be used only to receive localization service fees from smaller overseas clients, subject to their own risk limits and counterparty due diligence. HKMA-regulated banks remain mandatory for capital injection, custody of paid-up capital, statutory corporate treasury functions, and any form of borrowing or derivative execution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory where any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds is met: tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000; financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000; intangible-property or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000. A Master File is generally required when annual consolidated turnover exceeds RMB 1,000,000,000 and the entity engages in cross-border related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: The PRC JV is not governed by the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations (ESR). However, it must maintain actual localization personnel, client-facing contracts, technical resources, and risk controls in mainland China. The Hong Kong shareholder claiming treaty benefits must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including management, execution, invoicing, and booking of localization-agency transactions. Lack of CIGA will trigger the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule (CIT Law Article 47) and DTA abuse recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell company and DTA treaty-shopping abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority will deny treaty benefits under CIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), Article 121 of the Implementation Regulations, and the China-Hong Kong DTA provision for arrangement abuse. The JV is reassessed for the 5% WHT difference plus daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge and TCAL Article 63 penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. The shell may also be listed as a tax-debtor risk entity.

Director personal liability: Directors who formed or controlled the Hong Kong shell for the purpose of avoiding PRC WHT may have the corporate veil pierced, making them jointly liable for the reassessed tax, interest, and penalties. Exit-control/blacklisting travel bans may be issued under TCAL Article 44. If the scheme is intentional and evades tax above the criminal threshold, directors face imprisonment up to 7 years under PRC Criminal Law Article 201.
Cross-border tax evasion and underreporting of offshore receipts

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63 treats this as tax evasion by false declaration: the taxpayer must pay overdue tax plus a penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid amount and daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%. If false invoices are generated, the invoice administration rules add separate fines up to RMB 100,000 and possible suspension of invoices. If the concealed tax is large, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 applies.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed offshore account collections and false invoicing are subject to piercing of the corporate veil; their personal bank accounts and equity can be restrained, and they may be ordered to restore understated amounts. The tax authority may issue a travel ban/blacklisting order preventing exit from China until the tax is paid. Knowingly filing false localization returns can result in up to 7 years' imprisonment and personal fine for each responsible director.
Transfer pricing manipulation and failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may adjust the related-party price under CIT Law Article 41 and impose special tax adjustment interest calculated at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points for missing contemporaneous documentation under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 and related anti-avoidance rules. Disallowed expenses increase taxable income, creating additional CIT exposure, and failure to provide TP documents can be penalized under TCAL Article 62 (up to RMB 10,000 for each missing document set) and Article 63 if the concealment is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved and signed the deficient transfer-pricing documentation and intercompany agreements may be held liable if the JV cannot pay the adjusted tax; personal assets can be traced, and exit bans can be imposed for unpaid special-tax-adjustment liabilities. Where a director intentionally fabricates benchmarking reports or false service contracts, criminal liability as an accomplice to tax evasion includes imprisonment and monetary fines.
