Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Whether a virtual office or commercial mailbox is acceptable depends on the intended function. The Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) requires a Hong Kong registered office where legal documents may be served and statutory records may be inspected; a registered agent's address can satisfy this bare statutory rule. However, for a MedTech FDA/CE regulatory consulting joint venture, the Inland Revenue Department and banks will generally reject a pure virtual mailbox as evidence of economic substance. A real physical Hong Kong office or serviced office with a street-level address, local staff presence, access to original records, company chop custody, and client-facing operations is required for business registration renewal, corporate bank account opening, and any tax residence claim. A virtual office may be used only as a supplementary administrative address, never as the sole registered office or claimed place of business for IRD and banking purposes.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the national Electronic Tax Collection System e-tax portal; Hong Kong SAR is a separate tax jurisdiction administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via the GovHK eTax portal.

E-government filing portal

For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the foreign investor must first incorporate the JV as a limited liability company under the PRC Company Law and register with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the mandatory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier; China maintains no separate VAT ID. Within 30 calendar days of the SAMR business license, the JV must file tax registration in the STA Electronic Tax Collection System by submitting the foreign investment information report, articles of association, joint venture contract, bank account certificate, registered address proof, and legal representative identification. A foreign non-resident enterprise that does not have an establishment in mainland China should not attempt to obtain a mainland USCC; its Chinese client is the statutory withholding agent for VAT and enterprise income tax on China-source service fees, and the non-resident may file a tax treaty benefit application under the applicable double taxation agreement. The Hong Kong investor or parent must separately hold an active Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate from the Inland Revenue Department under the Business Registration Ordinance and use the Business Registration Number as the Hong Kong tax identification number, with all ongoing correspondence handled through GovHK eTax.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the mandatory threshold above which the entity must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below CNY 5,000,000 the JV may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Hong Kong has no indirect tax registration threshold because no VAT or GST is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The JV may claim a 0% VAT rate for qualifying cross-border supplies only if the recipient is located outside the customs territory of China, the services are supplied offshore or wholly consumed outside China, the consideration is received through a designated foreign exchange bank and repatriated or settled within the required window, and contemporaneous contracts, DEI invoices, service completion records, and bank remittance advices evidence the export. Physical goods exports additionally require actual exit from PRC customs territory via a lawful outward declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic outward goods declaration generated by the China International Trade Single Window and accepted by PRC customs)
  • Ocean bill of lading or equivalent multimodal/air transport document evidencing physical export of goods
  • Foreign exchange receipt confirmation issued by the designated settlement bank under the SAFE international receipts and payments reporting system
  • Fully digitalized electronic invoice (DEI) issued by the JV showing the zero-rated export supply
  • Service contract, offshore consumption statement, and completion evidence for qualifying cross-border regulatory consulting services

China mandates real-time electronic invoicing through the nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice (DEI) system and the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. VAT invoices must be generated through STA-approved channels, transmitted to the STA invoice service platform via tax control equipment or the official API, carry a digitally signed tax serial number, and be validated by the national invoice verification system before the recipient may take an input VAT credit. Hong Kong, not being a VAT jurisdiction, imposes no e-invoice clearance, no fiscal digital stamping, and no anti-fraud electronic invoicing system.

The STA may audit any zero-rating claim by matching DEI output, customs clearance data, bank foreign exchange receipts, and contract records. If the export substantiation bundle is incomplete or the transaction is recharacterised as domestic, the tax authority will reverse the 0% rate, impose VAT at the applicable domestic rate (standard 13% or reduced 6% for consulting services), recover any output VAT underpaid and input VAT refunded, charge daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05% under the PRC Tax Administration Law, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Fraudulent export refund claims may additionally constitute the criminal offence of false export tax refund under the PRC Criminal Law. In Hong Kong, because no VAT refund system exists, audit risk is limited to profits tax and false-claim penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, including fines and imprisonment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a hybrid convertibility system. Current account transactions, including intangible service fees, royalties, consultancy payments, and trade-related disbursements, are largely freely convertible once the underlying business is authenticated and the payment complies with SAFE's filing or verification frameworks. Capital account transactions, however, remain subject to registration and case-by-case approval. A Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture in the medical device regulatory consulting sector will primarily require current account settlement for its service revenues and operational expenses but must also obtain SAFE registration for foreign direct investment capital injection and profit repatriation. Export proceeds, if any, must be repatriated to China and may be retained in designated foreign exchange settlement accounts or converted into RMB at spot rates, depending on the corporate banking policy and SAFE's management of foreign exchange positions. Profit remittance for a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is permitted after the completion of statutory annual audit, settlement of PRC corporate income tax, and approval by the board of directors, accompanied by a SAFE registration officer sign-off, a dividend distribution resolution, and a CPA tax compliance certificate. Practical hurdles include reconciliation of expenditure authenticity, need for an enterprise to keep its capital account separate from current account, documentation of the underlying business reason for large remittances, and strict look-through scrutiny by the commercial bank executing the payment. These controls demand an honest and well-sourced document trail rather than simply accepting a customer's statement of purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods trade export proceeds must be repatriated into the PRC through a bank within 90 days after customs export date unless a deferred payment arrangement is registered with SAFE. No mandatory percentage surrender is currently imposed; an enterprise may retain the full amount of approved foreign exchange in a settlement account or voluntarily sell it into RMB. For service trade receipts, the funds must be repatriated within the statutory verification timeframe and matched against the service trade declaration; conversion ratio is at the entity's business discretion subject to bank scrutiny.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the domestic operating entity. PRC law requires all corporate bank accounts, RMB and foreign currency, to be held with locally licensed commercial banks that have SAFE-approved cross-border FX business licenses. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., UK, EU, or HK-licensed fintech firms) are not permitted to hold accounts for tax- and bank-regulated onshore PRC entities. An offshore digital EMI can only be used by the Hong Kong holding company in its own right for treasury concentration, although any intra-group funding into the Chinese JV must land through the formal FDI channel using a mainland bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must first complete SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration with the local branch of SAFE or through the designated PRC bank. The JV must open a dedicated Foreign Investment Foreign Exchange Capital Account with a local Tier-1 bank and instruct the Hong Kong parent to remit the funded monetary amount via SWIFT MT103 from a corporate bank account bearing the parent's name. The payment must carry the authorized SAFE transaction category for foreign direct investment capital injection, as defined in the bank's cross-border balance-of-payments reporting scheme, and must explicitly state 'capital contribution to a foreign-invested enterprise' in the remittance narrative. The bank will tie the remittance to the FDI registration certificate and the JV business license, conduct the admission by means of payment verification, convert the incoming foreign currency into RMB at the bank's spot buying rate, or hold it in the foreign exchange capital account as permitted. After disbursement, the JV's appointed CPA must issue a capital contribution verification report to prove the paid-in capital has been fully settled in the company's bank account.For outbound service trade payments such as FDA 510(k) filing fees, CE marking technical assessments, and consultant invoices, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 sent from the JV's foreign exchange settlement account or RMB basic account through Bank of China or ICBC. The payment instruction must be matched against valid commercial invoices, contracting agreement, and be accompanied by the tax withholding statement if the payment is subject to PRC foreign enterprise withholding tax. For larger equipment purchases related to regulatory lab or design work, Commercial Letters of Credit or Documentary Collections may be used, through the bank's trade services department, with the relevant bill of lading and inspection certificates. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong-incorporated company must have its financial statements audited by an independent certified public accountant (practising) unless it qualifies for audit exemption as a small private company, a small guarantee company, or a dormant private company. A private company qualifies as a small private company for exemption if it is not a member of a group or, if it is a member of a group, the entire group qualifies as a small group, and it meets at least two of the following three quantitative thresholds for the financial year: (a) total turnover or revenue does not exceed HK$20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,103); (b) total balance-sheet assets do not exceed HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,051); (c) the average number of employees during the financial year does not exceed 20. A small guarantee company is measured on the same revenue, asset and employee thresholds. The exemption is automatically unavailable if any shareholder or member holding at least 5% of the voting rights serves a written notice requiring an audit; if the company or a group member is a listed or public-interest entity; if the company is part of a group that is not a small group; or if the entity has ceased dormant-company status. Once the entity fails the small-company exemption because at least two of the thresholds are exceeded, or because of any disqualifying condition, a mandatory independent audit must be performed by an approved CPA under Hong Kong Standards on Auditing (HKSAs). A dormant private company may apply to the Companies Registry using Form NDR1 and is exempt from audit and from preparing financial statements while dormant, but loses the exemption when any significant accounting transaction occurs.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend WHT under PRC EIT Law Article 37 for non-resident enterprises without a PE; reduced to 5% under Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement Article 10 if the HK beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the JV's paid-up capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when any single related-party transaction class exceeds the following annual thresholds: tangible asset transfers > RMB 200 million; intangible assets or financial asset transfers > RMB 100 million; intercompany services and other related-party transactions > RMB 40 million. Master File required if the group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and the group engages in related-party transactions in two or more jurisdictions, or if the taxpayer's cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not have Hong Kong-style Economic Substance Regulations, the PRC GAAR under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Bulletin 9/2018 sponsor a functional and substance analysis. The JV must show that it performs core income-generating activities (CIGA), bears economic risks, owns and controls the intangibles it uses, and makes strategic decisions in China. The HK shareholder must demonstrate substantive business activities in Hong Kong to qualify as a beneficial owner; a pure conduit without real functions, assets or employees will be denied treaty benefits and the corporate structure may be recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False or Suppressed Revenue

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, and seizure of assets. If the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of tax payable, Criminal Law Article 201 applies, with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years for aggravated cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct or sign false returns may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20. They can be jointly and severally liable for the tax debt and penalties, face civil asset seizure and bank account freezing, be placed on the dishonesty blacklist with travel bans, and receive concurrent criminal sentences of up to 7 years for tax evasion.
Abusive Treaty Shopping / Shell Company Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under SAT Bulletin 9/2018 and EIT Law Article 47; additional tax withheld at the full 10% statutory rate plus 0.05% daily interest; penalties under Tax Collection Law Article 63 for false presentations; public disclosure as a serious tax dishonest organisation and revocation of treaty access for the structure.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create a conduit and sign false beneficial ownership statements can be held personally liable for the additional tax, civil restitution, and punitive damages. The court may impose an exit ban and property preservation order. Criminal liability arises under Article 201/205 where false tax instruments or VAT fraud are involved, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and confiscation of illicit assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Articles 41 and 46 allow the tax authority to re-characterise, adjust taxable income, and impose a 5-year retroactive look-back. Under Tax Collection Law, the TP adjustment may be penalised at 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. Failure to furnish contemporaneous documentation or annual related-party reporting is finable RMB 10,000-50,000; CbC reporting failures up to RMB 100,000.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length agreements without economic substance may be personally liable for causing tax loss to the company. If the adjustments render the JV insolvent, directors risk civil restitution and disqualification under PRC legal doctrines. In deliberate egregious cases, directors face criminal prosecution for tax evasion, travel bans, blacklisting, and imprisonment for up to 7 years.
