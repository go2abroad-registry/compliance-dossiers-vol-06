Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current account transactions, including clinical trial milestone fees, dividend distributions, service fees and payments for clinical supplies, are generally convertible after authenticity verification under SAFE's unified trade and payment reporting system. Banks independently verify contracts, invoices, customs declarations, tax certificates and the service-trade vs capital-trade background. Capital account transactions remain regulated: inbound FDI must be SAFE-registered; incoming foreign exchange must enter a monitored FDI capital account; conversion into RMB follows the 'safe and true' rule and must be spent only for registered business purposes. Profit remittance from the PRC joint venture requires an audited financial statement, board appropriation resolution, Enterprise Income Tax filing and paid-tax certificate, plus SAFE's registration record; the Hong Kong/China tax treaty reduces withholding leakage when treaty benefits are claimed. The Hong Kong treasury layer enjoys free convertibility under the HKMA's Linked Exchange Rate system and serves as a clean offshore payment complex, but every cross-border movement touching the PRC remains subject to SAFE scrutiny and reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current PRC trade foreign-exchange rules there is no mandatory surrender ratio. Export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange bank account and must be reconciled under SAFE's total-amount trade verification system. Delayed receipts exceeding 180 days from the customs declaration require the exporting entity to register the corresponding trade receivable with SAFE through its bank. Once collected, the enterprise may retain 100% of the foreign currency or may convert it to RMB at the commercial bank's market rate; every conversion requires genuine trade-background documentation and automatic bank reporting to SAFE.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the de facto central-banking supervisor for the Hong Kong treasury layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low-Medium for offshore digital EMI providers. Airwallex, Currencycloud, and similar non-bank EMI wallets may supply multi-currency IBANs for the Hong Kong treasury vehicle, but they are not licensed banks and cannot legally execute SAFE-regulated capital flows into mainland China. For the PRC joint venture, an offshore EMI cannot open a CNY account, connect to CIPS/CHATS as a bank originator, complete FDI registration, or hold the fiduciary capital escrow; therefore it must not be used for inbound injections, export proceeds, or profit remittance. It may only serve as a supplementary collection tool for sponsor payments in convertible currencies if the contractual flow is routed through Hong Kong and not touching the PRC regulatory perimeter.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Foreign investors should remit equity funds by SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into the JV's SAFE-registered FDI capital account. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a private company is exempt from statutory audit for a given financial year if it satisfies at least two of the following three tests: (i) annual total revenue does not exceed HKD 10,000,000; (ii) total assets do not exceed HKD 10,000,000; and (iii) the number of employees does not exceed 100. Therefore, a private company that fails to meet at least two of these tests (or exceeds two or more thresholds) is statutorily required to appoint a certified public accountant (practising) as auditor and to prepare audited financial statements. Audited financial statements must also be delivered to the Inland Revenue Department with the Profits Tax Return and presented to members. Irrespective of size, an independent audit is mandatory for public companies, companies limited by guarantee, non-private companies, and any entity regulated under banking, insurance, or securities legislation (e.g., licensed corporations under the SFO).
