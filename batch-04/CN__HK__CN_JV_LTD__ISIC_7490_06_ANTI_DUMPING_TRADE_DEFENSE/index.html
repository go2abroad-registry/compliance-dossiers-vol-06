Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current-account purposes as required under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but capital-account convertibility is tightly managed. Inbound foreign direct investment will be executed under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration system through SAFE's direct-investment information portal, under which the JV may open a foreign exchange capital account, convert RMB for approved operating purposes, and later remit lawful profits abroad through the current-account profit-remittance route after audit, tax clearance, and registration. Current-account transactions for services, including anti-dumping legal defense fees, expert assessments, and litigation support, must be genuine, contract-backed, and invoice-verified, with the bank acting as a controlled verification agent. There is no full market-determined convertibility: portfolio capital inflows, cross-border guarantees, and offshore loans require separate SAFE approvals or registrations. For outbound remittance of distributable profits, the JV must complete the annual audit under PRC Generally Accepted Accounting Principles, prove that all tax obligations, including any withholding tax under the Mainland-Hong Kong double tax arrangement, have been satisfied, and then submit the required board resolution, audited financial statements, and SAFE registration to the designated bank. Repatriation of export proceeds and service receipts is still required, so entities must not maintain non-approved offshore collections accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE regulations, there is no fixed mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds: the JV may retain 100% of foreign currency in a designated foreign exchange settlement account or instruct the bank to sell the full amount into RMB. The statutory requirement is repatriation to mainland China and entry into the banking system, with trade-related receipts generally collected by the expiry of the contractual credit period and verified in SAFE's cross-border trade monitoring system, typically within 90 days after customs clearance for goods exports unless an extension for open-account terms is registered. Any conversion executed by the bank must be reported with the proper current-account trade code and underlying transaction documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange control authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as a primary treasury rail. Offshore fintech EMIs and neobanks are legally barred from providing onshore Chinese banking, RMB clearing, SAFE-declared current/capital account services, or access to CNAPS/CIPS for the mainland JV unless they hold a valid PBOC banking or licensed clearing institution status and are connected to SAFE as designated foreign exchange banks. In practice, no offshore digital EMI holds such authorizations, so they cannot receive the JV's FDI capital injection, convert RMB, or remit dividends. At most, an offshore digital wallet can be used as a secondary sub-limit facility of a licensed Hong Kong bank for treasury convenience, but using it as the main FX/clearing platform would create AML, licence, and regulatory risk.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the equity injection from a licensed Hong Kong bank via SWIFT MT103 to the JV's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the designated mainland bank. Before the remittance, the JV and HK shareholder must complete SAFE FDI registration and obtain the FDI registration confirmation number. The MT103 field 70 must cite the SAFE FDI registration code, the equity subscription agreement number, and the statement 'FDI capital injection'. The bank will verify the payment against the FDI declaration, credit the foreign exchange capital account, and monitor the subsequent conversion of capital into RMB for self-use operating purposes. A PRC-registered accountant must later issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, which is filed with the market regulator and forms the evidentiary basis for future capital and profit registrations.Outbound payments for anti-dumping legal defense, expert witnesses, economic analyses, and overseas filing fees should be routed as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers through the JV's designated foreign exchange bank. Each payment must be supported by the underlying service contract, invoice, the bank's service-trade FX application, evidence of tax withholding or tax clearance where the foreign recipient is providing services used in China, and the appropriate SAFE service-trade purpose code. Letters of credit and documentary collections are not suitable for pure consulting services; they should be reserved for any physical goods export/import transactions ancillary to the JV's advisory work. All cross-border service payments must settle through licensed bank clearing rails, CNAPS for RMB legs and SWIFT for cross-border legs, rather than unregulated EMI systems.Onshore deliverable FX forwards between RMB and HKD/USD with a documented current-account or FDI-related underlying exposure, executed under a SAFE-registered master agreement with a domestic designated bank.Cross-currency swaps for the JV's FX positions arising from paid-in capital, service receipts, and expected profit remittances, subject to the real-demand principle and SAFE registration/approval.Offshore CNH NDFs are not permissible for the mainland entity because NDFs in RMB are non-deliverable onshore; only the Hong Kong treasury vehicle, if separately capitalised, may consider them offshore, and they cannot be booked at the PRC JV.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: A compulsory independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit is mandated for all Hong Kong companies unless the company qualifies for the small private company audit exemption under Section 359 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). The exemption applies only to a private company that (a) is not part of a group (or all group companies meet the small company criteria), and (b) satisfies at least two of the following three conditions in the financial year: (i) total revenue not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); (ii) total assets not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); (iii) not more than 100 employees. However, an audit is still mandatory if shareholders representing not less than 5% of the total voting rights serve a written notice requiring an audit. Any company with revenue, assets, or employment above these thresholds is compulsorily required to appoint a certified public accountant to audit its financial statements.

The BIR51 is issued on a block-cycle basis; the statutory due date is ordinarily one month from the date of issue for unrepresented taxpayers, and is extendable to three or four months from the issue date when filed by an authorised tax representative; the basis period coincides with the company's financial year ending in the relevant year of assessment.
