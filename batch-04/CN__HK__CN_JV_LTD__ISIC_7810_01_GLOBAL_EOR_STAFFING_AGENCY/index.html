None. Hong Kong law does not impose any minimum paid-up share capital or blocked escrow account requirement for a private company limited by shares. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mainland China: every entity engaged in taxable sales must complete VAT registration; the annual taxable turnover threshold distinguishing the simplified small-scale taxpayer regime from the general taxpayer regime is RMB 5,000,000. Cross-border EOR/payroll services are generally taxable at 6% VAT when not zero-rated or exempt. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT registration threshold because no VAT/GST is imposed.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, exported goods qualify for 0% VAT once the goods are cleared by Chinese Customs and the exporter holds an export customs declaration and foreign exchange receipt. For services, cross-border taxable services supplied to an overseas enterprise and consumed entirely outside China may qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption under the VAT reform pilot documents, including Cai Shui [2016] No. 36. For a Global EOR staffing agency, zero-rating of payroll, HR, or contractor-management services is available only where the recipient is non-resident, the service is performed and consumed outside China, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange and repatriated to China. Otherwise, the domestic 6% VAT rate applies. Qualifying offshore digital services follow the same cross-border consumption test for zero-rating.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form cleared by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (only for physical goods)
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill (only for physical goods; not required for pure service exports)
  • Bank receipt for inward foreign exchange payment from the designated foreign exchange bank, evidencing the offshore client's payment and repatriation
  • Signed cross-border service contract specifying the non-resident recipient, service description, and place of consumption outside China
  • Tax invoice issued by the JV to the non-resident customer, showing the zero-rated treatment or the VAT-exempt basis
  • Foreign exchange collection and settlement record in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) trade monitoring system

Mainland China: VAT invoices are issued through the STA electronic invoice platform. The fully digitalised e-invoice (fully digitalised electronic special VAT invoice) is transmitted in real time to the Electronic Tax Bureau, subject to anti-fraud risk control, automated data verification, and no requirement for physical stamping; paper special and ordinary VAT invoices remain subject to the national VAT invoice management and verification system. Hong Kong SAR: no real-time fiscal clearance, electronic stamping, or e-invoicing mandate is imposed for indirect tax purposes.

If the zero-rating or exemption cannot be substantiated with the required documentation, the STA will disallow the claim, recapture the VAT, and impose daily late-payment surcharges. Fraudulent or unverified export zero-rating and refund claims are treated as tax evasion under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, with penalties from 50 percent to five times the tax evaded and no limitation period for recovery in evasion cases. Where fraudulent export tax refunding constitutes a crime, liability arises under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment and fines. In Hong Kong, because no VAT/GST is administered, the IRD would instead re-assess any inaccurate offshore-source claim under the Inland Revenue Ordinance and impose additional tax and penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict segregation of current account versus capital account convertibility. Current account transactions such as trade in services and cross-border payroll are nominally convertible based on any bona fide underlying commercial transaction under the SAFE Queyuran digital verification system. Capital account transactions, including inbound registered capital injections, shareholder loans, and outbound foreign direct investment, remain subject to SAFE registration, pre-approval, and transaction-specific monitoring. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture operating as a Global Employer of Record and cross-border contractor payroll platform, the key practical hurdle is that CNY is not freely convertible offshore and all onshore FX conversion must be supported by real trade or service background documents. Profit remittance out of China requires an audited financial statement, board resolution declaring dividends, corporate income tax settlement and withholding certification, and a SAFE FX profit remittance form. The JV must maintain a capital account for registered capital injections and one or more current account FX settlement accounts for operational receipts and payments. Banks conduct authenticity checks on each transaction by matching contracts, invoices, customs data where applicable, and underlying employment/payroll records. Contractual payment for EOR services will be scrutinized to ensure that what is being routed is a genuine cross-border services fee rather than disguised capital flight or round-tripping. There is no immediate mechanism to convert unrestricted CNY into offshore currencies for speculative purposes; enterprises can only buy FX at the spot market with a validated underlying exposure.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Measures for the Administration of Export Proceeds Repatriation and Verification, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into a designated domestic foreign currency account within 180 calendar days following the date of customs declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to CNY; exporters may retain the foreign currency earnings in their FX settlement account or convert to CNY at prevailing onshore bank rates. Repatriation is monitored through the logistics-funds-customs matching system, and any extension beyond the 180-day threshold requires a written SAFE pre-approval.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise and other digital banking platforms are not licensed banking entities under Chinese law and are legally barred from maintaining onshore CNY current accounts or capital accounts for a mainland-incorporated Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture. They cannot provide SAFE-compliant FX settlement, capital account registration, or documentary examination services. Their viability for the JV is effectively Low; they may be used only by the Hong Kong parent for downstream contractor payments in other non-RMB jurisdictions, but they must not be used as a clearing rail for onshore inbound capital or statutory outbound disbursements from the Chinese entity. All regulated banking and FX functions must be conducted through authorized domestic commercial banks operating under PBOC and SAFE supervision.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital injection must be routed through a SWIFT MT103 payment from the Hong Kong parent’s account directly to the JV’s SAFE-registered Foreign Currency Capital Account at the tier-1 onshore Chinese bank. The remittance purpose code must be classied as foreign direct investment contribution into a foreign-invested enterprise, reference the valid SAFE Foreign Investment Registration certificate, and quote the JV’s unique social credit code. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the funds against the approved capital contribution schedule and obtains a Foreign Currency Capital Payment Confirmation in the SAFE system. A statutory capital verification report from a Chinese certified public accountant may be required to satisfy the bank’s internal underwriting policy; the funds must be used strictly within the registered business scope and cannot be used for debt repayment, wealth management, or any purpose outside the negative list restrictions.For outbound trade disbursements, the JV should use standard SWIFT clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct cross-border payroll settlements and contractor remuneration; commercial letters of credit (SWIFT MT700/MT710) for any procurement of goods or vendor payables where an incontrovertible payment undertaking is required; and documentary collections under URC522 for service-based trade flows where a cheaper structural settlement framework is preferred. Turnover: Hong Kong law imposes no quantitative balance sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption for an active private company. Every Hong Kong company must be audited by a Certified Public Accountant (practising) under the Companies Ordinance unless it qualifies as a dormant private company that has elected for audit exemption. An active Global Employer of Record and payroll agency is disqualified from the dormant exemption because it processes payroll transactions and holds client or employee funds; therefore a full statutory audit is mandatory regardless of revenue or staff count.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

16.5% (standard Hong Kong profits tax rate for corporations; a two-tiered concessionary rate of 8.25% applies to the first HK$2,000,000 of assessable profits, subject to the connected-entity limitation)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

0% - Hong Kong does not impose any withholding tax on dividends. A non-resident shareholder, including a mainland China parent, receives dividends from the Hong Kong JV gross, with no Hong Kong dividend withholding tax or advance corporation tax.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The return must be lodged within one month from the date of issue. Under the IRD Filing Programme for tax-represented cases, paper returns for a 31 December year-end are generally due by 15 August, and e-filed returns by 15 September; other accounting periods receive corresponding extensions, in all cases no later than approximately six months after the issue date. If no extension is granted, the default one-month deadline applies and an estimated assessment may be raised immediately.

Box 1 - Business Registration and Company Particulars Enter the company name, Business Registration (BR) number, BR key, registered office, nature of business and tax representative details. For the non-resident-owned Hong Kong JV, declare the real place where central management and control is exercised. If the board of directors meets in mainland China but payroll administration and client contracting are performed in Hong Kong, the IRD will treat the profits as Hong Kong-source; a declaration of offshore control must be supported by full board minutes, decision-making records and economic substance.
