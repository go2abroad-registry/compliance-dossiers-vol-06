Mainland China: zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from China with completed Customs export clearance; for cross-border services such as executive search and headhunting supplied to an overseas entity, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available if the service recipient is an overseas entity, the service is wholly performed outside China or consumed offshore, and sufficient documentary evidence of the offshore supply is retained. Mainland China: VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved electronic invoicing channels. Under the fully digitalized electronic invoice system, invoices are transmitted in real time to the taxpayer's STA tax digital account, with automated validation of taxpayer status, invoice uniqueness and anti-fraud controls; API-based issuance is required for high-volume integrated issuers and large enterprises. Hong Kong SAR: no fiscal clearance or e-invoicing mandate exists; IRD accepts accounting records and electronically filed returns for profits tax.

China Customs and STA may retrospectively disallow zero-rating/exemption claims where documentation is incomplete, false or inconsistent. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a bifurcated FX control regime. RMB is widely convertible for current-account transactions, including cross-border goods trade, services trade, and profit remittance, provided the underlying contract and tax certificates are submitted to a licensed bank. However, capital-account flows remain subject to rigorous SAFE approval and registration. For a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, inbound shareholder loans or equity injections require a SAFE FDI registration, a capital account banking arrangement, and an ultimate beneficial ownership declaration. The JV cannot deploy foreign exchange capital until the bank has performed a capital verification and confirmed the funds correspond to the business scope. Profit repatriation to a Hong Kong parent is permissible once annual accounting, financial audit, and corporate income tax settlement are completed; the bank will require a board resolution, audited P&L, and a tax clearance certificate. Dividends are taxed at withholding rates under the double taxation arrangement. In practice, convertibility restrictions emerge in the form of document-based verification, 'real need' review, and advance SAFE compliance, rather than daily conversion ceilings. Surrender obligations have been liberalised since 2012 and exporters may retain all FX receipts, but the funds must still be repatriated through designated bank accounts. The JV should maintain a cash pooling policy that avoids unused FX balances beyond the mandatory repatriation timeline.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Measures, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 calendar days of customs export release. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; 100% of the foreign currency may be retained in a domestic designated account or converted to RMB at the enterprise's discretion. Settlement requires presentation of the export declaration, invoice, customs form, and the bank's electronic monitoring record.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All FDI injection from the Hong Kong shareholder must be remitted through a SWIFT MT103 or equivalent transfer to the JV's SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The remitting bank must include the ultimate originator details required by PBOC and HK beneficial ownership rules; the payment message must include the SAFE FX purpose code for foreign investment in a joint venture's capital increase, plus the SAFE FDI registration certificate reference. The JV must obtain an independent CPA capital verification report within six months of each capital deposit. The funds cannot be used for current account operational spending until the capital is converted to RMB with the bank's FX settlement confirmation and a reconciliation against the business license. Any cross-currency mismatch between the capital contribution currency and the RMB usage must be executed at the spot market price under real-need rules.For executive search mandates, the JV pays foreign independent researchers, background-check agencies, or subcontractors. Use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with proper service trade purpose codes and submit the service trade invoice and tax withholding certificate to the remitting bank. For larger retainer contracts with overseas clients, permit cross-border RMB (CNY) settlement via CIPS or multicurrency clearing via a domestic designated FX account. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are legally acceptable for goods but are substantively inadequate for headhunting services; they do not match the C-Suite search commercial model. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 10,000,000 aggregate covered transactions with associated enterprises in a basis period for mandatory Local File preparation; Master File is required for multinational enterprise groups with global annual consolidated revenue above HKD 1,000,000,000; Country-by-Country Reporting is required for groups with consolidated revenue of EUR 750,000,000 or more, equivalent to approximately HKD 6,800,000,000.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong applies the Foreign Source Income Exemption regime, under which offshore passive income (interest, dividends, disposal gains, and IP income) is exempt from profits tax only where the taxpayer has adequate economic substance in Hong Kong. This requires sufficient premises, full-time employees, board decisions, and the performance of core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. Active executive-search and headhunting commission is business income governed by the territorial source principle rather than passive income, so the JV must prove that value-creating activities such as candidate identification, client negotiation, and engagement delivery are undertaken in Hong Kong; a shell arrangement using mainland China labour will cause the offshore exemption to be denied and transactions may be adjusted or disregarded under sections 61 and 61A.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) sections 80(1)(d), 82, and 82A: office-based additional tax up to three times the undercharged tax, fines up to HKD 50,000 on summary conviction, and imprisonment up to 3 years on indictment. The IRD may also disregard the document trail and assess additional profits under section 61.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed, prepared, or authorized the false return are personally liable as accessory offenders. The corporate veil may be pierced where the company operated as a mere facade; directors face civil asset restitution, disqualification under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), blacklisting/travel restriction for unpaid tax, and criminal imprisonment.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61 and 61A empower the IRD to disregard artificial or fictitious transactions and deny the tax benefit, causing the full fee income to be taxed at 16.5%; additional tax of up to 100% of the deficient tax may be imposed under sections 82B and 82C. The taxpayer also loses the two-tiered concession and may be subject to a complete reassessment for earlier years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structured the shell arrangement to avoid profits tax are jointly and severally assessable for the additional tax. Courts can pierce the corporate veil, order disgorgement of diverted fees, impose director disqualification orders, and issue travel bans or blacklisting where tax debt remains outstanding. Repeated abuse may lead to imprisonment for aiding tax evasion.
AML and Bribery in Executive Search

Statutory sanction: Prevention of Bribery Ordinance (Cap. 201) section 9 prohibits corrupt transactions with agents; conviction can lead to a fine up to HKD 1,000,000 and imprisonment for up to 7 years. The Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap. 615) imposes customer due diligence and record-keeping obligations; breach carries criminal penalties including fines and imprisonment. The Organized and Serious Crimes Ordinance (Cap. 455) section 25 criminalizes dealing with proceeds of crime, with penalties up to 14 years' imprisonment and fines up to HKD 5,000,000.

Director personal liability: A director who authorized, arranged, or acquiesced in the kickback and money laundering is personally criminally liable and cannot hide behind the corporate form. Civil liability includes restitution, disgorgement of the commission, damages to the client employer, and freezing of personal assets. The court may issue travel restrictions, blacklist the director, and impose consecutive prison sentences for corruption and money laundering.
