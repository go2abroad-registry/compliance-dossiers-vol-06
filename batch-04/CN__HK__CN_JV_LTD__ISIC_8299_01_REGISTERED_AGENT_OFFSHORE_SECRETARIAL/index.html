Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622). There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital, no authorised capital ceiling, and no blocked escrow account requirement for an ordinary private company limited by shares. The subscriber statement of capital in Form NNC1 must disclose the number of issued shares and the amount paid up, which is conventionally at least one ordinary share at HK$1 each. For this joint-venture vehicle, the board may authorise a higher issued capital according to the shareholders' investment agreement, but no deposit of funds with the Companies Registry or a licensed bank is required for incorporation.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; in Hong Kong, a company is separately assessable to Profits Tax on territorial-source profits, and there is no pass-through classification. If the JV is incorporated or effectively managed in mainland China, it defaults to a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law.

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box or domestic entity-classification election, and no hybrid mismatch election is available to the Hong Kong entity. China's Enterprise Income Tax Law treats a Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture as a resident enterprise without pass-through election rights; double taxation relief is obtained only through the tax credit method under binding double taxation arrangements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

None in Hong Kong SAR - Hong Kong imposes no VAT, GST, or sales/use tax; the indirect levies are Business Registration fees and Stamp Duty under the Stamp Duty Ordinance (Cap. 117). If the JV carries on a mainland China taxable activity, the PRC VAT system applies to that PRC nexus.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Hong Kong: no indirect tax turnover threshold exists; any person carrying on business must hold a Business Registration Certificate irrespective of turnover. PRC breakpoint (if applicable): RMB 5 million annual assessable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold simplified VAT taxpayer treatment applies unless voluntarily electing general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Hong Kong: not applicable because there is no VAT/GST to zero-rate. For a PRC export transaction, 0% VAT (exemption and refund) is available for goods physically exported and customs-cleared by a VAT-registered exporter, and for qualifying zero-rated offshore services, provided the export is declared to Customs, the exporter files the export tax refund declaration, and the foreign currency proceeds are repatriated through the designated banking system within the 180-day verification window.

  • Electronic Trade Declaration submitted via the Census and Statistics Department's Electronic Trade Declaration System (ETDS) under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) for Hong Kong export movements
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing dispatch of the goods from Hong Kong or the PRC customs port
  • Commercial invoice and packing list describing the goods or services
  • Bank credit advice / SWIFT MT103 or the bank's foreign exchange income declaration receipt proving repatriation of export sale proceeds
  • For PRC export VAT refund substantiation: the Customs Export Goods Declaration, the export sales invoice, the input-VAT special invoice set, and the export proceeds receipt confirmation from the designated foreign exchange bank

Hong Kong: no statutory fiscal e-invoicing clearance, no tax authority API, and no anti-fraud digital stamping; commercial invoices are retained under Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112) record-keeping rules. For a PRC VAT nexus, the State Taxation Administration requires issuance of fully digitalized E-invoices through the national electronic tax platform, with real-time upload, unique invoice/tax-control serial numbers, and QR-code digital authentication; no offline paper clearance exists.

Hong Kong: no export VAT clawback exists, but false trade declarations under the Import and Export (Registration) Regulations (Cap. 60) attract fines and imprisonment, and misstated offshore Profits Tax claims are re-assessed with penalties under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112). For PRC export VAT refunds, false or unverified claims are subject to retrospective clawback of the refunded VAT, late payment interest, penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax short-paid, and criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax rebates where the amount involved is severe.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Hong Kong SAR operates a free convertibility regime under the Basic Law and has no foreign exchange control law. The Hong Kong dollar is fully convertible and the Linked Exchange Rate System maintained by HKMA keeps the rate within 7.75-7.85 against the USD. HKSAR corporate treasury can hold HKD, USD, CNH, EUR and other currency accounts, and current-account items such as dividends, royalties and service fees may be remitted without SAFE approval if the beneficiary is outside mainland China. However, the ultimate controlling shareholder is a mainland Chinese entity, so the real regulatory chokepoint is China's managed capital account. SAFE/PBOC requires genuine underlying transactions for current-account convertibility, while capital-account outbound investment is permitted only after NDRC overseas-investment filing, MOFCOM certification and SAFE FX registration. A HK JV receiving equity from its PRC parent must evidence the ODI approval at the remitting Chinese bank; otherwise the inward payment may be characterized as unauthorized capital outflow. Profit repatriation from HK back to mainland is a cross-boundary dividend distribution requiring audited HK financial statements, board resolution, tax residence documentation, and, where applicable, withholding-tax proof; the HKD amount itself is convertible but the audit trail must match the underlying economic substance. There is no forced conversion or export surrender requirement at HK level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mainland PRC exporter rules: mandatory surrender was abolished; no set conversion ratio. Export foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic designated foreign-exchange account by the later of the customs-declaration date plus 90 days or the commercial contract payment due date; if unreceived beyond that, an exception report must be filed through the bank. For the HK-incorporated entity: no surrender requirements; all foreign-currency receipts may be retained, converted, or transferred freely under the HKMA linked exchange rate system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) for mainland China cross-border FX and RMB administration; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the Hong Kong SAR currency board, banking supervisor, and de facto central bank authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Limited and legally constrained. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut or Wise are not authorized institutions under the HKMA Banking Ordinance and cannot provide capital escrow, trade finance, CHATS access, documentary credits, or SAFE-compliant RMB clearing. Using an unlicensed EMI for a Hong Kong registered agent and company secretarial business would fail the banking AML underwriting standard, may expose the company to unauthorized money-service operation risk, and would not be accepted by a PRC shareholder for ODI capital routing or by HK counterparties for statutory fees. The entity must maintain its primary treasury accounts at an HKMA-licensed Tier-1 authorized institution; fintech virtual banks licensed by HKMA may support basic deposit accounts but are not viable for complex cross-border capital escrow or trade settlement structures.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The PRC shareholder must complete NDRC overseas-investment filing, MOFCOM certification and SAFE FX registration before remitting equity capital. The remittance should be routed through a Chinese designated bank using the SAFE ODI purpose code and wire instruction clearly marked capital contribution to the HK JV; the HK receiving bank will use SWIFT MT103 or CIPS for CNY and will require the underlying ODI certificate and board resolution before releasing funds to a segregated capital escrow or operating account. Upon receipt, the HK bank issues a credit confirmation, and a HK certified public accountant may perform capital verification for the Chinese parent's books and future dividend repatriation. The HK company must keep every capital injection separate from client fee income and reconcile the share-allotment form and company registry records with the bank statement.For ISIC 8299 corporate secretarial and registered agent services, outbound trade disbursements are primarily operating payments to contractors, government filing agencies and professional partners. Use SWIFT MT103 bank wires for cross-border supplier invoices, HKD CHATS RTGS for local statutory and registry fee payments, and USD CHATS or Fedwire for US-dollar clearings; commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are not appropriate for pure service fees and should be added only if the entity later expands into goods-backed trade. If ancillary trade is undertaken, the HK bank will require underlying transport documents, commercial invoices, customs declarations and sanctions screening for each documentary credit or collection, consistent with HKMA, OFAC and EU restrictive measures.Deliverable forward contracts in USD/HKD, EUR/HKD and CNH/HKD for committed operational cash flows up to 12 months, supported by underlying invoices or projected service fee receipts.Onshore CNY deliverable forwards arranged through a mainland bank only after SAFE real-need verification and within the approved ODI and current-account quotas.Offshore CNH deliverable forwards and CNH/USD FX swaps at HKMA-licensed banks for RMB surplus held by the PRC parent, subject to trade or investment documentation.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) only for onshore CNY exposure when the company lacks CNY deliverable account access, with tenors capped at 12 months and no speculative leverage.Cross-currency swaps for long-term USD/CNH liability hedging only with an underlying confirmed debt obligation and HKMA-licensed credit lines; no swap may be used to disguise dividend repatriation or circumvent capital controls.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Limited and legally constrained. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut or Wise are not authorized institutions under the HKMA Banking Ordinance and cannot provide capital escrow, trade finance, CHATS access, documentary credits, or SAFE-compliant RMB clearing. Using an unlicensed EMI for a Hong Kong registered agent and company secretarial business would fail the banking AML underwriting standard, may expose the company to unauthorized money-service operation risk, and would not be accepted by a PRC shareholder for ODI capital routing or by HK counterparties for statutory fees. The entity must maintain its primary treasury accounts at an HKMA-licensed Tier-1 authorized institution; fintech virtual banks licensed by HKMA may support basic deposit accounts but are not viable for complex cross-border capital escrow or trade settlement structures.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under DIPN 58 and the Inland Revenue Ordinance, a Hong Kong entity is required to prepare a Local File when its controlled transactions with overseas associated enterprises in an accounting period exceed HK$80 million in aggregate for property or financial transactions; a Master File is obligatorily required when the multinational enterprise group has consolidated global revenue of at least HK$680 million and the Hong Kong member engages in related-party cross-border transactions.

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the IRD applies the core income-generating activities (CIGA) doctrine and the anti-avoidance provisions of the Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61, 61A and 61B to disregard artificial or subcontractual arrangements. A registered agent and offshore secretarial business must demonstrate real economic presence in Hong Kong through an operating office, locally employed personnel with decision-making authority, board meetings conducted in Hong Kong, client contracts executed and managed from Hong Kong, and financial statements reporting functional independence from the non-resident parent; a mere letterbox entity claiming offshore profits or treaty residency without substance will be recharacterized.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Tax Returns

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance section 82(1) and 82A: on summary conviction a fine of HK$10,000 plus treble the amount of tax evaded and imprisonment for three years; for continued evasion, additional tax assessments plus mandatory surcharges apply.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under section 82C: every director, chief executive, company secretary, or agent who participated in, consented to, or knowingly permitted the evasion is personally liable to the same fines and imprisonment. The IRD may also apply civil asset restitution, orders compelling the repayment of tax, travel restrictions and Blacklisting of directors, and disqualification under the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years.
Failure to Maintain Proper Accounting Records and Transfer Pricing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance section 51C imposes a mandatory record-keeping obligation; breach of that obligation invites penalties under section 80(2), being a fine not exceeding HK$100,000, and the IRD may raise an estimated assessment together with additional tax of up to three times the deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors are separately liable where records are not kept and the absence is attributable to their dishonesty or gross negligence; personal fines, compensatory recovery orders, and in aggressive cases criminal prosecution and imprisonment under section 82C can be pursued. The court may disqualify the director and impose travel bans or Blacklisting to secure the company's tax liabilities.
Shell Company / Sham Arrangement and Ant-Avoidance Piercing

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61 and 61A enable the IRD to disregard the arrangement and assess tax on the real economic substance; additional tax of up to 100% of the tax evaded can be imposed under section 82A. If the case crosses into fraud, imprisonment up to three years and a fine of HK$10,000 plus treble tax apply.

Director personal liability: A director who is a knowing party to the shell arrangement faces civil liability for all unpaid corporate tax through piercing of the corporate veil, personal asset seizure, travel ban and Blacklisting, and restitution to the Inland Revenue; criminal liability follows where the director signed or caused the submission of the fraudulent profit claim, carrying a custodial sentence and mandatory disqualification under the Companies Ordinance.
