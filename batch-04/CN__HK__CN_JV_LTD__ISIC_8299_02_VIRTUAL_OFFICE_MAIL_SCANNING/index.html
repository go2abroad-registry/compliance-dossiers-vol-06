Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For the Hong Kong JV entity, a registered office address in Hong Kong is mandatory. A virtual office or commercial mail receiving agency may only satisfy the Companies Registry requirement if the service provider holds a valid physical suite in a commercial building in Hong Kong, displays the company name at the premises, is not merely a private letterbox, and is not at a disqualified address such as a police station or a non-commercial residential flat. However, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department's economic substance practice requires that the physical office space be more than a mailbox for any company claiming treaty benefits or engaged in active business. Because the business scope is virtual office, mail scanning, and digital forwarding under ISIC 8299, a pure virtual mailbox without staff, premises control, and business records will be rejected by the Inland Revenue Department for profits tax purposes, and most Hong Kong licensed banks will refuse corporate account opening for a CMRA whose only Hong Kong presence is a virtual office. Therefore, virtual office permissibility is limited to administrative statutory compliance, and the company must maintain an additional physical business office if it wishes to file a valid profits tax return, open a bank account, or qualify for the Hong Kong double taxation arrangement. For the WFOE in mainland China, a registered commercial address is mandatory under SAMR and the virtual office is generally not permissible for a physical scanning operation; a commercial rental agreement with an actual workspace is required.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, together with the local STA tax office; for Hong Kong SAR profit tax matters, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTAX portal

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested equity joint venture (EJV) incorporated in mainland China, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) obtained from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) at incorporation serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT identification number; tax registration is completed automatically by data interchange between AMR and STA, after which the entity must login to the STA electronic tax service platform to record its tax types, VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale), bank details, accounting policies and legal representative. For the Hong Kong SAR side, if the JV does not carry on a business in Hong Kong, no Hong Kong tax registration is required; if a Hong Kong branch or tax representative presence is established, the entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310) before the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) commences business operations, and the BRC number is the Hong Kong taxpayer identification number (TIN). A foreign non-resident shareholder supplying funds to the JV is not itself required to obtain a Chinese TIN; the JV withholds Chinese income tax on outbound dividends, interest, royalties and certain cross-border service fees and identifies the beneficial owner by the shareholder's home jurisdiction tax number (for a HK company, its HK BRC number/IRD file number) for purposes of the Mainland and Hong Kong tax arrangement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide pass-through check-the-box elections; the Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture is taxed at the entity level. Hong Kong profits tax also has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election; taxation follows the territorial source principle and entity status is not elective.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual VAT taxable turnover for mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; small-scale taxpayer status is available below that threshold, with a monthly VAT exemption for small-scale taxpayers of RMB 100,000

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For exported goods, 0% VAT applies where goods are loaded from Chinese customs territory to an overseas destination, the exporter files an export customs declaration, receives the sales proceeds in foreign exchange, and has no unapproved export tax refund restrictions. For cross-border services of this virtual office and mail scanning business, 0% VAT status is available only when the recipient is an overseas enterprise or individual, the service is consumed wholly outside China, the service is not one of the categories deemed to be consumed in China (e.g., services related to immovable property located in China), and the service fees are received in foreign exchange and repatriated into China. The taxpayer must keep contemporaneous evidence of the service recipient's overseas status and the offshore nature of the service delivery.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) with customs release endorsement
  • Bill of Lading for ocean freight or Air Waybill for air freight showing export from Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Collection Certificate issued by a Chinese bank evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds
  • Sales contract or service agreement identifying the overseas recipient and the offshore service destination

China operates a centralised e-invoicing system through the State Taxation Administration's fully digital electronic invoice (FDEI) platform. Taxpayers must issue quasi-real-time e-invoices on the national e-invoice service; VAT special invoices are controlled through the Golden Tax system and require tax control clearance; recipients validate digital invoices against the STA database through the National Electronic Invoice Public Service Portal. There is no private or free-form invoice allowed for VAT purposes.

Zero-rating is denied ab initio if the documented substance is absent or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the statutory deadline. The tax authority will claw back the previously refunded or zero-rated VAT, impose late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day of accumulated delay, and may impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid VAT. Fraudulent export refund claims constitute tax evasion and criminal fraud under PRC law, exposing responsible officers to criminal fines and imprisonment, and the clawback can extend over a retroactive limitation period of up to 10 years in cases of falsified documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under which current-account transactions (service exports, investment income, trade settlements) are broadly convertible subject to strict documentary and authenticity reviews by designated banks applying the 'know-your-client, know-your-business, know-your-customer' due diligence principles. For this Sino-Foreign Equity Joint Venture, the virtual office and mail scanning revenue derived from non-resident clients constitutes service-trade export receipts which may be credited to a current-account foreign exchange account held at a licensed commercial bank. The enterprise retains complete discretion to convert such foreign currency into RMB under the voluntamay settlement mechanism, but every conversion must be backed by real underlying service contracts, commercial invoices, and the bank must comply with the cross-border receipt reconciliation rules. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong investor is legally treated as a capital-account outgoing payment for equity distribution, requiring a fully audited balance sheet, corporate income tax clearance, Foreign-Invested Enterprise registration certificate, board resolution for dividend distribution, and the bank must verify that the amount equals the after-tax retained earnings after statutory reserves. No quota applies for lawful profits, yet the practical hurdles include enhanced beneficial-owner due diligence, the Chinese investor may need to prove the origin of the initial injecting funds, and the bank will require a pre-remittance risk assessment plus approval through the SAFE Regulatory Information System. Additional convertibility restrictions appear on short-term speculative flows, pre-payment of services above a certain threshold, and any arrangement that attempts to route payments without passing through the domestic, supervised treasury, thereby making full convertibility functional but only through bank-facilitated mechanisms.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 10-2019 and current-account administration rules, export and service-trade foreign currency receipts from overseas customers must be repatriated into mainland China within 180 days from the date of invoicing or the agreed contractual payment due date, unless an application for offshore retention is filed and approved by the designated bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio into RMB; the JV may voluntarily settle all or part of the foreign currency at the market rate, or leave it in an eligible current-account foreign exchange account at a tier-1 bank in China. The bank will verify the inflow against the service contract, invoice, and the SAFE digital system registration, and the enterprise may need to submit a supporting letter if the amount deviates from the contract by more than 25%. For recurring international card payments routed through third-party processors, the actual settlement must still be channelled through the domestic banking system with a proper service-trade purpose code for the bank to accept the credit.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the latter operating as the PBOC's executive body supervising all foreign exchange activity in the mainland, including China's special administrative territories for FX reporting.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Fintech EMIs (e.g. Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, PingPong or Payoneer) cannot function as the primary treasury or capital-escrow bank for this mainland-based, foreign-invested equity joint venture. They are not licensed by the PBOC to maintain capital-account escrow accounts, to settle RMB through the CIPS system with full documentary compliance, or to issue bank confirmation letters required for FDI registration and capital verification. SAFE allows certain cross-border e-commerce payment institutions to collect international card transactions on behalf of mainland registered enterprises, but the funds must ultimately settle into a domestic commercial bank account under the regulatory 'online payment business' pilot, and the fintech platform itself cannot hold the JV's statutory equity capital or disburse dividends to Hong Kong. The recommended approach is to utilise a tier-1 domestic bank for all clearing and custodial functions, while a licensed payment institution may be engaged as a front-end aggregator for Stripe-like recurring card receipts, provided the contracts are drawn as a collection agency agreement and all monies are swept daily to the JV's local bank under a service-trade declaration. Viability rating: LOW as a standalone bank, MEDIUM as an ancillary collection rail only after obtaining SAFE-recognised payment institution approval and maintaining a full audit trail.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every non-dormant Hong Kong company is generally required to prepare financial statements that are audited by an independent certified public accountant. A private company may be exempt from the statutory audit for a financial year only if it passes an audit-exemption resolution and qualifies as a 'small private company' or a member of a 'small group', meaning it satisfies at least two of the following three thresholds: (a) annual revenue does not exceed HKD 10,000,000; (b) total balance-sheet assets do not exceed HKD 10,000,000; and (c) the average number of employees does not exceed 50. The audit exemption is not available to public-interest entities, banks, insurance companies, licensed corporations, or other entities carrying on regulated financial activities. For all other companies, a mandatory independent CPA audit is legally required, and the Inland Revenue Department may in any case require audited financial statements to support a Profits Tax Return.
