Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Government of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region of the People's Republic of China; online portal: eTAX (etax.gov.hk). The Business Registration Office (BRO) is the IRD unit responsible for Business Registration Certificates.

E-government filing portal

Hong Kong does not issue a TIN/VAT ID. The statutory tax identifier for an entity is the Business Registration Number (BRN) shown on the Business Registration Certificate issued under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). For a company incorporated in Hong Kong under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), the Companies Registry automatically notifies the IRD upon incorporation, and the company is issued a Business Registration Certificate without a separate application. A foreign non-resident entity that is not incorporated in Hong Kong but carries on business in Hong Kong, or establishes a Hong Kong branch/place of business, must apply to the BRO within one month of commencing that business using the prescribed business registration application, accompanied by the entity's home-jurisdiction certificate of incorporation or equivalent registration proof, a Hong Kong registered office or principal place-of-business address, particulars of directors and the local company secretary if applicable, and, where required, notarized translations of foreign documents. The BRO issues the Business Registration Certificate containing the BRN; the BRN is the reference number used for subsequent Profits Tax, employer returns, eTAX filing, correspondence, and stamp duty matters. No separate VAT registration number exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/sales tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election for a limited company. A Hong Kong company is taxed at the entity level under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (Cap. 112); the territorial-source limitation applies because Profits Tax is charged only on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong, but this is a legal principle, not an elective classification. Personal assessment is only available to individuals and is not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

No VAT/GST/sales and use tax; Hong Kong does not operate a general indirect tax.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Not applicable. There is no VAT/GST/sales tax threshold. Business Registration under Cap. 310 is required of any person or entity carrying on business in Hong Kong regardless of turnover; the BRN is not an indirect-tax registration number.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Not applicable. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST, so there is no 0% export VAT rate and no statutory zero-rating claim for the WebNovel subscription platform or its cross-border digital services. The analogous Hong Kong relief is the territorial-source principle: under section 14 of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, Profits Tax is chargeable only on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong, so subscription income from non-Hong Kong users through Apple IAP / Google Play Billing will be outside the Hong Kong tax net if the key selling, contracting, execution, support, and management activities are performed outside Hong Kong. To substantiate this, the entity should maintain app-store settlement reports, platform traffic and customer geolocation data, server/hosting records, contracts with content licensors and payment processors, and board minutes evidencing offshore management and control.

  • No statutory export VAT documentation bundle exists because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST; no customs export declaration, bill of lading, or Inward Remittance Certificate is required to substantiate a VAT zero-rating.
  • For an offshore-profits claim, the IRD would expect to inspect Apple IAP / Google Play Billing settlement statements, app-store transaction reports, customer IP/geolocation logs, server and hosting contracts, digital content licence agreements, distribution and payment-processor agreements, bank statements showing receipts from non-Hong Kong counterparties, and board minutes showing substantive business decisions made outside Hong Kong.

None. Hong Kong does not mandate real-time electronic invoicing, tax authority API clearance, fiscal digital stamping, or transaction-level clearance for VAT/GST. The IRD eTAX portal is used for statutory returns (Profits Tax, Property Tax, Salaries Tax, Business Registration) and correspondence, but it is not an indirect-tax fiscal clearance system.

No VAT zero-rating clawback can arise because Hong Kong has no VAT. However, if an offshore-profits position is challenged by the IRD and the profits are found to be Hong Kong-sourced, the entity will be assessed to Profits Tax at 16.5% (for corporations) on the assessable profits, plus possible additional tax under section 82A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance of up to treble the tax undercharged for incorrect returns without reasonable excuse; fraudulent or wilful evasion under section 82 can attract heavier fines and imprisonment. IRD audit scrutiny in the digital-services context will focus on the location of personnel, servers, contract execution, customer acquisition, and day-to-day management; unsupported offshore treatment may be reversed with penalties and interest.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Hong Kong operates under a fully convertible currency architecture anchored by the Linked Exchange Rate System (LERS) at USD 7.75-7.85, with no advance import/export licensing on the Hong Kong-resident entity, no surrender requirements, no retained FX quota restrictions, and no central bank approval needed for current-account or capital-account movements from the perspective of the Hong Kong JV. However, the funding parent in Mainland China is subject to strict SAFE controls; any equity injection or shareholder loan from the Mainland parent requires outbound investment filing, SAFE registration, and meaningful-use evidence to satisfy both PRC and HKMA banking expectations. Profit remittances from the Hong Kong entity to the Mainland parent are not blocked by HKMA, but the receiving Mainland parent must evidence the receipt as investment income or service royalties in its SAFE current-account filing, otherwise conversion into RMB can be delayed. The practical treasury difficulty is not the convertibility of Hong Kong dollars, but the asymmetry between an unrestricted Hong Kong account and the Mainland parent's administered capital account. Foreign investors and joint-venture founders must demonstrate the underlying trade or investment purpose, use a designated FX settlement bank, and preserve full remittance documentation to avoid HK bank de-risking and SAFE non-compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the Hong Kong JV, no statutory surrender applies; 100% of earned HKD/USD export proceeds may be retained in any licensed bank account and freely used without mandated conversion or repatriation. For a Mainland China exporter parent, SAFE regulations require export foreign-exchange receipts to be repatriated to a designated domestic foreign-exchange account within the prescribed verification period, generally 180 days from export customs declaration, and although there is no compulsory surrender ratio, 100% of such proceeds must flow through the bank's foreign-exchange receipt/verification system before the enterprise may convert to RMB at market rate or retain the balance under SAFE-approved limits. This distinction matters because the Hong Kong JV should not be used as a permanent offshore parking vehicle for Mainland export proceeds, or it will be re-characterised as underground banking.

Central bank supervision: Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA); State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China for Mainland China capital controls

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low as a full treasury relationship and Medium as an incidental payout layer. Offshore digital EMIs not licensed by HKMA under the Banking Ordinance (Cap. 155) are legally barred from accepting deposits or providing escrow, trade finance, FX forwards, or documentary collections. Such EMIs are not eligible to issue capital verification letters, open a capital escrow account for the JV, or undertake regulatory reporting to SAFE. A HKMA-authorised Stored Value Facility or licensed virtual bank (for example ZA Bank or WeLab Bank) can be used for subscription collections from Apple/Google, but cannot replace a Tier-1 commercial bank for cross-border treasury, hedging, or AML-reliability. Foreign EMI providers such as Wise Business or Revolut can only be used to support minor payouts in supported corridors and must never hold the JV's statutory capital or serve as a safe-harbor clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity funding from the Mainland parent must be preceded by completed ODI filings at NDRC and MOFCOM and a SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate. The remitting bank in Mainland China will use SWIFT MT103 with the approved foreign-exchange purpose code and forward the funds to the HK JV's designated capital escrow account at HSBC, BOC (Hong Kong), or Hang Seng. The receiving HK bank will require a board resolution, the signed JV contract, the SAFE ODI certificate, and a CPA-issued capital verification report after every deposit. For operational pre-funding, shareholder loans should be documented with a loan agreement and priced at arm's-length interest to avoid thin-capitalisation challenges in both HK and Mainland tax reviews. Turnover: All companies incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) are unconditionally required to prepare financial statements and have them audited by a certified public accountant registered with the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants. There are no balance sheet, turnover, or headcount exemption thresholds. The sole exception is a 'dormant company' that has obtained formal dormant status from the Companies Registry; such an entity is exempt from audit only while it remains dormant with no accounting transactions.
