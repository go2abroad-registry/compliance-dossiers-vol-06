Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local competent tax authority in the jurisdiction where the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is registered; all statutory filings are submitted electronically through the China Electronic Tax Administration Portal (e-Tax Bureau / Electronic Tax Bureau) and the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

For a PRC-registered WFOE, the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued at the time of business registration with the Administration for Market Regulation is the statutory tax identification number; no separate VAT registration number is issued. The WFOE must complete tax identification confirmation with the local STA office within 30 days of business registration by submitting the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity documents, registered address proof, and bank account details through the e-Tax Bureau. The foreign corporate shareholder in Hong Kong SAR does not require a separate PRC tax identification number unless it has a taxable establishment or derives PRC-source income subject to withholding; in such cases, the non-resident enterprise must complete registration with the local STA office and obtain a PRC non-resident tax identification number before performing tax withholding and reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate a check-the-box election or hybrid branch/mismatch election mechanism. A WFOE is mandatorily classified as a PRC resident enterprise subject to entity-level enterprise income tax on worldwide income. Any cross-border hybrid mismatch treatment must be resolved by the Hong Kong SAR parent jurisdiction under its own territorial tax rules and by the PRC Special Tax Adjustment rules, not by taxpayer election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales excluding VAT triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. Regardless of turnover, every WFOE must undertake basic VAT registration upon establishment. A small-scale taxpayer with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 may qualify for the statutory small-scale taxpayer VAT exemption, subject to the prevailing policy validity period.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported UHT oat milk goods qualify for PRC export VAT zero-rating only when the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer and the goods are declared to Customs as ordinary exports, physically leave the PRC customs territory, are sold to an overseas buyer, and the export sales proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank. The statutory authority is the PRC export VAT exemption and refund mechanism under the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Circular Cai Shui [2012] No. 39, as amended. Supplies from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR are treated as exports for PRC VAT purposes when the goods are cleared through PRC customs and the free trade zone and cross-border logistics substantiation requirements are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (China Customs Single Window electronic declaration and customs-signed record)
  • Original Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or combined transport document evidencing physical export from a PRC port
  • Commercial invoice and packing list for the UHT oat milk and aseptic packaging lines
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with the overseas buyer
  • Foreign exchange receipt record from the designated bank (the PRC equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate, showing receipt of export proceeds)
  • PRC special VAT invoice(s) for inputs, packaging materials, processing, and production costs used in the exported goods
  • Production and traceability records including batch release logs, sterilisation records, and product quality inspection certificates
  • Export food safety licence, certificate of free sale, or health certificate required by the destination market and PRC export food safety regulations

All VAT invoices are issued through the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoice system, currently the all-digital electronic invoice (DEI) module integrated with the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Invoice issuance data is transmitted in real time to the STA; no physical fiscal stamping is required, but each invoice must carry a uniform digital invoice number and an anti-counterfeiting QR code. The WFOE must use an enterprise resource planning system or invoicing terminal connected via the e-Tax Bureau interface for automatic invoice issuance, red-letter cancellation, and real-time fiscal clearance verification.

Export VAT zero-rating claims are jointly audited by the State Taxation Administration and China Customs using electronic cross-matching of customs declarations, VAT invoice data, physical logistics records, and foreign exchange receipts. If the zero-rating cannot be substantiated, the STA will recover the VAT refunded, impose late-payment surcharge, and apply a penalty of 50% up to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Intentional export tax rebate fraud is prosecuted under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties up to life imprisonment and property confiscation in aggravated cases. Recovery is not barred by formal documentation where substantive fraud or evasion is established; false documentation creates unlimited clawback exposure and criminal liability.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB exchange rate regime with current account convertibility broadly permitted while the capital account remains strictly controlled. The WFOE, as a foreign-invested enterprise under the Foreign Investment Law, may receive foreign direct investment equity injections into a designated FDI capital account only after the receiving bank has confirmed SAFE registration. RMB conversion for genuine trade, service, salary, tax and related current account payments is permitted against authentic transaction documentation; however, every conversion must pass truthfulness, consistency and completeness checks by the settlement bank. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted only after the annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax payment, board approval, and presentation of audited financial statements and tax clearance certificates. Since 2012, there is no forced surrender of export receipts, so retained foreign currency may sit in a foreign currency settlement account or be sold at market rates. The main hurdles are the matching of each capital and current account payment to the underlying customs declarations, the prohibition on using unverified FDI capital proceeds for short-term working capital, the requirement to register intercompany foreign debt with SAFE under the macroprudential cross-border financing framework, and the need for a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification opinion before any capital account funds are transferred for daily operations. All fund flows require clear and consistent documentary evidence, making RMB not freely convertible outside licensed Chinese banking channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio or mandatory conversion ratio applies to export proceeds under SAFE rules effective since 2012; the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign currency receipts in an approved foreign currency settlement account or sell them voluntarily to a licensed bank at market rates. Under SAFE's goods trade monitoring system, export collection is generally expected to occur within 90 days from the customs export declaration date; if collection is expected to exceed that window, the enterprise must file the appropriate extension or exception reporting in the Trade Monitoring System. The receiving bank must verify that the SWIFT payment instruction, commercial invoice, export contract, transport documents and the electronically matched customs declaration correspond in value, commodity name and currency code before crediting the export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange control regulator for the People's Republic of China; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) oversees the Hong Kong dollar and the offshore Hong Kong treasury node associated with the parent entity.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for onshore Mainland treasury purposes. Offshore digital EMIs, including Hong Kong licensed virtual banks and global payment fintech platforms, are not authorized by the PBOC or SAFE to maintain RMB accounts for, or clear trade and capital payments on behalf of, a Mainland-incorporated WFOE. They may serve the Hong Kong parent for HKD and USD liquidity pooling, vendor payments or receivables aggregation outside mainland borders, but every inbound capital injection, export collection, import settlement and RMB conversion must ultimately traverse a licensed Mainland commercial bank connected to SAFE. The WFOE should therefore not rely on an EMI as its primary account layer for this stage; an EMI is legally constrained to non-mainland treasury sub-functions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is statutorily triggered under Public Notice [2016] No. 42 when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200,000,000 for tangible property transfers; RMB 100,000,000 for intangible property transfers or financial asset transfers; RMB 40,000,000 for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required when total related-party transactions in any year reach RMB 1,000,000,000 or when other specified multinational group criteria are met. Simultaneous filing of Country-by-Country Report applies to groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5,500,000,000 in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not impose a statutory ESR regime equivalent to the Hong Kong or UAE Economic Substance Regulations, but the PRC tax authorities enforce substantive business purpose through the General Anti-Abuse Rule under the CIT Law, the Beneficial Ownership rules in Public Notice [2018] No. 9, and transfer pricing substance requirements in Public Notice [2016] No. 42. The WFOE must demonstrate that it owns and controls the core functions, assets and risks that generate oat milk manufacturing income, including production planning, quality control, global packaging specification, procurement, logistics coordination and distribution decisions. If the Hong Kong parent merely holds shares and invoice flows without personnel or decision-making substance, the PRC tax authority can disregard the stated commercial form, reattribute profits, deny treaty benefits, and require the recharacterization of dividends or royalty payments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Shopping / Lack of Commercial Substance

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may invoke General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 123 of the Implementation Regulations, deny treaty benefits under Public Notice [2018] No. 9, recharacterize the distribution as a form of hidden profit allocation, and impose revised income tax, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Foreign and local directors who approved the false substance certification or signed the dividend resolution may be held jointly and severally liable for the underpaid tax under corporate veil-piercing doctrine; their personal bank accounts and property in China can be seized or frozen, and they may be placed on the joint disciplinary blacklist restricting exit, high-consumption activities, travel visas, and future directorships. If the falsity is intentional and the evaded tax exceeds criminal thresholds, directors face criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment, heavier 3-7 years for huge amounts, and confiscation of assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 114 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law require the tax authority to make an arm's-length transfer pricing adjustment; Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and Public Notice [2016] No. 42 impose penalties and mandatory interest on the resulting additional tax, with the interest surcharge no less than the benchmark lending rate. The failure to maintain contemporaneous documentation exposes the taxpayer to an upward adjustment without reliance on the taxpayer's data, additional interest, and a specific penalty up to 5 times short-payment or similar monetary sanctions.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally signed intercompany agreements at non-arm's-length prices or who destroyed/twinned documentation are jointly responsible for the tax shortfall, civil damages, and restitution to the Chinese treasury. Under cross-jurisdictional enforcement, their assets in Hong Kong may be subjected to recovery proceedings as fraudulent transfer or unlawful depletion of corporate assets. Criminal liability for false accounting and tax evasion applies where the adjusted tax exceeds the statutory threshold, carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years, together with personal fines of up to RMB 500,000, and fixed-term travel bans/blacklisting until the tax, penalties and interest are settled.
AML, False Treaty Claim and Illegal Cross-Border Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: This triggers the State Administration of Foreign Exchange Regulations and Tax Collection Administration Law; the tax authority may disallow treaty relief, impose withholding tax plus interest, and impose administrative penalties of 0.5% to 0.3% of irregular foreign exchange conversion amounts. If the false conduct reaches criminal threshold, Money Laundering Liability arises under Article 191 of the Criminal Law, with fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years for aggravated offences; fraudulent tax treaty benefit claims may also constitute tax fraud and customs smuggling if export declarations were false.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced to render directors personally liable for the civil restitution of all avoided withholding tax, foreign exchange penalties, and damages. Directors may be subjected to asset freezing, exit bans from China, curtailment of travel to Hong Kong and Macau, and HK-SAR blacklisting under cross-border cooperation. Criminal sanctions include imprisonment of between 3 and 10 years for money laundering or tax fraud, disqualification from managing any enterprise in China, and confiscation of personal benefits derived from the illegal transfer scheme.
