Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn (for VAT, corporate income tax, withholding and export refunds). Customs duties and export clearance are administered by the General Administration of Customs (GACC) via the International Trade Single Window (chinaport.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

For a foreign-invested limited liability company (WFOE), the unified social credit code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation upon business incorporation serves as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax matters. Procedure: (1) Incorporate the WFOE with the local Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the business license containing an 18-digit USCC; (2) The tax authority automatically receives the registration data, but the entity must complete supplementary tax registration within 30 days through the e-Tax platform by uploading the business license, passport copies of the legal representative and shareholders, articles of association, registered address proof, and bank account information; (3) Activate the taxpayer account online, selecting the VAT and corporate income tax regimes; (4) For export activities, complete separate registration with the General Administration of Customs to obtain the customs declaration registration code (used for export declarations) and register with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the foreign exchange receipt monitoring system. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (e.g., the Hong Kong parent) that derives Chinese-source income but has no permanent establishment, the Chinese WFOE or other withholding agent must have a tax ID to withhold and remit tax; the non-resident may also obtain a direct TIN by applying to the STA through the e-tax system, submitting a certificate of China income derivation and its Hong Kong tax residence certificate (for treaty benefits).

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (annual taxable sales) is the mandatory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below this, small-scale taxpayer status applies (currently simplified 3% levy). However, any entity with a business location in China must complete tax registration upon establishment, irrespective of turnover threshold; the RMB 5 million threshold triggers VAT status change, not initial registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Article 2 of the Provisional Regulations on VAT, exported goods (including processed frozen dim sum and dumplings) are eligible for a zero rating, which is implemented through a refund of the input VAT already paid on exported goods (export tax rebate). Eligibility requires: (1) the exporter is a registered VAT taxpayer with export licence and customs registration; (2) the goods are actually shipped out of the Chinese customs territory and customs declarations are complete; (3) the proceeds are received and repatriated in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules within the deadline; (4) the export goods are listed in the catalogue of goods eligible for export rebate (process food products such as frozen dumplings are normally eligible); (5) for goods manufactured by the WFOE, the factory’s VAT invoices and cost structures must support the refund claim. Offshore digital services are not applicable to this factory operation, but qualifying cross-border sales of software could be zero-rated under the VAT zero-rating rules for cross-border services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration) with the customs release stamp and electronic data transmitted to the State Taxation Administration
  • Bill of Lading (or Airway Bill) issued by the carrier evidencing actual export from a Chinese port or airport and showing the WFOE as shipper
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer, containing the full product description (frozen dim sum/dumplings), quantity, unit price, total value, INCO terms and country of origin
  • Packing List and weight list
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate (bank settlement slip from the designated forex bank or the electronic record in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange monitoring system) proving repatriation of the export proceeds
  • VAT special invoice (or export invoice) matching the declared goods and the export customs declaration
  • Export sales contract or purchase order signed by the overseas buyer

China mandates electronic issuance through the 'Golden Tax IV' (Jinshui IV) VAT invoice system. All VAT special invoices must be generated using the tax-control U-key (fiscal device) and uploaded to the State Taxation Administration's central database in near real-time. Since 2021, the newly established 'fully digitalized electronic invoice' (digital e-invoice) standard has been rolled out; new WFOEs in pilot areas issue these directly via the e-Tax platform, with automated validation against the national invoice inspection platform. Business-to-business invoices must include the buyer's unified social credit code. There is no separate physical pre-clearance stamping; fiscal clearance is achieved by the mandatory real-time data upload and the invoice's approval status in the national platform. For export transactions, the customs export declaration data is electronically transmitted to the tax authority and cross-matched with the issued invoices and foreign exchange receipts.

The tax authorities perform post-clearance audits using the cross-matching of export customs declarations, VAT invoices and foreign exchange receipts. If a zero-rated export claim is found to be fraudulent or the required supporting documents (customs release, bill of lading, forex payment) cannot be substantiated, the tax authority will: (1) claw back all exported VAT refunds paid, with interest from the date of refund payment at the statutory tax delinquency rate (currently 0.05% per day); (2) impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of tax sought to be evaded; (3) where the export is deemed domestic sales, apply the full VAT rate on the export receipts; (4) suspend or revoke the enterprise’s export tax rebate qualification; (5) refer criminal liability where the refund obtained exceeds RMB 50,000, with potential imprisonment. Additionally, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within 180 days without a valid extension may disqualify the export from zero-rating, requiring VAT to be paid as domestic sales.

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with pervasive, prudential foreign exchange controls. RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, and intra-company operational remittances, but the capital account remains heavily regulated. For the Cayman-style Hong Kong parent investing into this WFOE, the primary capital account routes are equity capital injection and shareholder loans, both of which require SAFE registration, domestic bank verification, and permissible purpose declarations. The WFOE's registered capital in foreign currency must be credited into a dedicated capital account at a licensed domestic bank, and although China has adopted discretionary settlement ('willing settlement') regulations, proceeds cannot be used for prohibited purposes such as entrusted loans, corporate acquisition beyond operating needs, or speculative real estate dealings. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder in the form of dividends is treated as a current account item: the WFOE must submit audited financial statements, a board resolution, tax settlement certificates, and prove that the earnings arose from genuine operational activity; banks will scrutinize the corporate income tax already paid, including the 10% withholding tax, which may be reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong DTA if the beneficial owner and substantive requirements are satisfied, thereby imposing documentary substance verification. Export dim sum proceeds are current account revenue, which must be repatriated into a designated export foreign currency account; exporters may retain the funds abroad only within SAFE-defined annual quotas and with documentary justification. Although the regime has liberalized significantly, any outbound payment not obviously underlying a bona fide trade transaction, intellectual property royalty, or service contract is extremely restricted. The WFOE cannot freely transfer foreign currency to mobile fintech wallets or offshore EMIs without a physical underlying transaction, and any monthly or quarterly cross-border flows beyond the declared trade cycle will trigger automatic bank compliance reviews. For treasury planning, the WFOE should anticipate that every major outbound flow (dividend, intercompany royalty, capital reduction, or liquidation distribution) will require a demonstrable paper trail, advance tax clearance, and branch-level approval. In practice, liquidity managers must plan 7-15 business days for required documentary checks, and must not assume same-day FX execution for non-trade related capital remittances unless pre-negotiated T+1 turntimes are embedded in the bank's service level agreement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, export proceeds arising from the sale of prefabricated dim sum must be repatriated to a domestic foreign currency account within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, so the WFOE holds 0% surrender obligation and may either retain the USD/HKD earnings or voluntarily settle to RMB at spot rates. However, all receipts must be matched to the electronic customs export records in the SAFE monitoring system, and unmatched receivables older than 90 days require a written explanation, supporting shipping documents, and possibly an audit fee.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or DBS Treasury Prism have a Low feasibility rating as statutory bank account providers for this WFOE. Chinese corporate banking law and PBOC banking settlement rules require non-bank payment institutions to operate under a payment business license, and even licensed institutions may not open a domestic capital account or basic deposit account for a WFOE's statutory corporate identity. EMIs can only serve as supplementary payment gateways for cross-border e-commerce collections, payment facilitation, or supply chain finance in partnership with a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE must maintain its primary treasury and capital verification accounts with a domestic licensed bank. Any attempt to use an offshore EMI as the primary settlement rail for capital injection, tax payment, or dividend distribution would violate SAFE's account classification system and PBOC's Anti-Money Laundering (AML) administrative measures, exposing the entity to account freezing, penalties, and potential revocation of the WFOE operating license.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The initial capital injection from Hong Kong must be wired using SWIFT MT103 with a specific purpose code (e.g., 'CHN-1626' for foreign direct investment, plus the correct FX code for the currency). The remittance must originate from the HK parent's bank account in the parent's name, never from a third-party or a client account; include the contract number and a reference to the CA20 Foreign-Invested Enterprise filing. The receiving domestic bank in China will lodge the funds temporarily in the 'Capital Escrow Account' and verify the signature against the SAFE registration form. For an amount exceeding USD 500,000 equivalent, the bank will require a copy of the FIE approval certificate (or the business license with a 'Foreign Investment' stamp), the written application for capital settlement, and the filed Articles of Association. After receipt, the WFOE must complete a capital verification report by a licensed Chinese CPA to claim fiscal and legal standing. Inbound funds designated as shareholder loans will be treated as foreign debt and require a cross-border financing quota registration with SAFE before the loan is disbursed. For the cash conversion cycle, the treasury should time the injection at least 3 business days before any planned payment commitment.For operating expenses and domestic supply chain payments, WFOE will predominantly use CNAPS (China Advanced Payment System) for RMB wires, but cross-border disbursements for raw materials, packaging, or cold-chain logistics secured from overseas suppliers should be executed via SWIFT MT103 with the participant bank's 'cross-border RMB or foreign currency' payment rail. Trade settlement may also be done via SWIFT MT700 (irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit) for high-value or freight-intensive shipments; an L/C is recommended for dim sum exports to mitigate payment risk. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, a Chinese taxpayer must prepare a Local File when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000. Master File obligations arise where the Chinese enterprise falls within the SAT's group documentation scope, typically where the group has significant Mainland-related-party turnover and cross-border intercompany dealings; contemporaneous documentation must be filed with the annual EIT return upon written notice.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE is a PRC tax resident and, as an operating frozen-food factory, must demonstrate real economic substance in China through physical plant, employees, production utilities, inventory risk, and procurement and sales decisions. For the Hong Kong intermediate shareholder, the Hong Kong tax regime requires economic substance under the territorial source principle and the relevant Hong Kong economic substance requirements; the HK company must show Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, including management, decision-making, financing, intellectual-property ownership, and risk control. A HK shell without employees, office, or operational substance cannot claim reduced treaty withholding and will be disregarded or treated as a resident of the Mainland under the place of effective management and general anti-avoidance rules.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 empowers the State Taxation Administration to re-determine taxable income on an arm's-length basis; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable, with imprisonment of up to 3 years for serious cases and 3 to 7 years for extremely large evasion with a proportion above 30%, plus criminal fines.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false financial reports and direct the pricing scheme can be personally liable under the Company Law piercing-the-veil doctrine if the corporate form is abused to evade tax or defeat creditors; personal exposure includes civil asset restitution, joint and several liability for the tax shortfall and penalties, travel bans and social-credit blacklisting restricting air, rail, and overseas travel, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion as directly responsible persons.
Thin Capitalization and Disguised Equity

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 46 and the implementation rules disallow interest on excessive debt unless the taxpayer proves the loan terms and interest rate are arm's length; the tax authority adjusts taxable income and imposes late-payment surcharge and Article 63 penalties. Failure to withhold the 10% withholding tax on the interest to the Hong Kong lender also triggers Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69, which imposes a fine of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign and approve the sham loan agreements and certify false debt-equity calculations face veil-piercing civil liability for the collective tax debt and restitution of diverted assets; they are exposed to travel restrictions and blacklisting through the joint-punishment system, and, if the thin-capitalization structure is an intentional tax-evasion vehicle, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment for the directly responsible officers.
Shell Holding Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership clause and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, recharacterizing the distribution at the domestic 10% withholding rate (or 20% nominal rate where applicable), and impose late-payment surcharge and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 further allows a fine of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount against the WFOE as withholding agent, and the shell company may be found to be a PRC resident enterprise if its place of effective management is in Mainland China, causing the HK entity to become fully taxable in China.

Director personal liability: The directors of both the WFOE and the Hong Kong shell are exposed to veil-piercing for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties, civil asset restitution, blacklisting and travel bans affecting business operations, and criminal liability under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 211 for deliberately preventing tax collection; imprisonment of up to 7 years and criminal fines attach to directly responsible persons where the shell structure is used as an instrument of fraudulent tax evasion.
