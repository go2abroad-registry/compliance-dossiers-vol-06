Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a general WFOE manufacturing company under the Foreign Investment Law; however, under the 2023 Company Law the shareholder must fully pay the subscribed capital within five years from the date of establishment and the amount must be reasonable for the stated business scope. E-government filing portal

For the mainland China WFOE: (1) complete company incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is accepted by STA as the taxpayer identification number (TIN); (2) complete statutory tax registration with the local tax authority within 30 days of the date of the business license through the e-Tax portal or tax service hall; (3) obtain small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status; if projected annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, apply for general VAT taxpayer registration; (4) register for the VAT invoice system and electronic invoice issuance through the STA National Electronic Invoice Platform; (5) if the WFOE exports goods, register for export tax refund/exemption status and complete export filing with the local tax authority. Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer but may elect general status upon application.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods, including self-heating exothermic-reaction instant meals, are eligible for the VAT export zero-rating and export tax exemption/refund regime where the goods are actually exported from mainland China's customs territory to a destination outside mainland China, including Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, the export transaction is genuine and supported by customs clearance records, the seller is a VAT-registered taxpayer, the goods are not sold or consumed within mainland China, and export proceeds are repatriated in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules within the prescribed period. Manufacturing exporters apply the exemption-credit-refund method under the export tax rebate system, with input VAT on the exported goods refundable only where the full statutory documentary chain and foreign exchange receipt records are maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) / Export Goods Customs Declaration
  • Export contract or sales confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Bank-issued export proceeds receipt record or Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) equivalent accepted by the settling bank under SAFE exchange-control rules
  • Export invoice issued for the export sale
  • Certificate of Origin where preferential tariff or origin claims are made
  • Food safety, health, or inspection certificate issued by the competent authority if required for the export of food products

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-controlled issuance channels. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are generated on the National Electronic Invoice Platform under the Golden Tax Project Phase IV architecture with real-time transmission of invoice data, automated fiscal clearance checks, and assignment of a unique electronic invoice number and digital signature. General VAT taxpayers must use the VAT special invoice system and are required to transmit issuance data to the STA in real time through the electronic tax administration interface; paper invoices carry tax-control stamps, while digital invoices rely on the electronic seal and STA anti-fraud signature. No offline VAT invoice may be created outside the STA system; red/offset invoices are used for corrections, and API integration with the e-tax platform is required for automated invoice issuance and customs/export data matching.

The STA and China Customs will jointly audit the export documentation, physical logistics, production capacity, and foreign-exchange receipt records underlying zero-rated export claims. If the export zero-rating or refund is found to be unsupported, fraudulent, or dependent on non-repatriated proceeds, the tax authority will disallow the zero rating, require conversion of the export into a taxable domestic sale, claw back all VAT refunded, add statutory late-payment surcharge at the daily rate of 0.05% of the tax due, and impose an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to five times the tax evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious cases involving fictitious export transactions or forged documentation may be prosecuted as export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law, with liability for imprisonment and confiscation of assets, and the exporter may be removed from the export tax rebate qualification list.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, current account transactions are convertible on a real-trade basis, whereas capital account transactions remain controlled. The mainland WFOE can receive equity capital from its Hong Kong parent only after SAFE-registered FDI information is updated at the receiving bank, and the funds must enter a designated capital account. Conversion of injected capital into RMB is permitted under the capital account settlement instruction system, but must be used for genuine operational purposes within the company's business scope and cannot be deployed into securities, wealth-management products, or prohibited sectors. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is possible after the annual statutory audit and tax clearance; the bank must verify audited financial statements, a board resolution approving the dividend, and SAFE reporting. Export receipts must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange current account and matched against customs declaration data through the trade monitoring system; although no blanket surrender is imposed, the collection-within-declared-period requirement functions as a soft convertibility restriction. All conversions, foreign payments and hedging activities require a genuine underlying transaction, and any deviation or false declaration triggers comprehensive penalties, suspension of FX operations, and adverse regulatory classification. Private money changers and offshore EMIs cannot lawfully provide onshore settlement rails for the WFOE, so treasury routing must remain within licensed onshore commercial banks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to the WFOE's own domestic foreign-exchange current account within the collection period declared through SAFE's banking administration service system, normally within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration unless an extension is approved for valid commercial reasons. There is currently no statutory percentage of proceeds that must be surrendered to the central bank; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero, and the WFOE may retain export receipts in foreign currency or voluntarily convert them into RMB at the onshore market rate. Any conversion must be supported by the corresponding customs declaration, commercial invoice, transport documents and a matching entry in the trade-in-goods receipts and payments monitoring system. Non-repatriation, false declaration, or use of unlicensed intermediaries is treated as a serious foreign-exchange violation under the Foreign Exchange Penalty Provisions.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. In mainland China, no unlicensed fintech EMI may hold deposits or process onshore RMB and foreign-currency current-account transactions for a WFOE. Hong Kong-licensed or Singapore-licensed EMIs are not recognized by SAFE as eligible settlement institutions for the WFOE's export proceeds, capital injection, or operational disbursements. They may serve only as treasury overlay tools for the Hong Kong parent, but cannot legally replace an onshore domestic commercial bank. Routing the WFOE's export collections through an offshore EMI would violate current-account collection rules and create irreconcilable mismatches in customs verification, tax returns and SAFE monitoring.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity capital from the Hong Kong parent must be wired to the WFOE's FDCA capital account at a licensed domestic commercial bank under SAFE FDI registration. The remittance instruction must carry the correct SAFE FX purpose code for inbound direct investment, and the receiving bank will verify the registered FDI information, investor identity, amount, currency and account type. After receipt, a PRC-licensed certified public accountant must prepare a capital verification report in accordance with PRC accounting standards. Conversion of the capital account funds into RMB is permitted only after the bank checks the user-receipt instruction and confirms the declared purpose falls within the WFOE's approved business scope. Every payment must be matched with customs import and export declarations, commercial contracts, invoices, packing lists and transport documents. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends distributed by the Chinese WFOE to a non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China, under Articles 3(3), 27(3) and 37 of the Corporate Income Tax Law. A reduced 5% rate applies under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement where the Hong Kong resident beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the WFOE's issued shares and satisfies substantive economic substance requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory if annual related-party tangible asset transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible or financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion under SAT Public Notice 2016 No.42.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 of the Corporate Income Tax Law and the beneficial ownership criteria in SAT Public Notice 2018 No.9. The mainland WFOE must carry on genuine manufacturing, procurement, inventory, sales support and financing functions, with Core Income-Generating Activities located in China. The Hong Kong parent must demonstrate substantive Hong Kong activity for treaty relief and, under Hong Kong's foreign-sourced income exemption rules, meet the applicable substantial activity test; a letter-box holding company with no CIGA will be denied treaty benefits and its income may be re-sourced or recharacterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Evasion and Non-Documentation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment under Article 41 of the Corporate Income Tax Law, impose late interest at 0.05% per day under Article 32 of the Law on Tax Administration, and impose a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax under Article 63. Fraudulent conduct may lead to criminal prosecution under Article 201 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly direct or approve the pricing scheme face personal joint liability where the corporate veil is pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law. The legal representative may be placed under exit control or blacklisted for outstanding taxes. Criminal liability for responsible officers can include restitution, criminal fines, and imprisonment of up to seven years for aggravated tax evasion.
Withholding Tax Failure and Sham Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: The withholding agent is liable for the uncollected tax and may be penalised under Article 69 of the Law on Tax Administration with a fine of 50% to three times the tax that should have been withheld. Denial of treaty benefits exposes the WFOE to the full 10% source withholding, plus interest and penalties for late payment.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who cause the company to remit income without withholding can be held personally liable in collection proceedings where company assets are insufficient. Exit restrictions, travel bans and publication on the State Taxation Administration's major tax violation blacklist apply to responsible legal representatives. Criminal fraud penalties can include imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets.
Shell Company and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: China may disregard the Hong Kong entity under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 and reformulate the income as China-source, subject to 25% Corporate Income Tax and withholding tax, plus interest and penalties. Hong Kong anti-avoidance provisions under sections 61 and 61A of the Inland Revenue Ordinance may also assess the Hong Kong entity on previously exempt passive income where no substantial activity exists.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Chinese WFOE and the Hong Kong parent risk personal civil liability for restitution of diverted assets, disqualification orders, travel bans and blacklisting. Where the structure is found fraudulent, directorial criminal liability can include fines and imprisonment under PRC criminal law principles for tax evasion and fraudulent tax arrangements.
