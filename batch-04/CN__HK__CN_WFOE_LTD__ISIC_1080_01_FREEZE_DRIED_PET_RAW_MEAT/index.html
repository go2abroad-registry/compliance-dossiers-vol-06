Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

A virtual office or commercial mailbox is generally not acceptable for a WFOE engaged in vacuum freeze-dried pet food manufacturing. The PRC Company Law and the Regulations on the Administration of Registration of Market Entities require a registered address that is a fixed, verifiable physical premises capable of receiving legal process and official correspondence, and the local tax authority and banks will reject a purely virtual address as a registered business address. For manufacturing operations, the registered address must correspond to an actual production site or an identifiable administrative/operational office in the same locality; a virtual mailbox may at most serve as ancillary correspondence accommodation but cannot satisfy the statutory registered address requirement. Post-establishment, the local market supervision bureau and tax authority may require the lease agreement, property ownership certificate, and proof of fire safety and environmental compliance for the physical premises, so the structure must reserve budget for a lease deposit and physical fit-out.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum statutory paid-up capital is required for a PRC WFOE under current law; the shareholder shall subscribe to the registered capital stated in the Articles of Association and contribute it in accordance with the agreed schedule. No escrowed or blocked capital account is mandated by PRC company law. Foreign currency contributions must be remitted through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account and converted into RMB at the designated bank. If the shareholder is a Hong Kong SAR company, the Hong Kong entity is only required to maintain the nominal issued capital required by its own articles, typically HKD 1, while the mainland WFOE capital terms are independently set forth in the mainland Articles of Association.

Funding & unlock path: Fire-protection design review and completion acceptance by the local Fire and Rescue Department; Civil Defense fire permit for the factory and warehouse; structural load certificate for freeze-drying racks and cold storage; workplace safety pre-evaluation and occupational disease hazard monitoring under the Work Safety Law and the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases; and special equipment registration for pressure vessels, boilers, and refrigeration systems.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and invoice management via the STA's e-Tax Bureau online taxpayer portal (www.chinatax.gov.cn) and the VAT Invoice Integrated Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor notarizes and authenticates its constituent documents and subscribes the registered capital of a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Step 2: Upon incorporation, SAMR issues the WFOE a business license containing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically recognized as the enterprise's taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax and VAT purposes. Step 3: The competent district or municipal tax authority completes tax registration ex officio via the SAMR-STA data sharing interface, without any separate tax registration application, and issues an electronic Tax Registration Certificate. Step 4: The WFOE applies to the competent tax authority for VAT General Taxpayer status, obtains its VAT invoice issuance quota, and activates a digital e-invoice certificate on the VAT Invoice Integrated Service Platform. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China, including the WFOE's immediate parent if it receives Chinese-source income such as dividends or royalties, the withholding agent must register the offshore entity and obtain a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Identification Number (NRE TIN) under the withholding registration procedure before any payment. Step 6: The WFOE completes foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for current account trade receipts, ensuring that future export proceeds can be legally repatriated and credited to its domestic bank account.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating applies to the WFOE's sale of vacuum freeze-dried pet treats and meal kibble (ISIC 1080 / NAICS 311111) where the goods physically depart China's customs territory, are sold under a genuine export contract to a non-resident buyer, are settled in foreign currency, and are not subsequently resold or used domestically. The WFOE must be a VAT General Taxpayer and must hold all statutory licenses for pet food production and compendium of export inspection and quarantine approvals.

  • Bill of Lading (original shipped-on-board bill of lading, or air waybill for air freight)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange settlement receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds in convertible currency

All VAT-taxable sales of freeze-dried pet food manufactured by the WFOE must be invoiced through the STA's VAT Invoice Integrated Service Platform using either tax-controlled equipment or fully digitalized electronic invoices (e-invoices). Invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA, and each e-invoice is embedded with an anti-counterfeit tax control code and an authorized digital fiscal stamp. For export transactions, the General Administration of Customs transmits electronic export declaration data directly to the STA, enabling cross-agency data matching for export VAT zero-rating determinations.

The STA and General Administration of Customs jointly conduct post-export audits of zero-rating claims. If any supporting document is missing, inconsistent, or unverifiable, the tax authority disallows the zero-rating, claws back the VAT rebate or exempt output tax, and imposes a late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day from the original refund date. Where fraud or false refund claims are established, the WFOE faces administrative penalties of 1 to 5 times the tax amount involved, revocation of export tax rebate qualification, downgrading to D-level tax credit status, and mandatory tax audit for at least three consecutive years. Criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax rebate evasion may result in fines, confiscation of illegal gains, and imprisonment of responsible officers for up to life in aggravated cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is largely convertible on the current account when genuine trade and service transactions can be verified by a licensed domestic bank, but the capital account remains strictly controlled. A mainland WFOE may freely settle export receipts for goods, pay import bills for raw meat and production inputs, and remit service fees once the underlying contract, customs declaration, invoice and tax documentation are authenticated by the settlement bank. By contrast, capital account inflows such as foreign direct investment equity injections, external debt, and certain cross-border guarantees require prior or post-event SAFE registration, bank-level electronic monitoring, and strict use rules for the resulting RMB settlement funds. The WFOE cannot arbitrarily convert CFD or bond proceeds into speculative onshore investments without meeting the designated business purpose. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current-account dividend item and is legally permissible without a separate SAFE quota, but the onshore bank must validate the audited financial statements, the resolved dividend distribution, proof that corporate income tax has been fully paid, and the beneficial ownership of the Hong Kong shareholder. In practice, remittance is delayed until the Chinese tax clearance is issued and the WFOE can prove that the dividend is derived from genuine after-tax retained earnings. Where the Hong Kong parent seeks the reduced 5% withholding tax rate under the Mainland/Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement, a Hong Kong tax residence certificate and substance evidence must be filed or the full statutory 10% rate will be withheld. The one-way convertibility history has shifted toward supervision of real demand rather than mandatory surrender, yet all cross-border currency movements remain subject to PBOC/SAFE data monitoring through SWIFT instruction reporting and the Foreign Exchange Business Information System.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign currency proceeds in a domestic foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily sell them to a licensed commercial bank at the prevailing interbank rate. Statutory repatriation requires export proceeds to be brought onshore and credited to a mainland account within six months from the date of the customs export declaration unless SAFE grants an extension for documented commercial reasons. For freeze-dried pet raw meat exports, the bank will match each inward remittance against the customs declaration, the commercial invoice, the packing list, and the logistics confirmation before allowing settlement into RMB.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating is legally barred as a primary settlement rail. Mainland China does not issue a banking or electronic money institution licence that permits overseas digital EMIs to open corporate operational accounts, hold WFOE deposits as bank funds, or provide proprietary foreign exchange conversion. A Hong Kong-licensed virtual bank or an offshore EMI such as those operating in Singapore or the United Kingdom cannot service the mainland WFOE as a bank of record without a licensed domestic branch and SAFE/PBOC approval. Non-bank cross-border payment institutions may only act as ancillary payment processors that collect buyer proceeds from global e-commerce platforms or remit supplier payments through a white-labelled partnership with a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE must therefore maintain its primary treasury, FX settlement, capital account, and clearing relationship with a domestic commercial bank; fintech rails are acceptable only for platform-based customer collections which settle into the designated onshore bank account.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the equity injection directly from its own bank account in Hong Kong via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account maintained with a designated Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The remitter name must exactly match the SAFE-registered shareholder; third-party funding is unacceptable. Prior to remittance, the WFOE must complete the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment registration through the bank and obtain an FDI control number, after which the bank credits the funds as direct investment equity. The remittance instruction shall carry the standard SAFE capital account transaction code for direct injection of foreign direct investment, and for cross-border renminbi contributions the transaction must also be filed in PBOC's Cross-Border Payment and Receipt Management Information System (RCPMIS). Upon receipt, the bank places the funds in the capital settlement pending-use account and a Chinese certified public accountant issues a capital verification report confirming that the full registered capital contribution was received at the audited spot exchange rate. The WFOE may then convert or disburse funds only for permitted operating purposes such as raw meat purchases, rent, salaries, tax, and production overheads, and every RMB settlement payment from the capital source account is monitored by SAFE.Supplier payments for raw meat, packaging, and production equipment should be executed by SWIFT MT103 over the bank's trade finance channels, with the commercial invoice and purchase order matched by the bank to the customs import declaration or a written explanation where the shipment is pre-cleared. For large or recurring commodity purchases, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued at sight is a safer clearing rail because the negotiating bank verifies bills of lading, sanitary certificates, weight certificates, and phytosanitary documentation against the LC terms. Documentary collections with payment against documents provide a middle-ground rail when the WFOE has established supplier credit. On the export side, the WFOE should procure its freeze-dried pet food sales using standard open account payments settled by SWIFT TT; each inbound payment must be reconciled by the bank against the customs export declaration and invoice. The transaction must carry the current-account goods trade purpose code and any settlement to RMB is permissible after the bank validates the actual export of the goods from the mainland customs record. A Hong Kong-licensed virtual bank or an offshore EMI such as those operating in Singapore or the United Kingdom cannot service the mainland WFOE as a bank of record without a licensed domestic branch and SAFE/PBOC approval. Non-bank cross-border payment institutions may only act as ancillary payment processors that collect buyer proceeds from global e-commerce platforms or remit supplier payments through a white-labelled partnership with a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The WFOE must therefore maintain its primary treasury, FX settlement, capital account, and clearing relationship with a domestic commercial bank; fintech rails are acceptable only for platform-based customer collections which settle into the designated onshore bank account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 6, contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory where annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property sales/purchases, RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transactions, or RMB 40 million for other controlled transactions; a Master File is additionally required where the multinational group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and the taxpayer is engaged in related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: China applies a general anti-abuse rule under Article 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Notice [2009] No. 2 to disregard arrangements lacking commercial substance. The WFOE must have real manufacturing activity, factory equipment, employees, risk-bearing capacity, and decision-making functions. The Hong Kong shareholder must also meet the Hong Kong economic substance expectation for foreign-source income claims, including adequate staff, premises, and actual management in Hong Kong; failure to establish Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong will expose the arrangement to DTA denial and Chinese special tax adjustment.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and artificial profit shifting through Hong Kong shell routing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late payment surcharge. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 100,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the amount due, with imprisonment of up to seven years where the amount is large and the conduct is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law (Article 21 as re-enacted in the 2023 Company Law) where directors abuse the limited-liability structure to evade taxes; directors, actual controllers, and shadow managers are jointly personally liable to restore diverted assets, may be placed on the State Taxation Administration tax blacklist with travel bans and restriction on departure, and may face criminal imprisonment, fines, and civil restitution for fraud, false invoicing, or evasion.
DTA treaty abuse and sham beneficial ownership of Hong Kong dividends

Statutory sanction: Denial of DTA relief under the beneficial ownership provisions of STA Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the General Anti-Abuse Rule under PRC EIT Law Article 47; the difference between the 10% domestic rate and the claimed 5% treaty rate, or the entire dividend withholding where the arrangement is treated as a sham, is assessed with underpayment penalties of 50% to 500% and interest. Criminal prosecution may be initiated if the avoidance crosses the intentional tax-evasion threshold.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly executed the treaty-shopping structure are personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties where the court finds that the corporate veil was abused; they may be subjected to blacklisting, departure restrictions, asset preservation orders, and criminal liability for tax evasion if the false DTA claim is characterised as fraudulent deception of the tax authority.
Cross-border AML integration and fraudulent export VAT rebate on pet food commodities

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 204 punishes fraudulent export VAT rebates with imprisonment up to fifteen years and heavy fines for large amounts; Article 191 punishes money laundering with imprisonment up to ten years and fines; the tax authority revokes the export-tax-exemption and refund qualification and imposes a penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the falsely refunded or underpaid tax, with daily late-payment surcharges on unreported VAT.

Director personal liability: Directors and beneficial owners are jointly and severally liable for the fraudulent refund and concealed funds; criminal conviction leads to incarceration, confiscation of personal assets, a travel ban and blacklisting making exit from China impossible until the liability is satisfied, and civil liability for restitution to both the tax authority and affected investors or lending banks, with disqualification from acting as directors of any company in China.
