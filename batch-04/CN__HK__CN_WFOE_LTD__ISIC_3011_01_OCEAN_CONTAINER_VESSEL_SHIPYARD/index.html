Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an ordinary WFOE under the 2023 PRC Companies Law. The registered capital is the subscribed capital stated in the Articles of Association, and it need not be parked in a blocked escrow account for formation purposes. The shareholders must fully inject contributions within the period prescribed in the Articles of Association, which may not exceed five years from the date of incorporation. Foreign capital may be contributed in RMB or convertible foreign currency and must be reported to SAMR and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) within the statutory reporting windows. If the shipyard scope were to include military or restricted vessels, a separate MIIT/NDRC approval would be required before capital injection.

Funding & unlock path: The shipyard must obtain a Fire and Rescue clearance certificate covering production workshops, paint shops, gas stores, welding areas and warehouses, together with a structural load inspection certificate for dry docks, slipways, gantry cranes and heavy lifting platforms issued by a registered construction inspection unit. Cranes, forklifts, pressure vessels and compressed gas installations require registration and use licenses under the special equipment safety regulations. A work safety pre-assessment and safety production registration must be completed under the Work Safety Law, and occupational disease hazard pre-evaluation and control verification are required under the Law on Prevention and Control of Occupational Diseases. Fire alarms, sprinklers, emergency exits and emergency response plans must be filed with the Fire and Rescue Department before production operations begin.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of The People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local in-charge tax authority for the WFOE; electronic portal: the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform for taxpayer registration, filing, and VAT administration, complemented by the China International Trade Single Window for customs and export declaration integration. The Hong Kong SAR reference in the payload denotes the offshore parent/holding jurisdiction and does not replace PRC tax registration for the WFOE.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued at incorporation by the Market Supervision Administration; the USCC automatically becomes the tax registration number and VAT invoice identity code under the integrated one-stop business registration and tax registration system. The foreign non-resident parents/shareholders must obtain their own Chinese non-resident enterprise tax registration with the in-charge tax authority when they have withholding obligations on dividends, interest, royalties, or other PRC-sourced income, even though they are not required to be incorporated in China. Procedure: (1) incorporate the WFOE and receive the business license containing the USCC; (2) complete the one-stop tax registration through the electronic registration system or file with the local STA within 30 days of license issuance if not automatically integrated; (3) register the legal representative, financial controller, accounting system, bank account(s), and place of business; (4) apply for VAT general taxpayer status (or maintain small-scale taxpayer classification) through e-Tax; (5) for the Hong Kong SAR parent, register as a non-resident enterprise taxpayer and, if required, be assigned a non-resident enterprise withholding tax identification number before remitting cross-border payments; (6) maintain the taxpayer registration record and update any changes via e-Tax.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not recognize a check-the-box election; the WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a PRC tax resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, with worldwide entity-level taxation. Hybrid mismatch elections are not available in the PRC. For VAT only, an enterprise whose annual turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer, but that is an indirect tax status election, not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover (over any 12-month period) triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer for enterprises supplying goods or services; below that threshold, a taxpayer is generally classified as a small-scale taxpayer and may voluntarily apply for general taxpayer status. A foreign non-resident enterprise that makes taxable supplies in PRC (including cross-border digital or onshore goods/services) is required to register for PRC VAT or have its VAT collected by a withholding agent irrespective of the threshold when it has no place of establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer that sells and physically exports goods from the PRC customs territory, including a newly built ocean container vessel from a WFOE shipyard, is entitled to a 0% export VAT rate. Eligibility requires a direct export sale to an overseas customer evidenced by a valid export contract and commercial invoice; the vessel/goods must actually clear PRC customs under the statutory Export Goods Customs Declaration; the exporter must be the legal exporter of record; and export proceeds must be repatriated into China and verified in compliance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) requirements. The exporter then applies for a refund of remaining input VAT under the PRC export tax rebate mechanism (for a production enterprise, the Exemption, Credit and Refund method). Qualifying offshore services may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt if rendered to entities outside China, consumed overseas, and supported by contract, payment and performance documentation.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (export customs declaration submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and released by PRC Customs)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading (original or endorsed electronic equivalent) evidencing shipment/export of the vessel from PRC customs territory to a foreign consignee/port
  • Export contract or sales agreement and exporter-issued commercial invoice
  • VAT special input invoices or legal VAT deduction vouchers for materials, components, equipment, fuel, and taxable services incorporated into or used in the production of the exported vessel
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt record or export collection verification record issued by a designated foreign exchange bank showing the foreign currency proceeds from the export sale have been repatriated to China
  • Packing list and, when applicable, vessel transfer/title documentation evidencing transfer of ownership and physical movement of the newly built vessel outside PRC customs territory

China operates a nationwide digital VAT invoice system under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV framework. All VAT invoices, whether paper or digital, must be generated in the STA VAT Invoice Management System or through approved e-Tax invoicing channels; fully digitalized electronic invoices (digital e-invoices) are transmitted to STA in near real-time and carry the statutory invoice code and anti-fraud countermark/verification code generated by the tax authority system. Taxpayers must use STA-approved invoicing software or application programming interfaces for B2B/B2C issuance and cannot lawfully issue invoices without connection to the tax authority information system. Special VAT invoices are authenticated before crediting; all export-related invoicing must reconcile with customs declaration data.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are automatically cross-matched by STA Golden Tax IV with customs export declarations, bank collection records, and invoice data. If the exporter cannot substantiate the physical export, the export sale is disallowed; the VAT output tax is re-assessed at the standard rate, any refunded input VAT is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest (0.05% per calendar day), and monetary penalties are imposed under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection and its implementing rules. Where a false customs declaration or false invoice is used: Customs may confiscate illegal gains, impose fines under the Customs Law, and transfer the matter for criminal investigation; tax authorities may impose an administrative fine of up to 50% of the tax involved for non-fraudulent irregularities and up to five times the fraudulent refund for deliberate fraud; criminal liability applies for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law, including imprisonment and fines. The retrospective period is generally three years for underpayments arising from computational mistakes, five years for underpaid/over-refunded tax due to failure to file or disclosure issues, and unlimited in cases of fraud or tax evasion; customs enforcement may also re-open export declarations within the statutory customs verification period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime administered by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Current account items, including trade settlement, service fees, and general business operational expenses, are largely freely convertible and can be completed through designated foreign exchange banks under the electronic trade monitoring and verification system. However, capital account items remain strictly controlled. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) established in mainland China, the injection of registered capital from a Hong Kong parent company must be processed through a dedicated foreign exchange capital account opened at a domestic bank, with the inward remittance code and transaction purpose clearly matching the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration. The capital contribution must be recorded in the SAFE Business Registration Certificate and the bank will verify the authenticity of the underlying investment agreement. Once the capital is converted into RMB, the funds must be placed into a domestic Renminbi basic deposit account and restrictions apply on the scope of permitted uses, specifically prohibiting direct investment in domestic securities, wealth management products, or real estate beyond the registered business scope. Dividends and profits remitted to the Hong Kong shareholder require the WFOE to provide a cumulative tax clearance certificate, audited financial statements, and a genuine board resolution declaring distributable profits, and such remittances are subject to bank review but generally permitted if the source of profits is documented. Cross-border capital movements of the shipyard are closely scrutinized because the commercial shipbuilding sector is regarded as a strategic industry, and any pre-shipment advance payment from an overseas buyer needs a corresponding registration with the bank under the trade credit regulations. Overall, RMB is convertible on the current account, but the capital account is fundamentally restricted; entities must plan for documentary redundancy and timing delays of 3 to 10 business days for each regulated transaction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE principles, export proceeds are not subject to mandatory foreign currency surrender; a WFOE shipyard may retain foreign exchange earnings in its domestic foreign exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB through a designated bank. However, the export proceeds must be repatriated into China within the prescribed period; for normal goods trade, settlement must be completed within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless a deferred payment arrangement is registered with the bank. Advance payments received from overseas buyers for vessel construction, which is common in shipbuilding, must be monitored under the advance receipt quota system and must be matched against the corresponding export declaration within the required reconciliation period, typically 30 days after the actual export shipment date for final settlement and verification. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio for the RMB/FX component; the enterprise may elect the proportion to convert, subject to bank counterparty risk and the commercial terms of the underlying shipbuilding contract.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are legally barred from opening domestic RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts for a China-based WFOE and they cannot directly interface with the SAFE reporting systems; they also lack the required banking license under the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration and the Measures for the Administration of Bank Accounts. Their permissible services are limited to facilitating international payment messages or customer referral support only if a licensed domestic bank remains the account-holding institution. The operational reality is therefore low viability; any treasury architecture must depend on a state-owned Tier-1 commercial bank with SAFE-qualified status for direct clearing and reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must complete SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration and obtain the FDI Business Registration Certificate from the local SAFE branch. The capital injection must be remitted from a bank account in Hong Kong to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account at the nominated domestic Tier-1 bank using SWIFT MT103 with an accurate remittance information field containing the corporate name, purpose code for direct investment, and a reference to the registered investment contract. The receiving bank will verify the amount against the remaining unpaid registered capital, the SAFE registration details, and the anti-money laundering declaration on the source of funds. After the funds are received, the bank will handle the FX conversion into RMB, but conversion is not permitted for activities unrelated to the business license; the bank must also complete a foreign exchange settlement declaration and the WFOE must maintain a foreign exchange capital account management ledger. For any subsequent increase of registered capital, a new SAFE record must be obtained before the incremental funds can be wired, and repatriation of the original injection requires tax clearance and capital repatriation registration.Trade disbursements for the shipyard are executed through multiple rails: direct raw material and equipment imports can settle by standard SWIFT MT103 or MT202 instructions through the domestic bank, but all outward remittances for trade must present underlying invoices, customs declarations, or transport documents. For the sale of complete ocean container vessels to overseas buyers, the preferred rail is a Commercial Letter of Credit (LC) issued by the buyer's bank, which is confirmed by a domestic Chinese bank and structured with installment drawings upon achievement of keel laying, launched vessel, delivery of outfitting, and final vessel delivery milestones. Documentary Collections are acceptable for smaller spare parts transactions, but they carry payment risk for the exporter and are layered with the shipyard's trade credit insurance. For high-value domestic subcontractor payments, the WFOE will use domestic RMB clearing through the China National Advanced Payment System (CNAPS) and may apply for corporate cross-border RMB settlement use where counterparties are also in China. All cross-border payment instructions must contain proper transaction codes, contract references, invoice numbers, and a clear trade or capital purpose classification for SAFE monitoring.Real trade-backed forward foreign exchange contracts with a domestic authorized bank, strictly aligned with actual receivables or payables of the shipyardCross-currency interest rate swaps permitted only where the underlying borrowing or receivable is evidenced by a genuine maritime financing facilityRMB options and swaps under the onshore interbank FX market subject to the existence of current or capital account exposureNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted onshore in China for RMB; offshore NDFs can only be used by the Hong Kong parent as a hedge overlay outside the PRC regulated banking wall, and the WFOE itself cannot actively participateCurrency funds pooling through the domestic bank's cross-border cash pooling product is permitted only after PBOC/SAFE approval for multinational group netting

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech Electronic Money Institutions (EMIs) are legally barred from opening domestic RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts for a China-based WFOE and they cannot directly interface with the SAFE reporting systems; they also lack the required banking license under the Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration and the Measures for the Administration of Bank Accounts. Their permissible services are limited to facilitating international payment messages or customer referral support only if a licensed domestic bank remains the account-holding institution. The operational reality is therefore low viability; any treasury architecture must depend on a state-owned Tier-1 commercial bank with SAFE-qualified status for direct clearing and reporting.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For PRC tax purposes, a Local File is statutorily required when annual related-party transactions reach RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers, RMB 40 million for related-party services, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; a Master File is required when annual aggregate related-party transactions exceed approximately RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations apply at consolidated group revenue thresholds above RMB 5.5 billion

Economic substance scope: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 permits reclassification and adjustment of transactions lacking economic substance; the Hong Kong parent is subject to Hong Kong Economic Substance Requirements for relevant activities such as holding companies, financing, and intellectual property, and must perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong with adequate staff and premises; a Hong Kong shell company that merely invoices shipbuilding royalties or channel finance will be denied DTA beneficial ownership and may have its corporate existence disregarded for PRC tax purposes

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, disallow treaty benefits under the principal purpose test and beneficial ownership doctrine, recharacterize transactions, impose special tax adjustments, add 50% to 100% penalty for tax underpayment, and apply a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the incremental tax and withholding shortfall; the HK shell may also be struck off under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance sanctions for non-compliance with filing and economic substance requirements

Director personal liability: Directors and ultimate beneficial owners may face civil liability for the WFOE tax debts if the corporate veil is pierced for misuse of corporate form; PRC Company Law imposes joint liability on shareholders who abuse limited liability and cause serious creditor or tax losses; they also face exit bans and blacklisting under the non-compliant taxpayer enforcement mechanism, travel restrictions, and criminal tax evasion liability under Criminal Law Article 201 if the assessed underpayment exceeds criminal thresholds, with imprisonment up to seven years for aggravated evasion
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Base Erosion via Overstated Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 42 and 43 authorize the tax authority to reassess taxable income by the arm length standard, to ignore sham transactions, to disallow excessive intercompany payments, and to impose a special tax adjustment; failure to maintain contemporaneous Local File and Master File documentation may lead to documentation penalties, deemed income determination, an increase of up to 5 percentage points in deemed interest on the unpaid tax, and a 50% to 100% fraud penalty under the Tax Administration Law

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve fraudulent transfer pricing may be jointly and severally liable with the company for the unpaid taxes, interest, and penalties if the court pierces the corporate veil; tax authorities can impose exit-restriction orders preventing directors from departing China until the tax liability is settled; intentional falsification of related-party returns can support criminal charges for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, with fines, forfeiture, and imprisonment from three to seven years for particularly large amounts
Money Laundering, Trade Finance Fraud and Forged Documentation

Statutory sanction: The conduct violates PRC Criminal Law Article 191 on money laundering, which targets concealment of proceeds derived from tax evasion, fraud, or other predicate offenses; the Tax Administration Law imposes confiscation of illicitly shifted assets, penalties up to 100% of the concealed tax, and criminal fines; AML regulators may separately assess administrative fines against the entity, compliance officers, and directors, and banking authorities may terminate shipyard banking relationships and blacklist the entity from trade finance platforms

Director personal liability: Directors, finance officers, and beneficial owners face personal criminal prosecution for money laundering with penalties including confiscation of criminal proceeds and imprisonment of up to ten years for serious laundering of substantial sums; civil asset restitution to the tax authority is mandatory; travel bans and blacklisting are applied immediately so the directors cannot leave mainland China while investigation is pending; any fraudulent use of letters of credit may additionally trigger criminal liability for false financial instruments and contract fraud, exposing directors to separate prison terms and permanent disqualification from corporate management in both mainland China and Hong Kong
