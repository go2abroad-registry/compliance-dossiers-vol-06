Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is imposed by the PRC Company Law on a limited-liability wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The sole foreign investor may determine the registered capital, subject to the requirement that the subscribed capital be fully contributed within five years from the date of obtaining the business license, and subject to the principle that the capital amount remain proportionate to the intended business scale and operational liabilities. No statutory blocked escrow account is required at formation for an ordinary WFOE. However, because the target business ISIC 3040 may involve armored or tactical vehicle manufacturing, the defense-industrial licensing authority may examine the company's paid-in capital and financial capacity as part of the license eligibility evaluation, and the bank undertaking the capital contribution settlement will apply enhanced due diligence under China's foreign exchange and anti-money-laundering rules.

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For the CN_WFOE_LTD, the foreign/non-resident party is the Hong Kong shareholder; the China-incorporated WFOE is a PRC tax resident and must obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is its Tax Identification Number. Exhaustive procedure: (1) The Hong Kong investor must notarize, apostille/legalize, and provide Chinese-language translations of its incorporation certificate, certificate of good standing, board resolutions, and constitutional documents; (2) apply to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its local bureau for name approval and issuance of a Foreign-Invested Enterprise business license, which automatically carries the USCC; (3) complete tax registration with the in-charge tax authority within 30 days of the business license date via the e-Tax portal, which activates the CIT and VAT taxpayer accounts under the USCC; (4) if annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000, or voluntarily, apply for general VAT taxpayer status; if not, register as small-scale taxpayer; (5) apply for VAT invoice issuance entitlement, including special VAT invoices and/or fully digital e-invoices, through the tax-control/e-fapiao system; (6) register with SAFE for the enterprise trade-in-goods foreign exchange account and export proceeds verification; (7) because the scope covers military tactical vehicles, obtain the required export licenses and end-user certificates from the PRC export-control authorities before any export transaction; (8) if the Hong Kong parent earns China-source income, it must separately obtain a non-resident tax identification/record with the STA for withholding tax purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a PRC tax resident limited liability company subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income at the standard 25% rate; it is not eligible for pass-through treatment, and its manufacturing income is not characterized as offshore or territorial income for PRC CIT purposes.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not recognize check-the-box entity classification elections or general hybrid-mismatch elections. The WFOE is mandatorily classified as a PRC tax resident enterprise. Only ancillary elections are available, including voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer and the small-scale taxpayer simplified levy method where statutory turnover thresholds are not exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold. All SAMR-registered enterprises must complete tax registration regardless of turnover; foreign/non-resident enterprises making taxable supplies in China are required to register for VAT when they have an establishment or, in the absence of an establishment, the Chinese purchaser may be liable under withholding/reverse-charge rules.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered general VAT taxpayer may claim 0% VAT on export sales where the goods physically leave the PRC customs territory, the exporter holds the customs export declaration, the buyer is an overseas person, export proceeds are repatriated to China through designated banking channels within the statutory period, and the goods are not on the prohibited-export list. For armored security personnel transport vehicles under ISIC 3040, the WFOE must also hold a valid military/defense export license and provide end-user and end-use certification under the PRC Export Control Law. Zero-rating applies to the overseas sale so that input VAT on export-related purchases is creditable and refundable.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) with customs-assigned declaration number
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading (ocean), Air Waybill, or international waybill evidencing actual export of the goods
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher proving repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export sales contract and purchase order from the overseas buyer
  • VAT special invoices and input tax deduction vouchers for purchases related to the export
  • Export tax rebate declaration schedule filed through the e-Tax portal
  • Military/defense export license, end-user certificate, and end-use statement for tactical vehicle exports

China uses the Golden Tax Phase IV system for VAT invoice control. Special VAT invoices (fapiao) and fully digital electronic invoices (e-fapiao) must be issued through State Taxation Administration-approved platforms; invoice data is transmitted in real time, encrypted, and validated against the national unified invoice service platform with QR codes and digital signatures. The commodity and service tax classification code must be accurately stated, and export invoices must be reconciled with the export customs declaration and export tax rebate schedule. No physical fiscal stamp is required, but real-time electronic clearance, anti-fraud digital stamping, and customer-side verification are mandatory.

Export VAT refund claims are jointly scrutinized by PRC Customs and the STA through document review, physical inspection, and cross-checking of customs, tax, and foreign-exchange records. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the WFOE must repay all refunded input VAT plus late-payment interest and may be subject to an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax under-collected or refunded. Deliberate fraudulent export-refund claims are criminalized under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China and can result in imprisonment, fines, loss of export rights, export-license revocation, and blacklisting; responsible officers and shareholders may also face money-laundering and export-control enforcement action.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with stringent foreign-exchange control administered by SAFE. Current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, dividends, and interest payments, are generally convertible upon presentation of authentic supporting documents and are processed through designated foreign-exchange banks. Capital-account transactions, however, remain heavily controlled: foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) requires prior SAFE registration, an approved capital account or capital project account, and bank-level verification of each inbound remittance against the registered investment schedule. A Hong Kong parent injecting equity into the mainland WFOE must ensure that the remitting entity is the actual investor named in the FDI registration; mismatched remitters are a common grounds for rejection. Once received, foreign-exchange capital may be settled into RMB only for the WFOE's approved business purposes under the current negative-list regime, not for equity investments, entrusted loans, or repayment of domestic RMB loans outside strict scope. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted on the current-account for post-tax distributable profits, but requires audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring the dividend, corporate income tax clearance, and a bank authenticity review. Export proceeds must be repatriated into domestic foreign-currency accounts or reported under SAFE's trade monitoring system; an enterprise may choose to retain the foreign currency or sell it to a licensed bank, and there is currently no mandatory conversion ratio. The overall regime remains restrictive in the capital account, and any investment involving armored tactical hardware will attract additional scrutiny due to defense-sector sensitivity and possible involvement of the Ministry of Commerce and SASTIND export-control authorities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE rules, export foreign-currency receipts for goods must be repatriated to China unless prior SAFE approval for offshore retention is obtained; once repatriated, the WFOE may keep the currency in a foreign-currency settlement account or sell it to a licensed bank at market rate. There is no fixed mandatory surrender percentage under the current post-reform regime, but all receipts must be collected through a designated bank, matched to the customs export declaration, and reported through the trade-monitoring information system within the statutory vouching and settlement cadence, typically requiring reconciliation before the next export vouchment cycle.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMIs have negligible viability for the mainland WFOE's principal banking and treasury operations. Under PRC law, only licensed commercial banks may open corporate deposit accounts and handle capital-account foreign-exchange business for foreign-invested enterprises. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE and are legally barred from maintaining RMB or foreign-currency accounts for a mainland WFOE, issuing trade settlement instruments, or executing FDI capital verifications. A foreign EMI may serve only as an ancillary utility for low-value overseas operating payments or supplier disbursements outside China, but it cannot replace the required domestic bank for SWIFT capital injections, export collections, LC confirmations, FX hedging, or SAFE reporting. Therefore the treasury blueprint relies exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, with EMIs excluded from the critical compliance and clearing rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must inject equity capital through a SAFE-registered FDI route: first complete FDI foreign-exchange registration at the designated local bank or SAFE; then open a foreign-currency capital project account in the WFOE's name; instruct the remitting bank to send a SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent's account to the WFOE's capital account, ensuring the remitter name exactly matches the registered investor and the payment purpose is stated as equity injection or capital contribution under the SAFE-supported purpose code for FDI. Upon receipt, the Chinese bank performs debit/credit confirmation and reports to SAFE's capital project information system. Each settlement of foreign-exchange capital into RMB requires the WFOE to submit a payment-use undertaking, and the funds must be applied exclusively to approved operating expenditures not on the negative list. A certified public accountant must later issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, which is then filed with the market-regulator and tax records to complete the compliance cycle.For trade settlements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for smaller direct supplier wires and urgent non-documentary payments; for high-value defense-component imports, the preferred rail is a confirmed irrevocable Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the Chinese bank and advised through a reputable foreign bank, with MT700 issuance and MT798 amendments as needed. Documentary collections DP/DA are permissible for lower-risk transactions and are transmitted under MT400/MT422 messages. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Fintech EMIs have negligible viability for the mainland WFOE's principal banking and treasury operations. Under PRC law, only licensed commercial banks may open corporate deposit accounts and handle capital-account foreign-exchange business for foreign-invested enterprises. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE and are legally barred from maintaining RMB or foreign-currency accounts for a mainland WFOE, issuing trade settlement instruments, or executing FDI capital verifications. A foreign EMI may serve only as an ancillary utility for low-value overseas operating payments or supplier disbursements outside China, but it cannot replace the required domestic bank for SWIFT capital injections, export collections, LC confirmations, FX hedging, or SAFE reporting. Therefore the treasury blueprint relies exclusively on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks, with EMIs excluded from the critical compliance and clearing rails.

Turnover: Under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) as auditor unless it qualifies as a 'small private company' for a financial year. A private company is eligible for audit exemption only if it satisfies at least two of the following quantitative thresholds: (a) annual total turnover/revenue not exceeding HKD 10,000,000; (b) balance sheet total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000; (c) average number of employees not exceeding 100; and further it is not a member of a group, not a regulated financial or insurance entity, and not otherwise disqualified. Exceeding at least two of these thresholds or being part of a group mandates an independent certified CPA audit. For the PRC WFOE, the PRC Company Law requires every enterprise financial report to be audited by an accounting firm, with no size-based exemption; the defense-manufacturing license also makes an independent audit mandatory regardless of turnover, balance sheet size, or headcount.
