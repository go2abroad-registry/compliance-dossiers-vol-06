None - no statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in non-restricted manufacturing under the current PRC Foreign Investment Law and Company Law, and no blocked escrow account rule applies. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China via the national Electronic Taxation Bureau (e-Tax) portal for the mainland WFOE; in Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) is the competent authority for profits tax and Business Registration under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), using the official eTAX platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor forms the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the SAMR-issued business license contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Within 30 days of license issuance, log into the Electronic Taxation Bureau with the USCC to complete initial tax filing status, report the WFOE registered address, shareholders, directors, and bank account, and obtain the VAT classification (general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer). Step 3: Enrol in the Golden Tax IV digital invoice system for VAT special and ordinary invoices, and if the WFOE is exporting goods, register as an export-tax-rebate taxpayer with the local STA export rebate section and obtain customs export registration through the China International Trade Single Window. Step 4: Register under the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) Goods-Trade Foreign Exchange Control system so that export proceeds can be reported and matched to the export declarations. Step 5: A foreign non-resident shareholder or Hong Kong parent without a permanent establishment in mainland China does not obtain a separate PRC TIN; the WFOE will withhold PRC taxes at source and the non-resident must obtain a Certificate of Tax Residence from the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong (or its home jurisdiction) and provide its Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate number as the foreign tax identifier.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election for a WFOE; the WFOE is mandatorily taxed as a resident enterprise at entity level. Available statutory elections are limited to the VAT general-taxpayer election (mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000), the small-scale taxpayer method below that threshold, and the High and New Technology Enterprise election for a 15% CIT rate where qualification is met. Hong Kong does not offer a U.S.-style check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; any WFOE with taxable business must obtain tax registration immediately upon business license issuance, and a WFOE may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer before reaching the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim 0% VAT on exported electric cargo bicycles and step-through scooters when the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, physically leave the China Customs territory, the WFOE is the legal exporter with valid VAT and export rebate registration, the export declaration matches the VAT invoice and sales contract, and the export proceeds are repatriated to China within 180 days of export. Qualifying offshore digital services (e.g. firmware or telematics supplied to non-resident customers and wholly consumed outside China) may also be zero-rated where separate cross-border service documentation is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (the electronic export goods declaration matched through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or equivalent multimodal transport document
  • Commercial export invoice and detailed packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order with Incoterms
  • Bank-issued Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / export proceeds repatriation certificate supported by the SAFE Goods-Trade Foreign Exchange Collection Report
  • VAT special invoices for input-credit substantiation and the VAT export invoice/ordinary invoice required by the STA export rebate system

All VAT invoices are subject to the State Taxation Administration Golden Tax Phase IV platform: they must be created as fully digitalised e-invoices or machine-printed fiscal invoices carrying a unique control number, encrypted digital seal, QR code, and anti-fraud features, and must be uploaded to the STA e-Tax invoicing database in near-real time. For export rebate purposes, the electronic invoice data must be reconciled with the China Customs export declaration through the China International Trade Single Window; no paper-only invoice can substantiate zero-rating.

STA and Customs audit zero-rating claims by cross-matching Golden Tax IV invoice data, Single Window customs data, SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data, bank statements, and logistics records. If the export zero-rating is false, unsupported, or proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days, the STA will reverse the zero-rate and rebate, assess unpaid VAT, apply a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original refund date, and impose an administrative fine of one to five times the fraudulently obtained refund under Article 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Administration and Collection; tax evasion penalties under Article 63 range from 50% to five times the evaded amount. Criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds may be referred, and recovery limitation periods are unlimited for fraudulent claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WFOE operates under the mainland Chinese foreign exchange control regime, which is materially different from Hong Kong's free convertibility. Current account items, including trade settlement, service fees and profit remittances, are generally convertible after genuine transaction verification and SAFE filing. However, capital account operations remain strictly controlled. Injection of equity capital from the Hong Kong parent into the WFOE requires an FDI registration certificate issued by SAFE, disbursement in accordance with the approved foreign exchange capital account and an actual-use conversion restriction (no purely speculative conversion or intra-group lending without SAFE approval). Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted only after the WFOE has completed annual audits, filed corporate income tax returns, obtained tax clearance and passed bank-level payment verification. In practice, an onshore RMB shortage, customs declaration mismatches or deficiencies in the SAFE Form FDI-1 and related true-transaction documents will delay outflow clearance. The currency, while RMB, is not fully convertible for capital-account purposes; all cross-border transfers of substance require documentary corroboration by both the onshore branch and the HK banking correspondent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 28 and the 2019 reform measures, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; a WFOE may retain foreign exchange in an onshore foreign currency settlement account or convert to RMB at will. Statutory repatriation generally requires delivery of the foreign currency into the onshore settlement account within 90 days from the date of export declaration or as stipulated by the customs/trade credit regulations; longer collection cycles must be pre-registered with SAFE as offshore trade credit or by the bank as an exceptional trade-finance arrangement.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign exchange administrative body under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), is the binding central FX authority for the mainland WFOE; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority governs clearing channels and offshore renminbi liquidity for the HK parent treasury

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Wise Business, Revolut Business, Airwallex and MultiPass, are not licensed to operate as deposit-taking or clearing members inside mainland China and cannot open or maintain onshore RMB settlement accounts for a WFOE. The mainland WFOE must transact through a licensed domestic CBRC/CSRC-regulated bank to satisfy SAFE reporting, capital account verification and RMB cross-border clearing. The Hong Kong parent treasury may lawfully use an EMI for pre-bank aggregation of customer e-bike pre-payments or short-term corporate spending, but the EMI cannot be a substitute for, or directly feed, the onshore WFOE's bank account. Legal viability for onshore use is therefore low-to-nil.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must execute a SWIFT MT103 from its fully funded corporate account to the WFOE's onshore FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account held at the designated Tier-1 Chinese bank. The beneficial owner and remitter fields must match the SAFE-registered shareholder, not third parties. The remittance information must contain the SAFE FDI registration number, the WFOE's unified social credit code, and the precise purpose code 'Capital Injection - Foreign Investor Purchasing Registered Capital' or equivalent SAFE code, together with the wording 'Equity Investment into WFOE for Production Capacity Construction'. The Chinese RMB receiving bank will verify the registration certificate, confirm the payment falls within the paid-in limit, then credit the capital account in FX or convert to RMB under the 'actual-need' principle. A certified Chinese CPA must subsequently issue a capital verification report evidencing receipt of the registered paid-up capital before the WFOE applies for operational funding conversion.Direct current-account payments to overseas suppliers (battery cells, display controllers, torque sensors, hub motors) should be executed via SWIFT MT103 or locally issued letters of credit through the onshore bank's trade finance desk. Commercial letters of credit (irrevocable at sight, payable in USD or RMB) are the preferred clearing rail for high-value components, with the presentation of packing list, commercial invoice, bill of lading and China Compulsory Certification (CCC) test reports. Documentary collections (documents against payment / documents against acceptance) may be used for established low-risk suppliers; all outbound FX payments require genuine transaction contracts and customs evidence to avoid SAFE penalty. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the PRC Company Law, every PRC limited-liability company, including a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, must have its annual financial statements audited by a PRC-licensed certified public accounting firm; there is no turnover, balance-sheet, or headcount exemption for a WFOE. Compulsory audit support is additionally and unconditionally required where: (a) the enterprise is an above-scale industrial enterprise with annual main business revenue of RMB 20 million or more; (b) the CIT annual return includes loss carry-forwards, tax-exempt or reduced income, merger, division, liquidation, or non-resident withholding positions; (c) the WFOE has related-party transactions requiring annual transfer-pricing reporting under SAT Public Notice No. 42 of 2016; (d) the foreign investment annual report discloses an ultimate beneficial owner, cross-border equity pledge, or acquisition of a PRC domestic enterprise; (e) the enterprise applies for bank credit, export tax rebates, or government grants requiring audited financial statements; or (f) the WFOE is placed in liquidation. The audit must be performed in accordance with PRC Standards on Auditing and China Generally Accepted Auditing Standards, using financial statements prepared under PRC Accounting Standards for Business Enterprises.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory on gross dividends paid by a China resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the WFOE's share capital.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed any of these RMB thresholds: tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the Hong Kong parent group's consolidated annual revenue is RMB 5.5 billion or more.

Economic substance scope: China does not maintain an OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations register, but applies substance-over-form, GAAR, CFC rules and anti-treaty-abuse principles. For the WFOE, Core Income-Generating Activities include physical factory production, procurement, inventory management, logistics decisions and risk assumption. For the Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits and territorial-source tax exemption, Hong Kong's Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime requires substantial economic activity in Hong Kong, including real premises, employees and decision-making; a mailbox entity fails beneficial ownership and CIGA tests and will be denied treaty relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Tax Administration Law Article 63 empower the tax authorities to recharacterize pricing, adjust taxable income, impose overdue interest and levy a penalty of 50%-500% of underpaid tax. Failure to maintain contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation triggers additional fines under SAT Announcement No. 42/2016; criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may apply where the underpayment is material.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative may be jointly and severally liable for restitution under PRC Company Law Article 20; personal bank accounts may be frozen; entry/exit travel blacklisting is imposed under the social credit enforcement regime; criminal conviction under Criminal Law Article 201 carries imprisonment of up to 7 years, fines and confiscation of personal assets.
Fraudulent Export VAT Refund / False Trade Documentation

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 204 punishes fraudulent export tax refund with imprisonment of not more than 5 years for relatively large amounts, 5-10 years for huge amounts, and life imprisonment for especially huge or serious cases, plus fines of 1-5 times the defrauded tax. Tax Administration Law Article 66 also imposes a 50%-500% financial penalty and revocation of export-tax privileges.

Director personal liability: The responsible directors, export manager and legal representative face personal asset confiscation, civil restitution equal to the defrauded refund, criminal imprisonment up to life, passport revocation, exit bans and customs 'blacklisting'; the corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law when the corporate form is used to commit fraud.
Abusive Treaty Shopping / Shell Company / AML Payment-Rail Abuse

Statutory sanction: China's GAAR under CIT Law Article 47 and the anti-abuse/limitation-on-benefits provisions of the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA deny treaty benefits, reinstating 10% statutory withholding; the Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes administrative fines on the entity and responsible persons; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with 5-10 years' imprisonment and fines; PRC Company Law Article 20 permits unlimited shareholder and director liability for abuse of limited liability.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and Hong Kong parent are jointly and severally liable for unpaid withholding taxes, interest and civil damages; personal assets are subject to restitution; travel bans and blacklisting prevent cross-border movement; knowing structuring of the conduit flow results in criminal imprisonment for money laundering and ineligibility to serve as a director.
