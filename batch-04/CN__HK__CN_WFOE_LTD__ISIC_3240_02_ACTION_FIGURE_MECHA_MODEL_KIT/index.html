Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None beyond the statutory timeline under Article 47 of the PRC Company Law: no legislated minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE, and no blocked escrow or government-monitored capital deposit is required. The sole Hong Kong shareholder is free to set the registered capital in the Articles of Association, but the full subscribed registered capital must be paid in within five years from the date of issuance of the business license unless a special law provides otherwise. The capital contribution must be remitted through a designated bank into a foreign exchange capital account, registered in the SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) information system, and settled in RMB in compliance with the current Foreign Investment Negative List. Paid-in capital disclosures must be updated in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System, and funds may not be withdrawn except through a lawful capital reduction procedure.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A China VAT general taxpayer exporting manufactured action-figure mecha model kits may claim VAT zero-rating with input VAT credit/refund under the 'exemption, credit, refund' method for production enterprises. Eligibility requires: (1) valid registration as an exporter with the STA and China Customs; (2) actual customs export and physical departure of the goods from China; (3) the exporting WFOE is the named seller/consignor on the China Customs export declaration; (4) the sales proceeds are received from the overseas buyer and reported in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System in accordance with trade-in-goods rules; (5) input VAT on materials and production inputs is supported by valid special VAT invoices and traceable to the exported products; and (6) the goods are not on the statutory list of prohibited or non-refundable exports. Qualifying offshore digital services are not applicable to this physical goods manufacturing scope.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (export rebate copy) showing the HS tariff classification, quantity, FOB value, destination country, and exporter identity matching the WFOE
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international multimodal transport document evidencing loading in China and overseas consignee/destination
  • Export Invoice required by the STA for export VAT refund filing
  • Foreign exchange collection evidence from the bank/SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, i.e. the bank export collection/settlement instruction that functions as the Chinese statutory equivalent of the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)
  • Supplier special VAT invoices (input VAT deduction copy) and production/inventory reconciliation records for the manufacturing VAT refund calculation

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-controlled Gold-Tax administration system. Under the current full digital electronic invoice ('digitalized e-invoice') regime, invoices are generated on the national electronic invoice platform and automatically transmitted to the STA in near real time; legacy special and ordinary VAT invoices remain valid but are also system-encrypted, numbered, and subject to mandatory periodic reporting. Input VAT deduction requires matching and verification of the invoice code, invoice number, buyer/seller taxpayer identification numbers, and amount in the national invoice verification system. Anti-fraud clearance is achieved through automatic near-real-time issuance reporting and the 'three-flow' matching of invoice, physical goods flow, and fund/payment flow. The national e-invoice platform exposes official service interfaces (APIs) for certified ERP/billing systems to issue and query invoices in real time; there is no physical fiscal stamping.

STA, China Customs and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating claims by matching the four data streams: customs export declarations, export VAT refund filings, special VAT invoices, and actual FX receipts in the trade monitoring system. If zero-rating is claimed without a genuine customs export, without actual collectible proceeds from the overseas buyer, with forged invoices or transport documents, or with unrepatriated FX proceeds, the authorities will deny or claw back the VAT refund, assess output VAT at 13% plus late-payment interest, and impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the refund amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and its implementing rules. Intentional export tax refund fraud is subject to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, and the WFOE's export refund status may be downgraded or suspended for a rectification period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with de jure current-account convertibility and de facto capital-account restrictions. The RMB is freely convertible for bona fide trade, service, and ordinary business remittances under the current account, but every transaction must be supported by documentary evidence and filed through SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Capital-account items, including equity injections from the Hong Kong parent, shareholder loans, dividends, and capital reduction proceeds, require separate foreign-exchange registration, designated purpose-specific bank accounts, and ongoing monitoring of the actual use of converted RMB. The WFOE must complete FDI reporting through its designated domestic bank before any capital contribution is credited, and each conversion of capital-account foreign exchange into RMB is matched against declared business scope and supporting vouchers. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted only after audited financial statements, enterprise income tax settlement, dividend withholding tax payment, and a tax certificate are produced. There is no mandatory surrender of export proceeds into RMB; the WFOE may retain 100 percent of export receipts in a designated foreign-currency account or settle them onshore at market rates. The practical hurdles are documentary precision, reconciliation of export invoices with customs declarations, and the risk that funds with unclear beneficial ownership or mismatched trade descriptions are suspended by the bank's compliance desk.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE does not impose a fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the WFOE may lawfully retain 100 percent of eligible foreign-currency receipts in its designated export proceeds account or settle all or part into RMB at the onshore market rate. The principal statutory timeline obligations are: export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic bank in China and credited to the designated foreign-currency current-account, with the default expectation of collection within 90 days after the date of customs declaration for standard open-account trade; if collection is expected after that window, a deferred receipt registration or an exception filing must be made through the bank before the overdue status is recorded. Once credited, the receipt must be reported in SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within 30 days, accompanied by the customs declaration, commercial invoice, bill of lading, and the underlying contract. Advances received more than 30 days before export must be documented with a contract and later reconciled against actual shipment evidence. Any settlement into RMB must be executed through the bank using the matching transaction code and the supporting trade documents.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) acting as the delegated foreign exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

PayPal Fit: High

Feasibility rating: not viable as a primary banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, DBS Treasury Prism, or similar are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as domestic designated foreign-exchange banks for a mainland-domiciled WFOE. Chinese foreign-exchange law requires all goods-trade receipts and payments made by a resident entity to pass through a domestic bank that has SAFE trade-in-goods processing capability. Offshore EMIs cannot open a SAFE-regulated FDI capital account, cannot process capital verification, cannot record trade under the SAFE monitoring system, and cannot issue RMB basic settlement accounts. PayPal and similar licensed third-party payment aggregators may be used only as auxiliary e-commerce collection rails after the funds are settled to the WFOE's domestic bank account and a complete cross-border payment declaration is lodged. The legal constraint is absolute: the WFOE must maintain its cash-management spine with a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank, while the Hong Kong parent can use an EMI for its own treasury only outside mainland regulatory soil.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: not viable as a primary banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Revolut, DBS Treasury Prism, or similar are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as domestic designated foreign-exchange banks for a mainland-domiciled WFOE. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong company, including a WFOE, must prepare financial statements and have them audited by a certified public accountant or licensed auditor unless it qualifies as a small private company under Part 9 Division 1 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A small private company is generally exempt if it satisfies any two of the following three criteria for the relevant financial year: total revenue not exceeding HKD 20 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 10 million, and not more than 50 employees. However, if the company is a member of a group, the group aggregate thresholds apply: any two of total revenue HKD 200 million or more, total assets HKD 100 million or more, and 100 employees or more, in which case the company is a larger private company and therefore not exempt. A company that is not exempt must have its financial statements audited by an independent certified public accountant licensed under the Professional Accountants Ordinance. Public interest entities and companies covered by group reporting requirements are also mandatorily audited. The audit must be performed in accordance with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing issued by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants (HKICPA), and the financial statements must comply with Hong Kong Financial Reporting Standards (HKFRS), HKFRS for Private Entities, or the SME Financial Reporting Standard where applicable.
