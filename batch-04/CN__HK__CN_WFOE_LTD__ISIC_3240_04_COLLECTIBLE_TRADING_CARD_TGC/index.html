Statutory Formation & Board Resolutions

Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

Companies Registry, 14/F, Queensway Government Offices, 66 Queensway, Hong Kong; electronic filing through the e-Registry portal at www.e-registry.com.

Governing statute

Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622 of the Laws of Hong Kong), including sections 5 (model articles), 67 (application for incorporation), 112 to 115 (registered office), 135 to 136 (allotment of shares), and 474 to 475 (directors and company secretary), together with the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310), section 6. Hong Kong does not maintain a separate WFOE registration statute; the WFOE-Limited structure is achieved by incorporating a private company limited by shares under Cap. 622, with full non-resident ownership permitted.

Formation / constitutional filings

Form NNC1 (Application for Incorporation of a Company Limited by Shares) together with the executed Articles of Association; where submitted online, the electronic NNC1 and digitally signed Articles.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating the nationwide Electronic Tax Office (e-tax portal) for registration, filing, and payment; post-registration interface with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) for the Unified Social Credit Code.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Non-resident investors incorporate the WFOE through SAMR, and the market registration automatically synchronizes the new entity to the local STA tax registration system with a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that serves as the primary statutory taxpayer ID. Step 2: The legal representative and finance officer complete the post-incorporation tax information confirmation through the Electronic Tax Office, submitting the WFOE's articles, shareholder identification, and supervision/resident director details. Step 3: The WFOE applies for a separate VAT taxpayer registration and, if it will export, files the Export Tax Refund (Exemption) Registration in the Electronic Tax Office and obtains a customs declaration code from the General Administration of Customs. Step 4: The STA issues the corporate TIN as the USCC; the WFOE's VAT taxpayer status becomes General VAT Taxpayer upon annual taxable turnover reaching RMB 5,000,000 or by voluntary election. Step 5: For any non-resident parent entity without a Chinese establishment, the non-resident entity must obtain a Non-resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number from the STA and appoint the WFOE or another Chinese payer as withholding agent for China-sourced passive income.

Available election forms

None. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, subject to applicable preferential collection rates. Export sales are zero-rated and are excluded from the turnover threshold for small-scale classification.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available on the physical export of security holographic cold-foil printed collectible trading cards when all cumulative conditions are met: (1) the goods are declared to the General Administration of Customs under an export customs procedure; (2) the selling WFOE is registered and validated as an exporter; (3) the export invoice value matches the customs declaration value; (4) foreign currency consideration is received and repatriated into China within the statutory deadline; (5) the STA export tax refund/exemption election is in force; and (6) the ultimate sale is to an overseas (non-mainland) buyer including Hong Kong SAR or other destinations. Services ancillary to exported goods may also qualify for zero-rating where the statutory cross-border services rules are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (China Export Goods Declaration form)
  • Master or House Bill of Lading / Air Waybill
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE electronic export proceeds verification record
  • Export commercial invoice
  • Packed goods / packing list
  • VAT special invoice or ordinary VAT invoice for the export transaction
  • Export contract and order documents
  • Customs-signed export goods inspection / release records
  • STA export tax refund (exemption) electronic receipt file

All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be issued via the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform using Tax UKey digital certificates, with real-time transmission of invoice-line data (seller, buyer, description, amount, tax rate, and tax payable) to the STA tax-control system. Since the fully digitalized e-invoice reform, invoices receive an STA-authenticated digital serial number, and paper invoices are only residual/parallel. Anti-fraud stamping is automatic and no manual pre-clearance is required before issuance; however, export transactions must also pass customs data matching under the STA-GACC joint data exchange.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-clearance audit and random checks by the STA and the General Administration of Customs. If supporting documentation is missing or inconsistent, the STA may deny the zero rate, recover the refunded VAT, impose late-payment interest under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, and levy administrative fines. Intentional fraudulent export declaration or forged invoices triggers clawback of all VAT refunds plus penalties of up to five times the defrauded amount, and the promoter or legal representative may be exposed to criminal liability for tax evasion and money laundering; export rights and customs clearance credit may also be revoked.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China administers a managed floating exchange rate with convertibility differentiated by transaction type. The RMB is broadly convertible on the current account, covering ordinary trade settlements, service fees, royalties, dividends, and similar business receipts, but every conversion must be supported by a verified underlying transaction through a designated domestic bank. The capital account remains heavily controlled; inbound foreign direct investment, shareholder loans, acquisition financing, and cross-border investment capital must first complete SAFE registration and be held in dedicated capital settlement accounts before any further movement. Export proceeds surrender requirements have been effectively relaxed: a WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency or convert them to RMB, but the funds must be repatriated onshore into a bank-designated export collection account and reconciled with customs declarations and commercial invoices. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted for verified after-tax profits but requires a full CPA audit, a board-approved profit appropriation resolution, the Chinese dividend withholding tax certificate, and corporate tax settlement documentation. The bank will cross-match the remitted amount against the statutory books; any mismatch generates a freeze and reinvestigation. This bifurcated convertibility creates a practical compliance framework in which all treasury movements must enter through either an FDI or a trade route, never through informal fintech channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE practice there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary export receipts; the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign currency in its onshore foreign exchange account or settle it into RMB. The statutory repatriation window is a maximum of 180 days from the export customs declaration date, with a monitoring threshold near 90 days for trade discrepancy review. Export proceeds must be collected in the designated export proceeds settlement account and matched to China Customs declarations and invoice data. Any mismatch above USD 50,000 or 5% of the transaction value requires the bank to demand additional documentary evidence, including bills of lading, packing lists, sales contracts, and transport waybills, before releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low institutional viability. PBOC and SAFE regulations require any entity opening RMB settlement accounts or performing FX conversion and cross-border clearing in China to be a licensed domestic bank or a licensed non-bank payment institution possessing a cross-border foreign exchange payment business permit. Offshore digital EMIs cannot lawfully provide a domestic RMB account, cannot hold an inbound FDI capital injection, and cannot execute SAFE FX registration or capital verification for a Mainland-registered WFOE. Stripe and Authorize.Net are acceptable only as offshore e-commerce card collection gateways in the foreign sales jurisdiction, with funds settled to an offshore entity or merchant account; those funds must then be repatriated to the WFOE through compliant trade collection channels. Using an offshore EMI for treasury clearing or inbound FDI would violate PBOC payment licensing rules and SAFE current/capital account administration, expose the entity to frozen settlements, and create an AML blocker.
