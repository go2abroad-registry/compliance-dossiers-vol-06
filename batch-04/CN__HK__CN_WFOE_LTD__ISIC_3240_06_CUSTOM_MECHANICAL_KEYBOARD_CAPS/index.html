Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital amount applies to a general manufacturing WFOE engaged in custom mechanical keyboard keycap injection (ISIC 3240), unless an industrial-policy threshold is otherwise imposed. Under the 2023 Company Law, shareholders must pay the subscribed capital within five years of establishment unless a shorter or longer period is prescribed by law, administrative regulation, or State Council rules. No blocked escrow account is required. The registered capital subscribed by the Hong Kong parent must be recorded in the Articles of Association and remitted through a SAFE-registered capital contribution account; paid-in capital must be credited to that account and applied to lawful business use, and it may not be withdrawn, lent, or returned to the shareholder before the contribution obligation is lawfully discharged.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online service portal

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China WFOE: incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation automatically generates an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN/VAT ID; the STA recognizes the USCC as the enterprise tax ID and no separate tax registration certificate is required under the one license one code reform. For a foreign non-resident enterprise such as the Hong Kong parent with no mainland establishment, no general WFOE TIN is required; instead, taxes on Chinese-source income are withheld and remitted under the withholding agent's TIN, or the non-resident enterprise must complete a non-resident enterprise tax registration with the competent tax authority at the place where the income arises or where the payer is located in order to obtain a non-resident TIN. Post-registration, the WFOE must obtain an e-tax digital certificate and use the Electronic Tax Bureau for all filings, payments, invoice issuance, and export tax refund declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all WFOEs become VAT-registered upon issuance of the USCC, while foreign non-resident enterprises are subject to VAT withholding when supplying taxable services into China without an establishment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE qualifies for 0% VAT on the export of custom mechanical keyboard caps only if it is a VAT-registered general taxpayer, the goods physically leave mainland China's customs territory (with Hong Kong SAR treated as outside mainland customs and VAT territory), the exporter files and obtains a customs export declaration through the China International Trade Single Window, receives the export proceeds in foreign exchange and repatriates them to China within the required period, and submits the corresponding export tax refund (exemption) claim with supporting documents. Export of qualifying services is zero-rated or exempt only when supplied to overseas customers and consumed entirely outside China under the cross-border service list; failure to meet these substantive and documentary criteria makes the supply taxable at 13% VAT.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration) accepted through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outbound shipment from mainland China
  • Commercial Invoice, Packing List, and export sales contract/sales order
  • Export Proceeds Receipt and repatriation record (bank foreign exchange collection and settlement voucher registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange)
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued by the WFOE
  • Export Tax Refund (Exemption) declaration filed on the State Taxation Administration Electronic Tax Bureau and linked to the customs declaration and foreign exchange collection data

China's mandatory e-invoicing uses the STA's fully digital electronic invoice platform and Golden Tax System Phase IV; each invoice is issued through the Electronic Tax Bureau or an approved billing system and transmitted in real time to the STA for fiscal clearance. Every invoice carries a unique digital signature and QR code for anti-fraud authentication; high-volume issuers may integrate via authorized APIs or e-invoicing service providers; export invoices are generated at zero rate and must be linked to the export tax refund (exemption) record, customs data, and foreign exchange collection records before refund eligibility is recognized.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs cross-check customs declarations, export manifests, forex collection data, and export refund claims. If zero-rating is asserted without actual export, without repatriated proceeds, or with false or incomplete documents, the tax authority will deny or reverse the zero rate, require output VAT at 13%, recover any refunded input VAT, impose daily late-payment surcharges, and apply administrative fines of 50% to five times the tax involved. Fraudulent export refunds are a criminal offence under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China and may result in imprisonment, confiscation of property, and debt to export-tax-refund compliance records; clawbacks are retrospective for the full statutory limitation period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with current account convertibility and a tightly regulated capital account. SAFE, under the PBOC, supervises all cross-border fund movements and enforces the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations. RMB is broadly convertible for bona fide trade, services, wages, and dividends; however, capital account transactions require specific registration, filing, and supporting documentation. For a WFOE in China, foreign direct investment from the Hong Kong parent must be registered with SAFE through a designated bank before capital is injected. Once registered, foreign currency capital may be held in an FDI capital account or converted into RMB on a voluntary basis, but the use of converted capital must not violate SAFE's prohibited-use list, such as real estate speculation, equity investment beyond the registered business scope, or non-current expenditure not directly related to the WFOE's operations. Profit remittance from the WFOE to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current account item and is generally permitted after statutory audit, tax filing, tax clearance, and board approval. The practical hurdles include authenticating the transaction, ensuring the underlying trade or service is real, and maintaining contemporaneous books and records. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China and either retained in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily converted into RMB at a designated bank. There is no mandatory conversion ratio, but every receipt and payment must pass through SAFE's trade verification and monitoring system. Financial institutions apply strict beneficial ownership and trade-based anti-money laundering screening. Treasury planning should assume full regulatory visibility of all cross-border flows and build evidence packs for each transaction to avoid suspended payments or frozen accounts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign currency receipts in China. A WFOE may retain up to 100% of its export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily settle them into RMB at a designated bank. However, export proceeds must be repatriated into a permitted domestic foreign exchange settlement account and reported under SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. In normal goods trade, export receivables are expected to be collected and verified within 90 days from the export customs declaration; any anticipated delay beyond 90 days must be reported in advance to the collecting bank with evidence of the underlying transaction and reason for delay. Advance receipts before export and delayed receipts beyond 90 days require supporting trade contracts and, where necessary, registration in the bank's monitoring system. The chosen tier-1 bank will validate customs declarations, commercial invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, and payment instructions against the reported export data before releasing funds.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs have low viability as the primary bank relationship for this WFOE and cannot legally replace a licensed Chinese commercial bank. The PRC does not recognise foreign EMI licences; institutions such as overseas digital banks or non-bank financial technology companies cannot open onshore RMB or foreign currency settlement accounts in the WFOE's name without a Chinese banking licence or a formal SAFE cross-border payment institution filing. Stripe and Shopify Payments may be used solely as overseas consumer card-acquisition rails for the Hong Kong or international sales operation, but the resulting proceeds must be repatriated to the WFOE through bank wires, commercial remittances, or a SAFE-regulated cross-border payment institution that ultimately settles through a licensed Chinese bank. Offshore EMIs cannot provide the statutory capital verification, SAFE FDI registration, customs and tax matching, or bank acceptance services required for a manufacturing WFOE. The treasury structure should therefore rely on tier-1 domestic banks for all onshore account infrastructure and treat any fintech platform only as an ancillary merchant settlement gateway with full audit trail.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 400 million aggregate annual related-party transactions, with category triggers of RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million each for intangible property and financial asset transfers, and RMB 50 million for related-party services. Master File and Country-by-Country Report apply to groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion; regardless of threshold, prudent contemporaneous documentation should be prepared for all material intercompany transactions in the custom keycap supply chain.

Economic substance scope: Although the WFOE itself is a Chinese resident operating entity, the group structure must pass the substance-over-form test under EIT Law Article 47 and the Mainland-HK DTA beneficial ownership rules. The Hong Kong parent must perform real Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, including strategic decision-making, contract negotiation, risk management, employment and financial record-keeping; a letterbox Hong Kong company without CIGA will be denied treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as a conduit with the China-source income re-attributed to the WFOE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Reporting of Cross-Border E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: restitution of evaded tax plus penalty of 50% to five times the amount evaded, and daily late surcharge of 0.05% under Article 32. Criminal Law Article 201 provides fines and imprisonment for tax evasion where the concealed amount is material and 10% or more of the tax payable for that tax period.

Director personal liability: Directors who instruct or knowingly accept false books can have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20, making them jointly and severally liable for the WFOE's unpaid tax and civil damages. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 permits exit authorities to prohibit non-compliant legal representatives and responsible persons from leaving China. A criminal tax evasion conviction can result in up to 7 years' imprisonment, asset confiscation and personal fines.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Related-Party Cost Shifting

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 to 48 authorize the State Taxation Administration to re-determine taxable income and impose interest on the adjustment; SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 sets documentation standards; failure to provide contemporaneous documentation can trigger a penalty of RMB 10,000 to RMB 50,000 under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 70; understatement caused by false related-party contracts is penalized at 50% to five times under Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign inflated intercompany agreements and falsify TP documentation have caused abuse of the corporate form; under PRC Company Law Article 20, they can be personally liable for the company's restitution obligation. If the manipulation constitutes intentional tax evasion, Criminal Law Article 201 applies to the responsible directors and financial officers, with custodial sentences of 3 to 7 years for substantial underpayments, in addition to fines and travel restrictions.
Shell Company Treaty Abuse and Dividend WHT Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The reduced DTA rate is denied and the full 10% statutory WHT is recaptured with late-payment interest; EIT Law Article 47 (General Anti-Avoidance Rule) permits the tax authority to recharacterize the arrangement and impute the dividend to the ultimate shareholder; tax blacklisting and a penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid tax may be imposed under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 if intentional.

Director personal liability: Directors who create or maintain the shell HK structure to obtain improper treaty benefits have misused the separate corporate personality; Chinese courts may pierce the veil under Company Law Article 20 and order the directors and shareholders to return the tax benefit with joint and several liability. Responsible officers face exit bans, asset freezing, and if fraud is proven, criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines.
