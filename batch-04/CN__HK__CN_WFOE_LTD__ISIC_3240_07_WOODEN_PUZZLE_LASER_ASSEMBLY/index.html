Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum registered capital for a general manufacturing WFOE under the PRC Company Law, and no blocked escrow account rule applies to ordinary wooden toy manufacturing. The shareholders must set a reasonable subscribed capital in the Articles of Association and contribute it in full within five years from the date of establishment unless a shorter schedule is adopted in the Articles or required by a specific industrial regulator. The registered capital may be paid in cash or in appraised non-cash assets, and a bank capital contribution account should be opened to route investment remittances from Hong Kong. No mandatory capital verification report is required for SAMR registration in a non-restricted manufacturing sector, although the bank may request source-of-funds evidence and the local foreign exchange regulator may require a capital account filing upon inward remittance.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, accessed through the China Taxpayer Service Portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the China Customs Single Window for trade-related data; for the Hong Kong SAR parent, the relevant authority is the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong but it is not the operative tax registration authority for the mainland WFOE.

E-government filing portal

For a Hong Kong-invested Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), separate tax registration is not a stand-alone application. The WFOE is first incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtains a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes. The SAMR establishment record is electronically synchronized to the State Taxation Administration, thereby completing the WFOE's basic tax registration under the multi-department integrated registration system. The WFOE must then log into the China Taxpayer Service Portal to complete supplementary registration details, including legal representative identity, registered and business addresses, bank account records, accounting and bookkeeping arrangements, and authorized tax agents or financial officers. If the WFOE seeks general VAT taxpayer status, it must file the corresponding status election through the portal. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without an establishment in mainland China but deriving China-source income, the foreign entity is separately registered as a non-resident taxpayer with the competent STA branch, or the Chinese withholding agent performs tax registration and withholding obligations on its behalf. All filings, assessments, invoices, and tax refunds are referenced to the USCC/TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (the Chinese WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxed at entity level on worldwide income; it is not a pass-through vehicle and does not qualify for Hong Kong territorial taxation in mainland China).

Available election forms

China does not have a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election regime. The available statutory elections are limited to VAT status and method elections: (1) election to be registered as a general VAT taxpayer on a voluntary basis where annual taxable sales are at or below CNY 5,000,000; (2) mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer once annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; (3) election by qualifying small-scale VAT taxpayers to use the simplified VAT calculation method; and (4) election by general VAT taxpayers in certain statutory sectors to use the simplified method where expressly permitted. No transparent entity or pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no mandatory minimum turnover threshold for basic VAT/tax registration; any business engaged in taxable activities in mainland China must register for VAT. However, mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Businesses with annual taxable sales at or below CNY 5,000,000 are automatically treated as small-scale VAT taxpayers unless they voluntarily elect general VAT taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are eligible for the export VAT zero-rating (0% VAT) rather than an exemption, meaning the exporter may credit and refund input VAT attributable to the exported wooden puzzle production and laser assembly process. The statutory eligibility criteria are: (1) the goods are sold to a customer outside mainland China and physically leave China customs territory; (2) the exporter is a registered general VAT taxpayer; (3) the export transaction is properly declared to and released by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC); (4) export proceeds are received in foreign currency and repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank in accordance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules; (5) the VAT invoice, commercial invoice, contract, packing list, and customs declaration data are consistent; and (6) input VAT on domestic purchases of raw materials, components, CO2 laser processing services, and related overheads is supported by valid fully digitalized e-invoices. Goods sold domestically within mainland China remain subject to standard VAT at 13% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form with GACC release status and customs supervisory records)
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill or multimodal transport document) evidencing physical departure from China customs territory
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Packing list for the exported wooden puzzle consignment
  • Export VAT invoice issued through the STA fully digitalized e-invoice system
  • Foreign exchange repatriation proof: Export Proceeds Receipt, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), or designated bank statement confirming receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Electronic matching data from the China Customs Single Window used by STA and SAFE for export verification

The State Taxation Administration requires the use of the nationwide fully digitalized electronic VAT invoice system. Qualified taxpayers issue VAT invoices directly through the China Taxpayer Service Portal or through STA-approved third-party electronic invoicing platforms connected to the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time at the time of issuance. Each fully digitalized e-invoice carries a unique global invoice identifier, a dynamic anti-fraud QR code, an encrypted ciphertext/electronically controlled tax stamp, and is validated by the STA's fiscal clearance system. Physical paper stamping and tax-control discs are no longer required for fully digitalized e-invoices. Taxpayers with high-volumne systems may obtain STA-approved application programming interface (API) integration for system-to-system invoice issuance and real-time clearance.

Export VAT zero-rating and refund claims are subject to post-export audit by the State Taxation Administration, which cross-verifies GACC customs release records, SAFE foreign exchange receipt data, and fully digitalized e-invoice issuance data. If a zero-rating claim is not substantiated, the STA will disallow and retrospectively claw back the refunded VAT and related input VAT credits, impose a late surcharge of 0.05% per day on the outstanding amount, and may impose an administrative tax penalty of between 50% and five times the refunded amount under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Where fraudulent export declarations, false invoicing, or deliberate export tax refund fraud are established, the case may be referred for criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law, which can result in imprisonment, confiscation of property, and additional fines.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB regime with dual-tier convertibility. Current account items (trade, service payments, wages, dividends) are effectively convertible under a genuine trade-based verification system: banks must examine supporting documents but no SAFE quota approval is required. Capital account items remain regulated. Inbound FDI equity into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is permissible after SAFE's Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration; the registered capital must be received onshore into a designated FDI foreign currency capital account and may be settled into RMB only after verification of the actual purpose and use is within the permitted business scope and not on the negative list. Profit remittance operates through the current account: the WFOE must provide audited financial statements, tax filings, payment vouchers, and board resolutions to evidence distributable profits, after which the bank remits via wire with a foreign exchange declaration. There is no free parity liquidity for converted RMB to be used for offshore investment or unapproved shareholder loans. Export proceeds are no longer subject to mandatory surrender into RMB, but must be repatriated and reported through the goods-trade monitoring system. This framework requires the WFOE to maintain bank-level compliance procedures, including real-time documentation of trade settlement and a full audit trail for every cross-border receipt and payment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory surrender percentage and no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds in China. Export receipts may be held in a foreign currency goods-trade settlement account or converted into RMB at a licensed bank at the prevailing interbank rate. Proceeds must generally be repatriated onshore within the contractual trade term and should be received no later than 90 days after the customs export declaration unless a delayed collection reason is pre-reported to SAFE through the goods-trade monitoring system. The exporter must support each receipt with the sales contract, customs declaration, logistics documents, and invoicing records. Overdue or unmatched export receipts may be flagged as foreign exchange trading anomalies, leading to settlement account restrictions and downgrading of the enterprise classification under SAFE.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low (1/10) as a primary settlement bank. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed deposit-taking banks in mainland China and cannot independently clear through CIPS, perform SAFE balance-of-payments declarations, open a PRC corporate current account, or provide SAFE-compliant capital account settlement. They may act only as supplementary collection gateways through a Chinese partner bank. For the WFOE treasury, all core cross-border banking, FX conversion, and regulatory reporting must be executed through a licensed domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must wire equity capital through a licensed bank via SWIFT MT103 for USD/HKD or via the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) if payment is made in RMB, into the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account. The remittance instruction must cite the SAFE FDI registration number and the inbound direct investment capital contribution purpose code from the balance-of-payments code list. Upon receipt, the bank credits the capital account, the WFOE issues the corresponding share register update, and a PRC certified accountant may be engaged to produce a capital verification report for the shareholders and bank. RMB settlement is executed by the receiving bank from the foreign currency capital account into a dedicated RMB Capital Settlement Account. The WFOE must preserve invoices and contracts supporting subsequent disbursements because converted capital cannot be used for prohibited investments, onshore equity speculation, or unapproved shareholder loans.Domestic purchases of wood panels, laser optics, packaging, and freight services are settled through the RMB basic account via CNAPS high-value transfers or online bank bulk payment rails. Cross-border supplier payments are made through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers after bank validation of the trade contract and trade purpose code. Import-heavy purchases from HK or Asia can use a bank-issued Commercial Letter of Credit and Documentary Collections routed through the bank's trade services centre. For export receipts arising from Amazon Pay and Stripe, the payment service provider or its payout bank aggregates customer settlements and sends net proceeds by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's Goods Trade Export Settlement Account; the bank reconciles the receipt against customs declarations and e-commerce order records before allowing onward use.Full-deliverable CNY/USD FX forwards entered into with a licensed PRC bank to hedge genuine current-account trade receipts and payments, subject to SAFE real underlying transaction documentation and a bank-approved exposure limit.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for a mainland WFOE; only the offshore Hong Kong parent may enter NDFs outside the PRC and never with onshore RMB settlement or linked to the WFOE's capital account funds.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps are permissible only for maturity-matching liquidity needs of trade settlement and are forbidden if used for speculation, arbitrage, or circumvention of China's capital account controls under SAFE regulations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low (1/10) as a primary settlement bank. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed deposit-taking banks in mainland China and cannot independently clear through CIPS, perform SAFE balance-of-payments declarations, open a PRC corporate current account, or provide SAFE-compliant capital account settlement. They may act only as supplementary collection gateways through a Chinese partner bank. For the WFOE treasury, all core cross-border banking, FX conversion, and regulatory reporting must be executed through a licensed domestic Tier-1 commercial bank.
