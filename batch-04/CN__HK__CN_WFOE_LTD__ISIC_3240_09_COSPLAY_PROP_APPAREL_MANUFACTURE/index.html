Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

Upon issuance of the AMR business license, the WFOE is automatically tax-registered under the multi-license integration reform; the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN). The competent tax authority completes VAT registration, determines VAT taxpayer classification (small-scale or general taxpayer), and activates the e-invoice account. Foreign non-resident investors are not required to obtain a PRC TIN for equity ownership, but if they earn PRC-sourced income subject to withholding, they must complete non-resident enterprise registration and may be granted a temporary TIN.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to goods sold by PRC general VAT taxpayers that are physically exported from PRC customs territory, including deliveries to Hong Kong SAR and Macau SAR, provided the exporter completes the customs export declaration, returns the export proceeds in foreign currency within the statutory deadline, and aligns the customs, VAT invoice, and foreign exchange data in the tax authority electronic system.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Goods Customs Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill
  • Export Commercial Invoice
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation (SAFE-compliant inward remittance statement)
  • Export VAT Rebate Application Form

China mandates the issuance of VAT invoices through the nationwide E-Invoice platform integrated with the Golden Tax System Phase IV. All VAT invoices must pass real-time digital clearance, carry a unique anti-fraud digital stamp and tax control code, and be uploaded to the State Taxation Administration central database before becoming valid. Production invoicing requires API or cloud-based e-invoice integration; B2B invoices require recipient verification and confirmation within the unified invoice service platform.

PRC tax authorities cross-verify export declarations with customs records, foreign exchange receipts with SAFE data, and VAT invoices with the Golden Tax database. Unsubstantiated zero-rating or export VAT refunds are clawed back with a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05 percent, administrative fines from 50 percent to five times the involved tax, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under Criminal Law Article 204. Repeat violations trigger enhanced monitoring and revocation of export tax concession privileges.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under China's post-2015 marketised exchange-rate framework, current-account transactions are generally freely convertible and do not require prior SAFE approval, while capital-account transactions remain tightly controlled and require a statutory licence, registration, or filing. A WFOE may settle export receipts in RMB or foreign currency, and no mandatory surrender applies, but every transaction must be reported under the International Balance of Payments Statistics and verified against customs declarations in the SAFE trade-monitoring system. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted after the annual statutory audit and a corporate income tax clearance; the remitting bank must see the audited financial statements, a board resolution declaring dividends, and tax payment certificates. Import payments for raw materials and services also need supporting contracts, invoices, and customs or e-commerce platform evidence. Capital-account inflows such as fresh FDI equity or shareholder loans must be routed through a dedicated capital-account foreign-currency bank account and subsequently converted into RMB for operational use only after SAFE registration. This dual-track system creates real friction: funds cannot freely offset between the Hong Kong parent and the PRC subsidiary, and intercompany current-account flows need strong commercial substance. Any attempt to use fintech wallets or offshore virtual accounts as the primary treasury node would circumvent the PRC settlement framework and trigger the loss of the WFOE's A-class trade facilitation status, prolonged document audits, and potential administrative penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory foreign-exchange surrender for exporters. There is no statutory conversion ratio; export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-currency settlement account or converted to RMB at the onshore market rate. However, the exporter must complete trade data conformity checks by matching each receipt with the corresponding customs declaration in the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Receipts that cannot be matched within 30 days may trigger a verification query and, if unresolved, a downgrade from A-class to B/C-class monitoring. Deferred collection beyond 90 days requires a trade-credit balance report to the settlement bank and SAFE. Settlement banks are obliged to verify the authenticity of the underlying trade before crediting the proceeds, and any inconsistency in invoice amount, commodity description, or consignee may cause a compliance hold.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: Low, and only as a supplementary acquiring rail for overseas e-commerce logic. Under PBOC and SAFE regulations, a foreign electronic-money institution (EMI) cannot act as the primary settlement bank for an onshore WFOE because domestic RMB clearing and current/capital-account FX processing are reserved to PRC-licensed depository institutions and regulated non-bank payment institutions. Offshore EMI platforms, such as the international affiliates of Airwallex or Currenxie, may provide front-end merchant acquiring, multi-currency settlement into a corresponding account, or card payment connectivity, but they cannot lawfully receive export proceeds on behalf of a PRC WFOE without a domestic banking intermediary. A treasury-grade design must place a Tier-1 PRC bank at the centre of all inbound and outbound flows; the EMI, if used at all, should only serve as a payment funnel to a domestic collection account, and the WFOE must maintain full audit trails for platform settlements, refunds, and chargebacks to satisfy SAFE matching requirements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent should remit FDI capital by SWIFT MT103 using the PRC transaction purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise capital injection; the beneficiary bank account must be the WFOE's dedicated FDI capital foreign-currency account opened with the designated Tier-1 PRC bank. Before crediting, the beneficiary bank verifies the SAFE FDI registration code, the capital contribution schedule, and the board resolution. After collection, the bank performs a capital verification receipt, and the WFOE may convert the funds into RMB through a capital-item settlement account subject to the PRC negative-list restrictions. All RMB conversion and subsequent operational payments must be supported by invoices, contracts, and authentic business vouchers; the WFOE must also update the SAFE foreign-exchange business registration if the injected amount exceeds the registered amount or if the shareholding structure changes.For raw-material imports, subcontracting fees, and overseas supply-chain disbursements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for priority operational payments; irrevocable documentary letters of credit for large or cross-border procurement; and documentary collections (D/P or D/A) for established counterparties with a stable track record. Where the counterparty accepts RMB, the payment can be routed over CIPS for faster settlement. All payments must be accompanied by the underlying contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration, and, when queried, proof of delivery or inspection. Trade-related FX purchases are executed with the local bank at the onshore exchange rate and recorded under the appropriate SAFE trade transaction codes, while payments for royalties require the withholding tax vouchers and IP licence agreement to clear the bank compliance review.Onshore deliverable forward contracts between the WFOE and a designated PRC bank for CNY/USD hedging on real trade or capital-account exposure, with no speculative stand-alone positions allowed.Cross-currency interest rate swaps on confirmed foreign-currency debt owed to the Hong Kong parent, limited to the notional amount of the actual loan and settled within PRC onshore rules.Foreign-exchange swaps for temporary conversion between RMB and USD covering operational liquidity, subject to SAFE registration and a maximum tenancy of one year under the real-demand rule.Not permitted: offshore Non-Deliverable Forwards (NDFs) and CNH speculative trades; the WFOE can only access onshore hedging instruments with an underlying trade, dividend remittance, or capital repayment event documented to the satisfaction of the settlement bank.

Turnover: Under Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) Part 9, every Hong Kong company must prepare annual financial statements and have them audited by a certified public accountant licensed by the Hong Kong Institute of Certified Public Accountants unless a specific exemption applies. The audit-exemption test for eligible private companies requires satisfying at least two of the following three conditions in the relevant financial year: total revenue not exceeding HKD 100 million; total assets not exceeding HKD 100 million; number of employees not exceeding 100. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory thresholds for contemporaneous TP documentation (Master File / Local File) under STA Public Notice 2016 No. 42: RMB 200 million for annual related-party tangible-asset transactions; RMB 40 million for annual related-party intangible-asset or service transactions; RMB 40 million for annual other related-party transactions; RMB 100 million for annual related-party financial-asset transactions. Master File additionally required when one group's total revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the annual consolidated group revenue meets certain threshold; CBCR filing due if consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese WFOE, as a Chinese resident enterprise, must demonstrate real economic substance: physical manufacturing premises, actual employees, managerial decision-making in mainland China, and meaningful control over core income-generating activities (CIGA). China's GAAR (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and Implementing Regulations Article 120) permits tax authorities to recharacterise an entity lacking substance. The HK jurisdiction's ESR applies to the Hong Kong parent with respect to relevant activities (e.g., holding company, banking, insurance, or intellectual-property business); the parent must maintain board minutes, employees, and offices in Hong Kong to obtain a favourable TRC. Failure on either side allows the STA to disregard the HK's intermediary posture and treat payments as directly flowing to substantial shareholders in China or blacklisted tax havens.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Under-Declaration of Cross-Border E-Commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Administrative: 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax (Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63); late surcharge of 0.05% per day. Criminal: under Criminal Law Article 201, if the amount falsely payable exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the accurate tax due, imprisonment for up to 7 years plus fines; repeat offenders subjected to bankruptcy order.

Director personal liability: Directors as actual accomplices or decision-makers may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid corporate tax, civil damages to the state, and confiscation of personal assets. They are subject to exit-restriction blacklisting by the STA and Alien exit control by the Exit-Entry Administration; criminal imprisonment can extend up to 7 years depending on the amount and nature.
Abusive Transfer Pricing / Profit Stripping under OECD BEPS and Chinese Special Tax Adjustment

Statutory sanction: STA may impose a special tax adjustment under Article 41/48 of EIT law and Public Notice 2015 No. 7. Determination of arm's-length transaction price; tax adjustment with 0.05% daily interest (Article 48) from the year of the transaction; the interest accrues and cannot be offset by tax credits. A 5-year back-adjustment window under Article 48 such that additional tax reaches the WFOE.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the falsified TP policy, intra-group agreements with inflated royalties, or directed the profit shift may face personal penalty equal to the evaded amounts under civil liability. Under Article 48 special-adjustment consequences include seizure of personal bank accounts of controlling directors through a court order; in aggravated circumstances, fraud is prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 or Article 208/210 on VAT-accountability if false invoices were used, leading to imprisonment and blacklisting.
Shell Company / Sham Substance & Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Denial of the DTA benefit under the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) prescribed in tax treaty Article 29; the tribunal may deem the income as if paid directly to the ultimate owner without Hong Kong interposition. The WFOE as payer faces the full 10% domestic WHT on dividends and 10% WHT on royalties, plus a 50%-500% fraud penalty, and the HK parent may be blacklisted under cross-border tax-information exchange measures. The arrangement is treated as an 'offshore shell' under Hong Kong's own Economic Substance Regulations for non-resident entities, triggering penalty of HK$50,000–HK$500,000 and possible initiation of criminal proceedings.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and Hong Kong parent can be seen as participating in treaty abuse. Civil liability: restitution of all unpaid withholding taxes, fines, and interest to Chinese tax authorities; the personal tax residence status of directors may be re-examined. Under China's Tax Collection Management law, personal assets of directors can be seized if the corporate shell operation is used to defraud the tax authority. Criminal exposure is from Article 201 (tax evasion), Article 194 (defrauding export tax rebates), and Article 265 (illegal acquisition of special VAT invoices). Convicted directors face imprisonment and post-conviction ban from acting as directors in any Chinese company.
