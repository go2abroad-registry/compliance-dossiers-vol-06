Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, exercised locally through provincial, municipal, and county tax service bureaus; the official online entry point is the national website (chinatax.gov.cn) and the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) filing platform of the relevant local tax authority, together with the China International Trade Single Window for customs-related export data exchange.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China WFOE, the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation. Immediately after incorporation, the WFOE must complete consolidated tax registration with the competent local tax service bureau within 30 days of obtaining the business license. The USCC is automatically used as the taxpayer identification number for enterprise income tax and VAT reporting, invoicing, export refunds, and social insurance contributions. For a foreign non-resident entity that does not create a permanent establishment but derives China-source income, the withholding payer must carry out non-resident tax registration and withhold at source; if the non-resident has a physical establishment, dependent agent, or fixed place of business in China, it must obtain its own Chinese taxpayer identification number through the same local tax service bureau procedures and file on the Electronic Tax Bureau platform. The Hong Kong SAR parent, as a non-resident shareholder, is not automatically required to obtain a mainland Chinese TIN merely by holding equity in the WFOE; any separate Hong Kong registration will follow Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department rules only if the parent itself carries on a business in Hong Kong.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax of the People's Republic of China on its worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate unless a reduced preferential rate applies. The WFOE is a separate legal person; no partnership-style pass-through election is available. For VAT purposes, the entity is classified as either a general VAT taxpayer or a small-scale taxpayer depending on its annual taxable sales, with mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. A WFOE engaging in manufacturing and domestic sales of tabletop dice will ordinarily hold general VAT taxpayer status because it must issue VAT special invoices and claim input VAT credits.

Available election forms

None for U.S.-style check-the-box or hybrid pass-through treatment; the PRC tax system does not permit a Chinese limited liability WFOE to elect pass-through classification. The relevant statutory elections are limited to the VAT small-scale taxpayer's voluntary election to register as a general VAT taxpayer upon meeting the tax authority's accounting and control requirements, and the VAT general taxpayer's election among the lawful export tax refund or exemption methods under the export tax administration rules. No hybrid mismatch elections are available under the Enterprise Income Tax Law for this structure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no monetary threshold for basic value-added tax registration: any entity commencing taxable activity must complete tax registration within 30 days of obtaining its business license. The distinct threshold applies to general VAT taxpayer status: an entity with annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer; an entity below that threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer with simplified levy treatment. For a WFOE manufacturing and supplying tabletop dice in China, the supply of goods is subject to the standard 13% VAT rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate and the related input VAT refund where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, the goods are declared to PRC customs and physically leave the customs territory, the export declaration and sales contract are in the name of the WFOE, the goods are not sold or consumed domestically, and the export proceeds are collected and verified through the banking and foreign exchange system. Shipments from mainland China to Hong Kong SAR are treated as exports for PRC VAT purposes because Hong Kong is a separate customs territory. Where sales are made through cross-border e-commerce platforms using payment rails such as Kickstarter/Stripe or PayPal, the e-commerce order data, payment settlement data, and logistics data must be filed through the cross-border e-commerce retail export regulatory channel and reconciled with the customs export declaration. The exporter must submit the refund claim within the statutory filing period and retain the complete supporting documentation package for future audit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (electronic and endorsed paper copy showing actual outbound movement, HS classification, quantity, value, and destination),
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing export and consistent with the customs declaration,
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer,
  • Packing list detailing the dice products, quantities, weights, marks, and container or package information,
  • Export sales contract or purchase order from the overseas customer,
  • Foreign exchange receipt certificate or bank-issued inward remittance and settlement statement evidencing receipt of export proceeds in a freely convertible currency,

  • China International Trade Single Window export declaration record, where applicable to the export mode used

China mandates issuance of VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration's VAT Invoice Management System, the national unified electronic invoice service platform, and the Golden Tax Phase IV digital control environment. Special VAT invoices and electronic special VAT invoices must be created within the tax control system or approved e-invoice software; invoice data must be transmitted in real time to the tax authority for fiscal clearance and comparison. The invoice receives an anti-counterfeit QR code and digital stamp identifier before it is valid for tax deduction. Invoicing software must align with the national data exchange and API protocols of the Electronic Tax Bureau and the invoice reconciliation system. Paper invoice sequences remain subject to local tax office registration and audit. Failure to issue or transmit invoices in real time triggers tax authority alerts and anti-fraud inspection.

The State Taxation Administration and customs authorities jointly conduct pre-export risk checks, declaration matching, and post-refund audit supervision. If the zero-rating claim is found to be unsubstantiated, the goods are re-imported or diverted into domestic circulation, the customs declaration is false, or the foreign exchange receipt is not properly matched within the prescribed period, the claimed zero rate may be denied and the refund clawed back. The tax authority will recover the erroneously refunded VAT, impose daily late payment surcharge at the statutory rate, and reassess the transaction as a domestic sale subject to the standard 13% VAT rate. For fraudulent export tax refunds, the Law of the People's Republic of China on Administration of Tax Collection and its implementing rules authorize an administrative penalty of one to five times the amount of tax refund fraudulently obtained. Where the amount is large and criminal liability is triggered, the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China provides for penalties up to life imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property for export tax refund fraud. The taxpayer bears the full burden of proving actual export and lawful receipt of foreign exchange; clawback is retrospective to the full extent permitted by the applicable tax limitation and recovery provisions.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a bifurcated FX system: current account items (trade in goods/services, profit remittances, and legitimate business expenses) are generally freely convertible upon genuine-verification by a designated bank, whereas capital account items (cross-border equity injection, foreign debt, and reinvestment) remain synchronized to an approval/registration regime under SAFE. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may open a foreign exchange capital account for inbound FDI equity; the registered capital does not require minimum paid-in amount, but funds must be injected in a currency and from an investor consistent with the FDI registration. After arrival, capital account FX may be settled into RMB under the payment-and-settlement arrangement against authentic payment obligations, not for unrestricted use. Export proceeds are subject to trade monitoring and repatriation rules, but there is no blanket surrender mandatory percentage; enterprises can retain foreign exchange in a domestic current account and sell at market rate. Profit remittance to the HK parent is permissible under current account, provided the WFOE submits audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificate, board resolution on dividend distribution, and shareholder registration proof. The principal hurdle is documentary sufficiency and the prohibition on using capital account funds for speculative domestic equity/securities investment. Overall convertibility is therefore high for trade and dividend flows, but tight at the capital account boundary.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods trade foreign exchange administration, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic account within the contractual trade settlement term, normally not exceeding 90 days from customs declaration for open-account arrangements unless a longer term is registered; no mandatory conversion ratio exists. The exporter may retain up to 100% of eligible receipts in a foreign currency settlement account, sell them to a bank for RMB, or use balance for verified offshore outbound disbursements, but all transactions must be reported within the As-One Trade Monitoring System and match customs declarations, shipping manifests, and platform settlement records.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign exchange administration arm of the People's Bank of China (PBOC); SAFE occupies the implementing role for cross-border payment, capital account registration, and balance-of-payments reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent must first complete SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration for the WFOE, obtaining the unique Investment Registration Code (FDI code). Subsequently, the WFOE opens a designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. The capital injection is remitted via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's account in Hong Kong, using the remitter name exactly matching the registered investor and the purpose message annotated with the FDI registration code. On receipt, the bank validates the authenticity and performs capital account settlement into RMB, transferring the RMB funds into a Capital Settlement Account. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Understated Revenue (E-commerce Payment Rails)

Statutory sanction: Art. 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law (TCAL): the tax authority shall recover the unpaid tax and late payment interest and impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of tax underpaid. Art. 201 of the PRC Criminal Law: tax evasion exceeding 10% of taxable income with amount above RMB 50,000 per evasion after two administrative penalties triggers criminal liability; larger amounts (above RMB 250,000 and 30% of taxable income) attract imprisonment of 3-7 years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors/legal representatives who ordered, participated in, or conspired to conceal revenue can be personally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax to the company; the court may pierce the corporate veil if the company is a sham. A travel ban and blacklisting/personal tax credit blacklist under the National Tax Blacklist Measures can be imposed. Criminal imprisonment of up to 7 years, plus confiscation of personal assets, applies to the responsible directors and finance officers.
Failure to Withhold and Remit Withholding Tax on Outbound Distributions / Interest / Royalties

Statutory sanction: Art. 69 TCAL: the withholding agent that fails to withhold/collect tax shall be fined 50% to 3 times the amount it failed to withhold; the tax authority also recovers the tax from the payee (the non-resident). Late payment interest at 0.05% per day applies from the due date. Art. 37 TCAL and Art. 106 of PRC CIT Law impose collection from the payer if WHT was not withheld and the payee fails to pay. Serious cases may be treated as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers as responsible persons of the withholding agent may be jointly and severally liable for the under-withheld amounts if they caused the company's insolvency or acted in bad faith. They may face personal administrative fines, travel bans, blacklisting, and, if the act involves fraudulent concealment, criminal liability as accomplices to tax evasion under Art. 201 Criminal Law.
Shell Company / Improper Treaty Shopping and Abuse of DTA on Dividends

Statutory sanction: Under GAAR (Art. 47 CIT Law and Art. 120 Implementation Rules) the tax authority may disregard the arrangement, recharacterize the transactions, deny treaty benefits, and tax the dividends at 10%. Penalties: Art. 63 TCAL applies where the treaty-inducing arrangement was achieved by false declarations; otherwise, a TP/GAAR adjustment and late interest under Art. 122 Implementation Rules may apply. The OECD MLI and PRC-HK DTA principal purpose test deny the treaty where obtaining a benefit was the principal purpose.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign documents asserting treaty benefits while knowing the HK entity has no substance face personal civil liability for the additional tax, interest, and penalties incurred by the WFOE. In egregious cases, courts may pierce the corporate veil to hold directors personally liable for company tax debts (Art. 20 of PRC Company Law, Art. 14 of the Supreme People's Court's Company Law Interpretation III). Criminal prosecution for tax evasion can result if false certificates are filed; travel bans and asset freezes are common preliminary measures.
