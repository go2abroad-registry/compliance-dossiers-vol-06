None. Hong Kong SAR does not impose a minimum paid-up capital, authorised capital threshold, or blocked escrow account requirement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal 'e-Tax' (China Tax E-Network) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China as a limited liability company, the foreign investor must first obtain a Business License from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Immediately upon issuance of the Business License, the WFOE must perform tax registration at the local STA office within 30 days, submitting the incorporation documents, articles of association, lease or ownership proof of the registered business address, and the legal representative identity documentation. Once submitted, the STA issues a Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) which functions as both the enterprise's tax registration number and unified social credit code. Registration is executed electronically through e-Tax, after which the entity must activate VAT and corporate income tax filings and obtain digital tax certificates. Foreign non-resident entities that have not formed a mainland subsidiary but have a taxable establishment in China must apply separately for a non-resident tax registration number through the same electronic tax bureau using the 'Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration' module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (Chinese Resident Enterprise): The WFOE is a resident taxpayer of China and is taxed on its worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at a standard 25% corporate income tax rate; it is simultaneously a VAT taxpayer for goods and services supplied within China's customs territory.

Available election forms

None. China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections comparable to the US or EU. A WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese tax resident by incorporation. Preferential regimes (e.g., High-and-New-Technology Enterprise status or Small-and-Micro Enterprise concessional rates) are administrative approvals, not taxpayer elections. No hybrid mismatch election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general taxpayer registration is required when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. A small-scale taxpayer registration is mandatory from the first taxable transaction, but monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 qualify for VAT exemption if threshold requirements are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China by a WFOE are subject to VAT zero-rating under the VAT Interim Regulation and the Export Tax Rebate (Exemption/Refund) system when (a) the sales contract and title transfer are made with an overseas buyer, (b) the goods are exported through a declared Chinese customs port, (c) the customs export declaration is completed in the exporter's own name, and (d) foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated into China within the statutory period. Applicable export goods from the ISIC 3240 manufacturing sector (sensory wooden toys) are classified as zero-rated goods, provided the products are not on the prohibited or restricted export list and the input VAT on purchased beechwood and materials is eligible for rebate or exemption.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Electronic Customs Manifest issued by the General Administration of Customs of China)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill (Air Export) evidencing physical movement out of Chinese customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or related bank export proceeds receipt voucher issued by a licensed Chinese bank
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list
  • Export VAT Procurement Invoice (Special VAT Invoice for Purchase of Export Goods)
  • Export sales contract and certificate of origin in applicable free-trade-agreement circumstances

China operates a real-time digital fiscal clearance regime through the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the state's fully digitalized e-invoice (all-electronic invoice) platform. Every newly registered WFOE must apply for an e-invoice quota and submit transaction-level invoice data through certified API integration. Invoices issued via the national e-invoice service must pass central validation, after which a dynamic QR code and cryptographic digital seal are generated, replacing the old physical stamping requirement. No invoice is valid unless real-time electronically cleared, and sellers must transmit issuance data to the STA within seconds of creation.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to rigorous joint audit by the State Taxation Administration and China Customs. If the custom declaration, logistics movement, or foreign exchange repatriation evidence is unverified or fraudulent, the STA will claw back all refunded VAT and assess a late payment surcharge. Under Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law, evasion of VAT via false export declarations carries a penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid or refunded tax, plus administrative cancellation of the entity's export tax rebate qualifications. Criminal liability may be imposed under the Criminal Law for smuggling, false export defrauding, and illegal foreign exchange settlement where the fraud amount exceeds RMB 20,000.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB exchange rate with current-account convertibility and strict capital-account controls. A CN WFOE may use RMB or foreign currency to pay for genuine imports of beechwood, auxiliary materials, production equipment, technology royalties, and cross-border logistics costs directly through a SAFE-designated bank if invoices, supply contracts, customs records, and transport evidence are complete. The capital account is highly restricted: inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong parent, shareholder loans, and liquidation proceeds require an FDI foreign exchange registration, a SAFE-banked payment profile, and specific purpose codes before the bank may debit or credit the entity's capital account. Export proceeds are treated as current-account receipts and must be repatriated into China, but the WFOE may keep them in foreign currency or settle them to RMB at the prevailing market rate. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is legally attainable once the WFOE has audited financial statements, has paid corporate income tax, has prepared a board-declared dividend, and submits the SAFE-registered capital account reference plus bank-level documentation. The principal operational hurdle is the bank's multi-layered verification of beneficial ownership, source of funds, and real economic substance; a shell Hong Kong holding company without audited books or physical operations will almost certainly trigger refusal, enhanced due diligence, or conversion suspension. In practice, the remittance bank will also require a withholding tax clearance certificate and a China-based certified public accountant's capital verification report for any capital construction.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of the export customs declaration. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% in ordinary commercial conditions: a WFOE is allowed to retain 100% of eligible export proceeds in foreign currency or voluntarily convert them into RMB through a licensed bank. If PBOC/SAFE activates a temporary macro-prudential surrender policy for extraordinary balance-of-payments pressure, the bank will enforce the percentage stated in the official directive for the duration of the measure. Every receipt still has to be matched with the customs declaration in SAFE's cross-border receipt/payment and electronic trade database; mismatches are frozen, reported, or returned to the remitter as fraudulent-if-uncorrected.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), delegated to and enforced by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability as a legal banking rail. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under PRC banking law and cannot open an onshore RMB or foreign exchange settlement account in the WFOE's name; they also lack standing as a SAFE-designated FX bank for capital conversion or regulatory reporting. The WFOE's inbound FDI, trade settlements, export proceeds repatriation, and dividend outflows must clear through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Offshore EMIs may appear only as payment-facilitation middleware: for example, Stripe and Amazon Pay can collect e-commerce receivables and route proceeds via local partner processors into the WFOE's bank, but they cannot replace the domestic bank account or the SWIFT/correspondent clearing requirement. Using an EMI without a local banking partner would violate SAFE's operational rules and expose the entity to fines and suspension of foreign exchange receipt/payment eligibility.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Prior to any wire, the HK parent's equity contribution must be recorded in the SAFE FDI registration through the WFOE's chosen bank, producing a unique foreign exchange registration number. The contribution is then remitted from the Hong Kong parent's licensed bank to the WFOE's SAFE-designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account via SWIFT MT103. In Field 70 the remitter must encode the SAFE FDI registration number and the payment purpose code for 'foreign direct investment share capital injection'; the remitter name and beneficiary name must match exactly the registered open-account identifiers. The receiving bank will credit the funds into the USD/HKD capital account, or settle to RMB at spot if requested, and will automatically report to SAFE. Within the statutory filing window, the WFOE must appoint a PRC-registered CPA to perform capital verification and issue a Capital Verification Report, which is then archived with SAFE and the bank. No withdrawals from this capital account may be made for unapproved purposes such as paying share issuance fees or shareholder acquisition costs.Trade disbursements should run through the SAFE-designated corporate bank using the entity's trade settlement module. For open-account raw-material payments, the entity initiates a SWIFT MT103 with the supplier's invoice number, purchase order reference, and SAFE purpose code, attaching the commercial contract, customs declaration, bill of lading/air waybill, and packing list; the bank retains these records for five years. For larger capital-equipment orders, the WFOE should establish an irrevocable documentary letter of credit with the supplier's bank via SWIFT MT700, governed by UCP600, with shipment documents presented through the payment chain. For recurring small-value shipments or consignment trade, documentary collections under URC 522 (D/P or D/A) are acceptable, with the collection order reflecting Incoterms such as FOB Ningbo-CIF Hamburg. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a legal banking rail. Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) are not licensed deposit-taking institutions under PRC banking law and cannot open an onshore RMB or foreign exchange settlement account in the WFOE's name; they also lack standing as a SAFE-designated FX bank for capital conversion or regulatory reporting. The WFOE's inbound FDI, trade settlements, export proceeds repatriation, and dividend outflows must clear through a licensed domestic commercial bank. Offshore EMIs may appear only as payment-facilitation middleware: for example, Stripe and Amazon Pay can collect e-commerce receivables and route proceeds via local partner processors into the WFOE's bank, but they cannot replace the domestic bank account or the SWIFT/correspondent clearing requirement. Using an EMI without a local banking partner would violate SAFE's operational rules and expose the entity to fines and suspension of foreign exchange receipt/payment eligibility.
