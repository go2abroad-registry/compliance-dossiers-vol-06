Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; taxpayer registrations and filings are administered by the local Municipal or Provincial Tax Service Bureau with jurisdiction over the WFOE registered address; online portal: State Taxation Administration Electronic Tax Bureau at etax.chinatax.gov.cn

        
E-government filing portal
For CN_WFOE_LTD, the statutory taxpayer identifier is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation. The competent tax service bureau completes tax registration automatically via inter-agency data sharing after incorporation; no separate TIN application is required. The WFOE must activate its tax profile on the State Taxation Administration e-Tax platform using the electronic business license, confirm its VAT taxpayer classification, and activate electronic invoice services. Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation: The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise subject to Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income at the standard 25% rate; it cannot be treated as a pass-through entity for Chinese tax purposes. The Hong Kong parent remains a separate taxpayer in Hong Kong SAR, and no branch passthrough, consolidation, or territorial exemption applies to the mainland WFOE.

        
Available election forms
None for CIT entity classification; China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. The WFOE may elect between simplified small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment and general VAT taxpayer treatment while annual taxable sales remain below RMB 5,000,000; general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
For a fixed establishment in China, VAT registration is mandatory upon business registration with no minimum turnover threshold. The statutory numerical threshold is the general VAT taxpayer threshold of RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales; below that threshold the WFOE may elect small-scale taxpayer status with a simplified levy rate, and above it general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
The WFOE may claim zero-rated VAT treatment, i.e. export exemption with input VAT refund through the export tax rebate mechanism, on silicone stress relief fidget toys sold to overseas customers if the goods are physically exported through Chinese customs, the exporter is registered as a general VAT taxpayer, the export customs declaration is filed in the WFOE's name, the sale proceeds are received from outside China, and the statutory export document set is retained. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to foreign recipients and wholly consumed outside China are also zero-rated, but the tangible goods export is the primary claim for this industrial scope.

China mandates electronic VAT invoicing through the State Taxation Administration electronic invoice service platform. The current all-digital electronic invoice regime requires issuance through the national platform with real-time data upload, unique invoice numbers, and digital signatures; special VAT invoices used for input VAT credit must be verified and confirmed on the platform before credit is taken. Invoice quotas are set by the tax bureau, and artificial intelligence-based risk controls continuously monitor issuance, clearance, and anti-fraud patterns. No physical fiscal stamp is added, but the mandatory digital invoice data flow operates as the fiscal clearance and anti-fraud mechanism.

      
Export zero-rating and tax rebate claims are subject to retrospective desk and on-site audits cross-validated against customs export declarations, logistics manifests, bank foreign exchange collection data, supplier invoices, and the WFOE's VAT ledger. If documentation is false, the goods are not actually exported, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory period and properly recorded, the erroneously refunded VAT is clawed back with late-payment interest, and a penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax may be imposed under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export tax refunds also attract criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, including imprisonment and fines. For fraudulent or unverified claims, tax authorities may recover tax without time limitation and may disqualify the taxpayer from export rebate processing and place it on the joint dishonesty punishment blacklist.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China operates a managed-floating exchange-rate system with full current-account convertibility and carefully guarded capital-account controls. For the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, current-account items such as export trade proceeds, service fees, royalties, and ordinary operating disbursements are convertible upon submission of authentic underlying documents through a designated licensed bank. Capital-account flows are far more constrained. The initial injection of registered capital from the Hong Kong parent must be registered with SAFE under the Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) regime before the SWIFT offshore transfer is accepted at a domestic bank. The funds land in a restricted capital-account escrow account, and any conversion into CNY for working capital, procurement, payroll, or intercompany payments must follow the approved capital-account payment use plan. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong shareholder is permissible only after annual statutory audit completion, corporate income tax final settlement, dividend withholding tax payment, and submission of a board resolution approving the distribution together with the SAFE registration number and bank-issued foreign-exchange payment verification. There is no outright ban on dividend conversion or remittance, but banks will reject transactions where accounting records, tax clearance, and underlying contract evidence are inconsistent. The overall assessment is that the WFOE can access full operating convertibility, but each cross-border capital movement is documentary, monitored, and subject to retroactive SAFE audit.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the current SAFE goods-trade foreign-exchange administration framework, a Category-A exporter such as this WFOE must repatriate export proceeds into a domestic foreign-currency account within 180 days from the date of the export customs declaration. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain up to 100% of export receipts in the original foreign currency or convert such proceeds into CNY at its discretion. Category-B and Category-C exporters are subject to a 90-day repatriation timeline, prompter documentary verification, and more restrictive bank scrutiny. The WFOE should implement a monthly reconciliation between customs declarations, bills of lading, and bank inflow records to maintain Category-A status and avoid SAFE penalties.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange control and monitoring authority; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority supervises the Hong Kong treasure center but imposes no capital or FX controls of its own.

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Not viable and legally impermissible for the WFOE's statutory treasury rails. Offshore electronic-money institutions and non-bank fintech platforms are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open domestic settlement accounts, receive export proceeds, perform FDI capital registration, or execute CNY conversion for a mainland WFOE. They cannot access CIPS or SWIFT clearing in a SAFE-compliant manner and are barred from the regulated capital-account payments architecture. All cross-border statutory flows must be routed exclusively through licensed Chinese commercial banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound registered-capital injection must begin with SAFE FDI registration and issuance of the Capital Account Information Registration Certificate. The Hong Kong parent then executes a compliant SWIFT MT103 remittance to the WFOE's dedicated FDI capital escrow account at Bank of China or ICBC. The remittance field 70 should reference the SAFE registration number and investment purpose code; the receiving branch will verify the capital account payment use plan before converting funds into CNY. After the funds are received, a Mainland-certified public accountant performs statutory capital verification and issues the Capital Contribution Verification Report, which is filed with the local Administration for Market Regulation and commerce authority as part of the ongoing foreign-investment reporting.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable and legally impermissible for the WFOE's statutory treasury rails. Offshore electronic-money institutions and non-bank fintech platforms are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open domestic settlement accounts, receive export proceeds, perform FDI capital registration, or execute CNY conversion for a mainland WFOE. They cannot access CIPS or SWIFT clearing in a SAFE-compliant manner and are barred from the regulated capital-account payments architecture. All cross-border statutory flows must be routed exclusively through licensed Chinese commercial banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory for the WFOE if annual related-party transactions exceed (i) RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; (ii) RMB 100 million for intangible or financial asset transfers; (iii) RMB 100 million for provision of services; (iv) RMB 50 million for equity transfers. Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting applies if the consolidated group revenue equals or exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

      
Economic substance scope: Mainland China's substance-over-form doctrine under Article 47 EIT Law and GAAR Implementation Measures (Announcement 2020 No. 3) allows the tax authority to disregard structures whose exclusive purpose is tax avoidance. The WFOE must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) — procurement, compression molding, quality control, and distribution decisions — are performed by its own staff in mainland China. For the Hong Kong parent claiming DTA benefits, the Hong Kong ESR (Inland Revenue Ordinance ss.61A and 61B) and the CIGA test require actual, substantive presence in Hong Kong; a letterbox company owning the WFOE's shares will be denied treaty relief and may trigger GAAR reassessment.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion via Underreporting of E-Commerce and Export Sales
        
Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and requires full back-tax and interest. Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and accounts for more than 10% of taxable income, with imprisonment up to 3 years for a large amount and up to 7 years for a huge or aggravated amount.

        
Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 20(3), directors who abuse the limited-liability structure to evade tax liabilities can be jointly and severally liable for corporate tax debts. Directors are also subject to exit control (travel ban / blacklisting) under Tax Administration Law Article 44 when taxes are owed, and personal criminal liability attaches to those who sign false tax declarations or approve fraudulent payment arrangements.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Contemporaneous Documentation Failure
        
Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 113 and 114 authorize the tax authority to adjust taxable income on an arm's-length basis and impose an interest premium on the underpaid tax. Under Announcement 2016 No. 42, non-compliance with TP documentation requirements leads to a penalty of up to RMB 10,000 (Statutory Notice No. 2016/42) and use of the tax authority's own best-judgment comparables. Severe adjustments can be treated as tax evasion, resulting in a 50% to 500% fine.

        
Director personal liability: If the pricing scheme is found to be a sham designed to strip profits from the WFOE, the court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20(3), holding directors personally liable for the principal, interest, and penalties. Directors may face asset freezes, loss of travel privileges, and personal responsibility for restitution if they personally benefited from the offshore profit diversion.

      

      
      

        Illegal Profit Repatriation and Treaty Relief Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 37-38 and Tax Administration Law Article 69 hold the WFOE as withholding agent liable for the uncollected withholding tax, plus a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount, and late-payment surcharges. Where the DTA claim is fraudulent, the tax authority will retroactively deny the treaty benefit and impose GAAR recharacterization with additional interest and penalties under Announcement 2020 No. 3.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or implement such unlawful repatriation schemes may be blacklisted and barred from leaving the country under Article 44; they may also face civil restitution for corporate tax debts under the veil-piercing doctrine, and personal criminal responsibility under Criminal Law Article 201 (tax evasion) if the withholding failure is treated as deliberate, carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years, a fine, and forfeiture of personal assets.
