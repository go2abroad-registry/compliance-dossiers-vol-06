Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filing and tax administration are conducted through the provincial/territorial Electronic Tax Bureau portals under the National Electronic Tax Administration framework, accessible via the STA official website (www.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

For a mainland China Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), tax registration is integrated into the multi-registration one-stop establishment procedure. After the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the business license, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically synchronized to the STA and serves as the enterprise's sole Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration identifier. The WFOE must, within 30 calendar days of license issuance, confirm its tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau accession procedure, complete the online taxpayer profile, bind the accounting and tax-filing calendar, register the bank settlement account, and formally elect or be classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer in accordance with turnover. A non-resident enterprise, such as the Hong Kong parent, that has no Chinese establishment but derives China-source dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees is not automatically granted an ordinary mainland taxpayer ID; instead, the WFOE as withholding agent performs Chinese income tax withholding and remittance, and the non-resident entity may need to register as a non-resident enterprise taxpayer with the relevant STA branch or apply for treaty benefits by submitting beneficial-ownership and Hong Kong tax-resident documentation. If the foreign non-resident enterprise creates a branch, office, or project establishment in mainland China, it must complete separate non-resident enterprise tax registration with the local competent tax authority before commencing business.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise for corporate income tax purposes, subject to tax on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate; it is not a pass-through entity for Chinese tax purposes.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; China does not maintain a check-the-box or fiscal-transparency election for WFOEs. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a resident enterprise. Limited administrative elections exist for VAT status (small-scale versus general taxpayer) and for export-tax-rebate settlement methods (tax rebate, tax exemption, or tax-free procurement in special customs supervision zones). China's anti-hybrid and general anti-avoidance rules continue to apply to cross-border payment and financing structures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover is the mandatory threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; all newly established WFOEs must complete tax registration regardless of turnover, and annual taxable turnover below RMB 5,000,000 may remain small-scale unless voluntary general taxpayer status is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of carbon-fibre FPV racing hobby drones qualify for VAT zero-rating and export tax rebate treatment when the goods physically leave mainland China customs territory, are sold by a registered export enterprise with valid export qualification, and the export proceeds are repatriated through approved bank settlement channels. The exporter must obtain an electronic customs export declaration and maintain consistent invoice, contract, packing, logistics, and payment evidence. Cross-border e-commerce export regulatory models applicable to B2C retail (including the dedicated express/courier export model) and B2B direct export may also support zero-rating or tax-free treatment, provided the goods are truly exported and the customs data match the payment and logistics trail.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) lodged through the GACC China International Trade Single Window and bearing the authorised customs release stamp
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or courier route manifest evidencing physical export from mainland China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate or bank foreign-exchange receipt record from the SAFE banking system evidencing repatriation of export sales proceeds
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, export contract or order summary, and, if applicable, the special VAT invoice or export electronic invoice matching the customs declared value

China operates a central fiscal invoice-control and clearance system under the Golden Tax Phase IV environment. All special VAT invoices and fully digitalised electronic invoices must be generated through the STA-approved electronic invoice platform, transmitted in real time to the tax authorities, and carry a unique tax-control number and anti-fraud digital stamp. Special VAT invoices require cryptographic authentication through tax-control equipment before use, and all VAT invoices must be reconciled on the electronic invoice platform before VAT filing and input-tax crediting.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to rigorous post-clearance audit by the STA and customs, with cross-matching against customs declaration data, bank FX settlement records, logistics manifests, and invoice platform data. Any false declaration, fictitious export, forged document, overstatement of rebate, or inconsistency between customs and FX repatriation records will cause immediate denial or clawback of the zero-rated VAT rebate, plus late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day and administrative penalties ranging from 50% to five times the tax involved. Where fraudulent export-refund conduct is established, criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law may attach to the responsible entity and individuals, export qualification may be suspended or revoked, and the enterprise is downgraded in customs credit classification with enhanced inspection for future shipments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with tight supervisory controls under SAFE. The renminbi is convertible on current account for genuine trade and services after bank verification, but capital account items remain highly regulated. The WFOE must complete FDI foreign exchange registration before receiving equity injections; capital contributions are received into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account and can be converted onshore only with a real transactional basis. Profit remittances as dividends to the Hong Kong parent are current-account payments, but the bank requires audited financial statements, tax clearance, a board resolution, and proof that the profit realised from operations is genuinely post-tax and remittable. Exchange settlement of export proceeds has been liberalised and there is no mandatory percentage surrender; however, all receipts must be repatriated into China and monitored through SAFE's digital trade surveillance system. The WFOE cannot freely lend paid-in capital to affiliated parties or invest in equity/securities with capital-account RMB without SAFE approval. Any promised convertibility via offshore CNH markets is not automatically available to the onshore entity; only onshore CNY through licensed banks is legal for operational clearing. These rules create timing and documentary hurdles for capital injection, cross-border supplier disbursements, and dividend distributions, particularly when the Hong Kong parent is used as a treasury pooling vehicle.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE's current rules require export proceeds to be repatriated to China once received; there is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage and the current mandatory conversion ratio is zero. The exporter must present export customs declarations and commercial documents to the bank, and receipts must enter the designated foreign-exchange current account within the contractually agreed term. Banking and trade authorities may raise overdue-trade alerts if export receipts remain uncollected beyond 90 days from the customs declaration date. Funds may be retained in foreign currency or converted into RMB at the onshore market rate according to the WFOE's operational needs.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low - not viable as a primary account solution. Pure offshore digital EMI platforms operating under foreign e-money licences are not legally permitted to open a bank account for a mainland WFOE or to clear onshore CNY trade flows. Mainland-licensed fintech payment institutions may provide collection channels as technology partners of licensed banks, but the WFOE must maintain its primary corporate and capital accounts at a SAFE-designated onshore bank. The Hong Kong parent may use fintech EMIs for its own offshore treasury aggregation, but the WFOE's receipts from global FPV drone sales must settle into an onshore licensed bank account and be reported to SAFE. Therefore, offshore EMIs should not be used as a clearing rail for the WFOE's China-based operations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low - not viable as a primary account solution. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible-property transfers exceed RMB 400 million; related-party intangible asset transfers, financial asset transfers or service transactions exceed RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is required where aggregate related-party transaction amounts exceed RMB 1 billion or the enterprise's annual revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion. The WFOE must also maintain a Special Issue File for cost-sharing arrangements, intangibles and loss-making related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: China's Economic Substance Doctrine is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the EIT Law, the Special Tax Adjustment Measures and the beneficial ownership test in the SAT Tax Treaty Benefit rules. The WFOE must conduct core income-generating activities such as procurement, manufacturing, inventory management, risk control and strategic decisions in Mainland China; an offshore Hong Kong affiliate must have real functions, assets and personnel to support its profit allocation. If the HK entity is a shell without substance, the SAT may disregard it and re-attribute profits to the WFOE, deny treaty benefits and impose interest and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Offshore Revenue Concealment

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, understating taxable income or receipts invites a penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded, in addition to the tax and daily surcharge at 0.05% per day. If the underpayment is disguised deliberately and the amount is large, Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law imposes criminal liability for tax evasion, including fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in the concealment can have the corporate veil pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law and be held personally jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax debt and civil damages. Individual directors face a travel ban or exit restriction (blacklisting) until the tax liability is settled, and may be criminally prosecuted with imprisonment up to 7 years if the evasion is on a huge scale.
DTA Abuse / Treaty Shopping on Dividends

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Treaty Benefit Rules and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, the tax authority will deny the 5% DTA rate, collect the 10% withholding tax difference, add interest at the statutory rate and impose a penalty equal to 50% to 5 times the evaded withholding tax. Providing false certification to the tax bureau constitutes tax fraud under the PRC Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who executed or certified the false treaty-benefit declarations face personal civil restitution for the under-withheld tax, travel blacklisting, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent misrepresentation. If the conduit arrangement was a sham, the court may disregard the corporate entity and hold directors personally liable for unpaid withholding tax and fines.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 and 48 of the EIT Law and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, the SAT may re-allocate income, adjust transfer prices, disallow excessive deductions and impose interest calculated from the tax year of the adjustment, plus a 5% penalty surcharge on the additional tax. Fraudulent concealment of related-party pricing triggers the 50% to 5 times civil penalty and criminal exposure under Articles 201 and 204 of the PRC Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: A transfer pricing adjustment that leaves the WFOE insolvent can expose directors to piercing of the corporate veil and joint liability for the shortfall. Directors who approved false transfer pricing documentation or used deceptive documents to reduce Chinese tax may be blacklisted, prohibited from leaving China, required to make asset restitution and, if the evasion is particularly egregious, imprisoned for up to 7 years.
