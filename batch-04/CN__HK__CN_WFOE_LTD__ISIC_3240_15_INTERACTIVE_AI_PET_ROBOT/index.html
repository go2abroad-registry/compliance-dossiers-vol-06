Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an ordinary manufacturing WFOE under the PRC Foreign Investment Law and the 2024 Company Law. The sole shareholder may determine registered capital in the Articles of Association, but the amount must be reasonably sufficient for the planned robotic-pet assembly operations, factory fit-out, equipment, first inventory, and at least six months of operating expenditure. Under Article 47 of the 2024 Company Law, the full subscribed capital must be paid in within five years after registration, unless a shorter period is stated in the Articles of Association. No blocked escrow account is required by statute; however, any capital remitted from the Hong Kong shareholder must enter a domestic foreign-exchange capital account and be registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through the appointed Chinese bank under the FDI registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC-resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide taxable income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. It is not pass-through eligible and is not located in a tax-exempt zone. For VAT, the WFOE is a VAT taxpayer classified as either a small-scale taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer.

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not provide check-the-box entity classification elections or hybrid mismatch elections for WFOEs. A WFOE is per se a resident corporate taxpayer. The only relevant status election is the VAT general taxpayer election available to small-scale taxpayers with annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000; after election, the entity is generally locked into general taxpayer status for 12 months.

No minimum turnover threshold for basic VAT registration; a WFOE must register for VAT within 30 days of incorporation. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported physical goods qualify for VAT zero-rating and export tax refund only if: (i) the seller is a VAT general taxpayer; (ii) the goods are actually exported through PRC customs with a completed customs export declaration; (iii) the goods physically leave the PRC customs territory; (iv) the sale is made to an overseas customer and title and risk pass after export; (v) sales proceeds are received from abroad in foreign exchange and repatriated; and (vi) all supporting export, logistics, invoicing and foreign-exchange documents are consistent and retained for audit. Cross-border e-commerce exports should use the applicable customs supervision code such as 9610, 9710 or 9810 to substantiate zero-rating.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible on the current account for genuine trade and service transactions after the bank verifies the underlying customs declaration, contract, and invoice. The capital account remains partially convertible: inbound FDI into a WFOE is lawful but is subject to SAFE's FDI foreign-exchange registration, capital-account opening, use-purpose supervision, and settlement-under-verification rules. Interest and dividends remitted abroad are treated as current-account overseas remittances after tax clearance and audited financial statements, but the bank must confirm that the WFOE has no unpaid taxes and that the RMB source of the dividend is consistent with the registered capital account records. Profits cannot be remitted before the annual audit and tax finalization are completed, and the bank will require evidence of the dividend resolution and withholding-tax payment certificate. Export proceeds have a repatriation duty, but the exporter may choose to retain the foreign exchange or sell it to the bank; there is no mandatory conversion ratio. In practice, the WFOE should maintain separate RMB and foreign-currency accounts, keep complete trade documents for each receipt/payment, and work only with SAFE-supervised banks to avoid an information-in-change violation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, the exporting WFOE must bring export proceeds into China within 180 calendar days from the customs export declaration date; later receipts require SAFE reporting and electronic entry revision. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0 percent: there is no general legal duty to surrender export FX, as the exporter may either retain the foreign currency in a current-account export proceeds account or settle it into RMB at any licensed bank. The bank will match the FX receipt against the electronic customs declaration and the trade monitoring system before the proceeds are released for operational use.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the statutory foreign-exchange control and licensing authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low and not treasury-grade for this jurisdiction: overseas fintech EMIs, including Stripe and Wise, cannot legally open PBOC-regulated RMB or foreign-currency corporate deposit accounts in the name of a Chinese WFOE, cannot engage in SAFE-supervised export receipt settlement, and cannot act as the principal clearing bank for the entity. SAFE-registered Chinese non-bank payment institutions can be integrated solely as origination channels for cross-border e-commerce collections, but the actual account ownership, FX conversion, and regulatory reporting must remain with a Tier-1 licensed commercial bank.
