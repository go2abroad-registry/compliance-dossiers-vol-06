Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

There is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a PRC wholly foreign-owned enterprise under current law. The shareholders must subscribe the registered capital in accordance with the Articles of Association, and contributions may be made in cash or in-kind within the five-year contribution window prescribed by the Company Law. No blocked escrow account requirement is imposed by the registration authority; however, the invested capital must be repatriated through a capital foreign exchange account and reported to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange after the entity is formed.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Approval: design review and final acceptance certificate from the local Fire and Rescue Department; structural load acceptance from a certified building inspector covering floor capacity and kiln installation; occupational disease prevention evaluation from the local occupational health authority; work-safety clearance from the Emergency Management Bureau before trial production; and employee occupational health training records.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) acting through the local competent tax office for the PRC WFOE; the statutory online portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice platform. For Hong Kong SAR treasury or parent entities, the competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, with the eTAX portal and Business Registration Office.

E-government filing portal

The PRC WFOE obtains a business license from the Administration for Market Regulation bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally functions as the enterprise's Taxpayer Identification Number. Under the multi-license integration and one-stop registration reform, the market regulator automatically transmits registration data to the STA; the WFOE must nonetheless activate its Electronic Tax Bureau account, confirm the taxpayer status, banking information, accounting records, VAT general taxpayer registration, export tax rebate filing, and foreign exchange goods-trade list within 30 days of business license issuance. The foreign non-resident Hong Kong shareholder does not obtain a PRC TIN merely by owning the WFOE; if it earns PRC-source income it must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration through the withholding agent and receive a non-resident tax reference number. For the Hong Kong entity itself, the Inland Revenue Department issues a Business Registration Certificate with a Business Registration Number; no VAT ID is registered because Hong Kong has no VAT/GST.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory VAT tax registration for every PRC enterprise within 30 days of business licence issuance; annual taxable turnover of RMB 5,000,000 is the statutory threshold above which general VAT taxpayer registration is compulsory and below which simplified small-scale taxpayer status may apply. A cross-border exporting WFOE should maintain general taxpayer status to claim input-VAT credit and export zero-rating. Hong Kong: no VAT/GST registration threshold exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of dental CAD/CAM zirconia blocks manufactured by the PRC WFOE qualify for VAT zero-rating under the export exemption-and-refund mechanism when the following statutory criteria are met: the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer; the goods are physically exported from mainland China through declared customs procedures; the export declaration reflects the true product, quantity, value and destination; title passes to an overseas buyer; the sales price is at arm's length; export proceeds are received through a bank in foreign currency and monitored by SAFE; and all customs, logistics, commercial, invoice and exchange-control documents are consistent. Qualifying offshore digital services are not part of this goods-export scope.

  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill as applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank-issued inward foreign exchange receipt voucher
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or purchase order

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-controlled channels. The WFOE must use the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system integrated with the Electronic Tax Bureau; each invoice is cleared in real time, assigned a unique nationwide invoice number, and stamped with the STA's electronic digital seal. Paper invoices remain subject to authorized tax-control equipment. ERP systems may connect through the STA-published e-invoice API for transmission and query only; the final invoice number, encrypted QR/pattern code, and digital seal are generated by the tax platform. Invoice data is automatically cross-matched with customs export declarations, export tax rebate declarations, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) payment records for anti-fraud monitoring.

Zero-rating is provisional until the document package is verified. If the Customs Export Declaration, Bill of Lading, FIRC and supporting records fail the STA/Customs/SAFE consistency check, the 0% rate is clawed back; output VAT at the standard rate is assessed, refunded input VAT is recovered, and a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% accrues. Non-fraudulent misdeclarations may draw an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax underpaid; fraudulent export-refund claims draw a penalty of one to five times the refund fraudulently obtained, loss of export-refund facilitation status, and criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. For fraudulent conduct there is no retroactive assessment limitation; otherwise assessments generally cover three years, and five years for special circumstances.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed foreign exchange regime administered by SAFE and the PBOC. Current-account transactions enjoy de facto convertibility: legitimate trade in goods and services may be settled, exchanged, remitted, and repatriated through designated banks without SAFE pre-approval, subject to authenticity and compliance verification. Capital-account transactions remain controlled and require SAFE registration, approval, or filing. For a Hong Kong-funded WFOE, the registered capital contribution is treated as inward foreign direct investment: it must be routed through the bank's capital-inflow system, credited to a Renminbi or foreign-currency capital settlement account, and can only be deployed after the receiving bank verifies the stated payment purpose against the business license and permitted scope. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are classified as overseas remittance of investment income and are permitted only after the annual statutory audit, tax clearance, and SAFE reporting are completed. The WFOE must present audited financial statements, tax payment proofs, and a SAFE-qualified payment application. Partial convertibility therefore exists: the regime is not free for speculative flows but remains workable for genuine manufacturing entities and trade-linked treasury activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's consolidated trade-in-goods administration, export receipts for dental zirconia blocks must be repatriated into China and credited to the WFOE's trade foreign-currency settlement account or RMB clearing account, where the bank automatically reconciles the receipt against the corresponding customs export declaration through the cross-border revenue verification system. The statutory timeline for standard A-class exporters is generally 90 days from the customs export declaration, unless an approved offshore deposit or extended payment term has been registered with SAFE. There is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage: current law has abolished universal forced exchange settlement, so A-class enterprises may retain up to 100% of verified export proceeds in foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB at the onshore spot rate. B/C-class enterprises may be required to convert 100% of new export receipts and operate under enhanced bank verification until their compliance classification is restored.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs and digital-only banking platforms are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions by the PBOC, NFRA, or SAFE, and they have no legal standing to open a WFOE's primary domestic CNY account, a SAFE-registered capital settlement account, or a trade-in-goods foreign exchange account. They also cannot perform the mandatory direct reporting to SAFE, cannot verify customs documents in the cross-border receipt and payment monitoring system, and cannot issue LC or documentary collection instruments. A limited set of licensed third-party cross-border payment institutions may support low-value export collection or employee expense cards, but they are legally barred from serving as the main treasury rail for FDI capital injection, corporate trade settlement, or profit repatriation. Overall viability rating: Low for this entity; use such platforms only as a supplementary merchant collection option, never as the primary clearing infrastructure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent shall instruct its overseas bank to remit the subscribed WFOE registered capital by SWIFT MT103 from the parent's treasury account into the WFOE's SAFE-registered capital settlement account at a Chinese Tier-1 commercial bank. The wire instruction must include the remittance purpose 'Foreign Direct Investment - Registered Capital Contribution' and the correct SAFE cross-border receipt transaction code for inward FDI. The receiving bank will require the FDI Information Registration Certificate, the WFOE's business license, articles of association, board resolution, and parent identity documents before crediting. After the funds are received, the bank completes the capital account filing in the SAFE systems, verifies that the use of funds complies with the business scope and the negative list, and then the WFOE's auditor issues a Capital Verification Report evidencing the paid-in capital. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by the Chinese WFOE to its Hong Kong shareholder under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, unless the reduced 5% rate under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement applies where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong tax-resident company directly holding at least 25% of the WFOE's capital and is not a conduit or treaty-shopping vehicle.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be maintained when the WFOE's annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transactions, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report obligations apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue of RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, 47 and 120, requiring Core Income-Generating Activities to be performed in the location where value is created: manufacturing, quality control, R&D, procurement, logistics, and key decision-making must remain in mainland China for the dental zirconia block business. The Hong Kong parent must demonstrate that its ownership, financing, distribution, and IP functions are real and not artificially arranged to divert profit; Economic Substance Regulations in Hong Kong are relevant only to the Hong Kong entity if it conducts finance, leasing, headquarters, intellectual property holding, or related activities, and proving such substance is an important administrative shield against Chinese transfer pricing recharacterization.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, fraudulent tax evasion is penalized with a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment surcharge; issue or use of false special VAT invoices constitutes a criminal offense under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205, with monetary fines, confiscation of illegal gains, and criminal prosecution when the evaded or falsely invoiced amount exceeds RMB 50,000; false special invoice crimes can attract life imprisonment for the most serious organizers.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the legal representative, financial director, or beneficial owner commingles personal assets with company funds or controls the sham invoicing; directors are jointly and severally personally liable for restitution of evaded tax and surcharges, may be placed on the tax authority travel-ban and blacklist, have personal bank accounts frozen, and face criminal imprisonment up to 3 years for ordinary tax evasion and 3 to 7 years for large-scale evasion; fines and confiscation apply to personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Shell Company Abuse

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration is empowered under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 44, 47 and 120 and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 36 to make primary and secondary transfer pricing adjustments, recharacterize the Hong Kong arrangement, impose interest and surcharges, apply administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax, and extend special tax investigation adjustments for up to 10 years; the thin-capitalization rules disallow excessive related-party interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and shareholders who orchestrate the conduit arrangement may be treated as the actual taxpayers; under PRC Company Law Article 23, shareholders abusing the separate legal personality of the company to escape tax bear joint and unlimited liability, and civil recovery extends to personal assets. The responsible directors may be subject to exit restrictions, asset freezes, and criminal tax evasion charges with imprisonment up to 7 years if the profit shift is intentional and reaches the criminal threshold; failure to maintain required transfer pricing documentation may also expose directors to personal administrative fines and disqualification orders.
Dividend Repatriation Non-Withholding and Fraudulent DTA Relief Claim

Statutory sanction: A withholding agent that fails to withhold is liable under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 to a penalty of 50% to 3 times the un-withheld amount; the Hong Kong recipient may be assessed directly, and fraudulent treaty claims that result in underpaid tax are subject to 50% to 5 times penalties, daily surcharges, and criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201; forgery of official tax residency documents may trigger separate criminal liability.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial director of the WFOE are personally responsible for the un-withheld amount when company assets are insufficient; they are jointly and severally liable with shareholders for restitution, face travel bans and blacklisting, and may be criminally imprisoned for intentional failure to withhold or for fraudulent DTA relief. The ultimate beneficial owner's abuse of the Hong Kong corporate form pierces the veil, leaving personal assets exposed to civil recovery, criminal fines, and imprisonment up to 7 years where the conduct constitutes aggravated tax evasion.
