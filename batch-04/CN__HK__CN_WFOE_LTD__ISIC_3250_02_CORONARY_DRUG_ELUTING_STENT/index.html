Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the national Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform; the parallel Hong Kong SAR jurisdiction is administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via GovHK/eTAX for profits tax. The indirect and export tax regime described in this dossier is the mainland PRC VAT and customs regime.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a limited liability company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued Business License contains the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Complete the integrated tax registration, legal-representative identification, and taxpayer reporting-information collection through the Electronic Tax Administration platform within 30 calendar days of business license issuance; no separate paper tax registration certificate is issued. Step 3: Register the WFOE in the VAT taxpayer system, electing general VAT taxpayer status so that the 0% export rate and input VAT refund can be claimed for exported medical devices. Step 4: File the Goods Trade foreign-exchange receipt directory registration through a designated bank with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Step 5: If a foreign non-resident parent, including an HK parent, has no permanent establishment in the PRC, it does not require a Chinese TIN for ordinary investment returns; the withholding agent applies the Mainland/HK tax arrangement and reports using the non-resident's home-country TIN. If the foreign parent creates a permanent establishment or derives other China-source income, a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration and Chinese TIN must be obtained from the competent STA office before filing. Step 6: For the Class III medical device business, obtain the NMPA product registration certificate, manufacturing license, and export credentials because these are required evidence in the VAT export-refund audit trail.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election exists in PRC enterprise income tax. The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise and cannot elect pass-through treatment. The relevant election is VAT status: where annual taxable sales are RMB 5,000,000 or less, the WFOE may elect small-scale taxpayer status under the simplified collection method; registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory above that threshold and is elected voluntarily by export-oriented manufacturers so as to access 0% export rating and refund of input VAT.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for the initial VAT registration of a WFOE; VAT registration is triggered by the issuance of the SAMR Business License. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. Small-scale taxpayers below that threshold may use the simplified method and benefit from the monthly RMB 100,000 / quarterly RMB 300,000 payment exemption, but a manufacturer exporting zero-rated goods must register as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC VAT general taxpayer exporting physical goods, including Class III coronary drug-eluting stents, to Hong Kong SAR or any foreign destination is eligible for the 0% export VAT rate when all of the following are satisfied: the goods leave the PRC customs territory and the export movement is evidenced by a customs export declaration; the sale is a genuine commercial transaction supported by an export contract and commercial invoice; the exporter receives the sale proceeds in foreign currency through a designated bank and reports the inward remittance to the SAFE monitoring system; the exporter is the legal exporter and holds the relevant NMPA manufacturing and export credentials; and the export VAT refund or exemption declaration is filed with the STA within the statutory filing period, normally by 30 April of the year following the export. Because Hong Kong SAR has no VAT or GST, no indirect-tax registration or zero-rating claim is required on the Hong Kong leg; the mainland export transaction is the taxable action eligible for 0% rating and refund.

  • Export Sales Invoice (commercial invoice generated in the VAT e-invoice environment)
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document evidencing export movement
  • Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form and bank inward-remittance or foreign-exchange settlement notice (the PRC equivalent of an inward remittance certificate)

All VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices and Ordinary Invoices, must be issued through the national e-invoice service platform integrated with the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Special VAT Invoices carry a unique invoice code, tax rate, tax amount, and cryptographic anti-fraud stamp; issuance data is transmitted in near-real-time to the STA Electronic Tax Administration. There is no third-party clearance stamp; instead, the tax authority performs digital fiscal validation of the invoice through the same system. Cross-border export declarations are processed through the China International Trade Single Window, which synchronizes customs release data with the export VAT refund system.

The export refund is clawed back if the foreign-currency proceeds are not repatriated through the designated banking system within the 180-day monitoring window or if the customs declaration, invoice, contract, transport, and receipt documents do not match. The STA may reverse the 0% rate, treat the export as a domestic taxable sale at 13% VAT, require repayment of the refunded input VAT, and charge overdue tax interest at 0.05% per day. Fraudulent export refund claims are subject to the PRC Tax Administration Law: fines of 50% to 500% of the tax amount for evasion and one to five times the export refund fraudulently obtained, in addition to loss of the refund and administrative publicity. Under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, deliberately fabricating export declarations or invoices to defraud export VAT refunds is a criminal offense punishable by imprisonment and confiscation of property; the responsible representatives and beneficial owners may also be held personally liable in the fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate system under which the RMB is fully convertible on current account but the capital account remains heavily controlled. For a Hong Kong-parented WFOE manufacturing coronary drug-eluting stents, the operational consequence is a bifurcated treasury model: trade-related cash flows, including import of high-grade polymer raw materials, export of finished Class III medical devices, and service fees, may be settled freely through licensed banks with genuine underlying vouchers and SAFE trade-in-goods reconciliation. However, pure financial capital movements, such as equity injections, shareholder loans, intragroup cash pooling, and profit remittances, are subject to full SAFE notification, verification, and post-event reporting. Profit repatriation from the WFOE to Hong Kong is not free of control; it requires audited financial statements, proof of tax payment, a board resolution, and prior completion of foreign exchange and tax registrations. The enterprise must maintain a clean A-class status in the SAFE goods-trade monitoring framework to avoid being downgraded to B or C status, which would trigger documentary expansion, increased verification frequency, and reduced flexibility in FX purchase, conversion, and retention. Capital injected into the WFOE must be used within the permitted business scope and negative list; conversion into RMB is permissible after capital verification, but the use of that RMB remains subject to the negative list for foreign investment and prohibited speculative uses. Currency convertibility is therefore real and workable for bona fide manufacturing operations, but only where proper SAFE filings, true transaction documentation, and Chinese CPA capital verification all run in parallel with bank execution.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE has abolished mandatory foreign exchange surrender for current-account export receipts under the modern goods-trade administration framework. A compliant A-class WFOE is permitted to retain export collections in a domestic foreign exchange settlement account indefinitely, or to convert such collections into RMB at any licensed settlement bank, provided that the underlying trade documents, customs declarations, invoices, and the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring records are accurate and reconcilable. The statutory timeline requires export proceeds to be repatriated onshore and reconciled with the customs export declaration within the regulatory verification window, generally 180 days from the date of export customs declaration, unless the commercial payment terms lawfully justify a later settlement. If the payment has not arrived within that window, an overdue receivable report and supporting explanation must be filed through the SAFE monitoring system or with the domestic bank. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds today; the enterprise may balance RMB and foreign exchange according to operational need. However, enterprises downgraded to B-class or C-class face stricter supervision, may be required to provide securities or deposits, and can be directed to convert proceeds into RMB to ensure traceability and settlement discipline.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its designated foreign exchange regulator, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), supported by provincial and municipal SAFE offices, jointly administer the RMB exchange control regime.
