Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. The PRC Company Law adopts a subscribed capital system with no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a general WFOE. The Articles of Association must stipulate a registered capital reasonably commensurate with total investment and operational scale; capital contribution may be in cash or in kind and payable within the statutory maximum period under the applicable transition rules. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account requirement outside the Foreign Investment Negative List.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain the Construction Project Fire-Protection Design Review and Fire Acceptance Certificate from the Fire Rescue Department, including verification of evacuation routes, fire alarm suppression systems, emergency lighting and structural fire resistance. A structural load certification for cleanroom floors, hygienic partition panels, heavy analyzer assembly equipment and reagent storage areas is required. Occupational health clearance must cover biohazard containment, chemical exposure control, personal protective equipment, dust and VOC monitoring, and emergency response drills. For use of flammable solvents or hazardous reagents, a work-safety facility completion acceptance review and a hazardous chemical storage permit are also mandatory before production launch.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through local tax service halls in the WFOE's registered address jurisdiction and the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) internet portal; VAT invoicing and electronic fiscal clearance are administered through the STA's National Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) formed in mainland China under the Foreign Investment Law and Companies Law, the legal representative or the authorised agent must complete taxpayer registration through the integrated 'One-Stop' market registration procedure with the local market supervision administration, after which the STA automatically assigns a Taxpayer Identification Number equal to the Unified Social Credit Code. The foreign parent (Hong Kong entity) must supply a notarised and either apostilled or legalised certificate of incorporation, certificate of good standing, board resolution approving incorporation of the WFOE, valid passport copies of the appointed legal representative and financial officer, the WFOE's Business License, articles of association, registered address lease and ownership documents, and a company chop. After issuance of the Unified Social Credit Code, the WFOE must activate the e-Tax Bureau account, file the initial tax registration particulars, select the appropriate tax bureau for VAT, enterprise income tax, individual income tax withholding, stamp duty, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge, and disability employment security surcharge, obtain the VAT taxpayer category determination (small-scale taxpayer initially or general taxpayer after annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or upon voluntary election), apply for VAT special invoice quotas and fully digitalised electronic invoice configuration, set up social insurance registration if employees are hired, and complete export tax refund (exemption) filing registration with the local tax authority and the customs export registration with the General Administration of Customs.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - the People's Republic of China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election regime for entity income tax classification; the WFOE is mandatorily classified as a China resident enterprise liable to Enterprise Income Tax on its worldwide income at the statutory rate of 25%. The only relevant election is for VAT administrative purposes: small-scale taxpayer status may be retained until annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, and general taxpayer status may be adopted voluntarily if the WFOE meets the prescribed accounting, invoicing and tax-filing conditions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold above which an entity must register as a VAT general taxpayer; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer, but for export goods manufacture and customs-bonded/export-refund eligibility, the WFOE is expected to register as a general taxpayer and to maintain full input VAT credit records. Small-scale taxpayers are granted a monthly sales exemption threshold of RMB 100,000 (or quarterly RMB 300,000) under current applicable relief measures.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of manufactured goods is zero-rated for Chinese VAT, subject to the goods being physically exported from mainland China and sold by a VAT general taxpayer to an overseas buyer for consideration. For the WFOE's automated point-of-care IVD microfluidic cartridges and immunoassay analyzers, zero-rating is available under the 'exemption, credit, refund' method: qualifying export sales are exempt from output VAT, input VAT attributable to the exported goods may be credited against any taxable domestic output VAT, and residual input VAT credits are refundable by the tax authority after verification of the export declaration and foreign exchange collection documentation. The goods must fall under the export goods catalogue, be customs-cleared as ordinary trade export, and be listed on the official export customs declaration. Zero-rating also applies to qualifying ancillary offshore services only if the services are performed entirely outside China and are of the type listed in the cross-border tax exemption/zero-rating catalogue; medical-device installation or calibration services connected to the exported analyzers may qualify if they meet the strict overseas-usage and documentary tests.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form) issued by the General Administration of Customs of China (GACC) and bearing the export commodity code, customs release mark, declared value, destination country, and statistical quantity;
  • Bill of Lading (or air waybill / multimodal transport document) naming the WFOE as shipper and an overseas consignee, showing port of loading in mainland China and port of discharge outside China;
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the underlying export sales contract or overseas purchase order in English and Chinese, reflecting the actual buyer, price terms, Incoterms, and payment terms;
  • Foreign Exchange Collection/Inward Remittance Certificate or bank receipt issued by a designated foreign-exchange bank in China evidencing receipt of the export proceeds from the overseas buyer by wire transfer or letter of credit settlement through Swift_LC or TT_Wire;
  • China Customs declaration of goods inspection/licensing release documentation, including the Medical Device Export Sales Certificate (where required by the buyer's jurisdiction), export license for dual-use or regulated IVD reagents if applicable, and any health authority certificate required for in-vitro diagnostic products;
  • Export tax refund (exemption) declaration file and the electronic record of the STA's export tax rebate system cross-referencing the customs export data, VAT invoices/export invoices, and the foreign exchange collection data.

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA's centralised Fully Digitalised Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform under the 'Golden Tax Phase IV' system. Sales data, customer tax-identification numbers, invoice amounts, tax amounts and commodity/service descriptions are transmitted in real time from the e-Tax Bureau account via approved APIs to the STA's national invoice database. Before any invoice is considered fiscally valid, the platform must assign a unique 20-digit invoice number, apply the digital signature and trusted electronic seal of the issuer, and synchronise the invoice document to the national voucher electronic archive. There is no offline clearance for ordinary B2B sales; the WFOE's point-of-sale and enterprise resource planning system must integrate with the STA's interface, and any paper/custom-format special VAT invoices must be verified through the statutory special VAT invoice check platform. The mandate is anti-fraud digital stamping complemented by automated cross-checking of purchase-side and sales-side invoice data (invoice-to-invoice matching) and transaction flow audits.

If the WFOE fails to collect and repatriate export proceeds into China within 180 days from the export customs declaration date (or within the period agreed with the designated bank where earlier approval applies), the zero-rating may be disqualified. The tax authority may restore the output VAT liability, disallow input VAT credits allocated to the export transaction, and order repayment of any VAT refund already received. Late payment surcharges accrue at 0.05% per day on the restored tax liability; administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Law and the export tax refund administration rules may range from 50% to up to 500% of the tax understated or fraudulently obtained. For deliberate fraudulent export refund claims, the WFOE and its responsible officers are subject to criminal prosecution under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which provides for imprisonment, fines and confiscation of property where the fraudulent refund amount is large or exceptionally large. In addition, the STA may suspend the WFOE's export tax refund qualifications, impose heightened document-verification control (grade 4 exit-invoice control), and refer the case to the State Administration for Market Regulation, SAFE, and customs for joint disciplinary action and delisting from the export rebate payment system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese capital account remains substantially non-convertible and requires prior SAFE approval or filing for almost all cross-border capital movements. A WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise for FX purposes; therefore, its paid-in capital contribution, external borrowing, and liquidation proceeds are classified as capital account items and must be routed through designated bank accounts under a SAFE-issued capital project registration. Current account transactions, such as import/export trade payments, royalties, and service fees, are fully convertible so long as the underlying transaction is genuine and properly documented. Profit repatriation by a WFOE to its overseas parent is a current account item, but banks will require an audited enterprise income tax settlement certificate, statutory financial statements, and evidence of prior withholding tax settlement. No de jure restriction exists on the amount of legitimate profit remittance, yet substantive scrutiny is applied to salary, service fee, and royalty streams to prevent disguised capital flight. All FX receipts must be repatriated to China; settlement or retention is at the enterprise's discretion under the willing settlement regime.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Rules, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a PRC-designated income account within 180 days of the export declaration date or within the contractual payment term, whichever is longer, provided the bank is notified through the SAFE monitoring system. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the willing settlement regime; the enterprise may freely retain the proceeds in foreign currency or convert them into RMB at market rates. However, every receipt must be matched against the customs declaration and invoiced trade transaction, and any amount exceeding USD 500,000 or involving high-risk counterparties is subject to enhanced documentary review.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Legal viability is Low for this WFOE structure. Chinese FX regulations require all cross-border receipts and payments by a PRC-resident entity to be settled through banks that are officially designated by SAFE and that report into the cross-border transaction monitoring system. Overseas digital EMI platforms without a PRC banking licence cannot act as the principal depository bank for capital project injection, for repatriation of export proceeds, or for profit remittance. A limited exception exists for licensed third-party payment institutions engaged in cross-border e-commerce retail settlement, but that channel is not applicable to a B2B manufacturer of IVD equipment. Any attempt to route the WFOE's capital contributions or trade payments through an offshore EMI would trigger void transactions under SAFE rules and expose the entity to penalties, mandatory repatriation orders, and freezing of the funds. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on domestic Tier-1 banks for all regulated flows.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Corporate Income Tax (CIT) on taxable profits of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the People's Republic of China; reduced to 15% for a certified High and New Technology Enterprise (HNTE).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory mainland China dividend withholding tax on distributions to non-resident shareholders is 20% under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; the Implementation Rules reduce this to 10% before treaty application. Under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA), the dividend WHT rate is reduced to 5% where the Hong Kong beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the capital of the company paying the dividend; otherwise the treaty rate is 10%.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, the WFOE must prepare a Local File if any single category of related-party transactions in a tax year reaches the following RMB thresholds: RMB 40 million for related-party services or other related-party transactions; RMB 100 million for related-party intangible asset transfers or financial asset transfers; RMB 200 million for related-party tangible asset transfers. A Master File is required if the group's consolidated annual revenue in the immediately preceding fiscal year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the Chinese entity has cross-border related-party transactions. The same RMB 5.5 billion group revenue threshold triggers Country-by-Country reporting obligations for the ultimate parent or a designated surrogate entity.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must have real manufacturing and decision-making substance in mainland China, particularly for its automated point-of-care IVD manufacturing activities, because the Chinese tax authorities will apply the substance-over-form doctrine and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules to recharacterize transactions lacking economic purpose. The Hong Kong parent must also meet the economic substance requirements embedded in the Mainland-Hong Kong DTA beneficial ownership analysis: it must have substantive business activities in Hong Kong, including employees, premises, bank accounts with real treasury functions, board meetings and management decisions in Hong Kong, and the capacity to perform Core Income-Generating Activities in relation to dividends, interest, royalties, and disposal gains. Under Hong Kong's Foreign-Sourced Income Exemption (FSIE) regime, a Hong Kong multinational enterprise receiving foreign-sourced passive income must satisfy an economic substance requirement to preserve the exemption in Hong Kong; if the Hong Kong parent is only a letterbox or shell with no CIGA, it will fail both the Chinese treaty benefit assessment and the Hong Kong substance test, causing the income to be recharacterized and taxed in mainland China at the statutory rate and exposing the directors to personal liability.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Underpayment of Corporate Income Tax

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may re-determine taxable income under Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and impose a 50% to 500% penalty for tax evasion under Article 63 of the TCAL, plus a daily late surcharge of 0.05%. If the underpaid tax is large and more than 10% of the tax payable, criminal liability under Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law carries imprisonment up to three years; for huge amounts and more than 30%, imprisonment of three to seven years, fines, and confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who consciously instruct or authorize the false transfer pricing will face personal liability by piercing the corporate veil under Article 23 of the PRC Company Law: personal assets may be used to satisfy the company's tax liabilities and creditor claims. Directors may be placed on the serious tax violation blacklist, subjected to exit bans/travel restrictions, prohibited from leaving mainland China, disqualified from serving as directors, and criminally prosecuted with up to seven years' imprisonment where fraud is proven.
Withholding Tax Evasion and Abusive DTA Relief

Statutory sanction: Under Article 69 of the TCAL, a withholding agent that fails to withhold or under-withholds is liable for a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount. An intentional false treaty claim constitutes fraud in tax administration, leading to additional penalties, daily late surcharge, and criminal liability under Articles 201 and 204 of the PRC Criminal Law; the tax authority may also invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to deny the DTA benefit retroactively.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership certificates or direct unauthorized dividend distributions can be personally liable for restitution of the unpaid WHT plus penalties. They face blacklisting, travel bans, asset freezing, and personal enforcement orders. Where the arrangement is a deliberate treaty-shopping or fraudulent tax evasion scheme, directors face imprisonment for up to seven years and court-ordered asset confiscation.
Shell Company Structure, Lack of Economic Substance, and Anti-Money Laundering Red Flags

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules and Article 120 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the tax authority may recharacterize the transactions, disregard the Hong Kong shell, and tax the total profits in mainland China at 25% plus interest and a 50% to 500% penalty. Under Hong Kong's FSIE regime, the shell may lose the exemption and face Hong Kong profits tax. Failure to report suspicious transactions under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law can result in fines, license revocation, and criminal liability under Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law for money laundering, with penalties including imprisonment and confiscation.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or maintain the shell arrangement for the purpose of evading Chinese tax will have the corporate veil pierced, making them jointly and severally liable for the WFOE's unpaid taxes, penalties, and interest. They are subject to cross-border asset tracing, freezing orders, travel bans, and inclusion on the tax blacklist. If criminal money laundering or tax evasion is established, directors face imprisonment up to seven years for tax fraud and up to ten years for money laundering, together with fines and confiscation of personal assets.
