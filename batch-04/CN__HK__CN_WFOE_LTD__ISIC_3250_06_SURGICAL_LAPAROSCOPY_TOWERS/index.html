Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the local competent tax bureaus at the WFOE's registered place of business; online filing and invoice portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau); export and customs/tax data exchange gateway: China International Trade Single Window

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporation and Unified Social Credit Code (USCC): The WFOE is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtains a Business License bearing the 18-digit USCC, which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT registration ID; no separate TIN certificate is issued. Step 2 - Integrated tax registration: Within 30 days after the Business License is issued, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the local STA office through the e-Tax Bureau using the USCC; the multi-license integration procedure synchronises SAMR registration, STA registration, customs registration and foreign-exchange records in the China International Trade Single Window. Step 3 - VAT general taxpayer status: Registration as a VAT general taxpayer is compulsory if annual VAT-taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below this threshold the small-scale taxpayer treatment applies by default, but the WFOE is strongly advised to elect general taxpayer status to preserve input VAT credit for export refunds. Step 4 - Customs/export registration: The WFOE must complete foreign-trade export filing and customs registration through the China International Trade Single Window with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) to obtain a customs registration code; this code is necessary for export declarations supporting zero-rated VAT treatment. Step 5 - Non-resident foreign entity TIN: A foreign non-resident entity without an establishment or place of business in mainland China is not issued a separate mainland VAT ID as of right; a contracting Chinese payer registers the contract and performs withholding through the e-Tax Bureau. If the non-resident entity is itself required to collect Chinese VAT or obtain an invoice refund, it must complete a temporary non-resident tax registration through the e-Tax Bureau by appointing a tax representative/withholding agent; mainland China does not provide a pass-through or check-the-box TIN for non-residents.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections or elective hybrid mismatch entity classifications. Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Statutory eligibility for 0% export VAT: (i) the goods are actually exported out of the customs territory of mainland China under a valid GACC export customs declaration; (ii) the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer with export-rebate status and completed customs registration; (iii) title and risk in the goods pass to an overseas buyer, supported by an export invoice and sales contract; (iv) the export proceeds are repatriated to the WFOE's PRC bank account within the statutory SAFE foreign-exchange collection period and are supported by bank/SAFE verification records; (v) the goods are not on the State Council prohibited/restricted export list; and (vi) the surgical laparoscopy towers satisfy all applicable medical-device production registration and export licensing requirements. Qualifying offshore cross-border services may also be zero-rated only if independently classified in the STA zero-rated service catalogue and supplied to an overseas recipient; embedded software in the endoscope towers does not by itself alter the goods classification. Where the statutory export rebate rate is lower than 13%, the non-refundable difference is treated as an irrecoverable VAT cost.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry / Export Goods Declaration) filed through the China International Trade Single Window and matched to the GACC export manifest, supported by commercial invoice, packing list, sales contract, and any required certificate of origin or medical-device export sales certificate.
  • Original or certified electronic Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) evidencing that the goods were physically exported from mainland China and delivered to the overseas buyer or consignee.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued export-proceeds receipt and SAFE foreign-exchange verification record proving that the sale proceeds were repatriated to the WFOE's PRC bank account within 180 days of export declaration.

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA Full-Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) module of the e-Tax Bureau; invoice data is transmitted in near real-time to the STA Golden Tax Phase IV platform, where each FDEI receives a system-generated unique invoice number and encrypted tax-control validation before delivery to the counterparty. No physical pre-printing or manual stamping is required, but an invoice that is not synchronised in the STA electronic invoice database is legally ineffective for input VAT credit and export-rebate substantiation. Export declarations, commercial documents, logistics data and packing information must be electronically transmitted through the China International Trade Single Window and linked to the export VAT rebate declaration.

STA and GACC electronically cross-match the customs export declaration value, FDEI invoice amount, logistics evidence and bank-reported foreign-exchange receipts. If the export declaration or documentation is false, incomplete or inconsistent, or if export proceeds are not received within 180 days unless SAFE has granted a permitted exception, the 0% VAT/export rebate will be denied in whole or in part. STA will claw back the refunded VAT, assess a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the tax underpaid, and impose a fraudulent-export penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Criminal liability for false export tax refund fraud under the PRC Criminal Law may result in detention and prosecution of the responsible individuals, and the mainland WFOE's intercompany pricing with the Hong Kong SAR parent may additionally be reviewed under transfer-pricing and BEPS substance rules.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is fully convertible for current-account trade and services transactions. Import settlements, export receipts, freight, insurance, royalties, and service fees can be executed through designated foreign-exchange banks using authentic supporting documents such as commercial invoices, customs declarations, transport documents, and service contracts. Capital-account convertibility remains restricted and governed by SAFE. Direct foreign investment into the WFOE requires completion of FDI foreign-exchange registration, opening of a dedicated FDI capital account, and bank-led authenticity and compliance review of the inbound remittance from the Hong Kong parent. Registered capital contributions may be settled into RMB for permitted operating purposes, but the funds cannot be used for securities investment, entrusted loans, or unauthorized real-estate purchases without additional regulatory approval. The capital settlement mechanism is monitored through SAFE's negative-list framework. Profit remittance from the WFOE to the Hong Kong shareholder requires an annual statutory audit, final corporate income tax settlement, verification of value-added tax and withholding tax obligations, and production of a board-resolved dividend declaration. Dividend remittance is treated as a current-account foreign-exchange payment under the bank's documentary examination, and the Hong Kong corporate shareholder may benefit from a reduced dividend withholding tax rate of 5 percent under the Mainland-Hong Kong tax arrangement if it is the beneficial owner and holds at least 25 percent of the WFOE's capital. A tax residence certificate from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is required to access the treaty rate. Export surrender rules no longer impose a fixed percentage conversion requirement for current-account export proceeds, but all proceeds must be repatriated to China and any balance exceeding the approved current-account foreign-exchange account ceiling must be converted into RMB. The overall structure gives the WFOE transparent trade convertibility while preserving strict regulatory control on cross-border capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE framework, all export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China and credited to a designated current-account foreign-exchange account. There is no fixed statutory percentage-based surrender ratio; however, SAFE and the receiving bank assign a maximum current-account foreign-exchange account balance, and any amount exceeding that approved ceiling must be converted into RMB promptly at a designated bank. Export receivables are expected to be collected within 180 days of customs declaration; overdue items require explanation to the bank and SAFE-supported evidence, including extension communications, collection agency documents, or litigation proof. Trade receipts must be matched to export customs declarations in SAFE's monitoring system within the bank's required reporting window after settlement, generally 30 days, and any mismatch creates a trade-based money-laundering flag that can suspend further FX transactions.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise Business cannot serve as the primary treasury or statutory banking rail for a mainland WFOE. PRC commercial banking law and SAFE regulations require cross-border capital injections, FDI registration, export-proceeds repatriation, letter-of-credit issuance, and trade-document verification to be executed through licensed domestic banks with direct CIPS and SWIFT clearing membership. Foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed as PRC banks, cannot hold segmented FDI capital accounts, cannot verify customs declaration matching, and cannot issue compliance-grade capital verification reports. An offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment tool for certain low-risk operational expenses outside China, but it must never be used for statutory capital contributions, dividend remittance, or trade settlement where SAFE documentary evidence and bank FXR reporting are mandatory. Attempting to route FDI capital through an EMI will likely trigger rejection, account freezing, or administrative penalties from SAFE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Prior to the remittance, the WFOE must complete FDI foreign-exchange registration with SAFE online or through the designated bank, obtaining the unique FDI registration code and registration confirmation. The Hong Kong parent must remit the paid-in capital from its own bank account using a SAFE-consistent foreign-exchange purpose code describing non-resident direct investment into a foreign-invested enterprise, clearly referencing the WFOE name and FDI code in the remittance instruction. The payment may be made in USD, HKD, EUR, or cross-border RMB through CIPS. Upon arrival, the receiving Tier-1 bank performs authenticity and compliance review, checks source-of-funds documentation, verifies the shareholder's identity, and credits the funds to the dedicated FDI capital account. The bank then applies the capital settlement rules, converting funds into RMB for negative-list-compliant operating purposes such as purchase of machinery, raw materials, salaries, and administrative expenses. A Chinese certified public accountant must subsequently issue a formal capital verification report confirming paid-in capital, and the WFOE must file that report with the company and treasury records before using funds for any purpose not declared in the original FDI registration. Any transfer back to the Hong Kong parent or onward lending requires separate regulatory and tax compliance.For imports of medical-device components and manufacturing equipment, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 or MT202 for documentary wire settlements, SWIFT MT700 for import letters of credit, and documentary collections for trusted counterparties; all transport documents, invoices, insurance certificates, and inspection certificates must fully conform to the LC terms. For exports of surgical laparoscopy towers, the WFOE should request a confirmed or usance export letter of credit from overseas buyers and may discount the accepted drafts at the Chinese bank to accelerate working capital. RMB-denominated cross-border trade settlements should be routed through CIPS to reduce correspondent-chain latency. All export receipts must be credited to the designated current-account foreign-exchange account and matched against customs declarations in SAFE's trade monitoring system within the required reporting window. The WFOE should establish standing counterparty lists in the bank's electronic banking system, pre-notify expected payment values and tenors, and maintain separate sub-accounts for USD, EUR, and RMB to simplify FX positioning and hedge effectiveness testing.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forwards between RMB and USD, EUR, or HKD, supported by genuine current-account or capital-account exposures evidenced by signed trade contracts, commercial invoices, custom declarations, or debt-service schedules.Cross-currency swaps and FX swaps with SAFE-qualified Chinese banks to hedge long-term foreign-currency liabilities and trade-cycle mismatches, subject to mandatory truthfulness and necessity review and requiring underlying loan, payables, or receivables documentation.Plain-vanilla FX options, including purchased calls and puts on RMB for real commercial exposures; onshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for mainland WFOEs and must be avoided at the regulated entity level, while the offshore Hong Kong parent may consider NDF hedging only outside the Chinese compliance perimeter.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise Business cannot serve as the primary treasury or statutory banking rail for a mainland WFOE. PRC commercial banking law and SAFE regulations require cross-border capital injections, FDI registration, export-proceeds repatriation, letter-of-credit issuance, and trade-document verification to be executed through licensed domestic banks with direct CIPS and SWIFT clearing membership. Foreign fintech EMIs are not licensed as PRC banks, cannot hold segmented FDI capital accounts, cannot verify customs declaration matching, and cannot issue compliance-grade capital verification reports. An offshore EMI may be used only as an ancillary payment tool for certain low-risk operational expenses outside China, but it must never be used for statutory capital contributions, dividend remittance, or trade settlement where SAFE documentary evidence and bank FXR reporting are mandatory. Attempting to route FDI capital through an EMI will likely trigger rejection, account freezing, or administrative penalties from SAFE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File triggering threshold: annual related-party tangible property transactions exceeding RMB 100 million, or intangible property, financial asset, or service transactions exceeding RMB 50 million; Master File triggering threshold: aggregate related-party turnover for the year exceeding RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply OECD-style Economic Substance Regulations as a separate off-shore code, but the State Taxation Administration applies the beneficial ownership test, General Anti-Abuse Rule under Article 47 of the CIT Law, and the OECD Principal Purpose Test to disregard shell or conduit HK entities. For IP-related royalty in-flows or out-flows, the functional analysis must show that Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA), namely development, enhancement, maintenance, protection, and exploitation (DEMPE), are performed in the jurisdiction claiming the return. A HK affiliate with no office, employees, or decision-making authority cannot receive treaty-protected dividends, interest, or royalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent invoicing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, unpaid taxes are recovered with a 0.05% daily late surcharge and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Where the amount is large and constitutes criminal tax evasion under Article 201 of the Criminal Law, fines and imprisonment of up to seven years may be imposed on the persons responsible.

Director personal liability: A director who arranged the false invoices and offshore diversion can be personally and jointly liable with the company for the tax debt under Article 20 of the Company Law by piercing the corporate veil; the director may be placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist, subjected to exit/travel bans, and sentenced for tax evasion or false special invoice issuing under Article 205 of the Criminal Law.
Transfer-pricing profit stripping and artificial shell arrangement

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 empowers the tax authority to make a primary transfer-pricing adjustment and assess deemed interest. Failure to maintain contemporaneous transfer-pricing documentation, or submission of false documentation, may result in penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law, plus a 10-year reassessment period under Article 48.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the non-arm's-length agreements and directed the profit shifting may be jointly and severally liable after piercing the corporate veil where the arrangement leaves the WFOE insolvent; they face tax-dishonesty blacklisting, travel bans, and criminal prosecution for intentional tax evasion if the adjusted unpaid tax is substantial.
Money laundering and shell-company abuse

Statutory sanction: Under the Anti-Money Laundering Law and Article 191 of the Criminal Law, laundering illegal proceeds carries imprisonment of up to ten years for serious cases, fines, confiscation of illicit gains, and corporate fines; the tax administration may also blacklist the company and revoke VAT export refunds if export documentation is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: A director who exercises effective control over the shell HK company and directs the laundering may be personally criminally liable for up to ten years' imprisonment, plus fines, confiscation, civil restitution to creditors, and exit restrictions; corporate veil piercing makes the director personally liable for the WFOE's unpaid taxes and trade debts.
