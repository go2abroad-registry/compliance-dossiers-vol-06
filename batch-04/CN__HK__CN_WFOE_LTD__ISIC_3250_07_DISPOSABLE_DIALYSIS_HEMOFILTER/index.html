Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

People's Republic of China: State Taxation Administration (STA) and the local in-charge tax bureau; electronic filing through the unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform operated by the STA. Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD), Business Registration Office, and the eTax portal for any separately registered Hong Kong entity or branch; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST institution.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License containing the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: The SAMR system automatically transmits the incorporation record to the local competent tax authority; within 30 days, complete supplementary tax registration details, including legal representative, financial accounting basis, bank deposit account, investors, business scope, and taxpayer type declaration, through the Electronic Tax Bureau or tax service hall. Step 3: Use the USCC as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for Corporate Income Tax, Value-Added Tax, payroll withholding, stamp duty, and other national taxes; no separate VAT certificate is issued. Step 4: Determine VAT taxpayer status: if annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration applies; below that threshold, the default is Small-scale VAT taxpayer unless voluntary General VAT taxpayer registration is elected. Step 5: For export zero-rating, complete customs registration with the General Administration of Customs (GACC), obtain the customs registration code, register foreign trade operating rights with the Ministry of Commerce (MOFCOM), and complete export foreign exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); after the first export, activate the export tax refund (exemption) taxpayer profile with the STA in the national export tax rebate system. Step 6: The foreign non-resident shareholder is not granted a PRC VAT ID; if the foreign parent or non-resident entity derives China-source income such as dividends, interest, or royalties, the Chinese payer must withhold Corporate Income Tax and VAT under the non-resident enterprise tax registration mechanism, and the non-resident may claim treaty benefits by submitting a valid Certificate of Tax Residence from its home jurisdiction. Step 7: If a Hong Kong sales or purchasing entity is established, apply to the IRD Business Registration Office for a Business Registration Certificate within one month of commencement of business and obtain a Hong Kong business registration number; Hong Kong has no VAT/GST registration obligation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not operate a check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election. Available elections under PRC tax law include: (i) voluntary registration as a General VAT taxpayer for taxpayers below the RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover threshold; (ii) Small-scale VAT taxpayer status using the simplified levy method, which does not permit input VAT credit; (iii) election of the applicable export tax refund mechanism, such as the production-enterprise exemption-credit-refund method or the foreign-trade-enterprise exemption-refund method, subject to STA conditions; (iv) statutory reduced Corporate Income Tax rate categories for qualifying technology or integrated-circuit enterprises, which are qualifications rather than free elections; and (v) Hong Kong profits tax rollover relief elections where replacement assets qualify. No hybrid mismatch elections are available in either PRC or Hong Kong tax law.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover. A taxpayer whose annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 must register as a General VAT taxpayer; below this threshold the default is Small-scale VAT taxpayer status, but voluntary General VAT taxpayer registration is available. The WFOE must complete tax registration from incorporation regardless of turnover; no turnover threshold exempts a limited liability company from tax registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law and the STA export tax refund (exemption) regime, a General VAT taxpayer may claim 0% VAT on exported goods when: (1) the goods are declared at customs and physically shipped out of the customs territory of China; (2) the goods are sold to an overseas buyer, including a Hong Kong or Macao buyer, under an export contract; (3) the export proceeds are received, or due to be received, in foreign exchange from the overseas buyer; (4) the export transaction is supported by a complete documentation trail; and (5) the goods are not classified as prohibited or non-refundable restricted exports. For the disposable dialysis hemofilter, the WFOE must also hold the required NMPA medical device registration and manufacturing license, and where necessary an export sales certificate, to obtain customs clearance. Small-scale VAT taxpayers generally obtain VAT exemption rather than zero-rating with input-VAT refund. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas recipients may be zero-rated where the services are consumed entirely outside the PRC and are not on the disallowed negative list, but the primary substructure here is goods. A Hong Kong trading leg is outside PRC VAT and is not subject to any Hong Kong VAT/GST zero-rating requirement.

  • Bill of Lading or air waybill issued by the carrier showing physical delivery to the overseas or Hong Kong consignee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or export proceeds collection confirmation issued by a designated foreign exchange bank, evidencing receipt and settlement of the foreign currency payment

Mandatory digital invoicing through the STA unified electronic invoice service platform. All VAT invoices, especially Special VAT Invoices used for input tax credit, are issued with a national invoice number and tax-control stamp generated in real time; invoice-level data is transmitted to the STA during issuance. The WFOE must implement electronic Fapiao accounting and reconcile invoice issuance with customs declaration data transmitted through the China International Trade Single Window. No off-system paper invoices are permitted as supporting evidence for VAT export zero-rating or export tax refund claims.

The STA and GACC jointly review export declarations, transport documents, VAT invoice data, and foreign exchange collection records. If zero-rating is claimed without physical export, without timely repatriation of export proceeds within the required period, or with false or incomplete documentation, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating, require repayment of refunded VAT, impose a daily late-payment surcharge at 0.05% of the outstanding tax, and may impose an administrative fine ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax understatement where evasion is established. Fraudulent export tax refund is a criminal offense under PRC Criminal Law and may result in imprisonment, confiscation, and unlimited retrospective clawback where fraud or evasion is proven. The clawback and investigation period is retrospective, and no time limitation applies when the inaccuracy is caused by intentional fraud or tax evasion.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial capital account convertibility. RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions such as trade in goods and services, provided that the underlying economic reality is substantiated through banking documentation, customs declarations, and invoices in compliance with SAFE verification procedures. For capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, inward equity injections, and external debt, entities must obtain SAFE quarantine and file with designated foreign exchange banks. The WFOE is permitted to convert injected foreign equity into RMB for operational use through the capital account settlement mechanism, but the resulting RMB funds are subject to supervisory conditions and cannot be diverted for prohibited uses such as equity investment without prior SAFE approval. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permissible after statutory annual statutory audits, completion of corporate income tax filings, and foreign exchange purchase from the designated domestic bank. Dividends are transferable on a current-account basis under the 'authenticity and conformity' principle. However, non-trade cross-border flows remain scrutinized to combat disguised equity outflows. The establishment of the dialysis hemofilter manufacturing WFOE is within the permitted foreign investment catalogue, but the entity must continuously maintain real-trade background documentation, local debt-to-equity ratios, and compliance with SAFE matrix reporting to ensure unimpeded repatriation of profits and operational liquidity. The overall convertibility is best characterized as administratively conditioned convertibility onshore, while offshore CNY rates may diverge, requiring treasury teams to monitor onshore/offshore spreads when structuring intra-group pooling or hedging.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Measures for the Administration of Foreign Exchange in Goods Trade, export proceeds from the WFOE's shipments of disposable dialysis hemofilters must be repatriated to China and deposited into the entity's domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 30 days after the customs export declaration date. The historical mandatory foreign exchange surrender requirement has been formally abolished; there is no forced conversion ratio into RMB. Exporters retain full discretion to hold the foreign currency in the designated account or to settle it into RMB at a domestic designated bank, subject to the review of underlying trade documentation, customs data, and the entity's genuine trade background. The entity must complete Balance of Payment reporting and provide matching invoices, bills of lading, and port-clearance data. Any settlement that exceeds the expected amount, or a failure to repatriate within the 30-day timeline, will trigger automatic monitoring by SAFE's cross-border transaction surveillance platform, potentially leading to suspension of trade facilitation status and administrative penalties. For the WFOE, a conservative treasury policy of immediate RMB settlement on invoice maturity is recommended to avoid keeping open foreign currency positions that contradict onshore reserve requirements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Chinese transfer pricing law requires contemporaneous Local File documentation when annual related-party transactions in tangible goods exceed RMB 200 million, or when annual related-party transactions in financial assets, intangible property, or services exceed RMB 100 million. A Master File is required when the aggregate amount of related-party transactions in the tax year reaches RMB 1 billion. A Country-by-Country Report must be filed by the ultimate parent or surrogate parent where the multinational group's consolidated revenue meets the statutory filing threshold, generally RMB 5.5 billion for the preceding year. The WFOE should also prepare a benchmarking analysis for its intercompany sales of disposable dialysis hemofilters and purchases of membrane raw materials regardless of whether the statutory threshold is exactly met, because filing compliance is a first-line defense against tax adjustment.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China enforces an economic-substance and reasonable-business-purpose doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule, beneficial-ownership rules, and transfer-pricing recharacterization powers. A Hong Kong intermediate holding company cannot serve as a mere mailbox to obtain DTA benefits. The Hong Kong entity must perform the core income-generating activities (CIGA) that align with its contractual claim to profits, including strategic decision-making, financing, treasury, IP ownership, and risk management. Conversely, the mainland WFOE must have real substance in China: factory premises, manufacturing equipment, production labor, quality certification, regulatory licenses for ISIC 3250, inventory risk, and significant operational management. If the Chinese tax authority determines that the WFOE lacks the capacity to perform its claimed functions or that the Hong Kong parent lacks substance, it may disregard the transaction, deny the foreign tax credit, impose withholding taxes on hidden profit distributions, and assess interest and penalties as a disguised profit repatriation.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Fraud and Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: The State Administration of Taxation may make a special tax adjustment under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Bulletin 42, re-determine the arm's length price, add back disallowed deductions, and impose an interest surcharge equal to the benchmark RMB loan rate plus five percentage points for the period of underpayment. If the false transactions are recorded with intent to evade tax, administrative penalties may reach between 50% and 500% of the tax underpaid, and criminal liability may arise under the PRC Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing statements, approve sham intercompany agreements, or instruct accountants to conceal related-party transactions may be deemed personally responsible. Under PRC Company Law principles of piercing the corporate veil, the court can hold directors jointly and severally liable if abuse of the limited liability form is used to evade tax and damage creditors. Directors may also be subject to exit restriction blacklisting, travel bans, freezing of personal assets, and personal tax collection for taxes owed by the entity where they are found to be the actual persons in control of the evasion scheme. In criminal cases, the responsible director faces imprisonment plus fines.
Unlawful Dividend Repatriation and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and Tax Collection Administration Law, the taxpayer or withholding agent that fails to withhold or under-withholds is ordered to remedy the failure, pay the outstanding tax, and is subject to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unpaid withheld amount. Late-payment surcharges of 0.05% per day apply. If the arrangement is found to be fraudulent tax evasion, criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law for tax evasion can result in detention or imprisonment and heavier monetary penalties.

Director personal liability: The directors and the legal representative of the WFOE can be treated as the withholding agents or organizers of the unlawful transfer. The tax authority may pursue them personally for the unpaid withholding tax, freeze their bank accounts and assets, impose exit bans, and list them in tax dishonesty blacklists. Where the court pierces the corporate veil, directors are personally liable for restitution of the tax loss and any interest or penalty. Deliberate use of offshore intermediary entities to disguise dividends is also an anti-money laundering and false-declaration offense.
Shell Company Abuse and False Invoicing / False Export Declarations

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may disallow all fictitious deductions, deny the export VAT refund, apply the GAAR to recharacterize the transactions, and obtain a full tax adjustment. False issuance or use of special VAT invoices to reduce tax liability is a serious criminal offense under PRC Criminal Law Articles 205 and 205-1, carrying fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment, potentially up to life imprisonment in particularly serious cases. The use of a Hong Kong shell to disguise profit is also treated as tax fraud rather than legitimate treaty planning.

Director personal liability: Directors, actual controllers, and the legal representative who arrange fake invoicing, sign false customs declarations, or use shell companies to evade tax can be stripped of limited-liability protection. The corporate veil will be pierced under PRC Company Law if the corporate form is the instrumentality of fraud. Directors may be ordered to return diverted corporate funds, personally pay the tax underpaid plus penalties, and suffer travel restrictions or exit bans. Criminal imprisonment penalties apply for tax evasion and VAT invoice fraud; the persons directing the shell structure are not insulated by the company's legal personality and may be jointly and severally liable for restitution.
