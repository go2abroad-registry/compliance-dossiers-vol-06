Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is prescribed by the Companies Ordinance for a Hong Kong private limited company; the shareholders are free to set the issued share capital as stated in the Statement of Capital, commonly HK$10,000. No blocked escrow account requirement applies at the Companies Registry level. The Chinese parent company, however, should align the WFOE's registered capital with the medical device business scope under the Foreign Investment Law and ensure actual contributions are made in accordance with the filed articles of association.

Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal; the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via eTAX is the separate competent authority for the Hong Kong SAR jurisdiction's territorial tax filings, where applicable.

E-government filing portal

A WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license upon incorporation, and the USCC is concurrently the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Under the multi-certificate integration reform, the tax registration is created automatically with the STA. Within 30 calendar days of the issuance of the business license, the management must log in to the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) to complete the post-filing, activate VAT taxpayer status, confirm the accounting method and annual taxable sales estimate, and file the legal representative identification, registered address, articles of association, and bank account information. Where the foreign non-resident owner has income sourced in China but no permanent establishment, it must obtain a temporary taxpayer identification code through the withholding agent or by direct application at the competent STA branch, and the WFOE must maintain the corporate registration and ownership records to evidence non-resident direct/indirect shareholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under China's entity classification regime; no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity election is available. However, a newly established WFOE may elect between small-scale taxpayer status and general VAT taxpayer status at registration, subject to the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales threshold, and may subsequently elect general taxpayer status on a voluntary basis.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales (the compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold); the WFOE must complete tax registration immediately upon incorporation regardless of turnover, and if its annual taxable sales exceed the threshold it is legally required to register as a general VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of physical goods from the PRC customs territory, including mechanically turbine-driven intensive-care ventilators under HS code 9019.20 (ISIC 3250 / NAICS 339112), is eligible for 0% VAT only where the exporter is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods have been declared to and physically cleared by Customs, the export proceeds are received from the overseas buyer in foreign currency, and the underlying transaction is recorded in the STA export tax exemption/refund system. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to non-residents and consumed outside China may also be zero-rated, but the WFOE's primary substantiation route is the physical export customs and foreign exchange collection record.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill if shipped by air)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract/Purchase Order
  • Input VAT Special Invoice(s) proving the purchase and cost of exported goods
  • Foreign Exchange Proceeds Collection Certificate / Export Proceeds Entry Record as reflected in the State Administration of Foreign Exchange trade monitoring system
  • Export Tax Refund (Zero-Rating) Declaration filed through the China Electronic Tax Bureau

China mandates issuance of tax-controlled VAT invoices through the National Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform under the Golden Tax System IV. The WFOE must use the all-electronic digital invoice mechanism, called the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI), where invoice data is generated, cleared and signed by the STA in real time through official APIs. Each FDEI carries a unique tax-controlled invoice number and digital tax-control signature; no offline invoice software can legally issue VAT invoices without real-time data synchronization to the STA. The Golden Tax System automatically cross-matches purchase-side input invoices and sales-side output invoices to detect divergence, thereby enforcing anti-fraud and electronic clearance.

The STA, General Administration of Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange exchange digital data on export declarations, invoices and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed on false or unverified documentation, the STA will deny or claw back the export VAT refund, impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per calendar day, and apply a penalty of 50% up to five times the amount of the tax evaded under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Law on Punishment of Tax Evasion. Where export documentation is fabricated or the physical export is not completed, customs anti-smuggling enforcement may treat the transaction as fraudulent tax refund, and under the Criminal Law those responsible may face criminal liability for the statutory crime of fraudulent export tax refund in addition to retrospective clawback of all refunded VAT and related corporate income tax relief.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates under a managed floating exchange-rate regime with a structurally bifurcated convertibility framework. Current-account items, including the export and import of goods, transportation, professional fees, and routine profit remittances, are convertible on a real-demand basis: a bank may buy or sell RMB against documentary proof of the underlying transaction without SAFE pre-approval. Capital-account items, such as direct equity injection from the Hong Kong parent, cross-border intercompany loans, external debt, hedges, and portfolio investments, remain subject to prior registration with SAFE, a qualified foreign-invested enterprise registration, and post-usage monitoring. There is no fixed statutory export-proceeds surrender ratio; however, the WFOE must repatriate export receivables to a designated domestic foreign-exchange settlement account and may either retain 100% in foreign currency or convert to RMB at the prevailing market rate, while the bank enters the trade receipts and payments in the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permitted only after the annual statutory audit has been completed, enterprise income tax has been settled, and the bank receives an audit report, tax payment voucher, board resolution for dividends, and the applicable current-account remittance purpose code; if the WFOE has unresolved SAFE penalties or negative equity, the remittance will be rejected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration framework, the exporting WFOE must repatriate export proceeds to a domestic designated foreign-exchange settlement account within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration unless the bank has approved an advance or delayed collection arrangement. No fixed mandatory conversion ratio applies; the exporter may retain up to 100% of the foreign-currency proceeds or convert any portion at the onshore spot market rate. The bank reconciles each collection against the customs declaration, commercial invoice, and bill of lading through the China Customs Single Window; overdue unrepatriated balances trigger trade-monitoring enquiries and may suspend the enterprise's trade facilitation status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low, and legally barred for the mainland WFOE. A non-bank fintech EMI does not hold a Chinese commercial banking license or a SAFE-designated foreign-exchange licence; it cannot open a RMB basic deposit account, issue letters of credit, or perform trade collection and verification under PBOC and SAFE rules. The WFOE must clear all cross-border receipts and payments through a licensed Chinese bank. Offshore EMIs may be used only by the Hong Kong parent as treasury aggregation tools, never as the bank of record for the mainland entity.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit equity capital through SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a licensed PRC bank. Before the remittance, the WFOE must complete SAFE Foreign Direct Investment registration and obtain the Foreign Exchange Registration Notice with the unique FDI registration code. The HK remitter should use bank purpose code 621011 for establishment contribution or 621012 for capital increase and include the SAFE registration number in the payment narrative. The receiving Chinese bank verifies the capital account, authenticates the MT103, credits the funds, and, on the WFOE's instruction, settles foreign exchange into RMB at the onshore market rate subject to the capital-account negative list. A Chinese CPA then issues a capital verification report; the bank files the capital contribution in the SAFE system and releases funds only against approved invoices and use vouchers. Statutory escrow is not required, but a bank may insist on a capital escrow or account-monitoring agreement when credit facilities are granted.For imports of components and raw materials, the WFOE should use either a documentary import letter of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 or MT701 for high-value turbine orders, or a direct SWIFT MT103 payment against commercial invoices after customs declaration, with the bank recording the goods-trade purpose code and matching the customs declaration number in the Single Window. For export sales of ICU ventilators to overseas government buyers, the WFOE should present full transport documents, including commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, NMPA export certificate, and customs export declaration, under an irrevocable confirmed LC at sight, or receive SWIFT MT103 wires on government procurement contracts. Documentary collections are acceptable only for pre-vetted distributors. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (mainland China corporate income tax on the WFOE as a resident enterprise)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% under PRC EIT Law Article 37; reduced to 5% under the China-HK DTA when the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company holding at least 25% of the WFOE's capital.
