Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online filing platform and administered by the local in-charge tax service bureaus for the WFOE's registered address.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Market Regulation Administration upon incorporation; the USCC is the mandatory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax, VAT, and customs purposes. Within 30 calendar days of incorporation, the WFOE must complete the tax-registration declaration in the Electronic Tax Bureau, submitting the business license, articles of association, legal representative identity documents, bank account information, and accounting/financial-institution information. For a foreign non-resident parent company with a China-source economic presence, the parent must either complete a non-resident enterprise tax registration directly with the in-charge tax authority or be registered by the WFOE as withholding agent using the non-resident enterprise registration and withholding-tax reporting workflow; the tax authority then issues a non-resident enterprise tax identification number upon presentation of the foreign incorporation certificate, board resolution appointing the withholding agent, and the relevant cross-border contract. A separate VAT registration is not required because the USCC is the unified taxpayer identifier; VAT taxpayer status is annotated in the same tax registration record.

Mandatory tax registration applies from incorporation with no minimum turnover threshold; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual VAT taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (CNY five million), and below that the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status for input VAT deduction/refund purposes.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies to the WFOE's export sales of manufactured intraocular acrylic lenses when the goods are cleared by PRC Customs as exported, title and risk pass to an overseas non-resident buyer, and sales proceeds are repatriated through an approved cross-border foreign-exchange settlement channel. As a production enterprise, the WFOE uses the exemption-offset-refund mechanism for export VAT rebate: output VAT is exempted on export sales, input VAT on exported goods is first offset against domestic output VAT, and the remaining credit becomes repayable by the tax authority. Qualifying cross-border services and digital services supplied for wholly offshore consumption may also qualify under the zero-rated export service list if no domestic beneficiary exists.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (electronic declaration record generated by the China International Trade Single Window and validated by the General Administration of Customs release system, functionally equivalent to a Bill of Entry/export customs declaration form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure from the PRC customs territory, showing the foreign consignee/destination and the actual date of export
  • Foreign-exchange receipt confirmation issued by the PRC receiving bank evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds; the bank cross-border receipt/settlement voucher serves as the modern functional equivalent of the legacy Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), supplemented by the SAFE trade-foreign-exchange collection verification record
  • Commercial invoice, sales/purchase contract or order, packing list, and the export VAT ordinary invoice

VAT invoices, including zero-rated export ordinary invoices, must be issued as fully digitized electronic invoices (digital electronic invoices) on the STA's e-Invoice Service Platform; taxpayers' billing systems must connect to the e-invoice platform via the official application programming interface (API) or browser-based portal so that each invoice receives a unique 20-digit invoice code and an anti-fraud digital QR stamp generated by the tax authority before release. Issuance, red-letter credit notes, and invoice data are transmitted in near real time to the Golden Tax Phase IV central reconciliation system. Export customs declarations are separately filed through the China International Trade Single Window, and the customs release data, VAT invoice data, and foreign-exchange receipt data are automatically matched for refund-control audit purposes.

The Golden Tax Phase IV system automatically links customs, VAT invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt data; any mismatch in product description, quantity, value, or receipt date generates a compliance alert requiring documentary explanation. Failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory deadline, use of falsified transport documents, or fraudulent overstatement of refundable input VAT results in retrospective revocation of the zero-rating, clawback of the refunded VAT plus statutory late-payment interest, and monetary penalties of up to five times the tax evaded. Intentional export tax-refund fraud constitutes a criminal offense under PRC law punishable by significant fines and imprisonment; responsible officers and the WFOE may be jointly liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (CNY) regime under the Foreign Exchange Administration Rules and SAFE regulations maintains full current-account convertibility for trade in goods and services, while capital-account transactions remain strictly controlled through a licensing, filing, and post-mortem verification framework. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) engaged in the manufacturing of intraocular acrylic lenses may freely settle and convert CNY for operating expenses, import of raw acrylic polymers, and routine business service payments, subject to genuine trade background documentation (contracts, commercial invoices, customs declarations). However, all foreign direct investment (FDI) capital injections, repatriation of registered capital, and profit remittance to its Hong Kong parent entity fall under capital-account supervision, requiring SAFE registration (via a designated Chinese Tier-1 commercial bank), factual and capital verification reports from a licensed Chinese CPA, and annual audit as well as tax clearance certificates before any outbound transfer. The WFOE must open a dedicated Capital Account (FDI RMB/Foreign Currency Bank Account) for inbound share capital, which is released in tranches based on a justified operational expenditure plan. No forced conversion ratio applies to export proceeds nowadays, but all export earnings must be repatriated to a domestic FX settlement account within the statutory time limit. Profit remittance in foreign currency is permitted after compliance with the annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and required documentary submissions; withholding tax on dividends at 10% (unless reduced under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement) must be discharged. In practice, currency convertibility is robust for bona fide trade but illiquid for speculative capital flows; Chinese regulators retain discretion to conduct on-site inspections upon suspicious transactions. The current account is effectively free, the capital account is moderately open with clear documentary hurdles, and no parallel market arbitrage is tolerated.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Administrative Rules for Trade in Goods (2012), there is no mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may keep export proceeds in a foreign exchange account or settle to RMB at will. However, all export receipts must be repatriated to China and credited to the entity's domestic FX settlement account within 90 days of the customs export declaration date. A maximum extension of 180 days may be granted by applying to SAFE through the designated bank, subject to justifiable trade delays, returned goods, or quality disputes. The intraocular lens manufacturer must submit the export declaration number, freight invoice, and bank credit advice to the bank monitoring system to close the trade files; failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers capital-account penalty and potential downgrade of the company's FX quota.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: NOT VIABLE (legal prohibition). Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Currenxie cannot act as primary transactional settlement rails for a mainland Chinese WFOE entity. Chinese banking law requires all Foreign Exchange and RMB corporate settlement accounts to be held with a banking institution licensed by the PBOC and SAFE; non-bank fintech EMI platforms are not permitted to hold a legal-entity bank identification number (CNAPS) or to route corporate funds directly from a Chinese WFOE without passing through a domestic commercial bank. Such EMIs could theoretically be used for a Hong Kong offshore company's foreign income, but they cannot be interposed as clearing agents between the WFOE and overseas counterparties for the WFOE's own trade payments. Domestic fintech subsidiaries (e.g., MYBank, WeBank) are likewise barred from providing cross-border FX conversion and FDI capital-account services. Therefore, all onboarding must be executed via a PBOC-licensed Tier-1 commercial bank with a separate approval to handle capital-account transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong parent entity to the WFOE will be executed by SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent's bank to the designated FDI Capital Account at a PBOC-licensed bank in mainland China. The remittance wire must include (a) beneficiary legal name exactly matching the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate; (b) explicit purpose code: 'FDI equity injection' (capital account item 621101) or 'Unrestricted euro/RMB foreign debt' as per SAFE's List of Foreign Exchange Revenue and Expenditure Items; and (c) the unique entity code (USCI). The foreign exchange bank will first verify the SAFE FDI registration (via the internet banking portal of the SAFE system) and then release the capital into a restricted 'FDI Capital Verification Account'. Within 15 working days, a Chinese licensed CPA must issue a Capital Verification Report for the FDI (验资报告) confirming the actual injection; the bank will then transfer funds to the operational current account upon presentation of the report and a business expense disbursement plan. All FX conversion from USD/HKD to CNY must be executed at the onshore prevailing FX rate (base rate via China Foreign Exchange Trade System) and cannot be done through an offshore ring-fenced market.For payment of imported raw materials (e.g., acrylic polymer pellets, hydrophobic coating solvents) and licensing fees, the WFOE will use the SWIFT MT103 (FIT wire) for urgent direct payments under open-account trade terms, referencing a genuine trade contract and post-customs import declaration. For high-value imports without open account capacity, Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) issued by a Tier-1 Chinese bank in favor of foreign suppliers provide an orthodox clearing rail. Documentary Collections (DP/DA) are also possible for small repeated shipments. All outbound disbursements require submission of the SAFE trade file: invoice, bill of lading, customs declaration, purchase order, and ID copy of the customs broker. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility rating: NOT VIABLE (legal prohibition). Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Currenxie cannot act as primary transactional settlement rails for a mainland Chinese WFOE entity. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million, or where related-party intangible, financial, or service transactions exceed RMB 100 million; Master File preparation is mandatory where the consolidated group's annual revenue reaches RMB 1 billion; any enterprise with cross-border related-party transactions must also file the Annual Related-Party Transactions Reporting Table.

Economic substance scope: The mainland WFOE must maintain actual economic substance through its manufacturing facilities, skilled workforce, procurement of raw materials, quality-control operations, and day-to-day management decisions located in China. The Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits must similarly demonstrate real business activity, substantive management, employees, premises, and core income-generating activities in Hong Kong. Under PRC GAAR (EIT Law Article 47) and contemporary anti-abuse doctrine, tax authorities may deny treaty benefits or recharacterize a transaction if the Hong Kong entity is a mere conduit or the arrangement lacks commercial rationality.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may impose a special tax adjustment under Articles 41 and 48 of the EIT Law, uplift taxable income, and charge interest at the benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points for each retroactive year; if the conduct amounts to deliberate concealment, Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law imposes an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid corporate income tax.

Director personal liability: Under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, directors who abuse corporate limited liability and the separate legal personality of the WFOE to evade tax may be ordered to assume unlimited personal liability for unpaid corporate tax. The legal representative may face a travel ban and exit restriction under Article 44 of the Tax Administration Law, and directors who knowingly certify false transfer-pricing documentation may be prosecuted under Article 201 of the Criminal Law for tax evasion, carrying imprisonment of up to seven years plus criminal fines.
Shell Company Treaty Shopping and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: A GAAR reassessment under Articles 47 and 120 of the EIT Law may deny the DTA benefit, restore the 10% statutory dividend WHT (or impose 25% CIT if a PE is found), add late-payment interest from the original due date, and impose a misdeclaration penalty of 50% to five times the under-withheld tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong shell who sign false beneficial-ownership declarations or artificial substance certificates expose themselves to civil liability for the resulting tax underpayment, travel bans, and company blacklisting from Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Department; criminal fraud prosecutions may follow under Hong Kong fraud law and mainland Criminal Law Article 201 where the arrangement is used to conceal mainland-controlled income, with custodial sentences available for large-scale evasion.
Withholding Tax Evasion on Outbound Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to three times the amount of withholding tax not collected and remitted; Article 32 adds daily late-payment interest at 0.05% on the unpaid WHT; if the failure is a deliberate fraudulent act to reduce tax, Article 63 elevates the penalty to 50% to five times the tax evaded.

Director personal liability: Directors who order, approve, or knowingly permit the unwithheld outbound remittance may be held jointly and severally liable to restore the WHT where corporate assets are insufficient, and may be subjected to exit bans, asset freezes, and credit blacklisting. Knowingly concealing the non-resident nature of the recipient constitutes criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to seven years for especially large unpaid tax amounts.
