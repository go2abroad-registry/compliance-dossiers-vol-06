Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: the STA 'Electronic Tax Service Platform' (e-Tax) accessible through the nationwide unified electronic tax service system, with export/customs data linked to the China International Trade Single Window. The Hong Kong SAR has a separate Inland Revenue Department and is treated as a separate customs and tax territory for PRC purposes.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), tax identification is integrated with corporate registration: upon issuance of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the legal taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID). Within 30 calendar days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform, providing legal representative, finance/accounting personnel, accounting system details, registered and business addresses, shareholding structure, and bank account information. The local competent tax authority then confirms the taxpayer profile, tax status, tax filing periods, and general VAT taxpayer qualification. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (e.g., the Hong Kong shareholder) that receives PRC-source income without a permanent establishment, the WFOE or paying agent performs non-resident withholding tax registration/records with the STA; the non-resident must supply its foreign tax registration identity data (Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and Hong Kong tax identification number) and is captured in the STA withholding tax ledgers. A foreign non-resident enterprise that later creates a permanent establishment must undertake full PRC tax registration using its home-jurisdiction TIN plus certified translations of its constitutional documents.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE, as a general VAT taxpayer, may claim 0% VAT on the export sale of goods (including surgical suture needle assemblies) when the goods are physically shipped from the PRC customs territory to a destination outside mainland China, including the Hong Kong SAR, and the title/risk passes to an overseas buyer. The claim requires a valid China Customs export declaration, genuine commercial documents, issuance of an export ordinary VAT invoice or commercial invoice, collection of export proceeds in foreign currency or offshore RMB through a designated bank, and repatriation/foreign exchange settlement within the statutory deadline. The exported goods must not be resold or consumed within the PRC before export. Zero-rating also applies to qualifying exported services rendered wholly outside mainland China to overseas customers, provided the service is included in the statutory zero-rated cross-border services scope and the required service contract, delivery evidence, payment receipt, and foreign exchange settlement records are maintained. Input VAT attributable to export sales is refundable or offset under the PRC export VAT rebate (exemption and refund) mechanism.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (the formal export declaration/customs bill of entry for export) and the electronically matched export information record transmitted to the STA
  • Signed Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or equivalent multimodal transport document proving physical exportation out of mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract or purchase order evidencing the underlying cross-border transaction
  • Foreign exchange receipt record: the PRC statutory evidence of inward payment, including the bank's International Transaction Receipt and Payment Declaration and the Foreign Exchange Collection/Settlement Record (the functional PRC equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate or FIRC)
  • Export VAT rebate/refund declaration data set generated from the STA Electronic Tax Service Platform, together with the WFOE's VAT return export schedule
  • Customs/export broker and logistics documentation (where applicable) showing pick-up, warehouse custody, export carrier, and overseas delivery

China requires VAT invoice issuance through the State Taxation Administration's tax-control invoice system under the Golden Tax framework. All Special VAT Invoices and Ordinary VAT Invoices must be generated using tax-control equipment or the STA's full-digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) platform, with real-time electronic upload of invoice data for fiscal clearance. Accepted invoices carry a unique encrypted invoice serial number, the STA's digital fiscal stamp, and an anti-counterfeiting machine-readable code. System-to-system issuance requires integration with the STA-certified e-invoice service interface (API) and digital certificate authentication. Negative/red invoices for cancellations or returns require pre-approval and must be mirror-matched against the original positive invoice. Export sales are declared in the VAT return export schedule and automatically matched by the STA against electronic customs export information.

The STA uses classified risk management and automated matching of customs export declarations, VAT invoice data, bank foreign exchange receipts, and logistics records to audit zero-ratings. If export proceeds are not received/repatriated within 180 days (or an approved extension) or the required documents are missing, the tax authority deems the sale taxable at domestic standard rate, assesses overdue VAT and daily interest, and may recharacterize the transaction as unsubstantiated. Where an enterprise obtains export VAT refunds by false declaration, counterfeit or unverified documents, no physical export, round-tripping of funds, or fraudulent invoice arrangements, the refund is clawed back retroactively for the statutory recovery period (indefinitely for fraud), late-payment interest is imposed, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax involved applies under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Criminal referral for fraudulent export VAT refund or unlawful issuance of VAT-specific invoices may result in criminal liability for executives and the enterprise.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating RMB exchange rate regime with a dual-track current and capital account structure. Current account convertibility is broadly liberalized: export proceeds and trade-related payments can be settled through designated banks after submitting customs declarations, commercial invoices, transport documents and underlying contract evidence. The capital account remains tightly administered. Each equity injection from the Hong Kong parent must be registered with SAFE through a designated bank, wired into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and subsequently settled into RMB in controlled transactions. The settled RMB capital must be applied only to the WFOE's licensed business purposes, such as raw material procurement, wages, lease obligations, or repayment of genuine onshore loans, and is prohibited from flowing into security investment, lending to unrelated companies, or speculative FX products. Profit remittance hurdles include the need to demonstrate after-tax distributable profits, submit audited financial statements, file the annual foreign exchange business report, settle full corporate income tax and withholding tax on dividends, and receive bank authorization for the outward dividend wire. Export proceeds are subject to repatriation and SAFE monitoring; although no blanket mandatory conversion ratio applies to ordinary goods trade, every transaction must be genuine, traceable and consistent with customs declarations. Non-compliance creates a Level-1 treasury event and can freeze the entire liquidity platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to the PRC within 180 days after the export goods are declared to customs. The funds must arrive in a licensed domestic bank account and be monitored through SAFE's Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration System. Overdue unmatched export collection requires a written explanation and, where necessary, a rectification plan approved by the bank. Mandatory conversion ratio: 0% for ordinary goods trade under the current legal framework. The WFOE may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in a domestic foreign exchange account or convert any portion into RMB at the settlement bank's quoted rate. This flexibility is conditional upon genuine trade matching, absence of dummy transactions, and prompt disclosure of any change in collection method.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Not viable for the onshore WFOE treasury. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE and cannot operate as the WFOE's statutory settlement bank. They cannot receive and verify FDI capital account injections, convert foreign exchange into RMB within China, maintain a regulated RMB basic deposit account, or route export proceeds through SAFE's goods trade monitoring system. They may serve only as an ancillary multi-currency wallet or payment collection tool for the Hong Kong parent, but all onshore RMB treasury functions for the WFOE must be performed by a licensed Chinese Tier-1 commercial bank. Attempting to use an unlicensed EMI as the primary clearing rail for export proceeds or capital contributions will create hard AML red flags, FX violation risk, and potential PBOC administrative penalties.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must wire FDI equity funds only to the WFOE's dedicated foreign exchange capital escrow account at a Chinese Tier-1 designated bank. Before the wire, SAEE-approved FDI registration must be completed and the bank must issue the corresponding foreign investment registration number. The SWIFT MT103 must identify the exact remitter as the registered shareholder, state the purpose as equity injection for registered capital using the correct SAFE business purpose code, and quote the FDI registration number in the remittance information field. The remitter name, recipient name and support documents must match the filed articles of association. Upon receipt, the bank will convert incoming foreign currency to RMB in tranches consistent with capital account settlement rules. After each tranche, a Chinese registered CPA must issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital against the subscribed capital. The cumulative amount must never exceed the registered capital shown in the WFOE's business license, and any future capital increase requires a separate SAFE filing through the bank.For ordinary export transactions, use SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for documented advance payments and open-account settlements. Attempting to use an unlicensed EMI as the primary clearing rail for export proceeds or capital contributions will create hard AML red flags, FX violation risk, and potential PBOC administrative penalties.
