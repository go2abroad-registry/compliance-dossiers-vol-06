Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

The PRC Company Law and the Foreign Investment Law impose no statutory minimum registered capital for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in pharmaceutical active ingredient manufacturing. China applies a subscribed capital regime. Nevertheless, for the ISIC 3250 heparin sodium anticoagulant manufacturing project, the registered capital must be commensurate with the production scale, expected fixed-asset investment, and GMP licensing requirements. A paid-in capital of no less than RMB 5,000,000, or the USD equivalent remitted into the company's designated foreign-currency capital account, is the recommended threshold to support drug manufacturing licensing, environmental approval and customs export registration. No government escrow or blocked capital account is required; funds are remitted directly into the company's own capital contribution account after the business license is issued and must be verified by a certified public accountant under the foreign exchange custody rules of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through provincial and county-level competent tax offices; online taxpayer portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform and the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a Chinese resident legal person, so the statutory TIN/VAT ID is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation upon business license registration. The foreign non-resident shareholder does not obtain the WFOE TIN; the WFOE itself completes tax information registration within 30 days after receiving the business license through the competent STA office or the Electronic Tax Bureau. Required filings include the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, shareholder identification, bank account details, registered office proof, and entity formation documents. The STA then activates the taxpayer profile for Corporate Income Tax, Value Added Tax, stamp duty, and withholding obligations. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment is separately registered by the WFOE as withholding agent when dividends, interest, royalties, or service fees are remitted, and a withholding taxpayer code is created at that time.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the standard 25% Corporate Income Tax rate; no pass-through or territorial exemption is available for the WFOE's operating income.

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists under PRC tax law. The WFOE may elect between small-scale VAT taxpayer status and general VAT taxpayer status below the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales threshold, but general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory above that threshold and is the necessary election to access export zero-rating and input VAT refund.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete tax registration at formation regardless of turnover; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Export-oriented WFOEs may voluntarily register as general VAT taxpayers below the threshold to obtain input VAT credits and export refund eligibility.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A general VAT taxpayer WFOE may claim 0% VAT on the export supply of heparin sodium anticoagulant only if the goods are physically exported outside PRC customs territory, covered by a valid Customs export declaration, sold to an overseas buyer under a genuine export contract, supported by a Special VAT invoice for export, and the foreign currency sales proceeds are received and repatriated into China within the statutory 180-day deadline. The corresponding input VAT embedded in the exported goods may then be recovered through the export tax rebate (export drawback) procedure.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document such as air waybill
  • Commercial invoice and signed export contract
  • Special VAT invoice for export
  • Bank receipt of export proceeds in foreign currency evidencing inward remittance and repatriation into China

China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) issued through STA-approved channels, with real-time fiscal clearance via the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform integrated with Golden Tax Phase IV. VAT invoices must carry standardized commodity tax classification codes, unique invoice identifiers, digital encryption, and be transmitted electronically to the STA through prescribed API/data exchange interfaces. For export transactions, invoice data is matched with customs declarations and foreign exchange collection records through the China International Trade Single Window.

Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized by STA and PRC Customs via matched data from the China International Trade Single Window, including customs declarations, electronic invoices, logistics transport records, and bank foreign exchange receipts. If a claim is unverified, fraudulent, or the export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the STA disallows the zero-rating, claws back any VAT refunded, imposes daily late-payment surcharges at 0.05%, and may assess administrative penalties of one to five times the amount of the fraudulently obtained refund under the Law of the PRC on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious cases are referred for criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund, and the exporter may be downgraded or removed from export tax rebate eligibility with retrospective reassessment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates an administered float with current-account convertibility achieved under the 1996 IMF Article VIII obligation, but capital-account items remain subject to comprehensive approval and registration. A mainland WFOE's equity capital injected from Hong Kong is treated as foreign direct investment and can be converted into RMB only through a designated foreign exchange capital account after SAFE registration. Routine current-account payments such as import settlement, royalties and profit remittance are permitted without pre-approval if a valid transaction document and a genuine underlying trade or service are presented, but banks must perform a genuineness and compliance review. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder are not freely automatic: the WFOE must provide certified audited financial statements, Chinese tax clearance certificates including withholding income tax on dividends, board resolutions on dividend distribution, and a bank verification on the fund-generating position. Surrender rules for export proceeds have been liberalised; mandatory conversion at a fixed ratio no longer applies, but all export foreign currency must be received into a domestic foreign exchange account, registered in the trade monitoring system, and either retained or settled within the permitted framework. The absence of full capital-account opening means cross-border financing, guarantee and derivative activities require registration or prior approval, and any attempt to access offshore hedging markets is restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the PRC Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring scheme, export proceeds must be repatriated and credited to the exporter's foreign exchange settlement account within the banking system; the exporter has a maximum of five working days after receipt to automatically match the proceeds against outstanding Customs export declarations in the monitoring system, and may then choose voluntary settlement into RMB or retention. There is no fixed compulsory conversion percentage. For exporters classified under Category B or C due to prior violations, SAFE may impose stricter settlement verification and documentary review for each transaction.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign-exchange administrator sitting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs (for example Revolut Business or Wise Multi-Currency) cannot serve as the primary banking or clearing account for a mainland China WFOE. Legal constraints restrict onshore RMB settlement and SAFE-registered foreign exchange transactions to licensed Chinese banks; EMIs without a PBOC or SAFE licence have no access to CIPS or CNY clearing, and are prohibited from holding enterprise funds under the Administrative Measures for E-commerce Payment. The only marginal role is as an auxiliary collection wallet for non-mainland customer receipts routed later to the onshore bank, but this does not replace the statutory accounts. Feasibility rating is Low; legally non-viable as a principal treasury account.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered when: (i) related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million; (ii) related-party intangible asset or financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; or (iii) related-party provision of services exceeds RMB 40 million; the same RMB 40 million threshold applies to interest and loans with related parties. A Master File is required for an enterprise whose group has consolidated annual revenue exceeding RMB 5 billion and whose Chinese related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million. An enterprise may also be required, upon SAT notice, to prepare a benchmarking study or other country-by-country report.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply a separate Economic Substance Regulation to resident WFOEs because the WFOE is already taxed on its substantive production. However, economic substance is decisive for the Hong Kong parent when invoking the DTA relief and for the entire group under the PRC General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, implemented by SAT Circular 6/2016 and Circular 5/2017). The Hong Kong entity claiming treaty benefits must perform the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, meaning it must have an actual office, employees, board direction and transaction decision-making in Hong Kong. If the Hong Kong parent is an empty shell, the dividend, interest and royalty income will be re-characterised by the Chinese tax authority as taxable to the substantial mainland beneficiary and treaty relief will be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing / cross-border profit shifting

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 empowers the tax authority to re-assess the transaction; Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 3 times the unpaid tax plus daily overdue surcharge. Criminal Law Article 201 applies where the under-reported tax amount is significant, leading to criminal fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorise the mispricing can be held jointly and severally liable for the tax shortfall through the Law of Tort or the Company Law Article 20 principle. They may be placed on the 'non-normal exit control' travel ban by the exit-entry administration, have personal assets frozen or confiscated, and risk criminal detention; in particularly severe cases a criminal court can sentence the responsible officer to imprisonment under Article 201.
Shell entity / DTA treaty abuse

Statutory sanction: Under STA Public Notice 2015/60 and the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement general anti-abuse clauses, the treaty right is denied; the tax authority re-characterises the dividend as subject to 10% withholding, plus penalty under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 (50%-3 times) and daily surcharge. The enterprise may also be blacklisted for tax credit violations.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the WFOE and the Hong Kong shell risk personal enforcement: they can be made civilly liable for the unpaid withholding and penalties, be prohibited from leaving mainland China (exit control / travel ban), lose personal assets through property seizure, and be criminally prosecuted for tax evasion where the under-withheld amount is large and the conduct is deliberate. The director who signed the dividend declaration and the WHT return bears the primary criminal exposure.
False VAT invoice / procurement fraud

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 punishes the issuance, use or creation of false VAT special invoices with a fine of 2 to 20 times the false amount and imprisonment up to 7 years for standard occurrences; aggravated cases can lead to life imprisonment. Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 additionally assesses the evaded tax with a 50%-5 times penalty. The false invoices are entirely non-deductible for CIT and disallow the input VAT credit.

Director personal liability: The managing director and CFO who signed or knowingly relied on the fictitious invoices face criminal arrest, asset confiscation, and a personal travel ban on leaving China. On a conviction, they may be sentenced to a prison term; they are also barred from acting as a director or honorary manager in China (director disqualification), and the civil court will order restitution of corporate losses directly out of their personal bank accounts and shareholdings.
