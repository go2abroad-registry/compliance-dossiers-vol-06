Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform and the nationwide 12366 taxpayer service portal. The Hong Kong SAR shareholder is a separate taxpayer of the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department, but the subject WFOE is registered as a mainland China enterprise with the STA.

E-government filing portal

Upon issuance of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation, the WFOE obtains an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which automatically serves as its Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID). The WFOE must complete tax registration confirmation with the STA through the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of establishment. Procedure: (1) log in with the Unified Social Credit Code and legal representative identity verification; (2) submit the taxpayer profile, including accounting system, registered address, bank account and expected taxable scope; (3) confirm VAT classification (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) and file for corporate income tax, withholding tax and relevant local surtaxes; (4) complete the registration application and receive electronic confirmation; (5) apply for VAT invoice issuance entitlements, including special VAT invoice quotas or Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice activation. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that receives China-sourced income is required to separately register for a non-resident tax record at the competent STA branch solely for withholding tax administration; no VAT registration is triggered solely by shareholding in the WFOE.

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election is available under PRC tax law. The WFOE may elect VAT general taxpayer status (with input VAT credit entitlement) instead of small-scale taxpayer status when annual turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000, subject to STA approval; once turnover exceeds that threshold, general taxpayer status becomes mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China imposes no exemption threshold for VAT registration; every enterprise engaged in taxable sales must register with the STA. The RMB 5,000,000 annual turnover limit is the statutory demarcation between small-scale taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer status, not a registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods physically departing from PRC customs territory are eligible for VAT zero-rating when the WFOE holds general VAT taxpayer status, the goods are not restricted/prohibited exports, the export declaration is made under China Customs, the export proceeds are collected through a SAFE-registered bank and repatriated to China within the statutory deadline, and the export tax refund declaration matches all invoice, logistics and FX evidence. Qualifying offshore services consumed wholly outside China may also be zero-rated or exempt under the STA's published service schedule.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Bill of Exit) with approved release notation and product export codes
  • Bill of Lading (ocean) or Air Waybill (air) showing export from PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate for Export Proceeds (FIRC) issued by a SAFE-designated bank
  • Export contract or purchase order matched with commercial invoice and packing list
  • Certificate of origin or other valuation/classification documentation supporting the declared export value

Under the STA Golden Tax IV system and the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) mandate, VAT invoices must be issued through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau or approved e-invoicing service providers connected by sanctioned APIs. Invoice issuance data is transmitted to STA systems in real time; each invoice receives a single national invoice code and an anti-forgery digital stamp; the FDEI platform performs digital clearance before the invoice becomes legally usable. The STA automatically cross-references invoice, customs export, foreign exchange and bank data to detect mismatches in export rebate claims.

The STA audits export zero-rating/refund claims retroactively using Golden Tax IV cross-matching; failure to substantiate export physical movement, invoice authenticity, or foreign-exchange repatriation within the deadline results in denial of the zero rate, recovery of any input VAT refunded, late-payment interest, and administrative penalties of 50% to five times the tax involved under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export refund declarations, including fabricated customs declaration numbers or false FIRCs, may be referred to criminal tax enforcement, exposing the responsible individuals and entity to fines and imprisonment under PRC Criminal Law for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with partial convertibility. Current account transactions such as trade in goods and services, profit remittances, and ordinary operational payments are generally convertible and can be settled through designated foreign exchange banks upon submission of supporting documents and through the SAFE cross-border payment surveillance system. Capital account transactions, however, remain tightly controlled. Foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is permitted only after registration with MOFCOM and SAFE, and the resulting RMB converted from foreign currency capital can only be used for legitimate business purposes, not for equity investments or speculative activities. Profit repatriation by a WFOE requires annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax completion certificates, and a board resolution declaring dividends; the remittance is then processed under the current account after tax documentary checks. Export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China and booked either in a foreign currency current account or settled into RMB at the prevailing spot rate. Surrender requirements do not impose a fixed conversion percentage as of the current legal framework, but the receipts must be reported and verified under the goods trade foreign exchange monitoring system within the statutory verification period. Overall, the capital account remains non-convertible without case-by-case SAFE approval, making onshore RMB financing and fully hedged trade flows essential for treasury planning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in goods trade must be repatriated to a designated foreign exchange bank within the statutory verification period established by SAFE, generally within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration for ordinary open-account trade. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in its current account foreign exchange settlement account or convert it into RMB at the commercial bank's spot rate. Any intended overseas retention or extension of the repatriation timeline requires a separate SAFE approval or filing under the trade credit supervision framework. The bank will reconcile the receipt against customs declarations and the enterprise's trade credit classification before releasing the funds into the operational account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability for the WFOE treasury operating in mainland China. Under PRC banking and foreign exchange regulations, only designated foreign exchange banks are permitted to handle capital account transactions, export proceeds verification, and RMB conversion for a resident WFOE. Fintech EMI platforms and offshore digital banks are not licensed as domestic designated foreign exchange banks and cannot lawfully execute the mandatory SAFE filings, capital verification, and trade monitoring obligations. They may only be used for incidental cross-border e-commerce collection if the platform holds a PRC payment institution license, but they are not an acceptable substitute for the primary treasury clearing rail. All capital injections, disbursement of trade proceeds, and FX hedging must be channelled through tier-1 domestic commercial banks acting as the WFOE's designated settlement bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection must first complete the WFOE foreign investment registration with SAFE through the local branch, obtain a Foreign Investment Information Report and SAFE Foreign Investment Registration Form, and then open a verified RMB capital account at a designated domestic commercial bank. The Hong Kong parent remits the capital via SWIFT MT103 or MT203 using the correct cross-border inward remittance purpose code, typically 'capital contribution' or equivalent FDI code, citing the WFOE registration certificate and the investment agreement. After receipt, the bank performs capital verification, and a Chinese registered CPA issues the capital verification report. Funds converted into RMB must be kept in the capital settlement account and may only be used for the approved business scope such as purchase of manufacturing equipment, raw materials, payroll, and operating expenses. To route the funds, the WFOE should always reference the four-digit SAFE trade/payment purpose code corresponding to the transaction and retain the full SWIFT receipt, bank offer, and transaction confirmation throughout the clearing lifecycle.The WFOE should clear outbound trade disbursements primarily through SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for direct remittances, SWIFT MT700/MT707 commercial letters of credit for large export orders requiring bank payment undertakings, and documentary collections for higher-risk or lower-value trade flows. All outbound payments require a completed Application for Payment of Foreign Exchange, an invoice from the overseas supplier or beneficiary, the underlying trade contract, customs declaration evidence if goods have been shipped, and a pre-defined payment due date matching the approved foreign exchange current account classification. The designated commercial bank will debit the RMB operational account, convert at the spot rate or an agreed forward rate, and transmit the funds over SWIFT with the proper purpose of payment code. For large disbursements, the treasury team should use bank-secure channels, batch payment portals, and pre-agreed counterparty whitelisting to reduce AML friction and clearing delays.Onshore deliverable foreign exchange forwards with a genuine current-account or trade-account underlying exposure, sold and confirmed through a designated commercial bankOnshore foreign exchange swaps for short-term working capital and trade settlement liquidity matching between RMB and USD, EUR, or HKDCross-currency interest rate swaps exclusively for external debt or long-term capital borrowing that has been registered with SAFE, with interest and principal payment schedules linked to the underlying loan agreementOptions and non-deliverable forwards are not directly permissible for a mainland WFOE; such instruments may only be managed by the Hong Kong treasury vehicle outside the onshore designated bank framework

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability for the WFOE treasury operating in mainland China. Under PRC banking and foreign exchange regulations, only designated foreign exchange banks are permitted to handle capital account transactions, export proceeds verification, and RMB conversion for a resident WFOE. Fintech EMI platforms and offshore digital banks are not licensed as domestic designated foreign exchange banks and cannot lawfully execute the mandatory SAFE filings, capital verification, and trade monitoring obligations. They may only be used for incidental cross-border e-commerce collection if the platform holds a PRC payment institution license, but they are not an acceptable substitute for the primary treasury clearing rail. All capital injections, disbursement of trade proceeds, and FX hedging must be channelled through tier-1 domestic commercial banks acting as the WFOE's designated settlement bank.
