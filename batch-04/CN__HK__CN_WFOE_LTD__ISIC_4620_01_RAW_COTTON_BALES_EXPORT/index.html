Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a WFOE engaged in international brokerage of ginned raw cotton and synthetic yarns (ISIC 4620), a virtual office, commercial mailbox, or sham address used only for mail forwarding will not satisfy Article 10 of the PRC Company Law or the SAMR registered-address requirements. The State Taxation Administration, the General Administration of Customs, and commercial banks uniformly expect an actual physically leased office in the registration district, evidenced by a valid lease agreement, a landlord's property ownership certificate, fire-safety acceptance, and a visible company nameplate. In practice, banks reject virtual addresses for opening basic deposit and foreign-currency settlement accounts, and municipal tax bureaus frequently refuse tax registration where the physical premise cannot be verified. Certain free-trade zones allow clustered or shared registered addresses only for prescribed industry categories, and pure cross-border raw-cotton brokerage is not enumerated in most pilot zone negative lists; as a result, the Hong Kong parent must secure a genuine leased office in the intended WFOE registration city to maintain acceptable commercial substance and avoid adverse permanent-establishment and anti-tax-abuse findings under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. A WFOE engaged in wholesale brokerage and export trading of raw cotton is not subject to any statutory minimum paid-up capital or blocked escrow requirement. Under Article 47 of the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision), the total subscribed registered capital must be fully paid up by shareholders within five years from issuance of the business license unless a shorter term is required by law or administrative regulation. Contributions may be made in cash or permissible non-monetary property, must accord with the registered capital schedule in the Articles of Association, and must be remitted through a lawful foreign exchange capital account monitored by the State Administration of Foreign Exchange through a designated Chinese bank.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for office-only brokerage operations; no Civil Defense fire permit, structural load inspection, or workplace safety production license is required because the WFOE will not occupy an industrial or storage facility. If incidental sampling or temporary storage of raw cotton bales is introduced, the company must obtain a fire safety acceptance record from the local fire rescue authority, maintain fire protection facilities, and complete occupational safety registration under the Law of the People's Republic of China on Work Safety.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the unified national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform operated across provincial and municipal tax service offices.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor incorporating the WFOE in the PRC must first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Upon issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete automatic or online tax registration in the STA Electronic Tax Bureau. The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) shown on the business license is the single statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) for corporate income tax, value-added tax, and all other PRC national taxes; no separate VAT identification number is issued. The legal representative and appointed financial/tax agent must activate the e-Tax account, verify the taxpayer classification (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer), and file the initial tax information return. Non-resident entities without a PRC registered enterprise that earn Chinese-source income must obtain a non-resident tax identification registration through their withholding agent using the non-resident registration module of the e-Tax platform.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim zero-rated VAT on exported raw cotton bales when: (1) the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory through a customs-supervised port; (2) the export sale is made to a non-resident purchaser and legal title passes outside the PRC; (3) the WFOE is registered as a VAT general taxpayer; (4) export proceeds are held/collected through designated foreign exchange banks in accordance with SAFE rules; and (5) the complete statutory customs and foreign-exchange documentation package is retained. Export zero-rating entitles the WFOE to refund or offset the input VAT attributable to the exported goods under the PRC export VAT exemption/refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the PRC bank-issued export foreign-exchange collection confirmation
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract
  • VAT invoice or fully digital electronic invoice issued by the exporter

VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the STA-authorized electronic invoice platform. Invoice data is transmitted to the Golden Tax Phase IV central system in near real time. Fully digital electronic invoices are centrally cleared, carry a unique invoice serial number and anti-counterfeit QR code, and can be issued through tax authority APIs or approved ERP integrations. Paper invoices, where permitted, require tax-control stamping via certified fiscal devices. Export invoices are reported and matched against customs export declaration data.

The STA may audit export zero-rating claims retrospectively. If the required customs declaration, transport, invoicing, or foreign-exchange repatriation evidence is missing, false, or inconsistent, the refunded VAT is clawed back with statutory late payment interest. Administrative penalties may also be imposed under the PRC Tax Administration Law for fraudulent claims, and intentional falsification of export or foreign-exchange records may lead to criminal prosecution for export VAT refund fraud. Repeated compliance failures can trigger suspension of future export-rebate processing and enhanced tax-control monitoring.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The WFOE is domiciled in mainland China and is therefore subject to the PRC foreign exchange control framework, notwithstanding the Hong Kong jurisdiction tag in the composite key; Hong Kong itself, through the HKMA, does not impose exchange controls. The Renminbi is fully convertible for current account transactions, including export trade settlements for ginned raw cotton bales, provided the underlying trade is bona fide and fully documented. However, capital account items—such as equity injection from the Hong Kong shareholder, cross-border intra-group loans, and profit repatriation—remain governed by SAFE registration, approval, or filing procedures and cannot be executed freely through commercial banks without the relevant Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate and transaction-by-transaction underwriting. Export proceeds received in foreign currency must be repatriated into a licensed onshore foreign exchange settlement account; while there is no universal mandatory conversion to RMB, any portion of collected proceeds exceeding the SAFE-approved retention quota must be settled into RMB within the prescribed timeline. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent additionally requires audited annual financial statements, tax clearance certificates from the tax authority, customs export declaration records, SAFE current-account payment verification, and a clean anti-money laundering review. The ability to convert RMB back into hard currency for genuine trade is operationally high, but for capital or financial flows, strict pre-verification of real economic substance and underlying commercial documentation is compulsory, and any deviation from the approved purposes will trigger immediate regulatory rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from raw cotton bale sales must be repatriated into the WFOE's onshore foreign exchange settlement account within 90 calendar days from the date of export customs declaration, or within the contractual collection period if shorter. China abolished the historical mandatory surrender regime; the mandatory conversion ratio is 0% for balances that remain within the SAFE-approved foreign exchange settlement account quota, which for a newly incorporated WFOE is typically assessed by the bank at a reasonable projected turnover and for an established exporter is customarily set at 30% of the prior year's current account foreign exchange receipts. If the account balance exceeds the approved quota at the close of any business day, the excess amount must be converted into RMB within 10 business days, effectively imposing a 100% forced conversion on the excess only. All export collections must be matched with customs declaration data through the bank's trade foreign exchange verification system before any inflow is accepted; overdue receipts require a report to SAFE and documented evidence of a revised collection plan.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for the onshore WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or similar platforms do not hold a banking licence from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and are not authorised by SAFE to conduct foreign exchange settlement, current account reception, or capital account custody services for a mainland Chinese legal entity. The WFOE must rely exclusively on licensed domestic commercial banks for yuan current accounts, foreign exchange settlement accounts, and FDI capital escrow accounts, and all statutory filings under the tax, market regulation, and SAFE regimes must reflect this onshore bank relationship. The Hong Kong parent may use an offshore EMI only for incidental receivables outside China or for treasury-style payment concentration, but any attempt to route raw cotton export proceeds from a Chinese WFOE through an EMI or to use an EMI as the settlement channel for the WFOE's own account will be rejected by Chinese customs validation, SAFE monitoring, and the receiving bank's AML framework.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 directly into the WFOE's dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed Chinese bank. The wire instruction must carry the SAFE-approved foreign exchange purpose code for direct investment equity formation (for example, the SAFE 'direct investment' transaction code 622015 for establishment of a foreign-invested enterprise) and must reference the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate number. Upon receipt, the bank will verify the incoming amount against the registered equity investment schedule, generate a foreign exchange capital account statement, and supervise the settlement of the capital into RMB only for lawful operational expenditures after presentation of a Capital Project Use Payment Letter and supporting underlying documents. For any non-equity inbound loan from the Hong Kong parent, the WFOE must complete foreign debt registration and comply with the macro-prudential foreign debt ceiling, typically capped at 2.5 times the WFOE's audited net assets. All capital injections require a certified capital verification report to be submitted to the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and the SAFE verification system.Export collections for raw cotton bales will be settled through the WFOE's onshore bank using three primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for open account trade and international telegraphic transfer settlements; commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT707) for documentary credit-backed transactions with buyers in Hong Kong, the ASEAN region, or other export destinations; and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) for traditional commodity flows where the WFOE retains title control. All export receipts must be matched against the corresponding export customs declaration numbers in the national cross-border foreign exchange payment system. Outbound disbursements to upstream domestic cotton suppliers will be made in RMB through the China Payment and Clearing Network (CNAPS); payments to overseas suppliers and logistics providers will be routed via SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate SAFE trade purpose codes for goods import or international transportation, supported by contracts, invoices, and customs documents. For settlements with Hong Kong counterparties, the WFOE may also access the RMB clearing window through Bank of China (Hong Kong) or the HKD/CHATS real-time gross settlement rail, converting only the required balance to avoid multiple conversion costs.Real-demand RMB/USD forward contracts with licensed domestic banks for hedging export receivables, entered into only on the basis of underlying documentary trade exposure with no speculative benefits, and maturing no later than the settlement date of the underlying cotton sale.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD for the WFOE's FDI capital injection or foreign debt servicing, permitted only with signed underlying capital flow documentation and SAFE-formulated macro-prudential registration.Plain vanilla RMB/USD options (call and put) for defined trade or capital account flows, subject to written bank board approved product terms and SAFE underlying transaction verification; onshore NDFs are prohibited for the WFOE because the RMB is deliverable in the onshore market, while the Hong Kong parent may engage in offshore NDF contracts strictly on its own books as a separate multi-currency treasury vehicle.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for the onshore WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, Currencycloud, or similar platforms do not hold a banking licence from the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) and are not authorised by SAFE to conduct foreign exchange settlement, current account reception, or capital account custody services for a mainland Chinese legal entity. The WFOE must rely exclusively on licensed domestic commercial banks for yuan current accounts, foreign exchange settlement accounts, and FDI capital escrow accounts, and all statutory filings under the tax, market regulation, and SAFE regimes must reflect this onshore bank relationship. The Hong Kong parent may use an offshore EMI only for incidental receivables outside China or for treasury-style payment concentration, but any attempt to route raw cotton export proceeds from a Chinese WFOE through an EMI or to use an EMI as the settlement channel for the WFOE's own account will be rejected by Chinese customs validation, SAFE monitoring, and the receiving bank's AML framework.
