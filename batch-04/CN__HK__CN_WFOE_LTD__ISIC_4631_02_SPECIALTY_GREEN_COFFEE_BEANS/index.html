Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through provincial and municipal tax offices; online electronic filing is performed on the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal, with the golden-tax control system supporting VAT invoice issuance and data monitoring.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and, within 30 days of issuance of the business license, must complete tax registration with the competent local STA office. The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the business license is the statutory TIN and VAT taxpayer identification number. Registration is submitted through the e-Tax portal using: the SAMR business license, articles of association, lease/registered address proof, bank account information, legal representative and senior manager identity documents, and investor ownership details. The STA then validates taxpayer status, assigns the WFOE to the VAT small-scale or general taxpayer category, and activates the payroll and social contribution reporting identities. A non-resident foreign shareholder not holding a Chinese TIN must, if it derives China-source income or should enter into contracts with the WFOE, separately complete non-resident enterprise withholding registration on the same e-Tax portal before the first payment is made.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law contains no check-the-box, partnership pass-through, or hybrid mismatch entity election. A WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise liable to 25% enterprise income tax on worldwide income; the only optional VAT classification is the election to register as a VAT general taxpayer despite annual sales below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, which is not a hybrid mismatch election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

VAT registration is mandatory from the date of business registration; there is no exemption threshold for a WFOE with a permanent establishment and taxable supply capacity. An annual taxable sales volume exceeding RMB 5,000,000 obliges the taxpayer to register as a VAT general taxpayer; taxpayers below RMB 5,000,000 may remain small-scale taxpayers or voluntarily elect general taxpayer status. Green coffee beans classified as primary agricultural products are taxed at 9% VAT on domestic supply and importation, while the standard VAT headline rate for other goods is 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export zero-rating for goods applies only when the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory and ownership passes to a genuine overseas buyer. The exporter must hold a valid export customs declaration, an actual shipping document, and a bank record evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds into China. Qualifying cross-border services listed in the STA export-zero-rating circulars may also be zero-rated, but specialty green coffee beans are goods; accordingly, the inbound green coffee import model does not create an export zero-rating claim unless the goods are first entered and later legally re-exported in accordance with customs supervision requirements.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration (Customs Export Declaration Form) with the correct HS commodity code for green coffee beans
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Bill of Lading for ocean containerized green coffee or Air Waybill for air cargo
  • VAT special invoice or commercial export invoice generated through the tax-control system
  • SAFE-authorized bank foreign exchange inward remittance receipt (the Chinese statutory equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate)
  • Destination-country import documentation, certificate of origin, or signed delivery proof evidencing physical arrival outside China

All VAT special invoices must be issued through STA-approved tax-control equipment or through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Invoice issuance data is transmitted to the golden-tax system in near real time for validation; each invoice receives a tax-control digital stamp and a unique invoice code. The fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) pilot requires issuers to interconnect through the official invoicing interfaces to obtain a fiscal number before the invoice is valid for input VAT credit. Reversals, offset invoices, and red-letter invoices must be filed electronically and reported to the STA before they become effective; the national invoice verification platform is used for anti-fraud audit.

Customs and STA jointly compare the customs export declaration data, bank foreign exchange receipts, and e-invoice records. If the export transaction is not substantiated or the proceeds are not repatriated within the applicable deadline, the claimed zero-rating is disallowed, the refunded input VAT is clawed back with the statutory daily late-payment surcharge, and an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax attributable to the false export may be imposed. In fraud or smuggling cases, the matter is referred to customs or public security authorities for criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

As a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) established in mainland China with a Hong Kong parent, the entity operates under the People's Republic of China's (PRC) current account convertibility regime while facing stringent capital account controls. Trade-related foreign exchange transactions, including imports of specialty green coffee beans, are fully convertible on the current account, subject to SAFE registration, documentation, and bank verification. The RMB is convertible for trade settlement without prior approval, but capital account items such as equity injections, loans from foreign shareholders, and repatriation of dividends require SAFE filing or approval, dedicated foreign exchange capital accounts, and supporting documentation. Export proceeds are generally not subject to mandatory surrender to the central bank; however, all foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within the prescribed trade verification period. Profit remittance hurdles include the requirement that dividends may only be remitted after the annual statutory audit confirms distributable profits, post-corporate income tax, and after the board of directors approves the distribution; the remittance also requires a SAFE-approved foreign exchange settlement for dividend payments and a withholding tax clearance certificate. Capital in-flows from the Hong Kong shareholder must be injected into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account opened with a licensed domestic bank, followed by capital verification by a PRC CPA firm and conversion into RMB for operational use. Overall, the regime is liberalized for trade but still requires careful regulatory mapping for cross-border capital movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under prevailing SAFE regulations, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio to RMB; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in its settlement account or convert it to RMB at a bank counter. However, each incoming remittance must be matched with the customs declaration via the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system, and any discrepancy or overdue receipt will trigger compliance queries. For the sale of green coffee into the domestic market, export proceeds are not expected unless re-exported; nonetheless, the same 180-day repatriation and verification obligation applies to any future export receipts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut, Wise, and Airwallex are not licensed to provide local banking or foreign exchange accounts to a mainland China WFOE. Under PRC banking law, every CNY-denominated account and foreign exchange capital account must be held with a bank licensed by the PBOC and the National Financial Regulatory Administration. These EMIs cannot fulfil SAFE registration, capital verification, or RMB settlement requirements. The Hong Kong parent may use an offshore EMI for treasury purposes, but the WFOE's statutory accounts, capital injection, and trade settlement flows must be routed through a domestic Tier-1 bank. The cross-border banking blueprint therefore segregates: the Hong Kong parent's cash management via licensed HK banks and the mainland WFOE's regulatory accounts via PRC domestic banks.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut, Wise, and Airwallex are not licensed to provide local banking or foreign exchange accounts to a mainland China WFOE. These EMIs cannot fulfil SAFE registration, capital verification, or RMB settlement requirements. The Hong Kong parent may use an offshore EMI for treasury purposes, but the WFOE's statutory accounts, capital injection, and trade settlement flows must be routed through a domestic Tier-1 bank. The cross-border banking blueprint therefore segregates: the Hong Kong parent's cash management via licensed HK banks and the mainland WFOE's regulatory accounts via PRC domestic banks.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

The statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise is 10%. Under the Mainland and Hong Kong Compact (DTA), the rate is reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the capital of the Chinese enterprise paying the dividends and satisfying the beneficial ownership/substance test.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible-asset transfers (e.g., coffee bean inventory) exceed RMB 200 million, or intangible-asset, financial-asset and service-related transactions exceed RMB 100 million. Master File may be required where the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion or group-wide cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million. These thresholds are set out in SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016; the WFOE must also file the annual Related Party Transactions Report (Form G16) regardless of size if connected transactions exist.

Economic substance scope: Under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law's General Anti-Avoidance Rule (Article 120 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures) and OECD BEPS principles, tax treatment follows economic substance rather than legal form. The WFOE must have viable core income-generating activities (CIGA) in China, such as physical inventory risk, procurement contracts, logistics management, sales staff, and management decisions. The Hong Kong parent must likewise demonstrate real business substance in Hong Kong; paper entities inserted solely to access the 5% dividend rate and 7% interest/royalty rates will be denied treaty benefits and may be re-characterized as Chinese residents or ordinary shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Thin Capitalization and Excessive Interest Deduction

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 46 and Caishui [2008] No. 121: the excess interest can neither be deducted nor carried forward; it is recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding tax (or 5% if treaty benefit is properly claimed). The unpaid tax attracts a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge, and under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, underreporting penalty can reach 50%-500% of the tax under-assessed.

Director personal liability: The tax authority can pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 where the shareholder or director abuses the company's independent personality to evade taxes and damage creditors. Directors and the legal representative may be personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties, subject to exit-restraint/blacklisting orders, travel bans, and disqualification as directors. If the arrangement is proven as intentional tax evasion, responsible persons face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to three years for tax underpayment exceeding prescribed thresholds and three to seven years for huge amounts.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Documentation Default

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 77, SAT Announcement 42/2016, and Special Tax Adjustment measures authorize re-computation at arm's length; a 5% interest rate add-on over benchmark lending rate may be imposed on under-taxed adjustments. Failure to keep or supply related-party documentation may lead to penalty of RMB 10,000-50,000 and the tax authority's use of its own estimates. In fraud cases, Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law adds 50%-500% penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany contracts or direct falsified invoices expose themselves to personal civil restitution, administrative blacklisting (social credit when the entity is listed as 'seriously untrustworthy tax payer'), travel and exit bans, and criminal liability for compliance evasion. Under the PRC Criminal Law, the person directly in charge may be jointly fined and imprisoned for up to seven years where tax evasion is massive; corporate veil-piercing applies where the HK parent uses shell entities to shift profit.
Shell Company Treaty Shopping and False Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: China's GAAR (CIT Law Article 120) and the special anti-treaty-abuse rules permit the tax authority to deny the Hong Kong company treaty benefits, recharacterize the payment as a 10% domestic WHT dividend, impose the 50%-500% penalty for fraudulent under-withholding, and levy interest. Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance Sections 61, 61A and 61B allow the IRD to counteract tax avoidance by the HK entity and void the shell claim. PRC anti-money laundering laws add fines to institutions and responsible staff for false UBO filings.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve dividend distributions to a conduit HK parent, sign false residence declarations, or misrepresent UBOs on SWIFT/LC documentation can be held liable in restitution for the withheld tax, face travel bans and asset freezes issued by tax authorities, and be barred from leaving China under security measures. Under PRC Company Law Article 20, the corporate veil is pierced when the shell company and director use the WFOE for illicit purposes; criminal imprisonment applies for tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 201, and for money laundering under Article 191 if the diverted funds are concealed, with maximum sentences of ten years or more in aggravated cases.
