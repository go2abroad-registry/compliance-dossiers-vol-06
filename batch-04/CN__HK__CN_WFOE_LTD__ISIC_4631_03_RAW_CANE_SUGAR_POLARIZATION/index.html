Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE, as a general VAT taxpayer, may apply the PRC statutory zero rate for exported physical goods when the sale is a genuine cross-border trade transaction; the raw cane sugar is actually cleared by China Customs and leaves the customs-supervised port or bonded logistics zone; the transaction is contracted with an overseas buyer (including a Hong Kong counterparty); and export proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign-exchange bank in the ordinary course. The zero rate is implemented by the exempt and refund method for trading businesses, meaning the export sale is exempt from output VAT and the WFOE may claim a refund for the input VAT on the purchased sugar evidenced by VAT special invoices, provided the export rebate rate applicable to the commodity and all required documents are matched in the Customs-Foreign Exchange-Tax data environment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a partial and asymmetric convertibility regime: current account trade and services are convertible on a genuine transaction basis, while capital account flows remain tightly administered by SAFE/PBOC. For this WFOE, the Hong Kong parent's equity injection is capital account defined; it must first complete SAFE FDI registration, open a dedicated capital account at an approved domestic bank, and receive the injection through compliant MT103/MT202 channel. Once settled, the proceeds enter RMB and can be used for wages, rent, and commodity prepayments within the approved business scope. Export proceeds in USD or other currencies must be repatriated into China through designated banks and may be retained in foreign exchange or converted voluntarily; there is no mandatory surrender ratio. Profit remittance is not prohibited but requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate or dividend withholding tax under the Mainland-Hong Kong tax arrangement, and bank compliance review. Practical hurdles include bank-level SAFE verification, strict invoice-vs-customs reconciliation, vessel/commodity documentation in sugar trades, use of correct purpose codes, and deadline discipline in cross-border clearing through CIPS or SWIFT.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account administration, export proceeds from the WFOE's sugar sales must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of the customs export declaration unless SAFE or the designated bank approves a documented extension. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero: the WFOE may retain up to 100% of export receipts in the foreign currency of invoice or voluntarily convert into RMB at the prevailing market price. The bank must reconcile the FX receipt amount against the export customs declaration in the Trade Monitoring System, and the WFOE must retain the original sales contract, invoice, BL, and survey report for 5 years.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong clearing window only

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The local-currency threshold for mandatory contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation is: (i) Local File must be prepared when the aggregate annual related-party transactions of the PRC taxpayer exceed RMB 40 million or when any single category exceeds RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for intangible property, RMB 100 million for financial transactions, or RMB 40 million for related-party services. (ii) Master File must be prepared if the global ultimate parent's consolidated annual revenue for the preceding year exceeded RMB 2 billion and the Chinese taxpayer engages in controlled transactions. A related-party transaction declaration (Schedule A100000) must be filed regardless of threshold.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not operate a Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations regime, but economic substance is a core requirement in the treaty and anti-abuse context. The Hong Kong shareholder must satisfy the beneficial ownership test under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the OECD Principal Purpose Test (PPT) by performing Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong: board decision-making, contract negotiation, freight and vessel logistics management, bank account oversight, and risk control over commodity positions. The WFOE itself must demonstrate substantive operational capacity: office premises, employed trade settlement staff, independent contract execution and timely tax filings. Absent these, the arrangement may be re-characterized as a shell structure under CIT Law Article 47 General Anti-Avoidance Rule, leading to treaty relief denial, WHT adjustment, transfer pricing adjustments and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Underreporting or Fictitious Invoices

Statutory sanction: Falsely making out special invoices violates PRC Criminal Law Article 205 and the Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, triggering tax re-assessment, disallowance of deducted costs, a penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and criminal confiscation. Input VAT credits from false invoices are denied. Criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 is punishable by up to 7 years' imprisonment plus unlimited fines for large amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false vouchers or arrange fake invoices face corporate veil piercing under PRC Company Law Article 20, becoming jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax, penalties and civil damages. Personal assets may be frozen and applied to restitution. The director is subject to an exit restriction or travel ban under Exit-Entry Administration Law Article 12 while listed on the tax arrears blacklist. Upon criminal conviction under Criminal Law Article 201, the director faces 3-7 years' imprisonment, a criminal fine, and disqualification from directorship.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Articles 41, 44, 46 and 47 and SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42, the tax authority may make an arm's length adjustment, re-document margins, disallow excess interest under thin-capitalization rules (Implementation Regulations Article 119), impose a 50%-500% penalty on the underpaid tax if the adjustment is intentional, and apply backcharged interest at the benchmark lending rate plus additional points. Failure to provide contemporaneous documents triggers special tax risk assessment; egregious abuse may allow a 10-year lookback.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed fictitious intergroup pricing or round-tripped cash flows are personally jointly liable for the adjusted tax deficit if the court finds the transactions were sham arrangements. Excess interest loans to the company may be re-characterized as hidden dividends, creating personal income tax exposure. A director of the Hong Kong controlling entity may face liability in mainland courts if extradition is avoided under the dual criminality principle; the director can be subject to an exit ban, asset confiscation and blacklisting with the State Administration for Market Regulation.
Treaty Shopping, Shell Holding and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Articles 3 and 37 and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, the PRC tax authority denies DTA relief, restoring the 10% statutory withholding rate on dividends, interest and royalties, and may apply a general anti-avoidance adjustment under Article 47. False treaty-benefit declarations constitute fraud under TCAL Article 63 and Criminal Law Article 201 if evaded tax exceeds the criminal threshold. The authority can issue a DTA denial order and reassess income for both the WFOE and the parent group.

Director personal liability: Directors of the PRC WFOE and the Hong Kong conduit who sign the treaty-benefit declarations become personally liable: civil restitution of under-withheld tax from personal assets, travel bans on tax authorities' blacklists, and frozen bank accounts. In cross-border fraud cases, directors may be prosecuted under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 225, facing 3-10 years' imprisonment, monetary fines, and corporate veil piercing because the conduit has no economic substance. Directors are also barred from holding officer positions in both jurisdictions after conviction.
