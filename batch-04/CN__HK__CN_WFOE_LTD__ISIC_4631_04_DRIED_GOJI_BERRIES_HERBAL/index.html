Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None for an ordinary WFOE under the PRC Company Law 2023. The articles of association must state a subscribed registered capital, and all subscribed capital must be fully paid in within five years from the date of establishment unless the articles adopt a shorter period. The Hong Kong shareholder must open a domestic capital account or foreign currency capital account through a designated bank and remit contributions in accordance with the foreign investment information reporting rules. No blocked escrow account is required for the wholesale and export of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals, as this activity is not on the foreign investment negative list. The bank, customs, and export food registration procedures may require evidence of paid-in capital and working capital sufficient to support the committed export volume.

Funding & unlock path: The office and warehouse must obtain a fire safety filing or acceptance certificate from the local fire rescue institution where required under the Fire Control Law and building fire protection regulations. Occupational clearance under the Work Safety Law requires a workplace risk assessment, fire extinguishers, marked emergency exits, emergency evacuation plan, and employee safety training. If racking storage is installed, a registered engineer must certify the structural load capacity of the floor, racks, and shelving.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: China Electronic Taxpayer / Electronic Tax Bureau of State Taxation Administration (https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign investor incorporates the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining a Business License that automatically carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). This USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN/VAT ID) for the WFOE; no separate TIN application is required. Step 2: Under the multi-department joint registration mechanism, SAMR synchronizes registration data in real time to the local tax bureau of the STA, completing basic tax registration. Step 3: Within 30 days of the Business License date, the WFOE must file supplementary taxpayer information through the Electronic Taxpayer Portal, including legal representative, accounting system and software, authorized personnel, bank account reports, and expected VAT taxpayer status (general or small-scale). Step 4: The WFOE must complete the Financial and Accounting System Registration Filing and Bank Deposit Account Filing through the same portal. Step 5: For a foreign non-resident entity with no Chinese establishment but with China-source taxable income, the foreign entity must separately file a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration/Filing with the competent STA branch before payment remittance; the withholding agent files the Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Withholding Return and the foreign entity obtains a local non-resident taxpayer reference number. Step 6: A VAT general taxpayer must implement the digital e-invoice system and obtain the electronic tax seal certificate through the STA platform.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election for corporate entities. An eligible WFOE may elect to register as a VAT general taxpayer before exceeding the CNY 5,000,000 threshold or may remain a small-scale taxpayer; it may also elect applicable double tax treaty benefits for non-resident withholding rates, but no entity classification election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No economic turnover threshold for basic tax registration; every WFOE must register for tax upon Business License issuance. The mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration threshold is annual taxable sales exceeding CNY 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million); below this threshold the entity may be registered as a small-scale taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals by the WFOE are eligible for VAT zero-rating (export exemption and refund) provided the goods physically leave the customs territory of the PRC, are declared to customs under export procedure, the WFOE holds valid taxpayer qualification, the export sales are evidenced by a VAT export invoice, the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign-exchange bank, and the exporter passes customs verification of the export declaration, including volume, HS code, valuation, and destination. The buyer must be located outside the PRC customs territory; where the buyer is a related party, transfer pricing documentation must support arm's-length pricing.

  • PRC Customs Export Goods Declaration processed through the China International Trade Single Window with a Customs Declaration Number
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing actual export shipment
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the WFOE to the offshore buyer
  • Packing List corresponding to the Commercial Invoice
  • Inward Foreign Exchange Settlement/Receipt Voucher issued by a designated foreign-exchange bank showing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Export Tax Refund Declaration Data filed via the STA Electronic Taxpayer Portal and matched electronically with Customs and banking data

China's Golden Tax Phase IV and Digital Electronic Invoice mandate require all VAT taxpayers to issue invoices through the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform using real-time fiscal digitalization. Fully digital e-invoices are cleared by the STA in real time; each invoice receives a unique anti-fraud electronic invoice number and is digitally stamped through the integrated tax-control infrastructure. XML/OFD data must be transmitted automatically at issuance; offline or hand-written VAT invoices are not permitted for B2B export transactions. API-based integration is required for high-volume exporters, and all invoice data is centrally captured for export tax-refund matching.

Export VAT refunds are scrutinized through the STA-Customs-People's Bank data reconciliation system. If zero-rating is granted and later found unverified or fraudulent, the tax authority recovers the refunded amount, collects late-payment surcharge, and imposes penalties under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levies. For fraudulent export-refund claims, the fine ranges from one to five times the refund amount obtained; for general underpayment or unverified claims, a fine of 50% to five times the amount involved may apply. The exporter may also be placed on a tax compliance blacklist, have export tax-refund qualification revoked, and be referred for criminal prosecution for tax fraud. Retrospective clawback is not time-limited for fraud; for ordinary unverified claims the assessment period is generally three years, extending to five years where concealment is found.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains a bifurcated current/capital account convertibility model. Current account transactions connected to the WFOE's production and trade of dried goji berries and herbal botanicals enjoy very high convertibility once the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and logistics documentation are authenticated by the designated custodian domestic bank. Capital account transactions are subject to strict registration, use and repatriation limitations. Inbound FDI equity contributions must pass through SAFE's registration system, be deposited into a specially designated foreign currency capital account, and may be settled into RMB only after bank-level authenticity screening and the issuance of an FDI capital verification report. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are permissible only after statutory audit, payment of enterprise income tax, and submission of board resolutions, financial statements, tax payment certificates, and audited profit appropriation evidence; banks must verify no historical capital account violation, unpaid foreign debt, or improperly grandfathered settlement. Non-trade current payments, service fees, and royalties may be challenged without economic substance evidence. Overall the RMB remains not freely convertible on the capital account, so all treasury plans must build in regulatory lead time.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from dried goji berry and herbal wholesale transactions must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic foreign exchange account through the designated bank rather than held offshore. The WFOE is not required to convert export receipts into RMB at a forced ratio; under current-account convertibility the enterprise may retain foreign currency in a settlement account or voluntarily convert at spot rate. The obligatory mechanics are administrative: bank-side collection verification, China Customs and SAFE data matching, and generation of trade settlement and payment records in the unified trade single window. Receipts must be collected within the payment term stated in the sales contract and customs declaration; overdue receipts beyond the bank's risk threshold, normally 90 days or earlier if exceptional false-export indicators are detected, require a signed explanation, amended invoice, collection order, and proof of foreign buyer insolvency or dispute. Any unrepatriated export revenues are flagged as suspected round-tripping or transfer pricing, triggering trade verification audits.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, functioning under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the consolidated foreign exchange regulatory and registration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Non-viable for the PRC operative entity. Offshore EMIs, including licensed Hong Kong stored-value facilities, are not recognized as domestic deposit-taking institutions by PBOC or SAFE, and therefore cannot host the WFOE's basic RMB account, receive FDI capital, process trade settlement verification, or execute profit remittance. The Cross-Border RMB clearing and CIPS participation are accessed only through licensed domestic banks. EMIs cannot satisfy the physical presence, corporate seal, or facial biometric identity requirements, and they would cause the WFOE to fail bank status verification and liquidity traceability. Use EMIs solely for non-CNY invoice data or HK parent side treasury syntax, never as a regulated onshore payment rail.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and fraudulent export revenue understatement

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63: recovery of unpaid tax plus daily late-payment surcharge and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; Criminal Law Article 201: tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and constituting more than 10% of the total tax due is punishable by criminal detention or up to three years imprisonment, and for especially large amounts with a proportion above 30%, seven years imprisonment plus fines.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 20 and the judicial doctrine of piercing the corporate veil, directors, shareholders, or actual controllers who abuse the limited liability form to evade CIT and WHT may be held jointly and severally liable to make good the tax loss; the legal representative and directly responsible directors may be placed on the tax exit blacklist and prohibited from leaving China under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 44; criminal prosecution may be imposed on the supervisory and management personnel directly responsible for the fraudulent invoicing and profit shifting.
Transfer pricing manipulation and deficient contemporaneous documentation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 48 and the regulations under State Taxation Administration Announcement No. 42 of 2016: the tax authority may re-price the controlled transactions, adjust taxable income, impose special-tax-adjustment interest, and levy a penalty of 50% to 500% of the adjusted tax under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 44 or 63; a special penalty may also be imposed for failing to provide contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement a transfer pricing policy with no arm's length basis and who sign or approve false intercompany agreements may be jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax and interest; the legal representative may be subjected to exit restrictions and inclusion in the serious tax violation blacklist; in cases where the TP scheme is a sham to hide taxable profits, criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may attach to the officers who arranged the artificial pricing.
Shell company and treaty shopping fraud in Dividend Repatriation

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 GAAR, SAT anti-treaty-shopping measures, and the DTA beneficial ownership rules: the tax authority may disregard the Hong Kong company as a conduit, deny the DTA claim, re-impose the full 10% dividend WHT, charge late-payment surcharge and special-tax-adjustment interest, and impose a 50% to 500% penalty under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 for false declarations or withholding failure.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false residency and beneficial ownership certificates may be personally liable for restitution of the suppressed withholding tax; their assets may be attached if the WFOE is used as their alter ego; they face exit bans and blacklisting, and if the scheme is proven as intentional tax evasion with public prosecution, responsible officers can be sentenced to imprisonment under Criminal Law Article 201, in addition to fines and compensation for the tax loss.
