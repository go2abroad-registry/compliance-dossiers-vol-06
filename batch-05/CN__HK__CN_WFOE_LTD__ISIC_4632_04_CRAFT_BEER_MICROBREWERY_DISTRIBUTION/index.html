Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer may apply 0% VAT on exports where the goods physically leave the customs territory of the People's Republic of China; the export declaration must name the WFOE as exporter; the buyer must be a non-resident enterprise or a Hong Kong/Macau SAR counterparty; and the full consideration must be received and repatriated through a licensed bank in China. Deliveries routed through Hong Kong as a free port qualify as goods export zero-rating for mainland VAT purposes when the China Customs export file and title transfer evidence are complete. Offshore services qualify only if the recipient is outside China and the service is performed outside China, which is not the primary activity of this ISIC 4632 cold-chain distribution scope.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (export clearance declaration)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill showing the consignee outside mainland China and cold-chain transport details
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List matching the export declaration value, weight, and goods description
  • Bank Export Proceeds Collection Certificate or foreign exchange receipt record evidencing actual remittance from the non-resident buyer
  • Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice set and input VAT voucher set used to calculate the export VAT refund and zero-rating claim

The WFOE must issue invoices through the State Taxation Administration Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice platform. Invoice data is transmitted in real time via certified API integration to the digital tax administration system under the Golden Tax Phase IV regime. Each electronic invoice is allocated a unique tax number and carries an encrypted anti-fraud stamp; no paper-only invoice can substantiate export zero-rating unless supported by fully digital electronic records and export tax refund data submitted through the electronic tax administration export VAT refund module.

The State Taxation Administration, China Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly scrutinize export refund files. Any false export declaration, forged customs or freight documentation, overstated invoice amount, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 180-day window disqualified the zero-rating claim; the VAT refund must be clawed back with interest and late fees; a tax penalty of up to five times the refund amount may be imposed under the Tax Administration Law; and serious cases carry criminal liability for export tax fraud and smuggling under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a dual-tier convertibility framework governed by the Foreign Exchange Control Regulations (1996 as amended) and the SAFE Circular on Promoting and Facilitating Cross-Border Trade and Investment (known as the Comprehensive Foreign Exchange Reform). The RMB is fully convertible on current account items, including trade in goods and services, allowing WFOE treasury operations to settle legitimate import payments for craft beer distribution without requiring SAFE pre-approval provided that genuine underlying transaction documents are retained. However, capital account convertibility remains strictly controlled: inbound equity injections, shareholder loans, cross-border debt financing, and profit remittances must be filed with SAFE through the FDIS (Foreign Direct Investment System), registered with the relevant financial institution, and matched against the verified capital base. Export proceeds from the Hong Kong distribution channel are subject to a mandatory receipt verification process under SAFE's export proceeds monitoring system; proceeds must generally be repatriated and settled into the WFOE offshore receipts account within the statutory period or reflected in a valid export proceeds retention ledger. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong intermediate holding company is permissible only after the annual statutory audit has been completed, corporate income tax has been fully settled, and the bank has verified prior years' undistributed profits through the SAFE-generated Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate. The key hurdle is the genuine transaction examination: any mismatched invoice, logistics trace, or customs declaration can trigger a Suspicious Transaction Report and freeze the clearing rail, so a robust documentary dossier is mandatory.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 2019/28 and the trade facilitation reforms, the mandatory repatriation requirement for export proceeds in physical craft beer shipments remains in force: registered export proceeds must be received into the domestic bank account within 90 days from the date of customs export clearance unless a prior SAFE-approved extension is granted for bona fide open-account trade terms. There is currently no fixed mandatory percentage surrender ratio for current account receipts under the post-2012 liberalization regime; however, 100% of export proceeds must flow through the SAFE-monitored current account collection system, and any retention of foreign currency outside China must be supported by a recognized multi-national treasury netting agreement or an offshore collection account approval from SAFE. For a China WFOE that is the manufacturing parent to a Hong Kong distribution affiliate, the prudent policy is to repatriate 100% of proceeds within the statutory window, maintain the export verification form (J-H2 form for physical goods), and reconcile the money, goods, and invoice flows to avoid triggering Article 45 Penalties for fraudulent cross-border arbitrage.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) via the SAFE Central Huijin system for foreign exchange authorization, with supervision from China's Ministry of Commerce for FDI capital account processing.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI banks such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Windmaster will not receive regulatory approval to act as the primary corporate operating account for the China WFOE. Under China's foreign exchange control system, only SAFE-accredited domestic banks can accept export proceeds, process the required trade verification, and execute the capital account conversion for inbound FDI equity injections. EMIs can be legitimately deployed at the Hong Kong treasury company level for the outsourced collection of overseas craft beer distribution sale proceeds and for vendor payments in HKD/USD outside mainland control, but these rails cannot be used for mainland WFOE's export proceeds because the cross-border linkage would be deemed illegal foreign exchange arbitrage, potentially triggering a fraud investigation and loss of the foreign trade qualification. The rational architecture is therefore a split-treasury model: domestic Tier-1 banks own the compliance-heavy capital and operational rails in mainland China, while the Hong Kong intermediate entity may use a licensed EMI for speed in non-regulated, non-onshore collections; all flows back to the WFOE must route via the bank channel and be properly documented as intercompany trade settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound injection from the Hong Kong parent must initiate via an MT103 wire from Bank of China (Hong Kong) denominated in USD or RMB with the remittance purpose code 622 (equity injection) or 621 (advance to foreign-invested enterprise) and the full WFOE name in the beneficiary line. The receiving domestic bank will first verify the WFOE SAFE registration and the Foreign Exchange Business Registration Certificate; then credit the funds into the Capital Escrow Account (FDI account) opened at the domestic bank. Within five business days, funds may be settled into CNY for operational use once the bank confirms the capital verification report from a licensed certified public accountant that matches the actual ledger, the shareholder resolution approving the equity subscription, and SAFE's capital account information system printout. A capital account settlement instruction must contain SAFE Form FIA-02 or the bank's standardized capital settlement application; any subsequent movement from the operational account toward supplier payments must be paid directly from the settlement bank rather than transferred to a third-party aggregated account.For trade disbursements to suppliers and the Hong Kong craft beer distributor, the primary clearing rail is a standard SWIFT MT103 message routed through the domestic Tier-1 bank under the trade in goods code 121010, with the full underlying invoice number and customs declaration ID monopolized into field 70. Documentary Collections (DP/DA) may be used for repetitive cold-chain keg exports to HK, where the domestic bank acts as remitting bank through SWIFT MT400 series and requires an original Bill of Lading endorsed to order, commercial invoice, packing list, health certificate, and certificate of origin as conforming documents. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI banks such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Windmaster will not receive regulatory approval to act as the primary corporate operating account for the China WFOE. Under China's foreign exchange control system, only SAFE-accredited domestic banks can accept export proceeds, process the required trade verification, and execute the capital account conversion for inbound FDI equity injections. EMIs can be legitimately deployed at the Hong Kong treasury company level for the outsourced collection of overseas craft beer distribution sale proceeds and for vendor payments in HKD/USD outside mainland control, but these rails cannot be used for mainland WFOE's export proceeds because the cross-border linkage would be deemed illegal foreign exchange arbitrage, potentially triggering a fraud investigation and loss of the foreign trade qualification. The rational architecture is therefore a split-treasury model: domestic Tier-1 banks own the compliance-heavy capital and operational rails in mainland China, while the Hong Kong intermediate entity may use a licensed EMI for speed in non-regulated, non-onshore collections; all flows back to the WFOE must route via the bank channel and be properly documented as intercompany trade settlement.
