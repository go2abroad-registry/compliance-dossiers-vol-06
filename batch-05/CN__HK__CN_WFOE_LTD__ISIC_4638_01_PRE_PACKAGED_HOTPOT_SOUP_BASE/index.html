Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital for a wholesale-trading WFOE and no escrow or blocked-account requirement at incorporation. Under PRC Company Law (2023), Article 47, the registered capital is subscribed under the Articles of Association and must be fully paid in within five years from the date of issuance of the business license, unless laws, administrative regulations, or the Articles prescribe a shorter or special period. For this WFOE, the sole shareholder may set registered capital at a level reasonable for the scale of pre-packaged food wholesale, for example RMB 500,000 to 1,000,000, and must remit the called capital from the Hong Kong investor's bank account into the WFOE's local RMB bank account when the board lawfully calls it. Capital verification reports are not mandatory for ordinary WFOEs absent industry-specific requirements; however, the annual report submitted through the enterprise information publicity system must accurately reflect paid-in capital.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety clearance: the building must hold a valid fire acceptance (construction completion fire inspection) certificate, and the company must file a self-inspection fire safety report with the Fire and Rescue Brigade if the warehouse floor area exceeds 300 square meters or if the occupancy is classified as logistics or storage use. The company must install and maintain ABC dry-powder fire extinguishers, illuminated exit signs and emergency lighting, an internal fire alarm if the building code demands it, and exit aisles with a clear width of at least 1.2 meters. Occupational clearance: the employer must conduct a workplace risk assessment, register in the occupational disease surveillance system of the local health commission, provide annual physical examinations for food-handling personnel under the Food Safety Law, and, for racking higher than 3 meters, obtain a structural engineer's certificate of floor-loading capacity and rack stability. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and compliance through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, which also integrates with the China International Trade Single Window for customs and export rebate processing.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) at incorporation, and this USCC automatically constitutes its Tax Identification Number (TIN). Within 30 calendar days after the issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA branch via the e-Tax platform or in person at the tax service hall. The registration procedure includes: (1) activating the e-Tax account using the USCC; (2) filing the financial institution deposit account details, accounting software information, and legal representative identification; (3) confirming the VAT taxpayer classification as either 'general taxpayer' or 'small-scale taxpayer' with the applicable effective date; (4) applying for VAT invoice quota, electronic invoice activation, and if engaged in export, the export tax rebate registration profile; (5) executing the bank-tax automatic withholding authorization agreement; and (6) completing the statutory tax filing calendar setup for monthly/quarterly corporate income tax (EIT), VAT, and other levies. The USCC is the sole tax identifier for all filings, invoice issuance, export declarations, and refund applications.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law provides no check-the-box or hybrid entity election. A WFOE incorporated in mainland China is categorically treated as a Chinese resident enterprise by place of incorporation, subject to worldwide Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) at the standard 25% rate. No entity classification election is available for domestic tax purposes; the only relevant optimization is the application of the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA) or the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement for withholding rate reduction on dividends (up to 5%) and other cross-border payments, subject to substantial business and beneficial ownership requirements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual aggregate taxable sales of goods and services; exceeding this threshold mandates registration as a general VAT taxpayer. Below the threshold, the entity defaults to small-scale taxpayer status with a simplified 3% or reduced 1%/2% levy rates (as applicable), but may voluntarily elect general taxpayer registration to claim input VAT credits, a prerequisite for claiming export zero-rating and refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of the pre-packaged spicy tallow hotpot soup base qualifies for VAT zero-rating (with a corresponding refund of input VAT) when the following statutory conditions are concurrently satisfied: (a) the goods are physically sold and shipped from the customs territory of mainland China to a destination outside China; (b) the WFOE is registered as a general VAT taxpayer; (c) the export sale is documented by a verified Export Goods Customs Declaration and the export proceeds are repatriated to China within the statutory foreign exchange collection period; (d) the export declaration data matches the commercial invoice, packing list, and VAT invoice details; (e) the unsold goods are not returned without customs formalities; (f) the goods are not listed as prohibited or restricted export commodities; and (g) the export tax rebate declaration is filed within the applicable time limit after the export date. The product, falling under HS Chapter 21 as a food preparation, is exportable with zero-rating subject to AQSIQ food safety and phytosanitary certification as applicable.

  • Original Commercial Invoice and the Sales/Purchase Contract covering the export transaction
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill) evidencing physical shipment departing mainland China customs territory
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Verification Form (出口收汇核销单) issued by the designated bank in the SAFE 'Service & Administration System', proving inward remittance of the export proceeds
  • Export Tax Rebate Declaration Packing List and the e-Tax/STA-generated export refund schedule

China operates the 'Golden Tax Phase IV' anti-fraud system. VAT general taxpayers must issue standard VAT invoices exclusively through the STA electronic invoice platform, which enables real-time transmission to the central STA database and affixes a unique digital stamp on every invoice. Special VAT Invoices (used for input credit) are subject to API-based reporting and automatic cross-matching between seller and buyer submissions. Any mismatch or anomaly in invoice data automatically generates an audit flag. For export transactions, the WFOE must transmit export declaration and refund application data through the e-Tax platform interfacing with the China International Trade Single Window (using API or EDI protocols). Blockchain-based electronic invoices are accepted in pilot areas and the national electronic invoice standard supports traceable, anti-tamper digital stamping.

The STA, in coordination with China Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), performs risk-driven audits of export zero-rating and refund claims. If the claim is based on forged declarations, false documentation, unrepatriated export proceeds, or mismatched customs data, the STA will retroactively claw back all refunded VAT and levy late-payment surcharges retroactively from the refund date, plus administrative penalties ranging from 50% up to five times the amount involved. Customs surcharges and fines for false export declarations may also be imposed. Where the fraud is systemic or involves a significant amount, criminal liability may arise under the People's Republic of China Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, exposing the legal representative and responsible officers to imprisonment, fines, confiscation of assets, and disqualification from future export rebate eligibility. In addition, Golden Tax Phase IV automated risk scoring may suspend the WFOE's export refund status, require mandatory in-field inspection, and trigger comprehensive tax re-audits for prior periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility and capital-account restrictions. A mainland WFOE is a resident enterprise; its ordinary trade-export receipts in foreign currency must be repatriated into a domestic current-account FX bank account unless SAFE has approved offshore retention. Once received, the enterprise may retain the funds in foreign currency or settle them into CNY at a licensed domestic bank at the prevailing market rate; no fixed surrender percentage applies today. Capital-account transactions, however, remain tightly controlled. Equity injection from the Hong Kong parent, shareholder debt, and cross-border guarantees require prior SAFE registration, filing, or bank-administered verification. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permissible only after annual audit, tax clearance, and a formal board distribution resolution; the remitting bank must verify audited cumulative distributable profits and withhold relevant PRC corporate income tax. Genuine and verifiable current-account trade flows therefore enjoy high convertibility and fast settlement, while any capital-account movement, round-tripping, off-balance-sheet financing, or unusual third-party payment pattern will trigger elevated compliance scrutiny.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current-account trade verification regime, export collection of goods must be repatriated to China through a designated domestic bank within the statutory collection period, normally 90 days after the export customs declaration date unless a longer contractual credit period is filed with SAFE through the bank. Since the 2012 SAFE reforms, there is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; a WFOE may retain up to 100 percent of its verified current-account export proceeds in a foreign-currency current account or convert all or part of them into CNY at the market rate. Every receipt must be matched against an electronic customs declaration in the SAFE monitoring system, and the trade-in-FX purpose code must correspond to 'Goods Trade - General Export Collection'. Non-compliance can lead to suspension of the bank's delegated verification authority and placement on SAFE's negative list.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and SAFE's local branches in the province where the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is registered.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the onshore WFOE in mainland China. Offshore digital EMIs and fintech payment institutions are not licensed by the PBoC or SAFE to open or maintain domestic CNY current accounts, FX capital accounts, or export collection accounts for a PRC resident entity. A mainland WFOE must bank with a licensed domestic commercial bank for capital-account flows, trade verification, and FX settlement. Such EMIs may only support auxiliary payment processing, payroll, cards, or expense management utilities where they have a licensed local partner; they cannot serve as the primary cross-border treasury rail or as a substitute for SAFE-regulated banking.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial asset transactions; and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. The Master File threshold is triggered when the multinational group has annual related-party transaction amounts exceeding RMB 1 billion, and a Country-by-Country Report is required when the group's consolidated annual revenue in the prior year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion. The WFOE must also file the annual related-party transaction report within the annual CIT return even if documentation thresholds are not met.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the beneficial ownership standard in SAT Announcement 2015 No. 7, and the special anti-avoidance rules for cross-border payments. The WFOE must perform real Core Income-Generating Activities in China, including procurement, inventory control, sales, shipping, credit and collection, and risk management for the hotpot soup base export business. A Hong Kong parent that merely holds shares and invoices dividends without employees, office premises, or decision-making substance will not qualify as a beneficial owner, and treaty relief will be denied. Hong Kong's own Economic Substance Regulations apply separately to the Hong Kong parent; failure to satisfy that regime can make the parent a passive shell in both jurisdictions and expose the group to recharacterization, deemed dividend treatment, and 10% withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Export Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of tax underpaid, plus daily late-payment interest. Fraudulent export tax refund conduct is punishable under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment and fines ranging from up to 5 years for a relatively large amount, up to 10 years for a huge amount, and up to life imprisonment where the amount is especially huge and the circumstances especially serious, together with fines.

Director personal liability: If the legal representative or director personally signs false tax filings, directs false invoicing, or commingles corporate assets with personal assets, the corporate veil may be pierced under the PRC Company Law. Directors may be jointly and severally liable to make restitution of diverted assets, forfeit personal gains, and may have personal bank accounts frozen. The tax authority will list the legal representative on the joint punishment blacklist for seriously dishonest taxpayers, triggering a travel ban, prohibition on high consumption, restriction from becoming a legal representative of another company, and potential criminal detention followed by imprisonment if convicted.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Shell Conduit Abuse

Statutory sanction: Article 41 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorizes the tax authority to reallocate related-party income. Article 44 permits deemed assessment when related-party documentation is not provided. The tax authority may impose an upward transfer pricing adjustment, assess interest at the benchmark bank lending rate plus 5 percentage points, and impose penalties from RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 for failure to file documentation. If deliberate underpayment is established, Article 63 imposes 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. Beneficial ownership denial under SAT Announcement 2015 No. 7 subjects outbound dividends, interest, and royalties to the full 10% statutory withholding rate rather than the treaty rate.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve non-arm's length pricing or sign false related-party disclosure schedules may be subject to personal asset seizure and civil restitution if the corporate structure is used to defraud tax authorities or creditors. The tax authority may demand personal interview and travel restriction for the legal representative pending assessment. Where the WFOE is left insolvent by shifted profits, creditors may apply to pierce the corporate veil, making directors personally responsible for unpaid amounts. Conviction for tax evasion carries imprisonment of up to three years for a relatively large amount and up to seven years for a huge amount.
Withholding Tax Default and Fraudulent DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: Article 37 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law requires the Chinese payer to withhold tax on non-resident income. Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law requires the withholding agent to pay the unwithheld tax and imposes a penalty of 50% to 3 times the unwithheld amount. If a fraudulent treaty claim is discovered, the unpaid tax is collected from the WFOE, late-payment interest accrues daily, and the GAAR under Article 47 may be invoked to disregard the Hong Kong entity, causing all payments to be recharacterized as China-source taxable income at the 10% rate.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and financial director who direct the unauthorized remittance may be personally assessed as actual or constructive fund controllers if corporate records are incomplete or funds were misappropriated. Civil restitution may be ordered where the director benefited personally. The director is placed on the tax blacklist and issued a travel ban preventing exit from China until the tax liability is settled. If the fraudulent treaty scheme is found to be deliberate tax evasion, criminal penalties under the Criminal Law include imprisonment for up to three years for a large amount, and up to seven years for an especially large amount, together with fines.
