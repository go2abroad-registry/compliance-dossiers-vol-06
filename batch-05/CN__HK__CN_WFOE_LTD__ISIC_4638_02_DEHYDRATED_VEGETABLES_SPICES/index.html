Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no escrow or blocked-account requirement under PRC law for a general trading WFOE. Registered capital is freely determined by the sole shareholder, but under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law all subscribed capital must be fully paid in within five years after establishment. Capital contributions must be remitted into a designated capital contribution account opened with a licensed bank in China, and foreign-currency contributions from the Hong Kong shareholder must be received and converted through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange banking rules. If paid-in capital gives rise to stamp duty, such duty is payable at the then-applicable rate.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in China, the legal entity is a resident enterprise for PRC corporate income tax (CIT) purposes and must obtain a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) as its Tax Identification Number (TIN). Step 1: Register the company with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) to obtain the business license (which automatically includes the USCC). Step 2: Within 30 days after receiving the business license, complete the tax registration with the competent local STA office either online via the e-Tax portal or in person. The USCC printed on the license serves as the TIN/VAT ID for all tax filings. Step 3: For a non-resident foreign enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that needs a TIN for withholding or VAT procedures (e.g., income sourced from China), the non-resident can apply for a temporary tax number with the STA when filing withholding returns or through the e-Tax system; the STA will issue a taxpayer identification code upon submission of the applicable registration form and supporting documents.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the VAT Export Refund (Exemption/Refund) policy, exported goods are zero-rated provided: (i) the exporter is a registered general VAT taxpayer or a production enterprise holding an export operating qualification; (ii) the goods physically leave China and the export is declared with customs; (iii) the sales invoice, export goods declaration, and collection of foreign exchange within the required period are evidenced; (iv) the exporter submits the export rebate application with the complete documentation set; and (v) the export proceeds are repatriated within 180 days (or extended where approved). Qualifying dehydrated vegetables and spices exported under HS chapters 07/09 meet these conditions.

  • China Customs Export Declaration (Customs Clearance Form for Export Goods)
  • Bill of Lading (Ocean) or Air Waybill (Air Freight)
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt/Settlement Voucher (Bank Certificate of Inward Remittance, formerly Export Collection Verification)
  • VAT Special Invoice (Export Deduction/Refund special invoice)
  • Commercial Invoice

China operates a comprehensive VAT invoice administration system under the STA. All VAT invoices (special and ordinary) must be issued through the official electronic invoicing system (since 2022, the nationwide All-Electronic Invoicing platform replaces legacy tax-control devices). Invoices are transmitted in near-real-time to the STA's central database, forming the basis of the Golden Tax Phase IV risk-monitoring. There is no separate fiscal clearance stamp but every invoice must pass validity checks and the issuance data must be reported during VAT filing. API-based integration with enterprise systems is required for high-volume issuers; automated data matching cross-validates invoice data against import/export customs data.

False or fraudulent export zero-rating claims are subject to severe penalties under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law and Criminal Law. The tax authority will claw back the wrongly refunded output VAT plus interest. Administrative fines range from 50% to 5 times the amount of tax evaded or refunded fraudulently (typically 1 to 5 times for export rebate fraud). Criminal liability: where the amount of deceitfully obtained export rebate exceeds RMB 50,000, the responsible parties may be convicted of the crime of defrauding tax export rebates (骗取出口退税罪), punishable by imprisonment and criminal fines. Additionally, failure to repatriate foreign exchange within the 180-day statutory window invalidates zero-rating eligibility and triggers the assessment of output VAT plus late payment surcharge.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates under a managed floating exchange rate system with full current-account convertibility and administratively controlled capital-account convertibility. The WFOE can settle bona fide export proceeds for dehydrated garlic flakes and crushed chili pepper through any authorized domestic bank, but every credit and debit must be traced to authentic trade documents, customs declarations, and the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system. Capital-account flows, including the initial equity contribution from the Hong Kong parent, foreign debt, and cross-border guarantees, must be registered with SAFE, must enter a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account, and can be converted into RMB only for real operating needs rather than speculative treasury purposes. Export receipts are no longer subject to mandatory surrender and may be retained in a current-account foreign-exchange account. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted as a current-account item after the WFOE has filed its audited annual financial statements, settled the corporate income tax on distributable profits, passed the bank's beneficial-owner verification, and presented a shareholder resolution. Profit remittance is therefore operationally accessible but requires full tax clearance, audit evidence, and a clean compliance record; capital repatriation by way of reducing registered capital or equity transfer requires SAFE and market-administration formalities.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE abolished mandatory export surrender: the WFOE may keep 100% of export foreign-currency receipts in a current-account foreign-exchange account and has no forced conversion ratio to RMB. Collection norms require export proceeds to be brought onshore and matched to the customs declaration; for ordinary shipments the bank monitors a 90-day post-customs collection period, and any expected delay beyond 90 days must be pre-registered with SAFE via the bank. Once received, each SWIFT credit must be reported through the bank's international receipts reporting system and retained, converted, or allocated to legitimate trade payments only.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulating the CNH/HKD clearing side of the Hong Kong treasury leg

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI cross-border viability is Low; offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the WFOE's primary treasury bank because they do not hold PBOC banking licenses, cannot maintain onshore RMB current accounts, cannot access SAFE registration and trade-in-goods monitoring, and are legally barred from executing mainland China FX capital settlement. The Hong Kong parent may use an EMI for its own CNH/HKD corporate wallet, but all WFOE regulatory capital, exports proceeds, tax, and dividend flows must be cleared through a licensed domestic bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must route the capital injection by SWIFT MT103 from its corporate account to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at Bank of China Limited or another Tier-1 bank, using the SAFE/BOP category for inbound direct investment equity injection into a foreign-invested enterprise. The bank will match the remittance beneficiary account number, registered capital cap, and SAFE FDI registration reference before crediting; after receipt, the WFOE can apply to convert the foreign currency into RMB through the bank's capital settlement mechanism, with the RMB transferred to a segregated conversion-awaiting-payment account and disbursed only for approved operating expenses. A local CPA should issue a capital verification report for the corporate records, and the WFOE must not use the capital account for general working-capital current-account transactions.For export trade, clearing runs through the licensed bank's trade-services desk: open-account and TT settlements use SWIFT MT103; documentary letters of credit are opened or advised via SWIFT MT700 with document presentations; documentary collections are handled as D/P or D/A through the bank's overseas correspondent network. For bulk dehydrated garlic and chili sales, the WFOE should always attach invoices, packing lists, inspection certificates, and transport documents to ensure the bank can file the SAFE international receipt/payment report. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A PRC enterprise must prepare and maintain contemporaneous Local File documentation when in a tax year any one of the following related-party transaction thresholds is met: tangible property transfers equal or exceed CNY 200,000,000; intangible property transfers equal or exceed CNY 100,000,000; financial asset transfers equal or exceed CNY 100,000,000; related-party financing average balance equal or exceed CNY 50,000,000; or other related-party transactions equal or exceed CNY 40,000,000. The Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions in the year reach CNY 10,000,000,000 or more. The WFOE must also file the annual related-party transaction report (Form A000000 and supporting schedules) via the eTax portal.

Economic substance scope: The PRC tax administration applies a substance-over-form and beneficial-ownership analysis when granting DTA relief. SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 presumes that a resident of the other jurisdiction is not the beneficial owner and cannot enjoy treaty benefits if it lacks actual business substance, such as employees, offices, substantial activities, and decision-making capacity in Hong Kong. Core income-generating activities for the Hong Kong holding or trading entity should include contract negotiation and execution, invoicing, ownership of warehouse or trading risk, board decision-making, maintenance of accounting books and bank accounts, and actual management and control in Hong Kong. Although Hong Kong does not have a general Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to offshore financial centres, Hong Kong tax residence and the China-Hong Kong DTA require central management and control in Hong Kong; therefore the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent must maintain board minutes, employee records, office information, and transaction files demonstrating genuine economic activities and avoid a shell-company structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Export Revenue Reporting

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of the underpaid tax, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment up to 3 years for tax evasion exceeding CNY 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax due, and 3 to 7 years for especially large amounts, together with criminal fines.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the limited-liability veil under PRC Company Law when shareholders or directors use the company to conceal tax evasion and cause serious harm to state tax revenue; directors, financial controllers, and signatories who prepared false tax returns can be jointly and severally liable from personal assets. They may be criminally prosecuted as directly responsible persons, placed on the tax dishonesty blacklist, prohibited from exiting China, and subjected to travel bans and freezing of personal bank accounts.
Shell Company Treaty Abuse / Artificial Tax Arbitrage

Statutory sanction: State Administration of Taxation Announcement 2018 No. 9 denies treaty benefits where the Hong Kong entity is not the beneficial owner; the PRC tax authority will reimpose the statutory 10% withholding tax and assess interest. PRC CIT Law Articles 41, 47, and 120 authorize special tax-adjustment recharacterization under the general anti-avoidance rule, with late-payment interest and penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false resolutions, appoint nominee shareholders, or approve the conduit structure may be held civilly liable for the recovered tax and interest if the corporate veil is pierced; if the sham entity is used for deliberate tax evasion, personal criminal liability may apply under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205, together with entry-exit restriction, asset seizure, and public blacklisting of responsible persons.
Withholding Tax Default on Outbound Dividends, Interest, and Royalties

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes a fine of 50% to 300% of the tax that was withheld or should have been withheld; the in-charge tax authority may also assess the WFOE as withholding agent for the unpaid non-resident tax together with the daily late-payment surcharge. Serious or repeated non-compliance may result in public tax violation notices and administrative penalties under Articles 62 and 64.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who control bank signing authority and knowingly approve the cross-border remittance without withholding can be held personally liable for the unpaid tax through asset attachment and exit restrictions. If the directors embezzle or fail to remit withheld taxes, criminal liability may be prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 and the responsible persons may be sentenced to imprisonment, fined, and prohibited from leaving China until all tax, interest, and fines are settled.
