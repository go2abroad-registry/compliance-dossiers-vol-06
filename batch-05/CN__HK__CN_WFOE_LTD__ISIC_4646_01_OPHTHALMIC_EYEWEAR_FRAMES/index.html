Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform and the STA national electronic invoice service platform.

E-government filing portal

For a China-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory TIN procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) complete basic tax registration at the competent STA office within 30 days of license issuance via the e-Tax portal; (3) obtain the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is legally the 18-digit USCC; (4) register for VAT, determine small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status, and complete corporate income tax, stamp tax, urban maintenance and education surcharge registrations; (5) if foreign exchange is to be received, open a designated foreign-exchange settlement account with a Chinese bank and register goods-trade enterprise information with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment, no general TIN registration is required; such an entity obtains a withholding tax clearance certificate only when claiming treaty benefits or receiving China-sourced income that is subject to withholding by a Chinese payor.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a China-incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory TIN procurement procedure is: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) complete basic tax registration at the competent STA office within 30 days of license issuance via the e-Tax portal; (3) obtain the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is legally the 18-digit USCC; (4) register for VAT, determine small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status, and complete corporate income tax, stamp tax, urban maintenance and education surcharge registrations; (5) if foreign exchange is to be received, open a designated foreign-exchange settlement account with a Chinese bank and register goods-trade enterprise information with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All businesses must complete tax registration upon receipt of the business license irrespective of turnover. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000; below that threshold, an enterprise may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the customs territory of the People's Republic of China and sold to an overseas buyer are zero-rated for VAT; the exporter collects VAT at 0% and may claim a refund of attributable input VAT. To qualify, the WFOE must hold export registration and customs registration, the goods must be declared to Customs and actually leave China, the export sales documents must be consistent, and the export proceeds must be received and repatriated through a designated Chinese bank within the statutory deadline. Qualifying offshore digital services are not relevant to this ophthalmic eyewear frames wholesale scope.

  • China Customs Export Declaration Form (export customs declaration)
  • Ocean Bill of Lading, sea waybill, or air waybill evidencing physical export
  • Export sales contract or confirmed purchase order from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin if required
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt/settlement proof showing repatriation of export proceeds (export proceeds settlement certificate)
  • Zero-rated export VAT invoice generated through the electronic invoice service platform

All VAT invoices are issued through the STA national VAT invoice management system. Under the full digital electronic invoice reform, fully digitalized e-invoices are created, authorized, transmitted and archived in real time on the STA unified electronic invoice service platform. Each invoice carries a unique 20-digit invoice number, an encrypted dynamic anti-counterfeit QR code and a digital e-seal; the tax authority clears and validates the invoice before it can be used for input VAT deduction or export refund claiming. Physical fiscal stamping is no longer required, but digital authentication of the e-invoice is mandatory, and offline issuance is non-compliant.

If the export is found to be unsupported, fictitious, or inconsistent with the customs declaration, the STA will deny or claw back the export VAT refund and impose late-payment surcharges plus an administrative penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax amount evaded. Fraudulent export rebate claims may be referred to judicial authorities and, if convicted, constitute the crime of defrauding export tax refunds under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with imprisonment and fines. The exporter may also be downgraded in STA export-rebate classification, suspended from zero-rating privileges, and placed under enhanced cross-departmental Customs and foreign-exchange audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a bifurcated convertibility regime. Current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, are generally convertible upon presentation of genuine transaction documents under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and SAFE trade foreign exchange rules. Capital account transactions remain materially controlled and require registration, approval, or quota-based administration. A WFOE established in mainland China with a Hong Kong parent can inject equity capital only through a SAFE-registered FDI capital account, and each disbursement must be matched to the approved capital purpose and commercial need. Profit remission to the Hong Kong shareholder qualifies as a current account outward transfer once the enterprise has demonstrated prior-year audited profits, settled taxes, and compliance with SAFE's trade and service verification protocols. Retained earnings may need to be converted from RMB to foreign currency at prevailing market rates, and onward forward hedging must evidence a real underlying exposure. The renminbi is not freely floating; the onshore CNY central parity references a managed currency basket, and offshore CNH rates can diverge transactionally. Export receipts are not subject to mandatory surrender, but they must be repatriated or retained in an approved foreign exchange settlement account, and any excess balances beyond a bank-determined ceiling must be converted into RMB. The overall regime requires a disciplined documentary trail linking cross-border treasury flows to real commercial operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of the export declaration. Once repatriated, the proceeds may be retained in a foreign exchange settlement account, but the account ceiling is determined by the bank based on the enterprise's previous-year current-account foreign exchange income, commonly capped at 50%. Balances exceeding the ceiling must be converted into RMB at the bank's ruling spot rate. There is no additional mandatory conversion percentage for ordinary merchandise export receipts, provided the WFOE maintains clean customs declarations and commercial invoices.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Council

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility Rating: Low - Not Viable. The PBOC and SAFE do not recognize offshore fintech electronic money institutions as licensed deposit-taking entities for servicing an onshore mainland WFOE. Such EMIs cannot open onshore settlement accounts, hold resident RMB balances, or process trade-related FX transactions. Engaging an unlicensed EMI would expose the entity to cross-border data compliance violations, KYC failures, and People's Bank administrative penalties. Only locally licensed commercial banks, through their approved overseas correspondent relationships, may lawfully clear the WFOE's treasury flows.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax under PRC CIT Law Article 3 and Article 37 on dividends paid by a PRC-resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise. Under the PRC–Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA), the withholding tax rate is reduced to 5% if the Hong Kong shareholder is the beneficial owner and has held at least 25% of the paying company's issued shares directly for a continuous period of 12 months immediately before the dividend distribution. If the shareholding is less than 25% or the beneficial owner condition is not met, the treaty rate is 10% (no reduction).

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The PRC transfer pricing documentation rules (SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42) require a local file when the total annual amount of related-party transactions reaches any of the following thresholds (RMB): (i) tangible asset transfer (purchase and sale combined) exceeding RMB 200 million; (ii) financial asset transfer exceeding RMB 100 million; (iii) intangible asset transfer exceeding RMB 100 million; (iv) other related-party transactions (including service fees, interest, royalties) exceeding RMB 40 million. A master file is required if the entire corporate group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion, or if the group has a presence in multiple jurisdictions with significant related-party turnover. For this wholesale WFOE selling optical frames to a Hong Kong affiliate, if the annual intercompany sales exceed RMB 200 million or other transaction types cross the RMB 40 million threshold, both a local file and a master file (if applicable) must be prepared and submitted with the annual CIT filing. Contemporaneous documentation must be completed by the filing deadline; otherwise a fine up to RMB 10,000 per document applies and the tax authority may make its own adjustments.

Economic substance scope: While China does not apply the Offshore-Regime Environmental/Economic Substance Regulations similar to the UAE, the PRC CIT Law contains a General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 47) and specific anti-avoidance provisions on controlled foreign companies (Article 45) and thin capitalisation (Article 46). The tax authority will scrutinise whether the WFOE has genuine business substance in China, including physical office, local employees who conduct sales, procurement, and management, and decision-making functions that generate the core income (CIGA). For the Hong Kong parent, to retain treaty benefits, it must demonstrate real and continuous economic activities in Hong Kong, such as staff, premises, receipts, and governance. If the Hong Kong entity is a pure pass-through, the dividends will be re-characterised as paid to a non-HK beneficial owner, triggering full 10% WHT and potential GAAR adjustments. In transfer pricing examinations, the tax authority examines whether the allocation of profits reflects the economically significant functions, assets, and risks (DEMPE). For this distribution WFOE, the entrepreneurial risk and marketing functions must remain in China to justify a manufacturing or wholesale margin consistent with TNMM or resale price method.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Criminal Law Article 201, a taxpayer who evades tax by deception or concealment (including falsifying accounts, under-reporting turnover, or refusing to remit tax) is liable to pay the dodged tax plus a fine of 1 to 5 times the evaded amount. The tax authority may also impose an administrative penalty under the Tax Administration Law Article 63 equal to 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. If the evaded tax amount is RMB 50,000 or more and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, the criminal threshold is triggered.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced for directors who directly engage in or consent to tax evasion schemes. They are personally liable for the unpaid tax and administrative fines, their personal bank accounts and assets may be frozen or seized for restitution, and a travel ban (exit restriction) can be imposed under the Exit-Entry Administration Law. In a criminal prosecution, the responsible director may be sentenced to up to 3 years' imprisonment plus criminal fines; for an evaded amount that is huge (e.g., over RMB 1 million) and with a high proportion of the tax payable, the imprisonment term can be 3 to 7 years.
Abuse of Tax Treaty through Shell Hong Kong Intermediary

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority may invoke the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 47 of the CIT Law) and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (State Council Order No. 672, Chapter 9) to re-characterise the dividend as paid directly to the ultimate non-Hong Kong shareholder, apply the full 10% withholding tax, and impose late interest at the central bank benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points. Administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Law Article 63 may also apply for the tax under-withheld, with fines up to 100% of the under-withheld amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly structure or approve the dividend flow through a shell Hong Kong company can be held personally liable for the under-withheld tax and penalties. The tax authority may obtain a court order to freeze and levy personal assets of the directors to recover the shortfall. Criminal liability for tax evasion may arise under Criminal Law Article 201 if the intent to unfairly reduce the tax is proven, with imprisonment up to 3 years (or up to 7 years for aggravated amounts). Additionally, the directors may face a personal travel ban under tax enforcement rules and be blacklisted by the Credit Office for tax offences, which bars them from leaving China, acquiring certain assets, or serving as directors in other companies.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Fraud and Intercompany Mispricing

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Special Tax Adjustment Measures (SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 and Order No. 672), the tax authority may make a primary transfer pricing adjustment, add interest at the benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points from the transaction date, and impose a penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax under the Tax Administration Law Article 64 for inaccurate filing. If the mispricing is found to be wilful evasion, the penalty can be as high as 500% of the evaded tax and criminal evasion charges under Criminal Law Article 201 may follow. Additionally, the failure to maintain or provide contemporaneous documentation triggers a fixed fine of RMB 1,000 to RMB 10,000 per missing document.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false transfer pricing documentation may be personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties as the tax authority can assimilate the company for tax purposes and seek recovery from directors if the company is insolvent or the veil is pierced for fraudulent conduct. The directors may face civil liability for restitution to the corporate estate, a travel ban initiated by the tax authority, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion (imprisonment up to 3 years) or for forgery of financial documents under Criminal Law Article 205-1, which carries imprisonment of up to 5 years. They may also be disqualified from managing any PRC entity for a period of up to 5 years. In extreme cases, the personal bank accounts and properties of the directors can be attached to satisfy the tax liability.
