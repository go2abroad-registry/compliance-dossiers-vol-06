Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal is the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau), with export-related customs and tax filings executed through the China International Trade Single Window

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), the 18-digit identifier issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) on the business license. Procedure: (1) complete SAMR company formation and obtain the Business License containing the USCC; (2) within 30 days of business registration, complete tax registration through the local STA e-Tax Bureau by submitting the charter, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, and foreign investor documentation; (3) the USCC then operates as the VAT registration number, Corporate Income Tax taxpayer number, and export tax refund identifier; (4) select VAT taxpayer status as general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer. For a foreign non-resident entity such as a Hong Kong SAR shareholder that does not itself carry on a PRC trade or business, no mainland TIN is required solely for holding shares; if the non-resident earns China-source income, the PRC payer or the non-resident must create a non-resident tax record and obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification code through the STA e-Tax Bureau withholding module.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No US-style check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election exists in the PRC. A small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect general VAT taxpayer status if it meets fixed premises, accounting, and compliance requirements; a general VAT taxpayer with annual taxable sales below the statutory threshold may apply for small-scale reclassification under STA transitional rules. Certain Corporate Income Tax special restructuring elections are available under PRC enterprise income tax rules, but these are not entity classification or check-the-box elections.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 0 for initial VAT/tax registration: every PRC-incorporated entity must register for tax immediately upon SAMR business registration. Mandatory economic turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales of goods; below this threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer with a reduced levy rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export sales of SSD and CPU chips are zero-rated at 0% VAT under PRC VAT law when the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are physically declared for export through PRC customs, the goods leave the customs territory, and the export proceeds are repatriated into the WFOE's mainland bank account. The WFOE must file the export refund declaration through the STA e-Tax Bureau using electronic customs data. Qualifying offshore digital services consumed wholly outside China are separately zero-rated only if supplied to an overseas client and paid in foreign currency; goods export is the relevant category for this wholesale semiconductor trading scope.

  • Customs Export Declaration filed with the General Administration of Customs of the PRC through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales invoice (Special VAT Invoice or export ordinary invoice)
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight or Air Waybill for air freight evidencing physical export
  • Export contract or purchase order
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher evidencing the cross-border receipt of export proceeds
  • Export tax refund application data filed through the STA e-Tax Bureau

VAT taxpayers must use the STA-controlled Golden Tax System and national electronic invoice service platform. General VAT taxpayers must issue Special VAT Invoices or fully digitalized electronic invoices, with invoice data transmitted to STA in near-real-time through certified ERP/API integration linked to the taxpayer's USCC. Buyers must verify invoices through the STA national VAT invoice verification platform before input VAT credit may be claimed. Anti-fraud controls include digital signatures, invoice validation codes, automated cross-matching of seller and buyer VAT returns, and systematic denial of credit for unverified or mismatched invoices; no third-party physical fiscal stamping applies.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange cross-check export declarations, VAT refund claims, logistics evidence, and foreign exchange repatriation records. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine physical export, without actual receipt of export proceeds within the required period, or with fraudulent or mismatched documentation, the STA may deny or claw back the refund, deem the transaction a domestic taxable sale and charge output VAT at 13%, impose a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and levy fines of 50% to five times the refunded or underpaid tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Serious or deliberate fraud is subject to criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refunds, suspension or disqualification from export tax refund filing, downgrade of the taxpayer's tax integrity rating, and public disclosure of penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a hybrid system where current account FX transactions are conditionally convertible and capital account items remain controllably liberalized. Businesses can buy and sell FX at designated banks on a real-need basis, but every flow requires SAFE registration or bank verification. The WFOE's HK parent equity capital enters as FDI and is registered as capital under the capital account; funds can only be used for bona fide operating purposes within the registered business scope, not for stock-market speculation or unauthorized intercompany lending. RMB cannot be freely converted; onshore corporates may use forward FX contracts with licensed banks for genuine trade exposures. Export proceeds in USD/HKD must be repatriated and settled through the bank's current account system, and profit remittances to the HK parent require tax clearance and audited financials plus evidence of surplus declared. Capital account liberalization has advanced through pilot Free Trade Account regimes and QFLP/QDLP schemes, yet the baseline remains an approval-and-verification regime. The WFOE must therefore rely on documented trade and investment flows, not free market convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current account rules, export proceeds from SSD/CPU wholesale transactions must be repatriated to a domestic designated forex account within the contract-specified payment term and generally within 180 days from customs declaration; ordinary exporters must convert received foreign currency into RMB at a licensed bank except for balances retained inside an approved current account quota. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 100% unless the enterprise has granted overseas retention eligibility; even then only a capped working-capital portion can be retained and must be justified by currency mismatch evidence.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as its delegated foreign exchange administration authority

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability as primary clearing route. The PBOC and SAFE do not recognize offshore fintech EMIs or neobanks as eligible settlement banks for PRC-incorporated WFOE treasury. Such providers cannot access CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System) settlement in RMB, cannot open SAFE-regulated capital accounts, and cannot furnish the capital verification feedback required for FDI. They may be used only as an auxiliary payment interface for small-value expenses, while all material volume must flow through licensed PRC tier-1 banks. Any attempt to route export proceeds through an unlicensed EMI will trigger bank de-risking and account closure.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant routing requires FDI registration through SAFE's digital system, after which the HK parent remits equity capital via SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 'Foreign Direct Investment' or 'Equity Capital Injection'. The receiving tier-1 bank opens a special foreign exchange capital account, performs capital verification against the registered equity amount, and converts USD/HKD into RMB under the SAFE-approved capital account framework. All incoming remittances must reference the SAFE FDI registration number, the WFOE's unified social credit code, and the parent company's full corporate identity. Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: In Hong Kong SAR, every non-dormant company is required to appoint a certified public accountant auditor and have its financial statements audited unless it qualifies as a 'small private company' or 'small guarantee company' under sections 359-362 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). A small private company is exempt from audit only when it satisfies at least two of the following three conditions for the relevant financial year: (i) total revenue not exceeding HKD 100,000,000; (ii) total assets not exceeding HKD 100,000,000; and (iii) not more than 100 employees. Therefore, statutory audit is mandatory for companies that exceed any two of these thresholds, for companies that are part of a larger non-small group, and for a small company whose members holding at least 5% of voting rights request an audit. A dormant company may suspend the audit requirement only after filing the applicable statutory declaration with the Companies Registry; an active semiconductor wholesale trading company will ordinarily not qualify for the dormant-company exemption.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; reduced to 5% under the China–Hong Kong DTA Article 10(2) if the HK shareholder is a company directly holding at least 25% of the Chinese company and is the beneficial owner.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million for annual related-party transactions in tangible goods (purchases or sales) or other related-party transactions; RMB 100 million for related-party financial transactions; preparers must file the annual related-party transaction report with the CIT return and maintain a Local File and Master File if the threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47) and substance-over-form principles allow re-characterisation of transactions lacking bona fide commercial purpose; the HK trading affiliate must have real employees, offices, risk-bearing capacity, and decision-making functions for SSD/CPU procurement and sales; if core wholesale brokerage functions are performed by Chinese WFOE staff and the HK entity only passes title, the tax authority may reallocate income, disregard the HK entity, or deem a PE in China; Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regulations (if applicable as reference) require adequate economic substance for pure equity-holding or IP companies, but PRC rules focus on functional and risk analysis under SAT Announcement 2017 No. 6.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Under-Reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of unpaid tax, 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and fine of 50% to 500% of evaded tax; Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years if evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and 10% of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil may be pierced where the company is a mere façade, capital not paid in, or assets stripped; directors, financial officers, and shadow controllers become jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, customs duties, and penalties; travel bans, blacklisting for serious untrustworthiness ('shixin' list), and asset freezes apply; criminal conviction leads to imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Hollow HK Affiliate Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41: primary TP adjustment with 15% annual interest (CIT Law Article 48); 5% compensatory surcharge for missing TP documentation under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administrative Law; administrative fine up to RMB 500,000; CFC rules under CIT Law Article 45 may impute HK passive income to the Chinese WFOE.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the veil where the HK entity is an alter-ego and the WFOE is left insolvent; directors face joint and several civil liability for tax arrears; cross-border enforcement through China–HK mutual legal assistance allows seizure of personal assets; imprisonment up to 7 years for fraudulent tax filing.
Shell Company / AML and Economic Substance Failure

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law requires enhanced due diligence for medium-risk wholesale traders; violation triggers fines up to RMB 5 million for entities and suspension/cancellation of licenses; Criminal Law Article 191 imposes imprisonment up to 10 years plus confiscation for money laundering; HK Inland Revenue Ordinance penalties: HKD 10,000–50,000 plus 300% tax undercharged for false profits tax returns; Companies Ordinance disqualification and criminal sanctions for nominee directors.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing permitted where the company is used for fraud; directors jointly liable for corporate debts and tax liabilities; travel bans and exit restrictions enforced; personal bank accounts frozen under shixin enforcement; AML criminal conviction: imprisonment up to 10 years; blacklisting in both mainland China and Hong Kong impedes cross-border business travel.
