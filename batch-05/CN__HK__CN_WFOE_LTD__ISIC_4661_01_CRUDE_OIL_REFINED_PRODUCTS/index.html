Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is conducted through the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform accessible via the STA's official website portal and the China International Trade Single Window for customs-export-related filings.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) established in China must first obtain a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The SAMR-issued Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) functions as the primary tax identification number. Within 30 days after business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local branch of the State Taxation Authority at the entity's registered address. Required supporting documents include the SAMR business license, articles of association, shareholder and ultimate beneficial ownership information, legal representative identification, registered office proof, capital contribution verification, and bank account opening certificate. The WFOE must file its entity tax profile, elect financial accounting standards, and apply for value-added tax (VAT) general taxpayer status where necessary for export zero-rating and input VAT refunds. A foreign non-resident enterprise that is the parent of a WFOE does not automatically require a separate Chinese TIN unless it derives China-source income through a permanent establishment, a withholding agent, or a China-located project; in such cases it must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration at the competent tax authority for the withholding agent or the site of economic activity. After registration, the WFOE uses the USCC as its unified tax identifier for CIT, VAT, customs, and foreign exchange reporting.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT taxable sales within any rolling 12-month period triggers mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; underlying tax registration is required for all WFOEs upon issuance of the business license, regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A VAT general taxpayer WFOE may apply zero percent VAT to exported goods if the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory, the export customs declaration is filed, the goods actually depart China, and the corresponding export proceeds are repatriated into China in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. Sales of marine fuel oil and refined petroleum products as ship stores to vessels engaged in international voyages are also eligible for zero-rating, provided the delivery is made to customs-supervised export outlets or otherwise qualifies as an export supply. The WFOE must maintain complete export contracts, commercial invoices, transport documents, customs declarations, and foreign exchange receipt evidence to substantiate the zero-rated treatment.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods customs declaration form filed through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal bill of lading evidencing shipment to a foreign destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt certificate evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export contract and related sales agreements
  • VAT invoice or export invoice issued in accordance with STA requirements
  • Customs cargo release receipt or export permit
  • Bank SWIFT wire credit advice or standby letter of credit negotiation documents
  • Foreign exchange collection verification report from the SAFE trade monitoring system

China operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV, under which VAT invoices are issued through the State Taxation Administration's central invoice systems. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are transmitted in real time to the STA through the official e-invoice platform, with unique invoice numbers, digital tax-control stamps, and automatic data matching against customs export declarations. Large taxpayers and export-oriented entities must integrate their enterprise resource planning systems with the STA e-invoice platform via official application programming interfaces for real-time invoice clearance and anti-fraud verification.

Export zero-rating is subject to retrospective STA and customs audit. If the claimed exportation is false, goods do not physically leave China, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory deadline, the tax authority may disallow the zero-rating, claw back refunded VAT, impose daily late payment surcharges, and apply administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Administration Law. Penalties can range from 50 percent to five times the tax evaded for fraudulent export refund claims, and serious cases carry customs penalties and criminal liability for tax evasion or smuggling.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a split-currency convertibility model. Under current regulations, all current-account receipts and payments are generally convertible on the basis of genuine underlying transactions, but capital-account items remain strictly controlled and require pre-approval or registration with SAFE. The WFOE's paid-in registered capital is regarded as inbound foreign direct investment and must be invested through a designated capital project account, followed by a CPA capital verification report; once converted into RMB, the capital funds may not be used for re-entering the foreign exchange market without a board resolution and SAFE approval. Export proceeds from the commodity brokerage activities are subject to repatriation into PRC and settlement or retention under the goods trade foreign-exchange management system, but can only be converted freely if the export declarations and sales contracts are authentic. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted through current-account dividends, but only after the annual audit confirms net after-tax profits, the Chinese tax clearance certificate has been issued, and the bank verifies the board distribution resolution. There is no unrestricted capital-account convertibility, and any cross-border shareholder loans or trade-finance advances typically require SAFE registration or an onshore mortgage over receivables. The WFOE must therefore design its liquidity rails with a dual-bank overlay: onshore PBOC/SAFE compliance for all RMB-denominated flows and an offshore Hong Kong clearing bank for trade documentary collection and standby letters of credit that do not touch the onshore capital system unless approved.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE goods trade foreign-exchange administration, export proceeds must be repatriated to a PRC domestic bank account within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. The enterprise may retain the foreign exchange in an export retention account or convert it to RMB; no fixed statutory percentage must be converted, but the aggregate foreign-currency retention quota is administratively capped and any excess has to be settled in RMB or otherwise held in a regulated margin account. The relevant export transaction verification certificate, Commodity Inspection Certificate and Customs Export Declaration must be presented to the settlement bank. For crude oil and refined products, the State General Administration of Customs and SAFE match the export/import data automatically under the cross-border verification system, and any mismatched cargo quantity or unit-price outlier triggers a bank-level rejection and a SAFE investigation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) does not administer exchange controls for PRC-domiciled WFOEs

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable in the PRC for onshore WFOE account services due to a regulatory ban on deposit-taking and foreign-exchange operations by non-bank fintech EMIs. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer and Currencycloud are not authorised to service PRC-domiciled WFOEs for onshore FX conversion or capital settlement; at best an HK-licensed EMI can provide a treasury sweep for the Hong Kong parent, but cannot hold the WFOE's registered capital or execute SAFE-regulated transactions. Therefore the primary treasury relationship must rest with PRC Tier-1 banks, and EMIs should be limited to ancillary payroll or expense cards outside PRC jurisdiction.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first obtain a Certificate of Approval for the WFOE from the relevant PRC Ministry of Commerce, then complete foreign exchange registration with SAFE under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable in the PRC for onshore WFOE account services due to a regulatory ban on deposit-taking and foreign-exchange operations by non-bank fintech EMIs. Offshore EMIs such as Airwallex, Payoneer and Currencycloud are not authorised to service PRC-domiciled WFOEs for onshore FX conversion or capital settlement; at best an HK-licensed EMI can provide a treasury sweep for the Hong Kong parent, but cannot hold the WFOE's registered capital or execute SAFE-regulated transactions. Therefore the primary treasury relationship must rest with PRC Tier-1 banks, and EMIs should be limited to ancillary payroll or expense cards outside PRC jurisdiction.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File threshold: aggregate related-party tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; financing above RMB 40 million; other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million (per SAT Public Notice [2016] No.42); a Country-by-Country Report is required if the Hong Kong parent's group annual revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion if the ultimate parent files in Hong Kong and such filing otherwise triggers reporting in China; in practice, for the CN WFOE with a Hong Kong parent, the Local File and Master File must be prepared if the thresholds above are met

Economic substance scope: Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) are specifically applicable to Hong Kong entities (in the offshore jurisdiction regime) requiring core income-generating activities such as strategic decision-making, commodity trading, hedging, logistics management, and risk governance to be performed in Hong Kong. For the Chinese mainland WFOE, the relevant doctrine is the 'substance-over-form' principle under CAS and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the EIT Law. The WFOE must demonstrate that it retains the staff, assets, and operational autonomy to conduct its marine fuel brokerage activity, or the PRC tax authority may reattribute profits to the Hong Kong parent and impose a deemed dividend withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Invoicing and Inflated Costs

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Criminal Law Article 205, issuing or using false special VAT invoices is criminal fraud punishable by imprisonment for life or up to 15 years and confiscation of property; administrative sanction under Law of the PRC on Administrative Penalties for Tax Evasion (Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63) imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; the tax authority may block the WFOE's export refunds and suspend all export procedures

Director personal liability: Civil liability: directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes if they directly caused the false invoicing; personal assets may be attached; criminal liability: director/principal can be sentenced to up to 15 years' imprisonment plus criminal fines; a 'blacklisting' travel ban under the Exit-Entry Administration Law prevents departure from China while the tax case is under investigation
Transfer Pricing Fraud and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 41 and Article 47, the tax authority may make primary and secondary adjustments and impose a levy surcharge of 0.05% per day on underpaid tax; for fraudulent documentation, a penalty of RMB 10,000 to 500,000 may be imposed under TCAL Article 44; additionally, cross-border money-laundering offences under PRC Criminal Law Article 191 may attach if transfers are disguised

Director personal liability: Director who signs inflated royalty or interest contracts or participates in a conduit arrangement may be found personally liable under the Company Law, requiring restitution to the WFOE; under anti-money-laundering law, officers may be subject to travel restrictions and asset freeze; if the arrangement is treated as fraudulent tax evasion, personal incarceration for 3 to 7 years with criminal fines can be imposed
Shell Company and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Public Notice [2018] No.9, beneficial ownership is denied; the WFOE will be assessed for 10% withholding tax with a 0.05% daily late surcharge from the original remittance date; and the case may be referred to the Anti-Tax Avoidance Unit; for repeated abuse, a 100% to 300% penalty on the unpaid WHT is possible under TCAL Article 64; the Hong Kong secretary company or nominee director may also be criminally liable for conspiracy to defraud the tax authority

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE who sign the dividend distribution resolution and execute a remittance without ensuring statutory WHT is withheld are personally liable under Company Law Article 147; the PRC tax authority may apply to the court to pierce the corporate veil and attach personal bank accounts of the WFOE's directors; in egregious cases, related natural persons can be banned from leaving China (blacklisted by the National Administration of Foreign Expert Affairs and immigration police) and prosecuted for criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment of up to 7 years plus fines
