Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (eTax) online portal; cross-border export declarations are cleared concurrently via the General Administration of Customs (GAC) China International Trade Single Window. For Hong Kong SAR distribution or offshore-profit matters, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong would be the separate competent authority, but the registered China WFOE is in mainland China.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN for all tax purposes. 2. Immediately upon SAMR issuance, the WFOE is seeded into STA's taxpayer registry; complete tax account activation through the competent local STA office or eTax portal within 30 days. 3. For a foreign non-resident parent or related party that itself earns China-sourced income and is not a Chinese tax resident, complete the non-resident taxpayer registration through the eTax portal to obtain a separate non-resident TIN or withholding tax identification reference; this is used for dividend, interest, royalty, service-fee and capital-gains withholding. 4. If VAT invoices are needed, apply for VAT general taxpayer status or small-scale taxpayer status, register the invoice-issuing profile, and receive the digital invoice configuration via the STA electronic invoice service platform. 5. Register banking facilities with a SAFE-designated bank for receipt of export proceeds, and complete the foreign investment information registration including foreign exchange registration through the bank or SAMR/SAFE system. 6. Update the WFOE tax registration for any changes in business scope, legal representative, address, capital structure or liquidation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections; a WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise for China Corporate Income Tax (CIT) and is subject to entity-level taxation at 25% on worldwide income. Only the VAT small-scale/general taxpayer status election and, where expressly available, the export-tax-rebate calculation election are permitted under the VAT implementing rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

In mainland China, every WFOE carrying on taxable sales must register as a VAT taxpayer upon incorporation. A legal person with annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer; a person with annual taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 is a small-scale VAT taxpayer under the simplified levy method unless general taxpayer status is elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered VAT general taxpayer may apply the 0% export VAT rate for export of tangible goods including containerized bitumen/asphalt when: (a) the goods physically leave the China customs territory and are declared under an export customs declaration; (b) the seller is the actual exporter holding title and is the exporter-of-record where relevant; (c) the export proceeds are received in a foreign currency from a genuine overseas buyer in accordance with SAFE rules; (d) the export invoice, customs declaration, transport documents and payment evidence are consistent; and (e) the taxpayer is not on the export-tax-rebate risk tainted list. The zero-rating is realized through China's export tax exemption, credit and refund mechanism; service exports and offshore digital services are governed by the separate cross-border VAT zero-rating rules but are not the primary goods scope here.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration form generated through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (or multimodal transport/house bill) evidencing physical export movement of the containerized bitumen/asphalt cargo
  • Commercial export invoice, packing list and cargo manifest for the exported goods
  • Export contract or purchase order containing the commercial terms including INCOTERMS and agreed foreign-currency consideration
  • Foreign exchange receipts confirmation issued by a SAFE-designated bank showing the transfer of export proceeds from the overseas buyer (the China-recognized equivalent of an Inward Remittance Certificate/FIRC)
  • Export tax rebate declaration package where applicable, including the special VAT invoice data, export freight and insurance documents, and the STA export tax rebate declaration file

China mandates the issuance of VAT invoices through the STA-controlled electronic invoice service platform. For general taxpayers, special VAT invoices are issued with digitized encryption, unique invoice numbers, and electronic fiscal seals; invoice data are transmitted to STA in real time through the platform APIs or certified third-party interfaces. The fully digitalized electronic invoice (FD-E.Invoice) has replaced paper-only processing for most commercial transactions; every issuance, red-issuance and voiding is cleared online to prevent fraudulent duplication. Export customs declarations are transmitted electronically from GAC to STA through the China International Trade Single Window, enabling automatic cross-matching of export invoices, declaration data, logistics documents and foreign-exchange receipts.

The STA and GAC jointly audit export zero-rating claims using cross-matched customs, invoice, logistics and foreign-exchange bank data. If the export basis is unsubstantiated, proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day deadline, documents are forged, or the purported exporter did not actually own or export the goods, the zero-rating is denied or retrospectively clawed back. The tax authority will assess VAT at the standard 13% rate on the export value, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and levy administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid or refund fraudulently obtained. Where the conduct meets criminal thresholds under the PRC Criminal Law for tax evasion or fraudulent export refund, responsible individuals and entities are subject to criminal prosecution, confiscation and imprisonment. Additionally, SAFE may impose foreign-exchange administrative sanctions for late or false export-proceeds repatriation declarations.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese yuan (RMB) is convertible on current account for genuine goods-trade transactions, provided the resident WFOE submits authentic commercial contracts, invoices, customs declarations and balance-of-payments reports through a licensed domestic bank. Capital account items remain controlled. Inbound equity injections require FDI registration with SAFE, opening of a dedicated FDI capital account, and RMB settlement only for verified operating purposes consistent with the PRC Foreign Investment Negative List. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted only after the annual statutory audit, PRC corporate income tax filing and payment, board approval of a dividend distribution, and confirmation that capital preservation requirements are met. There is no mandatory foreign exchange surrender ratio; the WFOE may retain export proceeds in a designated foreign exchange account or convert them to RMB voluntarily. The main operational hurdles are the burden of proof for authenticity, SAFE monitoring of delayed receipts, and restrictions on speculative cross-border derivative transactions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods-trade administration, an A-class enterprise must receive export proceeds through licensed bank channels within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration; overdue collections require an additional report in the SAFE trade monitoring system. There is currently no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; export foreign currency proceeds may be retained in a designated foreign exchange account or sold to the bank at the WFOE's discretion. B/C-class enterprises are subject to stricter per-transaction verification and shorter or enhanced compliance timelines.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) governs the Hong Kong offshore clearing layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low or non-viable. Overseas fintech EMI providers are not licensed banks in mainland China and cannot legally open settlement accounts for a PRC WFOE, perform RMB clearing, or handle SAFE reporting. Such EMIs do not hold a PBOC payment institution license, and relying on them for onshore CNY or cross-border trade settlement would create material regulatory and bank-account-freeze risk. The Hong Kong treasury parent may use a licensed Hong Kong virtual or traditional bank as an offshore collection wallet, but all funds must ultimately enter the WFOE through a PRC-tier-one bank with proper FDI or goods-trade purpose codes.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent remits equity or shareholder loan funds from its licensed HK bank to the WFOE's designated FDI capital account at a mainland tier-one bank. The incoming wire must reference the SAFE FDI registration number and use the bank-assigned SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code for foreign direct investment equity. Upon receipt, the bank performs foreign exchange authenticity verification, applies suitability checks and, after SAFE-related reporting, permits RMB settlement of the capital item only against verified business expenses. A PRC certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report; subsequent disbursements are cleared through the RMB basic account under the bank-monitored payment purpose list.For imports and re-exports of containerized bitumen and asphalt products, clearing rails include SWIFT MT103 customer credit transfers for direct telegraphic transfers, SWIFT MT700 letter of credit instruments for secured trade settlements, and documentary collections using SWIFT MT400 and related collection messages. RMB cross-border settlements should be routed through CIPS, while USD and HKD payments clear through SWIFT coupled with CHIPS/Fedwire or the HKD local RTGS system. Turnover: In accordance with PRC Company Law Article 164 and the Accounting Law of the People's Republic of China, every Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China, regardless of balance sheet size, annual turnover, or headcount, is statutorily obliged to undergo an independent annual audit conducted by a licensed Chinese Certified Public Accountant (CPA) firm. There is no de minimis exemption for zero-revenue SPVs, shell structures, or entities with containerized trading operations such as ISIC 4661 bitumen and asphalt wholesale. Additionally, the audit mandate escalates if the WFOE reaches a controlled transaction threshold, as evidenced by Related-Party Transaction Reporting (revenue > RMB 10 million) or if the entity holds a special trading license.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File mandatory if: annual related-party tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200 million (including bitumen sales); intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File required when the group's annual consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion. Country-by-Country Report required if the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: The Chinese WFOE must maintain real operational substance in China—permanent office, employees, management functions, and decision-making authority—to justify its taxable residency and profit allocation. The Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits must satisfy the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations (if applicable to its activities) by retaining adequate staff and premises in Hong Kong. A Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is essential: the WFOE's CIGA are the procurement, storage, logistics, and wholesale distribution of bitumen; if these are actually exercised in Hong Kong or elsewhere, the profit allocation may be challenged. Artificial substance arrangements will be disregarded under the general anti-avoidance rule, leading to recharacterization and potential 10% WHT instead of 5%.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC; Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. The tax authority will recover the underpaid tax plus a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded amount. Where the evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and accounts for more than 10% of the total tax payable, criminal liability attaches.

Director personal liability: Directors who plan or approve the evasion are jointly and severally liable to pay the taxes and penalties out of personal assets; they may be placed on a travel blacklist and barred from leaving China; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment from 3 to 7 years for major cases, plus substantial fines.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 114 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (State Administration of Taxation Announcement No. 6 of 2017), the tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment and impose an interest surcharge of up to 5 percentage points above the benchmark lending rate, plus a penalty of up to 50% of the underpaid tax if the taxpayer fails to provide documentary evidence or reasonable explanation.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the resulting additional tax and interest to the extent of company assets; if they knowingly participated in the pricing manipulation, they may face civil restitution orders and travel bans. If the documentation is falsified, criminal penalties for false invoices or tax fraud may apply, including imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Failure to Withhold Dividend Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 39 and 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, the WFOE as withholding agent is liable for the withheld tax and a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld amount. If the under-withholding leads to an underpayment of tax and constitutes tax evasion, Criminal Law Article 201 may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who directed or neglected the withholding obligation may be held personally liable for the tax and penalties if the company cannot pay. They face blacklisting, travel restrictions, and—if the conduct amounts to intentional evasion—criminal prosecution carrying imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines.
