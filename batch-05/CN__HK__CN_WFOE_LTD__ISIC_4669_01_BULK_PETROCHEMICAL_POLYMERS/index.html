Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

Virtual office and commercial mailbox arrangements are not permissible as the registered office of a Mainland China WFOE engaged in the wholesale distribution of virgin polyethylene and polypropylene granules. The PRC Company Law and the Regulations on the Administration of Company Registration require a fixed and actual business premises located within the same administrative region as the SAMR registration authority, with physical signage, personnel, and equipment. The local tax authority will reject a virtual address for VAT taxpayer registration and invoice quota issuance, and commercial banks will reject a virtual mailbox for opening a basic deposit account, RMB settlement account, or foreign exchange current account. A serviced office with a dedicated physical office, real lease agreement, rent invoice, property ownership certificate, and staff may be accepted only if the WFOE has genuine business substance; a pure mailing address without personnel and business operations will not be accepted.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for a wholesale-distribution WFOE under the PRC Company Law. The sole shareholder fixes the registered capital in the Articles of Association and must pay the subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of establishment, or within any shorter period prescribed by the Articles. There is no blocked escrow or capital deposit requirement at formation; however, any inbound capital remittance must be routed through a bank-supervised foreign direct investment capital account and reported as paid-in capital to SAFE and MOFCOM.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with the central public portal at www.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) contained in the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation. Under the one-license-one-code reform, the USCC is automatically transmitted to the State Taxation Administration and constitutes both the Enterprise Income Tax registration number and Value-Added Tax registration number. The WFOE must complete the supplementary tax registration through the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau, verify its tax type and filing cycle, appoint the statutory legal representative/financial officer, and maintain bank and customs records under the same USCC. A foreign parent/holding company that is not trading in China does not require a separate Chinese TIN; however, if it derives China-source income or is required to self-apply, it must obtain a non-resident enterprise tax registration reference number through the competent tax service bureau via the e-tax portal. Required supporting materials include articles of association of the WFOE, the business license and USCC certificate, board resolutions approving the establishment, identity and corporate registration documents of the foreign investor, proof of registered address, banking account opening confirmation, and authorization letters for the tax filing agent.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All newly incorporated WFOEs must be tax-registered from incorporation; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000 in a consecutive 12-month period. Entities below that threshold may be classified as small-scale taxpayers but remain VAT taxpayers.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exports of goods from the PRC customs territory, including sales from Mainland China to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, qualify for the VAT zero rate under the PRC Export Tax Rebate (Zero-Rate) regime. The WFOE must be a registered VAT general taxpayer, complete the export tax refund qualification filing with the STA, physically export the goods through a PRC customs port, obtain customs release and electronic export declaration data, issue the corresponding export VAT invoice and commercial invoices, collect the export proceeds from an overseas counterparty through the banking system, and maintain a complete documentary trail evidencing the export transaction. Pure cross-border paperwork or offshore invoicing without physical export does not qualify.

  • Customs Export Declaration (statutory export customs entry and electronic release data from the General Administration of Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (ocean or multimodal transport bill evidencing export shipment from PRC port to destination)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or PRC bank export-proceeds receipt confirmation issued through the SAFE banking system
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE for the export sale
  • Packing list
  • Export sales contract and supporting letter of credit or cross-border sales order documentation
  • VAT special purchase invoice(s) for the exported petrochemical polymer goods where input VAT refund is claimed
  • Electronic export tax refund declaration filed via the State Taxation Administration Export Tax Refund module

Under the STA Golden Tax Phase IV supervisory architecture, all VAT invoices must be generated through the Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform or STA-approved invoicing systems and transmitted electronically to the tax authority in real time. Full Digitalized E-Invoices carry a unique invoice code, a tax-authority digital signature, and machine-readable security features; general taxpayers issuing Special VAT Invoices must integrate their enterprise resource planning systems with the platform through approved APIs or electronic invoicing interfaces. Anti-fraud digital stamping and risk scoring are mandatory, and invoices failing real-time validation, customs matching, or foreign-exchange reconciliation are automatically blocked from further use.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct cross-referenced audit scrutiny matching customs export declarations, logistics manifests, VAT invoice data, and foreign-exchange receipt records. If a zero-rated export cannot be substantiated, the VAT output tax at 13% is retrospectively clawed back, with daily late-payment surcharges under the Tax Administration and Collection Law, administrative fines ranging from 50% to five times the amount of tax or refund involved, and suspension or cancellation of export tax refund eligibility. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may constitute criminal offenses under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, carrying additional monetary fines and custodial penalties, with no statute of limitation for recovery in cases of deliberate fraud or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with segmented convertibility. Current account transactions, including trade in bulk polyethylene and polypropylene granules, are largely convertible: the WFOE may collect export proceeds in foreign currency or RMB and pay import bills through a SAFE-designated commercial bank upon presentation of genuine underlying trade documentation, including contracts, invoices, customs declarations, transport documents and tax records. Capital account convertibility remains restricted and requires pre-approval or registration. Inbound equity funding from the Hong Kong parent must be registered under the SAFE FDI regime and credited only to a dedicated capital account at a licensed Chinese bank. Such funds cannot be used for speculative RMB products, repayment of domestic RMB loans, or non-business outflows. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permitted only after the WFOE has completed enterprise income tax annual filing, appropriated statutory reserves, obtained audited PRC GAAP financial statements and passed a board resolution declaring lawful distributable profits. In practice, RMB is convertible on current-account items with documentary transparency, while equity, debt, dividends and hedging flows demand strict SAFE filing, transaction-specific purpose codes and bank-level pre-screening. Any attempt to characterise capital-account flows as ordinary trade will trigger rejection, suspension or regulatory penalty.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, export foreign-currency receipts from bulk polymer sales must be received by the mainland WFOE through a domestic bank unless SAFE has approved offshore retention. There is currently no mandatory fixed conversion ratio: 0% of the foreign currency must be forced into RMB, and the exporter may choose to keep the proceeds in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert them to RMB at the onshore market rate. The statutory obligation is repatriation without unjustified delay after trade settlement maturity; in banking practice the collection notification and bank reconciliation should be completed within 180 days of customs export declaration unless a longer sales-contract credit period is documented. The WFOE must declare each receipt on SAFE's cross-border receipts and payments reporting system and cannot use export proceeds to repay domestic RMB loans or fund capital-account transactions without separate regulatory approval.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI viability is rated Low for this mainland WFOE because Chinese law and SAFE/PBOC account rules require the WFOE's primary corporate accounts to be held at a licensed onshore commercial bank. Pure fintech EMIs are generally barred from operating RMB CNAPS settlement accounts, obtaining SAFE FDI registration authority, accepting export proceeds surrender, issuing trade settlement documents or acting as a statutory deposit bank for a PRC entity. An EMI may only support ancillary multicurrency virtual card and expense-rail functions if all underlying funds ultimately clear through a licensed Chinese bank account. The treasury structure must therefore place a tier-1 domestic Chinese bank at the centre of all regulated cash flows, with any EMI used solely as a secondary spend overlay under the Chinese bank's control.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital from the Hong Kong parent must be routed by SWIFT MT103 or, if RMB is used, through CIPS to the WFOE's SAFE-designated FDI foreign exchange capital account at a tier-1 Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must contain the SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration number in the beneficiary reference, the remittance purpose code for foreign direct investment-equity subscription, and the receiving bank's dedicated capital account identifier so that funds are not booked into an ordinary operating account. On receipt, the bank verifies the amount against the SAFE registration, checks supporting board resolutions and share subscription certificates, and may require a PRC licensed CPA to issue a capital verification report if the constitutional documents or parent financing arrangements require it. Post-receipt, the WFOE must file the permitted use of capital documentation with SAFE, ensure the funds are applied only to allowed working capital, procurement or fixed asset purposes, and maintain a clean audit trail for future profit repatriation or capital reduction.Outbound trade disbursements for bulk polymer purchases should clear through the WFOE's Chinese banking platform using SWIFT MT103 for open-account maturities and documentary settlements, or through irrevocable letters of credit subject to UCP600 when supplier relationships warrant bank intermediation. For direct FX payments, the bank will require the SAFE cross-border payments declaration, commercial contract, invoice, customs declaration and proof of underlying trade; for RMB settlement, CIPS provides the clearing rail into the beneficiary bank's CNAPS account. Documentary collections under URC522 (D/P and D/A) are suitable for trusted counterparties where LC negotiation is commercially unnecessary.
