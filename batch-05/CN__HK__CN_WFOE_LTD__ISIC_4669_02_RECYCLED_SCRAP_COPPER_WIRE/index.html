Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; official online electronic portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform, accessible under the Unified Social Credit Code. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTAX portal govern the separate Hong Kong profits tax registration of the Hong Kong intermediate entity, but do not govern the PRC WFOE's tax registration.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE, the statutory tax identification number is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license. Step 1: Complete market entity registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the Foreign Investment Law, obtaining the business license and USCC. Step 2: Within 30 days of establishment, complete tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau; no separate TIN application is required because the USCC is cross-recognised as the taxpayer identification number. Step 3: Submit the WFOE's articles of association, shareholder identity documents, legal representative identification, principal place of business proof, bank account details, and financial accounting system information to finalise the tax registration and VAT taxpayer classification. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a PRC establishment that derives China-source income, a non-resident tax registration file is opened with the STA for withholding purposes; the PRC WFOE itself is a resident taxpayer and cannot use the Hong Kong parent's tax identification.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC-resident WFOE is taxed on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment; no default territorial exemption; no tax-exempt-zone classification is assumed absent a specific and approved preferential zone designation).

Available election forms

None under Chinese corporate income tax for check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections. For VAT purposes, a WFOE may elect simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment if expected annual taxable sales are below RMB 5,000,000, or may apply for General VAT taxpayer status to obtain input VAT credit and export refund/zero-rating; no other elective regimes apply.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory General VAT taxpayer registration threshold; below this level the entity may register as a small-scale VAT taxpayer, but a WFOE engaged in continuous cross-border physical consignment of scrap copper wire should register as a General VAT taxpayer to claim input VAT credit and export zero-rating. Hong Kong has no VAT-registration threshold because no VAT/GST is levied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC General VAT taxpayer may apply zero-rating with refund of input VAT on goods that are physically exported from China's customs territory and properly customs-declared. Eligibility requires: (1) the goods leave China and the export customs declaration is electronically matched; (2) the sale is made to a non-resident buyer and export proceeds are collected in foreign exchange; (3) the proceeds are repatriated to and recognised in China within the statutory period; (4) for recycled scrap copper wire, the WFOE must hold the relevant recycling business qualification, scrap-commodity export licence, and commodity inspection documentation; and (5) the export VAT refund declaration is filed through the e-Tax export rebate module with all transport, customs, invoice, and payment evidence. Deliveries from mainland China to Hong Kong qualify as exports only when the goods are formally declared at a mainland PRC customs point and actually leave the PRC customs territory.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) for Export Goods
  • Original Bill of Lading (or Sea Waybill / Air Waybill as applicable)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / bank credit advice evidencing receipt of export foreign exchange proceeds
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE
  • Packing list and weight/scale certificate for shredded non-ferrous scrap
  • Export contract or sales confirmation (Documentary Letter of Credit or Swift T/T terms)

  • Inspection certificate (CCIC/CIQ) for copper scrap quality and environmental compliance

The WFOE must issue electronic VAT invoices through the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform using a tax control UKey or the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice system. Invoice data are transmitted in real time to the STA and matched with China Customs export declarations. Mandated fiscal controls include anti-counterfeiting QR codes, unique invoice numbers, red-letter negative invoice processing for corrections, and Golden Tax Project Phase IV data inspection integrating customs, foreign exchange, banking, transportation, and logistics records.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims by cross-matching customs export declarations, electronic VAT invoices, transport documents, and foreign exchange receipts. If any document is missing, inconsistent, or unverified by the close of the 180-day repatriation deadline, the zero-rated treatment is reversed, the claimed input VAT refund is clawed back, and output VAT plus late-payment interest and daily surcharges become payable. Under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, administrative penalties range from 50% to 5 times the tax involved; under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, fraudulent export tax refunds may lead to imprisonment and fines, with the most severe cases punishable by life imprisonment; the WFOE may also be downgraded in the SAFE trade foreign exchange category and lose export tax refund qualifications.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility but strict capital-account controls. A China-incorporated WFOE is subject to these controls even when owning Hong Kong parentage. Export trade receipts may be credited to a domestic foreign-currency settlement account or converted into RMB, but the bank must verify each transaction's genuine trade background through contract, invoice, customs declarations and logistics evidence. Capital inflows, such as equity injections from the Hong Kong parent, must be registered with SAFE, held in a dedicated foreign-exchange capital account, and converted into RMB only after the receiving bank has checked the intended purpose against the PRC negative list. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current-account outflow and is allowed only after annual audits, corporate income tax filing and proof of after-tax retained profits; foreign-exchange banks also require the WFOE's tax documentation under the Hong Kong-China double tax arrangement. Practical hurdles include the absence of punitive surrender quotas, but an entity will still face bank-level KYC/EDD, documentation of real trade flows and SAFE monitoring of overdue export collections and investment-fund deployment. These requirements make treasury operations a compliance-critical function rather than a pure cash-management activity.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 reform, China no longer imposes a fixed mandatory surrender ratio on export proceeds; the statutory mandatory conversion ratio is effectively 0%. Export proceeds must still be repatriated into the PRC and credited to a current-account foreign-exchange settlement account maintained with a licensed bank, and the receipt must be matched with customs declaration data within the monitoring cycle. Where export collections remain overdue, generally beyond 90 days from customs declaration without justification, the WFOE must provide written explanation to the bank/SAFE and may be subject to enhanced supervision. The bank calculates the enterprise's current-account foreign-exchange account ceiling and any amount exceeding that ceiling must be converted into RMB promptly. Advance collections for future exports are subject to matching shipment evidence; if the goods are not exported within the monitoring period, refund or re-characterisation is required.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as its authorised foreign-exchange administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability is very low and, in practical legal terms, barred for the onshore WFOE. The PBOC and SAFE require corporate foreign-currency accounts and foreign-exchange settlement to be executed through licensed domestic banks; offshore digital EMIs do not hold a PRC banking licence, cannot obtain SAFE settlement-bank capacity, and cannot host a WFOE's capital account or receive export proceeds under the goods-trade reconciliation system. They should not be used as the primary treasury rail and their involvement is limited to ancillary non-PRC collection purposes where the counterparty bank fully identifies the WFOE.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must ensure the WFOE holds an SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account (FDI capital account) before remitting funds. The transfer should be sent by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's bank, with the beneficiary being the WFOE, the remitter being the HK parent, and the payment narrative stating 'equity contribution / capital injection under FDI'. The bank will classify the purpose as 'Direct Investment - inward equity capital' in the SAFE cross-border reporting module and attach the entity's SAFE FDI registration reference. After receipt, if conversion to RMB is required, the receiving bank applies the onshore spot rate and records the settlement against the approved payment purpose. The bank will perform SAFE capital-account receipt/use recording; where licences require proof of paid-in capital, a PRC certified public accountant should issue a capital verification report. Converted RMB may not be used for prohibited negative-list activities, off-plan real estate, security lending or other non-business purposes without SAFE permission.For outbound trade payments (e.g., purchase of shredded copper wire from overseas suppliers), the WFOE must maintain a goods-trade current-account foreign-exchange capability and customs/commerce import rights. Primary rails are: SWIFT MT103 T/T against verified invoices with underlying import contracts and customs declarations; Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) under UCP600 for high-value or relationship-building shipments; and Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for well-established suppliers where trust and credit risk are managed. All payments must match the declared customs value, weight, grade and currency; changes in price beyond bank screening tolerance need supporting addenda. The bank will require the import declaration number, contract/invoice, and the relevant SAFE purpose code before releasing FX. Export settlement received into China may be used within current-account limits; any hard-currency disbursement outside the WFOE's account ceiling must be settled in RMB and re-converted only on a real trade requirement.Onshore deliverable CNY foreign-exchange forwards (USD/CNY, HKD/CNY, EUR/CNY) with a SAFE-licensed bank, supported by genuine invoice/contract/trade exposure and maturity aligned to expected settlement cashflows.Plain-vanilla FX swaps and cross-currency swaps for asset-liability or working-capital hedging, limited to notional reflecting actual capital/trade exposure and not speculative cycles.Currency options (vanilla call/put) onshore for hedging real current or capital account positions, with approval/booking through the bank's derivative pre-trade assessment.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible for the PRC WFOE; a CNH NDF may only be used in the offshore Hong Kong treasury of the parent as a consolidated hedge overlay, provided no onshore settlement or capital outflow is involved.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, the WFOE must prepare a contemporaneous local file if it has related-party tangible-asset transfers above RMB 200 million, intangible-asset or financial-asset transfers above RMB 100 million, or service transactions above RMB 50 million in one year; a master file is mandatory where aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. The annual related-party transaction summary (Schedule A105000) must be included with every CIT return regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must itself carry on core income-generating activities in the PRC, including purchase negotiation, quality inspection of recycled copper, inventory management, invoicing, and risk assumption. The Hong Kong parent, if claiming treaty benefits, must satisfy the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations as a pure holding company or as an entity carrying on a relevant activity, and must retain custody, senior management decision-making, and control in Hong Kong. In both jurisdictions a paper structure without physical presence, real employees, local records, or actual managerial functions can be recharacterized as substance-less under PRC CIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and denied DTA relief.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41, 47, and 48; TCAL Article 63. The tax authority may make an upward transfer-pricing adjustment, impose a benchmark interest surcharge plus an additional 5-percentage-point adjustment interest, and penalize underpaid tax by 50% to 500%. The attributable criminal offense is tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or cause the false intercompany pricing can be ordered by PRC courts to pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20, becoming personally liable for the WFOE's lost tax and restitution of diverted assets. They face asset freezes, cross-border travel bans/blacklisting, administrative exit restrictions, and criminal imprisonment of up to three years, increasing to three to seven years for especially large evasion amounts.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under PRC CIT Law Article 47 and the Mainland/Hong Kong DTA, the claim of treaty benefit is denied, the payment is recharacterized at the full 10% non-resident withholding tax, and underpaid tax is subject to TCAL Article 63 penalties; in Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Regulations impose fines and daily default penalties for non-compliant companies, and the Inland Revenue Department may revoke the Tax Residency Certificate and report directors for false declarations.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where a Hong Kong shell is used to perpetrate treaty abuse; directors of both the HK parent and the WFOE are jointly liable for unpaid withholding tax, interest, and penalties. Director-level consequences include personal asset confiscation, blacklisting and travel bans, disqualification as directors, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to seven years.
AML/Customs False Invoicing and Export Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC Customs Law provides seizure of goods, confiscation of unlawful gains, and fines that can reach multiples of the goods' value or evaded duties; PRC Criminal Law Articles 153 and 205 penalize smuggling and forgery/abuse of VAT special invoices; TCAL Article 63 applies for false entries; SAFE rules impose administrative foreign-exchange penalties on unlawful outward remittances.

Director personal liability: Directors and officers who direct, authorize, or knowingly tolerate false customs declarations, forged invoices, or structured payments are individually exposed to piercing of limited liability, civil damages to the tax/customs authorities, asset freezing, blacklisting and entry/exit bans, and imprisonment of up to 10 years in serious smuggling or VAT invoice fraud cases. Revocation of the WFOE does not extinguish the personal criminal responsibility of the actual controllers.
