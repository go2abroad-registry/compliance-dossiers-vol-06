Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None; the PRC Company Law no longer imposes a minimum registered capital for a WFOE engaged in wholesale trading or brokerage, and no blocked escrow account is required. However, under Article 47 of the PRC Company Law, the registered capital is the total amount subscribed by all shareholders, and the shareholders must pay their subscribed capital in full within five years from the date of issuance of the business license, or within such shorter period as the Articles of Association require. The hazardous chemical licensing authority may later request proof of operating capital or financial capability when issuing a Hazardous Chemical Business License, so the capital schedule should be realistic and supported by a board-approved cash flow plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic taxpayer portal is the China Electronic Tax Service Platform ('Electronic Tax Bureau / e-Tax') operated by provincial STA bureaus; customs and trade data are processed through the China International Trade Single Window operated by the General Administration of Customs (GACC).

E-government filing portal

Step 1 - Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) under the Foreign Investment Law and the Company Law with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued business license carries the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer number. Step 2 - Complete the integrated tax registration with the competent STA tax bureau through the e-Tax platform within 30 calendar days after the business license is issued; the tax authority confirms the USCC as the TIN, opens the tax file, and activates enterprise income tax, VAT, stamp duty and payroll obligations. Step 3 - Register as an export enterprise with GACC through the China International Trade Single Window and obtain the Customs Registration of the WFOE; this is required to file export declarations and to claim zero-rated VAT. Step 4 - Register with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and open a foreign currency settlement account with a designated bank for goods trade; the bank links the WFOE to SAFE's Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Step 5 - For a foreign non-resident entity without an establishment or place of business in China, the non-resident itself, its agent or its withholding agent must register the non-resident with the competent STA tax bureau under the Non-Resident Taxpayer Registration procedure to obtain a Non-Resident Tax Identification Number before any China-source payment is made; such registration does not per se create a permanent establishment.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not operate a US-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election regime. A WFOE is compulsorily taxable as a resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and as a general or small-scale VAT taxpayer under the VAT law; the only relevant election is voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer under STA rules where the annual taxable sales threshold has not been exceeded.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all newly incorporated WFOEs must complete tax registration within 30 days of business license issuance irrespective of turnover. A WFOE below the general taxpayer threshold is classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless general VAT taxpayer status is voluntarily elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT (export tax rebate/exemption) applies to physical goods exported from mainland China's customs territory, including exports to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, provided: (1) the WFOE is registered with GACC and STA; (2) the goods are actually shipped out of the customs territory under an approved export declaration; (3) the export sale is made to a non-mainland buyer and paid for in foreign currency or RMB settled through designated channels; (4) the export proceeds are received within the statutory repatriation deadline; and (5) the export tax refund is claimed within the statutory declaration period, normally the next VAT filing period after export or by 30 April of the following year for annualised claims. Qualifying cross-border services may also be zero-rated under the cross-border service zero-rating rules, but the sulfur mineral ore sourcing and trading operation is treated as a goods export.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Declaration Form for Exported Goods) bearing the 18-digit GACC customs entry number, with customs export records from the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading (original or electronic ocean Bill of Lading, or Master Air Waybill for air cargo) evidencing physical shipment from mainland China customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Proceeds Collection Certificate comprising the bank foreign exchange credit advice and the SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System export collection record

China operates a fully digital e-invoicing mandate under the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform and the Golden Tax Phase IV system. VAT special invoices are issued through the STA-controlled e-invoicing system using the digital tax control device ('Tax U-key') with real-time online transmission, digital encryption and automated compliance checks. Tax return data, invoice data, customs export declarations and foreign exchange collection records are automatically matched to detect anomalies; no physical fiscal stamp is required for fully digitalised e-invoices.

STA Golden Tax Phase IV, GACC and SAFE data triangulation may trigger automatic desk audits for any mismatch between invoice data, export declarations and foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is denied or the statutory documentation is not produced within the deadline, the export is recharacterised as a taxable domestic supply and output VAT is collected at 13%, with late payment interest accrued at 0.05% per day and administrative penalties under the Tax Administration Law. Where export refunds are fraudulently claimed, the tax authority claws back the refund and imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the amount of the refund; serious cases are referred for criminal prosecution for export tax fraud, and the clawback limitation period is unlimited for fraudulent evasion, otherwise 3 to 5 years for ordinary assessments.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China maintains a dual-track convertibility framework. RMB is broadly convertible for documented current-account items such as trade in goods, transportation, insurance, and service fees, but capital-account items, including FDI equity injections, shareholder loans, and repatriation of invested capital, remain subject to SAFE registration, bank verification, and genuine-deal authentication before any currency conversion is permitted. The WFOE may open a foreign-exchange capital account only after completing the SAFE foreign investment information registration. Any conversion from foreign currency to RMB must follow the actual-use and payment-verification principle; funds cannot be converted simply for treasury arbitrage or onshore speculation. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is legal but requires clearance of PRC enterprise income tax and dividend withholding tax, finalisation of the statutory annual audit, tax payment certificates, and a board resolution confirming distributable profit. Dividend remittances are treated as current-account outbound payments after tax settlement, but banks still enforce strict anti-money-laundering and authenticity checks under SAFE monitoring. Export proceeds must be repatriated into the onshore banking system and submitted to the Goods Trade Monitoring System for verification, yet the current rules do not impose a mandatory conversion ratio. Exporters are free to retain 100% of valid foreign-currency earnings in approved accounts or voluntarily sell 100% to banks. Practical convertibility is therefore high for verified trade flows but tightly ring-fenced against speculative capital movements, with SAFE watching each cross-border transaction through the cross-border payment monitoring platform.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to a PRC domestic bank through legally designated payment channels within the statutory collection-monitoring deadline, generally the 180-day window from export customs declaration, unless SAFE has approved a longer offshore-retention period. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under current reform: an exporter may retain up to 100% of repatriated proceeds in a permitted foreign-exchange account or voluntarily settle up to 100% into RMB at a licensed domestic bank. Every receipt must still pass trade-authenticity verification against customs declarations, commercial invoices, contracts, bills of lading, and logistics documents in the SAFE Goods Trade Monitoring System.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated foreign-exchange administrator, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and generally not viable for the onshore WFOE account structure. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to hold deposits, open RMB clearing accounts, perform foreign-exchange settlement, or act as receiving banks for FDI capital injections. PRC corporate banking law requires onshore deposit-taking, cross-border payments, and FX conversion to flow through domestically licensed banks connected to CNAPS, CIPS, SWIFT, and the SAFE collection-and-verification platform. An offshore EMI may only support ancillary non-onshore wallet services outside China; using it for the WFOE capital account or trade settlements would constitute unlicensed deposit-taking and create unacceptable cross-border opacity, triggering automatic AML rejection.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong shareholder must execute an SWIFT MT103 remittance in the remitting bank, with the beneficiary named as the PRC WFOE's FDI foreign-exchange capital account and the remittance reference citing the SAFE foreign investment information registration code and the designated direct-investment purpose code for equity capital injection into an onshore enterprise. Before application, the bank must complete SAFE registration, open the capital account, and confirm the payment path through its correspondent chain. After receipt, the bank verifies remitter, registration code, funding purpose, and supporting capital-contribution resolution; where local practice requires it, a PRC-certified public accountant issues a capital verification report. Conversion from foreign currency into RMB must then be executed only for actual operating obligations and settled directly to third-party suppliers or payroll, with no conversion into the company's own RMB account unless expressly permitted by the SAFE collection-and-payment system.For foreign-currency supplier payments outside China, the WFOE will typically use SWIFT MT103 direct remittances against invoices and logistics documents. For bulk dry-bulk sulfur cargoes, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the Chinese bank from its USD or RMB facility is preferred, with payment at sight or on a usance basis upon presentation of clean on-board bills of lading, commercial invoices, inspection certificates, insurance certificates, and certificates of origin. Documentary collections, both documents against payment and documents against acceptance, remain acceptable for low-risk recurring counterparties but expose the client to non-bank settlement risk. Cross-border RMB disbursements can be cleared through CIPS with SWIFT-compatible messaging, and the bank applies trade-authenticity checks in the Goods Trade Monitoring System before releasing funds.Onshore RMB foreign-exchange forward contracts for trade-related cash flows, requiring a genuine and verified underlying trade exposure and a SAFE-licensed bank counterparty.Domestic foreign-exchange swaps for liquidity positioning on committed trade payments and export proceeds, documented against a real trade pipeline and forbidding speculative open positions.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps only when approved by SAFE and PBOC and supported by an actual cross-border loan or receivable, not for speculative gains.Non-deliverable forwards may be executed only offshore by the Hong Kong shareholder or a non-resident counterparty; the onshore PRC WFOE cannot use NDFs because such instruments fail the genuine-trade-background requirement and are non-deliverable inside mainland China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42: annual related-party tangible goods transfer exceeding RMB 400 million; intangible property, financial asset transfers or loans exceeding RMB 100 million; other related-party transactions (services, fees) exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File is mandatory where the total annual related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China does not enact offshore-style Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), but the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) and the DTA beneficial ownership requirement demand genuine commercial substance. Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) analysis is applied to test whether the Hong Kong shareholder performs real business functions; a shell HK entity with no office, employees or decision-making will be denied treaty benefits, and profits may be re-attributed to the Chinese WFOE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Transfer Mispricing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63: confiscation of evaded tax plus a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount, plus daily late-payment surcharges and administrative compulsory collection. Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion conviction for amounts above RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of taxable income, punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who designed or approved the mispricing are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of the evaded tax; courts may lift the corporate veil to reach personal assets; the directors face travel bans and blacklisting as serious untrustworthy tax debtors, and personal criminal imprisonment up to 7 years for tax evasion fraud.
DTA Abuse - Shell Conduit and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under PRC CIT Law Article 3 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules; WHT is re-assessed at the statutory 10% rate (or 10% for royalties) on all outbound income, with late surcharges, interest and a fine of 50% to 500% of the under-withheld amount under Tax Administration Law Article 63; the arrangement may be recharacterized in full.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial ownership certificates face personal civil liability for the resulting tax shortfall, piercing of the corporate veil, freezing of personal assets, travel bans and blacklisting; criminal liability for fraudulent treaty incentive claims can result in imprisonment of up to 7 years.
Cross-Border Withholding Tax Evasion and Unlawful Profit Strip

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 69: the withholding agent must pay the un-withheld tax and a penalty of 50% to 300% of the un-withheld amount; the tax authority may levy compulsory enforcement on bank deposits and property, and refer the case for AML or criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the distribution or signed the payment orders are personally liable for the corporate withholding obligation under veil-piercing doctrine; they face civil asset restitution, exit restrictions until the tax arrears are settled, blacklisting, and criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years for defrauding the state tax administration.
