Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal is the National Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) together with provincial, municipal, and county-level tax service offices. For the Hong Kong SAR limb present in this structure, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD) administers a separate Business Registration and Profits Tax regime, but the PRC WFOE is registered with the STA.

E-government filing portal

After the Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the WFOE business license, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The WFOE must complete initial tax registration within 30 days through the National Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax), filing enterprise income tax, VAT, customs-related data, legal representative, bank account, and accounting records. The Hong Kong non-resident investor should obtain a Hong Kong Certificate of Residence from the IRD to support treaty benefits for dividend withholding relief. If a Hong Kong-incorporated entity itself conducts a taxable business in China without establishing a permanent establishment, the Chinese payer must register the non-resident enterprise income tax withholding using the foreign entity's tax identification, and the STA will assign a non-resident taxpayer reference number where no foreign TIN exists. For the PRC WFOE itself, the USCC serves as the TIN for all China tax filings, and no separate VAT registration number is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. China has no check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections for a WFOE. The WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed at entity level under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law on worldwide income. The only statutory status election available is the VAT small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer method, which is not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must complete tax registration upon incorporation; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered by annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million). Below that threshold, the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to simplified collection rates.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from China, including goods supplied to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, may be zero-rated at 0% VAT with a refund of input credits if the WFOE is the customs exporter, the goods leave China customs territory, the foreign buyer is a non-resident, title passes offshore, and the export declaration is lodged through the China International Trade Single Window. Purely offshore re-invoicing where goods never enter China customs is not a taxable supply in China; however, any brokerage, re-invoicing margin, or intermediary service performed within China is generally taxable at 6% unless it qualifies for the cross-border service zero-rating or exemption supplied wholly outside China under STA schedules.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration) filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal/airway transport document evidencing physical export from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or export foreign-exchange collection receipt issued by a designated bank
  • Export sales contract, commercial invoice, and packing list matched to the customs declaration
  • VAT export invoice and export tax refund calculation schedule submitted through the E-Tax export refund platform
  • Foreign exchange repatriation record linked to the customs declaration and STA export database

China mandates digital-format invoicing under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. The WFOE must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) through the National Electronic Tax Bureau E-Tax VAT invoice module; invoice data are transmitted in near-real-time to the STA and each invoice carries a unique encrypted invoice code and digital anti-fraud stamp. The system reconciles invoice data with customs declarations through the China International Trade Single Window and with foreign exchange collection reports received from designated banks. API integration is mandatory for high-volume issuers. General VAT taxpayers must use the special VAT invoice platform to claim input-VAT credits, and export invoices must match the customs export declaration data before a zero-rating or export-refund claim is accepted.

The STA, Customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) jointly verify zero-rated export claims. Failure to produce the required customs declaration, transport document, or foreign-exchange collection proof, or failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, requires the exporter to repay or claw back any VAT refund already claimed, plus late-payment surcharges at 0.05% per day on the amount clawed back. Fraudulent, fictitious, or unsubstantiated export zero-rating results in penalties of one to five times the refunded tax, administrative fines, suspension or loss of export tax-refund qualification, and criminal liability under PRC law, including imprisonment for significant fraudulent export tax-refund schemes.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with full current-account convertibility for trade-related payments, but strict capital-account controls that directly affect a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE). A WFOE is considered a resident enterprise for foreign exchange purposes, and its registered capital injection from the overseas parent is a capital-account transaction requiring SAFE foreign direct investment (FDI) registration before the domestic designated bank can credit the funds. Once the capital is settled into RMB under the discretionary settlement mechanism, the funds must be used for verified operational purposes such as payroll, raw material procurement, overheads, and genuine re-invoicing trade settlements. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are treated as current-account payments only after the WFOE completes corporate income tax final settlement, obtains an audit by a licensed Chinese CPA, and provides the bank with the tax payment certification, audited financial statements, and a board resolution approving the dividend distribution. The re-invoicing hub model creates an inherent exposure to balance-of-payments mismatch because goods flow from mainland suppliers to third-party buyers may not cross the Hong Kong border, while contract title, payment flow, and logistics documents do. As a result, SAFE and the banking compliance committee will apply a look-through test comparing customs declarations, bills of lading, warehouse receipts, and the WFOE's trade-in-foreign-exchange monitoring records. Any inconsistency between the declared re-invoicing scope and the actual shipment data can trigger delayed FX settlement, downgraded bank risk rating, or mandatory surrender of the disputed transaction. The RMB remains only partially convertible for capital account purposes; cross-border outbound investments, loan guarantees, and intercompany financing beyond the registered equity capital require additional SAFE approvals, making the WFOE's onshore treasury operations highly sensitive to regulatory interpretation of 'genuine trade.'

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods exports, the WFOE must repatriate all export foreign currency receipts into its domestic foreign exchange settlement account, but there is no mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may freely retain the foreign currency or voluntarily settle it into RMB at a designated bank. Under the SAFE trade-in-foreign-exchange monitoring system, export receipts must be collected, on a statutory general timeline, within 90 days after the date of export customs declaration for standard general trade. If the actual payment term exceeds 90 days, the WFOE must apply for a registration/extension through the bank or SAFE with supporting sales contracts, letters of credit, or non-payment certification. The proceeds are subject to electronic matching with the export customs declaration, and any unrepatriated amount beyond the permitted window will trigger a risk-limit downgrade and intensify mandatory KYC and AML inspection.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as global digital banks or payment platforms without a PBOC-issued banking license are legally barred from opening deposit or clearing accounts for a China-domiciled WFOE. The PBOC and SAFE restrict non-bank payment institutions from handling capital-account transactions, and offshore EMIs cannot access local clearing rails (CNAPS, CIPS, or local RTGS) on behalf of a domestic entity. Only licensed Chinese payment institutions (e.g., Alipay, Tenpay) may participate in limited cross-border RMB settlement pilots for goods trade, but these are supplementary collection tools, not treasury accounts. Therefore, the viability of using fintech EMIs as the primary cross-border liquidity rail for the WFOE is materially low, and the treasury blueprint must rely exclusively on the domestic Tier-1 commercial banking channel.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent shall initiate the equity capital injection via SWIFT MT103 from a Hong Kong licensed bank to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account at the domestic designated Tier-1 bank. The remittance must include the SAFE-approved foreign exchange purpose code for inbound direct investment registered capital, along with the SAFE FDI registration certificate number issued to the WFOE. Upon receipt, the domestic bank will await the certified capital verification report from a Chinese licensed CPA and then record the funds in the WFOE's capital account ledger. The WFOE may convert the capital into RMB using SAFE's discretionary settlement mechanism, followed by the submission of a capital payment instruction detailing the intended use; the bank will verify that each funded expenditure matches the permitted operational scope and that no funds are diverted to prohibited items such as securities investment, real estate development, or intra-group lending.For outbound disbursements to mainland suppliers in RMB, the WFOE will use the domestic local clearing system (CNAPS) or CIPS for cross-border RMB payments to the Hong Kong counterparty. For foreign-currency outflows to non-mainland sellers, the WFOE will initiate SWIFT MT103 wire transfers from its trade settlement account, with each payment annotated with the correct balance-of-payments trade monitoring code for transit trade or re-export. For larger and more structured transactions, the WFOE may issue irrevocable documentary letters of credit, to be advised and confirmed through the Hong Kong parent's bank, and documentary collections on DP/DA terms.
