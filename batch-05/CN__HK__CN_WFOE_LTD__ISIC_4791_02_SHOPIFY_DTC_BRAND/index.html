Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None under Hong Kong law. A private company limited by shares may be incorporated with no minimum paid-up capital; the subscriber shareholders may allot one or more ordinary shares of no par value or of HKD 1.00 each, and no blocked escrow capital account is required. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal is the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax platform), which supports mainland China tax registration, filings, invoice management and export tax refund administration. Hong Kong SAR has a separate tax system and is not the registration authority for the CN WFOE LTD.

E-government filing portal

Because the CN WFOE LTD is a wholly foreign-owned limited liability company incorporated in mainland China, the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The foreign parent must incorporate the WFOE with SAMR and then complete China tax registration. Under the integrated one-time registration procedure, SAMR registration data is automatically pushed to the competent STA office; however, within 30 days of business establishment (or immediately when required under the electronic workflow), the WFOE or its legal representative must access the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal, file the Tax Registration Information Form, and upload the business license, articles of association, legal representative identification, beneficial-owner and shareholder information, accounting system details, and bank account opening certificate. The STA then activates the USCC as the taxpayer and VAT registration number, defines the taxpayer classification (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), creates the taxpayer administration jurisdiction, and issues an electronic tax registration certificate. Non-resident entities without a PRC permanent establishment generally do not receive a local TIN; their China-source income is handled through withholding at the payor level, and they may be required to make a non-resident tax registration with the withholding agent's tax authority.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; PRC law does not provide pass-through or check-the-box elections for a limited liability company, and the WFOE is mandatorily treated as a separate resident enterprise for Corporate Income Tax. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales; all PRC business taxpayers must complete VAT registration upon incorporation, and once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 the taxpayer is mandatorily classified as a general VAT taxpayer. Below the threshold the taxpayer may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer or elect general status to claim input VAT credits.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of physical goods from mainland China by the WFOE is eligible for 0% VAT with an input-VAT refund (export tax rebate) when the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods have actually left China, are declared in the WFOE's own name in a customs export declaration, and the sale is evidenced by export contracts or orders, commercial invoices, transport documents to the offshore buyer, and foreign-currency receipt from outside China. Cross-border e-commerce retail export (DTC brand sales) under the customs list-verification and summary-declaration model may also be zero-rated if the WFOE is customs-registered, the goods are sold through a regulated e-commerce platform and payment channels (e.g., Shopify Payments, PayPal, Stripe), and the platform or third-party payment settlement records clearly evidence offshore buyers and remittances. Qualifying offshore digital services supplied to a foreign recipient (where value is consumed outside China and payment is received from abroad) may also qualify for zero-rating or exemption under the PRC VAT export policy only when the required documentary proof is maintained.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form showing the WFOE as exporter, including the cross-border e-commerce retail export list where applicable
  • Export sales contract or offshore consumer order records evidencing the DTC transaction
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill or other logistics document proving physical departure from China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing receipt from the offshore buyer
  • SAFE export-proceeds verification record or bank settlement proof where required by export-rebate review

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through STA-certified tax-control equipment or the National Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FD-EI) platform integrated into the National Electronic Tax Bureau. Under Golden Tax Project Phase IV, invoice data (including invoice amount, buyer and seller identity, tax rate and tax payment code) is submitted to the STA in real time through API interfaces; each invoice carries a unique digital serial number, encrypted anti-counterfeit stamp, and automatic quota control. Issuance is cleared by the tax authority's fiscal system before acceptance; export sales require linkage with the customs export declaration and foreign-exchange receipt records.

The STA, the General Administration of Customs, and SAFE share real-time data on export declarations, VAT invoices, logistics, and foreign-exchange receipts. Export zero-rating claims are scrutinized for consistency across all documents; where the export is unverified or fraudulent, the zero rating is denied, the input VAT refund is clawed back retrospectively, late-payment surcharges accrue at 0.05% per day, and an administrative fine of up to five times the VAT evaded or refunded may be imposed. Financial loss to the State from fraudulent export-refund claims also triggers criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law, including imprisonment and financial penalties, and may result in cancellation of the WFOE's export-tax-rebate qualification, customs registration downgrading, and public blacklisting.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime under which the renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current account transactions but remains subject to strict capital account controls. The WFOE may receive cross-border e-commerce sales revenue in foreign currency, declare the trade background to SAFE, and voluntarily convert it to RMB at a licensed bank at the market rate. There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds; the enterprise may retain up to 100% of eligible current-account FX in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account. Capital account items, including inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong parent, require SAFE registration, a dedicated foreign-currency capital account, and proof that any RMB settlement of capital funds is used only for permitted business purposes within the Foreign Investment Negative List. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted as a current-account profit transfer but is subject to an audited balance sheet, tax clearance certificate, board resolution, and verification of distributable profits; banks will also review the historical submission of annual SAFE FDI reports. For an e-commerce DTC brand, cross-border receipts routed via payment service providers must be repatriated to China through licensed settlement channels and reconciled against customs export declarations. Overall, operational liquidity enjoys convertibility under documentary controls, while capital movements are tightly monitored.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration, export proceeds from physical goods must be repatriated to China within 90 days after the customs export declaration date. Where a deferred payment exceeding the statutory term is contractually agreed, the exporter must register the deferred collection with SAFE before the due date; unregistered proceeds are subject to penalty. Once repatriated, proceeds may be credited to the enterprise's foreign-exchange settlement account; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, meaning 0% of proceeds must be surrendered and 100% may be retained in foreign currency until the enterprise elects to sell to a licensed bank. For cross-border e-commerce retail export, the same 90-day repatriation norm applies, with banks and licensed payment institutions using the aggregate e-commerce export declaration list and payment flow to verify the transaction.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low-to-limiting. Chinese law reserves banking activities, foreign-exchange settlement, and capital account management to licensed PRC commercial banks. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut, Wise Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are legally barred from acting as primary corporate banks for a PRC WFOE because they are not licensed banks or licensed foreign-exchange institutions inside China and cannot hold FDI capital, clear RMB, or service profit remittances. At most, some EMIs may operate as payment facilitators through a licensed PRC bank or a licensed domestic payment institution (e.g., LianLian Pay, PingPong) for the collection of cross-border e-commerce sales from Shopify, PayPal, and Stripe; funds must then be aggregated and settled to an onshore bank account under SAFE monitoring. The mandatory nexus is therefore a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank for core treasury, FX conversion, and capital transactions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File required when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 100,000,000 for tangible assets, RMB 10,000,000 for intangible assets, RMB 10,000,000 for financial/interest transactions, or RMB 40,000,000 for other related-party transactions; Master File generally required for group-wide annual related-party turnover above RMB 500,000,000.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must have genuine commercial substance in mainland China for its e-commerce and fulfillment operations. The HK parent must satisfy Hong Kong territorial-source principles and BEPS-aligned substance requirements in order to qualify for treaty benefits: substantive board meetings, decision-making, staff/office, bank accounts and contract execution in Hong Kong. Allocation of brand intangibles and Shopify customer databases between mainland China and Hong Kong must reflect actual control of risk and CIGA; otherwise PRC GAAR (EIT Law Article 47) and beneficial-ownership rules may re-characterise the structure, deny treaty relief and reallocate profit to the WFOE.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Omitting E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of unpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05%. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes up to three years’ imprisonment plus fines for ordinary evasion, and three to seven years plus heavy fines for evading a huge amount with an evasion ratio above 30%, with confiscation of assets.

Director personal liability: A PRC court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law where directors used corporate assets for personal purposes; directors are jointly and severally liable for unpaid taxes, penalties and restitution. The director is placed on the joint disciplinary credit-blacklist, enjoys no high-consumption travel, suffers property seizure, and is barred from leaving mainland China by immigration exit control. Convicted directors face disqualification from corporate office and actual imprisonment.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Partner Abuse of 5% DTA Dividend Rate

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) authorises the tax authority to disregard the form and re-characterise the arrangement; the 10% statutory WHT is re-imposed and related royalty payments may be disallowed. Under Tax Collection Law Article 66, false treaty relief claims are penalised by a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus interest.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign the treaty declaration knowing the HK entity lacks economic substance are personally exposed; veil-piercing liability extends to direct compensation of the WFOE’s tax deficiency. Travel bans and asset freezes apply when a criminal investigation or tax enforcement is pending, and a director may face criminal liability for evasive asset transfer under Criminal Law Article 203, with imprisonment up to seven years.
AML / Foreign Exchange Settlement Violations in Cross-border DTC Flows

Statutory sanction: Regulations of the People’s Republic of China on Foreign Exchange Administration Article 39 imposes a financial penalty of 5% to 30% of the illegal cross-border amount, with any illegal foreign exchange funds confiscated; serious illegal foreign exchange operations fall under Criminal Law Article 225, punishable by imprisonment up to five years and fines, with heavier sentences for extremely serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who arrange or approve the split settlement schemes are personally liable for the unreturned foreign currency and related damages to the WFOE; SAFE blacklists the individuals and imposes exit/entry travel restrictions. The criminal court may order civil restitution of the illegal foreign exchange plus penalties, prohibit the director from acting as a director or senior manager for up to five years, and impose a term of imprisonment for serious money-laundering or illegal foreign exchange business.
