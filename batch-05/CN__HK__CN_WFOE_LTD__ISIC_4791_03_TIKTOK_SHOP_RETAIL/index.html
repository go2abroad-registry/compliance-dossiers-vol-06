Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the official STA website at www.chinatax.gov.cn. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is separately relevant for the Hong Kong shareholder side.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The foreign/Hong Kong shareholder prepares the WFOE articles of association, capital contribution plan, legal representative appointment, registered address contract, investor identity documents and bank details, and files with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) foreign-invested enterprise registration system. Step 2: The AMR issues a Business License with a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is used as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Step 3: Basic tax registration is completed by AMR/STA data exchange; if not automatic, the WFOE must complete tax registration on the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of the Business License. Step 4: The WFOE submits or confirms taxpayer particulars, including legal representative, place of business, bank account, accounting/finance policy, expected turnover and VAT status. Step 5: The competent STA office activates the TIN, determines whether the entity is a small-scale or general VAT taxpayer, and, where applicable, authorizes issuance of fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices. Step 6: If the Hong Kong parent has no permanent establishment in China but earns China-source income, the WFOE as statutory withholding agent must complete non-resident enterprise withholding registration and, where required, obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification reference before remitting dividends or other covered payments.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities are required to register for VAT upon incorporation. Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. For cross-border e-commerce retail exports, registration in the customs export administration and tax export-refund filing system is also required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods sold through TikTok Shop to overseas consumers qualify for the 0% VAT export rate, with credit or refund of input VAT, if the goods are physically exported from the PRC customs territory under a verifiable customs-cleared export movement; the sale is made by the WFOE to a foreign buyer/consumer; the order, payment, logistics and title-transfer evidence demonstrate that the goods are not consumed in China; and the export proceeds are lawfully repatriated. Qualifying exports include cross-border e-commerce retail export under customs supervision code 9610 and relevant B2B e-commerce export modes. Zero-rating does not apply to services consumed in China or to goods without complete export and foreign-exchange substantiation.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) approved by the General Administration of Customs of the PRC, including the cross-border e-commerce retail export declaration with customs supervision code 9610 or 9710/9810 where applicable
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, or international logistics manifest evidencing export movement from the PRC customs territory to the overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued foreign-exchange receipt/declaration and SAFE trade foreign-exchange collection verification record showing repatriation of export sales proceeds to the WFOE's PRC bank account
  • Commercial export invoice and, if issued, the fully digitalized electronic VAT invoice for the export sale
  • TikTok Shop platform order, payout and settlement statements showing the overseas consumer, transaction ID, payment rail (TikTok Shop Payout or Wise B2B), platform fees and net proceeds
  • Input VAT invoice set and the STA export-tax-refund application/acceptance record supporting the zero-rating refund calculation

China operates a real-time electronic invoice clearance regime under the fully digitalized electronic VAT invoice system. Invoices are issued through the Electronic Tax Bureau or through STA-approved API integrations connecting the taxpayer's ERP/order management system to the tax authority. Each invoice carries a unique encrypted invoice code and dynamic QR code, is transmitted to the STA for instantaneous risk verification, and must be recorded before input VAT credit can be claimed. The Golden Tax System cross-matches invoice issuance, customs declarations, foreign-exchange receipts, and platform settlement records. Taxpayers must retain digital invoices and platform transaction logs for audit examination; anti-fraud measures include dynamic encryption, invoice quota controls, and automatic blocking of unusual issuance patterns.

The STA and the General Administration of Customs jointly perform post-clearance audit using the Golden Tax System and cross-border e-commerce export supervision data. Any missing or inconsistent document, failure to repatriate export proceeds within the applicable deadline, artificial round-tripping, false declaration, or unverified export movement will cause zero-rating to be disallowed and the VAT refund or exemption to be clawed back retrospectively. Administrative consequences include payment of underpaid VAT, late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and fines ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax involved. Fraudulent export refund applications may trigger criminal liability under PRC law, suspension or revocation of export tax-refund status, and inclusion in the tax authority's dishonest taxpayer blacklist. Tax authorities may reopen prior refunds under statutory audit limitation periods and recover short-paid taxes with associated penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China manages a managed current-account convertibility regime under the unified supervision of SAFE. Current-account transactions, including import disbursements, service fees, platform commissions, and ordinary dividend remittances, are generally admissible once verified against genuine underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, or audited financials. Capital-account transactions remain subject to rigorous registration, purpose, quota, and compliance controls. The WFOE's paid-in capital from the Hong Kong parent is admitted through a dedicated FDI foreign-exchange capital account and may be converted into RMB only for permitted operating and construction expenses; redirected use for equity investments, entrusted loans, securities, real estate, or other speculative behaviours is prohibited. Export proceeds derived from TikTok Shop sales and collected through Hong Kong settlement wallets must be repatriated to the domestic designated bank under the trade-in-goods collection framework. While the People's Bank of China has abolished mandatory surrender ratios for current-account receipts in the modern SAFE context, the WFOE must either hold the receipts in a designated foreign-exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB while ensuring that every collection is linked to a matching customs declaration and posted in the trade monitoring system. Profit remittance to the HK parent is treated as a current-account dividend outflow, requiring a completed tax clearance, corporate income tax payment proof, a 10% dividend withholding certificate (treaty-eligible), audited financial statements, and the board's dividend resolution. Consequently, RMB convertibility is practically fluent for genuine trade and dividend flows but heavily process-driven, and non-underwritten cross-border capital flows are rejected.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China's SAFE applies current-account convertibility without a statutorily fixed mandatory conversion ratio: export proceeds are not subject to a specific percentage surrender to the central bank, but they must be repatriated to the WFOE's domestic designated foreign-exchange settlement account or converted into RMB at the company's election. The statutory collection timeline requires export-related foreign-currency proceeds to be received by the onshore bank within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless an extended trade cycle is approved under SAFE's goods-trade monitoring framework. Every receipt must be matched to the corresponding customs declaration and reported in the trade-in-goods foreign-exchange monitoring system; non-compliance triggers conditional automatic settlement, regulatory fines, and removal from the bank's trade facilitation list.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central monetary and macroprudential authority, together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as its delegated foreign exchange control and cross-border capital movement regulator; for the Hong Kong treasury node the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) is the relevant monetary authority.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Legally barred as principal bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Airwallex, Nium, and Currencycloud are not licensed by the PBOC/SAFE as Chinese domestic banks and cannot maintain onshore CNY settlement, receive FDI capital funds, or execute SAFE-registered trade reporting for a mainland WFOE. They may be used only by the Hong Kong treasury node to optimise foreign-currency receivables and payouts outside mainland China, provided funds are subsequently repatriated to the WFOE's designated onshore bank. Any attempt to use an EMI as the primary clearing agent breaches the requirement that all trade and capital transactions be handled by a bank that has completed the enterprise's foreign-exchange current-account registration and tariff-verification (reporting) enclosure. Thus, EMIs are limited to ancillary float management and cannot satisfy capital verification, customs reconciliation, or dividend withholding compliance.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a Chinese resident enterprise must prepare and submit a Local File for the year if it has any of the following related-party transaction amounts: tangible asset transfers (including purchases or sales of goods) totaling RMB 200 million or more; intangible asset transfers totaling RMB 100 million or more; financial asset transfers totaling RMB 100 million or more; or other related-party transactions (including service fees, management fees, interest, royalties) totaling RMB 40 million or more. A Master File is required if the ultimate parent's consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion and the Chinese enterprise meets certain related-party transaction thresholds. A Country-by-Country Report filing threshold generally applies to multinational groups with consolidated group revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China does not impose a separate Economic Substance Regulations framework like some offshore jurisdictions, but general anti-avoidance rules under EIT Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form doctrine require that the WFOE and its Hong Kong affiliates demonstrate real economic activity, personnel, assets, and management of core income-generating activities (CIGA). For treaty benefits, the Hong Kong shareholder must show substantive business operations in Hong Kong; a Hong Kong shell company without employees, premises, or decision-making functions will be denied treaty relief and may be recharacterized as a pure conduit, leading to reclassification of dividends, interest, and royalties as taxable Chinese-source income and the imposition of full 10% withholding plus penalties. Transfer pricing documentation must support that the related-party arrangements reflect what independent enterprises would have agreed under comparable circumstances.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Underreporting of E-commerce Sales

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law: the tax authority recovers the underpaid corporate income tax and VAT, adds late payment surcharges of 0.05% per day, and imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount. Article 201 of the Criminal Law: if the amount evaded is large (over RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of the tax due), the penalty is imprisonment up to three years and a fine; if the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax due, imprisonment is three to seven years.

Director personal liability: Under Article 20 of the Company Law, directors who abuse the corporate form to evade taxes and cause serious harm to the state tax claim may be liable with their personal assets to the extent of the tax loss if the court pierces the corporate veil. Directors who directly organized the income concealment are criminally liable as the responsible persons, subject to imprisonment and fines; the tax authority can also block departure from China under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Law until all taxes are settled, and the director may be listed in the dishonest taxpayer blacklist with restrictions on high-consumption behavior.
False VAT Special Invoicing and Inflated Costs

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law imposes fines of 50% to 500% of the short-paid tax and recovery of all fraudulent input VAT credits. Article 205 of the Criminal Law (crime of counterfeiting issuance or false invoicing) imposes criminal fines, imprisonment for up to ten years for serious cases, and life imprisonment where the amount is extremely huge and the circumstances are especially serious; additionally, the enterprise is subject to confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Directors who instigate, approve, or sign false invoicing arrangements are personally liable as principal offenders under Article 205; they may be sentenced to life imprisonment together with fines and confiscation of property. The company veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20, exposing directors personal assets to restitution of the fraudulent tax refunds and penalties, and they face permanent loss of director qualifications and exit bans.
Treaty Shopping and Absence of Beneficial Ownership in Hong Kong Intermediary

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the EIT Law, the tax authority may apply the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to disregard the Hong Kong intermediary and recharacterize the dividends as paid to the ultimate non-resident shareholder, deny the treaty benefit, and impose the statutory 10% withholding tax. The under-withheld tax is collected from the WFOE as withholding agent with late payment surcharges and a penalty of 50% to 500% under Article 66 of the Tax Collection Law for fraudulent tax avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who established the Hong Kong shell entity without commercial substance and who caused the WFOE to under-withhold tax may be jointly and severally liable for restitution of the underpaid tax and penalties under the civil law of tortious evasion of tax claims; criminal liability arises if the arrangement amounts to intentional tax evasion, with imprisonment up to seven years. The directors may also be prohibited from serving as legal representatives, and the Hong Kong shell may be reported to Hong Kong authorities for deregistration or penalties under Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations if applicable.
