E-government filing portal

STEP 2: The WFOE must complete tax registration through the provincial e-Tax Bureau within 30 days of issuance of the business license, electing taxpayer status (general VAT taxpayer or small-scale VAT taxpayer) and registering the legal representative, financial officer, banking accounts, invoicing limits, and withholding obligations for payroll and non-resident payments. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all enterprises with taxable operations in the PRC must complete tax registration upon incorporation without a turnover threshold. Below the RMB 5,000,000 annual threshold, the WFOE may be registered as a small-scale VAT taxpayer and may voluntarily elect general taxpayer status if it meets the statutory accounting and invoicing conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, exported goods are zero-rated where the goods are physically exported from PRC customs territory, ownership and risk pass to the overseas buyer, and the exporter receives documentary evidence of export. Mail-order retail with cash-on-delivery settlement qualifies for zero-rating only when the goods are delivered outside mainland China and the COD collections are remitted to the WFOE through approved payment rails such as Airwallex or local logistics-remittance channels. Qualifying offshore services and digital services consumed wholly outside the PRC may also be zero-rated or VAT-exempt if supplied to non-resident recipients and supported by cross-border contracts and foreign exchange receipts. Domestic fulfillment, warehousing and last-mile delivery inside the PRC remain subject to standard VAT.

  • Packing list and transport document (Bill of Lading for ocean freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or international courier consignment note)
  • Logistics delivery proof showing COD collection and overseas delivery confirmation

PRC VAT invoices are controlled by the State Taxation Administration. General VAT taxpayers that have not migrated to the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform use the VAT invoice management system; migrated taxpayers use the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA tax-control system before or at issuance through real-time application programming interfaces; fully digitalized e-invoices carry a unique electronic invoice number and are authenticated by the STA system, replacing physical stamps. The WFOE engaged in cross-border COD e-commerce must use the export declaration platform and may align with the cross-border e-commerce comprehensive pilot area reporting to obtain zero-rated clearance; domestic logistics invoices and all PRC sales must be cleared through the same e-invoice infrastructure.

The State Taxation Administration may audit export declarations, logistics chains, bank receipts, and e-commerce platform settlement data. If the zero-rating is not substantiated, the export sale is recharacterized as a domestic taxable sale, the VAT at 13% is assessed together with late-payment surcharges, and any export tax rebate already paid is clawed back with interest. Administrative penalties for inaccurate declarations include fines up to five times the tax underpaid, while fraudulent export tax rebate applications may trigger criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law and confiscation of the illegal proceeds. The WFOE must retain the full documentation bundle for at least the statutory limitation period and maintain reconciliation between customs data, foreign exchange data, and VAT filings.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China applies a conditional, registration-based convertibility regime. Current-account flows arising from e-commerce trade settlements are convertible in practice once the bank verifies the genuineness of the transaction through invoices, logistics records and customs declarations. Capital-account flows, including FDI capital injections from the Hong Kong parent, are not free; they require SAFE registration before inbound remittance, a designated foreign exchange capital account, and post-arrival capital verification by a licensed CPA. RMB conversion of capital does not require SAFE pre-approval if recorded under the capital settlement procedure but the use of settled funds is restricted to bona fide operating purposes. Profit remittances to Hong Kong shareholders are treated as current-account distributions but require audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement/clearance, and bank verification of prior-year undistributed profits. COD collection from domestic consumers is in RMB and cannot be freely converted into foreign currency without an underlying permitted current-account transaction. The result is a convertible trade account with tightly supervised capital inflows, strict purpose coding, and documentary defense rather than a fully open convertibility environment.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC has abolished mandatory surrender quotas. Export foreign currency proceeds must be repatriated to a PRC bank through a trade-in-goods current account within the time window declared in the SAFE trade monitoring system (normally on receipt after customs declaration; deferred receipts beyond 90 days require supplementary reporting). There is no compulsory conversion ratio; the exporter may retain the foreign currency in a designated forex account or sell it to a licensed bank, provided the transaction is supported by customs declarations, e-commerce platform order data, logistics evidence, and genuine transaction background. Conversion to RMB is executed at the onshore central parity/adjusted market rate, with the bank applying the applicable trade-in-goods monitoring classification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex do not hold PRC banking licenses and cannot legally maintain demand deposits or settlement accounts for a mainland WFOE. They may act only as payment facilitators by partnering with licensed PRC banks or licensed non-bank payment institutions, with funds ultimately cleared through a domestic sponsor bank. The WFOE cannot substitute an EMI for its statutory basic deposit account or capital account. Feasibility for cross-border COD collections is conditional at Medium: the EMI can process incoming consumer remittances from overseas platforms, but RMB received must be swept into a licensed interbank clearing account; for mainland domestic COD, collection must run through UnionPay/NetsUnion clearing under PBOC payment rules. Legal constraint: no foreign EMI may independently provide domestic RMB clearing for the WFOE.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard PRC Enterprise Income Tax (CIT) rate; 15% preferential rate for qualified High and New Technology Enterprises and certain encouraged industries in specific regions; effective 5% rate for Small Low-Profit Enterprises meeting the statutory thresholds

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% domestic CIT withholding on dividends distributed by the PRC resident WFOE to a non-resident Hong Kong shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement (DTA) Article 10, provided the beneficial owner is a company holding at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE directly and continuously for the 365-day period preceding the dividend declaration

Tax evasion constituting a crime is prosecuted under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 203, with imprisonment up to 7 years for substantial evasion; the competent tax authorities may also freeze assets, pursue exit bans or travel blacklisting, and notify border control in the case of outstanding tax or falsified filings.
Box 20 - Related-party transaction and transfer pricing schedules Provide annexes for related-party transactions, contemporaneous documentation, Local File, Master File, Country-by-Country reporting, and special tax adjustment items All transactions with the Hong Kong parent and affiliates must be disclosed in the annual related-party transaction report. Non-disclosure or inaccurate disclosure permits the STA to adjust under special tax investigation procedures and expose the entity to penalties. If the WFOE ships goods and manages COD logistics for the group, it must allocate profit to China based on functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed; booking only a risk-free cost-plus return to the Hong Kong parent while leaving operating profit offshore is a fatal red flag. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File contemporaneous documentation is mandatory when, in a fiscal year, the WFOE has related-party transactions exceeding: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (including services, management fees, and COD logistics charges). Master File is required when total annual related-party transactions reach or exceed RMB 1 billion, and Country-by-Country Reporting is required when the ultimate parent group's consolidated revenue reaches at least RMB 5.5 billion in the preceding accounting year.

Economic substance scope: The PRC WFOE is the operating and economic-substance centre of the COD e-commerce business: it owns inventory, executes sales contracts, assumes credit and returns risk, coordinates logistics, and controls cash collection through local remittance rails. Under PRC CIT Law Article 41 and the general anti-avoidance rule in Article 47, the STA may reallocate profit to the WFOE based on Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) performed in China. The Hong Kong parent must possess genuine substance under Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance residency principles; if the Hong Kong entity merely obtains a Tax Residency Certificate without real staff, premises, and decision-making powers, treaty benefits will be denied under the beneficial ownership requirement and the Principal Purpose Test. The WFOE should maintain payroll, warehouse records, logistics contracts, board meeting minutes, customer-facing contracts, bank statements, and COD settlement reconciliation reports in China to substantiate economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by Underreporting of Cash-on-Delivery Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law imposes a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax plus recovery of underpaid tax and late-payment interest; Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes fraudulent tax filings where the evaded tax exceeds the statutory threshold, with imprisonment up to 3 years for relatively large amounts and 3 to 7 years plus a fine for enormous amounts exceeding 30% of the tax payable; the competent tax authority may also impose exit restriction orders and a travel ban pending full payment

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false CIT returns, cause off-book COD revenue, or maintain dual account sets can be personally liable under the PRC Company Law Article 20 for abuse of the corporate form, requiring restitution from personal assets; criminal liability attaches under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 162 for accounting record concealment and evasion; the director may be blacklisted, prevented from leaving mainland China, disqualified from serving as a director, and subject to a personal fine and imprisonment.
Abusive Treaty Claim and Beneficial Ownership Sham

Statutory sanction: The STA will deny the DTA benefit under the PRC Gazette on Tax Treaties and the Principal Purpose Test, recharacterizing the dividend at the domestic 10% withholding rate plus late surcharge; if the underpaid withholding is deemed fraud, Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law applies with a 50% to 5 times penalty; GAAR under Article 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 120 of its Implementing Regulations permits a special tax adjustment resulting in additional deemed dividends, interest, and penalty interest

Director personal liability: Directors of the Chinese withholding agent and the Hong Kong holding company may be personally liable for failure to withhold under Article 69 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law (fine of 50% to 3 times the tax not withheld) and for causing an unlawful outbound profit-stripping arrangement; they may be subject to corporate veil-piercing civil liability where assets have been removed from China, travel bans and blacklisting by the tax authority, and criminal exposure for fraud if the false beneficial ownership documentation is knowingly submitted.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Persistent Loss Arrangement

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Articles 41 and 44 empower the STA to make a transfer pricing adjustment using the arm's-length standard; Article 42 imposes penalties for excess interest with special tax adjustment interest accruing on the underpaid tax; failure to provide required documentation permits the tax authority to estimate taxable income and imposes fines under the Tax Administration and Collection Law; if the arrangement is fraudulent, Article 63 penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax apply; the STA may also apply the 10% non-resident withholding tax on recharacterized dividends or deemed royalties

Director personal liability: Directors who approve intercompany agreements with no economic substance or sign transfer pricing documentation falsely asserting arm's-length compliance may be held personally liable for restitution where the WFOE has been rendered insolvent; under the PRC Company Law Article 20, directors who use corporate autonomy to harm creditors or the state's tax claim bear joint and several liability with personal assets; criminal exposure may arise under Criminal Law Article 162 for misrepresenting business records, in addition to administrative blacklisting, travel bans, and disqualification orders issued by the STA and cross-border enforcement authorities.
