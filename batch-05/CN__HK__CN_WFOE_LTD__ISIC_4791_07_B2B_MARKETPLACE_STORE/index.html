Competent registry, governing statute, capital lockup rules, and first board resolution template.

Competent corporate registry

State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), acting through the Enterprise Registration Bureau of the local municipal or district Market Supervision Administration having jurisdiction over the registered office.

Governing statute

Company Law of the People's Republic of China (2023 Revision, effective 1 July 2024), Articles 7, 23-29 and 46-50; Foreign Investment Law of the People's Republic of China (2019), Articles 2, 6, 28 and 31; Regulations on the Implementation of the Foreign Investment Law (2020); Special Administrative Measures for Foreign Investment Access (Negative List) (2021 Edition) where applicable.

Formation / constitutional filings

Foreign-Invested Enterprise Establishment Registration Application Form, together with the approved Articles of Association and the issuer documents, submitted through the SAMR nationwide enterprise registration online portal or the local one-stop foreign investment service window.

Incorporation processing time

⚡ 27 business days (SOP sum)

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Under the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision), the registered capital is a subscription-based amount set out in the Articles of Association; all shareholders must make their subscribed contributions in full within five years of incorporation, unless a shorter term is mandated by law or administrative regulations. The Company must maintain a reasonable contribution schedule and effect foreign direct investment (FDI) registration with SAFE through a designated bank before the paid-in capital is used.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for civil defense or industrial fire permits. The WFOE's commercial office premises must be covered by the landlord's fire safety inspection or acceptance certificate, and the Company must maintain standard fire extinguishers, emergency exits, and workplace safety measures. A separate Civil Defense fire permit is not required for an ordinary digital platform office.

Paid-in capital rule: No statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal = the electronic tax filing system operated by local STA service bureaus (e-Tax portal). For Hong Kong SAR nexus, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) governs profits tax and offshore claims via the eTAX portal, but no Hong Kong authority administers VAT/GST/Sales Tax because none is levied.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the WFOE, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues a business license bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA through the government data-sharing platform, and no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE legal representative must log into the e-Tax portal, activate the taxpayer file, complete or verify taxpayer qualification information (registered address, legal representative, banking account, accounting system, payroll and withholding agents), and confirm the VAT taxpayer classification; the USCC becomes the TIN. If export VAT refund is intended, the WFOE must apply for general VAT taxpayer status and complete export-related tax registration; export tax rebate qualification is managed electronically with the customs and tax authorities. Foreign non-resident shareholders generally do not need a PRC TIN; if a non-resident derives China-source income or is treated as having a taxable presence, the payer or the non-resident itself must register as a non-resident taxpayer with the STA, obtain a non-resident taxpayer code, and file withholding before payment is remitted.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; below this threshold a taxpayer may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer, but export zero-rating and input VAT refund eligibility generally require general taxpayer status. No registration threshold applies in Hong Kong for indirect tax because Hong Kong imposes no VAT/GST/Sales Tax.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory by the WFOE as exporter of record qualify for 0% VAT and refund of residual input VAT if the goods are declared to customs, actually leave the PRC customs territory (including exports to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory), and the export sales proceeds are repatriated through a SAFE-regulated bank within the statutory period. Cross-border B2B marketplace transactions should be structured under customs supervision code 9710 (B2B direct export) or 9810 (B2B export to overseas warehouse) when the WFOE is the seller or exporter of record. Qualifying services provided to non-resident recipients and consumed wholly outside mainland China may be zero-rated or VAT-exempt under the STA's cross-border taxable services

PRC VAT invoicing is controlled through the STA's digital tax control system (Golden Tax Phase IV). All VAT invoices, including fully digitalized electronic invoices, must be issued from the taxpayer's digital tax account; invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real-time or near-real-time through the national e-invoice platform. Certified enterprise resource planning integrations through STA-approved APIs are permitted, and invoices are validated with anti-counterfeit QR codes and tax authority verification numbers; high-risk issuance can be blocked automatically. Hong Kong has no electronic invoicing, fiscal clearance, API, or digital stamping obligation for VAT because Hong Kong levies no indirect tax.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a managed floating exchange rate regime where the Renminbi (RMB) is not freely convertible on the capital account, while current account convertibility is substantially permitted after genuine trade and service transaction verification. For this B2B cross-border marketplace WFOE, the critical restriction is on capital account flows: foreign shareholders' equity injections must be booked as Registered Capital under the Foreign Investment Law and require a SAFE foreign exchange registration (FDI Business Registration), after which funds are credited to a Capital Account-Foreign Exchange Settlement Account (Capital Escrow Account) and can be converted into RMB only for approved business purposes such as procurement of goods, operating expenses, and trade settlement within the scope of the entity's operations. Profit repatriation or dividend remittance abroad is allowed after the annual audit, corporate income tax settlement, and board resolution, but requires a SAFE-approved Bank to verify the authenticity of the earnings and the taxpayer identification number. Additionally, the WFOE must comply with reporting to the Ministry of Commerce and local market regulation authorities. Export proceeds from the marketplace's cross-border trade must be repatriated to China and either retained as foreign currency or settled into RMB; this settlement does not require mandatory surrender at a fixed ratio but is subject to the bank's verification of underlying trade documents under the 'Five-in-One' supervision involving customs, foreign exchange, tax, logistics, and marine insurance data. A practical hurdle is that funds flowing to offshore Hong Kong parent companies from the WFOE via service fees or intellectual property royalties face strict authenticity checks and may trigger transfer pricing documentation requirements if the amounts are disproportionate to the business volume. Overall, the RMB is convertible for daily trade operations, but strategic treasury decisions such as dividend upstreaming, intercompany lending, or capital reduction require layered SAFE approvals, making the regime functionally restrictive for unsupervised capital movements.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts from the B2B marketplace must be repatriated to mainland China within 6 months (180 days) from the date of settlement of exports, as per SAFE regulations. There is a mandatory repatriation requirement of 100% of the export proceeds, but the mandatory conversion ratio is 0%; the WFOE may retain such proceeds in a designated foreign currency current account or voluntarily sell them to a domestic bank for RMB at market rates. In practice, banks require the exporter to complete the SAFE 'Enterprise Income and Payment' declaration and present valid commercial invoices, customs declarations, bills of lading, and a contract to prove the genuineness of the trade.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., global neobanks, virtual account providers) are not viable as primary banking or clearing rails for a mainland WFOE under the current regulatory framework. The PBOC and SAFE mandate that all foreign exchange conversions, cross-border settlements, and capital account transactions be executed through licensed Chinese commercial banks that have a SAFE banking qualification. EMIs without a Chinese banking license cannot hold direct settlement accounts for a WFOE or perform capital verification account functions. They may only be used as auxiliary accounting interfaces linked to a bank's API, but the underlying legal money movement must transit through the domestic bank's SWIFT and local clearing systems. Therefore, reliance on such EMIs is legally barred for the entity's statutory capital injection and trade disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong parent to the mainland WFOE must be wired via SWIFT MT103 through a licensed domestic bank that serves as the receiving bank. The remittance instruction shall mention 'Registered Capital Injection' as the payment purpose, and the remitter must be the shareholder of record. The WFOE must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE via the local bank, obtain a unique FDI registration code, and open a Capital Account in the receiving bank. The bank will convert the incoming foreign currency into RMB in accordance with the Capital Account Settlement Declaration, after which funds are credited to the WFOE's RMB operating account. Capital verification for regulatory authority may be performed by a local CPA firm, which will issue a capital verification report based on the bank's statement and SAFE registration. For amounts exceeding USD 500,000, a prior registration with the Ministry of Commerce and a copy of the Foreign Investment Filing is required.Outbound trade disbursements for the B2B marketplace's operating expenses and supplier payments shall be executed through the following clearing rails: (1) SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign currency vendor payments over the global correspondent banking network; (2) Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C at sight) for high-value international inventory purchases where the WFOE is the applicant; and (3) Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) for lower-trust trade partners to reduce exposure until payment documents are completed. All outbound foreign exchange payments require the WFOE to submit an authentic trade-related application to the bank: import contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and shipping documents, and the bank will use its SAFE reporting to validate each transaction under the current account foreign exchange purchase system. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong incorporated private company must appoint a certified public accountant (practising CPA) to audit its financial statements unless it qualifies as a small private company and passes a resolution to dispense with audit under section 394. A small private company must satisfy at least two of the following three conditions for the financial year: (a) total annual revenue not exceeding HK$20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,103); (b) total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,051); (c) a total of not more than 50 employees. The audit must be conducted in accordance with Hong Kong Standards on Auditing and the resulting financial statements are subject to Chinese Government supervision to the extent the WFOE has mainland China operations.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice [2016] No.42, Local File required for: (a) tangible property related-party transactions exceeding RMB 200 million in a fiscal year; (b) intangible property transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (c) financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; (d) related-party service transactions (including financial services) exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File required if the aggregate value of all related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceeds RMB 1 billion. Annual related-party transaction reporting (Schedule A109000) is mandatory regardless of thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under EIT Law Article 47. A resident WFOE must demonstrate real economic substance in core income-generating activities (CIGA) including strategic platform management, merchant onboarding, control over payment and settlement risks, software development, and operational decision-making. The Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits must have adequate physical offices, employees, bank accounts, and board meetings in Hong Kong; mere letterbox or conduit arrangements will be pierced and treaty relief denied. Hong Kong's Economic Substance Requirements (Cap. 112) may also impose reporting and substance tests on the HK entity for pure equity holding or high-risk IP activities.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Invoicing and Off-the-Books Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Administration Law (TCAL) Article 63, underpayment of tax is punishable by a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid amount plus daily 0.05% interest surcharge. Under Criminal Law Article 201, if the evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, the responsible persons face imprisonment up to 7 years, criminal fines, and asset confiscation.

Director personal liability: The director and senior management who knowingly signed false financial statements or ordered revenue misreporting are personally liable under PRC Company Law Article 20 (piercing the corporate veil), triggering joint civil liability for corporate tax debts. Personal bank accounts, properties, and travel rights may be frozen; the courts may issue exit bans and blacklisting, and the individual may be sentenced to up to seven years' imprisonment for tax fraud.
Abusive Shell or Conduit Structure for Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 2 and SAT Public Notice [2019] No.35, the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, recharacterise the substance of the arrangement, and impose 10% statutory withholding tax (or higher if underlying income is reassessed), plus late interest and a 50%-500% penalty under TCAL Article 63. Fraudulent treaty abuse may also constitute criminal fraud under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 204, with up to seven years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the Chinese WFOE and the Hong Kong shell company who jointly executed the circular ownership and dividend flow are personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Piercing of the corporate veil leads to personal asset seizure, freezing of individual bank accounts, travel bans, and criminal prosecution. In aggravated cases, courts may impose personal restitution and disqualification from serving as directors of any PRC or HK company.
Failure to File Related-Party Transaction Documentation and Transfer Pricing Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 44 and SAT Public Notice [2016] No.42, if the taxpayer fails to provide related-party documentation, the tax authority may assess taxable income by analogy, impose a 50%-500% penalty on the underpaid tax, and charge daily interest surcharges (0.05% from the original due date to the date of payment). Transfer pricing adjustment may also trigger anti-avoidance penalties under EIT Law Article 47.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who knowingly omit required TP documentation or approve excessive related-party markups may be personally liable for the resulting corporate tax shortfall under Company Law Article 20. They face administrative penalties including blacklisting, exit bans, and revocation of passports; if the falsification is part of a scheme to defraud the tax authority, they may face criminal charges for tax evasion with imprisonment up to seven years, along with civil asset restitution and permanent disqualification from managerial positions.
