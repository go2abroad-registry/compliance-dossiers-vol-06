Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains current-account convertibility under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement; therefore legitimate subscription-box service fees received from overseas retail customers can generally be settled through licensed banks with supporting transaction documents. The capital account, however, remains heavily managed. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise receiving equity or shareholder loans from its Hong Kong parent must complete SAFE foreign exchange registration before the incoming remittance is credited. Foreign currency capital may be converted into RMB only after bank verification, and the funds must be placed in a designated capital account under the relevant FDI rules. Profit remittance out of China to the Hong Kong parent is treated as current-account dividend transfer; it is permitted once audited financial statements, tax clearance, board resolutions, and proof of cumulative contribution are submitted. The practical hurdles are operational: banks require exact matching of invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and payment rails, and while no mandatory conversion of export proceeds is currently imposed, all foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into a domestic current-account FX settlement account within the statutory period. Capital outflows for investment, acquisition, or speculative treasury purposes remain restricted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current framework, export foreign-exchange receipts must be repatriated to a domestic bank account within 180 days from the date of customs declaration or collection date; there is no statutorily fixed percentage of mandatory conversion to RMB. Exporters may retain foreign currency in a settlement account and convert voluntarily, subject to bank review of the underlying trade transaction through the digital foreign-exchange system and electronic customs data matching. In practice, banks encourage prompt conversion if the WFOE needs RMB to pay domestic suppliers, but no absolute ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore EMIs such as a Hong Kong-licensed stored value facility or a Singapore/UK EMI cannot legally open a corporate settlement account or maintain a local treasury relationship for a mainland China WFOE without a Chinese payment institution license. Fintech aggregators can only serve as technical payment gateways for e-commerce collections and cannot replace a regulated bank clearing account for capital injection, supplier disbursement, or FX conversion. A WFOE should route all cross-border receipts and payments through licensed Chinese commercial banks and may use a licensed third-party payment institution only for downstream collection, never as the primary treasury rail.

Root cause: Beneficial ownership opacity caused by a Hong Kong parent using nominee directors, trust structures, or unresolved bearer share exposure.

Defense evidence: Provide certified Hong Kong Certificate of Incorporation, Certificate of Incumbency from the registered agent, full shareholder register with ultimate individual disclosure, audited accounts, HK business registration certificate, passport copies of all UBOs, source of wealth statements, and a written legal opinion confirming no bearer shares or nominee concealment.
Inbound capital injection flagged as unverified FDI or possible round-tripping.

Root cause: Inbound capital injection flagged as unverified FDI or possible round-tripping.

Defense evidence: Submit SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate, China WFOE business license and Articles of Association, executed equity capital contribution agreement, board resolution approving the injection, proof of HK parent ownership, bank statement evidencing that funds came from the parent's own account, cash-flow projection for capital use, and a notarized undertaking that funds are not sourced from prohibited mainland financing.
Subscription-box revenues collected through offshore payment gateways create data mismatch and unexplained cross-border flows.

Root cause: Subscription-box revenues collected through offshore payment gateways create data mismatch and unexplained cross-border flows.

Defense evidence: Provide current Stripe and Authorize.Net legal entity contracts, sub-merchant agreements, monthly settlement statements, chargeback and refund reports, e-commerce platform order logs, customs declarations for shipped parcels, logistics tracking summaries, and a reconciliation ledger showing that gateway settlements map to actual client orders.
Transaction purpose and beneficiary mismatch for trade disbursements to third-party logistics or raw-material suppliers.

Root cause: Transaction purpose and beneficiary mismatch for trade disbursements to third-party logistics or raw-material suppliers.

Defense evidence: Provide purchase orders, invoices, logistics contracts, delivery confirmations, supplier KYC packs, FX purchase declarations, board-approved disbursement policy, and a written explanation demonstrating that each outbound payment has a direct underlying commercial agreement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital subscription from Hong Kong should be remitted by SWIFT MT103 from the HK parent's own bank account to the WFOE's onshore capital account held at an approved Chinese bank. The remittance instruction must quote the SAFE-registered foreign exchange business registration number and specify the relevant SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment capital injection. Before the bank can credit the account, the WFOE must complete foreign exchange registration with the local SAFE branch and open a foreign currency capital account. After receipt, the bank performs capital verification using the original SWIFT advice, capital account statement, and the verified registration certificate; settlement of foreign currency into RMB is permitted after bank review under the real-use principle, with proceeds restricted from entering securities, speculative real estate, or non-operating investments under the negative list.For subscription box production and fulfilment, the WFOE may disburse RMB to domestic suppliers through CIPS or through standard domestic electronic payments for Chinese counterparties. For merchandise moving through Hong Kong or overseas third-party logistics, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for direct supplier payments under sale and purchase contracts; Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT710) are recommended for high-value raw material imports requiring document control against bills of lading and inspection certificates; Documentary Collections (DP/DA) are suitable for low-AML-risk regular shipments with established trade history.
