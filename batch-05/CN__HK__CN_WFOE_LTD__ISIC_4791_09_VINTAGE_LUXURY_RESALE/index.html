Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an ordinary WFOE under the 2023 Company Law. The registered capital is fixed in the Articles of Association and must be fully subscribed by the sole foreign shareholder. Under Article 47 of the Company Law, all subscribed capital must be paid in full within five years from establishment, unless a shorter period is required by the Articles of Association or applicable sector-specific rules. There is no blocked escrow account requirement. The shareholder contributes capital in RMB or lawful foreign currency through the company's foreign exchange capital account at a designated bank, and the bank reports the foreign direct investment capital flow under the SAFE system. A capital verification report is not mandatory for ordinary ISIC 4791 retail activities, but it may be obtained for corporate governance comfort.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (e-Tax), administered through the provincial and municipal STA branches for the place of registration of the WFOE

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Register the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a PRC domestic company with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) using the National Enterprise Registration Online Service to obtain the Business License and Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: The USCC automatically constitutes the WFOE's statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC national tax purposes, including enterprise income tax, VAT and customs duties; no separately issued TIN certificate is created for a newly formed WFOE. Step 3: Complete the initial tax registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau portal within 30 days after the Business License is issued, including filing of the legal representative, registered address, bank settlement account details, accounting policy and statutory books and records. Step 4: Classify the VAT taxpayer status as either small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer in accordance with the annual taxable sales threshold, and register the applicable VAT categories. Step 5: If the WFOE will export vintage luxury goods, complete the export tax refund/exemption filing with the competent STA office and register with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for goods trade foreign exchange monitoring. Step 6: Non-resident shareholders are not issued PRC TINs; their ownership is documented through the foreign investment information reporting system and, if applicable, the non-resident enterprise withholding tax registration is maintained separately.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law does not provide US-style check-the-box elections, partnership tax classification elections, or hybrid mismatch elections. A WFOE incorporated under PRC law is automatically a PRC resident enterprise with entity-level taxation on its worldwide income; no elective regime exists to alter this treatment.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC-incorporated WFOEs must complete VAT registration upon incorporation; no economic turnover threshold applies to the initial registration of a corporate entity. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer and apply the simplified VAT method. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 (or quarterly sales not exceeding RMB 300,000) are exempt from VAT under current national rules. For the resale of secondhand and vintage luxury goods, if the goods qualify as used goods, a simplified VAT collection method at the statutory reduced rate of 3% reduced to 2% may apply instead of the standard 13% goods rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A PRC VAT-registered WFOE is eligible to apply the VAT zero rate to exported vintage luxury goods where the goods are physically dispatched from the PRC customs territory, including shipments to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory, to an overseas destination; the WFOE is the named exporter on a valid customs export declaration; the sale is supported by a genuine cross-border sales contract and invoicing; the overseas customer pays the consideration in foreign currency or through an approved cross-border payment method; and the export proceeds are received and repatriated into China through a licensed banking channel. Claiming the zero rate requires the WFOE to file an export tax refund/exemption declaration with the STA. Cross-border e-commerce retail exports using customs supervision mode codes 9610, 9710 or 9810 may qualify for VAT exemption/refund when the transaction is integrated through the China International Trade Single Window. Any qualifying offshore digital services would be zero-rated only if consumed entirely outside China and specifically recognized in the STA zero-rating category list; for this physical goods business, actual export and foreign exchange repatriation are the controlling criteria.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration (Customs Export Declaration Form) bearing the WFOE's customs registration code
  • Original Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international logistics receipt evidencing departure from China and delivery outside China
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list for the overseas buyer
  • Export sales contract or consignment agreement evidencing the cross-border transaction
  • Bank export foreign exchange collection certificate or bank credit receipt evidencing receipt and repatriation of export proceeds through a licensed Chinese settlement bank
  • China International Trade Single Window cross-border e-commerce order, payment and logistics matching data for e-commerce retail export modes
  • Declared export price list and customs valuation supporting documents
  • VAT invoices or export invoices issued through the ADEI platform for the zero-rated export transaction

The STA has implemented the All-round Digitalized Electronic Invoice (ADEI) regime and the Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud control system. VAT invoices, including Special Value-Added Tax Invoices and ordinary invoices, must be issued through the STA's official electronic invoice service platform using Tax U-Key or enterprise digital credentials. Each invoice is transmitted in real time to the STA central database, automatically assigned a unique invoice code and digital sequence number, and made available for immediate taxpayer verification through the National VAT Invoice Verification System. No physical fiscal stamp is required, but corrections must be executed through the prescribed normal invoice and red invoice workflow. Cross-border e-commerce retail export transactions must additionally submit order, payment and logistics data to the China International Trade Single Window to enable customs clearance, export monitoring and VAT treatment consistency.

Export zero-rating is subject to documentary and data matching review by the STA, PRC Customs and SAFE. If export proceeds are not received or repatriated within the required period and a valid deferred collection extension has not been recorded, the VAT zero rate is denied and any VAT refund already granted must be clawed back with statutory late-payment interest. Under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law, fraudulent export refund claims or fabricated export documentation attract an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount evaded or under-collected. Customs false declaration rules allow monetary penalties, downgrade of customs credit rating, and forfeiture of export privileges. SAFE may impose monetary fines, suspend certain trade foreign exchange operations, and downgrade the enterprise from A class to B or C class under the goods trade foreign exchange classification system. Where the fraudulent scheme meets the criminal threshold, the WFOE's responsible officers may be subject to criminal prosecution for export tax fraud or smuggling-related offenses under the PRC Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China applies current-account convertibility with strict capital-account controls. For a mainland WFOE engaged in online consignment and secondhand luxury goods retail, current-account transactions such as trade payments for goods, logistics, commissions and platform fees are normally convertible after the bank verifies the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration and proof of transaction. Export proceeds are not subject to a mandatory surrender ratio, but all receipts from overseas trade must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account and cannot remain offshore indefinitely. The capital account is materially more constrained: inward direct investment, shareholder loans, equity transfer proceeds and dividend remittances require either prior SAFE registration or acceptance of registration by the delegated bank. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent requires audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates for corporate income tax and withholding tax, a valid board resolution declaring the dividend, and evidence that registered capital has been fully paid in and verified. In practice, genuine current-account payments clear within one to three banking days once documentary compliance is complete, but any mismatch between the e-commerce platform settlement amounts and the WFOE book records will be treated as an FX red flag and may suspend disbursement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE trade-in-goods administration, there is no fixed statutory surrender ratio; enterprises may retain up to 100% of export proceeds in a domestic foreign exchange settlement account or convert them into RMB at a designated bank. However, the proceeds are required to be repatriated into the mainland banking system within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless an approved delayed collection filing is made with SAFE. The WFOE must match each receipt to customs electronic data and the business-name registration in the Foreign Exchange Trade-in-Goods Enterprise List; failure to do so will cause the receipt to be classified as a non-trade capital item and may expose the entity to administrative penalties under the Measures for the Administration of Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Foreign fintech EMIs do not have a lawful role as the principal bank of a mainland WFOE. Under the PRC Commercial Banking Law and the PBOC payment rules, a non-resident entity without a Chinese banking license cannot open the statutory bank accounts used for FDI capital injection, routine corporate tax payments, or employee salary settlement. Some cross-border e-commerce payment institutions such as PingPong, Airwallex and Payoneer can be used in an ancillary collection-only channel via a licensed mainland bank partner, but they cannot replace the SAFE-registered capital account or the domestic bank-compliant clearing anchor. The viable structure is to keep the WFOE statutory treasury at a tier-1 domestic bank and use the EMI only to collect offshore retail receipts that are then settled into the licensed bank through an approved cross-border collection arrangement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must complete SAFE FDI registration in the regional branch or through the delegated bank before wiring. The inbound payment should use SWIFT MT103, with the remitter being the registered foreign shareholder, the beneficiary being the WFOE Foreign Exchange Capital Account, and the remittance information referencing the SAFE ExCode and the purpose 'Inbound Direct Investment Capital Injection'. No third-party payer is acceptable absent a documented and SAFE-approved funding chain. Upon receipt, the bank can process the foreign exchange to RMB settlement for approved capital-account uses under the capital item negative list; a local CPA must then issue a capital verification report to record the paid-in capital, update the National Enterprise Credit Information Exchange, and complete tax and statistical filings. All capital account receipts must be retained in the designated account and cannot be used outside the approved business scope.For trade settlements, the WFOE may use SWIFT MT103 for supplier and logistics payments, commercial letters of credit for large-value purchase contracts, and documentary collections for low or mid-value consignment purchases. Each outbound remittance must be matched with the goods-trade foreign exchange verification system, using the contract, invoice, customs declaration, transport documents and, for service fees, evidence of actual service delivery. Where the vintage luxury goods are bought through the Hong Kong trading platform, a buy-sell or back-to-back contract must be clearly structured so that payments are correctly classified as import goods rather than service payments. If the WFOE settles cross-border e-commerce purchases through an international card acquirer, it should route the stream through a licensed cross-border payment institution partner bank and ensure all proceeds are settled into the domestic operating account with full transaction data.Deliverable CNY/USD foreign exchange forward contracts with an onshore designated bank, subject to the SAFE real-demand principle and supported by underlying trade receivables or payables documents.CNY foreign-exchange option contracts, including vanilla call and put and simple barrier structures, purchased from onshore licensed banks with the option premium settled in CNY and no embedded leveraged pay-off.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps used for restructuring shareholder loans or capital financing where the WFOE can demonstrate an actual medium-term FX exposure and obtains SAFE registration for the hedging transaction.Offshore non-deliverable forwards are not permissible as an onshore hedging instrument for the WFOE; any NDF must be booked only by the Hong Kong parent and cannot be used to hedge or settle the mainland entity accounts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Foreign fintech EMIs do not have a lawful role as the principal bank of a mainland WFOE. Under the PRC Commercial Banking Law and the PBOC payment rules, a non-resident entity without a Chinese banking license cannot open the statutory bank accounts used for FDI capital injection, routine corporate tax payments, or employee salary settlement. Some cross-border e-commerce payment institutions such as PingPong, Airwallex and Payoneer can be used in an ancillary collection-only channel via a licensed mainland bank partner, but they cannot replace the SAFE-registered capital account or the domestic bank-compliant clearing anchor. The viable structure is to keep the WFOE statutory treasury at a tier-1 domestic bank and use the EMI only to collect offshore retail receipts that are then settled into the licensed bank through an approved cross-border collection arrangement.
