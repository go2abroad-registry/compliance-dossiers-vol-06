Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a WFOE in the dietary supplement e-commerce sector under the PRC Company Law; no blocked escrow account or subscribed capital deposit is required at formation. The Articles of Association must state a total registered capital that is reasonable and proportionate to the operational scale, and the sole shareholder may contribute capital in installments as scheduled in the articles, with full contribution required within the maximum commitment period permitted by law. Foreign exchange registration for the capital account is mandatory before injecting funds, and the capital contribution remains subject to the Foreign Investment Negative List and anti-money laundering verification by the receiving bank.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Safety Acceptance Inspection Certificate or Fire Safety Inspection Record issued by the local Fire and Rescue Detachment; the office and warehouse must possess an evacuation plan, two unobstructed exit routes, emergency lighting, and dry-chemical fire extinguishers distributed at a ratio of at least one unit per 75 square meters. A structural load inspection is not required for an ordinary non-industrial leasing arrangement, but the landlord must provide a housing safety verification or urban land-use permit proving the premises may be used for commercial / storage purposes. Occupational health and safety approval is obtained through the Work Safety Law registration of the food storage area, appointment of a safety officer, annual fire drill records, and a first-aid kit in the workplace. Since the enterprise is not engaged in hazardous chemical manufacturing, no Civil Defense permit for chemical shelters is applicable, but the company must ensure compliance with all emergency response and civil defense evacuation requirements applicable to commercial premises.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; primary electronic portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (Electronic Tax Administration) operated at both national and provincial levels, and the China International Trade Single Window operated by the General Administration of Customs for export and customs electronic clearance.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation issues the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which is automatically accepted as the Tax Identification Number; the WFOE must complete supplementary taxpayer registration in the Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of business license issuance, select VAT taxpayer status (general VAT taxpayer vs small-scale taxpayer), register for corporate income tax, payroll withholding, stamp duty, and customs exporter registration, and open the export rebate tax ledger. For foreign non-resident entities without a permanent establishment in China, the WFOE paying dividends, interest, royalties, or services is the statutory withholding agent; the non-resident entity itself must complete simplified non-resident enterprise tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau at the competent tax authority where the withholding agent is located in order to obtain a non-resident TIN and to access treaty benefits. For the WFOE's cross-border e-commerce export activities, registration as a customs declaration unit and an export VAT rebate filer is mandatory to claim zero-rating.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None applicable as a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election under PRC law; a WFOE is automatically a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income. The only material election is the VAT taxpayer status election: below the CNY 5,000,000 mandatory general taxpayer threshold, the entity may elect simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment at a 3% levy rate, but such election forfeits input VAT credit and export rebate rights. Cross-border hybrid mismatch structures are subject to China's general anti-avoidance rules and special tax adjustment rules rather than elective mismatch regimes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Small-scale taxpayers below this threshold are subject to a simplified 3% VAT levy and do not qualify for input VAT credit or export zero-rating with refund. An export-oriented WFOE should elect general VAT taxpayer status despite the threshold because only general taxpayers may lawfully claim the export rebate and 0% VAT treatment.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating at 0% VAT is available for goods physically exported from mainland China to destinations outside the PRC customs territory, including Hong Kong SAR, provided the WFOE is a general VAT taxpayer, the goods are actually exported under customs supervision, and the sale is supported by genuine commercial documentation. For cross-border e-commerce retail and B2B export, eligible customs regulatory models include 9610 for retail parcels, 9710 for B2B direct export, and 9810 for exported goods stored in overseas warehouses. The nutraceutical products must satisfy PRC export food/health-food licensing requirements and any destination-market compliance rules; if the goods are not legally exportable or the entity lacks the required statutory license, the zero-rating claim will be denied. Qualifying input VAT on domestic purchases is refunded through the export rebate mechanism, using the exemption, offset, and refund method.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs pre-numbered export declaration with bill of entry details) evidencing actual physical export
  • Bill of Lading, or air waybill, or express logistics delivery certificate for cross-border e-commerce parcels
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or equivalent verified bank foreign exchange receipt voucher evidencing payment remitted from the Hong Kong buyer
  • Export sales invoice and commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas/Hong Kong purchaser
  • Purchase VAT special invoices and input VAT verification records for the exported nutraceutical goods

The WFOE must use the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System Phase IV and the Fully Digitized Electronic Invoice platform. All VAT special invoices and ordinary invoices must be issued through the approved e-invoice system, with real-time transmission and fiscal clearing of invoice data to the STA digital supervision platform. For exports, the export sales invoice, Customs Export Declaration, and logistics data must be reconciled through the China International Trade Single Window; the Export Rebate system cross-matches invoice data, customs declaration data, and foreign exchange receipt data before refund approval. Anti-fraud controls include digital invoice quotas, red invoice reversal controls, high-risk invoice detection, real-time risk classification, and automated blocking of irregular counterparties. There is no physical fiscal stamp in the traditional sense, but the electronic invoice number and digital stamp from the STA serve as the fiscal clearance evidence.

The State Taxation Administration and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct coordinated audits of export rebate files, matching customs declarations, e-invoices, logistics records, and foreign exchange collection data. Failure to repatriate the export proceeds in foreign exchange within the 180-day statutory window, or any false documentation, fictitious export, invoice fraud, or non-compliance with the cross-border e-commerce customs modes, triggers retroactive clawback of the full VAT refund received, late-payment surcharge interest, and administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax shortfall under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export tax refund claims may also constitute criminal tax fraud, causing disqualification from future export rebates, downgrade of customs credit rating, suspension of cross-border e-commerce export clearance, personal liability of responsible officers, and unlimited retrospective recovery where evasion or fraud is established.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls. The RMB is generally convertible for current account transactions (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties) subject to filing and review by SAFE and the remitting bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment, investment income, cross-border financing, and shareholders' loans, remain subject to SAFE registration, quota, and settlement restrictions. For this WFOE, the equity capital injected by the Hong Kong parent must be registered under the SAFE FDI system, and the capital can only be converted to RMB for approved operating purposes (salaries, rent, procurement, etc.) under the bank's verification. Profit remittances to the parent are permissible after Chinese CPA audited financial statements, tax settlement, and SAFE declarations; however, the payment must be made from the WFOE's post-registration settlement account and cannot exceed the verified distributable profits. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer a blanket compulsory conversion, but all export receivables in foreign currency must be repatriated to China and settled or retained in a designated foreign exchange account with the commercial bank, and the bank must verify the customs declaration and trade background. The overall assessment indicates a moderate-to-low convertibility environment for capital flows, with high compliance obligations but clear regulatory pathways if done correctly.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The standard repatriation window for export proceeds is within 90 days from the dated export customs declaration; enterprises with pre-export advances or documentary letters of credit may apply for extension up to a further 90 days, subject to SAFE approval. No mandatory conversion to RMB is enforced; exporters may maintain foreign currency balances in their settlement accounts but must eventually settle for any RMB expenditure, and all receipts must pass through the trade foreign exchange monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not legally viable as primary banking rail. Chinese domestic regulations reserve corporate payment accounts and FX custody to licensed onshore banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, WorldFirst) can support cross-border collection as aggregators, but cannot replace a WFOE's own mainland bank account for SAFE-registered capital flows, import/export settlement, or tax remittance. The WFOE must maintain an onshore bank account at a locally licensed bank; using an unlicensed EMI to settle trade transactions would violate SAFE and PBoC regulations and trigger penalty. Therefore, fintech EMIs are limited to supplementary receivables facilitation only for certain small-value cross-border e-commerce, provided the underlying transactions are cleared through a licensed local partner bank and reported in the trade monitoring system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent remits equity or shareholder loan capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated foreign currency capital account at the onshore bank, quoting the SAFE FDI purpose code in the instruction. The bank verifies the remittance against the WFOE's SAFE FDI registration and issues a transaction receipt. The WFOE, for eligible operating expenses such as salaries, rent, and raw materials, submits to the bank a board resolution, invoice, contract, and the capital verification report from a Chinese registered accountant. The bank then files a line to SAFE's Foreign Exchange Account System and executes the capital settlement into RMB, crediting the WFOE's basic RMB account within T+1. In case of shareholder loan, the loan must be registered with SAFE under the Foreign Debt registration regime and the principal plus interest are sent out only after the repayment date and tax withholding settlement.For payments to overseas supplement suppliers, the WFOE may issue a SWIFT MT103 from its foreign currency account after presenting import contracts, invoices, proof of customs goods arrival (bill of lading or customs declaration), and payment processing under the payment-after-verification mechanism at the onshore bank. For larger or open-account trade, use a Commercial Letter of Credit issued by the WFOE's bank (SWIFT MT700/707) or a Documentary Collection (URC522 DP/DA) via the correspondent bank, especially for relationship suppliers. In all cases, the bank must clear the payment in SAFE's trade monitoring system and ensure the purpose code matches the underlying goods classification (ISIC 4791, HS Chapters 30 and 2106). E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not legally viable as primary banking rail. Chinese domestic regulations reserve corporate payment accounts and FX custody to licensed onshore banks. Offshore EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Payoneer, WorldFirst) can support cross-border collection as aggregators, but cannot replace a WFOE's own mainland bank account for SAFE-registered capital flows, import/export settlement, or tax remittance. The WFOE must maintain an onshore bank account at a locally licensed bank; using an unlicensed EMI to settle trade transactions would violate SAFE and PBoC regulations and trigger penalty. Therefore, fintech EMIs are limited to supplementary receivables facilitation only for certain small-value cross-border e-commerce, provided the underlying transactions are cleared through a licensed local partner bank and reported in the trade monitoring system.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when, in a single tax year, related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible goods sales; RMB 100 million for intangible property contributions; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File is required when annual cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or the group-wide consolidated turnover exceeds specified State Taxation Administration thresholds. Documentation must be contemporaneous and filed within prescribed timelines upon request.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form under Article 47 of the EIT Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules. The WFOE must have actual contractual capacity, employees, and operational oversight of its e-commerce distribution activities. The Hong Kong shareholder must satisfy the beneficial ownership requirements and demonstrate real economic substance in Hong Kong, including a fixed place of business, hiring decisions, strategic management, and bank markets. Failure to demonstrate CIGA can result in denial of treaty benefits and impose a 10% punitive withholding tax, interest, and penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation Through Management Service Fees

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 44 of the EIT Law authorize recharacterization and transfer pricing adjustment. The Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late interest. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 applies where tax evasion exceeds RMB 100,000 and the evaded amount is over 10% of the payable tax, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or execute inflated service-fee contracts may be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax underpayment and forced restitution of diverted corporate assets. The tax authorities may impose travel bans and blacklisting under the Joint Discipline and Enforcement Measures for Tax Evasion, including prohibition from leaving the country. Where fraud is proven, individual directors face imprisonment and criminal fines for tax evasion.
Shell Company and Sham DTA Dividend Relief Claim

Statutory sanction: China's GAAR under Article 47 EIT Law and the recharacterization provisions exclude treaty benefits. The difference between the 10% statutory dividend WHT and the 5% DTA rate must be recovered with a 50% to 500% penalty, plus late surcharge. Criminal liability applies under Criminal Law Article 201, and the administrative fine for fraudulent tax preference claims can reach RMB 100,000.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and directors who executed the sham treaty claim face civil liability for the unpaid withholding tax and penalties. Personal assets may be attached for restitution; travel restrictions and blacklisting by tax and border control authorities apply under the Enforcement Measures for Tax Evasion. Criminal liability for tax fraud may result in imprisonment not exceeding 7 years, and directors who certified false documents may be prosecuted for forgery.
AML Failure and Undeclared Offshore E-commerce Revenue

Statutory sanction: Under the Tax Collection Law Article 63, concealed revenue is punished by a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax and daily interest surcharges. Violations of the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law attract monetary penalties up to RMB 2,000,000 for the institution and RMB 50,000 to 500,000 for responsible individuals, with license suspension. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 191 for money laundering carries imprisonment from 5 to 10 years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and the person-in-charge who participated in or condoned the concealment are personally liable for restitution of hidden revenue and unpaid taxes. The courts may impose personal asset freezing, travel bans, and passport surrender orders. Criminal prosecution for money laundering and tax evasion may lead to imprisonment for up to 10 years, in addition to personal fines and disqualification from acting as a director.
