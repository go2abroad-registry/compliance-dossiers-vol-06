Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on a WFOE under the PRC Company Law or the Foreign Investment Law. The sole shareholder must subscribe for the full registered capital set out in the Articles of Association, and such capital must be paid in within the contribution period specified in the Articles, which generally may not exceed five years unless a longer period is specifically approved. There is no requirement for blocked escrow accounts or any upfront deposit with the company registry. However, all foreign capital contributions must be remitted through a foreign exchange capital account and settled in accordance with the rules of the State Administration of Foreign Exchange; the total registered capital must be truthful and reasonably commensurate with the actual business scale.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) via the STA's official web portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The WFOE is first registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), and its business license carries the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days from issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) by filing its registered name, legal representative, registered address, business scope, shareholder details, bank account, accounting system, and estimated taxable activities. No separate VAT registration number is issued; the USCC is used for VAT, corporate income tax, and other national taxes. Step 3: For a foreign non-resident enterprise whose only PRC nexus is equity ownership of the WFOE, no mainland China TIN or VAT registration is required for passive shareholding; the WFOE, as withholding agent, withholds China-source corporate income tax on dividends and other payments to the non-resident parent under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Step 4: If the non-resident enterprise itself carries on taxable activities in the PRC through an establishment, branch, or place of business, it must separately register for tax affairs with the competent STA office and obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number before engaging in taxable business, issuing Chinese VAT invoices, or opening a tax-filed bank account.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for income tax purposes: the PRC does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch pass-through election. A WFOE is mandatorily taxed at the entity level on its worldwide income. The only relevant election is a VAT status election: an eligible small-scale taxpayer may elect to register as a general VAT taxpayer, and a general VAT taxpayer may in limited statutory categories elect simplified VAT assessment; neither election alters the WFOE's entity-level income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in cumulative taxable sales over any continuous 12-month period is the mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold; a business with lower taxable sales may also register voluntarily with sound accounting, and every established enterprise must obtain a Unified Social Credit Code and complete tax registration regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Physical export of cosmetics and personal care goods qualifies for the 0% VAT export rate when the goods are actually exported from the customs territory of the PRC, the sale is made to an overseas customer, the export customs declaration is completed and the goods are cleared by PRC customs, the exporter receives payment in foreign currency, and the foreign currency proceeds are repatriated to China through a designated foreign exchange bank. The WFOE must maintain the export documents, sales contracts, shipping evidence, and foreign exchange collection evidence to support the zero-rating and any corresponding VAT input credit refund under the export tax rebate system. Cross-border online retail export goods may also qualify under the statutory cross-border e-commerce export supervision arrangements provided the same physical-export and foreign-exchange repatriation tests are met.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration) submitted through the China International Trade Single Window and released by the General Administration of Customs of the People's Republic of China (GACC)
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill for air freight) evidencing shipment to the overseas buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Foreign Exchange Receipts Declaration Certificate issued by a designated PRC foreign exchange bank
  • Bank credit advice or foreign-exchange settlement record showing the export proceeds actually received and repatriated to the WFOE's bank account

The Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (e-invoice) system administered by the STA is mandatory for PRC taxpayers: invoice data must be created through the Electronic Taxpayer Service Platform (e-Tax) or STA-compliant integrated invoicing software and transmitted in real time to the STA data platform for clearing and anti-fraud review. E-invoices carry a unique electronic invoice number, a dynamic anti-forgery QR code or digital seal, and are verified automatically by the tax authority. Online retail transactions, including DTC beauty sales, must be recorded on the e-invoice platform or through the e-commerce service provider's tax-reporting integration, with transaction-level details made available to the STA for reconciliation against VAT returns and customs export declarations.

The STA, customs, and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange may jointly audit export zero-rating claims. If supporting documents are false, the export rebate is clawed back in full, the 13% VAT is assessed retroactively, late-payment surcharges accrue, and a penalty of one to five times the tax or refund amount may be imposed under the Tax Administration Law. Fraudulent export-rebate applications constitute criminal tax fraud under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, leading to criminal fines, confiscation of property, and imprisonment; false customs declarations are additionally sanctionable under the Customs Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Renminbi is not freely convertible on the capital account; current account transactions are generally liberalized for legitimate business with proper supporting documentation. The WFOE's cross-border profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is categorized as a current account dividend payment and is permissible after annual audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, board resolutions, and SAFE bank verification are submitted; no separate capital account approval is required. Foreign direct investment subscriptions from Hong Kong, shareholder loans, and eventual repatriation of invested capital remain capital account items that require FDI registration, opening of a SAFE-monitored capital account, and strict purpose-limited use. Export proceeds from online cosmetics retail are current account receipts and may be retained in foreign currency or voluntarily converted into RMB; however, each receipt must be reconciled with order data, customs declarations, and logistics records through the Trade Monitoring System. FX hedging and derivative transactions are allowed only when the WFOE can prove genuine underlying exposure. In practice, the WFOE can freely deploy RMB for daily operations, but cross-border capital flows must be pre-registered, verified at the bank, and kept separate from operating account flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE current account administration, there is no mandatory foreign exchange surrender or forced conversion ratio for the WFOE; export proceeds from the beauty retail business can be retained in a foreign currency settlement account or converted to RMB at the bank's spot rate at the WFOE's discretion. The statutory obligation is to repatriate the funds to a domestic bank account and to complete bank verification against customs declarations or cross-border e-commerce order data. In practice, the bank expects receipt and declaration within 90 days from the export customs date; longer settlement cycles require SAFE advance registration. For e-commerce platform sales, the WFOE must retain platform settlement reports, order files, product-level SKU data, logistics tracking numbers, and customs export lists so the bank can match each inflow before crediting the RMB settlement account.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), as the foreign exchange administration arm of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

10% statutory CIT withholding on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China under EIT Law Articles 3, 27 and 37. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File must be prepared when the total annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1,000,000,000. Local File must be prepared when related-party tangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000, financial-asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, intangible-asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000, or other related-party transactions in aggregate exceed RMB 40,000,000. Annual Related-Party Transaction Reporting Forms must be filed for any related-party transaction in the taxable year, regardless of amount.

Economic substance scope: China does not operate a separate Economic Substance Regulation filing regime like the UAE, but the substance-over-form doctrine and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under EIT Law Article 47 require that any Hong Kong entity claiming treaty benefits maintain real economic substance in Hong Kong. The tax authority will deny beneficial-ownership status and DTA relief where the Hong Kong affiliate is a mailbox, has no employees or premises, carries no risk, and lacks Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. Round-tripping arrangements may be recharacterized as domestic transactions, and the entire structure may be reassessed as a sham.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through round-trip conduit and false DTA claim

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may invoke EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and Article 120 Implementation Regulations to recharacterize the arrangement, deny the DTA benefits, impute the income to the WFOE, and impose 25% CIT plus interest. Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administrative Law, willful evasion is punishable by a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, and under Criminal Law Article 201 the evasion can result in criminal fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: The court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 if the directors and shareholders abuse the limited-liability structure to evade tax and harm creditors, imposing joint and several civil liability for unpaid taxes and restitution of diverted assets. The legal representative will be subject to exit-ban/blacklisting under Tax Collection Law Article 44 until all taxes and penalties are settled. Directors who knowingly execute the fraudulent structure are 'directly responsible persons' and face criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201/211, with imprisonment up to seven years and personal fines.
Transfer-pricing manipulation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 requires arm's-length pricing; Article 44 permits the tax authority to assess taxable income where documents are absent; Article 46 imposes thin-capitalization limits; Article 123 allows transfer-pricing adjustments within 10 years from the year of the transaction. False reporting attracts penalties under Article 64 of the Tax Collection and Administrative Law, and any underpaid tax is subject to a 50% to 500% penalty plus daily surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers who sign or approve false related-party returns can be held personally liable as responsible personnel under Criminal Law Article 201/211, with fines and imprisonment up to seven years. A director who caused the WFOE to be left without assets to pay tax may be ordered to make civil restitution, and under Company Law Article 20 the abuse of corporate form to defeat tax creditors can trigger personal joint and several liability. Exit restrictions are also applied to the legal representative until the tax authority is satisfied.
Non-resident withholding tax evasion on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 37 and 40 impose withholding obligations on the WFOE as payer; Article 69 of the Tax Collection and Administrative Law subjects the withholding agent to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the tax that was not withheld, and the tax authority may also recover the tax from the non-resident payee. Failing to pay withheld tax on time triggers daily late-payment surcharges; serious concealment leads to criminal tax-evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: The legal representative who authorizes the offshore remittance without proper withholding may be blocked from leaving China under Tax Collection Law Article 44. If the WFOE cannot pay, directors may be found personally liable for breach of fiduciary duty and for aiding tax evasion; assets transferred out of the WFOE to the Hong Kong parent can be clawed back through civil recovery, and directors face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to seven years and confiscation of illegally obtained assets.
