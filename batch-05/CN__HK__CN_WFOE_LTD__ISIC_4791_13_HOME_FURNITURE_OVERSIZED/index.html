Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through local provincial and municipal tax offices and the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), integrated with the Golden Tax System Phase IV, the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform, and the China International Trade Single Window for customs and trade data.

E-government filing portal

For the mainland China WFOE: (1) incorporate and register with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the national 'one-window' market entity registration system to obtain a business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). (2) Tax registration is completed automatically via data sharing between SAMR and STA; the USCC becomes the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number, and a separate tax registration certificate is no longer issued. (3) Activate the tax registration in the e-Tax portal within the statutory period and complete supplementary information including legal representative, accounting system, bank account filing, business scope, and shareholder details. (4) Determine VAT identity: elect small-scale taxpayer status where annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000, or register as a general VAT taxpayer, which is mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 or when the taxpayer needs to issue special VAT invoices. (5) Apply for the fully digitalized e-invoice module, obtain the taxpayer digital certificate (UKey), and configure invoicing APIs for the national e-invoice platform. (6) Complete export registration with China Customs under the International Trade Single Window, including foreign trade operator filing and Customs export tax rebate registration. For a foreign non-resident entity that has no WFOE or permanent establishment in mainland China but makes taxable supplies in China, the non-resident must register for non-resident tax status with the competent tax authority where it has an establishment; if there is no establishment, the PRC buyer or withholding agent must process non-resident withholding tax registration, and each remittance receives a case-specific tax identification reference for withholding collection.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No U.S.-style check-the-box election exists in China. Available statutory elections relevant to the WFOE include: (a) election between small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment and general VAT taxpayer treatment; (b) election of export tax rebate method, being 'exemption and rebate' for foreign trade enterprises purchasing goods for export, or 'exemption, credit, and rebate' for manufacturing/production enterprises exporting self-manufactured goods; (c) election to treat qualifying cross-border services supplied to non-resident customers as zero-rated where consumed wholly outside China; and (d) election concerning deemed-profit or actual-profit basis for non-resident withholding tax, subject to PRC tax law and applicable double tax agreements.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; all WFOEs are required to complete VAT registration upon incorporation regardless of turnover, but small-scale taxpayer treatment applies below RMB 5,000,000 unless general taxpayer status is voluntarily elected.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

PRC VAT zero-rating applies to goods sold by the WFOE that are physically exported from the mainland China customs territory to an overseas purchaser or consumer, including cross-border e-commerce parcel trade, provided that the goods are actually customs-declared for export, title and risk pass to the non-resident buyer, the export proceeds are received and repatriated in foreign exchange, the goods are not on the prohibited or no-rebate catalogue, and the taxpayer maintains a genuine commercial transaction with matching invoice, customs, logistics, and payment evidence. Hong Kong SAR is a separate customs territory, so goods exported from mainland China to Hong Kong customers qualify as zero-rated exports. For qualifying offshore digital services allocated to the WFOE, zero-rating requires that the service is supplied to a non-resident recipient, is consumed wholly outside China, and is documented with the prescribed cross-border service declaration and underlying contract.

  • Electronically accepted China Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) issued through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or multimodal or air waybill evidencing that the oversized furniture physically departed mainland China customs territory
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher or export proceeds credit notice and inward remittance certificate showing collected foreign currency export proceeds within the repatriation window
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract
  • Fully digitalized export e-invoice issued through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform
  • Export tax rebate application package matching the customs declaration number, invoice number, bill of lading number, and foreign exchange receipt record

China operates mandatory fully digitalized e-invoicing under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. All VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices and export invoices, must be issued through the STA National Electronic Invoice Service Platform, and invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in real time. Each invoice carries a unique invoice code, a cryptographic tax control stamp, and a QR code. High-volume online retailers integrate with the platform using mandatory APIs; traditional paper fiscal controls are replaced by digital tax control stamping. For export zero-rating, electronic invoice data is automatically reconciled with the China Customs export declaration, the logistics manifest, and the SAFE foreign exchange monitoring system through the International Trade Single Window before a VAT rebate or zero-rating claim is approved.

The STA, China Customs, and SAFE jointly audit export zero-rating through cross-system data matching. If the required customs clearance or proof of physical departure is absent, the foreign exchange is not collected within 180 days, or the transaction is determined to be artificial or fraudulently documented, the zero-rating is retrospectively reversed. The WFOE must repay the VAT rebate or exemption received plus statutory late payment interest at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties may reach up to five times the defrauded amount, and serious cases are referred for criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax rebate claims or false issuance of VAT invoices. Additional consequences include suspension or cancellation of export tax rebate qualifications, loss of good taxpayer status, inclusion in the national tax dishonesty blacklist, joint enforcement by Customs and SAFE, and personal liability of responsible directors and operators.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The offshore SAR domicile of the Hong Kong holding entity does not exempt the Chinese WFOE from onshore convertibility constraints. RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions such as trade settlement, service fees and profit remittances after tax verification, but remains subject to approval-based capital-account administration. The establishment of a WFOE requires SAFE registration of the foreign investment; the registered capital constitutes an FDI flow that can be converted into RMB only through a bank-designated Foreign Currency Capital Account and in compliance with the effective foreign-investment negative list. Profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, completed CIT settlement and corresponding withholding tax payment certificates. While trade-related payments benefit from current-account liberalisation, capital-account remittances for offshore treasury concentration, intercompany loans and shareholder distributions must pass a genuine transaction and use-of-funds review. Consequently, a disciplined documentary flow and pre-registration with SAFE are mandatory; conversion of surplus RMB into foreign currency without underlying trade or debt service exposure is generally not permitted.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account or via a licensed settlement institution no later than 180 days from the date of export declaration; there is no fixed mandatory percentage of forced conversion into RMB, but 100% of receipts must be surrendered either into a foreign currency account or sold to the bank at the client's instruction, with SAFE verification through the banking system; any deviation from the repatriation timeline requires written justification and SAFE approval.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its wholly administered State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the relevant special administrative region authority for any offshore HK treasury overlay.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for primary corporate treasury use. Offshore digital EMIs, including foreign-licensed e-money institutions and global payment service providers such as Stripe and Affirm BNPL, do not hold a domestic RMB business licence and cannot legally provide a WFOE's statutory capital account or bank settlement account. Under PRC law, only banks lawfully established in China can open local-currency and foreign-exchange accounts for WFOEs; cross-border payment institutions require a PBOC/CBIRC business licence and must clear through a licensed onshore bank. Therefore, fintech EMIs may be used only as downstream merchant settlement tools or ancillary payment collection channels in partnership with a licensed domestic acquiring bank, and never as the primary corporate treasury or forex clearing rail.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI equity injections must be remitted from the Hong Kong shareholder via SWIFT MT103 with an unambiguous reconciliation reference to the SAFE FDI registration code and the WFOE's Foreign Currency Capital Account number. The remitting bank must use the SAFE foreign-exchange purpose code applicable to direct-investment capital contribution, and funds may be credited to the WFOE account only after the Chinese bank records the transaction and verifies the underlying truthfulness. After receipt, the WFOE may settle the capital into RMB under the positive list; a certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report evidencing the contribution, which is then submitted to the relevant market-regulatory authority. Any intercompany advance or shareholder loan requires separate SAFE registration and is not permitted under ordinary capital-account restrictions.For direct raw-material, freight and overseas marketing payments from the WFOE to foreign suppliers or service providers, the standard clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate SAFE transaction code and supporting valid customs, contract and tax documents. For large or repeat purchases of finished furniture inventory, an irrevocable documentary letter of credit confirmed by a first-class bank provides supplier protection; alternatively, documentary collections (D/P and D/A) can be used once the supplier relationship is established and invoice values are moderate. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low for primary corporate treasury use. Offshore digital EMIs, including foreign-licensed e-money institutions and global payment service providers such as Stripe and Affirm BNPL, do not hold a domestic RMB business licence and cannot legally provide a WFOE's statutory capital account or bank settlement account. Under PRC law, only banks lawfully established in China can open local-currency and foreign-exchange accounts for WFOEs; cross-border payment institutions require a PBOC/CBIRC business licence and must clear through a licensed onshore bank. Therefore, fintech EMIs may be used only as downstream merchant settlement tools or ancillary payment collection channels in partnership with a licensed domestic acquiring bank, and never as the primary corporate treasury or forex clearing rail.
