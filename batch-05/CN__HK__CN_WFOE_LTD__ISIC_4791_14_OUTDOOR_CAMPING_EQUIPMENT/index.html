Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through local STA offices and the national Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online filing portal, with bonded/cross-border trade clearance integrated through the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in the People's Republic of China, the enterprise must complete its tax registration within 30 days after obtaining the business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The tax registration is processed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau using the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the sole statutory TIN and VAT identification number. For foreign non-resident entities without a Chinese establishment, the procedure requires: (i) appointing a locally authorised tax representative or process agent; (ii) registering through the e-Tax platform under the non-resident enterprise tax account using the home-jurisdiction TIN and a declaration of Chinese source income; and (iii) obtaining a VAT registration number where chargeable supplies are made in China. In addition to filing the Taxpayer Information Report, the WFOE must report its bank deposit accounts to the competent STA within 15 days, set its VAT tax-payer classification (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), obtain an invoice quota, and activate the digital invoice entitlement. No separate paper tax registration certificate is issued; the USCC on the business license is ratified by the STA as the legal taxpayer identifier.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 0 for ordinary VAT/tax registration (mandatory within 30 days of business license issuance); RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the turnover threshold that mandates registration as a General VAT Taxpayer, unless the taxpayer already elects general status voluntarily.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% export VAT may be claimed only by a registered General VAT Taxpayer on goods physically exported from the Chinese customs territory to a non-resident customer, where the goods are covered by a lawful Customs export declaration and the export sales proceeds are repatriated and verified in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. Qualifying cross-border offshore digital services are zero-rated only when they fall within the STA's published zero-rating service catalogue (e.g., certain software, R&D and information-technology services supplied to overseas recipients); other exported services are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated, with the burden of proving overseas consumption resting on the supplier. The enterprise must also maintain contemporaneous evidence demonstrating the actual export movement, the overseas customer, and arm's-length consideration in convertible foreign exchange.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (Customs Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill proving physical export from Chinese territory
  • Commercial Invoice for Export
  • Export Sales Contract, Purchase Order and any incoterm package
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt and Verification/Settlement Certificate (export proceeds receiving/settlement document)
  • Zero-rated VAT e-invoice or export invoice issued in accordance with STA forms
  • Logistics records, warehousing and export-shipping documents

China currently mandates e-invoicing under the STA's Fully-Digitalised Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system and the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Every VAT-registered entity must issue sales invoices through the State Taxation Administration e-invoice service platform, either directly via the e-Tax portal or by integrating enterprise ERP/billing systems to the STA's invoice APIs. Each invoice receives a unique national invoice number, anti-counterfeit digital signature, and real-time reporting to the STA at the time of issuance. The tax-control blacklist system screens abnormal invoices and applies anti-fraud controls; there is no separate physical stamping requirement for FDEI invoices, but the transaction data must be cleared electronically with the STA before the invoice is considered fiscally valid.

Where an export zero-rating claim fails any substantive or documentary test, the STA will claw back the VAT refund or input credits together with statutory late interest, re-impose the 13% output VAT on the transaction retroactively, and may impose an administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the tax evaded or refund improperly obtained under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. In addition, the exporter may be removed from the export-tax-refund facilitation programme for a compliance block period of up to 36 months, and the responsible persons may incur criminal liability for fraudulent VAT invoices or export rebate fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. The auditor may also apply General Anti-Avoidance Rules and adjust the transaction on substance-over-form grounds where the zero-rating is supported only by circular or sham cross-border logistics.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese RMB is convertible on current-account items such as legitimate trade in goods and services, remittance of earnings and interest, and travel/education expenses, subject to documentary verification through the bank and SAFE monitoring systems. Capital-account items, however, remain restricted and controlled: inbound FDI, outbound investment, external borrowing, and cross-border securities transactions require registration, approval, quotas, or filing with SAFE. For a HK-parent-owned WFOE, the initial injection of subscribed capital is classified as a capital-account inflow and must be made through a Foreign Currency Capital Account after SAFE FDI pre-registration. Once the funds arrive, they can be settled into CNY at the bank, but must be used within the approved business scope; the existing policy continues to ban investment in securities and wealth-management products outside the restricted scope. Profit remittance by a WFOE is generally permitted as current-account dividend remittance after audit, tax payment, and board resolution, provided the enterprise has accumulated unallocated profits and has completed the annual FDI multi-state meeting/examination requirements. Safeguards such as the Trade Monitoring System, AML questionnaires, and post-verification inspections remain in place. Overall, the regime is workable for a genuine e-commerce exporter, but it requires disciplined documentation, specific purpose declarations, and patience with bank compliance committees.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes mandatory foreign currency surrender. Under SAFE's current-account regulations, export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated within 180 days of the customs export declaration date and must be transferred to a materially active domestic foreign exchange settlement account. The WFOE may then retain the proceeds in foreign currency, convert them to CNY at a market rate, or use them for permitted current-account payments. A mandatory conversion ratio is not applicable. Every receipt is matched against customs declarations, invoices, packing lists, and logistics records via SAFE's Trade Monitoring System; discrepancies above the tolerance are frozen or rejected.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent originates the SWIFT MT103 payment to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Currency Capital Account at a domestic Tier-1 bank. The payment must reference the SAFE Foreign Exchange Registration Number and the SAFE capital-account purpose code for FDI, and the narrative must state 'Share capital contribution to WFOE under FDI Registration'. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the credit under the capital account and performs the FDI capital check. If the contribution exceeds the registered capital or the investment project changes, an amendment to the FDI registration is required. After verification, funds can be converted to CNY in the capital settlement account subject to the 'negative list' restrictions and can only be used business operations approved in the WFOE license. Extra care is needed to avoid a 'capital arrival and immediate rapid outbound transfer' pattern, which may trigger anti-reverse capital flight alerts.For import of raw materials or payment to overseas suppliers, the WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 payments via cross-border RMB (CIPS) or USD where allowed, after submitting the underlying trade contract, invoice, customs declaration, and tax exemption/form declarations. For large or customised order unit purchases, a commercial letter of credit (L/C) or documentary collection is appropriate, with documents presented through the bank's trade department. For small parcels of camping gear from suppliers, a bank remittance is the primary rail, to be reconciled with export proceeds under the current account. All outbound disbursements must be matched with the Trade Monitoring System to avoid false transactions.Real-demand RMB/USD forward contracts with domestic designated banks, up to the amount and tenor of verified trade or dividend exposures.Plain-vanilla FX swap agreements with domestic banks for the same real-demand exposure tenor, allowing the WFOE to bridge CNY liquidity needs without speculation.Currency options or corridor products are permissible only when underlying trade documents demonstrate a genuine hedging need.Offshore NDFs referencing CNY are prohibited for onshore entities; regulatory scrutiny arises if the WFOE attempts to hedge via HK branches or unlicensed fintech platforms.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42, a PRC taxpayer must prepare a Local File if its annual related-party transactions reach: RMB 200 million or more for tangible assets; RMB 100 million or more for intangible assets; RMB 100 million or more for services; or RMB 100 million or more for financial assets. A Master File is required when aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion. Country-by-Country reporting is required for a group with consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion. The WFOE should file the annual related-party transaction forms with the CIT return when related-party transactions exceed the reporting threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies the substance-over-form principle and General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47). Related-party arrangements without a reasonable commercial purpose may be recharacterised. For a mainland WFOE owned by a Hong Kong intermediary, the Hong Kong beneficial owner must carry on core income-generating activities and have real substance; otherwise, treaty relief and tax-free offshore claims fail. The WFOE should maintain actual e-commerce operation functions, inventory risk, customer returns, supplier negotiation, logistics oversight and strategic decision-making in China. If the Hong Kong company is only a nominee shareholder or invoice pass-through, SAT may tax profits as if they belonged to the WFOE or to the actual shareholder in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion by concealing e-commerce turnover

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 requires recovery of the underpaid tax and late payment surcharge plus a fine of 50%-500% of the amount evaded. Under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, a fraudulent declaration or concealment of taxable income constitutes tax evasion, punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years and an unlimited fine if the amount and proportional thresholds are met. Tax authorities may also publicise the enterprise and the responsible person as a serious tax violator.

Director personal liability: The legal representative, finance director and actual controller can face joint and several civil liability for restitution of the underpaid tax if the corporate veil is pierced. They may be prohibited from leaving China through exit-restriction mechanisms, disqualified from serving as company directors in serious cases, and criminally prosecuted as the directly responsible personnel for tax evasion.
Evasive transfer pricing and intercompany profit shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Article 41 empowers the tax authority to make an arm's-length adjustment of related-party transactions irrespective of the contractual labels. Article 44 authorises assessment where documentation is not provided; Articles 47 and 48 allow GAAR adjustments and collection. The adjustment may include late payment interest and a 5-year or 10-year look-back period. A TP adjustment can also be exposed as tax evasion if the artificial structure is knowingly used to reduce mainland taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or sign false intercompany contracts and TP disclosures may be considered joint tortfeasors in the evasion. The corporate veil can be pierced under the PRC Company Law if abuse of limited liability harms creditors or the tax authorities. Directors may be ordered to make personal restitution, face travel bans, and be subject to criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 where they knowingly participated in the artificial pricing scheme.
Shell Hong Kong intermediary without economic substance

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny beneficial ownership and treaty relief, applying the statutory 10% WHT on dividends, interest and royalties, plus late payment surcharge and a 50%-500% evasion penalty if deliberate misreporting is established. Under EIT Law Article 47, GAAR permits the transaction to be recharacterised; the HK conduit's tax benefits are ignored. The arrangement can also be treated as a fraud on the tax administration and referred for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: If the actual management of the HK shell is in mainland China through the same directors, the legal personality of both companies may be disregarded and the individual directors treated as the controlling taxpayers. Directors can be held jointly and severally liable for the corporate tax debt, may be blacklisted and face exit restrictions, and can be criminally liable under Criminal Law Article 201 for using a false corporate form to evade PRC taxation. When cross-border assistance is requested, Hong Kong courts can enforce PRC tax judgments or provide evidence to support criminal prosecution.
