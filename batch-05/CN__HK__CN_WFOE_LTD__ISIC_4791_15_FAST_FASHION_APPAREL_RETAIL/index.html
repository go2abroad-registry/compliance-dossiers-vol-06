Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under PRC law, an ordinary electronic-retail WFOE has no statutory minimum registered capital, no mandatory paid-up capital at incorporation, and no escrowed blocked capital account rule. The articles of association must state the registered capital and the capital contribution schedule; if no shorter period is stated in the articles, each shareholder shall pay subscribed capital in full within five years from incorporation. The foreign investor's contribution may be credited through a bank-designated foreign-exchange capital account and requires no certified public accountant verification unless the industry imposes a higher standard.

If the company later directly operates a warehouse or fulfillment center, the local Fire and Rescue Authority will require a fire safety inspection certificate and the Emergency Management Bureau will require a workplace safety registration and approved fire evacuation plan.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is conducted through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal operated by provincial STA offices and integrated with the China International Trade Single Window for customs and foreign-exchange-linked declarations.

E-government filing portal

For the cross-border fast-fashion WFOE, the primary statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) obtained from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) during business license registration. Step 1: file the WFOE incorporation and foreign investment record through SAMR's online registration system and receive the business license, which automatically contains the USCC. Step 2: within 30 calendar days of license issuance, complete tax registration on the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) platform, designating the taxpayer as a general VAT taxpayer, selecting ISIC 4791 / NAICS 458110 retail trade scope, and entering legal representative, bank settlement information, and beneficial ownership data. Step 3: the STA activates the tax registration record and the USCC becomes the functional TIN/VAT registration number; no separate VAT certificate is issued. Step 4: for a foreign non-resident enterprise that has no Chinese establishment but is making taxable supplies in China, the non-resident enterprise must apply to the competent STA office for a non-resident taxpayer ID, supported by its foreign registration certificate, commercial contract, and appointed tax withholding agent, and must register before the first taxable supply. Step 5: if the WFOE is also registered under Hong Kong SAR registration, the Hong Kong Business Registration Number and Chinese USCC must be mapped in the intercompany transfer-pricing and customs records, but only the USCC is used for PRC VAT and corporate income tax filing.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual VAT-taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. Taxpayers with annual turnover at or below RMB 5,000,000 may register as small-scale taxpayers, with voluntary general taxpayer registration available if accounting is compliant. A foreign non-resident seller making taxable supplies in China through an e-commerce platform has no RMB threshold and must register or appoint a withholding agent before the first taxable supply.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT applies to goods, including fast-fashion apparel and footwear, that are physically exported from the customs territory of China and sold to overseas buyers, where the seller is a VAT general taxpayer, export proceeds are collected through banking channels and repatriated to China, and the exporter submits an electronic export VAT refund application through the STA e-Tax platform. Cross-Border E-Commerce Retail Export enterprises may also qualify when the goods are purchased or sold through a registered e-commerce platform, shipped out of China by express or carrier, and supported by platform transaction records.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Bill of Entry) issued via the China International Trade Single Window
  • Original or electronic Bill of Lading, airway bill, or e-commerce shipment waybill evidencing physical departure from PRC customs territory
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued export proceeds collection advice showing receipt of foreign exchange from the overseas buyer
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the customs declaration and payment rails
  • Export contract or electronic purchase order record generated by the Shopify or Klarna BNPL payment ecosystem
  • STA e-Tax export VAT refund declaration and input-VAT allocation supporting statement

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV real-time fiscal clearing system. VAT special invoices and ordinary VAT invoices must be generated through the STA-approved e-invoicing platform, carry a tax control anti-counterfeit code, and be transmitted to the STA in near real time via API or the e-Tax interface. The WFOE must integrate its e-commerce order-to-cash flow with the fully digitalised electronic invoice technology so that sales invoices are cleared before dispatch; no parallel paper-only invoicing is recognized for VAT input credits or export refunds.

Export zero-rating claims are jointly scrutinised by the STA and China Customs. If the physical export, payment authenticity, or customs data does not reconcile, the refund is clawed back with statutory late-payment interest. Administrative penalties under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China range from 50% to five times the amount of tax underpaid or falsely refunded. Fraudulent export-tax-refund claims attract criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, including imprisonment, fines, and confiscation; additional customs anti-smuggling penalties, downgrade or revocation of export-tax-refund filing status, and inclusion in the tax-integrity blacklist may apply.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (CNY) is convertible on the current account for trade in goods and services, which includes cross-border e-commerce retail proceeds. Enterprises such as the CN WFOE LTD engaged in fast-fashion apparel retail are permitted to receive foreign currency from overseas sales and must either surrender the proceeds to designated foreign exchange banks within a statutory period or retain them in a foreign currency settlement account, subject to SAFE's verification of the underlying transaction authenticity via the digital foreign exchange monitoring system. Capital account convertibility remains heavily restricted: equity injections, shareholder loans, and cross-border funding movements require separate approval or filing with SAFE under the Foreign Investment Negative List and the Foreign Exchange Registration for Direct Investment (FDI) regime. For profit remittances, the WFOE must first complete the annual statutory audit, confirm tax clearance with the tax authority, and then provide a board resolution and audited financial statements to the bank, which will process the dividend remittance under the current account 'Repatriation of Foreign Investment' purpose code. There are no mandatory conversion ratios for dividend outbound remittances; the bank purchases foreign exchange on behalf of the entity at market rates. Practical hurdles include enhanced scrutiny on cross-border payments involving related parties, covenant-laden trade documentation for high-volume low-value e-commerce receivables, and potential volatility in USD/CNY during large conversion events. Overarching SAFE policy also requires all cross-border e-commerce transactions to be reported through the 'Cross-border E-commerce Retail Import/Export' information system, with payment institutions acting as agents only where licensed. Given the low-risk apparel retail profile, the convertibility assessment is favourable for current account items but requires rigorous compliance documentation for capital account transactions and any onward financing from its Hong Kong parent.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE Circular 28 of 2019 and the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations (2020 revision), export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into China and either sold to a designated foreign exchange bank or retained in a foreign exchange settlement account. For general trade and cross-border e-commerce, the statutory repatriation deadline is 90 days from the date of export declaration (or from the date of customs clearance for e-commerce parcels, subject to periodic extension for certain marketplaces). There is no mandatory conversion ratio; entities may opt to retain 100% of foreign currency earnings in their current account, but any sale is at market rate. However, for cross-border e-commerce where settlement is through third-party payment institutions (e.g., PayPal, PingPong, LianLian), the funds must be credited through a licensed payment institution to the entity's domestic settlement account, and the underlying order data, customs clearance data, and logistics evidence must be matched with the currency receipt within the same reporting period to satisfy SAFE's authenticity check.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File compulsory where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property or financial-asset transfers, and RMB 40 million for other related-party service transactions. Master File is required for groups with aggregate annual consolidated revenue exceeding RMB 5.5 billion in the prior year or where Chinese CbCR filing obligations apply.

Economic substance scope: The PRC WFOE is a Chinese tax resident by incorporation and must maintain real economic activity in mainland China, including management, employees, inventory, and decision-making related to its fast-fashion e-commerce operations. The Mainland-Hong Kong DTA and China's General Anti-Abuse Rule require that any HK entity claiming treaty benefits perform core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong with real people, premises, and risk-bearing capacity. ESR-type substance is not internationally imposed on the mainland WFOE directly, but the HK parent must avoid being classified as a shell or conduit for treaty shopping.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Article 46, SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42; the tax authority may recharacterise the payments, disallow excess interest, make transfer-pricing adjustments, impose correction interest, and apply the Article 63 Tax Collection and Administration Law penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded tax, plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false related-party contracts or false invoices may be ordered to restore corporate assets to the WFOE; the statutory legal representative may be blacklisted, subjected to travel bans, and if tax evasion is proved, may face criminal prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to seven years and criminal fines.
Tax Evasion Through E-Commerce Revenue Omission

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law authorises collection of the unpaid tax, a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge, and a penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. If the evaded amount is relatively large and exceeds 10% of the tax due, the case is criminally referred under Article 201 of the Criminal Law.

Director personal liability: Directors and the financial officer who sign false returns or direct concealment through offshore payment rails face personal civil liability for the underpaid tax if corporate funds were diverted; authorities may freeze personal assets, impose travel bans/blacklisting under Article 44 TAL, and upon criminal conviction responsible persons may be sentenced to imprisonment of up to seven years, with fines and confiscation of proceeds.
Treaty Abuse and Shell Company Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: The competent tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the DTA, the OECD/G20 base erosion principle, and CIT Law General Anti-Abuse Rule Article 47; the WFOE becomes liable for the full 10% WHT on dividends and the full 10% on interest/royalties, plus late-payment surcharges and a 50%-500% penalty for using false or misleading facts to obtain treaty relief; the beneficial ownership filing is revoked and tax is re-collected.

Director personal liability: The WFOE's legal representative and the directing minds involved in signing the sham HK arrangements may be blacklisted for outstanding tax debts, denied exit privileges, personally liable to restore diverted dividend funds under civil asset-restoration rules, and criminally prosecuted for fraudulent treaty relief or tax evasion under Article 201 if intentional concealment or false declarations are established, carrying imprisonment up to seven years.
