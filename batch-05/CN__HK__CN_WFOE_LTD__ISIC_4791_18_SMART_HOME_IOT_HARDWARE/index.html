Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

No statutory minimum registered capital for a WFOE under the PRC Company Law (2018). The registered capital is determined by the shareholders and must be fully subscribed within the period specified in the Articles of Association (typically 5-30 years). The capital may be paid in cash or in kind, subject to valuation. No escrow account is required; however, the capital must be injected into the company's bank account in China and used for legitimate business purposes. For certain restricted industries, additional capital requirements may apply, but ISIC 4791 (retail via internet) is not restricted.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, with electronic filing through the STA Electronic Taxpayer Portal (e-Tax / Electronic Tax Bureau); the General Administration of Customs (GACC) for customs and export registration; and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for foreign exchange registration and repatriation verification. For the Hong Kong SAR parent entity, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong handles profits tax matters, but Hong Kong does not impose VAT, GST, or sales tax.

E-government filing portal

STEP 1: The foreign non-resident investor, typically the Hong Kong SAR parent, must procure a notarized and apostilled certificate of incorporation and proof of directors/shareholders; STEP 2: Incorporate the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) as a limited liability company through the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) integrated online registration platform; retailing of smart home IoT hardware is generally outside the foreign investment negative list and does not require a separate investment license; STEP 3: Upon issuance of the SAMR Business License, the WFOE obtains a Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which simultaneously serves as the PRC tax identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number; tax registration is automatically synchronized with the STA through the government data-sharing architecture; STEP 4: Log on to the STA e-Tax portal to complete taxpayer status confirmation, tax official jurisdiction filing, accounting basis, tax filing cycle, bank account filing, and legal representative/tax officer identity verification; STEP 5: Confirm VAT taxpayer classification; if expected annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is required; below the threshold, the entity may elect general taxpayer status or remain a small-scale taxpayer; STEP 6: Apply for the electronic fiscal UKey/digital certificate and obtain approval for invoice types, invoice quotas, and maximum invoice amount limits; STEP 7: For export trade, register the WFOE at the local Customs office to obtain the Customs Registration Code (same as USCC), file the export tax rebate/exemption registration in the STA export-tax module, and complete SAFE foreign exchange entity registration; STEP 8: No physical tax ID card is issued; the USCC printed on the Business License is the sole statutory tax identification credential, and all tax filings are performed electronically.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not operate a US-style check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch election mechanism. The only relevant statutory election is the VAT general taxpayer registration election for taxpayers below the RMB 5,000,000 mandatory threshold, and the choice between the general VAT regime and the simplified small-scale taxpayer regime, subject to STA approval.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales (mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold); taxpayers below RMB 5,000,000 may voluntarily register as general VAT taxpayers or remain small-scale taxpayers with reduced rates and simplified compliance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply PRC VAT zero-rating and the corresponding input VAT refund (export tax rebate) for goods physically exported from China customs territory to overseas markets, including general trade exports and cross-border e-commerce retail exports of smart home IoT hardware. The statutory criteria require that the exporter is an eligible PRC VAT-registered enterprise, the goods are sold to an overseas buyer or overseas consumer, the goods leave China customs territory through a declared export customs procedure, payment is received in foreign exchange, and the foreign exchange proceeds are repatriated to and settled within China through a SAFE-registered bank. Qualifying offshore services consumed wholly outside China may also be zero-rated if all statutory cross-border service invoice and payment evidence is maintained.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Customs Declaration Form with customs declaration number and barcode)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (or sea/air freight forwarding receipt for cross-border e-commerce logistics)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Bank Export Proceeds Collection and Settlement Certificate evidencing FX repatriation through SAFE-registered banking channels
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Export Contract, Sale Order, or cross-border e-commerce platform transaction record
  • Export tax rebate schedule as filed in the STA e-Tax export rebate module
  • Customs export inspection/inspection certificates, if applicable for electronic/IoT hardware

China mandates electronic invoice issuance through the STA Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) system under the Golden Tax System Phase IV framework. All VAT special and normal invoices must be issued via the STA e-invoice platform or approved API connectors, encrypted in real time, assigned a unified national invoice number, and transmitted to STA for fiscal clearance before issuance is completed. The system applies anti-fraud digital stamping through the Golden Tax system's tax-control codes and real-time monitoring; paper manual fapiao is restricted to transitional cases, and high-risk vendors or large-amount invoices may require purchaser authentication and online verification.

The STA and Customs authorities may retrospectively audit the zero-rating claim by matching the export declaration, logistics documents, VAT invoices, foreign exchange receipts, and bank settlement records. If the export sale is unverified, deemed fictitious, or fails the FX repatriation deadline, the zero-rated treatment is disallowed, the WFOE must reverse output VAT at 13% on the export invoice value, repay any refunded input VAT, pay daily late-payment surcharge, and may face administrative fines ranging from 50% to 5 times the tax amount under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law. Fraudulent export tax refund claims attract fines of 1 to 5 times the refunded amount, revocation of export tax rebate qualifications, heightened customs inspection status, and criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for personnel involved.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the RMB is only partially convertible. Current-account items, including cross-border e-commerce export receipts, trade settlement for hardware components, and ordinary commercial service fees, are in principle convertible following verification by a SAFE-designated bank against genuine underlying documentation. Capital-account items remain tightly administered: foreign direct investment into the WFOE requires SAFE registration, a compliant capital settlement account, and proof that the investment is not in a prohibited negative-list sector. The HK parent cannot simply transfer funds into the WFOE as working capital without filing the appropriate FDI or foreign-debt registration. Dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent is a capital-account current-administration matter: the WFOE must distribute from after-tax audited profits, pay or withhold the relevant enterprise income tax and dividend withholding tax under the China-HK double taxation arrangement, and provide a board-approved dividend resolution, an audit report, tax clearance proof, and the bank's SAFE verification before an outward remittance is released. Funds received for export sales must be repatriated to China and reported in the SAFE cross-border monitoring system; they may be retained in a foreign-exchange settlement account or converted to RMB at the applicable rate, but they must remain supported by trade documents and cannot be used for speculative investment. Therefore, the overall theme is de facto convertibility on current account after documentation, but pre-clearance and registration gatekeeping on capital account.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from smart-home IoT hardware sold to overseas marketplace customers must be repatriated into the mainland banking system and collected through a SAFE-designated foreign-exchange bank. Under the SAFE trade-in-goods administration, the WFOE must declare and verify each export remittance in the bank's electronic system within 30 calendar days of receipt; if payment is received substantially before shipment or after customs clearance, the WFOE must pre-register the underlying exposure in the SAFE trade monitoring system as an advance receipt or deferred collection. There is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio under the current managed float; export proceeds may be retained in a foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntarily converted to RMB at the prevailing market rate, provided the balance remains traceable to genuine trade invoices, customs declarations, logistics records, and platform settlement reports. Retained foreign exchange cannot be used for speculative foreign-exchange positions and must be reconciled periodically with actual trade flows.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority supervises the Hong Kong treasury vehicle but does not impose exchange controls on the HK entity

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low feasibility as a primary treasury rail for the mainland WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Payoneer, and similar platforms do not hold a PRC deposit-taking licence and are not authorized by PBOC or SAFE to maintain local RMB or foreign-currency current accounts for an onshore Chinese corporate entity. They cannot perform the SAFE verification, export proceeds reporting, FDI capital registration, or trade-in-goods forms that a Chinese licensed bank is obligated to process. For the Hong Kong parent, an offshore EMI may be acceptable for merchant collections and multi-currency cash management, especially in support of Stripe or Shopify Payments settlement, but any funds destined for the WFOE must enter China through a genuine FDI or current-account remittance using a licensed domestic bank or a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution with a filed SAFE programme. The legally viable architecture is therefore a domestic Tier-1 bank as the WFOE's primary account holder, with offshore EMIs confined to the HK treasury as ancillary collection rails and never as the WFOE's bank of record.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All capital injections from the Hong Kong parent to the PRC WFOE must flow through the SAFE-registered foreign direct investment route. The WFOE first completes the SAFE direct-investment foreign-exchange registration at a designated domestic bank and obtains the unique FDI registration code. The bank then opens the capital contribution account and the RMB capital settlement account in the WFOE's name. The Hong Kong parent executes a board resolution authorizing the transfer and remits via SWIFT MT103, with the remittance narrative clearly stating that it is a capital contribution to registered capital. The receiving bank validates the remittance against the SAFE FDI code, the approved business scope, and the negative list; if conversion from USD to RMB is required, conversion occurs only after the bank verifies the registration and the WFOE submits the capital account payment requisition. A PRC-registered CPA should issue a capital verification report confirming actual contribution against the registered capital. No individual personal account, non-bank payment platform, or undeclared third-party settlement account may be used for capital injection, as that would void the FDI filing and trigger SAFE penalties.For the WFOE paying overseas suppliers, the primary clearing rail is a SWIFT MT103 corporate payment carrying the import contract, invoice, packing list, bill of lading, and the corresponding customs import declaration number. For high-value or complex procurements, the WFOE should use an irrevocable documentary letter of credit issued via the domestic bank as an MT700, or a documentary collection under DP/DA terms through the presenting bank, both of which provide documentary discipline and reduce payment fraud exposure. All trade payments must be reported in the SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring system and reconciled with customs data. If the beneficiary is in Hong Kong or another offshore jurisdiction and chooses RMB settlement, the WFOE should route the payment through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS), which preserves bank-grade clearing while eliminating FX conversion risk. Advance payments above the local bank prudential thresholds, or payments deferred significantly beyond standard trade terms, must be pre-registered with the bank under SAFE trade financing administration. The WFOE must retain all trade documents for at least five years for audit by SAFE, the tax bureau, and the bank's financial crime compliance team.Plain-vanilla onshore RMB/USD deliverable forward contracts, entered into for genuine trade receipts or payables, with the underlying invoices and contracts documented in the bank's real-demand derivative review file.Onshore FX swaps between RMB and eligible major currencies, used strictly to manage temporary liquidity mismatches from export receipts and import payments, with matched notional and maturity to the underlying business flow.Cross-currency swaps (CNY/USD or CNY/HKD) for long-term foreign-currency liabilities or offshore financing, provided the exposure is registered with SAFE under the real-demand principle and the hedge does not exceed the identifiable principal, interest, or cash-flow exposure.Plain-vanilla FX options, including call and put strategies, permitted for hedging projected trade cash flows where the WFOE can demonstrate a verified current-account exposure; speculative option positions and offshore non-deliverable forwards are not permissible for the mainland WFOE.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Prepare China Local File when annual related-party transactions exceed the following thresholds: tangible property transfers above RMB 400 million; intangible property transfers above RMB 200 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; and other related-party transactions above RMB 200 million. Economic substance scope: The WFOE must demonstrate real economic substance in China, with physical office premises, employees, inventory risk, procurement functions, logistics control, and board-level decision-making. The Hong Kong entity claiming treaty benefits and earning intercompany income must satisfy the Hong Kong economic substance requirements and perform its Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, especially for financing, IP licensing, and distribution activities. A pure paper or shell Hong Kong entity without CIGA will fail the beneficial ownership test and be denied treaty relief; China's general anti-abuse rule will recharacterize the structure and impose tax adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Invoicing via Artificial Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest of 0.05% under Article 32. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes tax evasion with fines and imprisonment; large-amount evasion can result in imprisonment of up to seven years. The State Taxation Administration may also blacklist the entity and its legal representative.

Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 23 (2023 revision), where shareholders or directors abuse the corporate personality to evade tax, liability is pierces and the responsible individuals are jointly and severally liable for tax restitution, fines, and civil compensation. The legal representative and finance director may be placed on exit-control blacklists, prohibited from leaving China, face asset freezes, and are exposed to criminal imprisonment for fraud if deliberate evasion is proved.
Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Non-Arm's-Length Intercompany Pricing

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016), the tax authority may make a special tax adjustment and impose interest on the additional tax at the PBOC benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points. If the mispricing is fraudulent, Article 63 penalty of 50% to 500% applies, and Criminal Law Article 201 may apply where the underpayment crosses criminal thresholds.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing documentation or approve non-arm's-length contracts face personal joint and several liability under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine; civil restitution of adjusted taxes and penalties; blacklisting as a serious-tax-violation taxpayer; travel bans and exit restrictions; and criminal imprisonment up to seven years for prosecutors proving fraudulent evasion.
Withholding Tax Failure and DTA Beneficial-Ownership Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% of the unwithheld tax and requires the withholding agent to be charged with the unpaid amount; Article 32 adds a daily 0.05% late-payment surcharge. The tax authority can deny the DTA relief under the beneficial ownership and anti-abuse rules, retroactively impose the full 10% or higher CIT withholding on the nonresident, and assess interest.

Director personal liability: Directors and finance officers who knowingly or negligently execute dividend, interest, or royalty remittances without verified treaty eligibility can be personally liable for the restitution of unwithheld taxes under veil-piercing principles. They may be blacklisted, banned from exiting China, summoned to civil asset recovery proceedings, and prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201 if the unlawful withholding failure is part of a deliberate tax evasion scheme leading to imprisonment of up to seven years.
