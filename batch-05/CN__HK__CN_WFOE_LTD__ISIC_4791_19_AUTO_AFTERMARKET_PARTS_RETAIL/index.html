Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to an online-retail WFOE under the PRC Company Law. The sole shareholder subscribes the registered capital in the Articles of Association and must complete payment within the five-year contribution period prescribed by the 2023 Company Law, or within any longer period permitted by transitional rules prior to 30 June 2032. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), exercised through local provincial and municipal tax service bureaus and the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform (the PRC e-Tax electronic tax portal); the Hong Kong shareholder parent falls under the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR for its separate Hong Kong tax position.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The Hong Kong parent incorporates the CN WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); SAMR transmits registration data to the STA, which opens an initial tax profile in the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform. Step 2: The WFOE must complete the supplementary tax registration within 30 calendar days after issuance of the business license, confirming its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the corporate TIN and VAT registration number, together with legal representative, registered and operating address, shareholder structure, registered capital, principal business code, accounting system, bank account and expected VAT taxpayer status. Step 4: For the foreign non-resident shareholder in Hong Kong, no PRC tax registration is required for passive shareholding; however, if the Hong Kong entity has a permanent establishment, branch, or Chinese-source income, the WFOE as withholding agent must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification record for the Hong Kong entity and claim double taxation arrangement relief only upon provision of Hong Kong residence and beneficial ownership evidence.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the CN WFOE is a PRC resident limited liability enterprise subject to entity-level Corporate Income Tax on worldwide income, with no pass-through or transparent treatment; the WFOE is also a separate VAT taxpayer in the PRC; the Hong Kong parent is not classified as a PRC resident taxpayer and remains territorial for Hong Kong Profits Tax purposes.

Available election forms

None analogous to a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election; PRC law does not permit pass-through classification. The WFOE may only elect small-scale VAT taxpayer status where annual taxable sales do not exceed RMB 5,000,000, or voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer where the statutory accounting and fixed establishment conditions are satisfied; treaty relief under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement is claimable, not a domestic election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC enterprises are tax-registered through the USCC; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period, and below that threshold the WFOE may be treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer subject to simplified collection rates. The small-scale monthly taxable sales exemption of RMB 100,000 (or quarterly RMB 300,000) remains available if statutory conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may zero-rate and obtain input VAT refund/export tax rebate on automotive replacement parts and tuning accessories that are physically exported from PRC customs territory to Hong Kong or other overseas destinations in genuine cross-border transactions, including online retail or e-commerce B2B export modes using customs supervision codes such as 9610, 9710 or 9810 where applicable. The zero-rating claim requires a valid customs export declaration, physical exit of the goods, transfer of title and risk to an overseas buyer, receipt of foreign exchange from outside the PRC, and preservation of all documentation required by the STA and SAFE.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) generated by PRC Customs through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading, sea waybill, airway bill, or integrated logistics manifest showing physical departure from PRC customs territory
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and weight certificate
  • Export sales contract, purchase order, or cross-border online transaction listing with the overseas buyer
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt confirmation issued by the SAFE-authorized settlement bank, including the international receipts declaration and inward remittance confirmation (the bank-level FIRC-equivalent evidence)
  • Export VAT invoice set issued by the WFOE in the PRC fully digitalized electronic invoice system
  • Export tax refund claim record filed on the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform
  • Cross-border e-commerce platform transaction reports and payment rail statements from Stripe, PayPal or similar PSPs, showing buyer identity, order number, currency, amounts and settlement events
  • Customs supervision channel order-level data for cross-border e-commerce modes such as 9610, 9710 or 9810, if applicable

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice system on the National Unified Electronic Tax Service Platform; invoice issuance is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax System Phase IV for number allocation, validation, comparison and anti-fraud risk control, using the tax U-Key as the fiscal authorization device. No separate physical fiscal stamp is applied to ordinary VAT invoices. For export movements, goods must clear through the China International Trade Single Window, and the electronic customs declaration, logistics, payment and e-invoice data must reconcile for export tax refund or zero-rating purposes.

The STA and Customs apply joint post-clearance audit and data matching to export zero-rating and tax refund claims. If the export document bundle is incomplete, contradictory or fraudulent, the tax authority will deny or withdraw the zero-rating, claw back the refunded VAT, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the unpaid tax, and may levy administrative penalties of between 50% and 500% of the tax involved. Fraudulent export tax refund cases may be referred for criminal prosecution under the PRC Criminal Law, with consequences including imprisonment, criminal fines, confiscation and forfeiture of export tax facilitation status. In addition, SAFE may downgrade the WFOE to a B or C class goods-trade foreign-exchange enterprise, triggering transaction-by-transaction verification, stricter bank controls and restrictions on cross-border e-commerce collection through Stripe, PayPal or other PSPs.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB operates under a managed conditional convertibility regime. For the mainland WFOE, current-account items - export proceeds, service payments, dividends remitted after audit - are generally convertible once a licensed bank can verify the underlying transaction with invoices, customs declarations, contracts, and tax certificates. Capital-account items are far more constrained: an equity injection from Hong Kong must enter through a dedicated FDI capital account, be recorded in SAFE's direct investment information system, and be settled into RMB on a pay-at-use basis, not merely at the corporate treasurer's discretion. There are no blanket surrender ratios for ordinary export proceeds today, but foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated and either retained or sold to a licensed bank before they can support the WFOE's operational currency needs. Profit remittance is possible under current-account rules, yet banks require a board distribution resolution, CPA-audited financial statements, full corporate income tax settlement, and proof that the parent is not a shell with hidden sanctioned connections. The major practical hurdles are document fragmentation, strict seasonality of e-commerce refunds, and the inability to net offshore PSP receivables against mainland expenses without a SAFE/PBOC-recognized collection arrangement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's goods-trade foreign exchange verification, export foreign currency receipts of the WFOE must be brought into mainland China within 90 days of the customs export declaration; the exporter may keep up to 100% of the receipt in a designated foreign exchange current account or settle into RMB with a licensed bank at its discretion. Mandatory conversion is not fixed at a percentage for ordinary goods trade; the bank instead matches the invoiced value, customs declaration, logistics evidence, and the BOP declaration before accepting settlement. For returns and chargebacks from online sales, the WFOE must show that any net-off between export proceeds and customer refunds has been reported to the bank in real time; unreconciled refunded amounts must be re-classified or reversed in the next verification cycle. Where the WFOE uses an offshore PSP, the PSP collection is not an onshore forex receipt until the funds are swept through a PBOC-licensed clearing agent or the WFOE's own SAFE-reported merchant account.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) as the central bank, with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign-exchange authority for the mainland WFOE; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) serves as the banking regulator for Hong Kong SAR clearing but does not operate exchange controls.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business cannot be used as the primary treasury account for a mainland WFOE because they are not licensed Chinese deposit-taking institutions and do not participate in SAFE reporting, FDI capital verification, or goods-trade forex verification. Such platforms may serve only as receivable collection points in Hong Kong or Singapore before funds are swept to a licensed domestic bank; even then, the arrangement must be legally structured as a third-party payment collection facility through a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution or a bank-recognized cross-border settlement agency. The viability rating for using an offshore EMI as a direct account tier is Low/Prohibited; as a secondary collection conduit it is Medium only if the EMI has local payment licences and the WFOE establishes a full audit trail from beneficiary order to onshore bank reconciliation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital injection from the Hong Kong parent into the China WFOE must be routed via SWIFT MT103 from the parent's corporate account into the WFOE's FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed domestic bank. The remittance instructions must state the beneficiary as the WFOE with its unified social credit code, the remittance currency in USD or HKD, and the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code referencing direct investment equity injection; the overseas remitting bank must record the purpose as equity investment and the receiving bank will reflect direct investment in the foreign-exchange registration. After receipt, the WFOE's auditor issues a capital verification report and the bank submits SAFE FDI information registration and payment feedback in the capital project information system. To use the RMB proceeds, the WFOE applies for capital-gold account settlement with an invoice, contract, board resolution, and proof that the transaction is within its business scope; the settled RMB must be credited to a supervised RMB payment account and cannot be transferred to an unsupported related party or a domestic securities pool. The entire cycle must be completed through the applicable SAFE transaction code for FDI equity injection; any deviation such as splitting the capital injection into small deposits to avoid review is itself an anti-money-laundering red flag.For payments to overseas vendors, logistics providers, marketing platforms, and occasional bulk suppliers, the WFOE should use ordinary SWIFT MT103 wire transfers for below-threshold operational payments, supported by invoices, contracts, customs declarations, and tax vouchers. For high-value purchase orders for aftermarket parts, a commercial letter of credit at sight is recommended to provide documentary protection; the required documents must include clean bills of lading or airway bills, commercial invoices, packing lists, a certificate of origin, and any required quality inspection certificates. Documentary collections are acceptable for established counterparties with recurring part-shipment business, where the bank presents documents for payment against previous trade history; however, the WFOE should confirm each collection is included in its SAFE goods-trade total and that the underlying foreign exchange declaration code matches the actual goods description. Domestic supplier payments can be made via CIPS in RMB and standard CHIPS or Fedwire USD wires for foreign-currency expenditure; all outbound payments need to be tagged with the proper SAFE purpose code and applicable tax serial number.Onshore deliverable FX forwards with licensed Chinese banks are permitted for hedging genuine current-account exposures such as expected export receipts and import payables; the forward must be closed by actual delivery of currency within the maturity.FX option combinations, including risk reversals and collars, are permissible as hedges for underlying trade transactions when purchased from a licensed bank; the bank requires a signed hedging authorization and evidence of exposure.Cross-currency swaps are permissible only for matched real-term exposure with SAFE-covered debt or approved capital transactions; speculative swaps on anticipated profits are not permitted without registration.Offshore NDFs cannot be used by the mainland WFOE because the RMB is not a non-deliverable currency onshore; non-residents may trade offshore NDFs, but a Chinese entity entering an offshore NDF would fall outside its legal banking boundary and would likely be rejected by compliance review as an unhedged speculative derivative activity if no SAFE approval exists.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business cannot be used as the primary treasury account for a mainland WFOE because they are not licensed Chinese deposit-taking institutions and do not participate in SAFE reporting, FDI capital verification, or goods-trade forex verification. Such platforms may serve only as receivable collection points in Hong Kong or Singapore before funds are swept to a licensed domestic bank; even then, the arrangement must be legally structured as a third-party payment collection facility through a PBOC-licensed cross-border payment institution or a bank-recognized cross-border settlement agency. The viability rating for using an offshore EMI as a direct account tier is Low/Prohibited; as a secondary collection conduit it is Medium only if the EMI has local payment licences and the WFOE establishes a full audit trail from beneficiary order to onshore bank reconciliation.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under State Administration of Taxation Announcement No. 42 (2016), Local File preparation is mandatory when annual related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000; tangible property transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000; intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000; or financial asset transactions exceed RMB 100,000,000. Master File requirements apply when the multinational group's related-party transaction amounts reach RMB 10,000,000,000 or any group member qualifies under statutory criteria.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not apply the Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations directly, but the GAAR in Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and the substance-over-form doctrine permit recharacterization of any arrangement lacking commercial substance. The WFOE must perform substantive business functions in China, including procurement, inventory, logistics, marketing, customer service, and assumption of entrepreneurial risk. Separately, the Hong Kong parent must satisfy Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations and demonstrate that core income-generating activities are performed in Hong Kong; otherwise treaty benefits and offshore profit classifications may be denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

E-commerce revenue suppression and tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Tax Administration Law Article 63 authorizes recovery of underpaid tax, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 3 years for relatively large tax evasion and 3 to 7 years plus fines for large evasion, with confiscation of property in serious cases.

Director personal liability: A director who approves false revenue entries may be found to have abused the corporate personality and be jointly and severally liable for the company's tax debts under PRC Company Law. Directors face civil asset restitution, disqualification, placement on the tax blacklist, exit and travel bans, and criminal imprisonment as the responsible person in the tax evasion.
Shell holding company and treaty shopping through Hong Kong

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulations Article 120 allow the tax authority to disregard the Hong Kong entity, recharacterize the structure, reassess the Chinese taxable income, and deny treaty relief. Hong Kong anti-avoidance provisions under Inland Revenue Ordinance sections 61 and 61A can nullify tax benefits, and the Hong Kong Registrar may strike off a sham company. If recharacterization results in underpaid tax, the 50% to 500% tax evasion penalty may also apply.

Director personal liability: Directors of the shell entity may be personally liable for fraudulent treaty claims, civil restitution of diverted profits, disqualification as directors in Hong Kong for up to 15 years, cross-border enforcement of Chinese judgments, travel bans, and criminal liability for tax fraud if false declarations are made.
Transfer pricing manipulation and documentation failure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 empowers the tax authority to adjust related-party income to arm's length, and Article 44 permits deemed income determination when documentation is absent. The adjustment attracts the daily late-payment surcharge, additional interest under transfer-pricing enforcement rules, and administrative fines under Tax Administration Law Article 62. Falsifying related-party invoices or customs declarations supporting the inflated price may constitute tax evasion punishable under Tax Administration Law Article 63 and Criminal Law Article 201.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve inflated cross-border transactions and unlawful profit extraction may be required to make personal restitution to the company and the tax authority, to return illegal dividends, and to bear personal liability for false financial statements. In willful conduct, directors and persons in charge may be prosecuted criminally and subjected to travel bans and cross-border asset freezing orders.
