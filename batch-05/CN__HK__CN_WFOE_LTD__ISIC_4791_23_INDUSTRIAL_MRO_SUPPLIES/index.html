Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online electronic portal: STA Electronic Tax Bureau (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the China International Trade Single Window for customs/export declarations. For this PRC-incorporated WFOE, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is not the registration authority; Hong Kong status is relevant only to the foreign parent/shareholder tier.

        
E-government filing portal
The WFOE must first be incorporated with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain a business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must register with the local competent STA tax bureau via the Electronic Tax Bureau or in-person tax service hall. Required documentation includes the business license, articles of association, shareholders' resolution, legal representative identification, and proof of registered address. The WFOE then completes the statutory tax registration form, taxpayer type identification (VAT general taxpayer or small-scale taxpayer), accounting system filing, filing period assignment, bank account filing, and withholding registration for payroll, dividends, and cross-border payments. The STA assigns the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN), which is identical to the USCC, and activates the entity in the Golden Tax System IV for VAT invoice issuance and tax filing. Where the WFOE has no domestic permanent establishment counterpart, the non-resident shareholder itself does not need a separate PRC TIN for the WFOE; however, a non-resident enterprise with PRC-source income is registered for withholding purposes through a payer-side registration procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. PRC tax law does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch entity elections. The WFOE is a resident enterprise under Article 2 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxed at entity level on worldwide income; no pass-through treatment is available.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is required when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 on a 12-month rolling basis; entities below this threshold may register as small-scale VAT taxpayers, but a B2B MRO e-commerce operation selling to VAT general taxpayers should ordinarily be registered as a general taxpayer to issue VAT special invoices.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Zero-rating of VAT at 0% is available for goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory to an overseas buyer, where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the export sale is declared to China Customs, the goods clear customs under an export declaration, the sales contract and export price are genuine, and the export proceeds are received from a non-PRC counterparty in foreign currency and repatriated through a PRC-designated settlement bank within the prescribed period. Qualifying offshore digital services are outside the core industrial MRO goods scope and are analysed separately under cross-border service zero-rating rules.

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice regime under the Golden Tax System IV. VAT general taxpayers must issue VAT special invoices or VAT ordinary invoices through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau and the national electronic invoice platform; invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time. All-digital electronic invoices (digital electronic VAT invoices) are cleared by the STA through pre-allocated invoice number pools and cryptographic tax-control validation. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; the clearance mandate is achieved by real-time electronic reporting, anti-fraud digital authentication, and automated cross-reconciliation of invoice issuance with VAT returns, bank receivables, and customs export declarations.

      
The STA and China Customs cross-match export declarations, VAT refund claims, bill-of-lading data, and foreign exchange receipt records through the Golden Tax System IV and the SAFE monitoring system. If the claimed export zero-rating is unsupported or fraudulent, the STA reverses the VAT refund, denies input VAT credit, and assesses output VAT at 13% from the date the export invoice was issued; late payment surcharges accrue at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties may range from 50% to 500% of the tax evaded or improperly refunded, and the WFOE may be disqualified from export tax rebate treatment, placed on the tax blacklist, have its export registration suspended, or face criminal prosecution for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law. All export zero-rating files must be retained for at least five years and made available for audit inspection.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions, including B2B MRO e-commerce import/export payments, under SAFE's verification regime; capital-account items such as inbound foreign direct investment, equity/loan restructuring, and outbound treasury sweeps remain tightly controlled. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise in mainland China may receive parent equity or shareholder loan capital through a foreign exchange registration under the Foreign Investment Project Confirmation, after which the funds may settle into RMB for permitted operational purposes. Profit and dividend remittances to the HK parent are permitted once annual audit is completed, and after corporate income tax settlement, with SAFE registration and service-provider deducting withholding tax; frequent intracompany payments for inventory purchases must be documented as genuine connected transactions to avoid trade mis-invoicing. Surrender of export proceeds is no longer mandatory, but all receipts must be brought onshore through a domestic foreign exchange current account within trade-receipt timelines. The practical hurdle lies not in RMB convertibility but the required documentary trail and the matching of every FX transaction to an underlying commercial transaction recognized in the SAFE trade surveillance system.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE's current trade-facilitation framework, there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; the WFOE may retain 100% of eligible export receipts in a domestic foreign currency current account or voluntarily convert them into RMB. Export proceeds must be repatriated onshore to a Chinese domestic bank account by the earlier of the commercial payment due date or 90 days after the date of customs export declaration; if payment is not collected within that period, the enterprise must file a deferred-receipt explanation through the Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. For a high-volume, small-ticket MRO catalog exporter, export amounts can be aggregated under a single customs declaration, but each outstanding receivable beyond the 90-day window will generate an automatic non-compliance flag and must be formally registered with SAFE.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the latter acting as the delegated foreign exchange control authority for all mainland China cross-border capital and trade transactions

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Rating: Low feasibility. Under PBOC licensing rules for payment institutions, offshore fintech EMIs, including Hong Kong stored-value facility holders, are not licensed to open domestic RMB deposit accounts or to execute SAFE-regulated foreign exchange conversion on behalf of a mainland WFOE. The WFOE is therefore legally constrained from using a fintech EMI as its primary treasury banking channel. An offshore EMI may only be engaged as an auxiliary e-commerce card-acquiring gateway, with all customer proceeds settled through a licensed Chinese acquiring bank and then credited to the WFOE's onshore Tier-1 bank account; it cannot serve as the capital escrow account, the operational currency account, or the trade settlement account. The EMI route is recommended only for card-present checkout payment data and merchant reconciliation, never for statutory FX compliance or capital verification.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Opaque ultimate beneficial ownership chain through a Hong Kong holding company and multiple intermediary e-commerce entities, triggering suspicion of shell-company or conduit risk.
        
Root cause: Opaque ultimate beneficial ownership chain through a Hong Kong holding company and multiple intermediary e-commerce entities, triggering suspicion of shell-company or conduit risk.

        
Defense evidence: Certified certificate of incumbency and register of members for the Hong Kong holding company issued by a registered agent; a visual corporate ownership map with share certificates; CRS and FATCA self-certifications; beneficial ownership declarations for every shareholder holding 25% or more; notarized passports and residential proof for all directors and controlling parties; plus a board-approved written rationale for the Hong Kong treasury intermediary covering cash pooling, FX netting, and cross-border procurement efficiency.

      

      
      

        High-volume, low-value MRO catalogue receipts fragmented across multiple e-commerce channels may appear to the bank as structuring, layering, or money laundering through repeated deposits.
        
Root cause: High-volume, low-value MRO catalogue receipts fragmented across multiple e-commerce channels may appear to the bank as structuring, layering, or money laundering through repeated deposits.

        
Defense evidence: Export sales ledger at order level, exported from the B2B MRO catalogue platform with unique transaction references, payment methods, and timestamps; compatible Chinese customs declaration records and export manifest summaries; logistics waybills and courier tracking numbers; warehouse inventory cycle-count reports; twelve months of bank statements showing a stable earnings profile; and a formal treasury policy explaining expected ticket size, payment mix, and counterparty concentration.

      

      
      

        Trade mis-invoicing and transfer-pricing risk between the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent in MRO supplies, where over/under-invoicing could be used to shift taxable profits or evade FX controls.
        
Root cause: Trade mis-invoicing and transfer-pricing risk between the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent in MRO supplies, where over/under-invoicing could be used to shift taxable profits or evade FX controls.

        
Defense evidence: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation prepared under the Chinese Transfer Pricing Administrative Measures; a benchmark study of MRO distribution margins; an approved intercompany pricing list; sourcing invoices and bill of materials demonstrating the actual cost build-up; SAFE trade monitoring records matching invoice value, customs value, and cash receipt; and annual audited financial statements of the WFOE showing consistent gross margin performance.

      

      
      

        Industrial MRO hardware shipments may include dual-use components or be delivered to an embargoed end-user, creating sanctions and export-control screening concerns.
        
Root cause: Industrial MRO hardware shipments may include dual-use components or be delivered to an embargoed end-user, creating sanctions and export-control screening concerns.

        
Defense evidence: Automated sanctions and dual-use screening reports for every consignment covering OFAC, the China Unreliable Entity List, the United Nations, the European Union, and Hong Kong restrictive lists; an HS code classification memorandum from a licensed customs broker confirming that goods are general MRO maintenance hardware and are not weapons-defense items; end-user declarations from B2B buyers; certificate of origin and technical specification sheets; and an export compliance manual signed by the designated WFOE compliance officer detailing transactional screening procedures.

Turnover: Under Cap. 622 (Companies Ordinance), every Hong Kong company must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) as auditor and have its financial statements audited unless it qualifies as a small private company or small private group and all members consent to the exemption. A small private company must satisfy all three thresholds for the financial year: (1) total annual revenue does not exceed HK$20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,564,103); (2) total balance-sheet assets do not exceed HK$10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,282,051); and (3) average number of employees does not exceed 100. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: No statutory monetary threshold for Master File/Local File in Hong Kong; however, IRD's DIPN 59 advises contemporaneous documentation where controlled transactions are material, generally HK$10 million per transaction category; CbC reporting applies when the consolidated group revenue exceeds HK$6.816 billion (approximately EUR 750 million).

      
Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not maintain UAE/Bermuda-style ESR or CIGA definitions, but the IRD applies the general economic-substance principle through the offshore profits exemption, transfer-pricing adjustment provisions (IRO s. 50A) and the GAAR (IRO ss. 61A/61B). A Hong Kong company claiming treaty benefits or an offshore status must demonstrate that its CIGA – e.g. catalogue pricing, supplier negotiation, inventory ownership and e-commerce order acceptance – are actually performed by substantive staff and offices in Hong Kong; a letter-box company lacking employees, premises and board meeting records will be ignored for treaty purposes and reassessed.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / False Tax Return
        
Statutory sanction: IRO s. 82 – upon conviction on indictment: fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years; s. 82A imposes an additional penalty of up to twice the tax undercharged.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who signed, or who authorised the signing of, the false BIR51 are jointly and severally liable to the tax and penalties; the IRD may issue a departure prohibition order preventing the director from leaving Hong Kong, and if the tax debt is outstanding, a charging order may attach personal assets; subsequent disqualification under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) is likely.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Misdeclaration / Thin Capitalisation
        
Statutory sanction: IRD may recharacterise the transaction under IRO s. 61A (GAAR), adjust profits under s. 50A, and impose additional tax of up to 100% of the undercharged tax under s. 82A; late adjustments may result in a restrictive assessment and an IRD request for missing documentation within 30 days, failure of which carries a fine of HK$50,000 and further penalty.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced when the intercompany structure lacks fair commercial or economic substance; directors who knowingly signed misleading documents can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty, ordered to compensate the company for the tax, subject to an assets freeze, and disqualified for up to 15 years on summary/indictment proceedings.

      

      
      

        Aggressive Offshore Claims / Artificial Shelter
        
Statutory sanction: IRO s. 61A (GAAR) nullifies any tax benefit from the artificial arrangement; the IRD may assess tax at 16.5% on the real profit and impose a surcharge under s. 82A up to 100% of undercharged tax; in flagrant cases, tax evasion investigations are opened under s. 51(2).

        
Director personal liability: Where the WFOE is reduced to a shell, the court may disregard its separate legal personality because it was used for a fiscal fraud; directors become personally liable for restitution of unpaid tax, jointly and severally, and may be blacklisted through a departure prohibition order and disqualified; criminal prosecution can result in confiscation orders and imprisonment.
