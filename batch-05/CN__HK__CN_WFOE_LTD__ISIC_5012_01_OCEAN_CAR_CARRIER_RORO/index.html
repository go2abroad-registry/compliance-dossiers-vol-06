Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Under current PRC law there is no statutory minimum registered capital or mandatory paid-up capital for the WFOE, and no blocked escrow account is required. The sole shareholder shall determine the registered capital in the Articles of Association entirely at its commercial discretion. However, under Article 47 of the Companies Law of the PRC, each subscriber's subscribed capital must be fully paid within five years from the issuance of the business license, or within the shorter period stated in the Articles of Association. The Hong Kong parent's capital contributions must be remitted through a licensed bank into the WFOE's RMB or foreign currency capital contribution account, a bank capital contribution certificate must be obtained, and the capital contribution record must be filed in the SAMR corporate credit information system. For the intended international deep-sea Ro-Ro vehicle carrier operation, the WFOE or its controlled vessel-owning affiliate must also demonstrate sufficient owned or bareboat-chartered tonnage, operating funds, P&I insurance and financial capacity to the Ministry of Transport; if any restriction in the Foreign Investment Access Negative List requires Chinese control of Chinese-flag international vessel ownership, the WFOE shareholding and vessel ownership structure must be adjusted accordingly before the shipping permit is granted.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) administered through the STA and accessible via the registered province-level tax bureau website. For the Hong Kong shareholder overlay: Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR (IRD) eTax services for non-resident treaty and residency issues.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the local Market Supervision Administration (SAMR) under the PRC Foreign Investment Law. The business license issued by SAMR carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes. Step 2: Through the one-license-one-code data exchange, the local STA completes the WFOE's tax registration automatically from SAMR registration data; no separate TIN certificate is issued. The WFOE must then complete supplementary tax registration particulars through the e-Tax Bureau: legal representative, registered address, financial accounting system, bank deposit accounts, and VAT taxpayer type (general or small-scale). Step 3: A foreign non-resident entity such as the Hong Kong shareholder that merely holds shares and has no PRC permanent establishment or China-source income is generally not required to obtain a PRC TIN. If the Hong Kong shareholder derives China-source income, obtains dividends, or seeks treaty benefits, it must complete non-resident enterprise tax registration through the STA e-Tax Bureau or through the WFOE as withholding agent, obtain a Non-resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number, and provide a Certificate of Resident in a Contracting Party issued by the IRD. For Hong Kong profits tax, the parent must separately protect any territorial-source or offshore claim with the IRD.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity election exists under PRC tax law. The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise and is automatically taxable at entity level on worldwide income. Applicable elections: (1) VAT general taxpayer status may be elected voluntarily before annual taxable sales reach the RMB 5,000,000 mandatory threshold, provided the accounting system is adequate; (2) small-scale VAT taxpayers may elect the relevant simplified VAT methods; (3) the Hong Kong shareholder may elect to claim PRC-Hong Kong DTA relief through a tax residence certificate application to the IRD, but this is not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration in China; below that threshold the WFOE remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status. For reference, the PRC headline VAT rate on goods is 13%, the standard VAT rate on domestic transportation is 9%, and international ocean carriage is zero-rated.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, international transportation services supplied by VAT general taxpayers qualify for the 0% VAT rate. The Ro-Ro ocean car carrier operation qualifies as an international transportation service when the WFOE carries vehicles by ocean-going vessel between a PRC port and an overseas port. To claim the 0% rate, the WFOE must: (1) hold the required PRC and/or Hong Kong shipping registrations and the statutory shipping licence; (2) issue a VAT invoice at 0% for the ocean freight; (3) evidence through a signed service contract, voyage charter party, slot agreement, and bill of lading that the foreign customer receives the carriage service; (4) evidence through the China International Trade Single Window export declaration for the underlying Ro-Ro cargo that the vehicles physically left PRC customs territory; and (5) receive the freight payment under State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules, with bank records showing receipt of funds from outside mainland China. The 0% rate is a credit-and-refund mechanism, not an exemption: input VAT attributable to the zero-rated voyage may be deducted and residual input VAT may be

China operates a centralized real-time VAT invoice data-collection and anti-fraud clearance model. Taxpayers included in the full digital electronic invoice (FDEI) pilot must issue all VAT invoices through the STA electronic invoice platform; each FDEI is digitally signed with the STA electronic seal, assigned a unique invoice number, and transmitted to the STA in near real time at issuance. Recipients verify FDEIs on the STA national invoice verification platform. System-to-system API integration is available through the STA platform or an approved third-party e-invoice service provider. Legacy taxpayers not yet in the FDEI pilot must use the VAT Invoice Management System with tax-control devices, and invoice data must be reported to the STA before input VAT credit can be recognized. For the Ro-Ro shipping business, the WFOE must submit cross-border service and export zero-rated sales schedules in the VAT return through the e-Tax Bureau, ensuring reconciliation of invoice data, customs declarations, bills of lading, and foreign exchange receipts.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime and a bifurcated convertibility system. On the current account, RMB is largely convertible for transactions in goods and services, provided a designated foreign-exchange bank obtains a truthful underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration or service evidence. RoRo international shipping revenue will normally be classified as a current-account service receipt, allowing the WFOE to retain USD or HKD in a foreign-exchange settlement account or convert a portion to RMB at a negotiated onshore rate. On the capital account, RMB is not freely convertible: every inbound equity subscription, cross-border loan, or outbound investment requires SAFE registration, a dedicated capital/escrow account, and compliance with the Foreign Investment Law, the Negative List, and FDI statistical regulations. After-tax profits may be remitted to the Hong Kong parent as dividends, but the bank will require audited financial statements, tax filing evidence, a shareholder dividend resolution, and a SAFE capital-account registration. Dividend remittance is not subject to an annual hard quota if the underlying authenticity file is complete. Capital-account balances cannot be used for unregistered outbound investment, domestic on-lending, or securities trading without special regulatory permission. A WFOE in the international RoRo transport space must keep separate current-account and capital-account flows to avoid mixing freight receipts with paid-in capital, because SAFE examines purpose codes and reclamation paths at both entity and bank level.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under prevailing SAFE practice, the statutory mandatory conversion ratio for ordinary current-account export receipts is effectively zero: the WFOE is entitled to retain 100% of freight foreign currency in its designated settlement account or convert it into RMB voluntarily at the bank's onshore rate. The mandatory element is repatriation and reporting. For a standard A-class trade enterprise, freight/export proceeds should be received and reported no later than 90 days from the invoice or bill-of-lading date; where the contract is open-account or deferred settlement, the bank may register an extension up to 180 days or longer with supporting commercial documentation. Receipts beyond the registered timeline trigger SAFE scrutiny, possible downgrading to B/C enterprise class, and temporary suspension of voluntary settlement or clearing privileges.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) as central bank and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange control authority of the State Council

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Digital EMIs based in Singapore, Hong Kong, or Europe are not legally viable as the primary onshore banking layer for a PRC WFOE. They are not licensed by PBOC as domestic commercial banks and cannot open SAFE-designated foreign-exchange settlement accounts, FDI capital escrow accounts, or RMB basic deposit accounts for tax and payroll. They cannot perform capital verification or issue the required Chinese bank seal and custody documents. Their practical role is limited to offshore treasury overlay at the Hong Kong parent level: pooling USD/HKD freight receipts, executing offshore CNH conversions, and pre-arranging payment corridors before funds are remitted to or from the mainland Tier-1 bank. Feasibility rating: Low for onshore regulated settlement; Medium as an offshore cash-management tool only.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must inject equity through a SAFE-compliant FDI route. First, the WFOE files the required foreign investment information with MOFCOM/SAFE and confirms that the business scope is not on the Negative List. Second, SAFE issues the FDI registration or Confirmation of Foreign Exchange Registration, after which the WFOE opens a dedicated FDI capital escrow/account at a designated Tier-1 bank. Third, the parent remits USD or HKD by SWIFT using the SAFE capital-account purpose code for equity capital injection into a PRC foreign-invested enterprise; the incoming credit will be monitored against the registered FDI amount. Fourth, the bank will convert the injection into RMB only after verifying the SAFE registration, the remittance purpose, and the payment plan for rent, salaries, vessel operating costs, spare parts, freight, taxes and other approved uses. Fifth, a certified public accountant issues the FDI capital verification report and the bank keeps the conversion and payment trail for the PBOC/SAFE audit. Cross-border RMB injections can alternatively be routed through the PBOC RMB settlement accounts but still require the same SAFE and Foreign Investment Law documentation. No injection should be routed through a digital EMI or personal account, because the bank must recognise the source as a direct equity remittance from the Hong Kong corporate shareholder.Outbound settlement will run through the WFOE's settlement accounts at the Tier-1 bank. SWIFT MT103 is the default clearing rail for operational disbursements such as bunker suppliers, canal tolls, port handling, tug and pilot services, agency fees, hull and machinery premiums, P&I calls, classification surveys, crewing payroll, and charter-hire remittances; each payment must carry a matching contract, invoice and, where required, SAFE declaration. Commercial Letters of Credit are recommended for larger purchases such as RoRo vessel acquisitions, marine engines, spare parts or automotive cargo trade; the Chinese bank issues a sight or usance LC and settles against presented bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, inspection certificate and any required maritime certificate. Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for low-risk, recurring trade with established charterers and suppliers where the WFOE is prepared to take documentary risk without a bank-payment undertaking. All outbound payments are subject to PBOC/SAFE authenticity review, including screening of the payee name and the underlying transaction against sanctions databases and the entity's approved business scope.Deliverable RMB/FX forward contracts: permitted as plain-vanilla hedging for the WFOE's genuine current-account freight revenue and operating expenses, with a signed ISDA/NAFMII master agreement and an underlying commercial or charter-party document; naked or speculative forward positions are prohibited.Cross-currency swaps: permitted only when they exactly match a registered capital/funding position or a long-term charter-payment cash-flow mismatch, remain within the bank's SAFE-approved derivatives quota, and never create a synthetic RMB loan or artificial capital-account arbitrage.FX swaps (spot/forward combination): permitted for temporary liquidity clearing between USD/HKD and RMB arising from actual freight receipts or import payments, subject to the same underlying authenticity file.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): not permissible to be booked onshore by the WFOE; the Hong Kong treasury centre may use CNH NDFs solely to hedge residual RMB exposure on upcoming dividend remittances, provided settlement does not involve onshore RMB cash and the position is recorded in the group treasury policy.Plain-vanilla FX options: possible only after bank-specific eligibility and SAFE authorisation; the option premium and payout must be linked to a verified underlying commercial exposure, while barrier structures, leveraged or knock-out/callable features are prohibited.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Digital EMIs based in Singapore, Hong Kong, or Europe are not legally viable as the primary onshore banking layer for a PRC WFOE. They are not licensed by PBOC as domestic commercial banks and cannot open SAFE-designated foreign-exchange settlement accounts, FDI capital escrow accounts, or RMB basic deposit accounts for tax and payroll. They cannot perform capital verification or issue the required Chinese bank seal and custody documents. Their practical role is limited to offshore treasury overlay at the Hong Kong parent level: pooling USD/HKD freight receipts, executing offshore CNH conversions, and pre-arranging payment corridors before funds are remitted to or from the mainland Tier-1 bank. Feasibility rating: Low for onshore regulated settlement; Medium as an offshore cash-management tool only.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare a Local File when annual related-party transactions exceed any of the following RMB thresholds: RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers and uses, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers and licensing, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions including management services, shared services, interest and freight support. A Master File and Country-by-Country Report threshold is triggered where the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the preceding year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the WFOE has reportable related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: PRC general anti-avoidance rules under CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Bulletin 118 (2017) allow recharacterization of arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose or economic substance. For Mainland-HK treaty relief, the Hong Kong shareholder must itself meet the economic substance test; a shell or conduit is not a beneficial owner. Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regulations and the Inland Revenue (Amendment) Ordinance 2023 require specified geographically mobile income, including certain dividends, interest, royalties, and shipping income, to pass a substance test in Hong Kong with core income-generating activities (CIGA) performed there. For an ocean Ro-Ro carrier, CIGA include negotiation of charter parties, route and fleet deployment, cargo solicitation, freight billing, management of demurrage claims, and strategic risk management. If these functions are actually performed in Mainland China while the Hong Kong company only holds legal title, the Hong Kong entity has a taxable permanent establishment in China and the WFOE payments are subject to allocation and 25% CIT on the attributed profit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Treaty Abuse and Shell-Conduit Arrangement

Statutory sanction: The PRC tax authority will deny DTA benefits under CIT Law Article 47 and SAT Bulletin 118 (2017), recharacterize the payment as a dividend to the ultimate non-resident owner, and impose the full 10% WHT differential, daily late surcharge of 0.05% under TCAL Article 32, and an evasion penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax under TCAL Article 63; criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for fraudulent tax declaration follows if the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the treaty declaration and approved the conduit structure face piercing of the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 for abuse of limited liability; they are jointly and severally liable for the differential tax and civil restitution of corporate assets; travel bans and tax blacklisting under the Joint Punishment for Tax-Related Dishonesty apply, preventing departure from China; imprisonment up to 7 years applies for tax evasion, and if false documentary evidence is used, imprisonment for the false invoice or document offences.
False Invoicing and Revenue Suppression (Tax Evasion)

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 63 imposes a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax, plus a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and re-assessment on reconstructed revenue; issuance or use of false invoices triggers Criminal Law Articles 205 and 205-1 with fines, asset confiscation, and imprisonment for the unit and responsible persons; tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 is punishable by imprisonment up to 7 years for major evasion and fines of up to 5 times the evaded amount.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers are personally liable under Company Law Article 20 for abuse of corporate independence where corporate assets are used to conceal revenue; personal assets may be attached for restitution of the evaded tax; an exit ban and travel blacklist are imposed during enforcement; criminal imprisonment up to 7 years applies for tax evasion, and life imprisonment can apply for serious false special VAT invoice crimes involving fabricated export freight transactions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Disclosure and Thin Capitalization Stripping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41, 46, and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures authorize the tax authority to adjust income and disallow interest deductions above the thin capitalization limit; SAT Bulletin 6 (2017) imposes special adjustment interest at 5% per annum from the tax due date; TCAL Article 62 assesses fines up to RMB 10,000 for failure to file documentation; if the non-disclosure was intentional to evade tax, TCAL Article 63 penalty of 50%-500% of the unpaid tax applies and criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may follow.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized non-arm's-length transactions without economic substance are jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax, penalties, and restitution under Company Law Article 20; travel restrictions and blacklisting are applied as enforcement measures; deliberate understatement of related-party income leads to criminal prosecution and imprisonment up to 7 years; destruction of related-party transaction books and records implicates Criminal Law Article 162-1 with additional prison terms.
