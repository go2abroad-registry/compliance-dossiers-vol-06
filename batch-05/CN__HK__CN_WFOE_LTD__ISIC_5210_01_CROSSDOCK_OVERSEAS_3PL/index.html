Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the National Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax portal; cross-border export documentation is filed through the China International Trade Single Window administered by the General Administration of Customs (GACC). For Hong Kong jurisdictional purposes, the Inland Revenue Department remains the separate revenue authority, but this Mainland WFOE is not under IRD direct taxation for its PRC registration.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the WFOE obtains a business licence from the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), which assigns an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code. The WFOE must complete initial tax registration through the STA e-Tax portal within 30 days of the business licence issuance; the Unified Social Credit Code becomes the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. The SOP for a foreign non-resident enterprise without a registered presence is to apply for Non-resident Enterprise Taxpayer Registration through a PRC withholding agent or directly via the e-Tax non-resident registration module; the resulting number is the non-resident TIN for withholding/entity classification. A foreign parent of a newly formed WFOE does not need a separate VAT ID for the WFOE; the WFOE's 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code is used for all VAT, corporate income tax, customs, and export VAT zero-rating declarations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All incorporated WFOEs must complete VAT registration as part of tax registration; there is no initial turnover threshold for registration. Mandatory VAT General Taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that amount, the WFOE may remain a Small-scale Taxpayer (current special collection rates apply). Small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000, or quarterly sales not exceeding RMB 300,000, are currently eligible for VAT exemption. Export sales of goods, including cross-border e-commerce exports through overseas warehouse fulfilment, are not counted as ordinary taxable sales and are subject to the export VAT zero-rating/rebate regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from Mainland China customs territory, including goods sold via cross-border e-commerce platforms and shipped to an overseas warehouse or foreign buyer under the overseas warehouse fulfilment model, are eligible for export VAT zero-rating and tax rebate/exemption. Eligibility requires a genuine export transaction, physical passage of goods through customs clearance, a corresponding commercial transaction and export consideration, and compliance with the special export declarations applicable to cross-border e-commerce enterprise/individual export. Qualifying cross-border services may also be zero-rated only if enumerated in the STA zero-rated cross-border taxable services catalogue and consumed wholly outside Mainland China; domestic order-fulfilment services physically rendered in Mainland China for an overseas warehouse platform are generally not zero-rated unless they fall under the catalogue. The taxpayer must be registered with Customs and STA as an export enterprise, issue export e-invoices, and maintain complete supporting documentation for audit by STA, GACC, and SAFE.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Export Goods Declaration) cleared through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading (sea, air, or multimodal transport contract) evidencing physical transport of goods outside Mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Export sales contract or cross-border e-commerce order transaction records
  • Export VAT electronic invoice / special e-invoice for export
  • Bank-prepared Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation (Inward Remittance Certificate equivalent) evidencing receipt and settlement of foreign currency proceeds
  • SAFE Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System export proceeds verification/cancellation record

China operates a mandatory electronic VAT invoice system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA e-Tax platform or the State Taxation Administration Electronic Invoice Service Platform, using STA-approved digital signatures and encrypted electronic stamps. Fully digitalized e-invoices carry a unique 20-digit invoice number and are transmitted in near real-time to the STA tax clearance system through the e-invoice API. Exporters must log export declarations, customs clearance, and logistics data in the China International Trade Single Window; VAT e-invoice data, Customs export data, and SAFE foreign exchange proceeds data are jointly reconciled for anti-fraud control. Hong Kong has no VAT/GST or e-invoice clearance obligation.

STA, GACC, and SAFE perform joint data matching of customs export declarations, export e-invoices, and export forex receipts. Failure to substantiate export physicality, export proceeds repatriation, or customs clearance within the statutory period causes the zero-rated or rebated VAT to be clawed back in full with a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulent or unverified zero-rating claims are penalised as tax evasion: administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved, downgrade of customs credit rating, suspension of export tax refund qualifications, and possible criminal liability under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax rebates. Retrospective recovery may extend beyond the standard limitation period if the claim is found fraudulent, with no statutory limitation in evasion cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The mainland WFOE operates under a managed float and a hybrid current-account and capital-account convertibility framework. Current-account transactions, including trade receipts, logistics service fees, freight settlements, and routine operating payments, are generally freely convertible once banks verify the underlying economic substance via the Goods Trade Monitoring System and cross-border receipt and payment declarations. The RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account without SAFE registration or approval. Registered FDI equity and shareholder loans require a SAFE Foreign Investment Joint Report, capital-injection registration, and opening of a Capital Account Foreign Currency (FDI) account before conversion into RMB for permitted operating purposes. Profit and dividend remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permissible after annual audit, tax filing, and tax clearance certification, but banks require evidence of prior withholding tax settlement. Dividend remittances are not subject to statutory quotas, yet funds must be sourced from after-tax audited retained earnings. Repatriating registered capital upon liquidation or capital reduction requires SAFE registration update and settlement balance verification. For cross-border e-commerce fulfilment, the WFOE may convert trade receipts in the current-account foreign exchange settlement account but must not use that account for capital-account transactions. The Hong Kong jurisdiction itself imposes no foreign exchange controls; however, mainland restrictions dominate the consolidated group treasury planning.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE goods-trade administration, there is no mandatory surrender ratio for export foreign-currency receipts; an exporting WFOE may retain 100 percent of verified export proceeds in a designated current-account Foreign Exchange Settlement Account or convert them to RMB at a licensed domestic bank. Receipts must be repatriated to a SAFE-registered account no later than 30 days after the export customs declaration date, with bank reconciliation against customs details. Export proceeds associated with the overseas warehouse and cross-dock 3PL model should be booked under ISIC 5210 and supported by logistics service entries, warehouse contracts, and customs bills. Any retained balance exceeding the enterprise quota must be either converted to RMB promptly or supported by a quota adjustment application submitted through the designated bank.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), as the controlling authority for the mainland WFOE; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) remains the prudential and currency-issuing authority for the Hong Kong parent or treasury leg.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

For the mainland WFOE, fintech EMI viability is Low from a regulatory standpoint. International EMI providers such as Airwallex or Currencycloud do not hold banking deposit-taking licences in mainland China and cannot maintain SAFE-registered capital accounts or current-account foreign exchange settlement accounts for the WFOE. They may act only as auxiliary payment facilitators through licensed third-party payment institutions for small-value B2C collections, but they cannot support capital verification, export proceeds reconciliation, or statutory profit remittance. For the Hong Kong SAR treasury leg, fintech EMIs are viable and effective for higher-frequency cross-border payouts to overseas warehouse operators and last-mile carriers, provided the HK parent retains a primary commercial bank account for settlement and only passes through trade instructions to the EMI platform.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent must first complete a SAFE Foreign Investment Joint Report through a designated mainland bank and obtain the Foreign Investment Information Report for the WFOE. The remittance should then be made by SWIFT MT103 from the HK parent's commercial bank account to the WFOE's Capital Account (FDI) at the designated domestic bank, with the wire narrative expressly stating 'Registered capital injection' and using the appropriate FX purpose code, typically 622 for FDI capital injection into equity. The domestic bank performs capital verification upon receipt and converts the funds into RMB in the Special RMB Capital Settlement Account where required. The converted capital may be applied only to the WFOE's permitted operating purposes, including warehouse lease payments, cross-dock facility costs, labour, technology, and logistics system expenditure, and may not be used for entrusted loans, wealth management products, property investment outside the entity business, or domestic equity investment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: For the mainland WFOE, fintech EMI viability is Low from a regulatory standpoint. International EMI providers such as Airwallex or Currencycloud do not hold banking deposit-taking licences in mainland China and cannot maintain SAFE-registered capital accounts or current-account foreign exchange settlement accounts for the WFOE. For the Hong Kong SAR treasury leg, fintech EMIs are viable and effective for higher-frequency cross-border payouts to overseas warehouse operators and last-mile carriers, provided the HK parent retains a primary commercial bank account for settlement and only passes through trade instructions to the EMI platform.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A contemporaneous Local File is mandatory if the annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible goods, RMB 100 million for intangible property, RMB 100 million for financial assets, or RMB 100 million for services. A Master File may additionally be required when total related-party turnover exceeds RMB 1 billion. All cross-border related-party transactions must be supported by transfer pricing documentation regardless of amount when the tax authority requests it.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) statute, but the substance doctrine is enforced through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) in Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, the beneficial ownership test under SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, and the principal purpose test (PPT) under the Multilateral Convention and the China-HK DTA. For a cross-dock overseas 3PL WFOE, the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) include order reception and processing, warehouse inventory custody, cross-dock consolidation, last-mile delivery arrangement, and customer service. The WFOE must maintain a physical premises, a substantive workforce, full operational control, and insurance/risk management capacity in China. Lack of economic substance will cause the tax authority to recharacterise intercompany payments, deny treaty benefits, and impose tax adjustments with penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Under-declared Revenue and False Invoices

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law mandates recovery of underpaid tax, a daily late surcharge, and a fine equal to 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law penalises tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax payable with fines and up to seven years in prison. Article 205 of the Criminal Law sets imprisonment of up to life for issuance of false scientific invoices and VAT invoices.

Director personal liability: Where the WFOE is used as a sham vehicle, the court pierces the corporate veil under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, holding directors jointly and severally liable for the unpaid taxes, penalties and interest. The tax authority can blacklist the director and impose an exit ban (restricted departure under the Exit-Entry Administration Law) until the debt is settled. Personal criminal liability may result in asset seizure, travel ban, and imprisonment terms of up to seven years.
Treaty Shopping / Shell Company Abuse (China-HK DTA Misuse)

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty benefits under the principal purpose test (PPT) and the beneficial ownership requirement of SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9. Withholding tax is recomputed at the 10% statutory rate, and a penalty equal to 5% of the adjusted taxable income may be imposed under SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42. Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law authorises GAAR adjustments with proposed tax, interest, and hot-interest surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors who design or execute the shell arrangement may be personally liable for the full tax shortfall under the substance-over-form doctrine and Article 20 of the Company Law. Assets of the directors can be frozen to collect the tax debt; exit bans may be imposed; and if the arrangement results in actual understated tax, criminal tax evasion penalties under Article 201 can lead to imprisonment of up to seven years. Regulatory blacklisting of directors of treaty-abusive structures may also be reported publicly.
Failure to Maintain Transfer Pricing Documentation and Arm's Length Compliance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and SAT Bulletin 2016 No. 42, failure to furnish transfer pricing documentation attracts a fine ranging from RMB 10,000 to RMB 100,000. Where an adjustment is made, the total taxable income is increased and interest at the central bank benchmark lending rate is charged from the due date. A Penalty Equalisation Adjustment of 5% of the adjusted income may also be imposed. If the transfer pricing violation is intentional and reaches criminal thresholds, the case is referred to the public security authorities for tax evasion prosecution.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally responsible for ensuring the truthfulness of the signed tax return and transfer pricing files. Upon tax adjustment, the corporate veil may be pierced if the WFOE is left insolvent or if the transfer pricing arrangement was a device to defraud the state; directors can then be personally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties. Exit bans and blacklisting are applied as enforcement measures. Criminal conviction for intentional tax evasion can carry imprisonment for up to seven years, with courts also ordering restitution of corporate tax losses from directors' personal assets.
