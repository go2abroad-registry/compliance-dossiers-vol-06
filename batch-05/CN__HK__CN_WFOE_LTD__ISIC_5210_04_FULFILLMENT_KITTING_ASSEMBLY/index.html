Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed on an ordinary CN WFOE; however, under the currently effective PRC Company Law, the registered capital of a limited liability company must be fully paid in by shareholders within five years after establishment unless a shorter or longer period is otherwise provided by the State Council. No blocked escrow account is required by corporate law, but the foreign shareholder must remit its subscribed capital through a dedicated RMB capital account opened at a domestic bank authorized for foreign direct investment, complete SAFE/FDI foreign exchange registration through the bank, and apply the settled funds only to lawful business purposes consistent with the approved business scope. The registered capital amount must reasonably correspond to the scale of the warehousing and kitting operations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety and civil defense clearance: obtain the Construction Engineering Fire Protection Design Review and Fire Protection Acceptance certificates, or the applicable fire protection filing receipt for smaller warehouse premises, from the Municipal Fire and Rescue Department before commencing operations; obtain the Fire Safety Inspection Certificate for use and occupancy before the warehouse receives goods, and maintain the automatic sprinkler system, fire hydrants, smoke detection, emergency lighting, fire separation, and evacuation route compliance required by GB 50016-2014; if the warehouse includes any underground civil air defense space, comply with the PRC Civil Air Defense Law and obtain the civil defense authority approval for use. Occupational clearance: submit the occupational disease hazard project declaration to the Municipal Emergency Management Bureau if the kitting line uses adhesives, heat-shrink equipment, or mechanical cutting devices; file the emergency response plan with the Emergency Management Bureau; engage licensed forklift operators and certify pallet racking structural integrity through a licensed Chinese structural engineer; maintain personal protective equipment programs, fire drills, material safety data sheets, and workplace safety training records, and register the company for the social insurance work injury scheme before any employees begin physical work.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its local tax service bureaus; online electronic portal: the Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) national platform. For the Hong Kong parent/offshore holding layer, the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong (IRD) operates the separate e-Tax portal.

E-government filing portal

For the Mainland China WFOE, the statutory TIN is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) obtained from the State Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation; the USCC automatically serves as the tax identification number, and the WFOE must complete tax registration formalities through the Electronic Tax Administration portal within 30 days after the business license is issued. The WFOE should file its taxpayer profile, confirm VAT taxpayer status as either small-scale taxpayer or general VAT taxpayer, register for payroll/individual income tax withholding, and complete the circular foreign exchange and export-related filings. For a foreign non-resident enterprise (e.g., the Hong Kong parent) that has no establishment in Mainland China but derives Chinese-source dividends, interest, royalties, or service income, the China payer must apply for a tax registration and non-resident TIN at the competent tax authority in the course of withholding registration; the non-resident must supply proof of Hong Kong tax residence and, if treaty benefits are claimed, a Certificate of Hong Kong Resident Status under the China-Hong Kong double tax arrangement before the reduced withholding rate may be applied. Supporting documents for non-resident registration include the overseas business registration certificate, articles of association, board resolution, valid passport of authorized signatory, power of attorney, and the relevant economic substance documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification; China does not provide a check-the-box election for WFOEs and all resident enterprises are taxed on worldwide income at entity level. A small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect to be taxed as a general VAT taxpayer once its accounting system is sound, and a general VAT taxpayer may not generally revert to small-scale status. No hybrid mismatch partnership/check-the-box elections are available under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; hybrid mismatch outcomes are controlled through anti-avoidance rules.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for basic VAT registration; every enterprise with taxable activities in China must register after incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, while small-scale taxpayers with monthly sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 or quarterly sales not exceeding RMB 300,000 may qualify for the VAT exemption policy.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for a 0% VAT rate and input VAT refund when the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods have physically left the People's Republic of China customs territory (including export to Hong Kong SAR as a separate customs territory), a formal export customs declaration has been filed and released, and the export sales proceeds are received from an overseas customer and repatriated through proper banking channels. The export VAT refund is applied under the exemption-offset-refund method for production/manufacturing enterprises and the exemption-refund method for foreign trading enterprises. For cross-border services directly and wholly consumed outside China, zero-rating or VAT exemption may also apply if the recipient is an overseas entity and all relevant supporting service-delivery and payment documentation is maintained.

  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outbound shipment
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List issued by the WFOE
  • Export Sales Contract or Purchase Order
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt record and export proceeds collection/settlement certificate
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the Chinese bank export proceeds collection receipt equivalent
  • China Customs electronic declaration data and release records
  • Processing trade manual or bonded warehouse documentation, if applicable

China operates a mandatory electronic invoice and fiscal clearance framework. VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be issued through the STA electronic invoice service platform; the current national rollout uses Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices, featuring real-time issuance, unique document numbers, digital anti-fraud verification codes, and automatic transmission of invoice data to the tax authority. No separate tax-control device is required for fully digitalized taxpayers. Enterprises may integrate ERP systems with the e-invoicing platform through secure APIs authorised by provincial tax authorities. Daily sales and invoice data must remain reconciled with VAT returns, and any mismatch will trigger automated tax-risk alerts.

The tax and customs authorities may audit export declarations, bank receipts, logistics records, and VAT input credits retrospectively. If export zero-rating is disallowed due to false declarations, missing documents, unrepatriated proceeds, or fraudulent invoice matches, the tax authority will claw back the refunded VAT and export rebate with late payment surcharges and may impose an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax involved under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Fraudulent export tax refund claims are also punishable under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which carries criminal fines and imprisonment up to life sentence for serious cases. Customs may additionally impose penalties for false customs declarations, downgrade the WFOE's credit classification, and suspend export tax rebate eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a hybrid convertibility regime. Current account items, including trade settlement, service fees, dividends, and interest under ordinary trade contracts, are generally freely convertible without SAFE approval as long as the bank verifies genuineness and consistency. Capital account items, however, remain restricted; foreign direct investment in the WFOE must be registered with SAFE/MOFCOM, and the paid-in capital must arrive through a designated onshore bank account. Profit remittance requires audited annual financial statements, corporate income tax filing proof, tax clearance certificate, and the original FDI registration record; the bank will conduct post-transaction verification before releasing dividends. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China, either into a foreign exchange settlement account or an RMB account, and there are no mandatory conversion percentages at present; however, banks require matching customs declarations and collection records. In practice, the WFOE cannot freely keep funds offshore beyond the permitted retention quotas, and any future capital outflows such as repayment of shareholder loans or return of liquidation proceeds require SAFE registration and written approval. The regime therefore rewards real-trade documentary discipline and penalizes unbacked or aggregated payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Inbound export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to an onshore foreign exchange settlement account in China through a licensed bank. The funds should be monitored against the relevant customs declaration; under SAFE's trade-receipt verification framework, receipt must be completed by the contract payment maturity date and in the absence of an explicit documentary due date, no later than 90 days from the export customs declaration. Retention in foreign currency is permitted without a fixed mandatory conversion ratio; conversion into RMB may be made at the entity's discretion. For payment terms extending beyond 90 days, a trade-based foreign credit/debit registration must be filed with SAFE before collection.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the mandate of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Assessed as Low / Architecturally Restricted. Payment institutions such as Airwallex, Stripe, or other EMIs holding only a foreign license cannot open a SAFE-designated capital account in China, nor can they act as a formal FX bank. A WFOE must use a PBOC/SAFE-licensed commercial bank for equity capital, statutory foreign registration, FX settlement, and tax/liquidation remittance. An offshore EMI may be retained only as an operational disbursement rail for low-value, non-capital trade costs if and only if the onshore treasury controller can push payments through a licensed Chinese bank clearing system and reconcile against transaction tracking numbers; even then, it is not a substitute for bank-issued settlement reports. Legal constraints under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Payment Services effectively require the WFOE's onshore RMB and foreign exchange business to be executed by licensed domestic bank account rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Capital contributions must be sent via SWIFT MT103 solely from the HK parent's account in Hong Kong to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a designated tier-1 bank in mainland China. The remittance must carry the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for FDI Paid-in Capital / Equity Capital and, if RMB-denominated, be processed under the cross-border RMB capital settlement framework. The bank will verify the remitter name against the SAFE Registration Record; a Foreign Investment Registration Certificate or electronic SAFE record must be produced. Capital verification is performed by a mainland-China registered accounting firm after funds are credited and converted into RMB; it will examine the bank receipt, closing balance letter, and updated shareholders' ledger.For operational import and trade settlements, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for direct cash instructions, SWIFT MT700 series for irrevocable documentary credits opened through a strategic bank, and documentary collections bank-to-bank instruction under URC522 for low-risk goods. For RMB-denominated cross-border payments, route through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) instead of a non-compliant USD intermediary. All outbound trade payments must be backed by import customs declarations, commercial invoices, packing lists, and delivery orders, and must be tagged with the correct SAFE payment purpose codes; avoid unwarranted consulting service or management fee categories.Onshore CNY forward contracts for genuine trade exposures, executed through a licensed domestic bank and documented with underlying purchase and sale agreements.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) permitted only at the Hong Kong parent or group level, not for the onshore WFOE, because NDFs sit outside SAFE's onshore hedging framework.Currency swaps (cross-currency swaps) with SAFE-approved domestic banks, limited to no more than 100 percent of the underlying exposure and matching tenor and notional to FDI capital, dividends, or import settlement cash flows.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Assessed as Low / Architecturally Restricted. Payment institutions such as Airwallex, Stripe, or other EMIs holding only a foreign license cannot open a SAFE-designated capital account in China, nor can they act as a formal FX bank. A WFOE must use a PBOC/SAFE-licensed commercial bank for equity capital, statutory foreign registration, FX settlement, and tax/liquidation remittance. An offshore EMI may be retained only as an operational disbursement rail for low-value, non-capital trade costs if and only if the onshore treasury controller can push payments through a licensed Chinese bank clearing system and reconcile against transaction tracking numbers; even then, it is not a substitute for bank-issued settlement reports. Legal constraints under the PBOC Administrative Measures for Payment Services effectively require the WFOE's onshore RMB and foreign exchange business to be executed by licensed domestic bank account rails.
