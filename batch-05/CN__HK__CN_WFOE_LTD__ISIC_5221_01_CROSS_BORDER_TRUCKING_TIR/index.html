Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law does not impose a minimum paid-up share capital for a private company limited by shares. The company may be incorporated with an issued share capital of HK$1.00 or such nominal amount as the subscriber(s) determine. The subscribed amount must be stated in Form NNC1, and share allotment must be reported under Section 68 (Return of Allotment) on Form NNC1 within one month. No escrow, blocked account, or capital contribution receipt is required as a condition of registration; however, for banking and operational certification purposes, the directors may maintain a designated capital deposit account at the principal bank and provide a banker’s confidence letter at no statutory minimum threshold. If a higher capital amount is voluntarily declared, full consideration for issued shares must be paid by cash or other lawful consideration prior to allotment to avoid unlawful financial assistance and breach of Section 195.

Funding & unlock path: The Company must obtain a Fire Services Installation (FSI) certificate or equivalent letter of compliance from the Hong Kong Fire Services Department for the registered office and any vehicle depot operated in HK, including portable fire extinguisher coverage and emergency evacuation plan approval. Civil defense clearance from the Hong Kong Buildings Department (Form BA1-Certificate of Compliance for Fire Resisting Construction) is required if the entity owns or leases a goods vehicle depot/warehouse above the historical fire load limit; the structural load inspection certificate from an authorized person must confirm the floor loading meets the fully loaded pallet and truck service requirements. For the office, a fresh air supply rate of at least 10 liters per second per person under the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) must be certified by an independent building services engineer. All drivers and forklift operators must hold valid driving licences and Dangerous Goods Handling certificates where cargo is classified as dangerous; the company must provide Personal Protective Equipment, a fatigue-management program aligned with the Road Traffic (Driving-Offence Points) Regulations, and medical surveillance for night shift gate staff. For PRC-side operations, the cross-border vehicle park must obtain the fire protection acceptance certificate (消防验收意见书) from the local fire rescue authority and a workplace safety pre-assessment (安全预评价) if the park stores any hazardous or TIR-bonded cargo; the State Administration of Work Safety / Emergency Management Bureau approval is mandatory before commencing bonded cargo handling where refrigerated or flammable goods are carried.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; online compliance and registration portal is the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) integrated with the STA national portal (www.chinatax.gov.cn). The Hong Kong parent entity, if separately required, would register with the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong SAR, but this dossier concerns the PRC WFOE as the registered taxpayer.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE, the statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). On incorporation, the SAMR one-stop/multi-department joint registration automatically transmits the establishment record to the STA, which completes the basic tax registration and assignment of taxpayer status without a separate standalone tax registration form. The WFOE must then activate its e-Tax Bureau account, obtain tax-control digital credentials or UKey-like e-tax certificates, and file the online taxpayer registration supplementary information (bank account information, accounting/tax method, and responsible person details) within the statutory period. For a foreign non-resident entity without a PE in China, including the Hong Kong shareholder when deriving royalties, dividends, interest or service fees from China, a separate non-resident tax registration record with the competent STA office of the withholding agent is required; this does not displace the WFOE's resident taxpayer identity. The WFOE must also complete the foreign investment information reporting with the MOFCOM/SAMR-linked reporting platform to preserve its legal status.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

None for corporate income tax: a PRC WFOE is a resident enterprise and has no check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch election. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general VAT taxpayer status. There is no economic threshold for the WFOE's initial tax registration because incorporation automatically creates the taxpayer record; an entity engaged in taxable services must register for VAT even if below the general taxpayer threshold, normally as a small-scale taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status. The applicable PRC VAT rate for the ISIC 5221 road freight transportation service is 9%, while the general goods rate is 13% and the residual services rate is 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China zero-rates a cross-border taxable service where the service is supplied to a non-resident consumer and is consumed entirely outside China. For ISIC 5221 cross-border bonded trucking under TIR Carnets, international road transport of goods between China and a foreign destination qualifies as an international transportation service under the zero-rating rules of CaiShui [2016] No. 36, provided the exporter/trucking operator holds the required customs exit evidence, a TIR Carnet establishing international customs transit, a CMR waybill evidencing the international road carriage, and proof that the freight receivable was collected from abroad in foreign exchange or RMB under SAFE cross-border receipt rules. Domestic segments within China are generally subject to 9% VAT unless they form an inseparable part of the exported supply under the applicable zero-rating guidance.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form with the customs-assigned export declaration number and border exit endorsement
  • TIR Carnet (Transports Internationaux Routiers) serving as the international customs transit and guarantee document
  • CMR consignment note (Convention on the Contract for the International Carriage of Goods by Road)
  • Multimodal or through Bill of Lading when road carriage is combined with an ocean/air leg
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the equivalent bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher and SAFE trade-in-goods monitoring record showing repatriation of export proceeds

China requires all VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices and ordinary invoices, to be issued through the STA-controlled e-invoice system and reported to the tax authority in near real time. The Golden Tax Phase III/IV architecture enforces the use of tax-controlled issuing devices; every invoice carries a unique 20-digit invoice number, a tax control code, and an anti-counterfeit QR code. Fully digital electronic invoices are generated through the STA full-digital e-invoice platform, replacing physical paper/fiscal stamp requirements. Export and zero-rated supplies must be supported by the electronic customs declaration data and bank foreign-exchange receipt files, and the VAT return must be submitted through the e-Tax Bureau portal with the relevant zero-rating declaration schedules attached.

If the STA or customs audits the zero-rating claim and finds that the goods did not physically leave China, the service was not consumed abroad, the TIR/CMR/customs documentation is false, or the export proceeds were not repatriated within the prescribed monitoring period, the zero-rating claim will be denied retroactively. The WFOE will be assessed output VAT at the applicable rate (9% for transport services or 13% if the supply is recharacterized as a goods sale), with additional input VAT clawbacks, late-payment surcharges of 0.05% per day, and an administrative penalty of between 50% and five times the underpaid tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export refund or zero-rating claims may also be referred for criminal liability under the Criminal Law provisions on fraud against export tax rebates, and the entity may be placed on the abnormal tax-payer list with enhanced audit scrutiny and restricted export refund facilitation.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a bifurcated foreign-exchange regime under SAFE: the current account is broadly convertible, while the capital account is strictly controlled. The WFOE's TIR road-freight business generates service income, fuel and toll expenses, and TIR guarantee settlements, all falling within current-account transaction codes. These can be settled freely through a designated domestic bank after the bank validates the transport contract, waybills, invoices and customs stamps. Surrender requirements have been abolished for goods exporters, but a carrier still needs to repatriate service proceeds through the Trade in Goods or Service Trade FX reporting system and can choose to hold foreign currency or convert it to RMB. On the capital account, the initial equity injection from the Hong Kong shareholder, any subsequent shareholder loans, and non-trade external debt must be registered with SAFE before the transfer arrives. Funds are held in a special capital settlement account; conversion into RMB is allowed, but the resulting RMB funds are subject to payment-use verification and cannot be diverted to unapproved purposes without an additional SAFE declaration. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent requires audited annual financial statements, tax clearance, a board resolution, and the bank's post-payment verification against SAFE's annual FDI report. Thus liquidity planning must strictly separate current-account convertibility from capital-account pre-registration and post-remittance compliance obligations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No statutory mandatory conversion percentage applies after the 2012 SAFE current-account settlement reform. The WFOE must repatriate export and service proceeds derived from TIR trucking into a domestic designated foreign-exchange account and complete verification against the actual trade underlying; for cross-border road freight governed by TIR Carnets, the bank expects proceeds to be credited within 90 days of the TIR departure or customs exit stamp unless a justified extension is approved. The WFOE may retain 100% of the foreign-currency receipt in its foreign-exchange account or convert the entire amount into RMB at the market rate; there is no residual surrender ratio, but each receipt must be supported by the transport invoice, CMR waybill, customs records, and the relevant SAFE trade-monitoring system filing.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. The PBOC and SAFE legal framework does not authorise foreign-origin electronic money institutions to operate local bank-valued settlement accounts for mainland legal persons. An offshore EMI such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex or Currenxie can issue a multi-currency wallet to the Hong Kong treasury parent, but it cannot clear the WFOE's domestic RMB payroll, VAT, customs duty, fuel-card funds, or SAFE-controlled capital flows. It cannot host the FDI capital settlement account or the export-proceeds collection account. Therefore the treasury blueprint must use tier-1 Chinese commercial banks for all regulatory cash rails and treat an EMI only as a potential invoicing and payment convenience for non-mainland counterparties, with its funds routed exclusively through licensed banking partners outside China.
