Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. No minimum paid-up capital requirement is imposed on a wholly foreign-owned company limited by shares in Hong Kong. The subscribers may set the issued share capital at a nominal value of HK$1 or any higher amount; no blocked escrow capital account is required by the Companies Ordinance. However, maritime counterparties, marine insurers, banks and charterers may impose their own financial strength requirements for offshore vessel leasing operations.

Funding & unlock path: Fire Services Department - issuing of a certificate of fire service installation for shore office, fuel storage and workshop areas; vessel fire protection equipment survey and structural stability certification, including fire detection, extinguishing systems, fire dampers and escape routes; Buildings Department - structural load certificate for any quayside crane or heavy lifting appliance and approval of fixed shore installations; Labour Department - industrial safety clearance and mandatory safety training for employees handling hazardous cargo; Marine Department - load line and stability endorsement for supply vessels, together with an occupational safety and health approval for the vessel crew before commercial operation commences.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

Primary: State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), online portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau). Hong Kong SAR secondary: Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong (IRD), online portal: eTAX via GovHK, only if a Hong Kong branch or separately registered Hong Kong taxpayer is created.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE: (1) Incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or the relevant local Market Regulation Administration; (2) obtain the Business License carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which functions as the taxpayer identification number for both enterprise income tax and VAT; (3) complete the automatic or supplementary tax registration with the STA within 30 days of incorporation at the competent tax office of the registered address; (4) apply for VAT general taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover will exceed RMB 5,000,000 or if the WFOE voluntarily requires input VAT credits and export zero-rating; (5) register for VAT invoice issuance through the STA electronic invoice platform. For a foreign non-resident entity with no PRC establishment, no local TIN is ordinarily obtained; the PRC payer must withhold VAT and enterprise income tax at source, unless the foreign entity registers with STA for non-resident tax purposes, in which case STA will issue a non-resident taxpayer registration number. For Hong Kong, if a separate HK taxpayer is established, apply to the Companies Registry and subsequently to the IRD for a Business Registration Certificate under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310); the Hong Kong Business Registration number is the tax file identifier and there is no separate VAT registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box election and no hybrid mismatch election are available in PRC law. The WFOE is automatically an entity-level CIT taxpayer at 25 percent on worldwide income. Available administrative elections include: (i) election of small-scale taxpayer treatment while annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5,000,000; (ii) voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer; (iii) application to the STA for cross-border zero-rating or export VAT refund status; and (iv) in a separate Hong Kong taxpayer, territorial-source profits tax assessment with an offshore claim, but no pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration. All established PRC WFOEs must register for tax without a turnover threshold; if annual taxable sales are RMB 5,000,000 or below, the taxpayer may remain a small-scale taxpayer subject to the 3 percent collection rate and cannot use export zero-rating with input VAT credits unless general taxpayer status is voluntarily elected; once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, general taxpayer registration is mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating is available under the PRC VAT Law and the STA cross-border VAT rules for: (a) goods physically exported from PRC customs territory, including an ocean vessel that is sold or exported with a customs export declaration; and (b) international transport services supplied by a PRC general VAT taxpayer, including time-charter and voyage-charter supply of offshore support vessels engaged on international routes or used wholly outside PRC territory. The WFOE must be a general VAT taxpayer, the charterer or purchaser must be an overseas entity, and the vessel or service must be consumed outside China. Bareboat or demise chartering is prima facie taxable leasing of tangible movable property at 13 percent; to claim export zero-rating or exemption, the WFOE must evidence that the vessel departed PRC customs territory or that the chartered vessel is used exclusively outside China and that the charter hire is received from outside China. Hong Kong is not a VAT territory and provides no separate zero-rating; zero-rating for this structure is governed entirely by PRC VAT law.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (the China Customs export declaration, released and stamped by customs, with the approved export customs code and waybill number)
  • Overseas sales or charter contract (charter party, bareboat charter, or time charter agreement) identifying the foreign charterer, vessel, charter hire, and delivery or redelivery points
  • Marine Bill of Lading or sea waybill evidencing cross-border movement of the vessel or the performance of offshore transport
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt confirmation / Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Chinese bank showing collection of the charter hire or export sales proceeds from abroad
  • Commercial invoice and packing list where the vessel or goods are physically exported
  • Certificate of vessel ownership and registration, and, if applicable, the customs export declaration of the vessel as a means of transportation
  • STA export tax refund declaration and underlying accounting records evidencing input VAT attributable to purchase, construction, or charter-in of the vessel

China mandates issuance of VAT invoices through STA-controlled platforms: the VAT Invoice Management System and the nationwide Fully Digital E-Invoice (FDEI) platform under the Golden Tax Phase IV project. General taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices through the FDEI platform or tax-control UKey infrastructure, and invoice data, including the national goods-and-services tax classification code, the Unified Social Credit Code of buyer and seller, and the applicable VAT rate, are transmitted to the STA in near real time. Recipients authenticate invoices through the STA invoice verification platform. There is no separate physical anti-fraud stamping mechanism; fiscal clearance and anti-fraud control are achieved by Golden Tax IV automated matching of invoice issuance, VAT credit claims, customs declarations, and foreign-exchange receipts. Hong Kong has no electronic invoicing or fiscal clearance mandate.

Export zero-rating claims are selected by STA risk-control and anti-fraud analytics for documentary, invoice, customs, and foreign-exchange matching. Failure to maintain the customs export declaration, Bill of Lading, or foreign-exchange receipt evidence, or failure to repatriate export or charter proceeds within the 180-day foreign-exchange deadline, will cause the zero-rating claim to be rejected or retrospectively clawed back; the WFOE must reverse the 0 percent treatment, charge output VAT at 13 percent or 9 percent as applicable, repay any input VAT already refunded, pay the daily late-payment surcharge under the Tax Collection Administration Law, and may be subject to a penalty of 50 percent to 5 times the tax involved. Fraudulent export refund claims are criminalized under article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties ranging from fixed-term imprisonment to life imprisonment, confiscation of property, and disqualification from the VAT export tax refund and exemption regime.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is fully convertible for current-account transactions such as service trade and normal trade remittances, but remains strictly controlled on the capital account. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) operating in the offshore marine charter sector is treated as a domestically incorporated onshore entity and must therefore route all inbound foreign direct investment through licensed banks that are registered with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). Foreign currency capital paid by the Hong Kong parent must be credited to a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account and then converted into RMB only after an exact purpose declaration under the SAFE Business Operating System. Dividends and profit remittances to the non-resident shareholder are conditional upon completion of annual audits, tax clearance with the in-charge tax bureau, and a formal board resolution; the remittance is processed with a current-account purpose code and requires the bank to verify historical paid-in capital and retained earnings. Although export proceeds are generally repatriation-bound, no mandatory conversion ratio is currently imposed, allowing the WFOE to retain export earnings in foreign currency or settle them at commercial bank prices. Settlement and sale of foreign exchange for capital-account items are subject to a genuine transaction verification standard, with penalties for diversion of capital-account funds into current-account use.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade and Service Trade controls, export foreign currency receipts arising from vessel leasing and charter hire must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange settlement account promptly. For goods trade, banks enforce the statutory collection period as the earlier of the contractual payment date or 90 days from the customs declaration date, unless SAFE grants a written extension through the foreign exchange monitoring system. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; mandatory surrender was abolished in 2012, so the WFOE may retain export proceeds in foreign currency or convert to RMB at the onshore commercial spot rate with the relevant contract, invoice, and vessel-operational supporting documents.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low feasibility rating. Offshore digital EMIs are legally barred from directly servicing a Chinese WFOE with proprietary current accounts, domestic RMB clearing, or FX conversion because they do not hold a local banking licence issued by the National Financial Regulatory Administration and the People's Bank of China. A Hong Kong or Singapore digital EMI may only be used as a front-end user interface if it is connected to a licensed SAFE-authorized domestic tier-1 bank through a compliant agency-collection model; otherwise it cannot perform SAFE registration, capital account handling, or onshore settlement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must effect the payment via SWIFT MT103 through CHATS or SWIFT to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a SAFE-authorized tier-1 bank. The bank must complete the SAFE FDI registration and issue a Foreign Exchange Business Registration Notice before funds are accepted. The wire is tagged with the foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment capital injection; upon receipt, the bank credits the escrowed capital account. The WFOE then submits a capital conversion application describing the exact planned business use, and the bank converts the foreign currency to RMB only after verifying the underlying transaction documents. A certified public accountant issues a capital verification report after confirming the bank statement and the SAFE underlying system record.Trade settlements for offshore supply vessels are executed through SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for direct payments, commercial letters of credit issued via SWIFT MT700 with tenor-matched settlement, and documentary collections under URC 522 using D/P or D/A. Cross-border RMB payments may be routed via the CIPS system directly or via SWIFT for other currencies; every payment is linked to a SAFE trade purpose code and supported by the invoice, bill of lading, charter party, or port call certificate. Turnover: Mandatory independent certified CPA audit applies to every Hong Kong company unless it is (i) dormant with no accounting transactions and no business activity, or (ii) a small private company that has validly elected audit exemption by unanimous written resolution and, in the relevant financial year, satisfies at least two of the following three quantitative tests: annual total revenue not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); total assets not exceeding HK$10 million (approximately USD 1.28 million); number of employees not exceeding 50. Stage 06

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% under PRC domestic law; reduced to 5% under the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA) where the Hong Kong shareholder is the beneficial owner and has held no less than 25% of the WFOE's equity for the 12-month period preceding the dividend distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Annual return must be filed and the final CIT settlement completed within five months after the end of each tax year, i.e. on or before May 31 of the following calendar year, together with audited financial statements and related-party transaction reporting schedules.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Provisional CIT payment must be made within 15 days after the end of each quarter: April 15, July 15, October 15, and January 15 of the following year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: CNY 200 million for related-party tangible property transactions; CNY 100 million for related-party intangible property transfers; CNY 100 million for financial asset transfers; CNY 40 million for related-party service transactions; CNY 40 million for all other related-party transactions; and CNY 1 billion for aggregate related-party transactions triggering Master File preparation. Related-party disclosure forms are mandatory whenever any related-party transaction exists, irrespective of these thresholds.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies substance-over-form and the GAAR under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 47 and 48, and Special Tax Adjustment rules under Articles 41-46, to disregard shell intermediaries and recharacterize transactions without commercial rationality. For the Hong Kong leg, the Hong Kong tax administration, relying on CIGA frameworks used in similar offshore centres, requires the HK shareholder to demonstrate core income-generating activities in Hong Kong, such as board meetings, decision-making, invoicing, bank accounts, and employment of staff. A Hong Kong shell entity lacking substance cannot claim DTA benefits or the 5% withholding reduction. The WFOE must equally maintain genuine operational substance in China: vessel ownership or long-term charter rights, crew employment contracts, port state control records, maintenance yards, and commercial management functions, or the corporate veil may be pierced.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of unpaid tax and late surcharge plus an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. PRC Criminal Law Article 201: criminal tax evasion where the evaded amount exceeds CNY 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the total tax payable. The entity may also be blacklisted for cross-border tax enforcement under the public credit management system.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers may be jointly and severally liable under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine, and face civil restitution of diverted profits, seizure of personal assets, travel bans (exit blacklisting), disqualification from serving as directors, and up to seven years imprisonment for tax evasion, in addition to fines.
Transfer Pricing Abuse and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 44 authorise special tax adjustments, reassessment of taxable income, and interest surcharge calculated from the underpayment date. The State Taxation Administration may, under Announcement No. 16/2016, impose a 5% interest uplift. Failure to provide contemporaneous documentation on schedule attracts a CNY 10,000 administrative fine, and will shift the burden to the taxpayer.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false related-party statements and divert profits may face personal civil liability for the tax deficiency, a personal 50%-500% penalty in severe cases, asset attachment, travel restriction, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion or collusive fraud where intentionality is proven.
Shell Company / AML and Economic Substance Violation

Statutory sanction: Improperly claimed treaty relief is clawed back; the withheld tax underpayment is reassessed at the full statutory 10% plus a 50%-300% withholding penalty under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. In Hong Kong, failure to comply with economic substance obligations may render the offshore status invalid and expose the entity to financial penalties and disgorgement; where money laundering channels are exploited, the Chinese Anti-Money Laundering and Hong Kong AML ordinances allow significant fines and imprisonment for officers.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for restitution of the extra withholding shortfall, civil fines, and any unlawful dividends taken. Travel bans may be imposed to prevent departure from China until tax liabilities are secured. Criminal liability includes imprisonment for up to seven years under PRC Criminal Law for tax fraud, and under Hong Kong law responsible officers may face imprisonment for up to fourteen years where laundering of the vessel charter income is proven.
