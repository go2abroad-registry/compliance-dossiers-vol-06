Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong imposes no minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement at incorporation; a private company limited by shares may validly incorporate with one issued share of HK$1. If the sole shareholder is a PRC-resident corporate investor, overseas direct investment approval and SAFE foreign exchange registration may require the agreed capital amount to be remitted after incorporation, but Hong Kong law does not mandate any government escrow or capital block.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to a pure digital and office-based crew recruitment agency. No Civil Defense fire permit is required. The office premises must nevertheless comply with the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) means-of-escape requirements and the Buildings Department structural load requirements under the Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123). If a crew training room, classroom or rest facility is operated, a Fire Services Department premises inspection and certificate may be required, and an occupational safety and health risk assessment must be documented under the Occupational Safety and Occupational Health Ordinance (Cap. 509).

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

CNY is fully convertible for current-account transactions under IMF Article VIII, so trade/service receipts, crew wages, and operational fees can generally be settled without SAFE prior approval. Capital-account transactions remain regulated and require prior registration or approval. The WFOE must complete the SAFE Foreign-Investment Information Registration before accepting inbound equity capital; once registered, the incoming capital contribution is credited to a capital account and can be converted to RMB on a payment-based settlement basis. There is no inbound surrender requirement, but each RMB settlement must be tied to valid business expenses such as salaries, rent, service contracts, and taxes. Profit remittance is permitted only after the annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, and SAFE verification at the paying bank; an audited profit that is distributable under Chinese company law can be wired out with a tax clearance certificate and dividend resolution. Foreign currency export or service receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated account, and while no forced conversion ratio exists, enterprises may retain the currency or settle it. A practical guardrail is that the capital account cannot be used for unrelated domestic lending, securities or real-estate investments except for specifically permitted working-capital uses.

Export proceeds surrender rules

SAFE currently does not impose mandatory foreign exchange conversion on export or service revenue; the legal requirement is repatriation through a licensed domestic bank. The WFOE must credit the FX receipts to a current-account foreign currency account within the contractual settlement period, and any receivable not collected within 90 days from the invoice date must be reported to the SAFE monitoring system with delinquency, write-off, or extension justification. No percentage of proceeds must be surrendered to the central bank; 100% of legally received export/service foreign exchange can be retained in the account or converted at the enterprise's discretion. Overseas retention requires an advance SAFE filing and must be reconciled within 12 months.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viability is low and structurally barred as the primary treasury account. A PRC WFOE cannot open a bank account with an offshore fintech EMI because EMIs do not hold a PRC banking licence and are not deposit-taking institutions under PRC banking law. The RMB settlement, FX receipt, and capital-account clearing rails must run through a bank authorised by PBOC and SAFE. Licensed non-bank payment institutions may be used only as ancillary payment processing layers, never as the corporate treasury account or capital-account clearing channel. Using an unlicensed EMI to receive crew-agency revenue or shareholder capital would trigger AML/KYC failure, cannot complete SAFE cross-border declaration, and is not accepted for capital verification. The compliant architecture therefore requires a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank account as the principal clearing house, with EMIs limited to card-disbursement or low-value payroll sub-processes that have no independent settlement status.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Compliant inbound routing begins with SAFE Foreign-Investment Registration completed at the local market supervision authority. The Hong Kong parent then instructs an SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated domestic bank using BOP purpose code 622011 for foreign direct investment equity injection, with the WFOE name and capital-account number reflected in the beneficiary details. The receiving bank verifies the FDI registration, corporate KYC, remitter identity, and cross-border reporting instruction, then credits the capital account. Conversion of the incoming foreign exchange to RMB must follow the payment-based settlement rule, presenting invoices, lease agreements, payroll schedules, tax vouchers, or service contracts for each disbursement. After the agreed subscription is fully paid, a PRC certified public accountant issues a capital verification report. Any supplemental treasury funding should be separately registered as foreign debt under SAFE macro-prudential rules; however, equity capital injection remains the cleanest and most audit-proof route.Use SWIFT MT103 for seafarer payroll, agency fees, crew supply payments, and non-documentary operating expenses; every instruction must carry the correct BOP purpose code and be backed by contract, invoice, tax payment certificate, and underlying title documents where applicable. For high-value marine equipment or vessel supply purchases, use a commercial letter of credit governed by UCP 600 issued by the same Tier-1 bank. For lower-value recurring trade flows, documentary collections (DP/DA) are permitted and should be routed with bills of lading, invoices, and inspection certificates. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: For a PRC WFOE, contemporaneous Local File documentation is required for the fiscal year if annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transactions, RMB 100 million for intangible property transactions, or RMB 40 million for loans or other related-party transactions. Master File preparation is generally triggered where the multinational group's consolidated prior-year revenue is at least RMB 5.5 billion and the enterprise has cross-border related-party transactions; Country-by-Country Reporting thresholds are aligned with the RMB 5.5 billion group revenue test. The annual Related-Party Transaction Reporting Form must be filed with the EIT annual return.

Economic substance scope: Economic substance is a live requirement in both the PRC and Hong Kong layers. For the WFOE, the ISIC 5222 statutory license requires a substantive local office, licensed crew managers, payroll records, and actual seafarer deployment activities. For the Hong Kong shareholder, Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) require the company to carry on core income-generating activities in Hong Kong, including holding board meetings, employing staff, maintaining a physical office, and exercising management and commercial decisions; this is also indispensable for claiming Tax Residency Certificates and DTA relief. The PRC GAAR under EIT Law Article 47 and Implementing Regulations Article 120 empowers tax authorities to disregard an intermediate entity without substance and reattribute income to the ultimate beneficial owner.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Documentation and Special Tax Adjustment Violation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 48 and SAT Bulletin 42/2016, the tax authority may re-determine taxable income using the most appropriate Transfer Pricing method, add a special tax adjustment interest margin of 5 percentage points above the benchmark loan rate, impose daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% on the underpaid tax, and, in cases of intentional concealment, impose a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve fabricated intercompany invoices, false service agreements, or fraudulent transfer pricing documentation may face civil asset restitution to the WFOE under PRC Company Law duties of loyalty and diligence. Tax authorities may impose exit control and travel bans on the legal representative and key responsible directors under the tax dishonesty joint punishment mechanism. Where the scheme results in substantial tax evasion, directors face criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment of up to 7 years, fines, and confiscation of personal gains.
Shell Company and Tax Treaty Abuse / Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: China's GAAR under EIT Law Article 47, Implementation Regulations Article 120, and SAT Announcement 2015/16 permits the tax authority to disregard the Hong Kong company, recharacterize the dividend as paid to the ultimate non-resident beneficial owner, deny treaty relief, and collect the full statutory 10% withholding tax plus daily 0.05% late-payment interest and administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid withholding tax under Tax Collection Law Articles 32 and 63. Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Department may also enforce Hong Kong ESR penalties for absence of economic substance, including financial penalties and daily default surcharges, and may revoke tax resident status retrospectively.

Director personal liability: Directors of the Hong Kong shell and the PRC WFOE may be treated as alter egos and jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax, civil penalties, and restitution of dividends to the company. Both jurisdictions may impose travel bans against tax debtors and fraudulent directors. Criminal exposure includes imprisonment for tax evasion and fraud under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 with maximum custodial terms of up to 7 years, plus asset disgorgement, corporate veil piercing, and personal fines for directors who signed false residency certificates or beneficial ownership declarations.
Tax Evasion, False Invoicing, and AML Violations in Crew Payroll

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law Article 63, underpayment caused by false invoices, forged books, or concealment of revenue attracts a penalty of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax and a criminal referral. PRC Criminal Law Article 205 imposes severe punishment, including life imprisonment in the most extreme cases, for the issue or use of false special VAT invoices. Financial and AML violations under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and foreign exchange controls expose the WFOE to fines, suspension of operating license, forfeiture of cross-border payment capabilities, and exclusion from the SWIFT payment network.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who sign fraudulent returns, authorize false invoices, or direct off-book payroll payments are personally liable to make the company whole, disgorge personal profits, and pay civil restitution. They are subject to exit bans and blacklisting as seriously dishonest taxpayers. Criminal sentences for willful participation in false VAT invoice schemes can range from 3 years to life imprisonment depending on the amount, with mandatory fines and confiscation; tax evasion convictions under Criminal Law Article 201 carry imprisonment of up to 7 years for substantial underpayments.
