Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account. Current account foreign exchange operations, including trade receipts for bunker deliveries, export proceeds, and service fees, are generally convertible in practice because designated banks may execute settlement or purchase transactions upon verification of authentic underlying documents under the trade-and-payment authenticity system. Capital account transactions are tightly controlled: equity contributions into the WFOE require FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE and must be booked through a dedicated capital account; capital cannot be freely converted to RMB or transferred among group members beyond approved business scope or working capital limits. Profit and dividend repatriation from the WFOE to the Hong Kong parent is treated as current-account remittance but requires audited financial statements, proof of corporate income tax settlement, a board profit distribution resolution, and bank-level authenticity review. Surrender rules do not impose a fixed statutory conversion ratio, but export proceeds must be repatriated to China and either retained in the WFOE's designated domestic foreign exchange account or settled through a licensed bank using verified customs and cargo documents, with no discretionary offshore retention or booking through unlicensed fintech intermediaries. The practical hurdles are documentary rather than administrative discretion: invoice authenticity, cargo evidence, customs alignment, tax clearance, and transparency of beneficiary ownership drive convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current-account rules, export proceeds must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange settlement account in the name of the WFOE within 90 days from the relevant customs export declaration date. The receiving bank performs transaction authenticity verification, matching the SWIFT sender, invoice, bill of lading, quantity certificate, and export customs declaration. Mandatory conversion ratios are no longer applied to ordinary commercial exporters; however, 100% of offshore bunkering-related receipts must be declared, repatriated, and either retained in a domestic foreign exchange account or converted into CNY through a SAFE-approved designated bank. Any deviation in title, amount, counterparty, or declared purpose must be self-corrected and reported to SAFE within one month.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the offshore reference regulator for the Hong Kong holding layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally barred in practical terms. Offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed or supervised by SAFE, PBOC, or CBIRC for opening accounts or executing foreign exchange settlement on behalf of a mainland WFOE. They cannot issue CNY clearing accounts, cannot provide the required tax refund trail, cannot support capital verification, and would expose the WFOE to unauthorised cross-border payment flow risk and potential AML violation. The only acceptable treasury architecture is a direct Tier-1 domestic commercial bank relationship; fintech solutions may be used solely as non-accounting data aggregation tools, never as payment, custody, or clearing rails for bunkering transactions.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong parent to the mainland WFOE must follow the foreign direct investment framework: first, complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration through the receiving bank using the signed capital contribution contract and WFOE business licence; second, open a dedicated FDI capital account in the WFOE name; third, receive the remittance from the parent's own corporate bank account via SWIFT MT103 under the purpose code for direct investment capital injection; and fourth, engage a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report after receipt. Conversion of the capital into CNY should be executed only under the payment-before-settlement principle by submitting the intended use documents to the bank, such as fuel purchase contracts, port service agreements, payroll, and rent. Proceeds must never be used for equity investment, security trading, lending, or activities outside the direct business scope without SAFE approval.Outbound treasury disbursements for bunker inventory, lubricants, port services, and vessel-related expenses should be placed on SWIFT MT103 for straightforward operational payments; irreversible commercial documentary letters of credit in USD or HKD for large marine fuel procurement contracts; standby letters of credit for security in high-value master supply agreements; and documentary collections for deferred payment settlements against port documents. Each disbursement must be supported by the underlying bunker contract, debit note, bunker delivery receipt, independent quantity or quality certificate, port charge invoice, import or export customs declaration where applicable, and valid tax invoice to permit the Chinese bank to complete authenticity verification without delay.Onshore CNY/USD and CNY/HKD deliverable forward contracts under the real demand principle with SAFE-approved underlying bunkering trade or dividend exposureOnshore FX swaps in CNY against USD or HKD for matched working capital tenors, subject to trade background documentation and bank credit approvalPlain-vanilla forward deals, including average-rate forwards for monthly marine fuel cost rolls, where the notional does not exceed the contracted trade flowOffshore CNH-based NDF or speculative option structures are not permissible for the mainland WFOE and may only be considered by the Hong Kong holding entity on a fully standalone basis outside the Chinese onshore treasury perimeter

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB-denominated thresholds (SAT Announcement 2016/No. 42): Local File is mandatory if any single related-party transaction category in a fiscal year equals or exceeds: (a) tangible asset transfers (excluding financial assets) - RMB 200 million; (b) intangible asset transfers - RMB 100 million; (c) financial asset transfers - RMB 100 million; (d) related-party services - RMB 40 million. Master File is mandatory if the group's aggregate intercompany transactions in the previous year exceed RMB 10 billion OR total revenue exceeding RMB 5.5 billion and related-party transactions exceeding RMB 50 million, or if the group has a special-purpose/reverse-EBITDA company (specific thresholds). Even if below thresholds, contemporaneous documentation must be prepared when the competent authority requests it within the filing red-flag review.

Economic substance scope: Although China lacks a formal 'economic substance regulation' akin to the UAE, substance is central to treaty and GAAR analysis. The WFOE must demonstrate that its core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as fuel procurement, pricing, vessel scheduling, risk management, contract negotiation, and inventory title transfer are actually performed by employees physically located, economically staffed, and decision-made in China. If the Hong Kong shareholder does not have office, staff, and real business activity to justify the shareholding and financing, the SAT can recharacterise the arrangement under CIT Law Art. 47 and the 2018/No.9 Circular (GAAR), disallow treaty benefits, impose WHT at 10%, levy interest and a penalty equal to 50% of the underpaid tax. Additionally, the PRC's CFC rules (Art. 45) can attribute undistributed profits of a HK subsidiary back to the WFOE if the HK subsidiary is not a genuine economic enterprise; must keep proper governance and separate books.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via False Bunkering Invoices and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 6, Art. 41; Tax Collection Administration Law (PRC) Art. 63: defrauding tax authority leads to recovery of underpaid tax and a monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid amount; additional daily overdue surcharge of 0.05%; if serious, under Criminal Law Art. 201, a fine and imprisonment of up to 7 years for the entity's direct responsible personnel.

Director personal liability: Directors who engaged in the fraudulent invoicing or knowingly executed illegal tax avoidance can be named as 'directly responsible persons' under Criminal Law Art. 201. They face personal fines, asset seizure, up to 7 years' imprisonment, and a tax-confidence blacklist (public announcement) forbidding overseas travel through exit control (travel ban). Personal liability pierces the corporate veil where the WFOE is used as a sham vehicle to disguise the HK shareholder's true revenues (GAAR recharacterisation); the court may order civil restitution and liquidation.
Shell/Conduit Mischaracterisation for Treaty Shopping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 47 (GAAR); SAT Announcement 2018/No. 9 (Beneficial Ownership); Administrative Measures for Non-Resident Enterprises (SAT Order No. 32). The tax authority may recharacterise the entire ownership structure, deny DTA benefits, impose withholding at the statutory 10% (or higher applicable tax), plus interest and penalties of up to 3 times the underpaid tax for breaches of administrative transparency rules. Criminal exposure for forged TRC or false declarations can arise under Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax fraud) or Art. 280 (forgery of official documents).

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised the issuance of false TRC representations or signed off on artificial conduit arrangements may be held personally liable under the PRC Company Law for causing asset dilution (derivative liability) and under the GAAR regime for recouping taxes. They can face a travel ban (blacklist), personal bank release of assets, and the court may pierce the veil if they used the WFOE/HK company to evade taxes or harm creditors; criminal penalties include up to 3 years' detention for perjury if documents are falsified.
AML & Economic Sanctions Violation via Ship-to-Ship (STS) Bunkering Financing

Statutory sanction: PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law (2006, revised 2018) Art. 30-34; State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules; Customs Law Art. 82 for false declarations. An enterprise may be fined up to RMB 5 million, its bunkering licence suspended, and the tax authority can disallow all connected expenses as non-deductible because illegal payments (sanctions-breach expenses) are not deductible under Tax Collection Art. 46. Violation of the UN Security Council sanction adoption (Foreign Relations Law) creates criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 191 (money laundering), with imprisonment up to 10 years and fines of up to 50% of the laundered amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorised or recklessly permitted the non-compliant wire transfer and LC operations are personally criminally liable. Under Art. 191, individuals may receive up to 10 years' imprisonment and confiscation of property; under the AML Law, the regulator can blacklist and ban them from serving as senior executives in any financial or corporate entity for 5-10 years. The corporate veil will be pierced if the WFOE is a thin-capitalised shell, allowing creditors and regulatory authorities to execute civil judgments against the directors' personal assets; travel bans and asset freezes will apply by the PBOC/SAFE and MPS red-listing mechanisms.
