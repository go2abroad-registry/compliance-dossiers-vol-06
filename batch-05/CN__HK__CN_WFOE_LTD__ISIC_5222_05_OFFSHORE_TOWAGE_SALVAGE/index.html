None. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the local competent tax service bureau in the jurisdiction where the WFOE is registered; electronic filing and registration are completed through the STA e-Tax electronic taxpayer platform. For any Hong Kong-resident parent or investor tax identity matter, the separate competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident establishing a PRC WFOE does not apply for a separate mainland TIN before incorporation; the WFOE is formed through the Administration for Market Regulation and is issued an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number and VAT registration identity. Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent STA office through the e-Tax platform, confirm the industrial classification (ISIC 5222 marine salvage and towing support services), select VAT filing status as small-scale or general taxpayer, apply for invoice types and issuance limits, and link the bank account to the tax file. If the WFOE exports goods or supplies cross-border zero-rated services, it must also complete GACC customs registration, obtain export tax refund qualification in the STA e-Tax platform, and maintain the electronic customs declaration linkage. The Hong Kong non-resident shareholder or lender may file a Non-Resident Enterprise Tax Registration Code application or certificate of Hong Kong tax residence with the competent tax authority when claiming reduced withholding under the Mainland-Hong Kong tax arrangement; absent a permanent establishment, the non-resident parent is not required to obtain a full mainland TIN, and the Chinese withholding agent will report and withhold any China-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None comparable to a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; the WFOE is mandatorily an entity-level taxpayer. Available limited elections are: voluntary registration as a general VAT taxpayer before reaching the CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover threshold, and, for a qualifying Hong Kong beneficial owner, election to apply the 5% reduced withholding rate on dividends, interest, royalties, and technical fees under the Mainland-Hong Kong tax arrangement instead of the statutory 10% CIT withholding rate.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover; exceeding this amount makes general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory. Below this threshold, small-scale taxpayer status applies with a simplified levy of 3% unless temporarily reduced by STA policy, and applicable small-scale taxpayer exemptions may be available.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods physically exported from mainland China through GACC customs clearance can be zero-rated if the export is genuine, the customs export declaration is filed, the goods leave the PRC, the proceeds are received from a non-resident buyer, and the supplier is a registered export taxpayer with an eligible commodity code. Cross-border taxable services listed in the cross-border service zero-rating and exemption catalogue, including international transportation services and offshore services wholly supplied and consumed outside mainland China, may also be zero-rated. For offshore towage and maritime salvage, zero-rating is defensible where the recipient is non-resident, the tug or salvage operation is performed wholly outside PRC territorial waters or between foreign ports, the contract expressly so provides, and contemporaneous GPS/vessel log evidence confirms overseas performance. Qualifying offshore digital/electronic services supplied to non-resident customers and consumed entirely abroad are similarly eligible; domestic services or services consumed in mainland China remain taxable at the applicable VAT rate, being 6% for logistics auxiliary/salvage services or 9% for transportation services as determined by the competent tax authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) approved by GACC
  • Bill of Lading (or sea waybill and foreign-port sailing statement)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a designated foreign-exchange bank
  • Cross-border towage/salvage service contract with the non-resident customer
  • Commercial invoice and detailed service-performance statement including GPS tracks, vessel logs, and salvage report
  • Export tax rebate or zero-rating declaration in the STA e-Tax platform

VAT invoices must be issued through the STA unified invoice management system and, in digitalised jurisdictions, through the fully digitalised electronic invoice (FDEI) platform; invoice details are transmitted nearly in real time, with each invoice assigned a nationwide unique invoice number and anti-counterfeiting verification code. The Golden Tax Phase IV system cross-matches invoice data with GACC export declarations and bank/FX receipt records. Special VAT invoices require block-level approval, and export zero-rating claims must be supported by the electronic customs declaration and matching e-invoice/FIRC data; no separate physical tax stamp is issued, but all invoice data is cleared through STA systems.

STA, GACC, and SAFE perform risk-based matching of export declarations, VAT invoice data, vessel movement records, and FX receipt filings. A zero-rating claim lacking the required customs, FX, or performance evidence is reversed retrospectively; output VAT at the correct domestic rate becomes payable, and late-payment interest accrues at 0.05% per day. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, deficiencies arising from inaccurate or fraudulent zero-rating claims attract penalties of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax, tax blacklisting, and potential revocation of export tax refund status. Criminal exposure for fraudulently obtaining export VAT refunds under PRC Criminal Law includes fines, confiscation, imprisonment, and in aggravated cases imprisonment up to life imprisonment, together with customs sanctions and clawback of any refunded input VAT.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains RMB current-account convertibility under IMF Article VIII for trade and service payments, but capital-account transactions remain strictly controlled by SAFE through registration, quota and negative-list requirements. For the WFOE, current-account receipts arising from offshore towage and salvage contracts must be repatriated onshore, declared through the bank's service-trade monitoring system and matched to the underlying invoice/contract before they can be freely converted into RMB or retained as foreign currency. There is no mandatory surrender ratio but the flow must be evidenced and reported. Equity capital injected by the Hong Kong parent is a capital-account item: it requires a complete SAFE FDI registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, a committed use statement and negative-list compliance before any settlement into RMB. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permissible only after the annual statutory audit, corporate income tax settlement, dividend-withholding tax payment and a board resolution declaring the dividend; SAFE and the bank must verify the tax payment certificate and the source of distributable profits. In practice, the main convertibility hurdles are evidence of genuine service transactions, strict use limitations on capital-settlement funds, and documentary consistency between the offshore contract, the escrow arrangement and the actual bank gateway

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods-trade and service-trade export receipts, the WFOE must repatriate the foreign currency proceeds onshore through a licensed domestic bank within 90 days after the contractually agreed payment date, and in all cases no later than 180 calendar days from the date of the customs export declaration, commercial invoice or completion certificate applicable to the towage/salvage milestone. Any deferred-payment marine salvage arrangement beyond this window must be filed with SAFE through the designated bank as a deferred collection registration supported by the signed salvage contract. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100% of the foreign currency receipts in its current-account FX account or convert them at the bank spot rate, but the bank must report the transaction to SAFE through the As-One trade monitoring platform and issue a trade-in-services receipt

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises licensed banks in Hong Kong but does not impose foreign exchange controls on the Hong Kong parent

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable for the mainland WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Currenxie or Revolut Business are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE to hold current accounts, capital accounts or capital-settlement renminbi accounts for mainland-registered entities. The WFOE must clear all statutory capital, export receipts, trade invoices and dividend remittances through a licensed Chinese commercial bank ; any attempt to route CN_WFOE_LTD corporate funds through an offshore EMI would breach the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations and expose the entity to SAFE penalties, frozen payments and reputational damage. The Hong Kong parent may use an EMI only for incidental offshore treasury expenses outside the mainland flow, but such a channel must never be used for the WFOE's onshore account or capital verification processes

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must instruct its licensed Hong Kong bank to remit the subscribed equity via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account held with ICBC or BOC. Before the remittance, the WFOE must complete FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE through the onshore account bank, open the capital settlement account, and upload the Articles of Association, business licence and FA/change records. The wire instruction must include the WFOE's unified social credit code and clearly state 'Registered capital injection CN_WFOE_LTD' with no reference to trade invoicing. After receipt, the bank verifies the remitter is the registered shareholder, checks the transaction against the FDI registration, and settles the foreign exchange into RMB only upon receipt of the enterprise's capital-payment commitment letter and confirmation that the funds will be used within the permitted negative-list scope. A certified public accountant then issues the capital verification report used for the final business licence filing and SAFE system updateThe WFOE will disburse funds to international tug owners, salvage subcontractors, fuel suppliers, P&I clubs, repair yards and equipment vendors using a compliant tier of clearing rails. Ordinary operational payments below the bank's high-risk threshold are executed by SWIFT MT103 with invoices, towage completion certificates, service acceptance reports and, when withholding tax is due, the relevant tax payment voucher. For high-value procurement such as a new salvage tug, subsea equipment or emergency repairs, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit (MT700) is the preferred clearing rail because it protects both buyer and seller. Documentary collections (D/P and D/A) are permissible for medium-risk recurring transactions with well-known counterparties. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation is mandatory if annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million for any single type of service or RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; Master File required for groups exceeding RMB 2 billion in annual related-party turnover.

Economic substance scope: Although China has no separate ESR, the beneficial ownership substance requirement under tax treaties demands that the Hong Kong shareholder carry on substantive business activities. Core income-generating activities (CIGA) analysis applies in transfer pricing audits, and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 may recharacterize transactions lacking economic substance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraud

Statutory sanction: Under Law on Administration of Tax Levying and Collection Article 63: recovery of unpaid taxes, late-payment surcharge, and penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; where criminal liability is triggered, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes a fine and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Directors may have personal assets seized to satisfy unpaid corporate taxes if they participated in the fraud; they may face criminal prosecution with imprisonment; blacklisting as a serious dishonest taxpayer leads to travel bans, restrictions on high-consumption, and being barred from leaving China.
Non-Withholding of Dividends

Statutory sanction: Article 69 of the Tax Administration Law: the withholding agent shall be fined 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld; interest runs on unpaid sums.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the company to omit withholding are jointly and severally liable for the withheld tax; may face administrative penalties and criminal liability under the Criminal Law for tax evasion; travel bans may be imposed for non-payment.
Transfer Pricing Mis-pricing / Empty Shell

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 47: the tax authority may make transfer pricing adjustments, add interest surcharge for underpaid tax, and impose penalties if the mispricing resulted from fraud or concealment; Special Tax Adjustment (STA) may apply with surcharges.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for joint tax obligations if they authorized the sham arrangements; could be banned from serving as directors or senior executives; under GAAR, personal assets may be used to offset tax liabilities; criminal consequences for intentional tax evasion may include imprisonment up to 7 years and disqualification.
