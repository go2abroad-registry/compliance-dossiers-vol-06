Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

People's Republic of China - State Taxation Administration of the PRC (STA); online electronic portal is the unified National Electronic Tax Administration (eTax) platform linked from the STA official website (www.chinatax.gov.cn) and operated through provincial and taxpayer-specific electronic tax bureau portals. For the Hong Kong SAR limb: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR and its eTAX portal (www.ird.gov.hk); Hong Kong has no VAT/GST/sales tax registration, only profits tax, property tax, and salaries tax administration.

E-government filing portal

The CN WFOE is a foreign-invested but domestically incorporated Chinese resident enterprise. The statutory tax identification procurement path is: (1) Incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN and VAT taxpayer identification number for all Chinese legal persons. (2) Within 30 calendar days of business license issuance, complete new taxpayer onboarding in the National Electronic Tax Administration by filing the Tax Registration Form for Unit Taxpayers, articles/certificate of incorporation, legal representative identity proof, registered address certificate, bank account opening certificate, and board resolutions; the competent tax bureau authenticates the taxpayer, assigns tax categories (corporate income tax, VAT, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharges, stamp tax, etc.) and issues the tax registration conclusion. (3) If annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or if the entity needs to claim input VAT credits, file the General VAT Taxpayer registration application through the same eTax portal; the tax authority approves general VAT taxpayer status and authorizes issuance of VAT special invoices. (4) For a genuinely foreign non-resident enterprise without a PRC establishment supplying services or deriving PRC-source income, there is no USCC-based TIN issued; the PRC payer must file a non-resident tax registration/withholding case with the competent tax bureau and withhold VAT and corporate income tax at source using the payer's reference number. (5) The HK shareholder or parent entity is not required to obtain a PRC TIN merely by holding equity in the WFOE; dividend repatriation is handled under the PRC-HK double taxation arrangement and the HK shareholder remains subject to Hong Kong territorial profits tax to the extent applicable.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election regime. A Chinese resident WFOE is always entity-level taxed for corporate income tax and VAT. The only available statutory elections are (a) the small-scale VAT taxpayer status until annual taxable turnover reaches RMB 5,000,000 and (b) the simplified VAT levy method for prescribed service categories; neither constitutes a pass-through or ignored-entity election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover (mandatory General VAT Taxpayer registration threshold); a newly established WFOE is subject to mandatory tax registration from day one regardless of turnover, while Hong Kong has no VAT registration threshold because no indirect tax exists.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A qualifying PRC General VAT Taxpayer may apply the 0% rate and claim an export VAT refund for goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory with a valid customs export declaration, a genuine cross-border commercial transaction, and repatriation of export proceeds within the statutory foreign exchange deadline. Zero-rating also applies to qualifying cross-border services, especially international transport services, where the service begins or ends outside the PRC or constitutes an international carriage segment between a PRC customs port and a foreign country or the Hong Kong SAR. For the customs-bonded road feeder truck fleet, zero-rating/exemption is available only when the truck movement is genuinely part of a customs-supervised export/import transit movement, evidenced by the PRC customs bonded transport manifest, GPS-tamper-evident seal records, and an international main transport document; purely domestic airport-to-warehouse feeder movements remain taxable at 9% for transport, or 6% if recharacterized as logistics support. For qualifying offshore digital services, the PRC zero-rating rules may apply when the service is rendered entirely outside the PRC and no PRC permanent establishment/consumption occurs, but that is not the primary activity of this bonded truck fleet. The Hong Kong SAR itself has no VAT/GST, so no zero-rating or refund substantiation is required on the Hong Kong side.

  • Original through Bill of Lading, Air Waybill (MAWB/HAWB), or multimodal combined transport document showing the international movement and identifying the bonded feeder operator as carrier

China operates Golden Tax Phase IV and the Fully Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) clearance regime. All VAT ordinary invoices and VAT special invoices must be issued from the State Taxation Administration-controlled electronic invoice service platform, with invoice data transmitted to the tax authority in real time or near real time. VAT special invoices carry encrypted digital invoice codes, anti-counterfeit QR/cipher codes, and must be reconciled by the buyer through the national VAT invoice verification platform. General VAT taxpayers must issue special invoices through the tax-control UKey or e-tax system; any non-platform, manually produced invoice is invalid for input VAT deduction. There is no physical fiscal stamping, but digital clearance and anti-fraud stamping are embedded in the electronic invoice platform through automatic transmission, inventory control, and cross-matching with customs and banking data.

The State Taxation Administration cross-matches export VAT refund claims with customs export declarations, bonded transport GPS and seal records, and SAFE foreign exchange receipt data. If export zero-rating is claimed without the complete documentation bundle, or if foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated within 180 days or an officially approved extension, the tax authority will deny the refund and claw back the VAT input credit or tax exemption, plus late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Administrative penalties range from 50% to 500% of the tax incorrectly refunded or underpaid. Fraudulent export tax refund behavior may trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, including imprisonment, where false customs declarations, forged transport documents, or fabricated foreign exchange receipts are involved. Customs authorities may separately impose fines, suspend or revoke the bonded transport clearance, and the transport regulator may revoke the road freight licence. The limitations period for tax adjustment is 3 years generally, 5 years for negligent under-declaration, and unlimited for fraudulent evasion or misrepresentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is only partially convertible. Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, current account transactions such as trade payments, service fees, and profit remittances are generally convertible once genuine and compliant supporting documents are submitted to the designated foreign exchange bank. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment injections into a WFOE, debt financing, equity transfers, and liquidation proceeds repatriation, require SAFE registration, filing, or approval. Export proceeds are subject to a mandatory surrender system administered through the SAFE monitoring platform, though in practice exporters may retain earnings in foreign currency accounts up to a verified quota. The WFOE's paid-in capital from its Hong Kong parent must be remitted through the FDI capital account, converted to CNY only for approved operating purposes, and cannot be arbitrarily transferred to related parties without supporting trade or investment documentation. Profit repatriation requires a prior-year audited financial statement, corporate income tax settlement clearance, board resolution on dividend distribution, and a SAFE-controlled bank remittance application using the purpose code for dividend remittance. The Customs Bonded Truck Fleet business is a low-risk operational activity, but the logistics invoices, import/export customs declarations, and GPS trip logs must be preserved for at least five years to substantiate the underlying real trade nature and prevent misclassification of capital flows. Overall, the regime is administratively strict but operationally navigable for a licensed WFOE with proper compliance infrastructure, and no parallel market or offshore conversion shortcuts should be contemplated under any circumstance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into China and reported to SAFE within 180 days of the date on the customs export declaration, and the exporter is required to either retain the funds in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert the proceeds through a designated bank. For ordinary trade items, mandatory conversion applies to the portion exceeding the verified operating foreign currency retention quota; the standard benchmark for the customs bonded trucking sector is that all RMB conversion amounts above a 10 percent automatic retention ceiling must be surrendered into CNY, subject to bank verification against the customs declaration and logistic service invoices. Any extension beyond the 180-day window must be filed with SAFE before the original deadline, and the supporting evidence must include the unpaid overseas receivable aging report, the commercial contract, and the carrier's bill of lading.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBoC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Fintech EMI providers such as digital-only neobanks, offshore e-money institutions, and virtual account platforms are legally barred from serving as primary transaction accounts for a mainland Chinese WFOE. The PRC banking regulatory framework requires any entity accepting RMB deposits or settling foreign exchange to hold a commercial banking license issued by the PBoC/CBIRC, and no offshore EMI is permitted to open a domestic nostro or settlement account on behalf of a Chinese operating entity. Domestic fintech payment companies, such as licensed third-party agencies, may only handle the collection and disbursement of trade payments as ancillary processors under the umbrella of a licensed commercial bank, and they cannot perform capital account settlement, export proceeds surrender, or customs duty payment facilitation independently. Therefore, the WFOE must use a domestic Tier-1 commercial bank as the sole clearing counterparty for all inbound capital injections, outbound trade disbursements, tax payments, and FX hedging instruments. Offshore EMIs are only permissible for the Hong Kong parent company's internal treasury operations outside mainland China, but that does not replace the mainland bank account mandate.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first complete the Foreign Investment Filing with the Ministry of Commerce and register the FDI quota with SAFE through the designated bank. The capital injection is then routed via SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent's account to the WFOE's Capital Escrow Account at Bank of China or another licensed designated bank. The MT103 must reference the SAFE FDI registration number, the purpose code 1100 for equity injection capital, and the exact matching invoice or shareholder resolution reference. Upon receipt, the bank automatically reports the transaction to SAFE through the capital project information system, and the WFOE must then submit the capital verification report generated by a local certified public accountant within 30 days to confirm the registered capital has been actually paid. Funds may be held in the escrow account in foreign currency and subsequently converted to CNY under the bank's delegated settlement authority for payroll, warehouse rental, vehicle acquisition, fuel, customs deposits, and other bona fide operating expenses. No conversion may be used to repay a domestic RMB loan or to invest in an unrelated securities product, as this would violate the negative list of capital account use.All outbound trade disbursements for the customs bonded trucking operation must be cleared through the designated bank's foreign exchange settlement ledger using SWIFT MT103 for cross-border supplier payments, customs duty deposits, and overseas terminal fees. For letter-of-credit payments, the WFOE must open a Commercial LC at the designated bank covering purchase contracts for vehicles or equipment, with documents including the bill of lading, commercial invoice, customs transit declaration, and insurance certificate. Documentary collections are appropriate for lower-value recurring counterparties, but the bank will still require the underlying freight service confirmation and the GPS trip log for cross-border cargo runs. Every outbound payment must be synchronized with the enterprise's SAFE trade payment verification system, and the bank will cross-check the business scope, the actual transport distance, and the vehicle registration plate to ensure the transaction is consistent with the licensed ISIC 5222 activity. Since the bonded truck fleet crosses between mainland China and Hong Kong, the disbursement file must also include the green customs seal record for the vehicle to satisfy the anti-smuggling compliance checks.

Offshore EMIs are only permissible for the Hong Kong parent company's internal treasury operations outside mainland China, but that does not replace the mainland bank account mandate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions in a tax year reach: RMB 200 million or more for tangible property transfers (the bonded truck fleet or spare parts), RMB 100 million or more for intangible property transfers or financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million or more for other related-party transactions (management fees, services, interest). Master File is required where the enterprise is a member of a group whose consolidated annual revenue in the previous fiscal year was RMB 5.5 billion or above; Country-by-Country reporting notification applies at the same group revenue threshold.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form under General Anti-Abuse Rule (EIT Law Article 47) and the special anti-treaty-abuse rules in State Administration of Taxation Public Notice No. 60 of 2015. The Hong Kong parent claiming treaty benefits must have actual offices, employees, board decision-making, bank accounts, and risk-bearing capacity in Hong Kong; a pure conduit or mailbox entity will be denied benefits and reclassified as the beneficial owner of the WFOE or as a permanent establishment. For the WFOE itself, Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) for the bonded road feeder service must evidence operational substance in China: GPS-sealed fleet management, driver employment, customs compliance, route optimization, maintenance, invoicing, insurance, and contract negotiation. Outsourcing these functions to Hong Kong without real personnel and premises will be disregarded for tax purposes and may expose the group to treaty denial and to Chinese permanent establishment risk if the Hong Kong entity is deemed to carry on business in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Customs Bonded Regulatory Falsification / Smuggling

Statutory sanction: PRC Customs Law (2021 Revision) Article 82 empowers customs authorities to confiscate smuggled goods and means of transport, recover evaded duties, and impose a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. Criminal Law Article 153 makes smuggling ordinary goods and articles a criminal offence where evaded duties exceed RMB 50,000: imprisonment up to 3 years and fine for relatively large evasion, 3 to 10 years for huge evasion, and 10 years to life imprisonment with fine or confiscation for extremely huge evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who order or knowingly participate in the falsification are personally criminally liable as co-principals under Criminal Law Article 153. The court may pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Articles 20 and 21, holding directors jointly and severally liable for customs duties, VAT, fines, and third-party losses. Legal representatives become subject to travel bans, border exit restrictions under the Exit and Entry Administration Law Article 12, and the Integrated Social Credit System blacklist, which bars them from new company incorporation and government procurement.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44, and 48 allow the tax authority to re-determine taxable income by applying the arm's length principle and impose special tax adjustment interest of the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points, with a 5-year look-back period. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax for fraudulent underdeclaration. If the underpaid tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and is more than 10% of the amount properly payable, Criminal Law Article 201 applies, with fines and imprisonment of up to 7 years for severe evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly create false intercompany contracts or sign fraudulent transfer pricing documentation risk personal criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201. The civil court may pierce the corporate veil to impose joint and several personal liability for corporate tax debts where the corporate structure is utilized to evade tax. The legal representative can be prohibited from leaving China until all tax, penalties, and interests are satisfied, and may be disqualified from serving as a director or manager in new enterprises.
Sham Beneficial Ownership / Dividend Withholding Abuse

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 requires the withholding agent to pay 50% to 300% of the tax amount that was not withheld or recovered. Under SAT Public Notice No. 60 of 2015 and EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the beneficial owner, and impose the full statutory 10% dividend withholding tax plus daily late surcharge of 0.05% and special interest. If the false declaration constitutes fraudulent tax evasion, Criminal Law Article 201 applies to the responsible corporate representatives.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or cause the filing of false beneficial ownership declarations are personally exposed to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment up to 7 years for high-amount evasion, fines, and possible confiscation of personal assets. The company veil may be pierced to recover unpaid withholding taxes from the directors who abused the corporate form; travel bans, asset freezes, and banking blacklisting are routinely imposed on statutory directors as enforcement measures, and the Hong Kong affiliate may be delisted from treaty benefit access in China.
