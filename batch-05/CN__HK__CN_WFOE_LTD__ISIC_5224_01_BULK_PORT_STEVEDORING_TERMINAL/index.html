Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital applies to a Hong Kong private company limited by shares; one ordinary share may be issued. However, the Marine Department and the Director of Lands will require the terminal operator to demonstrate financial capability through a blocked escrow arrangement or performance guarantee. The sole member shall maintain a restricted escrow deposit of not less than HKD 30 million (approximately USD 3.85 million) in a licensed bank in Hong Kong, to be applied only upon award of the berth concession and as security for pollution, fire, and operational liabilities.

Funding & unlock path: Fire safety certification from the Fire Services Department for all terminal structures, including approved hydrants, extinguishers, means of escape and emergency lighting; a structural stability clearance from the Buildings Department for the wharf, crane foundations and elevated work platforms; an occupational health and safety registration from the Labour Department confirming appointment of a safety officer, certified crane operators, safe cargo handling procedures, personal protective equipment and first-aid provisions; and dangerous goods approval under the Dangerous Goods Ordinance (Cap. 295) where diesel, coal dust or chemical residues are present.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE, as a PRC-incorporated resident limited liability company, obtains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) as its tax identification number. SOP: (1) incorporate with the Administration for Market Regulation and receive the business license containing the USCC; (2) registration data is automatically transferred to STA under the multi-license integration system, completing tax registration without a separate tax registration visit; the USCC becomes the TIN for enterprise income tax, VAT, and local taxes; (3) the legal representative or authorized financial officer activates the tax profile through real-name authentication on the Electronic Tax Bureau, confirms tax types, fiscal year, filing frequencies, and digital invoice entitlements; (4) separately obtain customs registration through the China International Trade Single Window if the entity exports goods; (5) for any foreign non-resident parent or overseas enterprise deriving PRC-source income, that non-resident enterprise must obtain a Non-resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number by filing with the competent county-level tax authority or through a qualified withholding agent, using a tax-residence certificate, incorporation certificate, relevant contract, and a designated agent appointment; the WFOE must withhold PRC income tax and VAT on non-resident payments such as dividends, interest, royalties, and cross-border service fees and report them under the non-resident's TIN.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that amount the WFOE may be a small-scale taxpayer under simplified VAT and may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer if it meets accounting and control requirements. The stevedoring and bulk cargo loading/unloading service is a logistics auxiliary service, normally taxable at 6% VAT for a general taxpayer, while the PRC headline VAT rate for goods is 13% and other prescribed rates include 9%, 6%, and 0% for specific supplies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT, a 0% rate applies to exported goods that physically leave the PRC customs territory under a valid export customs declaration and are sold to an overseas buyer, and to narrowly listed cross-border services supplied by a PRC taxpayer to an overseas recipient and consumed entirely outside China. The WFOE's port stevedoring and bulk cargo loading/unloading services are performed in China and are therefore not eligible for zero rating; they attract 6% VAT even where the cargo is destined for export. If the WFOE separately exports goods or supplies a qualifying zero-rated cross-border service, it must satisfy all documentation and foreign-exchange repatriation requirements below to substantiate the 0% VAT rate or export refund.

  • PRC Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Declaration Form) with customs release and clearance record from the China International Trade Single Window
  • Carrier-issued Bill of Lading or equivalent sea waybill/multimodal transport document evidencing shipment from a PRC port to a destination outside China
  • Commercial invoice and export packing list issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer
  • Signed export sales contract or purchase order for the exported goods or services
  • Bank-generated Cross-Border Income Declaration and SWIFT payment confirmation evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer, filed through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange digital foreign exchange administration system
  • Export VAT invoice (digital e-invoice) and the applicable STA export tax refund or zero-rating declaration schedule with supporting calculations

China mandates fully digitalized electronic invoicing. VAT invoices (fapiao) must be issued through the STA national electronic invoice service platform; the platform assigns the invoice number, validates the taxpayer's status, and receives invoice data in near-real-time. General VAT taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices for buyers to claim input credit or ordinary digital invoices, as appropriate. There is no pre-issuance physical fiscal stamp; instead, the electronic tax-control system enforces invoice quotas, red-letter offset procedures for credit notes and cancellation, and cross-matching to VAT returns and customs data. Large-volume taxpayers can use STA-approved API and ERP integration tools to issue digital invoices automatically. Anti-fraud controls are embedded in the unified invoice management system, with real-time monitoring of abnormal issuance, related-party patterns, and mismatches between declared sales and VAT.

Export zero-rating and refund claims are subject to intensive post-clearance and tax audit scrutiny by STA and Customs. If documentation is incomplete or foreign exchange is not collected within the prescribed repatriation deadline, the claimed refund will be disallowed and must be repaid with interest; unpaid tax attracts late-payment interest at 0.05% per day. Fraudulent or false export declarations can lead to penalties of 50% to five times the tax involved, revocation of export tax-refund qualification, customs penalties, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the PRC. The statutory assessment period is generally five years and is unlimited in cases of fraudulent or intentional non-compliance.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates under a managed floating exchange rate regime with highly regulated capital account convertibility. Current account transactions are generally convertible for genuine trade and service activities, allowing a WFOE to receive export proceeds and settle them into RMB or retain them in foreign currency accounts subject to SAFE verification. However, capital account movements remain strictly gated. For a bulk port stevedoring WFOE, inbound foreign direct investment from its Hong Kong parent must first be registered under the FDI regulations with SAFE or a designated domestic bank, after which a foreign exchange capital settlement account can be opened and equity capital remitted via SWIFT. Every downstream settlement of capital proceeds into RMB requires submission of a capital account payment notice and supporting documents evidencing real operating expenditure in the approved business scope. The WFOE may not deploy settled capital for prohibited purposes such as securities investment, entrusted loans, or real estate outside its scope. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are lawful only after annual audited financial statements, tax clearance, and a SAFE-approved foreign exchange payment, making the overall convertibility profile conditionally convertible on the capital account while the current account is effectively liberalized for trade.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Mandatory foreign exchange surrender for export proceeds was abolished in 2012. There is no fixed statutory conversion ratio; a Chinese export WFOE may retain up to 100 percent of export proceeds in a SAFE-designated foreign currency account or settle any portion into RMB. All export receipts must be repatriated to China or held in an approved offshore account and reported through the SAFE Trade Monitoring System. The receiving bank must verify each receipt against the corresponding customs declaration, commercial invoice, and logistics documentation within 30 days of receipt. Export receivables with payment terms exceeding 90 days, or advance receipts more than 30 days before customs declaration, require separate SAFE registration and transaction-by-transaction reporting.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are not viable as primary treasury banks for this Chinese WFOE. They do not hold deposit-taking banking licenses in China and are not authorized by PBOC or SAFE to open RMB basic deposit accounts, operate SAFE capital accounts, process FDI capital verification, or clear statutory foreign exchange settlement for port stevedoring operations. These institutions may offer ancillary cross-border payment services only through a licensed domestic payment provider under the PBOC payment services regime, and even then their legal scope is limited to low-value payment facilitation, not commercial treasury management. The lawful onboarding route for a WFOE engaged in regulated port terminal stevedoring requires a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank able to handle SAFE filings, trade verification, RMB payroll, and capital account reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must instruct its bank to execute a SWIFT MT103 credit transfer to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Account at a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank. The remittance instruction must reference the SAFE FDI control information record serial number, the investment contract number, and a purpose code consistent with inbound foreign direct investment equity injection. Upon receipt, the Chinese bank will perform currency conversion into RMB through the capital account payment notice mechanism, and a Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report for filing with SAFE and the local market regulator. All subsequent use of the settled RMB capital must be traced against the approved business scope and cannot be redirected toward prohibited capital-account purposes without new regulatory approval.For settlement of imported equipment, terminal construction services, spare parts, and operational supplies, the WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 payments for direct remittances, commercial letters of credit issued by the Chinese Tier-1 bank in favor of overseas suppliers, and documentary collections when trade terms require presentation of shipping documents. All outbound payments must be matched with customs declarations, invoices, bills of lading, and service contracts under the SAFE trade-in-goods and services monitoring system. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are not viable as primary treasury banks for this Chinese WFOE. Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

China's statutory dividend withholding tax rate is 10% on dividends paid by a resident enterprise to its non-resident enterprise shareholder; under the Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Arrangement for the Avoidance of Double Taxation on Income, the rate is reduced to 5% where the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company that directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the paying company.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory China Local File preparation is triggered when the aggregate annual related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 400 million for tangible-asset transfers, RMB 200 million for intangible-asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial-asset transfers, or RMB 200 million for other related-party transactions; the Master File must be prepared where the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the prior year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and related-party transactions reach the disclosure thresholds; contemporaneous documentation must be prepared in Chinese and submitted within the statutory period under the Special Tax Adjustments measures of EIT Law and SAT Announcement 42/2016.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China does not have a standalone Economic Substance Regulations regime equivalent to Hong Kong or UAE, but substance-over-form applies through the GAAR under EIT Law Article 47, the beneficial ownership doctrine under SAT Announcement 9/2018 and the Core Income-Generating Activities analysis applied in transfer pricing and permanent establishment cases; a mainland WFOE carrying on bulk port stevedoring must have real employees, terminal equipment, operating premises, management and decision-making functions in mainland China, and all profits from cargo handling, terminal services and stevedoring labor must be attributable to CIGAs performed in China; the HK parent claiming DTA benefits must itself maintain real economic substance in Hong Kong and must not be a conduit for mainland-owned capital.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion by False Financial Statements, Fake Invoices and Shadow Payroll

Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 201 punishes false tax declarations and concealment of taxable income; fines can reach 50% to 500% of underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharges under Tax Collection Administration Law Articles 63 and 32; criminal penalties range from up to three years for relatively large evasion to up to seven years for extremely large evasion exceeding 30% of the taxable base.

Director personal liability: Directors, the legal representative, the chief financial officer and the tax manager may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and fines where they authorized or recklessly tolerated the fraud; courts may pierce the corporate veil and impose personal asset restitution; border exit bans and blacklisting under the Dishonest Taxpayer List apply; criminal conviction may result in imprisonment and confiscation of personal assets.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Failure to File Related-Party Disclosures

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 48 allow the tax authority to make special tax adjustments, add overdue interest at the PBOC loan benchmark rate plus 5 percentage points, and impose a 50% to 500% penalty on the underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63; absence of contemporaneous documentation reverses the burden of proof and permits the tax authority to estimate taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false transfer pricing declarations or direct the use of shell HK service companies may be jointly and severally liable as responsible persons for the enterprise tax debt; the civil veil can be pierced where the WFOE is undercapitalized and the related-party structure is fraudulent; personal consequences include disqualification of directors, travel bans, blacklisting, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax evasion if false documentation is knowingly used.
DTA Abuse, Shell Company Conduit and Beneficial Ownership Misrepresentation

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement 9/2018 denies treaty benefits where the HK shareholder is not the beneficial owner; EIT Law Article 47 applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule to recharacterize the transaction and impose the statutory 10% withholding on dividends, interest and royalties; underpayment triggers daily 0.05% surcharges plus a 50% to 500% penalty; the HK entity may be treated as a mainland Chinese tax resident if its place of effective management is in China, subjecting its worldwide income to full CIT.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers who design, execute or conceal the conduit arrangement may be personally liable for the unpaid taxes, penalties and interest through veil-piercing where the corporate structure is a sham or intended to evade compulsory enforcement; individual criminal exposure includes up to seven years in prison for tax fraud and money laundering across the mainland-Hong Kong corridor; asset seizure, exit bans, director disqualification and personal restitution orders may be imposed by Chinese and Hong Kong courts.
