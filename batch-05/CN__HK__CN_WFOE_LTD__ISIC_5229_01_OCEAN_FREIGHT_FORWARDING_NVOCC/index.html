Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese FX regulatory framework draws a sharp distinction between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account items, including cross-border freight forwarding service receipts and operational disbursements, are broadly RMB-convertible once an authorized onshore bank verifies the underlying trade authenticity. The WFOE may freely settle qualified service trade invoices into CNY through its current account. By contrast, the capital account remains heavily managed: every inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong parent must be registered with SAFE through a delegated onshore bank before the funds are credited to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account. Profit remittance is permitted only after the WFOE completes its annual statutory audit, files the required corporate income tax returns, proves dividend distribution through a valid board resolution, and submits the audited financial statements to the bank. The bank then checks the SAFE FDI registration, tax clearance, and foreign exchange transaction purpose before allowing the dividend to exit. Where the WFOE uses the Chinese Renminbi for cross-border settlement under the RMB cross-border trade scheme, the bank records the payment in the RMB cross-border payment information management system, and the funds remain subject to the same underlying transaction authenticity verification and anti-money-laundering screening as foreign currency flows. There is no free offshore conversion pool for the WFOE's onshore equity proceeds, and surplus capital must be deployed within the permitted capital account scope rather than arbitraged through uncontrolled currency markets.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory percentage surrender of export proceeds. The WFOE must repatriate all export and service-trade foreign currency receipts to a domestic foreign exchange current account promptly after collection, generally within the credit term granted to the overseas counterparty and subject to the bank's trade authenticity audit under the SAFE cross-border receipt and payment verification framework. For NVOCC ocean freight and logistics service exports, open-account receivables should typically be collected within 90 days from the invoice or service completion date unless a longer commercial term is documented; overdue receivables require an explanation and supporting contracts. Once credited, the foreign currency may be retained, converted into CNY at the prevailing onshore rate, or used directly for lawful foreign currency disbursements with no fixed minimum conversion ratio, provided the bank records the transaction under the proper BOP purpose code.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Strike-off trigger

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every PRC company, including a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE), is unconditionally required by the PRC Company Law and the PRC Accounting Law to have its annual financial statements independently audited by a certified public accountant licensed in the PRC; there is no legal exemption merely because the enterprise is small or loss-making. For audit scoping, materiality, and classification of the statutory audit dossier, the customary quantitative triggers are: year-end total assets exceeding RMB 50 million, annual operating revenue exceeding RMB 30 million, or average headcount exceeding 100. A WFOE crossing any of these indicia is presumptively subject to a full-scope audit with extended procedures; a WFOE below all three is still required to obtain a statutory audit report and may apply simplified small-enterprise audit materiality under the applicable PRC standards.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local file threshold under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42: tangible asset transfers with related parties RMB 200 million or more; intangible asset transfers RMB 100 million or more; financial asset transfers RMB 100 million or more; other intercompany services (including NVOCC logistics services) RMB 40 million or more. Master file required where aggregate related-party revenue is RMB 1 billion or more. A WFOE with Hong Kong parent intercompany service fees above RMB 40 million must prepare Local File and related-party transaction reporting schedules (A109000/A109010).

Economic substance scope: China does not implement an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime. However, to secure Hong Kong DTA benefits, the Hong Kong owner must meet the OECD minimum standard of economic substance and the China GAAR under CIT Law Art. 47-48 and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9, including having its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) (route-setting, container procurement, contract negotiation, risk-bearing) in Hong Kong. If CIGA are located in mainland China, the Hong Kong entity will be regarded as having a permanent establishment in PRC and the WFOE's payments will be recharacterized as taxable trading gross income.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

CIT Annual Return Underreporting / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 63: tax authorities shall pursue the tax and overdue interest and impose an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the amount of tax evaded. Criminal Law Art. 201: where tax evasion exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the taxable amount, punishable with imprisonment up to 7 years, criminal fines and unpaid tax restitution.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly instructed, participated in or concealed the underreporting are jointly and severally liable for the unpaid CIT, interest, penalties and criminal fines. The SAT may freeze personal bank accounts and shares under TCAL Art. 40, impose a travel ban (exit-restriction order, 'blacklisting') under the Exit Administration Law, and the court may impose personal imprisonment up to 7 years for criminal tax evasion.
Withholding Tax on Outbound Dividend / Interest / Royalties

Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 69: the WFOE, as withholding agent, is liable for the uncollected tax and is subject to a penalty of 50%-300% of the WHT not deducted. Interest accrues at 0.05% per day. Where the SAT recharacterizes the remittance as a constructive dividend, CIT deficiency is assessed at 25%, with no deduction for the remitted base.

Director personal liability: Under TCAL Art. 65 and Judicial Interpretation, directors who signed the remittance, or caused the omission, are personally liable for the withheld tax, statutory surcharge and penalty to the extent of the corporate deficiency. Repeat offenders face blacklisting and trading restrictions; diversion of funds through personal accounts triggers Criminal Law Art. 191 anti-money-laundering liability and up to 10 years' imprisonment.
Transfer-Pricing Documentation Omission / Shell/TP Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Art. 41: SAT has authority to make a transfer-pricing adjustment. TCAL Art. 44: where no contemporaneous documentation is provided, the tax authority may assess taxable income by deemed profit (often 15% of gross ocean freight turnover). SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes a RMB 10,000 fine for failure to submit required TP reports, and adjustment interest is usually charged from the due date with a benchmark rate plus 5%. The transaction is recharacterized and the tax is recaptured with a penalty of 50%-500%.

Director personal liability: If the director signs a false intercompany agreement or a false transfer-pricing certificate, the legal-person 'shell' doctrine applies: courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Art. 20/21 to hold directors personally liable for the unjust enrichment. The SAT can block exit, and under Criminal Law Art. 203 the responsible officer can be imprisoned for up to 7 years for failing to pay assessed tax following a TP adjustment, with travel bans and property preservation.
