Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China, operating through the electronic tax portal (e-tax.chinatax.gov.cn) and local provincial/municipal tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

For a PRC Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE): (1) Complete incorporation with the local Administration for Market Regulation, obtaining the business license carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) for income tax, VAT, and customs purposes. (2) Within 30 days of business license issuance, file tax registration and activate taxpayer records at the competent State Taxation Administration bureau using the electronic tax portal. (3) Complete VAT taxpayer status confirmation, electing small-scale or general taxpayer status, and register for the VAT invoice system. (4) Where a non-resident enterprise has no PRC establishment but derives PRC-source income, it does not receive a separate TIN; the withholding agent or the WFOE must withhold corporate income tax and VAT using the non-resident enterprise's legal identity and contract details, and may apply for a non-resident enterprise tax registration number for refund or treaty purposes. (5) Activate the Golden Tax System modules and obtain digital certificates (UKey) for electronic invoice issuance.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

No check-the-box or hybrid mismatch elections exist under PRC tax law. The only statutorily permitted election relevant to VAT is the choice between small-scale taxpayer status and general taxpayer status, subject to the RMB 5 million annual sales threshold; enterprises may also elect to apply VAT exemption or zero-rating for certain cross-border services under the applicable STA framework.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales (mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration for entities exceeding this threshold; voluntary registration permitted below it).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A registered VAT general taxpayer that exports goods or supplies cross-border services qualifying under the State Taxation Administration's zero-rating rules may apply a 0% output VAT rate. For goods: legal export through a PRC customs port, completion of customs export declaration, sale to an overseas buyer, receipt of foreign exchange, and actual departure of the goods from China are required. For services (including international air cargo transportation, freight forwarding support services, or qualifying offshore services to non-resident enterprises): the service must be supplied to an overseas customer and wholly consumed outside China, the provider must be a general taxpayer, supporting contracts and service-scope documentation must evidence beneficial receipt by the overseas party, and consideration must be received from abroad in foreign currency or cross-border RMB. The taxpayer must file the export declaration using the export tax rebate (exemption) system and present substantiation within the statutory period.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Customs Export Goods Declaration Form)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill (master and house, as applicable)
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract
  • Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt and repatriation certificate (Inward Remittance Certificate or equivalent bank receipt evidencing receipt of foreign currency/cross-border RMB)
  • Proof of export tax rebate filing (electronic acknowledgement from the export tax rebate system)

All VAT invoices (including full digital e-invoices) must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's consolidated e-invoice service platform / Golden Tax System (Phase IV). Invoice data is transmitted to the tax authority in near-real time using national standards; API integration is facilitated by authorised service providers, while small-scale taxpayers may use the taxpayer's e-invoice UKey. Each invoice carries a government-generated anti-counterfeit invoice code and the tax authority encrypts the invoice content. Buyers must authenticate invoices through the national VAT invoice verification platform to claim input VAT credits; no separate fiscal clearance stamp is used, but issuance is electronically monitored and anomalies trigger automatic tax controls.

If the export zero-rating claim fails audit, including missing customs documentation, falsified export invoices, unreconciled foreign exchange, or failure to meet the 180-day requirement, the tax authority will claw back the refunded input VAT and impose VAT at the standard rate plus daily late payment surcharge (0.05% per day). Under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection, fines from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax may be levied for fraudulent claims. Customs may assess penalties for false export declarations. Criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebates arises under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, carrying imprisonment and additional monetary penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi (RMB) is not freely convertible under the capital account; all cross-border capital flows are conditional on SAFE registration, approval, or filing. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, are nominally convertibile under the principle of genuine transaction review, meaning the underlying contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and payment evidence must support each remittance. For a China WFOE engaged in international air freight forwarding and cargo charter brokerage, recurring current account payments for charter fees, loading fees, landing charges, and brokerage commissions must be submitted with transaction receipts; any mismatch between currency, counterparty, or declared purpose triggers refusal. On the capital account side, paid-in registered capital requires FDI registration with SAFE and the opening of a dedicated capital foreign exchange settlement account; funds cannot be used for RMB equity investments or entrusted loans without additional approval. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent requires audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, an annual foreign exchange payment review, and evidence that the WFOE has settled all Chinese tax liabilities, including withholding tax on dividends. The central bank also applies a macroprudential cross-border financing limit under a cap on external debt, which must be monitored. In practice, full convertibility is denied; every inbound and outbound remittance is independently adjudicated against the submitted dossier, making strict documentary discipline a condition of treasury survival.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished mandatory foreign exchange surrender for current account receipts; there is no fixed conversion ratio and the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or convert them to RMB at market basis. However, the repatriation obligation is strict: export proceeds must be received and brought back into China within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless the enterprise has registered a deferred payment with SAFE for up to 180 days. Failure to repatriate within the statutory timeline constitutes an illegal retention of foreign exchange offshore and exposes the WFOE to penalties and administrative blacklisting.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low direct viability. Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Airwallex, Stripe Treasury, and Revolut Business, cannot act as the primary account-holding bank for a Chinese WFOE because PBOC regulations require all corporate foreign currency and RMB accounts to reside with license-holding commercial banks supervised by the national banking regulator. Fintech EMIs may partner with licensed Chinese banks to provide cross-border receipt or payment aggregation services, but they cannot onboard the WFOE as a customer holding a local account in its own name. Therefore, the treasury structure must place a Tier-1 domestic commercial bank as the account owner, while EMIs are limited to narrow auxiliary roles such as downstream vendor payout consolidation outside China, still subject to the bank's compliance approval.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Every Hong Kong incorporated company must be audited unless it qualifies for the small private company audit exemption. A private company qualifies for exemption if it satisfies any two of the following quantitative thresholds in the relevant financial year: (a) annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately US$1,282,051); (b) total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approximately US$1,282,051); (c) average number of employees not exceeding 100. A member of a group must use the consolidated/aggregate equivalents on the same HK$10,000,000 and 100-employee thresholds. Any company that exceeds at least two of those thresholds, or that is public, listed, a licensed bank, insurer, licensed company service provider, or otherwise disallowed from the exemption, is legally required to undergo an independent audit by a practising Certified Public Accountant appointed under Cap. 622.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise if no treaty relief applies, under the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law (EIT Law) Article 37 and Social Welfare / Tax Collection Implementing Regulations.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual CIT return must be filed by May 31 of the following calendar year. For calendar-year taxpayers using 31 December year-end, the final filing deadline is 31 May. Quarterly provisional prepayments are due by the 15th day of the first month following each quarter-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds are triggered when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (such as service fees and brokerage commissions). A Master File must also be prepared where the WFOE has cross-border related-party transactions and its worldwide group had consolidated revenue of at least RMB 2 billion in the prior fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine and a General Anti-Abuse Rule; the WFOE must conduct actual decision-making, employment, office presence, and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in mainland China, including charter negotiation, booking management, logistics coordination, invoicing, and risk management. The Hong Kong intermediate shareholder must itself satisfy Hong Kong's substantive business requirements and beneficial ownership standards; if the HK entity is a bare shell, treaty relief will be denied under the beneficial ownership doctrine and the anti-abuse provisions of the PRC-Hong Kong Tax Arrangement.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through False Related-Party Fee Arrangements

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 8 and Article 41, such expenditures are non-deductible; under the Tax Administration Law Article 63, the underpaid tax leads to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax unpaid, daily late payment interest of 0.05%, and criminal referral in serious cases.

Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate fraudulent fee arrangements may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20 and be jointly and severally liable for the tax deficiency. They may be added to the tax authority's serious-tax-honesty blacklist, subjected to travel restrictions and exit bans, and, where tax evasion exceeds prescribed thresholds, prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment ranging from 3 to 7 years plus substantial fines.
Thin Capitalization and Hidden Dividend Distribution

Statutory sanction: China's thin capitalization rule under EIT Law Article 46 and State Council's implementing regulations disallows the interest deduction for the excessive portion of related-party debt. If the recharacterization applies, the disallowed interest is deemed a dividend distribution subject to statutory 10% withholding tax (or treaty-rate if qualified), with additional 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge and a 50% to 500% penalty for fraudulent manipulation.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or signing such loan transactions may be held personally liable as withholding agents for the unpaid dividend WHT; their personal assets may be reached in civil restitution proceedings, they may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving China, and criminal liability can arise under Criminal Law Article 201 for intentional non-withholding and tax evasion, carrying imprisonment up to 7 years and property fines.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Hong Kong Beneficial Ownership

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will invoke the General Anti-Abuse Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and deny treaty benefits, re-imposing the statutory 10% withholding tax. Additional 0.05% daily late-payment interest applies to the withheld tax deficiency; under SAT Public Notice [2018] No. 9, a special tax adjustment may be made retroactively for up to 10 years, with the full potential penalty being 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax if the abuse is fraudulent.

Director personal liability: Directors of both the WFOE and the HK parent who execute or file the tax residency certificates, beneficial ownership declarations, or dividend distribution resolutions in the abusive structure may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid withholding tax. The civil 'veil piercing' theory allows tax authorities to attach directors' assets in restitution; travel bans and blacklisting are automatic at high underpayment levels; and if the treaty-abuse scheme crosses over into criminal tax evasion, directors face imprisonment up to 7 years, personal fines, and corporate integrity disqualification.
