Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, together with the provincial/municipal Electronic Tax Bureau e-filing platform maintained within the official chinatax.gov.cn domain; for any Hong Kong SAR parent or branch presence, the statutory counterpart is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of the Hong Kong SAR under the Business Registration Ordinance and the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

        
E-government filing portal
The China WFOE must first register with the local Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) and obtain the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory tax identification number for enterprises in Mainland China. Within 30 days of obtaining the business licence, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau platform, submitting the business licence, articles of association, registered-address evidence, ultimate beneficial ownership and investor identity materials, bank account details, and a description of the customs brokerage and bonded transit business scope. The tax registration yields the taxpayer classification, VAT filing profile, and mandatory access to the STA e-invoicing and tax control systems. The WFOE must also separately register with the General Administration of Customs (GACC) as a licensed customs broker; that customs registration is not a substitute for tax registration. If annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed CNY 5,000,000, the WFOE must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold it may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. A foreign non-resident entity, including a Hong Kong shareholder, with no permanent establishment in Mainland China is not required to obtain a PRC USCC solely for holding shares; however, if it derives China-source income subject to withholding, the PRC payer must file withholding registration and the non-resident will be assigned a non-resident tax registration number in connection with treaty relief, withholding settlement, or non-resident contract registration. If the Hong Kong entity establishes a permanent establishment in Mainland China, it must undergo full foreign-enterprise tax registration and obtain the corresponding tax identification code from the STA.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid mismatch election. The WFOE is automatically treated as a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Corporate Income Tax Law. The only material election is the VAT status between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer, subject to accounting capability and the CNY 5,000,000 annual turnover threshold. Hong Kong’s territorial profits tax operates separately for the Hong Kong parent, but no Hong Kong election can change the PRC WFOE’s entity-level taxation classification.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
CNY 5,000,000 aggregate annual taxable turnover is the mandatory legal threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration in Mainland China; every entity making taxable supplies must complete VAT registration regardless of turnover, and small-scale taxpayers whose aggregate sales do not exceed CNY 5,000,000 may apply the simplified collection rate of 1% or 3% as applicable.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exports from Mainland China qualify for zero-rated VAT and the export tax rebate (refund of prior-stage input VAT) where the WFOE is the recorded exporter on the electronic customs export declaration, the goods physically depart China, the export sales proceeds are repatriated through official banking channels, and the export tax rebate declaration is filed through the STA Export Tax Rebate platform. For qualifying offshore digital or cross-border services, zero-rating is allowed only for services listed in the Cross-Border Taxable Services Zero-Rated and Exemption Catalogue where the recipient is an overseas entity, the service is performed and fully consumed outside Mainland China, no benefit is derived in China by a PRC person, and payment evidences receipt from outside China. Customs brokerage and bonded transit management services physically performed inside Mainland China are generally standard-rated VAT services at 6%; zero-rating is construed narrowly and may apply only where the WFOE proves the service is contractually supplied to and wholly consumed by an offshore service recipient, with no local warehouse, customs, or import/export clearance benefit arising to a PRC-domiciled party.

All VAT invoices, including Special VAT Invoices and Ordinary VAT Invoices, must be issued through the STA-controlled VAT Invoice Management System (Golden Tax System) and the fully digitalised electronic invoice platform. Qualified taxpayers use all-electronic invoices requiring API-based real-time transmission, logic validation, encrypted fiscal stamping, and automatic upload to STA servers before issuance or delivery. Paper-invoice users must operate STA-approved tax-control devices with periodic clearance and electronic reconciliation. For customs brokerage services, the VAT invoice must accurately classify the service under the taxable services category and apply the 6% service rate, with any cross-border fee component supported by contracts and customs or transport documentation to avoid recharacterisation as domestic taxable services.

      
The STA automatically cross-matches zero-rated declarations and export rebate claims with customs clearance records, transport waybills, e-invoice platform data, and SAFE foreign-exchange receipt data. If the exporter cannot evidence actual export, genuine offshore service consumption, or receipt of proceeds within the statutory 180-day repatriation window, the zero-rating or export rebate is retroactively clawed back, input VAT credits are denied, standard-rate VAT is reassessed on the original invoice value, and late-payment surcharge accrues from the original filing date. Under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection, penalties range from 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax for inaccurate or fraudulent declarations, and deliberate fraudulent export rebate activity constitutes criminal tax fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, which also attracts blacklisting and loss of future export-tax-rebate eligibility. Any Hong Kong parent structure must satisfy commercial substance and beneficial ownership documentation requirements to prevent recharacterisation as circular invoicing, treaty shopping, or offshore tax arbitrage.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China's RMB is not freely convertible on the capital account, and a PRC WFOE must navigate both current account and capital account FX controls. Current account items are broadly convertible if the bank authenticates a genuine trade or service background; the WFOE can collect and settle export-related receipts and pay for freight, customs duties, broker commissions and software through licensed banks using the appropriate SAFE transaction purpose code. Capital account items, including direct investment inflow, shareholder loans and dividend remittance, remain restricted. Before the Hong Kong parent injects equity, the WFOE must complete foreign direct investment (FDI) information registration with SAFE or a SAFE-designated bank; a foreign exchange capital account is then opened in a licensed onshore bank. Conversion of foreign currency capital into RMB is permitted on a payment-purpose basis and any material change in the intended use requires an amendment to the registration or fresh documentary verification. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permitted only after the annual statutory audit and Chinese corporate income tax final settlement; the bank must review the audited financial statements, statutory reserve allocation, shareholders' profit distribution resolution, tax clearance and evidence that registered capital was fully contributed. Export proceeds are not subject to mandatory conversion under the managed-float regime, but repatriation and verification rules remain strict. FX controls therefore create timing delays and documentary friction, so the treasury must pre-fund customs duties and tax obligations while avoiding any unverified capital account conversion.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE total trade receipt and payment monitoring, export proceeds must be repatriated into a licensed domestic bank account within 180 days from the export declaration date unless a specific SAFE offshore retention approval is held; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in the designated settlement account or convert it into RMB based on operational requirements. No statutory mandatory conversion percentage is imposed on ordinary merchandise export receipts in the current regime, but every receipt must be matched against the customs declaration within the bank's trade verification system, and expected proceeds retained abroad for more than 90 days must be reported to the bank for inclusion in SAFE's monitoring framework, failing which banks may classify the item as an exception and suspend future trade payments.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Feasibility rating: Low. An offshore digital EMI is legally barred from being the onshore WFOE's principal banking rail. Such entities are not licensed by PBOC as Chinese banking institutions, cannot open a CNY current account for the WFOE, cannot hold SAFE-monitored foreign exchange capital, and cannot provide the statutory payment verification needed for customs duties, tax, or cross-border profit remittance. Using a non-bank EMI for CN treasury would expose the company to compliance freeze and possible foreign exchange violation. The only permissible supplementary use is for the Hong Kong parent's own offshore multi-currency collections outside mainland, and even then the WFOE's SAFE-registered receipts and capital account transactions must pass through a licensed Chinese bank.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Hong Kong parent is newly incorporated and cannot prove the source of funds behind the equity contribution into the China WFOE.
        
Root cause: Hong Kong parent is newly incorporated and cannot prove the source of funds behind the equity contribution into the China WFOE.

        
Defense evidence: Original Certificate of Incorporation and Business Registration Certificate of the HK parent, 24 months of corporate bank statements, audited financial accounts demonstrating net worth, notarised UBO register with passport copies, board resolutions approving the investment, a source-of-wealth declaration signed by the ultimate beneficial owner, and a professional reference letter from the parent's Hong Kong bank confirming transaction history and character.

      

      
      

        Customs brokerage business creates rapid small-value payments from multiple unrelated importers and third-country freight counterparties, suggesting possible trade-based money laundering.
        
Root cause: Customs brokerage business creates rapid small-value payments from multiple unrelated importers and third-country freight counterparties, suggesting possible trade-based money laundering.

        
Defense evidence: Customs broker license and customs registration certificate, client onboarding files with executed brokerage service agreements, underlying customs declaration records, bills of lading, air waybills, commercial invoices, insurance certificates, the company's transaction monitoring policy, and a sample reconciliation ledger matching Automated Broker Interface collections to duty payments for the past 12 months.

      

      
      

        The legal representative is non-resident and the ultimate beneficial owner sits inside a Hong Kong trust or discretionary structure, complicating individual identification.
        
Root cause: The legal representative is non-resident and the ultimate beneficial owner sits inside a Hong Kong trust or discretionary structure, complicating individual identification.

        
Defense evidence: Government-issued passport and residential address proof, mainland work permit or residence permit if any, personal tax clearance from the country of tax residence, trust deed or settlement instrument with an independent legal opinion confirming beneficial ownership, PEP status self-certification, professional references from a licensed bank previously serving the individual, and an undertaking to comply with annual onshore tax filing and SAFE information duties.

      

      
      

        Inbound foreign exchange capital will be converted quickly into RMB and paid to third-party bonded logistics providers at inception, raising concern over synchronisation with the declared customs brokerage scope.
        
Root cause: Inbound foreign exchange capital will be converted quickly into RMB and paid to third-party bonded logistics providers at inception, raising concern over synchronisation with the declared customs brokerage scope.

        
Defense evidence: Detailed capital usage and cash-flow budget, executed bonded warehouse and transit management agreements, invoices from each third-party logistics provider, tax registration and duty-payment vouchers, Chinese tax invoices for planned expenses, payroll register in the bonded team, an independent audit engagement letter confirming that the ultimate third-party payees are contractually related to the declared customs brokerage operations, and a signed legal-owner declaration that no fund will be released without a corresponding commercial document.

      

      
    

    

      
Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The WFOE must complete FDI information registration in the SAFE/AMATS system before receiving any capital. The Hong Kong parent then wires the equity through its licensed Hong Kong bank via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's Capital Escrow Account at the selected mainland tier-1 bank using the SAFE-approved transaction purpose code for inbound direct investment and referencing the capital contribution under the sole shareholder capital schedule. The receiving bank verifies the sender, the purpose code, the SAFE registration and the beneficiary name, then credits the account under capital-monitoring restrictions. Conversion of the equity injection into RMB is executed under the settlement-by-payment system only upon submission of tax invoices or commercial contracts that evidence the intended use; a licensed certified public accountant may issue a capital verification report after the funds are received. Any proposed change of use must be re-filed or supported by the bank's compliance judgement before settlement.Domestic customs charges, VAT and corporate income tax are paid through the tax treasury information processing system (TIPS) using the broker's Automated Broker Interface ACH sub-account in conjunction with the customs declarant licence. Cross-border trade disbursements are routed as follows: SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for freight, brokerage fees, overseas software and service payments under underlying contracts and the corresponding SAFE current account purpose code; Commercial Letters of Credit issued over SWIFT MT700/MT707 for high-value bonded goods imports where the WFOE acts as import agent; and Documentary Collections for lower-risk parcel transactions in which a full LC is not economic. RMB-denominated cross-border payments may be cleared through CIPS, while USD clearing uses SWIFT/CHIPS; each payment is matched to the corresponding customs declaration or service agreement and must survive the annual SAFE trade verification.Onshore deliverable forward foreign exchange contracts for CNY/USD, CNY/HKD and CNY/EUR, supported by genuine trade receipts or payables and executed within the WFOE's bank-approved margin and credit line.Onshore foreign exchange swaps and date-swap overlays used to smooth near-term CNY and HKD cash-flow peaks, provided the underlying exposure arises from actual customs brokerage fees or trade invoices.Exchange rate options and range forwards available from tier-1 Chinese banks for current account and approved direct investment exposures.Offshore CNH non-deliverable forwards are permitted only at the Hong Kong parent level for protecting the group treasury value of future dividends; the mainland WFOE may not use an NDF as an onshore hedge because SAFE accepts only deliverable and real-demand derivatives for the licensed entity.

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: All Hong Kong companies, regardless of size, must prepare financial statements that give a true and fair view; every limited company (except small entities exempted under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 with qualifying conditions) is required to have its financial statements audited by a certified public accountant (CPA) in Hong Kong. The exemption applies only to small private companies or groups meeting at least two of the following three thresholds in a financial year: annual revenue not exceeding HK$25 million, total assets not exceeding HK$25 million, and average number of employees not exceeding 50; however, the exemption is automatically lost if the company is a member of a corporate group that is not eligible, or if the shareholders holding 5% or more of the voting rights request an audit, or if the company is required to be audited under other law. For a licensed customs brokerage house (ISIC 5229) acting under the Hong Kong Customs and Excise Department license regime, the licensing authority typically requires a full statutory audit certificate as a fit-and-proper condition regardless of small company thresholds. Therefore the mandatory independent audit applies in practice for this WFOE entity.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)
25%

      
Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
10% on gross dividends distributed by the WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement where the Hong Kong shareholder is the beneficial owner and has directly held at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE throughout the 12 months preceding the dividend distribution and the shares are not effectively connected with a permanent establishment in Mainland China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation (Local File) is mandatory in RMB terms where related-party transactions in the preceding fiscal year reach the following thresholds: RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions, RMB 100 million for intangible asset or financial asset transfers, and RMB 200 million for tangible asset purchases or sales. A Master File may also be required for the China-resident entity when the multinational group meets the STA's group-level threshold, currently RMB 10 billion of related-party revenue or a comparable consolidated group revenue size, as specified in STA Public Notice 2016 No. 42.

      
Economic substance scope: The WFOE is subject to Chinese EIT on its worldwide income, and economic substance is decisive for claiming treaty benefits and for rejecting any recharacterization by the tax authorities. Under the PRC General Anti-Abuse Rule (GAAR) in EIT Law Article 47 and the beneficial ownership doctrine, the Hong Kong parent must demonstrate real functions, assets, and risks in Hong Kong; a pure mailbox company will be denied treaty relief. The Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the customs brokerage business, such as licensed broker decision-making, customs declaration preparation, risk assessment, and client contract negotiation, must actually be performed in Mainland China; failure to do so may shift profits or expose the structure to treaty abuse adjustment.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Corporate Income Tax Evasion / False Filing
        
Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law: recovery of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law permits fines and imprisonment of up to three years for tax evasion above RMB 100,000 and more than 10% of the tax payable, or three to seven years for very large evaded amounts.

        
Director personal liability: Under Article 23 of the PRC Company Law (2023 revision), courts may pierce the corporate veil and hold directors personally jointly liable for the WFOE's tax debts when the abuse of limited liability directly caused underpayment. The legal representative and actual controller may be placed on tax-related blacklists, subject to travel bans and exit restrictions, and face civil asset restitution; if criminal tax evasion is established, the responsible director can be sentenced to imprisonment and fined personally.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing / Thin Capitalization Non-Compliance
        
Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 46 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law empower the tax authority to adjust taxable income at arm's length and disallow excessive interest. Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law imposes penalties for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation. The tax authority may assess interest at the benchmark loan rate plus five percentage points on the adjusted tax and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the additional tax.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false or missing transfer pricing documentation may be held jointly liable under the corporate veil piercing provision for the resulting tax deficiency. Their personal travel may be restricted through exit-ban enforcement, their assets may be subjected to civil restitution, and if the transfer pricing adjustment forms part of a broader tax evasion scheme, criminal liability under Article 201 of the Criminal Law may extend to the legal representative and actual controller.

      

      
      

        Treaty Abuse / Shell Company / AML
        
Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law (General Anti-Abuse Rule) permits the tax authority to recharacterize transactions and deny treaty benefits, resulting in additional tax, interest, and penalties. The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines of up to RMB 5 million for failing to report large or suspicious cross-border transactions, and the customs brokerage license may be revoked under the Customs Law for dishonest or unlicensed clearance activity.

        
Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent may face joint and several civil liability for the diverted assets and unpaid tax through corporate veil piercing; they may be blacklisted and banned from departing China or Hong Kong; criminal liability can include imprisonment under Articles 191 and 201 of the PRC Criminal Law for money laundering and tax evasion where directors knew of or authorized the shell structure and concealed income streams.
