Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement. The company may be incorporated with a nominal share capital of HK$1.00 (or any amount) and shares may be issued at par or premium. No escrow or blocked account is required.

Funding & unlock path: The company must obtain a Fire Safety Certificate from the Fire Services Department for any premises used for storage or office. The certificate confirms compliance with the Fire Services Ordinance, including adequate fire escapes, extinguishers, and alarm systems. Additionally, the company must comply with the Occupational Safety and Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) by conducting risk assessments, providing safety training, and maintaining a safe working environment. For heavy haul operations, the company must ensure that all vehicles are roadworthy and that drivers hold appropriate licenses. The Labour Department may conduct inspections to ensure compliance.

Paid-in capital rule: None. Hong Kong law imposes no minimum paid-up capital requirement. The company may be incorporated with a nominal share capital of HK$1.00 (or any amount) and shares may be issued at par or premium. No escrow or blocked account is required.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) accessible via the STA national electronic tax service network.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Step 2: The USCC serves as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Step 3: Within 30 calendar days after issuance of the Business License, complete tax registration with the local STA office through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal, uploading the Business License, articles of association, legal representative identity proof, registered address lease agreements, bank account opening certificate, and notarized certificate of incorporation of the foreign investor. Step 4: Upon submission, the STA system verifies the taxpayer qualification, assigns tax categories and filing frequencies, and activates the USCC as the permanent tax ID without issuing a separate tax registration certificate. Step 5: Register for the Export Enterprise status with the customs authority and apply for VAT General Taxpayer status to enable export zero-rating and refund claims.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not have a check-the-box regime; a WFOE is automatically a PRC resident enterprise subject to Enterprise Income Tax on worldwide income. Hybrid mismatch and restructuring elections require prior STA approval under special tax treatment provisions and are subject to anti-avoidance review.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales for mandatory general taxpayer registration; VAT registration is required upon establishment regardless of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods (including out-of-gauge heavy industrial project cargo) are eligible for the 0% VAT export rate under the Measures for the Administration of Export Refund (Exemption) and the VAT Law of the PRC. The exporter must be a registered VAT General Taxpayer, the goods must be physically exported and cleared through customs, and foreign exchange proceeds must be collected and verified. Qualifying cross-border services supplied to overseas customers may also be zero-rated or exempt under Ministry of Finance and STA Announcement No. 36 of 2016.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (Bill of Export) evidencing customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing outbound international movement of the heavy-haul cargo
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued Export Proceeds Collection Confirmation evidencing repatriation of foreign exchange

All newly registered enterprises must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices (FDEI) through the STA national E-Invoice Service Platform. Each FDEI is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax System Phase IV, carries a unique invoice code and embedded tax-control anti-fraud stamp, and is cleared by the tax authority instantly. Paper special VAT invoices are only permitted for taxpayers not yet enrolled in the FDEI program; digital publication is the default for the project cargo entity.

The STA is empowered to retroactively disallow zero-rating and claw back any refunded VAT plus interest where supporting customs and FX documentation is false or incomplete. Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, misdeclaration attracts a fine of 50% to five times the tax amount involved; fraudulent export tax refund claims trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, with penalties of up to ten years' imprisonment, or life imprisonment for particularly heavy cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a hybrid convertibility regime: current account items such as genuine trade in goods, services, and investment income remittance are generally convertible through licensed foreign exchange banks, while capital account items remain heavily controlled and require specific SAFE registration, approval, or quota allocation. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may remit dividends or profits to its Hong Kong parent only after (i) the annual financial statements have been audited by a certified public accountant registered in China, (ii) all corporate income tax and applicable withholding tax have been settled, (iii) prior accumulated losses have been fully recovered, and (iv) the board has passed a lawful dividend resolution. The remitting bank must receive the SAFE-designated income remittance application, audited accounts, tax payment proofs, and board resolutions. Thus, current account convertibility is operationally accessible for genuine trade and profit distribution, but capital account convertibility is restricted and every inflow, conversion, and outflow must satisfy authenticity verification and be traceable to a lawful underlying transaction. Capital injected as registered capital is converted into RMB only for permitted operating expenses, and cannot be used for prohibited investments, securities markets, or the repayment of unlawful domestic RMB borrowings.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Administration Measures, export proceeds earned by a WFOE must be repatriated into mainland China through a licensed bank, and the export-related foreign currency payment should generally be received within 90 days after the customs export declaration date unless a longer period has been pre-registered with the bank or SAFE. There is no mandatory fixed-percentage conversion ratio; an exporter may either retain the foreign currency in an authorised current-account foreign exchange settlement account or convert it into RMB on a discretionary basis for genuine operational needs. All export receipt transactions must be reported in the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System and supported by the export customs declaration, commercial contract, commercial invoice, and transport documents. Foreign currency proceeds left offshore or retained without repatriation constitute a compliance breach and can trigger SAFE investigation, penalties, and cancellation of the WFOE's streamlined trade facilitation status.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low and legally unviable as a primary treasury rail. Mainland China does not recognise offshore digital banks, electronic money institutions, or challenger EMI platforms as authorised deposit-taking institutions for a WFOE's onshore RMB or foreign currency clearing needs. Foreign EMIs cannot open domestic bank accounts, clear RMB payments through CIPS bearing bank settlement member codes, or perform SAFE-funded capital account functions without a licensed mainland banking partner. At best, an EMI may serve as an ancillary funding rail for small pre-funded foreign currency card operations, but the corporate treasury of a China WFOE must be concentrated in tier-1 domestic commercial banks to satisfy PBOC, SAFE, and KYC account-opening requirements. Any proposal that relies on an offshore EMI for principal liquidity is operationally deficient and will fail AML underwriting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must initiate an inbound SWIFT MT103 from its own corporate bank account in Hong Kong to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account held with a tier-1 mainland bank. The remittance narrative must reference the SAFE FDI registration number, the WFOE's full legal name, the subscribed registered capital contract, and the specific SAFE-designated purpose code for paid-in investment of registered capital. Upon receipt, the bank will perform authenticity verification against SAFE's FDI system, credit the foreign currency capital account, and, upon receipt of genuine supporting documents, allow conversion into RMB for permitted current expenditures. A Chinese certified public accountant must subsequently issue a statutory capital verification report for filing with the Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE. The WFOE must not use the injected capital for prohibited purposes outside its business scope, securities investment, wealth management products, or repayment of unrelated domestic RMB debts. Any future capital reduction, equity transfer, or dividend repatriation must follow a separate SAFE account and approval protocol.Outbound operating disbursements for project cargo logistics should be cleared via SWIFT MT103 from the WFOE's RMB basic account or foreign exchange settlement account to subcontractors, port authorities, freight forwarders, vessel operators, heavy-haul trucking companies, and insurers. For high-value equipment procurement, marine charter hire, and milestone-driven international logistics contracts, documentary instruments such as commercial letters of credit and documentary collections should be used to align payment with presentation of shipping documents and proof of performance. All outbound trade-in-services payments require underlying contracts, commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, tax-related evidence, and SAFE trade reporting. Where payment is made in RMB, the transaction may clear through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) alongside the SWIFT messaging rails, enabling faster settlement among Chinese banks and their cross-border counterparts. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) under Article 4 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law applied to the WFOE as a Chinese-tax-resident limited liability company.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory WHT on dividends distributed to a non-resident enterprise that has no permanent establishment in China (EIT Law Art. 37; SAT Circular 37/2009). A Hong Kong resident company holding directly at least 25% of the WFOE's registered capital may qualify for the reduced 5% rate under Art. 10 of the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement. If the beneficial ownership test fails, the 10% statutory rate is restored.
