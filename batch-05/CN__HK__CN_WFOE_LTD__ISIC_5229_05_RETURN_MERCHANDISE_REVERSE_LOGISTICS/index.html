Stage 03

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the complete e-Tax Bureau online electronic service portal for taxpayer registration, filing, invoicing, and export refund clearance (for Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department is the separate competent authority under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, but the CN_WFOE entity is a PRC taxable entity and not a Hong Kong tax resident)

E-government filing portal

For the PRC WFOE: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the 18-character taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes; (2) complete tax registration with the competent local STA office within 30 days after the business license issuance through the integrated One-Stop business registration system or e-Tax Bureau; (3) register separately for Enterprise Income Tax, Value-Added Tax, and social insurance from the date of formation; (4) if the foreign investor is a non-resident entity, that non-resident parent must obtain a Chinese Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number through the STA's non-resident registration procedure for withholding tax and indirect tax compliance, providing its foreign certificate of incorporation, the WFOE's incorporation certificate, a director's authorization passport or corporate shareholding proof, and registered address documentation; (5) the WFOE must also complete bank account registration and, where relevant, foreign-exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the designated bank for the receipt of export proceeds.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — the CN WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax headline rate of 25%; it is not a pass-through entity. For VAT, it is a taxable person and must classify itself either as a general VAT taxpayer (for zero-rating and input tax credit) or, if eligible, as a small-scale taxpayer. — For the PRC WFOE: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the 18-character taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes; (2) complete tax registration with the competent local STA office within 30 days after the business license issuance through the integrated One-Stop business registration system or e-Tax Bureau; (3) register separately for Enterprise Income Tax, Value-Added Tax, and social insurance from the date of formation; (4) if the foreign investor is a non-resident entity, that non-resident parent must obtain a Chinese Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number through the STA's non-resident registration procedure for withholding tax and indirect tax compliance, providing its foreign certificate of incorporation, the WFOE's incorporation certificate, a director's authorization passport or corporate shareholding proof, and registered address documentation; (5) the WFOE must also complete bank account registration and, where relevant, foreign-exchange registration with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through the designated bank for the receipt of export proceeds.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation — the CN WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at the corporate income tax headline rate of 25%; it is not a pass-through entity. For VAT, it is a taxable person and must classify itself either as a general VAT taxpayer (for zero-rating and input tax credit) or, if eligible, as a small-scale taxpayer.

Available election forms

None (China has no check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch entity elections for corporate tax purposes). The only relevant statutory election is the VAT taxpayer status election: a WFOE with annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment under the simplified levy method, while a general VAT taxpayer election is mandatory once the RMB 5,000,000 turnover threshold is exceeded (subject to the VAT law's registration requirements).

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC-established WFOEs must complete VAT registration at formation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that threshold the WFOE may remain a small-scale taxpayer (with simplified collection rates of 1%, 3%, or 5%, depending on category, subject to current STA preferential measures).

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to (a) goods physically exported from China under customs supervision and (b) cross-border taxable services provided to overseas recipients that are statutorily deemed to be consumed entirely outside China, including logistics/ancillary services directly linked to export goods, provided the seller is a general VAT taxpayer. For this reverse-logistics scope, fees for returned-merchandise quality grading, handling, sorting, refurbishment-related ancillary logistics, and re-export services supplied to the non-resident principal are eligible for zero-rating if the underlying goods are exported through customs, the transaction is supported by an export customs declaration, and proceeds are received from outside China. Small-scale VAT taxpayers cannot apply zero-rating and may only access VAT exemption, with no input VAT refund.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) with a valid electronic release record from PRC Customs
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent multimodal transport document evidencing physical movement out of China
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued Foreign Exchange Collection Receipt evidencing settlement of the export proceeds
  • Commercial invoice and export sales contract (or service agreement) with the non-resident customer/principal
  • Customs import/export supporting records showing the returned goods entered China under the applicable customs regime and subsequently exited
  • Quality-grading and reverse-logistics operational records demonstrating that the services were performed on the export-bound goods
  • Electronic customs verification and tax refund application (export VAT refund declaration) filed through the STA export-tax-refund module

The STA operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the Full Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform. VAT invoices must be issued via the e-Tax Bureau or the STA-integrated enterprise tax-control device (tax UKey) and transmitted in near real-time to the STA in structured XML format. There is no generic pre-issuance stamping for every invoice under the FDEI model, but the STA's anti-fraud system performs automatic digital clearance/verification on invoice data; high-risk or cross-border invoices may require manual validation before the buyer can credit input VAT. The invoice must state the buyer's USCC/TIN and the applicable VAT rate, and the seller must retain the corresponding customs and logistics evidence to support any zero-rating.

The STA, together with customs and SAFE, cross-verifies export declarations, e-invoices, logistics records, and bank foreign-exchange receipts. If the zero-rated export is found to be unsubstantiated or the proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period (unless a permitted overseas retention exception applies), the zero-rating is retroactively reversed, output VAT becomes payable, and any input VAT refund is clawed back with overdue surcharge. A taxpayer that made a false or fraudulent export declaration is subject to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved and may be criminally prosecuted for fraudulently obtaining an export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertible under the current account for genuine trade and services, while the capital account remains heavily controlled. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) can receive foreign direct investment (FDI) as registered capital only upon completing SAFE’s Foreign Exchange Registration, followed by opening a Capital Account for foreign exchange settlement. Profit remittances to overseas shareholders are permitted under the current account but require a certified audit report, tax clearance, board resolution on dividend distribution, and a bank-level authenticity review. Funds converted from capital injection must be used within the approved business scope and cannot be deployed for securities, entrusted loans, or real estate outside strict SAFE parameters. Return merchandise reverse logistics, being a current-account services transaction, is subject to proof of the underlying return service contract, customs declarations, and payment reconciliation with the foreign buyer. The practical hurdles include periodic SAFE reporting, strict verification of beneficial ownership, and the mandatory use of designated domestic banks for cross-border settlement. Surrender requirements are no longer a fixed percentage compulsory conversion; export proceeds must be repatriated onshore but can be retained in a foreign currency account or converted at the enterprise’s choice, subject to authenticity checks. Overall, the WFOE treasury must maintain a dual-bank structure: a designated bank for SAFE-managed capital account flows and an operational RMB account for settlement of reverse logistics fees, refunds, and supplier payments.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless approved deferred under SAFE special procedures. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the enterprise may retain up to 100% of the foreign currency in a settlement account or choose to convert to RMB at the prevailing market rate, provided the transaction is supported by genuine documentation and passes SAFE’s monetary/filing review. Any non-compliance triggers administrative penalties and suspension of FX services.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low for primary onboarding and capital account maintenance. Foreign fintech EMIs, including those offering global payment rails, are not licensed commercial banks in China and cannot legally open a Capital Account, perform FDI registration, verify paid-in capital, or process SAFE Form FDI-related foreign exchange settlement. They may only be engaged as ancillary payment facilitators for low-value operational current-account flows, and even then must route through a SAFE-registered bank or licensed Chinese payment institution. Using an offshore EMI as the main treasury account for a China-based WFOE would constitute a regulatory breach and expose the entity to SAFE administrative penalties, frozen outbound remittances, and audit qualifications.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital must be remitted in RMB or freely convertible foreign currency directly from the Hong Kong shareholder to the WFOE’s Capital Account at a designated SAFE-approved bank in China. The remittance instruction must carry the SAFE FDI registration code and a transaction purpose code of '202010' or equivalent for investment in registered capital. Upon receipt, the bank will perform capital verification against the audited statement and issue a Foreign Exchange Settlement Notice; the funds cannot be used until the settlement and account monitoring are completed. For additional working capital, shareholder loans must be covered by a separate foreign debt registration with SAFE and conform to macroprudential borrowing limits.Outbound settlements for reverse logistics service fees, repair costs, and platform refunds will be executed primarily via SWIFT MT103 for wire transfers under current-account approved purposes. For high-value or repeat freight forwarding and re-export settlements, a commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) issued by the Chinese bank and advised through the overseas beneficiary bank is recommended to reduce counterparty risk. Documentary collections may be used for small-ticket, low-risk transactions where the service terms and prior invoice history are fully documented. All outbound payments must match the underlying service agreement, customs declaration if applicable, and SAFE reporting category to avoid rejected clearing.Onshore RMB/Foreign Currency Forwards with real trade and servicing background, sold only through SAFE-authorized domestic banks, limited to current-account exposures under the notice period.Non-deliverable Forwards (NDFs) executed offshore against RMB only for risk management of non-resident parent exposure; onshore WFOE is not permitted to enter NDFs without SAFE granular approval.Cross-currency swaps (RMB vs USD) permissible for servicing capital-repayment and profit-remittance structural exposure, subject to real interest and principal matching and current-account documentation.FX Swaps for temporary liquidity repositioning between RMB and foreign currency where the WFOE has a an actual settlement timing mismatch in reverse logistics vendor payments.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), section 359, every Hong Kong company must appoint a certified public accountant auditor unless validly exempted. For private companies, a small company exemption is available only if the company qualifies as a 'small company' under section 360 and passes an ordinary resolution of shareholders to dispense with the audit, without objection from any shareholder holding 5% or more of voting rights. A small company must satisfy at least two of the following three quantitative tests for the financial year: (a) annual turnover not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approx US$1,282,000); (b) total assets not exceeding HK$10,000,000 (approx US$1,282,000); (c) number of employees not exceeding 50. A company is mandatorily subject to a certified CPA audit if it fails any two of these thresholds, or if it is a specified company such as a listed company, banking undertaking, insurance company, securities dealer, or part of a disqualifying group. Even a small company must procure an audit if any member with at least 5% voting rights serves notice requiring an audit for that year.
