Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a general WFOE exists; the sole shareholder must subscribe to all issued shares, and Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law requires paid-in capital within five years after incorporation. There is no statutory blocked escrow account requirement; however, a bank capital contribution account must be opened and the registered capital contribution must be remitted in the currency and form described in the Articles. For dangerous-goods forwarding licensing, the transport authority may require proof of insurance, vehicles, and safety management capacity as a functional substitute for paid-up capital evidence.

Funding & unlock path: The Fire Rescue Bureau must issue a Civil Defense Fire Protection Acceptance Certificate before any warehouse is occupied. A structural load inspection certificate for racking and mezzanine areas is required. An Occupational Safety and Health clearance from the Emergency Management Bureau must be obtained for any DG handling site. The WFOE must maintain a full-time safety officer, document annual DG training, and hold Dangerous Goods Handler Certificates for all involved personnel.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China (PRC), administered through local Tax Service Bureaus; online portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax). For Hong Kong SAR: Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its eTAX electronic filing portal.

E-government filing portal

1. Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining the PRC Business License carrying the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); the USCC is the sole statutory tax identification number (TIN) used by the STA and China Customs for income tax, VAT, export rebate and invoice purposes. 2. Tax registration is completed automatically through the 'one code, integrated tax registration' data-sharing mechanism; no separate TIN application is required for the PRC entity. 3. If the WFOE exports regulated dangerous goods or provides cross-border freight-forwarding services, complete export-tax-rebate/zero-rating registration and link the taxpayer file to China Customs' Single Window for export declaration data exchange. 4. For any Hong Kong parent or branch, apply to the IRD for a Business Registration Number (BRN) within one month of commencement of business through the eTAX system; the profits tax file is opened upon issuance of the first Profits Tax Return. 5. For a foreign non-resident entity with no PRC establishment, the domestic withholding payer must file the statutory non-resident tax recordal/withholding registration with the local STA bureau so that a TIN is created for withholding on China-source income.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the US check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch sense. Mainland China: a WFOE is a taxable legal entity at the enterprise income tax level; no pass-through election exists. The VAT taxpayer classification provides an election between small-scale simplified levy and general VAT taxpayer status (general registration is mandatory above RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales). Hong Kong SAR: no check-the-box election; offshore profit treatment is a factual territorial-source claim under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for tax registration itself; every WFOE must obtain tax registration at incorporation. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold: annual taxable sales above RMB 5,000,000. Below that amount, small-scale VAT taxpayer status applies unless the taxpayer elects general VAT taxpayer status. Hong Kong SAR: no VAT/GST registration regime.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods qualify for the 0% VAT rate when sold by a registered PRC VAT taxpayer to an overseas buyer, the goods are physically exported through PRC customs territory, and the export proceeds are collected from the overseas buyer in foreign currency. The claim is substantiated by matching the export contract, customs export declaration, transport documents, commercial invoice, packing list, and bank foreign-exchange receipt. Qualifying cross-border services, including eligible international freight-forwarding/logistics services supplied to an overseas recipient and entirely consumed outside the PRC, may be zero-rated or exempt under the cross-border taxable services catalogue; wholly offshore digital services must evidence receipt outside China and foreign-currency payment from the overseas customer.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form) accepted by China Customs and matched with electronic customs release data
  • Bill of Lading (sea freight) or Master/Consolidated Air Waybill (air freight) evidencing outbound movement of dangerous goods from PRC customs territory to an overseas destination
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the equivalent export-proceeds receipt/collection verification document issued by a PRC-designated bank under State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and export sales contract/purchase order
  • Dangerous Goods Declaration (DGD), Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS), and applicable IMDG/IATA/ADR transport documents evidencing lawful DG carriage

China operates the STA's fully digitalized electronic invoice service platform. Taxable persons must issue e-invoices containing a unique invoice code and a dynamic anti-fraud digital stamp/QR code; issuance data is transmitted in real time to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV national fiscal clearance system. Input VAT credit is denied for invoices not authenticated/cleared through the platform. Export declarations and e-invoices must reconcile through the automated electronic data exchange between China Customs and the STA. Hong Kong SAR has no real-time fiscal clearance or digital stamping mandate; records may be maintained electronically for IRD audit.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export zero-rating and export tax-rebate claims through Golden Tax Phase IV data matching. Failure to substantiate the export, or discrepancies among the customs declaration, transport documents, invoice, and foreign-exchange receipt, will result in denial of 0% treatment, immediate clawback of any VAT refunded, late-payment surcharge, and administrative fines. Fraudulent export tax-refund claims are subject to recovery of the refund and a fine of one to five times the amount under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection; serious cases constitute the crime of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law of the PRC, carrying imprisonment of up to life and property confiscation. Incorrect dangerous-goods declarations may additionally attract customs penalties under the Customs Law of the PRC.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PBOC and SAFE administer a managed floating exchange-rate regime under which the RMB is fully convertible on the current account while the capital account remains subject to registration, approval, and post-event verification. The WFOE conducting dangerous goods freight forwarding may settle export proceeds for freight and logistics services into RMB without prior SAFE approval, provided the underlying transaction is genuine and supported by contracts, invoices, customs declarations, and ocean or airway bills. Dividend repatriation to the Hong Kong parent is classified as a capital-account profit remittance and requires a prior FDI foreign-exchange registration, an audited annual financial statement, tax clearance certificates, and documentary evidence that the original capital contribution was properly settled through the SAFE-designated bank. Inward equity injections must be recorded in the capital information system and can only be used after a licensed CPA issues a capital verification report. Intercompany loans, debt-to-equity swaps, and cross-border guarantees require separate SAFE filings. The RMB cross-border clearing system may be used for trade payments, but speculative conversion is prohibited; every FX transaction must be supported by a genuine trade or investment background, and onshore entities cannot access offshore NDFs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For an A-class goods-trade enterprise, there is no mandatory conversion ratio and no obligatory surrender of export foreign exchange proceeds; the exporter may retain receipts in a foreign-currency settlement account or convert them into RMB voluntarily. However, goods-trade export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China within 180 days after the export customs declaration date and must be matched against customs data before bank release. Enterprises classified as B-class are subject to mandatory settlement of export proceeds within five working days, while C-class enterprises require pre-verification for every receipt, effectively requiring immediate conversion through a designated bank. All such rules are administered by SAFE through the cross-border payment and collection monitoring system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Feasibility: LOW for the mainland WFOE. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE to open CNY corporate bank accounts, RMB settlement accounts, or SAFE-regulated foreign exchange settlement accounts for an onshore WFOE. They cannot receive FDI equity capital, cannot complete capital account registration, and cannot produce the capital verification documentation required by the Hong Kong parent's auditors. Such EMIs are legally barred from acting as the primary clearing relationship for a mainland Chinese entity under the Administrative Measures for the Financial Institutions' Foreign Exchange Business and the PBOC deposit-taking rules. The Hong Kong parent may utilise an offshore EMI treasury wallet for its own non-China cash management, but all WFOE-related receipts, disbursements, and hedging flows must be executed through a licensed onshore commercial bank with direct SAFE reporting capability.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All equity contributions from the Hong Kong parent to the mainland WFOE must be routed as SWIFT wire transfers to the SAFE-designated onshore bank capital account. The remittance must identify the transaction as a direct-investment equity contribution under the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code for FDI capital injection. The receiving bank will require a copy of the SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Form, the Foreign Investment Information Report, and the HK parent's certificate of incorporation before crediting the funds. After receipt, the funds may be credited to the capital account and cannot be used for payments unrelated to the WFOE's business scope until a licensed Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report confirming the contribution has been fully subscribed. The capital account balance is then migrated to a dynamic registration record, and any future RMB conversion is executed via the bank's settlement system with trade background documentation.Outbound treasury settlements for DG freight forwarding should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for operating payments such as overseas carrier charges, port fees, insurance premiums, and agent commissions. High-value or term-backed carrier contracts should be settled through commercial letters of credit opened by the WFOE's onshore bank in favour of the overseas freight carrier, with the corresponding documentary package comprising the dangerous goods declaration, bill of lading or airway bill, certificate of insurance, and freight invoice. Documentary collections may be used for time-lagged payment arrangements with established overseas agents, subject to full transport documentation and customs export records. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: The China WFOE has no size-based exemption: an independent Chinese CPA audit is mandatory for every wholly foreign-owned enterprise under PRC Company Law and the PRC accounting regulations. The Hong Kong private company must appoint a licensed certified public accountant auditor unless it qualifies as a small private company meeting at least two of the following three quantitative conditions in the relevant financial year: (i) total annual revenue not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approx USD 1,282,000); (ii) total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approx USD 1,282,000); (iii) average number of employees not exceeding 50. If the Hong Kong entity is part of a group, the group must also satisfy the small private company group conditions; otherwise statutory audit by an independent CPA is compulsory under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance.
