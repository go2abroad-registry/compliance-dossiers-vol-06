Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); official web portal: https://www.chinatax.gov.cn; electronic tax filing and e-invoice platform: https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. The Hong Kong SAR is a separate tax jurisdiction administered by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) for profits tax purposes and does not impose VAT/GST; this dossier addresses the mainland China WFOE entity and its China tax identity.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE, the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN) is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) at incorporation. The foreign non-resident shareholder must first complete the applicable foreign investment and business registration with SAMR. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete tax registration on the STA e-Tax portal by linking the USCC, legal representative identity, business scope, and registered address. No separate VAT registration number is issued; the USCC functions as the VAT registration number. A WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise and must register for both corporate income tax and VAT regardless of whether it has started revenue generation. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a fixed establishment in China may obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification code from the STA through the e-Tax portal or through withholding agent registration when it incurs China-source taxable income; that code is used only for China withholding and non-resident filing obligations.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic tax registration; every licensed enterprise must register for VAT from establishment. Mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration is triggered when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; voluntary registration is permitted below that threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Chinese VAT export tax rebate regime, export of tangible goods by a registered VAT general taxpayer is zero-rated (0% VAT with entitlement to refund of residual input VAT) when the goods are physically exported from China, the customs export declaration is completed through the Single Window, the supply is supported by genuine commercial documents, and export proceeds are repatriated and verified through the SAFE Trade-in-Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System within the statutory period. Cross-border international transportation services supplied by the WFOE may also qualify for zero-rate treatment. Freight-forwarding agency services connected with international cargo are generally VAT-exempt under

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the STA-controlled electronic invoicing platform. Mainland China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (digital e-invoice) service platform, under which invoice data are transmitted to the STA in real time with a national unified invoice number, anti-counterfeiting QR code, and electronic fiscal seal. General taxpayers must validate Special VAT Invoices against the STA VAT invoice checking system before claiming input VAT credits. For export cargo, customs declarations are filed through the China International E-Trade Single Window, which is digitally linked with VAT, customs, and foreign exchange supervision systems. System-to-system integration via the e-Tax platform is available for high-volume issuers.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate system with full current-account convertibility and strict capital-account administration. The RMB is freely convertible for current-account activities such as trade in goods, freight, insurance, royalties, and dividend remittances, provided the receiving bank is satisfied that the transaction is genuine and supported by the required underlying documents. Capital-account transactions, including FDI equity injections, external borrowing, shareholder loans, offshore guarantees, and cross-border securities investment, remain regulated and require registration or approval by SAFE, and the funds are tied to a single approved purpose. The WFOE's initial share-capital injection from the Hong Kong parent is a capital-account flow and must be registered under the FDI foreign-exchange regime before the remittance arrives. After the funds are credited, they may be used only for lawful operating purposes; conversion from foreign currency to RMB is allowed for approved business expenditures. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is then treated as a current-account dividend flow, subject to completion of the statutory annual audit, corporate income tax clearance, and the bank's authenticity check. In practice, profit repatriation is legally available but operationally demanding because the enterprise must present the complete documentary chain; there is no mandatory surrender of export receipts and no minimum local conversion ratio for ordinary operating receipts.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Since the 2012 SAFE reform, China imposes no mandatory foreign-currency surrender and no fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds. The WFOE may retain 100% of export-derived foreign currency in a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account or voluntarily convert it to RMB. However, all export receipts must be repatriated to a domestic designated bank and reported through the cross-border receipts/payments declaration; any offshore retention requires a specific SAFE exemption. Banks monitor export receipts under a total-amount screening framework: each receipt should be matched against export customs declarations within normal commercial credit terms, generally 90 to 180 days. A receivable that remains unmatched after 180 days without documented commercial justification will generate a SAFE monitoring alert and may trigger a temporary restriction on the entity's current-account foreign-exchange operation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the foreign-exchange administration body under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Non-viable for primary statutory accounts and capital flows. Offshore fintech EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Currenxie are not licensed by the PBOC or SAFE as deposit-taking institutions, cannot open an RMB basic deposit account, capital escrow account, or FX settlement account for a Chinese-incorporated WFOE, and cannot participate in CNAPS or CIPS as settlement members. They are legally barred from performing SAFE FDI registration, capital verification, and statutory current-account authenticity checks. At best, a foreign EMI can act as an ancillary payment facilitator through a licensed Chinese partner bank, but the treasury backbone for the WFOE must sit with an onshore Tier-1 commercial bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 or, where both sides settle in RMB, through CIPS, into the WFOE's dedicated FDI foreign-exchange capital account or RMB capital account at the chosen Tier-1 bank. Before the wire, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI registration through the bank-led direct registration, bank handling, SAFE supervision workflow. The remittance narrative must clearly identify the payment as equity injection / paid-in capital, and the bank will verify the SAFE registration number before crediting. Upon receipt, the bank may allow foreign-currency conversion to RMB only for genuine operating uses; capital funds cannot be redirected to entrusted loans, securities investment, wealth management products, or real estate outside the approved scope. After the funds are credited, a licensed Chinese CPA must issue a capital-verification report under the current company law regime if the articles of association require paid-in capital, and the WFOE's books, bank records, and SAFE registration must reconcile. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory PRC withholding tax on dividends paid by a Chinese resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise shareholder; reduced to 5% under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement (DTA) for a Hong Kong beneficial owner holding at least 25% of the WFOE's capital continuously for the 12-month period preceding the dividend distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

Quarterly prepayments due within 15 days after each calendar quarter; annual return and final CIT settlement due within 5 months of tax year-end, i.e. 31 May for the standard 31 December year-end; tax year is the Gregorian calendar year.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Mandatory Local File preparation is triggered by annual related-party transactions of RMB 200 million or more for tangible-asset transactions, or RMB 100 million or more for intangible-asset, financial-asset, or service transactions; a Master File is required when total intercompany transactions reach RMB 1 billion or when the taxpayer holds all entities in a consolidated group meeting the threshold.

Economic substance scope: For treaty benefit entitlement, the Hong Kong parent must have actual economic substance and cannot be a letterbox company; its core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong must include board meetings, staff, and management of its holding and investment functions. The PRC WFOE itself must maintain physical premises, qualified GDP-compliant warehouse and cold-chain infrastructure, employed labor, and decision-making substance in mainland China; empty corporate form, circular financing, or booking profits without operations will be recharacterized under China's general anti-avoidance rule (EIT Law Article 47), and the DTA benefit will be denied under the principal-purpose test.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer pricing manipulation / tax evasion

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 the tax authority may perform an arm's-length adjustment; SAT's special tax adjustment measures impose penalty interest at 5 percentage points above the benchmark lending rate; Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus a 0.05% daily late surcharge; failure to maintain the required Local File or Master File also attracts a separate administrative fine.

Director personal liability: Where the corporate form is used as a sham to evade tax, PRC courts pierce the corporate veil under Company Law Article 20 and hold the shareholders and directors jointly liable for the tax debt; the legal representative and finance officers are listed on the Major Tax Default Public Information list, triggering exit bans, asset freezing, high-consumption restrictions, and credit blacklisting. If evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and is more than 10% of the tax payable, the responsible officers face criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment of up to 7 years for serious amounts.
False invoicing and GDP logistics revenue fraud

Statutory sanction: Issuing false VAT special invoices violates the VAT invoicing regime and PRC Criminal Law Articles 205 and 208; administrative sanctions include confiscation of illegal gains, cancellation of invoices, and fines up to 3 times the amount involved; criminal sentencing ranges from fixed-term imprisonment for substantial false invoice amounts up to life imprisonment and forfeiture of assets for especially huge amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors and other directly responsible personnel are personally liable for fraudulent invoicing because such intentional prohibited acts pierce the corporate veil; personal assets can be subject to civil restitution orders, travel bans, and blacklisting in the National Enterprise Credit Publicity System; criminal conviction can result in imprisonment or life sentence for the legal representative, chief financial officer, and logistics operations director who knowingly signed the false documentation.
Withholding tax evasion and sham DTA relief on profit repatriation

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Law Article 69 makes a withholding agent that fails to withhold liable to a fine of 50% to 300% of the unpaid withholding tax; Article 63 additionally imposes a 50% to 500% penalty for evasion by false declaration; the unpaid withholding tax is recovered from the WFOE and, in certain cases, from responsible officers; penalty interest accrues daily.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize an under-withheld distribution cannot hide behind limited liability when the entity is used as a mere conduit; courts may disregard the WFOE under Company Law Article 20 and impose personal restitution of the unpaid tax and interest. The legal representative and beneficial owner are subject to exit-entry management measures, asset freezing, and corporate blacklisting; where the evaded withholding tax is criminally material, the responsible directors face imprisonment under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 for evasion on behalf of the withholding agent.
