Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, exercised through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online service portal and the Golden Tax System Phase IV real-time fiscal clearance platform; the local competent tax office with territorial jurisdiction over the WFOE's registered address administers registration, VAT, and export-rebate filings.

        
E-government filing portal
A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China obtains its statutory tax identification number through the following mandatory sequence: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License carrying the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the binding taxpayer identification number under the integrated one-license regime (no separate CIT or VAT certificate is issued); (2) within 30 calendar days of Business License issuance, complete tax registration at the competent State Taxation Administration office through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal and activate the electronic tax account; (3) submit VAT taxpayer status registration and, where annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or input VAT recovery is strategically required, complete general VAT taxpayer registration; (4) register as withholding agent for payroll and individual income tax, given the hub will employ operational staff; (5) for the Hong Kong SAR shareholder route, the PRC WFOE must register as statutory withholding agent for any remitted dividends, interest, or royalties to the Hong Kong shareholder and file withholding CIT electronically; no Hong Kong tax registration is triggered merely by holding a mainland WFOE; and (6) complete SAFE foreign direct investment registration and open a multi-currency settlement account with Airwallex plus a local clearing bank account so that the foreign exchange repatriation evidence chain for export zero-rating is legally usable. Foreign directorial and shareholder documents must be notarized, apostilled, and translated by a certified Chinese translation service before SAMR and SAFE filings are accepted.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
The PRC tax system has no check-the-box entity classification election and no pass-through regime for limited liability companies; the WFOE is mandatorily taxed at entity level as a Chinese resident enterprise. Available statutory elections relevant to this formation include: (a) the VAT general taxpayer election to recover input VAT at the 13% output rate; (b) the small-scale taxpayer election where annual taxable sales remain below RMB 5,000,000; (c) the CIT quarterly prepayment method election based on actual taxable profits; (d) the foreign tax credit election to offset Hong Kong profits tax and qualified overseas taxes against PRC CIT within the per-country basket limitation; and (e) for hybrid financing instruments from the Hong Kong shareholder, the mandatory substance-over-form recharacterization under the STA hybrid investment rules applies, with no elective hybrid mismatch protocol available.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Mandatory tax registration is imposed on every WFOE within 30 days of Business License issuance regardless of turnover; the compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales (exclusive of VAT), below which the WFOE may elect small-scale taxpayer status attracting simplified rates and a monthly RMB 100,000 (quarterly RMB 300,000) small-scale VAT exemption; non-resident suppliers making taxable cross-border supplies into mainland China must register or obtain a withholding agent from the first yuan of taxable turnover.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Export VAT zero-rating at 0% (the exemption-credit-refund method) is available for the hub's goods exports where: (1) the consolidated parcels physically depart the PRC customs territory to an overseas buyer; (2) the WFOE holds general VAT taxpayer status (small-scale taxpayers receive exemption without refund); (3) the customs export declaration matches at shipment level the commercial invoice, packing list, and transport documents; (4) the export consideration is genuinely repatriated in foreign currency through SAFE-supervised channels into the Airwallex or local clearing account; (5) outbound parcels are dispatched under China Customs' cross-border e-commerce export supervision modes (B2C parcel and B2B direct-injection linehaul) with manifest data transmitted to the customs system; and (6) parcels transshipped via the Hong Kong SAR remain in transit and are not released for home consumption in Hong Kong. Qualifying offshore digital services are outside the hub's core scope and are not covered by this zero-rating analysis.

China enforces a mandatory real-time fiscal invoice clearance regime under the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the fully digitalized e-invoice (Digital E-Invoice) reform. All VAT invoices issued by the WFOE must be created through the National Electronic Invoice Service Platform or an STA-certified e-invoice pipeline, transmitted in real time to the tax authority's central taxpayer digital account; offline non-integrated invoice issuance is illegal. Enterprise resource planning and parcel-sorting systems must integrate with the platform APIs for automatic invoice issuance, red-letter reversal, and acceptance verification. Anti-counterfeiting is secured by the unified invoice code, the fiscal digital stamp, and real-time pairing of e-invoices against customs export declarations, logistics manifests, and payment-rail data; any invoice bypassing the platform is treated as false issuance and triggers immediate tax inspection and penalty.

      
Where the Golden Tax System Phase IV risk engine or customs post-clearance audit identifies any mismatch among the export declaration, e-invoice, logistics route, or FX repatriation, the entire zero-rated benefit is retrospectively clawed back: the tax authority reinstates 13% output VAT on the export proceeds, disallows the related input VAT credits, recovers any VAT already refunded, imposes the 0.05% per-day late-payment surcharge from the original due date, and levies administrative penalties of 50% to five times the evaded tax under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law. Export-rebate status may be suspended pending full inspection, and the WFOE may be downgraded to the C/D tax credit risk grade, forcing transaction-level verification on every subsequent shipment. Failure to repatriate export proceeds by the statutory monitoring deadline (overdue non-collection beyond 90 days without registration, or non-collection within the 180-day export verification window) voids the zero-rating claim; where the Hong Kong transshipment trail reveals fictitious export or invoice-stamping fraud, the file is referred to customs anti-smuggling authorities and may constitute criminal export-tax fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, exposing responsible officers to criminal liability.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime. The RMB is generally convertible on current-account items such as trade settlements, service fees, and profit remittances after tax, but remains heavily controlled on capital-account items. A WFOE may lawfully inject foreign direct investment, receive export or service revenue, and remit distributable profits, but each movement must be backed by a genuine transaction and submitted to SAFE-monitored banking verification. Inbound equity capital must be registered through the bank under the FDI system and held in a dedicated foreign-currency capital account until a real operating need is proven before RMB settlement. Profit remittances require audited financial statements, corporate income-tax filing certificates, and proof that no undisclosed intercompany obligations were used to convert RMB. Commodity or service receipts must be collected in the ordinary course and cannot be diverted to overseas accounts without authorization. Any arrangement seeking to avoid capital controls via fake invoices, round-trip transfers, or delayed export collection is treated as illegal foreign-exchange arbitrage and will trigger severe administrative penalties. The treasury function must therefore maintain an audit trail that matches every FX flow to a customs declaration, tax filing, invoice, contract, and board-approved corporate purpose.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under the current SAFE framework there is no mandatory conversion ratio for export proceeds; a Category-A WFOE may retain up to 100% of its goods-export foreign-exchange receipts in a designated enterprise current-account FX account or convert 100% into RMB at the bank of its choosing. However, all export proceeds must be repatriated to the Chinese banking system within 90 days from the customs release date unless the underlying sales contract documents a longer deferred-payment term. For the parcel hub's cross-border service revenue, proceeds should be collected within 90 days after invoice issuance: the bank requires a matching trade-in-services declaration and operational proof such as parcel manifests and linehaul route schedules. Any extension beyond these timelines must be explained through the bank's trade-monitoring system, and a failure to do so will trigger a SAFE compliance investigation and potential downgrade of the WFOE's foreign-exchange Category status.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
An offshore digital EMI such as Airwallex has only ancillary viability for this WFOE. It cannot legally serve as the primary onshore bank because it does not hold a PBOC-authorized commercial banking or payment-institution license to open domestic RMB settlement accounts or clear payments through CNAPS. An offshore EMI may be used as a multi-currency collection gateway for overseas e-commerce platforms and as a low-cost payout rail for overseas last-mile couriers, provided the entity is onboarded in the WFOE's full corporate name and all balances are settled back into the domestic Tier-1 bank with complete SAFE declarations. Legal constraints make it non-viable as the sole or principal treasury rail for the enterprise's onshore payroll, tax payments, RMB supplier payments, or capital conversion. The bank compliance view is therefore that the WFOE must maintain a domestic Tier-1 banking relationship; the EMI is treated as an unregulated payment add-on rather than a substitute for a licensed clearing bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        Inbound capital must flow from the HK parent via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete the FDI foreign-exchange registration with SAFE and obtain the registration confirmation that references the entity's unique foreign-investment code. The remittance instructions must carry the SAFE balance-of-payments transaction code for foreign direct investment equity injection, not a goods or service trade code. Upon receipt, the bank credits the capital account and verifies the remitter, purpose, and source of funds. RMB release is governed by the payment-based settlement or elective settlement rule: the WFOE submits the underlying contracts, invoices, and actual-use statement for each tranche, after which the bank converts and disburses RMB to the designated supplier or operational account. A PRC-certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report, which supports the annual FDI inspection and the final registration of the contributed capital. Complete documentation permits this cycle in two to five business days.Outbound disbursements for the parcel consolidation hub will use three primary clearing rails: SWIFT MT103 for direct vendor payments in USD, HKD, or RMB where the counterparty is an overseas carrier or last-mile provider; commercial letters of credit for high-value equipment purchases or logistics outsourcing contracts requiring a bank undertaking; and documentary collections for repetitive, low-value trade flows where the bank controls the documentation but no LC is needed. Onshore RMB vendor payments clear through CNAPS, while cross-border RMB trade settlements can also be cleared through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) via the Tier-1 bank. All outbound USD/HKD payments must be reported under the correct SAFE trade or reinvestment purpose code, and each payment must be mapped to an approved tax invoice and the supporting parcel or service documentation maintained for audit.Onshore deliverable CNY forward contracts with a licensed bank, restricted to the value and maturity of a confirmed trade or service contractOnshore CNY/USD foreign-exchange swaps for operational cash-flow matching, with no speculative leverage or extra notional amountsCross-currency interest rate and principal swaps used only for a verified external-debt or equipment-lease exposure of the WFOE, with maturity and notional matched to the underlying obligationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for the WFOE; any NDF hedging must be executed by the offshore HK parent and ring-fenced so that it is never intermingled with the WFOE's SAFE-monitored transactions
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: An offshore digital EMI such as Airwallex has only ancillary viability for this WFOE. It cannot legally serve as the primary onshore bank because it does not hold a PBOC-authorized commercial banking or payment-institution license to open domestic RMB settlement accounts or clear payments through CNAPS. An offshore EMI may be used as a multi-currency collection gateway for overseas e-commerce platforms and as a low-cost payout rail for overseas last-mile couriers, provided the entity is onboarded in the WFOE's full corporate name and all balances are settled back into the domestic Tier-1 bank with complete SAFE declarations. Legal constraints make it non-viable as the sole or principal treasury rail for the enterprise's onshore payroll, tax payments, RMB supplier payments, or capital conversion. The bank compliance view is therefore that the WFOE must maintain a domestic Tier-1 banking relationship; the EMI is treated as an unregulated payment add-on rather than a substitute for a licensed clearing bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local file required if any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds are exceeded (SAT Announcement 42/2016): tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions (including services) above RMB 40 million. Master file required if related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion in total (with cross-border element). Contemporaneous TP documentation must also be prepared for significant related-party transactions to avoid penalty.

      
Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form principle under Article 47 of the EIT Law (GAAR) and the Implementation Regulations; any arrangement lacking business rationale or economic substance may be re-characterized. The WFOE must maintain real operational presence (personnel, premises, assets, and decision-making) in mainland China. For treaty benefits, the beneficial ownership rule under SAT Circular 9/2012 requires substantive economic activities in Hong Kong for the parent; the OECD MLI and Principal Purpose Test are also relevant in bilateral context. While China does not have an offshore ESR regime, a mainland WFOE is a resident entity subject to worldwide income tax, and thin capitalization, CFC rules, and transfer pricing rules collectively demand genuine economic substance.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion / False VAT Invoicing and Revenue Underreporting
        
Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 63: penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax, plus daily interest; Criminal Law Art. 205 (for counterfeit VAT invoices) imposes severe fines and imprisonment based on amount; Criminal Law Art. 201 (tax evasion) applies to intentional underpayment.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who orchestrate or approve such acts are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of taxes and penalties; they face criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 with up to 7 years' imprisonment for large amounts; travel bans (exit restrictions), entry into tax-related blacklist, and asset freezes are routinely imposed.

      

      
      

        Withholding Tax Non-Compliance on Outbound Dividends
        
Statutory sanction: TCAL Art. 69: withholding agent is fined 50% to 300% of the tax not withheld; the foreign shareholder remains liable for the tax; serious cases trigger criminal liability under the Criminal Law for evasion.

        
Director personal liability: Directors as responsible persons are liable for the fine and civil compensation to the company; willful failure to withhold may be prosecuted as tax evasion, leading to imprisonment up to 7 years, disqualification from directorship, entry into the national tax blacklist, and travel restrictions.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Thin Capitalization
        
Statutory sanction: EIT Law Art. 41: tax authority may adjust income; Art. 44: assessment based on deemed profit upon failure to provide documentation; Implementing Regulations Art. 119 and SAT Announcement 34/2012 impose interest at benchmark loan rate plus 5 percentage points; TCAL Art. 63 penalties (50%-500%) if fraud is established.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who engineered the pricing are personally liable for the additional tax, interest, and penalties to the extent of company loss; criminal charges for evasion under Art. 201 Criminal Law if willfulness is shown; civil derivative suits may be brought; blacklisting and exit bans are possible for severe cases.
