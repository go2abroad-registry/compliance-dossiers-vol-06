Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. PRC law sets no uniform minimum paid-in capital for a general WFOE and imposes no blocked escrow or guarantee deposit at incorporation. The shareholders are free to determine registered capital in the Articles of Association, but the subscribed capital must be fully paid by the deadline stated in the articles and must be economically commensurate with the business scale and express-delivery licensing conditions; notarized evidence of paid-in capital will be required when updating SAMR and foreign-exchange records.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online electronic portal: Electronic Taxpayer Service Portal (e-Tax) accessible at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn (provincial e-Tax portals through local STA offices).

E-government filing portal

The WFOE, as a PRC-resident enterprise, must complete taxpayer registration within 30 days from the issuance of the SAMR business licence. The Unified Social Credit Code printed on the business licence is automatically used as the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT taxpayer identification number; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Procedure: (1) file incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation and obtain the business licence; (2) activate the taxpayer account on the STA e-Tax portal using the electronic business licence or financial certificate; (3) complete the supplementary tax registration covering taxpayer type (general or small-scale VAT taxpayer), industry classification (ISIC 5320 / logistics auxiliary services), bank account details, authorised personnel, and accounting standard; (4) apply for the Digital Electronic Invoice scheme and, if a general VAT taxpayer, for the use of Special VAT Invoices; (5) complete export-related registration with customs and the trade in goods foreign exchange monitoring system if the WFOE is an exporter or export-related service provider. A non-resident enterprise without an establishment in China that receives cross-border payments from Chinese counterparties must instead register for withholding tax identification through the payer under the STA withholding registration rules.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation (PRC-resident corporate taxpayer; the WFOE is taxed separately from shareholders on worldwide income; no pass-through or transparent treatment under PRC Corporate Income Tax).

Available election forms

None. PRC law does not permit U.S.-style check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch elections for WFOEs. Entity classification follows the legal form stated in the articles of association and business licence. PRC general anti-avoidance rules and BEPS hybrid mismatch adjustments are applied by the tax authority to deny unintended deductions.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for mandatory taxpayer registration by a limited liability enterprise; for mandatory general VAT taxpayer classification the annual taxable sales threshold is CNY 5,000,000. A WFOE below the threshold may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily registers as a general taxpayer with sound accounting.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the PRC customs territory are zero-rated if the exporter makes a true customs export declaration, title passes to an overseas buyer, payment is received in foreign exchange, and the export proceeds are repatriated within the statutory deadline. Cross-border express courier services supplied by the WFOE to an overseas recipient and consumed wholly outside China may also qualify for zero-rating or exemption when the recipient is a non-resident and remuneration is received in foreign exchange. Domestic collection, line-haul, and delivery legs within China before the goods leave the customs territory are generally taxable at the logistics auxiliary service rate of 6% and cannot be zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration form generated via the China International Trade Single Window and approved by the PRC General Administration of Customs)
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical departure of the consignment from the PRC customs territory
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation (bank record of the inbound export proceeds or trade-in-goods foreign exchange monitoring system record)
  • Commercial invoice and packing list matching the customs declared value and quantity
  • Export contract or customer purchase order
  • Logistics service contract, shipment routing, and delivery confirmation for cross-border courier services

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA National Electronic Invoice Platform in the form of Fully Digitised Electronic Invoices (Digital Electronic Invoices). General taxpayers issuing Special VAT Invoices must transmit invoice data in real time to the STA e-Tax system through the official tax-control electronic interfaces; printing or offline issuance without STA data transmission is prohibited. Each Digital Electronic Invoice must contain a statutory verification code and digital signature; buyers verify invoices on the STA invoice verification platform. Self-developed billing software must pass STA compatibility testing and use the authorised data-exchange interface before production activation.

Export zero-rating is verified through post-export matching of customs declaration data, VAT invoice data, and foreign exchange receipt data. If the export is found to be false, the documents are incomplete, the goods are not physically exported, or the foreign exchange is not repatriated within the deadline, the tax authority revokes the zero-rating and assesses VAT at the applicable domestic rate (13% for goods or 6% for courier/logistics services), plus late payment penalty and daily interest. Under the PRC Tax Administration and Collection Law, fraudulent export refunds attract penalties of 50% to five times the tax understated and clawback of any refunded tax; the criminal offence of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law may lead to imprisonment and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates separate current and capital account frameworks. The RMB is convertible on goods and service current account items, but actual transactions are subject to genuineness review by banks and recording in SAFE's monitoring system. For a WFOE in the cross-border express courier business, registered capital from the Hong Kong parent is a capital account inflow that must be booked through a foreign exchange capital account and filed under SAFE's FDI registration; unrestricted conversion to RMB for domestic uses is no longer automatic because settlement follows the negative list and supporting documentation. Export and service proceeds are current account receipts and may be retained in foreign currency or converted to RMB after customs or contract verification. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is broadly convertible once the distributable dividend is certified in audited financial statements, prior-year losses are recouped, enterprise income tax and withholding tax are settled, and the remitting bank performs an authenticity check on board resolutions; no separate SAFE approval is required, but evidence of all fiscal obligations must be presented. Outbound cross-border guarantees, equity investments and capital redemptions require additional approvals or registrations, reflecting capital account conservatism. Overall, liquidity for operational courier and settlement purposes can be managed lawfully through onshore and offshore rails if documentation is strong.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no longer any mandatory surrender percentage for export earnings in China. A PRC WFOE may retain 100% of export or service foreign currency proceeds in an approved bank account or sell them in the interbank market. However, export related proceeds must be repatriated into China through a designated receiving bank, and for goods exports the bank will require the customs electronic declaration number and the receivable to be settled within 180 days from the date of export declaration unless the contract stipulates a longer credit period, in which case the bank must maintain documentation supporting the extended term. For cross-border courier service income, the bank verifies the service invoice, signed courier contract and internal delivery records on a transaction-by-transaction basis, and normally expects funds to be received within the contractually agreed settlement cycle. If the WFOE receives payment from overseas for merchandise sold through its e-commerce fulfillment and courier logistics operation, the same goods export timeline applies.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the delegated foreign exchange administration

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable as primary onshore banking infrastructure. China's Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations require a corporate entity receiving FDI or executing current account foreign exchange transactions to use a licensed financial institution regulated by PBOC and SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed to maintain RMB/CNY accounts or foreign exchange capital accounts in mainland China and cannot perform the SAFE FDI registration, capital verification or tax certificate verification required for a WFOE. Some EMIs may service the Hong Kong treasury layer by providing multi-currency virtual IBANs to the Hong Kong intermediate company, but for the mainland WFOE they are legally barred from accepting capital injections directly or making domestic RMB disbursements. The blueprint therefore mandates domestic Tier-1 commercial bank accounts for onshore liquidity, with fintech EMIs limited to supporting treasury functions outside China.

China's Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations require a corporate entity receiving FDI or executing current account foreign exchange transactions to use a licensed financial institution regulated by PBOC and SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed to maintain RMB/CNY accounts or foreign exchange capital accounts in mainland China and cannot perform the SAFE FDI registration, capital verification or tax certificate verification required for a WFOE. Some EMIs may service the Hong Kong treasury layer by providing multi-currency virtual IBANs to the Hong Kong intermediate company, but for the mainland WFOE they are legally barred from accepting capital injections directly or making domestic RMB disbursements. The blueprint therefore mandates domestic Tier-1 commercial bank accounts for onshore liquidity, with fintech EMIs limited to supporting treasury functions outside China.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File: HK$5,000,000 for intercompany goods transactions; HK$3,000,000 for services. Master File: group revenue ≥ HK$1 billion and cross-border connected transactions > HK$50 million per category. (Per IRO s.58C and related rules)

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong does not have separate ESR; however, the territorial source principle requires substantive CIGA in HK to support offshore claims or residency. For courier operations, CIGA include contract negotiation, route planning, billing, and dispatch control. Artificial structures will be challenged, leading to full HK taxation or denial of treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Offshore Mischaracterization

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) s.82: maximum fine of HK$50,000 plus treble the tax undercharged and imprisonment up to 3 years. s.82A adds penalties of up to HK$100,000 and 3 years.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns face personal civil liability for the tax shortfall and treble penalties. Courts may disqualify directors for up to 15 years, impose travel bans, and blacklist the company with the IRD. If the company is a mere alter ego, personal assets may be frozen and seized.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: IRO s.50AAP permits reassessment of profits. Non-compliance with documents under s.58C(10) incurs HK$50,000 fine; on conviction HK$100,000 and 6 months imprisonment. Additional 10% of tax undercharged may be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors cannot delegate compliance. They are personally liable for penalties if they knowingly direct failure. The IRD can pierce the veil where the company is a conduit, holding directors jointly liable for unpaid tax, interest, and penalties.
AML / Counter-Terrorist Financing Failure via Courier Cash Smuggling

Statutory sanction: Anti-Money Laundering and Counter-Terrorist Financing Ordinance (Cap 615) s.5: failure to conduct CDD - on summary conviction HK$100,000 fine and 6 months; on indictment HK$1,000,000 and 2 years. Failure to file STR under s.9 can lead to 7 years imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Directors are 'responsible officers' and may be prosecuted individually. They may be disqualified, issued travel bans, and have personal assets restrained. Continuous violations may lead to company strike-off and personal criminal indemnity.
