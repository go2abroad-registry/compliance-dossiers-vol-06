E-government filing portal

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales in excess of RMB 5,000,000 (CNY 5 million) makes general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory; a WFOE below that threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer but should voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer if it exports goods or supplies qualifying cross-border zero-rated services, because only general VAT taxpayers may apply export zero-rating and claim input VAT refunds.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may apply 0% VAT to qualifying exports of syrups, powders, packaging materials, and other physical goods if the goods are actually exported from China through a formal customs export declaration and the WFOE is a registered general VAT taxpayer. Cross-border services, including franchise licensing fees, royalties, and management services supplied to an overseas franchisee, may qualify for zero-rating only if the service is supplied to a recipient located outside China, the service is consumed wholly outside China, and the STA's cross-border taxable services zero-rating documentation requirements are satisfied. Domestic on-premise sale of bubble tea, local franchise support, and unrelated domestic services remain taxable at the standard goods or services rates and cannot be zero-rated merely because the business operates a global franchise model.

China operates mandatory real-time digital VAT invoice control through the STA's Golden Tax Project Phase IV and Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (e-invoice) system. VAT invoices must be issued through the official Electronic Taxpayer Hall or a certified electronic invoice service platform; invoice data, the unique invoice code, and the digital signature are transmitted to the STA immediately at or after issuance. There is no optional private fiscal stamp or third-party pre-clearance stamping mechanism. Medium and large taxpayers using ERP or sales billing systems must connect through the STA's standardized API interface for electronic invoice data exchange; paper or offline invoices are subject to UKey digital signature and same-day or batch statutory reporting.

The STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system automatically cross-matches the customs export declaration, export and purchase VAT invoice data, bill of lading, transport documentation, bank foreign-exchange collection records, and the WFOE's VAT and Corporate Income Tax returns. If any required document is missing, the goods cannot be traced to a genuine export, or export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory 180-day period, the 0% claim will be rejected and the WFOE must charge output VAT at the standard rate, repay any VAT rebate already received, and pay late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day on the underpaid tax. The tax authority may also impose a penalty from 50% to five times the tax underpaid under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, and intentional false export declarations for VAT rebate fraud are punishable under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with additional penalties available under the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese mainland operates a dual-account convertibility system. RMB is fully convertible for current-account purposes, i.e., trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties, and interest, provided that the underlying transaction is genuine, documented, and processed through a designated foreign-exchange bank. The capital account, however, remains heavily administrated. A WFOE that receives equity or debt investment from its Hong Kong shareholder must first complete foreign-invested enterprise establishment registration with the market regulator and then file the foreign exchange registration with SAFE before any capital inflow can be booked. Capital must enter through a designated foreign currency capital account, be verified by a Chinese certified public accountant, and only then may be settled into RMB under the bank's 'capital settlement' mechanism. All subsequent outbound remittances of dividends, royalties, branch profits, or liquidation proceeds are subject to a condition-by-condition review: the bank requires audited annual financial statements, tax clearance certificates, evidence of SAFE registration, and proof that the remittance is consistent with the approved business scope. In practice, repatriation of profits is possible but requires a cumulative tax-payment certificate (withholding income tax), and the payment cannot precede annual audit completion. There is no blanket surrender obligation on current-account receipts; entities may retain foreign currency in settlement accounts, but every receipt must be reconciled with customs or contract documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated onshore within 90 days from the date of export declaration and must be either deposited into a foreign-currency current-account or settled into RMB at the entity's designated bank. The current mandatory conversion ratio for export receipts is effectively 0%, i.e., the old full-surrender rule has been abolished, so the WFOE may maintain its export earnings in foreign currency. However, the bank must verify the customs declaration, invoice, bill of lading, and contract for each receipt, and the transaction is reported to SAFE through the cross-border receipts-and-payments system. If the entity chooses to convert to RMB, the conversion is executed at the spot market rate on the same day. For any export proceeds not repatriated within the 90-day window, the WFOE must apply to SAFE for an extension and can be subject to foreign-exchange violation penalties if the delay is unjustified.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability as a primary banking rail. Mainland China does not permit unlicensed offshore EMI/neobank providers such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Mercury to maintain local-currency or foreign-currency accounts for a domestic WFOE, because all foreign-exchange and RMB settlement must flow through a licensed PRC banking entity connected to CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System) and CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System). EMIs also cannot perform SAFE FDI registration, capital settlement, or tax declaration blocking. An EMI may be used at best as an ancillary collection and payment tool for the Hong Kong holding company outside the PRC, but the WFOE's underlying trade payments must be routed via a domestic authorized bank. Attempting to use an EMI to bypass onshore control will trigger immediate AML and regulatory review.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Equity injections from the Hong Kong parent must be wired via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign exchange capital account at a licensed PRC bank. The payment instruction must include the SAFE foreign exchange business code for FDI capital injection (equity contribution), the name of the WFOE, and a reference to the FDI registration certificate. The bank will first check that the amount does not exceed the registered equity stated in the Articles of Association; after funds are received, the WFOE must obtain a capital verification report from a PRC certified accountant, and then the bank will transfer the money to a RMB capital settlement account. From that account, the RMB may be used only for the company's normal operating expenses (wages, rent, raw material purchases, trademark royalties and other current account items); strictly prohibited uses include stock market investment, interbank lending, real estate development beyond the approved scope, and entrustment loans. The same documentation package must be retained for SAFE post-event audits.For outbound payments, the WFOE will use a mix of SWIFT MT103 for services such as royalties, technical licensing, and management fees, after withholding Chinese enterprise income tax and VAT on cross-border payments where applicable. For physical imports of raw materials (e.g., tea leaves, tapioca pearls, packaging cups), the bank will issue commercial letters of credit (L/C at sight or usance) where the supplier requires payment against presentation of documents; for smaller open-account imports, SWIFT MT103 payments are made under a purchase contract, invoice, customs declaration, and bill of lading. Documentary collections (D/P, D/A) can be used for transactions with moderate trust. All RMB cross-border settlement can route through CIPS via the domestic bank, while foreign-currency settlements remain on SWIFT. Each outward remittance must be linked to an underlying verified transaction, and the bank will file the outflow with SAFE under the corresponding trade or service code.Deliverable FX forward contracts with an onshore licensed bank, under the SAFE 'real need' principle, with a maximum maturity aligned to the underlying exporting or importing exposure and full documentation of the trade transaction.Cross-currency swaps between RMB and USD/HKD on the onshore interbank market, permitted only for hedging of actual capital account items such as shareholder loans or FDI equity if the notional and maturity match the registered debt certificate.FX options (plain vanilla call or put) purchased from an onshore bank to hedge genuine trade collections or payments; option writing by the corporate treasury is not permitted.Offshore NDFs are prohibited for onshore WFOEs because they are classified as speculative derivatives; SAFE will only accept deliverable forward contracts onshore. A 'CNH' deliverable forward booked in Hong Kong may be used by the HK parent for its own exposure, but may not be brought onshore for the WFOE's books.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low viability as a primary banking rail. Mainland China does not permit unlicensed offshore EMI/neobank providers such as Revolut, Airwallex, or Mercury to maintain local-currency or foreign-currency accounts for a domestic WFOE, because all foreign-exchange and RMB settlement must flow through a licensed PRC banking entity connected to CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System) and CIPS (Cross-Border Interbank Payment System). EMIs also cannot perform SAFE FDI registration, capital settlement, or tax declaration blocking. An EMI may be used at best as an ancillary collection and payment tool for the Hong Kong holding company outside the PRC, but the WFOE's underlying trade payments must be routed via a domestic authorized bank. Attempting to use an EMI to bypass onshore control will trigger immediate AML and regulatory review.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong company must prepare financial statements and obtain an independent auditor's report for each financial year unless it qualifies for the small private company audit exemption. A private company is exempt in a financial year only if it is not a member of a group, or falls within a small private company group, and satisfies at least two of the following three criteria: (a) annual revenue does not exceed HK$100,000,000 (approximately USD 12.8 million); (b) total assets do not exceed HK$100,000,000; and (c) the average number of employees does not exceed 100. A company that exceeds two or more of these thresholds, or whose group in aggregate exceeds any two of these thresholds, is legally required to appoint a certified public accountant (practising) to audit its financial statements. In addition, audit is always mandatory for listed companies, financial institutions, insurance companies, licensed corporations, registered trust companies, and any company that is not eligible for the exemption. A dormant private company that has passed a special resolution for dormancy may also be exempted from audit while dormant.
