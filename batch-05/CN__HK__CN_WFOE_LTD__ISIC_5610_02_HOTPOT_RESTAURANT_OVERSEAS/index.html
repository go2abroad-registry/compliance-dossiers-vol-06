Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; operational compliance is administered through the provincial/municipal competent tax authorities and the national unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal; export customs and trade data are exchanged with the General Administration of Customs via the China International Trade Single Window.

E-government filing portal

A PRC-incorporated WFOE must proceed as follows: (1) Complete incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is also the national tax identification number. (2) Complete tax information registration with the competent State Taxation Administration (STA) tax office within 30 calendar days of the business license date via the Electronic Tax Bureau or in-person service window; the USCC is directly adopted as the taxpayer ID, so no separate TIN certificate is issued. (3) Submit the WFOE articles of association, registered address documents, legal representative identification, Hong Kong parent company registration and ultimate beneficial ownership particulars, and bank account information; materially changed particulars must be reported within 15 days. (4) Complete VAT taxpayer classification (general versus small-scale taxpayer), register for payroll and individual income tax withholding for employees, and, if exporting hotpot condiments/goods, apply for export tax refund/exemption qualification and maintain the required export sales and receipt ledgers. (5) For foreign non-resident entities with no PRC establishment or place of business, no full tax registration is required; the PRC paying entity must complete withholding tax registration for non-resident enterprise income tax and, where treaty relief is sought, obtain the China-Hong Kong residency certificate/beneficial ownership declaration before reduced withholding rates are applied.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise liable to Corporate Income Tax at 25% on worldwide income, is not pass-through, and is not fiscally transparent into its Hong Kong parent. Dividends paid to a qualifying Hong Kong shareholder may benefit from the 5% treaty withholding rate under the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement, subject to beneficial ownership and anti-abuse tests; the Hong Kong SAR itself operates a separate territorial profits tax regime with no tax consolidation with the PRC WFOE.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; the PRC does not provide check-the-box elections or hybrid mismatch entity elections. Available statutory elections are limited to VAT general versus small-scale taxpayer registration, permitted accounting/tax computation methods, and direct/indirect foreign tax credit elections on the annual Corporate Income Tax return; treaty relief under the China-HK DTA must be claimed through the standard non-resident withholding procedures and local filing package.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC businesses must complete tax registration upon incorporation; the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer status is annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000. Below the threshold, an entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer using the 3% collection rate (with applicable temporary reliefs), while general taxpayers account for VAT under the multiple-rate regime (13% standard goods, 9% agricultural/transport/construction goods and services, 6% services, including catering where the PRC taxable service is supplied; exported goods and certain cross-border services are zero-rated). Small-scale taxpayers with monthly VAT sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 may be exempt from VAT under current relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

VAT zero-rating applies to goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory, including hotpot condiments and packaged food supplied by the WFOE to overseas licensed restaurants or distributors, provided the supplier is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are declared to and leave China through the customs export procedure, the foreign currency sales proceeds are received from the overseas buyer and repatriated to China within the statutory deadline, and the electronic VAT invoice and customs declaration match in the STA export tax rebate system. Cross-border services consumed wholly outside China and supplied to overseas recipients may also be zero-rated under the PRC cross-border services VAT rules; overseas restaurant operating income of foreign subsidiaries is outside PRC VAT scope.

  • PRC Customs Export Declaration (export goods declaration form in the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Air Waybill (air) or international combined transport document
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the bank-issued foreign exchange receipt/settlement certificate generated from the SAFE-authorized designated forex bank system
  • Commercial invoice and detailed packing list matching the customs declaration
  • Electronic VAT special invoice (export invoice) issued through the all-digital e-invoice platform
  • Export contract, purchase orders, and overseas restaurant/distributor identity evidence
  • Customs valuation and export declaration data synchronized to the STA export tax rebate module
  • Warehouse outbound records and, if required, inspection/quarantine certificate for food exports

All VAT invoices must be issued through STA-approved tax-control systems. The nationwide all-digital electronic invoice platform transmits invoices in real time to the STA cloud through the Electronic Tax Bureau API, with a unique 20-digit invoice number, encrypted tax-control stamp, and automatic QR code; paper special VAT invoices require tax-control UKey or approved software. Golden Tax Phase IV applies continuous risk-based data matching between invoices, customs export declarations, bank/foreign exchange receipts, inventory, and corporate income tax filings. For export zero-rating, the electronic export invoice must be reconciled with the customs export declaration in the export tax rebate module before refund or exemption is cleared.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to post-export audits by STA and customs risk-selection reviews. Where documentation is inconsistent, declarations are manipulated, invoices are false, proceeds are not repatriated within the required period, or circular trade is detected, STA will claw back the export VAT exemption/refund and impose late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day of overdue tax, and may assess monetary penalties of 50% to 500% of the tax amount under the Tax Administration Law (with a one-to-five-times penalty specifically for fraudulent export rebate claims). Serious fraud is referred to customs anti-smuggling and public security authorities under PRC Criminal Law, Article 204, and may result in imprisonment up to life, confiscation of property, revocation of export tax rebate qualification, and unlimited retroactive tax assessment in cases of fraud. Exporters must retain the full documentation bundle for at least five years and cooperate with extended anti-circular and transfer-pricing audit procedures.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime for the renminbi. The RMB has current-account convertibility under the IMF Articles of Association; current-account transactions, including trade in goods and services, may be settled through designated foreign-exchange banks upon submission of authentic commercial documents. Capital-account transactions, however, require case-by-case approval or registration. For a Hong Kong-owned WFOE, paid-in capital injection is permitted only after FDI registration with SAFE and the local Administration for Market Regulation; funds enter through a capital settlement account and cannot be withdrawn for unauthorized purposes without the full tax and accounting verification package. Dividend and profit remittance to Hong Kong requires audited financial statements, corporate income tax final settlement documents, board resolutions, and a bank certificate evidencing the WFOE's SAFE registration. Capital reductions, liquidation proceeds, and foreign debt repayments are subject to stricter SAFE control and may require a separate registration. Therefore, an integrated treasury architecture must combine onshore bank accounts, SAFE reporting, and trade-backed FX hedging rather than relying on offshore digital EMI solutions.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current goods-trade FX management framework, there is no mandatory conversion ratio; export proceeds are credited to an export settlement account and may be retained in original currency. However, proceeds must be repatriated onshore within 180 days after the date of customs declaration and must be matched against the electronic customs declaration via SAFE's trade monitoring system. All receipts must be supported by export contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, and transportation documents; discrepancies exceeding the SAFE tolerance threshold trigger verification and potential punitive measures.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China, acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMIs are legally barred from maintaining clearing or settlement accounts for mainland China-domiciled WFOEs because PBoC licensing only extends to qualified onshore institutions. They cannot serve as the primary treasury rail for the PRC entity, cannot receive FDI capital, and cannot support export settlement with customs verification. Feasibility rating: Low. The Hong Kong parent may use an offshore EMI as a non-bank cash overlay outside PRC perimeter, but all RMB and foreign-currency flows belonging to the WFOE must pass through a domestic designated FX bank.

Turnover: Under Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) ss.405-408, every Hong Kong company must appoint a certified public accountant to audit its financial statements unless the company is dormant; no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount threshold provides an audit exemption. Under PRC Company Law and Foreign Investment Law, every wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) must engage a licensed PRC certified public accounting firm to audit its annual financial statements; again, the mandate is entity-wide and not subject to any RMB asset, revenue, or employee-size threshold. For the hotpot restaurant overseas chain, internal/group quantitative triggers such as total assets above RMB 50 million, annual operating revenue above RMB 50 million, or 300 or more employees may additionally trigger consolidated IFRS/PRC GAAP audit requirements for financing, banking covenant, or cross-border reporting purposes, but these do not displace the WFOE's statutory audit mandate.
