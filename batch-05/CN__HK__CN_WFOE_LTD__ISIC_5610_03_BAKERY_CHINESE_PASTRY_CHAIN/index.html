Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None (no statutory minimum for an ordinary WFOE). The shareholder must subscribe all shares in the Articles of Association and, under the 2023 Company Law, the paid-in capital must be fully contributed within five years from the issuance of the Business License. The contribution must be received into the WFOE's bank account from the Hong Kong shareholder's account; if contributed in foreign currency, it must be settled through an authorized bank capital account. No statutory blocked escrow account is required for this bakery/central kitchen WFOE, but the contribution record must be publicly reported through the enterprise credit information system.

Civil defense clearance is required if the registered site or warehouse includes underground spaces subject to civil defense protection obligations. The WFOE must obtain occupational safety approval under the Work Safety Law for industrial mixer guarding, flour handling dust explosion mitigation, oven gas/fume zoning, ventilation controls, personal protective equipment and food-handler health certificates. Employees in the central kitchen must complete fire safety and food hygiene training before operations begin.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: the Electronic Tax Administration (e-tax) platform (www.chinatax.gov.cn, with unified provincial e-tax logins).

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the PRC WFOE is assigned an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) by the State Administration for Market Regulation; the USCC is the statutory TIN and VAT ID. The competent tax authority completes tax registration automatically using SAMR data. If automatic registration is incomplete, the legal representative or authorized agent must finalize tax registration within 30 days through the e-tax platform or the competent tax service hall, presenting the business license, articles of association, foreign investment information report, director/legal representative identification, and Hong Kong parent company incorporation certificate. The tax authority then issues the Tax Registration Status Notice in the e-tax account. Non-resident foreign entities without a permanent establishment in China do not receive a TIN; the withholding payer must use the WFOE's USCC and file the non-resident's contract details and payments through the statutory withholding returns in the e-tax system.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all taxpayers supplying taxable goods or services must obtain a tax registration regardless of turnover level, with small-scale simplified rate applicable below the threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from the PRC customs territory and sold to overseas buyers are VAT zero-rated (0%), with input VAT refund, provided the exporter is a registered VAT general taxpayer, holds customs export/foreign trade operating status, the goods physically leave PRC customs territory, export proceeds are repatriated through official banking channels, and the export declaration matches the sales/proceeds documents. For the bakery/pastry chain, qualifying exported goods include finished pastry products declared under the correct export tariff classification, and may also include central-kitchen cold-chain goods sold to overseas franchisees on an FOB/ex-works basis. Cross-border digital/offshore services (e.g., master franchise know-how, trademark royalties) may be zero-rated only where the beneficiary is outside China and the services are consumed outside China; documentary evidence must demonstrate the offshore recipient and actual use abroad.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form (customs electronic data)
  • Bill of Lading (or Air Waybill / Express Waybill, as applicable)
  • Commercial Invoice and Export Packing List
  • Export Sales Contract / Order Confirmation
  • Bank receipt evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas buyer (SAFE/CBFT foreign exchange monitoring record, including FIRC-style remittance confirmation where required)
  • VAT special invoices for taxable purchases of ingredients and production inputs used to manufacture the exported goods

China operates a nationwide Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (Full Digital E-Invoice) system under the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure. VAT invoices must be issued from the authorized e-tax/e-invoice platform; issuance data is transmitted in real time to the STA. Taxpayers already on the Full Digital E-Invoice scheme do not use traditional physical tax-control invoices; each digital invoice carries a unique verification code, invoice number, and the issuer's USCC, enabling real-time anti-fraud digital stamping and automatic classification control. The e-invoice platform provides APIs for enterprise software/ERP third-party integration, subject to STA technical accreditation. All VAT deduction, red-letter credit notes, and export rebate invoices must clear through this digital ecosystem.

Claims of export zero-rating are subject to full desk-based and field audit by the STA, SAFE, and Customs. If supporting documentation is missing, mismatched, or proves a fraudulent/overclaimed refund, the tax authority will deny the refund, reverse and claw back the input VAT already refunded, charge late-payment surcharges and statutory penalties on the underpaid tax, and may suspend or revoke export tax-refund qualifications. Fraudulent export VAT-refund claims are criminalized under PRC anti-smuggling and Criminal Law; responsible officers may face imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of the amount involved; the tax authority may also publish the entity as a serious-tax-violation taxpayer.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a bifurcated convertibility framework. Current account items, including trade in goods, service fees, and royalties, are generally fully convertible, provided that a genuine underlying transaction is documented and a SAFE-designated bank handles the payment. Capital account items, however, remain heavily regulated. Foreign direct investment into the WFOE requires completion of FDI foreign exchange registration through a SAFE-approved bank, followed by deposit of the inbound equity remittance into a dedicated capital account (capital settlement account) before conversion into RMB for permitted business use. Profit repatriation by the WFOE to its Hong Kong parent demands an audited Chinese statutory financial statement, a board resolution on dividend distribution, and a corporate income tax withholding payment certificate, with the distribution executed through the current account after a so-called 'tax clearance' representation to the remitting bank. Repatriation of paid-in capital upon liquidation or reduction requires a separate SAFE exit registration and a winding-up verification report. The rampant but legal use of voluntary settlement (意愿结汇) means there is no forced surrender, yet all FX transactions must be traceable to real economic activities, and any attempt to bypass these rules will trigger immediate bank compliance suspension and PBOC anti-money laundering inquiries.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE regime, no mandatory surrender ratio applies. Export proceeds earned by the WFOE must be repatriated to China and credited to a foreign exchange settlement account within the statutory collection period, generally not exceeding 90 days from the date of customs export declaration, unless a commercial contract specifies a longer period or prior SAFE approval is obtained for offshore retention. The enterprise retains full discretion to convert the foreign currency into RMB through a designated bank (意愿结汇) or to hold the currencies in the settlement account for future valid current account payments, but onward use must remain within the registered business scope and comply with the Negative List for Market Access.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as the designated enforcement agency

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Feasibility is very low for pure offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., neobanks, digital EMI licenses, or BaaS providers). Under PRC law, only domestically licensed commercial banks approved by PBOC and SAFE can open RMB basic deposit accounts, foreign exchange settlement accounts, or FDI capital accounts for a WFOE. Non-bank fintech entities are barred from servicing residents for foreign exchange purposes, cannot provide custody, and cannot clear RMB trade payments without a banking partner. Cross-border EMIs may offer auxiliary treasury management dashboards or reporting tools, but they cannot legally act as the sole account provider for a Chinese WFOE. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely on traditional Tier-1 domestic banks for all regulatory accounts; EMIs may be used only for non-core, outbound-side support invoicing in Hong Kong after the funds have legally exited China.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong parent must be remitted via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-designated capital settlement account at its chosen Tier-1 bank. The wire instructions and remittance advice must identify the transaction as 'Foreign Direct Investment - Equity Injection' and use the SAFE foreign exchange purpose code 6210 (Investment of foreign equity). The bank will verify the FDI registration certificate, the business license, and the authenticity of the subscription. Upon receipt, the bank converts the foreign currency to RMB under the payment-in-kind principle only after the WFOE submits a genuine payment application for permitted operational expenses (e.g., rent, salaries, equipment). For capital verification, a PRC certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report confirming the contribution has been credited and is held in the designated capital account. No advance outbound disbursement may be made from the capital account prior to verification.For outbound current account payments such as master franchise royalties, license fees, or imported baking equipment purchases, the WFOE must route through SWIFT MT103 for service items and use Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) or Documentary Collections for cross-border goods transactions. All outbound wires must be submitted via the bank's corporate internet banking or at the branch with an FX payment application form, underlying contract, commercial invoice, tax withholding certificate (for royalties and services), customs declaration (for goods), and accurate SAFE purpose codes such as 'Royalties and License Fees' for intangible services. Settlement fails if the supporting documents show a mismatch with either the contract value or the business scope. For goods trade, the WFOE can also use cross-border RMB settlement directly, bypassing FX conversion, but the counterparty must consent. Liquidity buffers must be maintained in the foreign exchange settlement account for quick outbound royalty payments to avoid forced conversion.Real-demand-backed RMB forward contracts (远期结售汇) for current account receipts and payments, available at all Tier-1 PRC banks with a 365-day typical maturity.Foreign currency forward swaps and interest rate swaps on external debts and foreign currency loans to manage cash-flow repricing risk, subject to SAFE real-need verification.FX option combinations for business customers approved by SAFE, provided the notional aligns with underlying trade or capital account obligations.Onshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are strictly prohibited; offshore NDF hedges are not permitted for a PRC WFOE without a physical outbound leg.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local Files are mandatory when annual related-party tangible asset transactions exceed RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 20 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the multinational group's aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion (or when the group otherwise satisfies STA Public Notice [2016] No. 42 thresholds).

Economic substance scope: For the Chinese WFOE, the Enterprise Income Tax Law requires economic substance and real business purpose; the STA can re-characterise transactions under the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Article 47) if the arrangement lacks commercial rationale. For the Hong Kong intermediate holding company, the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance requires a real and substantive business presence, and the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test evaluates where strategic, sales, HR, and management functions are performed. The Hong Kong entity must carry out active management in Hong Kong to maintain its tax resident status and qualify for DTA benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / False Tax Return Filing

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, the tax authority may recover the underpaid tax and impose a fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; interest accrues on the overdue tax. If the amount evaded is relatively large and accounts for a high percentage of tax payable, Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes criminal liability on the enterprise and responsible persons.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced on a showing of fraud or abuse of corporate personality: directors and senior officers become jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and civil damages. Criminal prosecutions can lead to fixed-term imprisonment of up to three years for relatively large evasion, or three to seven years for extremely large evasion, plus fines. Directors are also placed on the State's tax dishonesty blacklist, resulting in travel bans, credit restrictions, and disqualification from board positions.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance / Missing Documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 47 (and STA Public Notice [2016] No. 42), the tax authority may make an adjustment to re-determine taxable income on an arm's length basis and impose a special tax adjustment interest of at least the base lending rate plus 5 percentage points for the adjustment period. The adjustment can be made retroactively for up to 10 years, and a late-payment penalty under Article 48 of the Tax Administration Law may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly participated in falsifying intercompany pricing may be personally liable for restitution of the underpaid tax, subjected to a travel ban as part of the enforcement blacklisting of tax evaders, and criminally prosecuted for the underlying tax fraud if criminal thresholds are met, leading to imprisonment for up to seven years and asset seizure.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Withholding Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: China's General Anti-Abuse Rule (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47) permits the tax authority to disregard the conduit and deny the treaty benefit, recharacterising the payment as a dividend subject to 10% withholding (or 20% for individual look-through). Additional interest at the base rate plus 5 percentage points under special tax adjustment rules, and a fine under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law for underpayment, will be imposed.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable for the shortfall of withholding tax as both arrangement-makers and beneficiaries. Civil asset restoration orders can attach personal bank accounts; the State may impose travel bans and blacklist the directors individually. Willful participation in the abusive scheme is a criminal offence under the Criminal Law Articles 201 and 210, with penalties ranging from fines to imprisonment up to seven years, and the corporate veil is pierced in order to recover the tax.
