Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; taxpayer-facing services are conducted via provincial portals of the China Electronic Tax Bureau, accessible through the official STA website at www.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

The CN WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise and therefore its statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on its Business License issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). There is no separate TIN registration certificate. The mandatory procedure is as follows: (1) incorporate the WFOE with the provincial SAMR and obtain the Business License; (2) tax registration is automatically completed through the 'one license, one code' information-sharing mechanism between SAMR and the tax authority, with the USCC serving as the enterprise tax ID; (3) within 30 days of license issuance, the entity must log in to the China Electronic Tax Bureau to complete post-registration verification, confirm the taxpayer type, register the legal representative's credentials, and set up VAT invoice quota; (4) if the WFOE will directly export goods or services, it must separately obtain a customs registration code via the International Trade Single Window and complete the foreign trade operator filing with the local commerce authority; (5) where the Hong Kong parent (an offshore non-resident enterprise) derives China-source income from the WFOE, it may apply for non-resident enterprise tax registration through a withholding agent and submit a Hong Kong tax residency certificate to claim benefits under the Mainland and Hong Kong Closer Economic Partnership Arrangement (CEPA); (6) the WFOE should also register its payroll and social insurance arisings through the administrative human resources and social security platform for withholding income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no economic turnover threshold for basic VAT registration; any entity holding a business license must complete tax registration within 30 days. However, annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory registration as a General VAT Taxpayer, requiring standard VAT rates to be charged and input VAT to be creditable. Enterprises with annual turnover not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may remain Small-Scale VAT Taxpayers and pay VAT at the statutory levy rate (currently 3% or 1% where applicable), with monthly sales up to RMB 100,000 exempt from VAT.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT Law and the Administrative Measures for the Refund (Exemption) of VAT on Export Goods and Services, zero-rating is available for (a) goods that are exported from China and legally cleared by customs, and (b) a narrow list of cross-border services supplied to non-resident enterprises and wholly consumed outside China, including certain offshore information technology, licencing and consulting services. The ghost kitchen's underlying onshore catering and food-delivery services are subject to VAT at 6% and are not zero-rated. If the operator exports packaged food products as goods, the sale of goods qualifies for zero-rating upon proper customs clearance. Commission income earned from offshore delivery platforms such as UberEats or DoorDash may potentially qualify as zero-rated cross-border information technology services if the service is rendered to an overseas enterprise, the consideration is received in foreign currency, and the service is not used or consumed within China, but the burden of proof is on the taxpayer and the tax authorities scrutinise such claims strictly.

  • Bill of Lading (ocean or air waybill) issued by a licensed carrier evidencing physical shipment for goods exports
  • Foreign Exchange Income Declaration Form (bank receipt voucher showing inward remittance of convertible foreign currency)

China operates a mandatory nationwide electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance system under the Golden Tax Phase IV infrastructure. VAT invoices must be issued via the China Electronic Tax Bureau's Fully Digital Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform, either through the government-furnished free invoicing software or compliant API integrations. Every invoice is transmitted in real time to the central tax database, assigned a unique 20-digit invoice number and an anti-counterfeit QR code, and subjected to automatic risk control and audit-matching. There is no separate fiscal stamping; instead, the FDEI platform performs instantaneous tax-control validation and invoice status verification. The system supports fully digital e-invoices and eliminates paper invoice requirements, while special VAT invoices used for input deduction must be uploaded to the VAT Invoice Comprehensive Service Platform for authentication.

Export zero-rating is verified through electronic cross-matching of customs declaration data, foreign exchange receipt data and VAT invoice data under the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Any mismatch or missing document results in an unqualified export declaration and the tax authority will typically suspend or deny the refund. If the exporter fails to repatriate foreign exchange within the statutory 180-day period or submits false declarations, the tax authority will retroactively claw back the zero-rated refund, impose a daily overdue surcharge of 0.05% on the unpaid tax, and levy a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax amount involved under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration. In cases involving fraudulent export tax refunds, criminal liability arises under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties ranging from fixed-term imprisonment to life imprisonment, plus confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a strictly managed and partially convertible currency regime. Renminbi is convertible on current account transactions for qualified enterprise flows after the bank performs verifiable authenticity checks, but capital account transactions remain subject to SAFE registration, approval or quota mechanisms. For a Hong Kong-invested WFOE operating ISIC 5610 ghost kitchens, local RMB collections from delivery platforms do not require separate SAFE approval, but any use of funds abroad and any repatriation of profits to Hong Kong require documentation. Dividends are treated as current account remittances only after the WFOE completes annual statutory audit under Chinese GAAP, files its corporate income tax returns, approves the profit-distribution resolution, and withholds 10% dividend tax subject to possible reduction to 5% if the Hong Kong parent qualifies as a beneficial owner under the Mainland/Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement. Capital contributions from HK must be recorded via SAFE's Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration, received in the designated foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-designated bank, and settled into RMB after authenticity verification. There is no mandatory surrender ratio for export proceeds at collection date; enterprises may retain 100% of foreign exchange earnings in current accounts, but receipts must be repatriated and reconciled with customs declarations and trade monitoring under the ASOne system. The principal hurdle for profit remittance is a bank's demand for accounting reports, tax payment proof, and evidence that undistributed prior-year losses are fully offset.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For goods-trade export receipts, the PRC has adopted a repatriation with provisional reporting rule rather than forced conversion. The exporter must repatriate export proceeds through a designated bank within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration unless SAFE has approved an extension for receivables financing or long-lived supply chains. The mandatory conversion ratio is zero; however, 100% of the export proceeds may be retained in a foreign exchange current account or settled into RMB at the client's discretion, and any balance retained beyond the bank's prescribed limit must be flagged to SAFE through the trade monitoring system. On conversion, the exporter provides the customs declaration, export invoice, packing list and transaction code in the bank's ASOne interface.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity injections from the Hong Kong parent shall be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 or MT202 into the SAFE-registered FDI capital account at the designated Chinese bank. The remittance must carry the standardized SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment paid-in capital, the SAFE FDI registration number, and the exact WFOE legal name in both Chinese and English. The receiving bank will screen the routing against the FDI registration, conduct a funds-origin check, settle the foreign exchange into RMB using the onshore spot rate, and record the transaction in the ASOne system. After settlement, a certified public accountant issues a capital verification report confirming that the contribution has been paid in accordance with the registered nominal timetable; the company seal register must evidence the respective board approval and the enterprise must keep the verification report for subsequent dividend repatriation and tax filings.Clearing rails for trade settlements include SWIFT MT103 for high-value open account payments to overseas suppliers, commercial letters of credit at sight or usance for structural procurement financing of kitchen equipment and packaging, and documentary collections for intra-group supplies requiring proof of delivery before payment. All outbound remittances require authentic underlying documents such as purchase contracts, commercial invoices, packing lists, customs declarations and the SAFE payment purpose code. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viability Rating: Low (not legally feasible). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required for related-party transactions exceeding: RMB 200 million for tangible goods transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (SAT Bulletin No. 42/2016). Master File required when aggregate annual related-party turnover exceeds RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Under PRC law, general anti-avoidance rules (GAAR) in EIT Law Art. 47 and CFC rules in Art. 45 permit the tax authorities to recharacterize entities lacking commercial substance. The Chinese WFOE must demonstrate real operational substance, including actual kitchen facilities, employees, and decision-making in China, as its place of effective management; the Hong Kong holding company also must satisfy Hong Kong Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) if it conducts relevant business (equity holding usually benefits from reduced substance tests).

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion – Transfer Pricing Manipulation

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Administration Law (TAL) Article 63, this constitutes tax evasion: assessment of the underpaid tax, 0.05% daily late interest (TAL Art. 32), and a fine of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax amount; criminal liability under Criminal Law Art. 201 for tax evasion where the amount is substantial.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or endorse such false pricing are 'directly responsible personnel' and face criminal imprisonment (up to 7 years for major evasion) and fines; civil liability extends to restitution of the tax loss from the company's assets; may be personally joint and severally liable if the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Art. 20 for abusive control.
Withholding Tax – Failure to Withhold on Outbound Dividends/Royalties

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 69: the tax authority will recover the deficient tax from the payee and impose a penalty on the withholding agent equal to 50% to 300% of the un-withheld tax; late-payment interest also accrues. If the failure is intentional to facilitate evasion, criminal liability may attach.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers who formally authorize the gross distribution without withholding may be subject to administrative fines and, in egregious cases, criminal liability as abettors to tax evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201; personal assets may be reachable if they caused the company's liability through willful misconduct.
Shell Company – Fabricated Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Art. 47 (GAAR), the tax authorities may re-develop the transaction to align with economic substance, increase taxable income, and assess interest plus late-payment penalties. Hong Kong ESR Act imposes fines and potential prosecution for a company that fails the economic substance test; Chinese tax authorities may deem the HK entity as having its place of effective management in China, subjecting it to 25% corporate tax on worldwide income.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE and HK shell may be subject to blacklisting (exit bans), asset freezes, and personal restitution for facilitating the abusive scheme; if the arrangement is characterized as fraudulent, imprisonment for tax evasion under Criminal Law Art. 201 applies; under PRC Company Law Art. 20, shareholders abusing limited liability may face unlimited personal liability for company debts.
