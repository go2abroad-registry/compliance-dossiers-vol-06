Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the electronic tax portal 'Chinatax' – National Electronic Tax Administration Bureau (electronic tax bureau, e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn; for Hong Kong SAR matters, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong and the Hong Kong SAR Customs and Excise Department for Dutiable Commodities and export-related controls.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in China must complete the following statutory procedure: (1) business entity incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its local counterpart, which triggers a unified Social Credit Code (USCC) automatically used for tax registration; (2) obtain the tax registration record certificate (Tax Registration Certificate) as part of the 'multi-license integration' under the 'one license, one code' reform; (3) register the entity's tax status with the local State Taxation Administration office for both enterprise income tax (EIT) and value-added tax (VAT) by filing the Tax Registration Form (Report on Basic Taxpayer Information) within 30 days of business license issuance; (4) designate a legal representative, financial officer, and tax agent in China; (5) complete the 'real-name tax filing system' for the legal representative and financial officer via the electronic tax bureau; (6) apply for VAT general taxpayer status if projected annual turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 or elect such status voluntarily; (7) apply for the official VAT taxpayer identification number (the same as the USCC) and complete system activation through the e-Tax platform, including bank account filing for tax payment via the 'Taxpayer-Bank-Enterprise' tripartite agreement; (8) for Hong Kong SAR, a WFOE that is not a Hong Kong tax resident has no automatic Hong Kong tax registration requirement unless it carries on a trade, profession, or business in Hong Kong; if such economic nexus exists, the entity must apply for a Business Registration Certificate (BRC) under the Business Registration Ordinance and, where required, an IRD File Number under the Inland Revenue Ordinance. For pure cross-border export catering services, China VAT zero-rating or exemption is substantiated through export-related filing, not Hong Kong IRD registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not have a check-the-box regime comparable to the United States. A WFOE is a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at 25% standard rate. However, statutory elections and designations include: (1) VAT general taxpayer vs. small-scale taxpayer election for eligible businesses subject to annual turnover thresholds; (2) election for the Small Low-Profit Enterprise preferential tax regime (reduced 20% rate with current preferential reductions for qualifying small low-profit enterprises); (3) Hong Kong territorial-source election only to the extent an entity is a Hong Kong tax resident; foreign-sourced income may be excluded from Hong Kong profits tax on territorial basis; (4) hybrid mismatch elections are not applicable; China's anti-avoidance rules and general anti-avoidance provisions address hybrid mismatch outcomes under the OECD BEPS Inclusive Framework; (5) a qualifying WFOE may elect to apply preferential tax treatments such as High and New Technology Enterprise status (15% EIT rate) if engaged in qualifying R&D; (6) for indirect tax purposes, exporters may elect to use the 'export tax rebate (export VAT refund)' system for input VAT refund on zero-rated or exempt exported goods. State 'None' only where no analogous election mechanism exists under Chinese law.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating eligibility applies to goods and certain services exported outside the customs territory of the People's Republic of China. For an in-flight catering kitchen physically located in a China customs-supervised bonded zone or airport bonded warehouse, meals delivered to departing international flights qualify for VAT zero-rating or exemption and input-VAT refund, provided title in the goods passes to the overseas airline customer and the goods physically leave Chinese customs territory. The catering supplies must be identified as export goods, not consumed within China's customs territory. Domestic in-flight catering for domestic routes, and meals consumed within bonded premises without leaving the territory, are not zero-rated and remain taxable at the standard rate (13%) or applicable reduced rates. Qualifying cross-border services may also be zero-rated under the cross-border service VAT rules (e.g., services supplied to overseas clients and consumed wholly outside China), but physical aircraft catering is principally goods export. Zero rating is subject to full documentary proof and STA verification; the Export Tax Rebate (export VAT refund) claim is made through the electronic tax bureau after the export customs declaration is submitted.

China operates the national Unified Electronic Invoice Service Platform administered by the STA. VAT taxpayers must issue Special VAT Invoices via the 'Golden Tax' system (Golden Tax IV phase digital anti-fraud architecture). Fully digitalized electronic invoices have real-time reporting, mandatory secure electronic invoicing through the e-Tax API interfaces, and use national encrypted digital stamping. Paper invoices, if used, are printed via tax-controlled equipment. E-invoices must carry the statutory invoice code, invoice number, and encrypted anti-counterfeit code. Full invoice data is transmitted in near real-time; high-risk taxpayers may face mandatory invoice-to-invoice intelligent matching and automated exception reporting. For airline in-flight catering, VAT-inclusive invoices must be issued to airlines for meal services; export sales from bonded catering kitchens are subject to customs export procedures and electronic clearance through the China International Trade Single Window.

The STA and customs authorities conduct rigorous post-export verification requiring full traceability of export documents, payment receipts, logistics chain, and input VAT invoices. If foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days (or approved extended period), or if documentation is incomplete, fabricated, or inconsistent, the zero-rated treatment is denied, the enterprise must reverse the VAT refund paid, pay underpaid VAT, and remit overdue tax; late payment penalties accrue at 0.05% per day and an administrative fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax may be imposed for fraudulent claims. Cases involving false customs declarations, forged invoices, or fraudulent export rebate claims trigger criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law for the crime of fraudulent export tax refund (骗取出口退税罪) and smuggling-related offenses, imprisonment, confiscation, and blacklisting under the tax credit system ('serious untrustworthy tax offenders'), plus revocation of preferential export treatment.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime. Current account items, including trade in goods and services, are generally convertible for resident enterprises, provided that transactions are genuine, documented and reported through the SAFE monitoring system. Capital account items, however, remain subject to substantive supervisory approval or registration, including foreign direct investment inflows, cross-border financing, securities investment and cross-border guarantees. Inbound capital for a WFOE must be declared via the Foreign Exchange Registration System and deposited in a dedicated capital account at a local bank; after bank verification it can be converted into RMB for permitted operating purposes. Profit remittances by a WFOE to its Hong Kong parent are treated as current account dividends, but banks must see post-tax audited financial statements, shareholder resolution declaring dividends, and proof that corporate income tax and dividend withholding tax have been settled. There are no mandatory surrender ratios for export proceeds today, but the proceeds must be repatriated into China within the statutory deadline and kept in an export foreign exchange settlement account or sold to a licensed bank. Conversion from RMB back to original currency for onward remittance is restricted unless the payment qualifies under current account genuine transaction rules. Overall, convertibility is partial and asymmetrical: trade flows are free, while capital movement remains heavily documented and permission-driven.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE rules, export receipts must be repatriated to China within the latest statutory filing deadline, normally by the final day of the month following the export customs declaration or within 90 days depending on the bank's trade credit classification, and the enterprise must submit export proceeds verification reports through the regulators' electronic system. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; a WFOE may retain qualified foreign currency proceeds in a foreign exchange settlement account or sell them to a domestic licensed bank for RMB, provided the underlying export transactions are genuine, document-matched and within the approved trade credit balance.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound FDI wires must be sent from the Hong Kong parent to the WFOE's dedicated capital account through a correspondent bank using SWIFT, with the payment message referencing the SAFE FDI registration code and stating the foreign exchange purpose code for foreign direct investment. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when annual related-party transactions of tangible property (e.g., food supplies, aircraft meal equipment) exceed RMB 400 million; related-party intangible property, financing, and financial asset transactions exceed RMB 100 million; or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File is mandatory where the multinational group's aggregate related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion or the group revenue threshold for Country-by-Country Reporting is met; separate annual related-party transaction declaration schedules are required regardless of thresholds when related-party relationships exist.

Economic substance scope: China is not an offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) jurisdiction, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and beneficial ownership rules under SAT Announcement 2018 No.9 require the WFOE and the Hong Kong shareholder to demonstrate real economic substance. The inflight catering WFOE must show actual airport kitchen licences, cold-chain plant, staff payroll, procurement contracts, production records, ramp delivery logs, and operating risk assumption; the Hong Kong shareholder claiming DTA benefits must be more than a mailbox, with real Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) such as investment decisions, dividend policy, funding oversight, and legitimate treasury functions performed in Hong Kong. A pure conduit arrangement will be recharacterized and treaty relief denied.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Accounting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 empowers the tax authority to pursue unpaid tax and late surcharge and impose a fine from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Where evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and constitutes a deliberately false tax return, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes a criminal fine and imprisonment for up to 3 years for ordinary evasion, or up to 7 years for serious evasion involving huge amounts.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law and the Civil Code if directors use the limited liability entity as an instrument of fraud, intermingle personal and corporate funds, or misappropriate assets. Directors may be jointly and severally liable for the State's unpaid tax, face personal asset attachment, be placed on the credit blacklist (discredited judgment debtor blacklisting), receive travel bans and exit restrictions from border authorities, and be barred from leaving China; criminal convictions may result in imprisonment of the legal representative and responsible directors.
Abusive Tax Treaty Shopping and False DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will deny treaty relief under SAT Announcement 2015 No.60 and SAT Announcement 2018 No.9, collecting the additional 5% dividend withholding with late-payment interest and penalties. Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47) the transaction may be recharacterized, resulting in a significant tax adjustment. A false or fraudulent claim to treaty benefits may trigger a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid and criminal liability for tax fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations or who allow the Hong Kong company to operate without substance may be held personally liable for the withholding shortfall when the corporate form is abused, and may suffer conduct disqualification, blacklisting, exit bans, and travel restrictions. Hong Kong and PRC authorities may cross-report on shell company practices; a director of the conduit may face personal tax assessments in Hong Kong and criminal charges if false tax returns are used to obtain treaty benefits.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation, Thin Capitalization, and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44, 46, and 48 allow the tax authority to make a transfer pricing adjustment and to charge interest on the resulting underpaid tax at the central bank benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points. Thin-capitalized interest in excess of the amount an independent enterprise would pay is disallowed. Failure to document related-party transactions under the relevant SAT Public Notice may expose the company to fines up to RMB 10,000, and where the mispricing is found to be fraudulent or conceal income, penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus criminal liability for tax evasion may apply.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve false transfer pricing policies, set derivatives or loans to strip Chinese profits, or conceal intercompany contracts may be personally liable for the WFOE's unpaid tax if the entity is left with no collectible assets. Courts can pierce the corporate veil when directors use the corporation to perpetrate tax fraud. The director may be blacklisted, prohibited from exiting China, ordered to repatriate assets, and prosecuted criminally; senior executives may also be subject to competent tax authority penalties for obstructing investigation and falsifying records.
