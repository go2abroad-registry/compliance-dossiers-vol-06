Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China - official electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online system.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is automatically synchronised as the legal Tax Identification Number (TIN) for all PRC tax purposes (corporate income tax, VAT, and customs). Within 30 days of issuing the business licence, the WFOE must confirm its complete tax registration record with the competent local tax office via the e-Tax portal. A foreign non-resident shareholder or parent company does not require a separate PRC TIN unless it establishes a permanent establishment in mainland China; for China-sourced passive income, the WFOE acts as withholding agent and must file a withholding return through the e-Tax system. If the non-resident entity directly conducts taxable activities in China, it must apply for a non-resident tax identification code in the form designated by the STA (no generic or invented form number is cited here).

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (approximately USD 700,000) requires general VAT taxpayer registration; non-resident enterprises conducting taxable supplies in China must register irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Export of goods (e.g., packaged loose-leaf tea) by a general VAT taxpayer qualifies for 0% VAT and export rebate when goods are physically exported from mainland China, the export sale is bona fide, sales proceeds are repatriated in foreign currency within the statutory period, and the export declaration is accepted by China Customs. Qualifying cross-border services provided by the WFOE to overseas recipients may be zero-rated or exempt under the cross-border service list. Sales made at the specialty tea salon to customers within mainland China remain subject to the standard VAT rate for catering services.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form (stamped by China Customs)
  • Bill of Lading (sea) or Airway Bill (air) evidencing departure from Chinese customs territory
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas buyer
  • Packing list
  • Export sales contract
  • Bank foreign exchange receipt voucher or export collection verification certificate evidencing repatriated foreign exchange

China mandates the use of the official Digitalized Electronic Invoice (fapiao) system. VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's online e-invoice platform, transmitted in near-real-time to the central tax database. Business taxpayers use the special VAT invoice verification platform to authenticate digital stamps; invoices with declared authenticity are cleared. The phased nationwide rollout of fully digitalised e-invoices with anti-fraud digital stamping is mandatory for registered VAT taxpayers.

The State Taxation Administration and China Customs jointly audit export declarations, refund claims, and foreign exchange receipt records. If zero-rating or export VAT refunds are claimed on false, unverified, or deliberately fraudulent documentation, the tax authority shall recover the rebated amount plus interest and impose administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times the amount wrongfully obtained under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Intentional fraud may trigger criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law's fraudulent export refund provisions, with penalties ranging from a fine to life imprisonment when the aggregate illegal refund equals or exceeds RMB 2.5 million. Additionally, the WFOE may be downgraded in tax credit rating, subjected to enhanced monitoring, and barred from simplified refund procedures for a prescribed period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with differentiated convertibility between current account and capital account transactions. Current account items are broadly convertible: a registered Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise may buy or sell foreign currency for trade in goods, services, dividends, royalties, salaries, and other bona fide operating needs upon submission of authentic transaction documents to a designated domestic bank. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled. Foreign direct investment equity injections, shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, repayment of offshore borrowings, and outward investments must be registered with SAFE and conducted through designated domestic banks using specific foreign exchange purpose codes. Export proceeds in goods trade must be repatriated to China and reported through the SAFE Trade Monitoring System; however, there is no mandatory fixed surrender ratio, and qualified enterprises may retain foreign currency in current account export proceeds accounts or voluntarily convert into RMB according to their treasury needs. Profit remittance is permissible only after audited financial statements, corporate income tax final settlement, value-added tax filings, and foreign exchange registration records are verified by the custodian bank. Practical hurdles include negative list licensing, source of funds verification for capital injections, matching of invoices with contracting and customs records, and strict anti-money-laundering scrutiny of any remittance whose declared purpose is inconsistent with the entity's tea salon operating model. Thus, current account convertibility is operationally accessible, while capital account and dividend remittance flows require layered documentary defense and SAFE-compliant banking channels.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE goods trade administration, export foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated to China and collected by the declaring exporter through a designated bank. Banks verify each collection against export customs declarations and contracts. There is no statutory universal conversion ratio, but proceeds not held in an approved export foreign exchange account must be settled into RMB. Export receivables or payables exceeding the standard 90-day trade related collection period must be registered with SAFE's Trade Monitoring System; advance receipts, delayed receipts, and offset arrangements require underlying contracts, bank statements, customs declarations, and written explanations. The 'who exports, who receives, who settles' matching principle is strictly applied to prevent routing funds through unrelated parties.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Unviable for mainland clearing and custody. Offshore digital EMI platforms, Hong Kong virtual banks, and fintech payment firms without a mainland PRC banking license are legally barred from opening CNY basic deposit accounts or foreign exchange capital accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. PBOC and SAFE recognize only licensed domestic commercial banks as custodians and FX settlement banks. A Hong Kong fintech EMI may provide ancillary treasury dashboarding or cross-border payment notification tools, but it cannot clear RMB through CNAPS, receive export proceeds, or hold the WFOE's statutory capital account.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

The annual return and final settlement for the calendar year must be filed within 5 months after year-end (i.e., by 31 May); quarterly provisional returns must be filed and tax prepaid within 15 days after the close of each quarter.
