Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), including its provincial and municipal offices; online electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax) at https://tpass.etax.chinatax.gov.cn

E-government filing portal

A foreign investor incorporating a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must first complete business registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the One-Stop integrated enterprise registration platform. Upon issuance of the Business License, SAMR automatically transfers the enterprise filing data to the STA, which triggers an ex officio tax registration. The WFOE then enters the National Electronic Tax Service Platform using the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory TIN and VAT registration number, and completes the taxpayer profile including legal representative identity, financial institution account, accounting basis, invoicing limits, and applicable VAT tax categories. No separate paper TIN certificate is issued. A foreign non-resident parent that does not maintain a permanent establishment in China but derives China-source income (such as royalties or service fees) must separately apply for Non-Resident Enterprise Temporary Tax Registration through the competent tax authority, furnishing the incorporation certificate, the underlying contract, and evidence of an appointed tax withholding agent.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating applies to physical goods exported out of the PRC customs territory when the exporter completes customs declaration, obtains the electronic export declaration, collects the foreign exchange proceeds through a licensed bank within the prescribed SAFE period, and submits the VAT refund and zero-rating return on the National Electronic Tax Service Platform. For cross-border digital services such as the digital magazine portal and sponsored media placements, zero-rating applies when the recipient is an overseas entity or individual, the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, payment is received from outside China in foreign exchange, and the supply is within the zero-rated cross-border service catalogue jointly published by the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration. If a part of the digital magazine content or advertising targets users physically present in mainland China, that portion must be allocated and taxed at the domestic 6% rate; only the offshore-consumed portion qualifies for 0%.

  • China Customs electronic Export Customs Declaration with customs clearance number through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document for physical exports; for digital services, a contractual supply record and service completion certificate
  • Commercial invoice and signed contract or purchase order clearly identifying the overseas buyer or recipient and stipulating the 0% VAT treatment
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) issued by a Chinese bank, or a SWIFT MT103 credit advice, evidencing receipt of foreign-currency payment from the overseas customer
  • Overseas consumption evidence for zero-rated digital services: IP geolocation logs, server access records, subscriber registration data, and advertising delivery reports demonstrating complete offshore use
  • VAT zero-rated export refund schedule and SAFE foreign-exchange verification record as prescribed by the State Taxation Administration

China mandates issuance of full digital electronic invoices through the National Electronic Tax Service Platform and the Golden Tax Phase IV system. Invoices are cleared in real time; each statutory invoice carries a unique invoice code and must be transmitted electronically to the STA central database before delivery. All newly registered enterprises are enrolled in the full-digital e-invoice pilot and must issue VAT special or ordinary invoices using the official platform or an STA-certified API integration. The anti-fraud tax control device previously required for traditional invoice software has been replaced by cloud-based real-time digital stamping for full-digital e-invoices; tax-only invoicing software without real-time STA clearance cannot be used for business-to-business VAT special invoices.

The tax authority is entitled to conduct post-clearance audits of every zero-rated export refund. If the supporting documentation is incomplete or inconsistent, the zero-rating will be reversed and VAT assessed at the applicable domestic rate (6% for digital services, 9% for electronic publications or 13% for goods), plus daily late payment surcharge of 0.05% from the original due date. Fraudulently claimed zero-rating or export refunds attract a civil penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Cases constituting criminal tax fraud or false export refund are prosecuted under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, with penalties ranging from heavy fines to imprisonment up to life; the WFOE and its responsible legal representative are jointly and severally liable. The normal orderly clawback period is five tax years, extended to ten years in cases of intentional concealment or evasion.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, RMB is fully convertible for current-account items—trade in services, subscriptions, advertising, and ordinary dividends—but capital-account items remain regulated. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must register its inward FDI with SAFE, obtain a unique FDI code, and open a foreign-currency capital account at a domestic licensed bank. Inbound equity contributions may be converted into RMB only through the bank after the authenticity and conformity of the proposed use is verified. The WFOE cannot freely convert RMB back into foreign currency for general investment or lending without a separate capital-account registration. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is allowed only after the annual financial statements have been audited by a PRC CPA, corporate income tax has been finally settled, and a withholding tax certificate has been issued; the remittance is then executed by the Chinese bank as a current-account dividend payment. The dividend payment is not subject to a fixed conversion quota, but banks will require the tax clearance certificate, board resolution, audited accounts, and the SAFE business registration number. In practice, the WFOE should maintain a dedicated dividend-clearing dossier to avoid multi-week delays, because each bank also applies an internal compliance review over the source of profits.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The mandatory foreign-exchange surrender regime for current-account receipts has been abolished. There is no mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain export receipts in foreign currency or settle them into RMB at a licensed bank. For export of physical goods (for example printed magazines), the proceeds must be repatriated to China generally within 180 days from the Customs declaration date. For service-trade exports such as digital magazine subscriptions and sponsored media, the receipt should be repatriated within the payment date set out in the commercial contract; absent a specific contract date, the invoice period is used and banks will normally expect the inward remittance to be filed in the SAFE Trade in Services platform within 30 days of the due date. All proceeds must be supported by contract, invoice, proof of service delivery, and a BOP transaction code assigned by the receiving bank.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Non-viable as a regulated treasury account. Digital EMIs such as Stripe and Airwallex are not PRC-licensed domestic banks and cannot receive FDI equity capital, complete SAFE capital-account registration, or issue tax-recognised banking documents. They may be used only as customer-facing payment collection gateways for Stripe or AdSense payouts, with settlement ultimately channelled into the WFOE's RMB or foreign-currency account at a PBOC-licensed commercial bank. Legally, an EMI cannot substitute for the domestic basic deposit account or the capital account, and it will fail the Chinese tax authority's requirement that bank receipts be verifiable through the SAFE surveillance system.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent instructs its bank to issue a SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's foreign-currency capital account at a PBOC-licensed Chinese bank. The optional/additive remittance information field must include the WFOE's SAFE FDI code and state the purpose as 'Registered Capital Injection'. The receiving Chinese bank will debit the incoming foreign currency, perform a BOP declaration under SAFE's direct-investment code, and, once authenticity is verified, permit conversion to RMB for permitted operating uses. A PRC CPA must then issue a capital verification report after checking the bank statement and the book entry, and this report is required by the SAMR for the final FDI registration. Funds in the capital account cannot be used for prohibited purposes such as spot cross-lending, secondary-market securities, or off-balance-sheet guarantees unless separately registered.For cross-border royalty, content-licensing, advertising-technology, and editorial-collaboration payments, the WFOE uses SWIFT MT103 from its domestic account after converting RMB into the required currency under SAFE's current-account rules. The bank requires the commercial contract, the vendor invoice, proof that the service was received, and a tax withholding certificate showing that non-resident enterprise income tax and VAT have been paid. For physical printed magazine merchandise sold to foreign distributors, documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable; a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) is reserved for bulk procurement when the supplier requires a bank undertaking. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable as a regulated treasury account. Digital EMIs such as Stripe and Airwallex are not PRC-licensed domestic banks and cannot receive FDI equity capital, complete SAFE capital-account registration, or issue tax-recognised banking documents. They may be used only as customer-facing payment collection gateways for Stripe or AdSense payouts, with settlement ultimately channelled into the WFOE's RMB or foreign-currency account at a PBOC-licensed commercial bank. Legally, an EMI cannot substitute for the domestic basic deposit account or the capital account, and it will fail the Chinese tax authority's requirement that bank receipts be verifiable through the SAFE surveillance system.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare a Local File when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset or financial asset transactions, or RMB 40 million for other service transactions. A Master File is required when the multinational group exceeds the statutory consolidated revenue threshold of RMB 2 billion and has cross-border related-party transactions exceeding RMB 100 million. All WFOEs with reportable related-party balances must file the annual related-party transaction reporting schedules with the enterprise income tax return.

Economic substance scope: The Hong Kong parent is subject to the Hong Kong Economic Substance Requirements if it is a pure equity holding company; OECD Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in Hong Kong with adequate staff, premises, board meetings, corporate records, and investment management decisions. For the PRC WFOE, the State Taxation Administration applies the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and beneficial-ownership doctrine to deny treaty relief or recharacterise transactions where economic substance is absent. A failure of substance in the HK parent will cause the 5% treaty dividend rate to be rejected and expose the arrangement to a 10% final withholding assessment plus penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Transfer Pricing Artificially Shifting PRC Profits

Statutory sanction: The State Taxation Administration may make transfer-pricing adjustments under Articles 41 and 48 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law. Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China imposes imprisonment and fines where tax evasion is serious, typically where evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve sham invoices or unilateral pricing policies may have the corporate veil pierced under Article 20 of the PRC Company Law, exposing personal assets to restitution. Directors may be placed on the State Taxation Administration serious-breach blacklist, subjected to exit bans, denied cross-border travel, and criminally prosecuted with imprisonment of up to seven years for aggravated tax evasion.
Shell Company and Double Tax Arrangement Abuse

Statutory sanction: The PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 120 of its implementing regulations permits recharacterisation of the abusive arrangement, denial of treaty benefits, and assessment of the full 10% dividend withholding tax with late-payment interest. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Ordinance further imposes penalties for incorrect claims of tax residency or shell status.

Director personal liability: Directors who establish or maintain an artificial HK holding structure to abuse the treaty may be personally liable for restored taxes to the extent of distributions made while the company was insolvent. Courts may impose director disqualification orders, asset freezing orders, travel bans, and criminal liability under anti-avoidance and fraudulent-trading provisions.
AML and Cross-Border Payment Fraud

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes fines and confiscation for failure to perform customer due diligence and transaction monitoring. Article 39 of the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations imposes penalties of up to five times the illegal foreign exchange amount. Criminal liability may arise under Articles 191 and 312 of the Criminal Law for money laundering, with penalties including imprisonment up to ten years and fines.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior compliance personnel who deliberately operate undisclosed payment rails face individual criminal prosecution, personal asset confiscation, passport revocation, entry bans, and blacklisting of personal bank accounts. The corporate veil may be pierced where directors commingle personal and corporate funds or direct fraudulent payment routing for the purpose of evading Chinese foreign exchange controls and tax.
