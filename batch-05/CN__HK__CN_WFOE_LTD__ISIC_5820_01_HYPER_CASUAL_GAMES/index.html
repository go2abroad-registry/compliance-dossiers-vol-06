Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through provincial, municipal and district tax service bureaus; the national online service portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax Bureau) of the State Taxation Administration, accessible on the official STA web platform (www.chinatax.gov.cn).

E-government filing portal

For the PRC-established Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the statutory Tax Identification Number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) shown on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); tax registration is completed automatically through the multi-license joint registration data-sharing mechanism, followed by online tax-type confirmation, legal representative and finance-officer filing, bank account reporting, and accounting-standard declaration via the E-Tax Bureau within 30 days of establishment. The WFOE must then determine its VAT taxpayer identity: small-scale taxpayer (annual taxable sales at or below RMB 5,000,000) or general VAT taxpayer (mandatory above that threshold or voluntary election). For the Hong Kong non-resident parent, no PRC TIN is required unless it directly derives PRC-sourced income through a taxable presence; if dividend or other China-source payments are made to the Hong Kong parent, the Hong Kong tax residence certificate issued by the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong SAR (along with beneficial-owner declaration) is used to support reduced treaty withholding under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement. If a foreign non-resident enterprise is itself required to register for PRC tax purposes, it must register with the STA through the E-Tax Bureau as a non-resident enterprise taxpayer and obtain a PRC taxpayer identification number before filing and paying China-source income tax and VAT withholding.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (annual taxable sales) is the mandatory threshold for general VAT taxpayer status; below this threshold a Chinese small-scale taxpayer rate applies, although voluntary general taxpayer registration is available. A foreign company with no PRC establishment does not have a registration threshold for supplies consumed in China; instead, the PRC buyer or payer withholds VAT at source unless the overseas supplier voluntarily registers for cross-border e-commerce VAT settlement under STA practice.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

0% VAT on exported goods applies when the goods are declared to customs, physically leave the customs territory of China, and export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC bank account in accordance with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange rules. 0% VAT export treatment for qualifying cross-border services applies to services provided to an overseas entity and fully consumed outside China, including software and information-technology services, advertising services, and related digital services supplied by the WFOE to overseas ad networks such as AdMob and AppLovin, under the cross-border taxable services zero-rating and exemption provisions of the VAT pilot framework (including the cross-border services catalogue and the STA management measures for zero-rated services tax refunds or exemptions). The business must prove the foreign residence of the service recipient, the delivery and performance of the service outside China, and the receipt of payment in foreign exchange.

  • Goods export: Customs Export Declaration (electronic export declaration filed through the China International Trade Single Window), original or electronic Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, commercial invoice, packing list, and the export contract.
  • Goods export foreign-exchange record: Bank inward remittance certificate or bank foreign-exchange settlement and repatriation record (equivalent to Inward Remittance Certificate, FIRC) evidencing receipt and settlement of export proceeds within the statutory deadline.
  • Cross-border services zero-rating file: Cross-border service agreement with the non-resident ad network, PRC VAT special or general invoice or zero-rated export invoice, bank remittance certificate for the service fees, certificate of Hong Kong or other non-resident tax status of the service recipient, and service-performance logs (user geolocation, ad-serving reports) proving the advertising services were consumed outside the Chinese customs and tax territory.
  • Export refund or exemption application package: Software copyright registration file where required, software product assessment report where an exported software product refund is claimed, and the export tax refund or zero-rating credit declaration submitted through the E-Tax Bureau.

All VAT-invoicing taxpayers must use the national E-Tax Bureau electronic invoicing system with a tax UKey or the fully digitalized electronic invoice (all-digital electronic invoice) module; each invoice is generated with a unique invoice code and number and digitally signed before transmission to the State Taxation Administration's national VAT invoice system. General VAT taxpayers must maintain a tax-control device, issue VAT special or general invoices in real time, and submit invoice data during the prescribed filing cycles; the authority conducts automated real-time data matching and anti-fraud digital stamping of invoice information. API-based e-invoicing is available for enterprises with high-volume invoicing through approved electronic invoice service platforms integrated with the STA, while all-digital electronic invoices eliminate the physical invoicing seal requirement and are cleared in the STA's invoice database.

Misapplied export zero-rating, false customs declarations, forged Bills of Lading, or fabricated foreign-exchange remittance records expose the WFOE to retrospective clawback of the previously zero-rated or refunded VAT, recovery of underpaid tax plus daily late-payment interest at 0.05%, and administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded under the Tax Collection Administration Law of the People's Republic of China. Where the conduct is classified as fraudulent export tax refund, the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China applies, and convictions may result in confiscation of proceeds, fines, confiscation of property, and criminal imprisonment for the responsible persons. The State Administration of Foreign Exchange may additionally impose penalties for non-repatriation of export proceeds or false foreign-exchange documentation, and the tax authority may disqualify the taxpayer from future export VAT refund eligibility and audit the prior three to five years' filings, with no time limitation in cases of fraudulent concealment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed, partially convertible foreign exchange framework. Current-account revenue, including export sales, software royalties, and ad-network settlements, is generally convertible after a licensed bank performs a genuine transaction review. Capital-account flows are not freely convertible: inbound equity, shareholder loans, guarantees, and outward remittances require State Administration of Foreign Exchange registration or approval, dedicated capital accounts, and post-transaction reporting. The renminbi is deliverable onshore at the CNY rate and offshore in Hong Kong at the CNH rate; a WFOE must keep its operating foreign currency receipts onshore and route settlement through a licensed PRC bank. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is lawful only when supported by audited financial statements, a board resolution, income tax settlement certificates, and SAFE-monitored bank documents. Material deviation between declared ad revenue and bank deposits triggers regulatory review. The entity therefore has no free convertibility for speculative capital movements, and current-account convertibility remains conditional on document authenticity. The Hong Kong side is not subject to China's FX controls, but is only an offshore clearing node, not a substitute for onshore SAFE compliance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The old compulsory surrender ratio has been abolished. A compliant WFOE may retain 100 percent of export and service-trade foreign currency in a domestic FX settlement account or voluntarily convert it to RMB. However, the proceeds must be repatriated to a qualified domestic bank account by the contractually agreed payment date and reported in the SAFE trade monitoring system. For goods trade, unreceived export proceeds beyond 90 days after the declared export date require special anomaly reporting unless a valid extension has been registered; for service trade, ad-network payouts follow the platform payment schedule, but overdue receipts beyond 180 days must be explained with contract and reconciliation evidence. Banks apply a real-need verification to every settlement, requiring contracts, invoices, and a transaction code declaration. The mandatory sale ratio is nil; conversion is at enterprise discretion.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulates the offshore CNH clearing node

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: LOW and legally inappropriate for the onshore WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC/SAFE and cannot operate a RMB deposit account, FX settlement, or FDI capital account for a Mainland China entity. Their cross-border collection tools would expose the WFOE to illegal third-party settlement charges and undermine bank trust. The Hong Kong parent may use reputable EMIs as an ancillary collection layer for USD/HKD ad-platform payouts only before netting the treasury into the onshore WFOE; this does not satisfy PRC treasury or SAFE requirements. All onshore liquidity must be cleared through a Tier-1 domestic bank.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: There are no quantitative turnover, total-asset or employee thresholds that waive audit for a non-dormant Hong Kong company. Every company incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) must appoint a certified public accountant as auditor and have its financial statements audited, unless it is dormant and validly claims exemption under section 373. A private company may qualify as a small private company under section 359D if it satisfies at least two of three tests: annual revenue not exceeding HKD 100 million, total assets not exceeding HKD 100 million, and employees not exceeding 100. That status permits simplified financial reporting under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance but does not eliminate the statutory audit obligation. Dormant exemption is available only when the company has passed a dormant resolution, filed the prescribed declaration with the Companies Registry, and has not entered into any pecuniary transaction during the exemption period.
