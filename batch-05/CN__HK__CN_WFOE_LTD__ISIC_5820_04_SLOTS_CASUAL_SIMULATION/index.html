Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; tax registration, VAT, and e-invoicing are administered through the national STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online taxpayer portal operated by the STA and its provincial-level tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

The foreign non-resident investor first obtains the WFOE’s business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) or its local counterpart; the license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the mandatory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identity for the WFOE. Within 30 days after the business license is issued, the WFOE must complete tax registration at the competent local STA tax authority by filing the taxpayer registration application, articles of association, investment and shareholder documents, registered address proof, legal representative identification, finance officer/agent authorization, bank account details, and anticipated business scope. The STA branch activates the electronic tax registration, issues the tax registration/TIN record in the national tax system, classifies the WFOE as a VAT taxpayer, determines small-scale or general taxpayer status, sets up invoice/electronic-invoice quotas, and registers withholding obligations for cross-border payments. A foreign non-resident investor without a permanent establishment in China is not issued a separate Chinese TIN merely by incorporating the WFOE; if the foreign investor has direct PRC-source income subject to PRC withholding tax, a non-resident tax registration/withholding record may be established through the payer WFOE’s tax file.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on an export, the WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer and either (i) physically export goods—including software stored on tangible media—out of PRC customs territory and file an electronic Customs Export Declaration with the General Administration of Customs, or (ii) supply a cross-border taxable service expressly listed in the PRC VAT zero-rating scope. The counterparty must be located outside China and the service/goods must be used and consumed wholly outside China; consideration must be received from outside China; and the WFOE must retain a complete audit chain from contract, order and invoice to customs release, foreign-exchange receipt and technical consumption logs. For the purely online delivery of social casino / virtual slots game software or game services, the applicable cross-border VAT relief is typically VAT exemption (not 0% output VAT with refund) under the cross-border software/information technology services exemption category, unless physical software media are exported through customs or another listed zero-rated service applies; the documentation requirements below substantiate either relief.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export goods) with electronic release data from the General Administration of Customs of China—mandatory for physical software/goods exports; not applicable to pure digital downloads delivered through app stores.
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international consignment note proving physical exit from China and delivery to the overseas buyer—mandatory for the physical export route.
  • Digitally delivered service evidence package: executed game/distribution agreement with the offshore app-store operator or end customer, platform settlement statements from Apple App Store / Google Play, server logs, user geolocation/consumption records, and digital delivery receipts showing consumption outside China.
  • Cross-border foreign-exchange receipt voucher and SAFE (State Administration of Foreign Exchange) monitoring record evidencing collection of the export proceeds in China, functionally equivalent to the traditional Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC); the bank-issued cross-border receipt/declaration record is required for all export routes.
  • Export contract or service agreement, valid VAT export-commercial invoice or e-invoice, and proof of payment via Apple IAP / Google Play Billing or direct offshore wire transfer, with reconciliation to the WFOE’s tax and customs filings.

The WFOE must issue invoices through the STA’s national fully digitalized electronic invoice (e-invoice) platform integrated with the Electronic Tax Bureau; invoice data, including issuance, rejection/red-letter invoices, and credit notes, is transmitted in real time to the STA/Golden Tax information network. VAT returns, input credit matching, anti-fraud tax-control digital signatures, invoice quotas, and automated cross-checks with customs export declarations and bank foreign-exchange receipts are all processed through that platform. No paper-based pre-printed invoice may be used for this export-oriented digital business unless authorised by the competent STA branch.

Export zero-rating and cross-border VAT relief are subject to post-clearance audit by STA and Customs using the Golden Tax and customs data platforms. If documentation is missing, inconsistent or falsified, the WFOE must repay the zero-rated/exempt VAT, pay daily interest of 0.05% on the underpaid tax from the date it was due, and may be assessed penalties under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection ranging from 50% to five times the amount of tax underpaid. Fraudulent claims—including false customs declarations, fake app-store settlement evidence, or unrepatriated foreign-exchange receipts—trigger retrospective clawback of all claimed refunds, revocation of zero-rating status, and criminal liability for export-tax-rebate fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a managed floating RMB exchange rate regime in which the current account is largely convertible, while the capital account remains regulated and subject to SAFE approvals, registrations, and information reporting. For a WFOE, current account receipts and payments such as software royalties, licensing fees, service fees, and distributable profits can be settled through domestic banks after verification of genuine transaction documents. Capital account flows, including the HK parent's equity injection, external loans, and exchange of equity against foreign currency, must be routed through SAFE-registered capital accounts or cross-border RMB mechanisms. The WFOE's registered capital from the Hong Kong investor must enter via a SAFE Foreign Direct Investment registration-linked capital account, after which funds can be converted into RMB for permitted business expenditure against underlying contracts. Profit and dividend remittance to the HK shareholder is permitted as a current account item, but the bank and SAFE require audited financial statements, tax clearance or withholding tax payment certificates, a board resolution approving distribution, and compliance with the real-need principle. The practical convertibility friction is documentary rather than absolute: mismatched remittance references, missing SAFE codes, nominee ownership, and the 'no real trade' red flag will cause rejection or prolonged compliance review.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory export surrender requirements. A WFOE may retain 100% of export-generated foreign currency in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert it freely into RMB at a licensed bank. SAFE operates a trade-in-goods monitoring system under which export proceeds should be collected and repatriated into the domestic banking system within the statutory verification window, generally 90 days after the customs declaration date, with extended timelines requiring a justified SAFE registration. There is no forced conversion ratio; the enterprise may choose the timing and amount of conversion as long as the underlying trade transaction, customs declaration, and collection record are consistent.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers are not viable as the WFOE's primary banker. PBOC and SAFE require corporate foreign exchange settlement, capital account custody, and current account clearing to be conducted through licensed domestic banks. Non-bank fintech EMIs are legally barred from opening statutory settlement accounts or performing mandatory regulatory reporting on behalf of a PRC entity. Their only permissible ancillary role is as a payment processor collecting app-store or e-wallet inflows into a segregated client trust account ultimately held at a licensed bank; all KYC, AML, FX, and tax compliance must remain with the domestic bank and the WFOE.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap 622), every non-dormant Hong Kong company is mandatorily subject to statutory audit regardless of balance sheet size, turnover, or number of employees. There are no quantitative balance sheet, turnover, or headcount thresholds that exempt a Hong Kong private company from audit. The only audit exemption available is the dormant company exemption: a private company may pass a special resolution not to appoint an auditor only if it has no significant accounting transactions during the financial year, apart from incorporation fees, share allotment fees, registry fees, or statutory penalties, and it remains dormant during that period. Registered non-Hong Kong companies under Part 16 may also be exempt only if they meet specified private company conditions and obtain an exemption certificate.
