Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) via the National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, together with the local Administration for Market Regulation and the local tax service office where the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise is domiciled.

E-government filing portal

A non-resident investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must first obtain the business license and Unified Social Credit Code from the local Administration for Market Regulation. Within 30 days after issuance of the business license, the WFOE must complete taxpayer registration on the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal, submitting the Unified Social Credit Code, incorporation certificate, articles of association, legal representative identity proof, office establishment proof, accounting system documentation, and foreign-investment filing records. Upon approval, the STA activates the taxpayer account and assigns a taxpayer identification number that is identical to the Unified Social Credit Code; this number simultaneously serves as the VAT registration identifier for general or small-scale VAT taxpayer purposes. A foreign non-resident legal entity that has not formed a WFOE but has a permanent establishment, place of business, or otherwise derives China-source income must complete non-resident tax registration through the same e-Tax portal and obtain a separate Chinese Taxpayer Identification Number in the statutory non-resident tax registration module.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer; taxpayers below this threshold are treated as small-scale VAT taxpayers and may elect to register as general VAT taxpayers under statutory conditions.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods exported from mainland China may qualify for the VAT zero-rating and export rebate regime when the export transaction is genuine, customs-cleared, and the foreign exchange is duly received and repatriated to China. Qualifying cross-border digital services supplied to wholly offshore recipients, including software services and esports tournament platform services that are consumed entirely outside China, can also qualify for zero-rating or exemption where the service recipient is a non-resident enterprise or individual, the service is not generated from domestic consumption, and the payment is received from outside China. The taxpayer must retain documentary evidence proving the offshore destination, offshore consumption, and actual receipt of foreign currency.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form accepted by Chinese customs
  • Commercial Invoice issued by the exporting WFOE
  • Packing List
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Export sales contract or order
  • Foreign inward remittance certificate or equivalent bank/SAFE electronic foreign exchange receipt record
  • Statement or certificate evidencing offshore consumption for qualifying cross-border digital services

China operates a mandatory national electronic invoice control system administered by the STA. All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA-approved full digital electronic invoice infrastructure, with real-time transmission of invoice data to the national tax control database before delivery to the customer. The system assigns a unique digital invoice identity code and applies automated anti-fraud validation, risk screening, and blocking controls. Input VAT deduction is permitted only on verified and legally issued electronic invoices obtained through the statutory e-invoice platforms and APIs; offline or non-conforming invoices cannot support input VAT claims and may trigger fiscal stamps, tax risk flags, or taxpayer lockouts.

The STA and customs authorities conduct joint audit and risk-verification reviews of export zero-rating claims. If the claimed export is later found to be unsupported, fraudulent, or not in compliance with foreign exchange repatriation requirements, the tax authority retrospectively cancels the zero-rating, claws back any VAT exemption or refund granted, imposes a late-payment surcharge, and levies an administrative penalty of 50 percent to 500 percent of the tax involved. Criminal liability, including fines and imprisonment, may also arise under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law for fraudulent export tax refund schemes or intentional false export declarations.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a split current/capital account regime. Current account transactions are generally convertible and may be settled through designated foreign exchange banks, provided the authenticity and legality of the underlying trade in goods or services is established; the bank verifies invoices, contracts, customs declarations, or service certificates using SAFE's digital verification systems. Capital account items, however, remain heavily regulated: FDI equity contributions must be registered with SAFE, deposited into a dedicated capital account at a licensed domestic bank, and cannot be deployed until the bank completes a post-approval authenticity review of the intended use. RMB settlement after such capital injection is permitted for wages, procurement, rental, and other operating expenditures, but sweeping cross-border sweeps, intercompany financing, or repayment of foreign debt require separate macro-prudential registration. Profit remittance is possible only after the annual audit is completed, corporate income tax returns are settled, and the board resolution plus shareholder distribution minutes are presented; banks also check that no undistributed profits are used to repay external loans. In short, the RMB is not freely convertible, and all material outbound flows require transaction-level documentation and regulatory clearance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. Under the current SAFE regime, there is no fixed mandatory conversion percentage; the WFOE may retain 100% of verified export proceeds in a domestic foreign exchange account or voluntarily convert the full amount to RMB through a licensed bank at the market rate. Failure to repatriate or verify by day 180 triggers SAFE scrutiny, inclusion on the abnormal list, and 100% transaction-by-transaction verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) as its delegated foreign-exchange regulator

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: annual related-party transactions exceeding RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. Master File threshold: the group has total related-party transactions exceeding RMB 1 billion or the ultimate parent meets the SAT consolidated revenue and comparability threshold under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42. Even below these thresholds, the tax authority may demand transfer pricing documentation where the WFOE's profit margin falls outside the interquartile range of reliable comparables.

Economic substance scope: The PRC WFOE must maintain substance in China because China taxes worldwide income and the core income-generating activities of the esports platform, including software development, matchmaking algorithms, tournament operations, payment processing, user data management and platform risk control, must be exercised by WFOE employees, assets and management in China. For the Hong Kong parent to obtain DTA benefits, it must also satisfy the PRC beneficial-ownership criteria and demonstrate real Hong Kong economic substance; a shell Hong Kong affiliate with no CIGA will not obtain treaty relief. Hong Kong ESR concepts are relevant only through the IRD's tax-residency and substance questions, but the anti-abuse and substance-over-form doctrines under PRC law and the China-HK DTA cannot be avoided merely by contracting through a Hong Kong intermediary.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Contemporaneous Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 authorizes a special tax adjustment; Article 44 permits deemed income assessment where documentation is missing; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax plus a 0.05% daily surcharge; SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 imposes contemporaneous documentation obligations; late or incomplete documentation triggers administrative penalties under Article 62.

Director personal liability: If the arrangement abuses the separate legal personality of the WFOE, courts may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law and hold directors jointly and severally liable to the extent of their personal assets; tax authorities may apply exit restrictions and travel bans under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44 against the legal representative and finance director; if directors knowingly fabricate service contracts and false invoices, Criminal Law Article 201 punishes tax evasion with up to 3 years imprisonment for relatively large amounts and 3-7 years for particularly large amounts and high evasion ratios, with fines and confiscation.
Dividend WHT Evasion and DTA Relief Abuse

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 37 imposes withholding at source; Article 39 makes the PRC payer liable for uncollected withholding tax; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes a fine of 50%-300% of the unwithheld amount on the withholding agent; SAT Bulletin 2018 No. 9 and EIT Law Article 47 deny treaty benefits and permit recharacterization of the distribution; daily late surcharges of 0.05% and public blacklisting also apply; foreign exchange authorities may impose penalties for false remittance documents.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign dividend resolutions and false beneficial-ownership declarations risk civil liability for restitution of the tax loss, freezing of personal bank accounts, and court orders to repatriate assets; exit bans apply to the legal representative and senior managers under the tax enforcement framework; if the false treaty claim is used intentionally to evade a large tax obligation, Criminal Law Article 201 attaches with imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines equal to 1-5 times the evaded tax; directors may also face Hong Kong criminal exposure if they make false statements in the TRC application.
AML, Economic Substance and Shell Company Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes corrective orders, confiscation of illegal gains, and fines up to RMB 2 million on institutions and RMB 50,000-500,000 on directly responsible personnel; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with imprisonment of up to 7 years in serious circumstances; foreign-exchange regulations impose penalties of 0.5%-5 times the illegal outward remittance; EIT Law Articles 37 and 47 require withholding on the true economic character of the payment and disregard sham contracts; treaty anti-abuse rules strip tax benefits from shell entities.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the Hong Kong shareholder and the PRC WFOE are managed as one economic unit with no board independence, no capital substance and no separate records; directors are personally liable for civil damages, unpaid taxes and foreign-exchange penalties; travel bans and asset freezes are available against directors involved in shell formation and cross-border fraud; criminal exposure includes money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191 and fraud if payment contracts are false, with imprisonment of up to 7 years plus monetary fines and blacklisting preventing the director from serving in any new Chinese enterprise.
