Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online filing and registration are performed through the STA's official electronic tax portal (Electronic Taxpayer Service Hall, e-Tax) at the local competent STA office for the registered address of the WFOE.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the PRC taxpayer identification number and VAT registration number. Step 2: Complete tax registration with the competent STA local office through the multi-department one-window data-sharing system within the statutory period after SAMR issuance; no separate tax registration certificate is issued because tax registration is integrated into the USCC. Step 3: File the WFOE's legal representative, finance officer, registered address, registered capital, bank accounts, expected business scope and taxable activities in the taxpayer profile. Step 4: Make the VAT taxpayer-type election (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) within the statutory timing; general VAT taxpayer status is mandatory once projected annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000. Step 5: For the Hong Kong parent company, no PRC tax registration number is issued unless the non-resident enterprise derives China-source income or establishes a taxable presence in the PRC; where withholding applies, the WFOE must complete withholding registration and declaration, and the HK parent may claim benefits under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement by furnishing its Hong Kong tax residence certificate and supporting materials to the competent STA office.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities with a PRC business license must complete VAT registration on incorporation; general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Small-scale taxpayers with monthly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 100,000 or quarterly taxable sales not exceeding RMB 300,000 are eligible for temporary VAT exemption while that policy remains in force.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT export zero-rating and export rebate apply to goods physically exported from the PRC customs territory, declared under the China Customs export declaration system, and paid for with foreign exchange collected within the statutory period. Services: export zero-rating or cross-border VAT exemption applies to qualifying offshore services supplied to a party outside the PRC, paid by that foreign party, and wholly consumed outside the PRC. For the vertical micro-drama streaming business, revenue received through offshore app stores (Apple IAP, Google Play Billing and Stripe) may be treated as exported digital/cultural services where the app consumer's billing address and IP/device evidence place consumption outside mainland China, the funds are received from outside the PRC, and no in-app services, advertising or rights exploitation are performed for PRC users. The WFOE must retain a complete documentary chain proving offshore supply, offshore consumption and foreign exchange receipt; if the service category is not one of the zero-rated exports, the WFOE must instead claim the cross-border VAT exemption, which does not allow refund of input VAT.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration Form issued by China Customs (required for physical export; for digital service export, replaced by platform transaction records and app-store settlement reports)
  • Commercial invoice, packing list and export sales contract or purchase order
  • Bill of Lading / Air Waybill / international express consignment note evidencing physical departure from PRC customs territory
  • Foreign exchange receipt voucher or bank settlement certificate from a PRC bank showing collection of export/service proceeds within the repatriation deadline
  • Export invoice issued through the STA invoice system and, where applicable, the VAT export refund declaration and verification records in the tax portal

All VAT invoices must be issued through the STA's unified electronic invoice service platform integrated with the Golden Tax System. Special VAT invoices, ordinary VAT invoices and fully digitalized electronic invoices must be transmitted to the tax authority in real time for authenticity verification and are protected by unique invoice codes, invoice numbers and anti-fraud verification features. Input VAT credit is allowed only on the basis of a compliant VAT invoice. The system conducts near real-time matching of invoice issuance, sales data and tax filings; anomalies, cloned numbers or false invoices trigger automatic risk alerts and tax authority inspection.

The STA and local tax authorities will verify the entire export document chain, customs status and foreign exchange collection record before accepting zero-rating or refund. Failure to remit export proceeds within the 180-day deadline, inability to match the customs declaration with bank receipts, or failure to prove offshore consumption will result in retrospective clawback of the VAT refund and recharacterization of the export as a domestic taxable supply at the applicable VAT rate (6% for services or 13% for goods), plus late payment surcharge at 0.05% per day. Fraudulent export invoices, forged customs or transport documents, false refund declarations and foreign-exchange violations also expose the WFOE and its responsible officers to administrative penalties of 0.5 to 5 times the falsely refunded or underpaid tax, suspension of export rebate eligibility, penalties from the State Administration of Foreign Exchange, and criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refund and tax evasion under the PRC Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate system with restricted capital-account convertibility. Current-account transactions are generally convertible after documentary verification through designated foreign-exchange banks, which enables trade receipts, service fees, and royalties to be settled in RMB or retained in foreign currency accounts. Capital-account items, including equity injections, shareholder loans, and dividend repatriation from a WFOE to its Hong Kong parent, remain subject to SAFE registration, account restrictions, and post-verification checks. FDI capital inflows must enter through a SAFE-registered capital settlement account and can only be used within the WFOE's registered business scope; capital-account fund transfers to related parties are scrutinized. Profit remittance is permitted only after annual audit, corporate income tax final settlement, withholding tax settlement on dividends, and submission of board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, and bank reporting. The government can suspend conversion in abnormal cross-border flows and requires genuine underlying transactions. The WFOE must therefore separate current-account receipts from capital-account funds and maintain full documentary trails to avoid administrative penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds; companies may retain export foreign currency in domestic foreign-exchange accounts or voluntarily settle into CNY at the spot market rate. However, export proceeds must be repatriated to China through approved banking channels and verified under SAFE digital monitoring, generally within 180 days from the export declaration date, unless SAFE grants an extension for genuine trade or collection delays.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms generally have low cross-border viability for this WFOE. They are not licensed as banks by the PBOC or CBIRC and cannot act as the primary FX settlement bank or provide capital-account services, SAFE registration, or RMB clearing for a domestic WFOE. Some licensed non-bank payment institutions may assist with collecting global app-store revenues through contractual channels, but they cannot replace a tier-1 bank for capital injection, statutory capital verification, corporate income tax, or dividend repatriation. Therefore, the blueprint must rely on physical Chinese commercial banks as the legal clearing backbone.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every Hong Kong private company must prepare audited financial statements unless it qualifies as a small private company or a small private company group. A private company qualifies for audit exemption for a financial year if it satisfies at least two of the following three criteria in both the current and immediately preceding financial year (or on a consolidated basis for the group, where applicable): (a) total revenue not exceeding HKD 20,000,000 (approximately USD 2,560,000); (b) total assets not exceeding HKD 10,000,000 (approximately USD 1,280,000); and (c) not more than 50 employees. A mandatory independent certified CPA audit is triggered when the company fails to satisfy at least two of the three criteria, where the group thresholds are not met, where the company is a public company, listed company, bank, insurer, stock exchange participant or other regulated entity, where members holding at least 5% of the voting rights demand an audit, or where the company has a statutory obligation under another enactment to present audited accounts. The audit must be performed by an independent certified public accountant registered with the Accounting and Financial Reporting Council (AFRC).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory dividend withholding tax on outbound profit distributions paid by the PRC WFOE to its non-resident HK parent. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42, contemporaneous transfer pricing Local File documentation is mandatory when annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 400 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; and RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is required when total related-party transactions in the group reach the statutory threshold or when the WFOE is part of a multinational group with consolidated group revenue above RMB 5 billion, subject to the Public Notice's omission rules.

Economic substance scope: The PRC WFOE is not itself subject to offshore Economic Substance Regulations, but the HK parent must satisfy Hong Kong's Economic Substance Requirements under the Inland Revenue Ordinance where the HK entity performs pure equity holding, financing, distribution, or related functions; in particular, the HK entity must prove adequate employees, premises, direction and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. For PRC EIT purposes, the STA can apply general anti-avoidance under Article 47 and the beneficial ownership provisions of Notice 2018 No. 9 to disregard shell or conduit vehicles that lack commercial substance and re-attribute income and withholding liability to the substantive Chinese entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Cost Invoicing

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law imposes recovery of underpaid tax, daily late surcharge, and a monetary fine of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax. Where intentional evasion is criminal, Article 201 of the Criminal Law applies, with fines and imprisonment thresholds measured by the amount and ratio of evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed falsified invoices or directed the fraud may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax under veil-piercing doctrines; personal assets may be subject to civil restitution orders; exit restrictions/travel bans and blacklisting can be imposed; imprisonment of up to 7 years applies in aggravated evasion cases.
Abusive Treaty Shopping and Beneficial Ownership Denial

Statutory sanction: The STA may deny DTA benefits under Article 47 EIT Law, general anti-abuse principles and Notice 2018 No. 9; apply a look-through recharacterization; impose the full statutory 10% WHT; add a 0.05% daily surcharge; and impose administrative penalties of 50% to 5 times short-paid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who certified false beneficial ownership documentation are personally exposed to civil restitution, joint liability for the unpaid withholding tax, travel bans/blacklisting, and criminal tax evasion prosecution if the underpayment is material.
Shell Company and Abusive Intercompany Financing

Statutory sanction: Thin-capitalization rules under EIT Law Article 46 and SAT Notice 2009 No. 2 disallow interest above the 2:1 debt/equity ratio; transfer pricing adjustments under Article 41 repricing rules add deemed interest, deemed dividends, 10% WHT (or 7% if treaty is still accepted), surcharges and a fine of 50% to 5 times the tax deficiency.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial controllers who executed the sham loan and failed to document arm's length pricing may face personal civil liability, rigorous exit bans and blacklisting, plus criminal liability under Article 201 where deliberate underpayment crosses criminal thresholds.
