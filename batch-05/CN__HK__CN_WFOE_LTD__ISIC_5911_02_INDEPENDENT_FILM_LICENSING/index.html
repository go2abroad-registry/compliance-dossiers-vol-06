Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation. The CN_WFOE_LTD is a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, with independent legal personality and limited liability. It is subject to PRC enterprise income tax at the standard 25% rate on worldwide income and is not a pass-through entity. Profit distributions to the Hong Kong non-resident shareholder are PRC-source dividends subject to withholding tax at 10%, which may be reduced to 5% under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement if the Hong Kong shareholder satisfies beneficial ownership and anti-abuse substance conditions. For PRC VAT, the WFOE is a VAT taxpayer and is classified as a small-scale taxpayer unless it registers, voluntarily or mandatorily, as a general VAT taxpayer.

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a U.S.-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch entity election; legal form determines tax residence and classification. The only material classification choice is the VAT taxpayer type election: an entity with annual taxable sales below RMB 5 million may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer if it has sound accounting and a fixed place of business, and once registered as a general taxpayer it may not ordinarily revert to small-scale status. There is no statutory election to treat the WFOE as a transparent entity for PRC enterprise income tax purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales is the mandatory economic turnover threshold for registration as a general VAT taxpayer; below RMB 5 million the WFOE remains a small-scale VAT taxpayer. There is no separate economic threshold for the initial VAT registration of an incorporated enterprise because the WFOE automatically becomes a VAT taxpayer upon incorporation and tax registration. For ISIC 5911 independent film copyright distribution and licensing, the applicable mainland China VAT rate is 6% unless the cross-border zero-rating conditions are met.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Cai Shui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4 and STA Announcement No. 29 of 2016, zero-rating applies to cross-border supplies of services provided to overseas entities where the services are wholly consumed outside China, including international transportation, research and development, design, software, transfer of technology, and broadcasting and film/TV programme production and distribution services. A WFOE engaged in independent film international copyright distribution and licensing may claim the 0% VAT rate only where the transaction is properly characterised as cross-border broadcasting/film programme distribution or licensing services to a non-resident recipient, the licensed rights are wholly exploited outside China, the licensing agreement and payment flows evidence delivery and consumption outside China, and the WFOE retains the required source documents. If the transaction is characterised as a domestic transfer of copyright or a licence that is partly exploited within China, the supply falls outside the zero-rating list and is taxable at 6% or potentially exempt under applicable rules. Physical export of film media in tangible form is zero-rated as an export of goods only upon actual exit of the goods from the PRC and completion of customs declaration.

  • For physical goods exports only: Customs Export Declaration Form (Bill of Entry) issued by the General Administration of Customs of China, together with the export manifest and the customs-signed export bill.
  • For physical media exports: Bill of Lading or airway bill issued by the carrier evidencing that the film deliverables were transported from a PRC customs port to the overseas consignee.
  • For services and intangible licensing: a signed cross-border film copyright licensing or distribution agreement with the overseas licensee, clearly stating the licensed rights, territory, royalty or licence fee, and that all exploitation and consumption occur outside China.
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange receipt/settlement voucher evidencing that the licence fee or export proceeds were actually repatriated to the WFOE's bank account in China from outside the PRC.
  • Overseas recipient's tax residence certificate or equivalent corporate registration proof, together with the WFOE's written self-certification or statement that the film licensing services were wholly consumed outside the PRC.
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, and the VAT invoice (including fully digitalised electronic invoice or zero-rated invoice) issued through the STA e-invoice platform, as required for the export zero-rating declaration.

The WFOE must issue all VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration's VAT Invoice Management System under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Fully digitalised electronic invoices (FDEIs) are created and validated on the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with invoice data transmitted to the tax authority in near real-time through the official tax-control Ukey and API integration. General VAT taxpayers may issue Special VAT Invoices that carry a digital tax-control stamp and anti-fraud code to support downstream input VAT credits. FDEIs carry an encrypted digital stamp and do not require physical manual stamping. Monthly VAT returns must reconcile all issued and received invoice data with the electronic platform records.

The State Taxation Administration cross-checks export zero-rating claims against customs declaration data, bank payment records, State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) foreign exchange receipt data, and the underlying licensing agreements. If the WFOE cannot substantiate a bona fide cross-border supply, fails to repatriate the foreign currency proceeds within the applicable 180-day deadline unless a recognised statutory exemption applies (for example export credit insurance or SAFE-approved offsetting), or submits false or incomplete documents, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating, claw back any input VAT credit or refund, and assess VAT at the statutory domestic rate. Under Articles 63 and 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection, the taxpayer must repay the improperly claimed refund or zero-rated tax benefit, pay a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the overdue tax amount, and may be subject to an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax involved. Where the conduct constitutes fraudulent export tax refund, criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China may be imposed, including fines and imprisonment for the responsible persons. The China-Hong Kong tax arrangement does not provide any shield against PRC VAT clawback or penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict separation between current account and capital account convertibility. For the Hong Kong-owned WFOE engaged in independent film licensing, the current account—including film royalties, license fees, and service remuneration—is generally freely convertible subject to SAFE verification of genuine trade and transaction documents. However, the capital account remains heavily controlled: foreign direct investment capital injections, shareholder loans, and subsequent capital reductions or exit proceeds require case-by-case approval, registration, or filing with SAFE. The WFOE's registered capital contribution from the Hong Kong parent must be remitted into a designated Capital Account (FDI) at a domestic bank, followed by a Foreign Exchange Registration ('FDI Registration') and participation in the SAFE system. Profit remittances to Hong Kong are permitted under current account status, but the entity must demonstrate audited financials, tax payment certificates, and a resolution approving dividend distribution, and the remittance must be routed through a bank with the appropriate 'Profit Repatriation' purpose code. A key hurdle is that the WFOE must surrender or convert a portion of its FX proceeds into CNY if it holds an export-oriented arrangement, though pure domestic licensing income in CNY does not require conversion. The capital account convertibility of any future external debt or equity revival remains subject to macro-prudential quotas (e.g., the 'cross-border financing macro-prudential regulation' parameter) and must be separately registered. The practical effect is that intra-group treasury centralization via notional pooling with Hong Kong is not permitted without a qualified domestic and cross-border RMB pooling scheme approved by PBOC/SAFE. All FX hedging for outbound royalty disbursements must be conducted through onshore licensed banks under real demand principles, and offshore NDF markets for CNY are prohibited for onshore entities. The overall regime is workable for a well-documented licensing business, but treasury operations must be strictly transaction-backed, with documentary trails matching the underlying copyright or distribution agreement.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a WFOE engaged in cross-border licensing, export proceeds in foreign currency (e.g., USD received from overseas licensees) must be brought onshore and settled or held in a foreign exchange account within 30 days of receipt; the current statutory requirement mandates that 100% of these proceeds be surrendered to the banking system via the bank's FX settlement system, though the entity may retain the funds in a retained FX account with prior registration. The applicable conversion ratio is effectively 100% for current account receipts, unless the entity qualifies for an exemption under SAFE's 'outstanding current account balance' rules for certain service trade categories. For film royalties, the bank will apply the 'Service Trade - Copyright Royalties' purpose code and verify the underlying contract, invoice, and tax withholding certificate. The entity must file the receipt with SAFE's 'Trade in Services FX Administration' system within the prescribed 15 to 30 calendar days; failure to surrender in time triggers penalties and may suspend the entity's ability to receive further FX.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable. Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, or neobanks domiciled in Hong Kong or the UK) are legally barred from opening domestic CNY or onshore foreign currency accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. The People's Bank of China and SAFE solely grant banking licenses for onshore RMB and FX settlement to commercial banks domiciled in mainland China. While such EMIs may provide multi-currency virtual accounts in Hong Kong for the parent entity, they cannot act as a primary clearing rail for the WFOE's domestic tax payments, payroll, or VAT filings, nor can they satisfy the SAFE capital registration requirements for FDI injection. The WFOE must maintain a primary onshore relationship with a licensed PRC bank; offshore EMI accounts can only serve as ancillary treasury tools for the Hong Kong parent, and funds must flow through a documented wire chain that reconciles to the onshore entity's books. The feasibility rating is 'Low' for replacing domestic banking rails and 'High' only as an auxiliary collection gateway for overseas film streaming proceeds before repatriation to the onshore WFOE Bank of China account, provided that the EMI is not used to obscure the ultimate beneficiary.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must wire the registered capital through SWIFT MT103 (or MT202 for interbank cover) to the WFOE's designated FDI Capital Account at the onshore bank, using the remittance purpose code 'Investment - Paid-in Capital' and the beneficiary's National Taxpayer Number. The WFOE must first apply to the local SAFE branch for an 'FDI Business Registration' (or perform the online registration through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange's 'Capital Project Information System'). The remitter must be the exact entity named in the FDI approval, and the remittance narrative must contain the reference 'Registered Capital of [WFOE] per [Business License Number]'. Upon receipt, the bank will perform a 'capital account receipt verification' and then allow transfer to the RMB basic deposit account after 100% conversion, or retain in a FX account subject to the SAFE 'foreign exchange capital settlement' restrictions. Any later equity contribution above the registered capital must be handled as a separate 'capital account change' involving a revised business license and SAFE filing. The bank will issue a 'Capital Verification Report' (though formal third-party capital verification has been abolished, the bank's receipt statement is sufficient for regulatory purposes) and the treasury must retain the MT103 confirmation, SAFE registration, and electronic contract to reconcile for future audits.For outbound royalty payments to overseas copyright owners or co-producers, the WFOE must route the payment through the onshore bank via SWIFT MT103, using the purpose code 'Business Travel / Royalties - Copyright License Fee' under the current account. The bank will require the tax withholding certificate for the 10% overseas withholding tax (or applicable double-tax treaty rate), the original or electronic copy of the license agreement, the invoice approved by the Chinese tax authority, and a payment application form. Where the contract exceeds USD 50,000 equivalent, the bank may require the WFOE to present a 'Service Trade, Foreign Exchange, and Tax Certificate'. For trade-related disbursements involving physical media master shipments, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C at sight) can be issued by the bank in favor of the overseas supplier, with documents including the bill of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and a certificate of origin. Documentary collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for lower-risk repeat transactions, but the film licensing industry commonly uses SWIFT wire for royalty settlements and L/C only for physical delivery of film prints or equipment. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable. Offshore fintech EMIs (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business, or neobanks domiciled in Hong Kong or the UK) are legally barred from opening domestic CNY or onshore foreign currency accounts for a China-incorporated WFOE. The People's Bank of China and SAFE solely grant banking licenses for onshore RMB and FX settlement to commercial banks domiciled in mainland China. While such EMIs may provide multi-currency virtual accounts in Hong Kong for the parent entity, they cannot act as a primary clearing rail for the WFOE's domestic tax payments, payroll, or VAT filings, nor can they satisfy the SAFE capital registration requirements for FDI injection. The WFOE must maintain a primary onshore relationship with a licensed PRC bank; offshore EMI accounts can only serve as ancillary treasury tools for the Hong Kong parent, and funds must flow through a documented wire chain that reconciles to the onshore entity's books. The feasibility rating is 'Low' for replacing domestic banking rails and 'High' only as an auxiliary collection gateway for overseas film streaming proceeds before repatriation to the onshore WFOE Bank of China account, provided that the EMI is not used to obscure the ultimate beneficiary.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Statutory related-party transaction documentation thresholds under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016): RMB 200 million for tangible-property transactions, RMB 100 million for intangible-property transactions, RMB 100 million for financial or loan transactions, and RMB 40 million for other cross-border related-party transactions; Local File preparation is triggered at the per-category thresholds, while a Master File is required when consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 10 billion or other special criteria apply.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must demonstrate substantive business purpose, local contracting capacity, board decision-making for license acquisition, and Chinese office and staff; the SAT applies GAAR and BEPS-oriented conduit tests, and Hong Kong DIPN 58 in parallel requires the Hong Kong parent to prove substantive core income-generating activity for treaty claims. A Hong Kong shell with nominee directors and no film-curation, contract-negotiation, or risk-bearing functions will fail both the PRC beneficial-ownership test and Hong Kong economic substance expectations and will be disregarded or re-characterised.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion Through Fictitious Cross-Border Film Licensing

Statutory sanction: Under PRC Tax Collection Law Article 63, the tax authority shall recover the underpaid tax, impose a late-payment surcharge, and levy an administrative penalty of 50%-500% of the evaded amount; under GAAR and Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47, the SAT can disregard the Hong Kong entity and re-attribute the licensor functions and income to the WFOE; if tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, Criminal Law Article 201 applies with imprisonment of up to 3 years, or up to 7 years where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 50,000 and 30% of the tax payable, plus fines.

Director personal liability: Courts may pierce the corporate veil under the PRC Company Law and Civil Code by finding that the director used the WFOE and the Hong Kong shell to avoid tax, ordering civil restitution of diverted profits and joint liability for the tax, penalties, and surcharges; the SAT may list the director on the serious tax violation blacklist, which imposes an exit-ban barring travel out of China, prohibited high-consumption acts, and seizure of personal assets; criminal conviction under Article 201 also exposes the signing director personally to imprisonment and a criminal fine.
Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalisation Abuse

Statutory sanction: The tax authority may make a transfer-pricing adjustment and disallow the excessive interest or royalty deduction under Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 46, applying the 1:1 debt-to-equity ratio for thin capitalisation; underpaid tax is assessed at 25% plus benchmark-lending-rate interest, with potential 50%-300% penalties on the adjustment; the adjusted payment can be re-characterised as a deemed dividend and subjected to 10% or 5% withholding under the DTA only if true beneficial ownership is proved.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false related-party loan agreements or issue board resolutions that knowingly implement non-arm's-length conditions may be held personally liable for fraudulent capital withdrawal under the PRC Civil Code doctrines of abuse of incorporation; the director may be sued for damages, restricted from leaving China, and criminally prosecuted under Criminal Law Article 162 for fraudulent transfer of property or under Article 201 for tax evasion if the structure conceals taxable income.
Withholding Tax Default and Unlawful DTA Claim

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Collection Law Article 69, the WFOE as withholding agent is penalised with 50%-300% of the amount under-withheld and must pay the forgotten withholding tax; the non-resident recipient is also traced and reassessed; if the DTA rate is denied, back taxes plus surcharge and penalties are imposed, and the SAT may reject the treaty claim and impose an additional GAAR penalty if the Hong Kong company is a conduit.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize wire transfers or letter-of-credit instructions without first obtaining the Tax Residency Certificate and the beneficial-ownership file use the corporate form to conceal a statutory withholding obligation; courts can pierce the corporate veil and make the directors jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax, and the director may be blacklisted and prohibited from travel pending payment and audit; where intent is proved, a director is exposed to criminal accountability for failure to remit taxes under Criminal Law Article 201, with imprisonment risk up to 7 years.
