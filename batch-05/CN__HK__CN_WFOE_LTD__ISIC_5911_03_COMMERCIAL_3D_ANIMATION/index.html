Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); the national online portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) reached through www.chinatax.gov.cn and provincial STA e-Tax portals, with export/customs declarations handled through the China International Trade Single Window. For the Hong Kong SAR component, the competent authority is the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) via GovHK; Hong Kong SAR has no VAT/GST/Sales Tax registration obligation.

E-government filing portal

On grant of the business license by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE is automatically registered with the STA under the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the TIN/VAT ID for all PRC tax purposes; no separate TIN certificate is required. The WFOE must then complete supplemental tax registration in the e-Tax Bureau, including banking account, finance/accounting policy, legal representative/owner identity, production and business address, ISIC/NAICS-linked industry scope, VAT taxpayer status, and payroll/individual income tax withholding registration. A foreign non-resident entity without a China establishment normally does not register for a PRC TIN; its PRC payor is the withholding agent. If the non-resident shareholder or a foreign service provider establishes a branch or project site in China, the non-resident must submit the non-resident enterprise tax registration application, home-country registration certificate, contract establishing the Chinese establishment/project, and authorized-person identity document to the competent local STA, which will issue a dedicated non-resident TIN. Where the Hong Kong parent intends to rely on the China-Hong Kong double taxation arrangement or treaty benefits, it must obtain a Certificate of Resident Status from the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department before the WFOE effects reduced-rate withholding.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for entity classification. PRC law does not provide a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election; a WFOE is mandatorily a resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at entity level. On the indirect tax side, a taxpayer whose annual taxable turnover is below RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer simplified method or may elect, with STA consent, to be registered as a general VAT taxpayer; exceeding the threshold makes general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory tax registration is immediate upon incorporation with no turnover threshold for the WFOE. For indirect tax status, annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000 obliges registration as a General VAT taxpayer; below RMB 5,000,000 the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. The taxed 3D animation/CGI services are subject to the 6% VAT service rate while the national headline general VAT rate is 13% for goods.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT system, exported goods and qualifying cross-border taxable services are zero-rated. For physical goods (e.g., exhibition masters or physical deliverables), zero-rating requires the goods to be actually exported by the WFOE under a PRC customs declaration, not returned to China, and export proceeds to be collected in foreign exchange. For the WFOE's commercial 3D animation/CGI services delivered digitally to overseas clients, zero-rating is available where the service recipient is outside the PRC, the service is wholly consumed or used outside the PRC, the written offshore service contract and invoice correspond, the consideration is paid by the offshore recipient in a freely convertible currency, and the case falls within the STA's list of zero-rated cross-border taxable services; the domestic 6% VAT rate therefore becomes 0% and input VAT attributable to the export may be claimed as a refund.

All VAT invoices (special VAT invoices and universal VAT invoices, including fully digitalized electronic invoices) must be issued through the STA-controlled taxpayer invoice system using tax UKey/authorized tax-control equipment. Invoice data is transmitted to the e-Tax Bureau on a real-time or near-real-time basis through the Golden Tax System Phase IV; buyers and sellers are required to log in to the national electronic invoice service platform and perform invoice verification before input VAT credit is claimed. Special VAT invoices carry an encrypted tax-control code and digital stamp; no commercial third-party stamp is recognized. The anti-fraud framework includes statutory invoice control number allocation, real-time reporting, national invoice verification platform inspection, and cross-matching of invoice data against VAT returns, customs, and foreign-exchange receipts.

STA, Customs and SAFE operate joint data matching; any missing, inconsistent, or fraudulent customs declaration, foreign-exchange receipt (FIRC), contract, or delivery proof invalidates the zero-rating claim. The tax authority will claw back the VAT refunded and any input VAT credit disallowed, assess late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax improperly refunded under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Deliberate falsification of export documentation may constitute the criminal offense of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under the Criminal Law of the PRC, carrying severe custodial penalties and confiscation. The clawback may cover all open statutory periods where fraud or failure to substantiate is established, with interest and penalties running from the original refund date.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible on current account for legitimate trade and service transactions, including payments for 3D animation and CG services, provided the transaction has real commercial substance and is supported by contracts, invoices, and tax records. Capital account transactions remain heavily controlled: foreign direct investment into a WFOE must be registered with SAFE through a designated bank before funds can enter; the equity capital contribution is then settled into RMB only when underlying business uses are evidenced. Export proceeds and service fees are subject to repatriation and the bank's post-verification review. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are permissible once annual statutory audit is completed, tax clearance in the form of corporate income tax filings and withholding certificate is obtained, and there is no outstanding foreign debt or SAFE registration violation. Dividends transferred out require the declaration of the SAFE code for overseas direct investment and are treated as current account payments, but banks will require the WFOE's audited financial statements, board profit distribution resolution, and the schedule of shareholders. There is no unrestricted same-day convertibility of large corporate flows; each movement is screened against sanctions, PEP, and suspicious transaction reports. The WFOE may not legally access onshore RMB through offshore non-bank fintechs or unlicensed EMI structures.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export or service-related foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to China within 180 calendar days from the date of the underlying export customs declaration or, for service trade, from the contractual receivable maturity, unless SAFE approves overseas retention. There is no current statutory mandatory conversion ratio; a licensed bank may convert the entire amount into RMB at the prevailing market rate, or the enterprise may retain the FX balance in a foreign exchange settlement account. The designated bank uses the trade monitoring system and receipt verification measures to confirm that 100% of the proceeds have been received into China and reported under the correct transaction code.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign exchange regulatory and enforcement power to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - Not viable as primary treasury bank. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, or Currencycloud are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open domestic RMB accounts or operate as settlement institutions for a mainland WFOE. They cannot satisfy SAFE FDI registration, capital account verification, or tax withholding requirements. They may be used only as an auxiliary payment corridor from the Hong Kong parent to non-mainland vendors if the initial movement is out of HKD/USD banking accounts and the WFOE funds are already legally outside China; but any entrance or exit of RMB or foreign exchange into the WFOE's onshore banking system remains prohibited via EMIs. The onshore WFOE must maintain its primary treasury in a PRC licensed bank and use HK banking rails only at the holding company tier.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong parent to the CN WFOE must use the designated Tier-1 bank branch as the SAFE-designated settlement bank. The remitter should execute SWIFT MT103 with the exact legal name of the HK shareholder in field 50K; the beneficiary field must show the WFOE's name and FDI capital settlement account number; field 70 must include the SAFE FDI registration number and reference to equity capital contribution. The bank receives the funds, checks the FDI Information Registration, confirms the amount matches the contribution schedule and Articles of Association, and credits the capital settlement account. Before converting to RMB, the WFOE must provide board resolution on capital use, tax registration, and invoices or contracts supporting business expenditure. A PRC licensed CPA issues the capital verification report to confirm paid-up capital for subsequent equity registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory when related-party transactions involving tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 20 million, intangible asset or financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million in the fiscal year. A Master File is required where the consolidated group revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion or cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: Although mainland China does not apply Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regulations (ESR), the WFOE must demonstrate actual management, employees, premises, and decision-making in China to support its tax classification and pricing. For any Hong Kong affiliate in the structure, Hong Kong ESR require the entity to carry on core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, such as actual negotiation, management, and execution of contracts; failure to do so may result in fines and treaty benefit denial. In China, General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law empowers tax authorities to disregard arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose, recharacterise transactions, and deny tax benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Falsified Transfer Pricing and Fabricated Services

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law: recovery of the underpaid tax, 0.05% daily late surcharge, and a monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the unpaid tax. Under Criminal Law Article 201, tax evasion involving a large amount and exceeding 10% of the tax due is a crime; where the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of the tax payable, the penalty is 3 to 7 years imprisonment plus fines.

Director personal liability: Under Article 23 of the Company Law (2023), directors or shareholders who abuse corporate personality to evade tax are jointly and severally liable for corporate debts. Directors who knowingly sign false returns can be personally assessed for unpaid taxes, blacklisted as serious tax violators, prohibited from exiting China under Article 44 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, and subject to criminal imprisonment for tax evasion.
Thin Capitalisation / Excessive Interest Deduction and Deemed Dividend

Statutory sanction: Under Article 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Cai Shui [2008] No. 121, excessive interest is not deductible; the disallowed amount is treated as a distribution triggering dividend withholding tax of 10% (or 5% if the DTA conditions are satisfied). The tax authorities may impose penalties of 50% to 5 times the tax shortfall under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, plus compensatory interest under the special tax adjustment rules.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the tax underpayment and the failure to withhold are jointly liable for the unpaid withholding tax, interest, and penalties. Their personal assets may be seized, they may be placed on the blacklist with travel bans, and if funds were transferred abroad with evasion intent, criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Failure and General Anti-Avoidance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Articles 115-120 of its Implementation Regulations, the State Taxation Administration may disregard the arrangement, recharacterise the transaction, adjust taxable income, and impose penalties of 50% to 5 times the short-paid tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. Separately, Hong Kong's Economic Substance Regulations impose fines of up to HKD 50,000 per failure and up to HKD 300,000 for significant non-compliance, with potential imprisonment of up to 2 years for wilful default.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced under Article 23 of the Company Law; directors who set up a sham entity to evade taxation are personally liable for corporate tax debts. Courts may order confiscation of personal assets, impose exit bans, and criminally prosecute for intentional tax evasion if the director knowingly misrepresented the business, leading to imprisonment of up to 7 years.
