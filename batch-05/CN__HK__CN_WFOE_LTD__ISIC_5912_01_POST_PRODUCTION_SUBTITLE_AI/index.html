Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the in-charge local tax bureau (主管税务机关) where the WFOE is registered; online electronic filing is through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn. For the Hong Kong parent, the relevant authority is the Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong, but the parent is not the primary tax registration subject for this Chinese WFOE.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor does not directly obtain a Chinese tax ID. The Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) is automatically registered for tax under the multi-certificate integration reform: when the State Administration for Market Regulation issues the business license, the Unified Social Credit Code becomes the WFOE's taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID) and is transmitted in real time to the STA. Within 30 days of obtaining the business license, the WFOE must complete the supplementary tax information filing through the Electronic Tax Bureau by submitting its articles of association, lease or ownership proof for the registered address, legal representative's passport or ID, board resolutions, bank account confirmation, and, for a foreign parent, the parent's certificate of incorporation and Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate. The WFOE is then configured as either a small-scale VAT taxpayer or a general VAT taxpayer and may apply for the tax control UKey and digital invoice quota. If the foreign non-resident parent has Chinese-source income without an establishment, the Chinese payer or withholding agent must file the contract with the in-charge tax authority and obtain a non-resident enterprise tax identification number for withholding reporting and treaty benefit administration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

China does not operate a US-style check-the-box regime and does not permit hybrid mismatch elections for domestic corporate entities. The WFOE is mandatorily a PRC resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income at a 25% corporate income tax rate. Available elections are limited to (i) VAT taxpayer status election: small-scale taxpayer with a reduced levy rate and annual sales below RMB 5,000,000, versus general VAT taxpayer with the standard 6% rate for modern services and input VAT credit; and (ii) selective access to preferential 15% CIT rate if the WFOE qualifies as an encouraged industry enterprise or is registered in an eligible free-trade zone and meets the principal-business-revenue test. No pass-through election is available.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete tax registration upon issuance of the business license irrespective of turnover. As a VAT classification threshold, mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; an enterprise below this threshold may remain a small-scale taxpayer. For imported services consumed in mainland China, the foreign supplier is not required to register; the domestic purchaser must self-assess VAT under reverse charge.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim the 0% VAT rate for qualifying cross-border services under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement No. 29 of 2016. The automated subtitle transcription, AI voice cloning, and dubbing studio falls within the zero-rated category of broadcast and television program production and distribution services and/or offshore service outsourcing services. To qualify, all of the following conditions must be met: (1) the recipient is an overseas company, institution, or individual; (2) the service is consumed entirely outside mainland China; (3) no Chinese domestic unit or individual benefits from the service; (4) consideration is received from outside mainland China in foreign exchange or RMB through a traceable banking channel; and (5) the WFOE retains the written contract, delivery records, digital output logs, invoices, and payment evidence. Zero-rating entitles the WFOE to a refund or credit of the related input VAT on production software, AI infrastructure, studio facilities, and overhead inputs. Payment received through platforms such as Stripe or Wise must be substantiated by the underlying export contract and bank traceability; use of a payment intermediary does not by itself satisfy the zero-rating evidence requirement.

  • Cross-border service trade contract signed with the overseas client, specifying the post-production, subtitle transcription, AI voice cloning, and dubbing deliverables and the jurisdiction of consumption
  • Digital invoice issued to the overseas client describing the zero-rated service and providing the overseas recipient's identity details
  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) if physical media, masters, or accompanying encoded goods are exported; for pure online delivery, the electronic cloud delivery log may be substituted
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill if physical goods or media are exported
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or the equivalent Chinese Foreign Exchange Income Declaration bank-issued receipt evidencing repatriation of the export consideration within the statutory collection period
  • Delivery evidence such as the subtitle or dubbing master delivery receipt, encrypted watermark proof, cloud delivery logs, or technical acceptance certificate signed by the overseas client
  • Cross-border service trade contract registration record with the Ministry of Commerce or local commerce department, including the service outsourcing registration number if applicable

China mandates real-time issuance of Full Digital Electronic Invoices through the STA's Electronic Invoice Service Platform. General VAT taxpayers must issue invoices through the tax control system or an approved API interface, and each invoice carries a tax authority-assigned 24-digit invoice number; there is no separate physical fiscal stamp. The Golden Tax Phase IV platform cross-references invoice data with customs, banking, foreign exchange, and corporate income tax records in real time. Input VAT is creditable only when the invoice data has been matched and cleared by the tax authority. Small-scale taxpayers issue digital invoices through the same platform, and anti-fraud controls automatically verify invoice authenticity, frequency, and counterparty risk.

The STA and local tax bureaus audit zero-rated export services by matching Golden Tax Phase IV invoice data with customs records and State Administration of Foreign Exchange receipt data. If the overseas-consumption condition, payment-traceability requirement, or 180-day repatriation deadline is not satisfied, the tax authority will retrospectively reverse the 0% rate, assess VAT at 6%, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date, and may impose administrative penalties under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. For fraudulent claims, false contracts, shell overseas recipients, or fabricated foreign exchange receipts, the activity constitutes fraud to obtain export tax refunds; the tax authority will order recovery of all refunded tax plus a fine of 50% to 500% of the refunded amount, and the collection period is unlimited for fraudulent underpayment. Criminal liability may arise under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, with penalties ranging from imprisonment for a term of years up to life imprisonment and confiscation of property for particularly large amounts, together with disqualification of the enterprise's export tax refund status and travel restrictions on responsible persons.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with current-account convertibility and capital-account controls. SAFE and PBOC supervise all cross-border fund movements. A WFOE is treated as a resident onshore entity for FX purposes: its paid-in capital injections from the Hong Kong parent must be routed through a designated FDI capital account and registered with SAFE under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system. The current account, including trade in services, royalty payments, subtitle licensing fees, AI software subscriptions, and post-production vendor disbursements, is generally convertible once invoices and contracts are submitted, but the bank carries out real-need authenticity verification before remitting. Profit remittance to the HK shareholder is permitted only after the statutory annual audit and tax clearance; dividends are subject to withholding tax under the double tax arrangement, and funds must be sourced from verified after-tax retained profits. There is no mandatory conversion of incoming FDI capital, but the RMB settlement process is controlled and capital-account transactions such as cross-border loans, loan guarantees, and external debt require separate registration and quota compliance. The WFOE cannot freely deploy foreign currency for non-trade purposes, and any cross-border guarantee, treasury investment, or offshore loan must be declared to SAFE. Payments for AI-related intangible services are highly scrutinised and require detailed transfer-pricing and delivery evidence to clear the bank's compliance committee.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods-trade export proceeds must be repatriated to a SAFE-registered onshore bank account within 90 days of customs declaration unless an extension is approved; service-trade export income must be collected under the applicable invoice and contract terms and reported through the bank's trade monitoring system. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain 100% of export receipts in foreign currency in an export proceeds retention account or settle them into RMB at its discretion, subject to authentic transaction evidence and SAFE's trade verification requirements.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise and Stripe are not licensed by SAFE or PBOC as deposit-taking settlement institutions for onshore WFOE entities and cannot serve as the primary treasury account for statutory capital injection, export receipt collection, tax remittance, or capital repayment. They are legally barred from providing direct onshore RMB settlement rails for the WFOE's corporate treasury. Wise and Stripe may only be used as ancillary front-end collections for low-value overseas B2C transactions, but all funds must ultimately be repatriated through a licensed domestic bank account using standard FX clearing rails. The overall feasibility rating is low as a bank replacement; the WFOE must rely on tier-1 commercial banks for all principal capital and trade flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound FDI capital injection from the HK parent to the WFOE must be routed via SWIFT MT103 or CIPS to the bank-designated FDI capital account, using the SAFE-approved foreign exchange purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise capital injection. Before the remittance, the WFOE must complete the foreign investment information reporting through the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System and open a capital settlement account. Upon arrival, the bank verifies the source of the remittance, watches for any conflict with the capital account control list, and either retains the funds in foreign currency or converts them into RMB. A CPA capital verification report must be produced after the funds are received, and the capital account funds are restricted: they cannot be used for securities investments, entrusted loans, or real estate speculation without specific regulatory approvals. For shareholder loans, separate external debt registration and quota limits apply.For outbound payments to overseas subtitle agencies, AI tool vendors, voice talent, dubbing engineers, and post-production subcontractors, the WFOE must use SWIFT MT103 from its operating RMB or foreign-currency account after presenting the trade contract, commercial invoice, service completion certificate, tax withholding vouchers, and the correct payment purpose code. The domestic bank performs a real-need verification and records the payment in the SAFE trade-monitoring system. For physical media equipment purchases, commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are acceptable clearing rails because they provide title and payment protection. CIPS may be used for RMB-denominated settlement to the Hong Kong parent and to shorter-cycle counterparties, while larger service payments require standard SWIFT messages to satisfy counterparty banking compliance. The overall feasibility rating is low as a bank replacement; the WFOE must rely on tier-1 commercial banks for all principal capital and trade flows.

Box 4 / Lines 4-6 (Selling, Administrative, and Financial Expenses) Marketing, business development, general administrative costs, management fees, bank charges, interest expense, and financing costs. Management fees paid to the Hong Kong parent are deductible only where specific and identifiable services were actually rendered, with evidence of necessity, benefit, and no duplicate cost absorption. Intercompany interest is deductible subject to the thin-capitalisation debt-to-equity limit of 2:1 for non-financial enterprises and must be priced at an arm's-length CUP. Box 5 / Line 13 (Total Profit) Accounting profit before tax after all operating revenues, costs, expenses, gains, and losses. Reconcile this line precisely to the audited financial statements. This is the baseline for CIT adjustments. Box 6 / Lines 14-15 (Tax Adjustment Additions and Reductions) Permanent and temporary differences, transfer-pricing adjustments, disallowed expenses, accelerated depreciation or amortization differences, tax-exempt income, and R&D super deductions.
