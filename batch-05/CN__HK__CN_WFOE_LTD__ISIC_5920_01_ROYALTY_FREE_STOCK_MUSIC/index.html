Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), supported by the nationwide Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal and the National Unified Invoice Service Platform for e-invoicing and real-time invoice clearance.

E-government filing portal

For the PRC-resident WFOE: the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code is automatically assigned at incorporation by the State Administration for Market Regulation and serves as the statutory Tax Identification Number and VAT taxpayer identification number; tax registration is completed through the integrated one-stop 'multi-license' registration link to the STA, without a separate VAT ID application. The WFOE must then complete supplementary tax records within 30 days, including filing the legal representative identity, financial accounting system, bank account information, and tax-deposit bank account through e-Tax, and, if applying for general taxpayer status, submit the general VAT taxpayer identification filing. For a foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in mainland China (including a Hong Kong parent that only holds equity), no separate PRC VAT registration or TIN is required; where it earns China-source income, the WFOE or other withholding agent remits VAT and corporate income tax through the e-Tax non-resident withholding modules using the foreign entity's home-jurisdiction tax reference. If the non-resident entity actually establishes a PE or performs taxable services in China, it must register through the STA non-resident temporary tax-registration procedure, after which the STA will issue a temporary tax registration/TIN reference tied to its Unified Social Credit Code.

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for mandatory tax registration of a PRC-resident WFOE; registration is simultaneous with incorporation. For VAT status, annual taxable sales in excess of RMB 5,000,000 require general VAT taxpayer registration; sales at or below RMB 5,000,000 qualify for small-scale taxpayer treatment. Non-resident suppliers without a PRC establishment have no turnover threshold and are subject to transaction-by-transaction VAT withholding by the Chinese buyer/payer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods: zero-rating is available where the WFOE exports goods under a customs export declaration, title to the goods passes to an offshore buyer, the goods are loaded for transportation out of China, and the corresponding export proceeds are repatriated to a PRC bank account. For digital and offshore service supplies, including stock-music licensing subscriptions, zero-rating is available only where the particular service falls within the statutory cross-border zero-rated catalogue under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and STA Announcement No. 29 of 2016, the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise/customer, the service and the related intangible right are wholly consumed and used outside the PRC, the supply is not connected with PRC-located goods or immovable property, and the consideration is collected in foreign currency remitted into China. A general 'royalty-free music subscription' license that does not meet the consumption-location test or is not enumerated in the zero-rated catalogue will remain standard-rated at 6% (or VAT-exempt, if the exemption category applies); zero-rating is not presumed.

General VAT taxpayers and eligible small-scale taxpayers must issue invoices through the State Taxation Administration's electronic invoice infrastructure, currently the digitalized e-invoice system operated on the National Unified Invoice Service Platform. Invoices are cryptographically protected, given a unique invoice code and anti-counterfeiting check code, and the issuance data are submitted to STA servers in near-real time; batch and system-to-system integration is available through published e-Tax data-interchange APIs for enterprise ERP/billing platforms. The Golden Tax Phase IV anti-fraud system automatically cross-matches invoice issuance, VAT declarations, customs export data, bank settlement data, and counterparty tax information; offline paper special VAT invoices are being displaced by the fully digitalized e-invoice, and any non-platform invoice will not be accepted for input-credit, refund, or customs clearance purposes.

The STA and Customs cross-verify e-invoice issuance, export customs declaration metadata, transport documents, license/contract terms, and bank foreign-currency receipts through the Golden Tax Phase IV platform. If an export zero-rating or VAT refund claim is not substantiated, the zero-rated treatment is reversed, the underpaid VAT is assessed with late-payment interest, and the refunded VAT must be clawed back. Intentional fraudulent export refunds attract a penalty of 100% to 500% of the falsely refunded tax under Article 66 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law of the People's Republic of China, and larger amounts can constitute the criminal offense of fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law, with responsible officers and the entity subject to criminal fine and imprisonment. Export tax refund eligibility may also be suspended and the taxpayer placed under enhanced audit scrutiny.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a hybrid current-account convertible and capital-account controlled framework under the Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations. For the CN WFOE engaged in royalty-free stock music licensing, current-account cross-border receipts and payments are permissible without prior SAFE approval so long as genuine underlying contracts and tax invoices are presented to the designated bank. Nevertheless, intangible service flows attract enhanced due diligence because digital audio files create no physical goods trail, increasing transfer-mispricing and valuation scrutiny. The capital account remains tightly supervised: the Hong Kong parent's equity injection must be registered with SAFE through the receiving bank, deposited into a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and verified by a Chinese certified public accountant before operational use of the funds. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is lawful after corporate income tax, VAT, and surtaxes are fully settled, but the remitting bank requires an audited profit distribution resolution, a Foreign Exchange Payment Application (F.31), and evidence of tax clearance. Export proceeds from subscription fees must be repatriated within the statutory timeline; there is no fixed surrender ratio, but enterprises are expected to convert working capital into RMB through the interbank market as ordinary business practice.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from the WFOE's overseas subscription fees must be repatriated to China within 180 calendar days from the date of export declaration or from the contractual payment due date, whichever is later under SAFE practice. The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% - there is no forced sale requirement; the WFOE may retain eligible foreign currency balances in its categorical foreign exchange settlement account. However, any conversion to RMB must be made at a licit bank rate and reported under the bank's foreign exchange settlement and sale supervision system.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the supervision of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility rating: Low for primary treasury rail and Medium for ancillary inbound collection of low-value subscription fees. In mainland China, any institution that performs cross-border payment clearing or foreign exchange settlement must hold a PBOC Payment Business License and operate under SAFE-monitored banking channels. Leading offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe and PayPal are generally not licensed to open settlement accounts for a mainland WFOE; PayPal has a local licensed affiliate (through GoPay), but it is not permitted to replace a full corporate banking relationship. The WFOE may use EMIs as a sales collection gateway for small-ticket stock-music subscriptions, but all collected funds must be periodically swept to the WFOE's Tier-1 domestic bank account through compliant repatriation filings. EMIs cannot handle FDI capital, profit remittance, or formal trade settlement, and they are legally barred from servicing the WFOE as a stand-alone treasury counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent wires HKD or USD to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital account at a Tier-1 domestic bank via SWIFT MT103. The wire must carry the SAFE business registration certificate number, the WFOE's unified social credit code, and the bank's transaction purpose code for direct equity investment (often mapping to SAFE project code 601, 602, or 622). Upon crediting, the WFOE must submit its joint-venture/shareholders' agreement, board resolution, tax registration certificate, and original remittance advice; a licensed PRC certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report before the capital account balance can be converted to RMB and transferred into the RMB basic deposit account. In mainland China, any institution that performs cross-border payment clearing or foreign exchange settlement must hold a PBOC Payment Business License and operate under SAFE-monitored banking channels. Leading offshore digital EMIs such as Stripe and PayPal are generally not licensed to open settlement accounts for a mainland WFOE; PayPal has a local licensed affiliate (through GoPay), but it is not permitted to replace a full corporate banking relationship. The WFOE may use EMIs as a sales collection gateway for small-ticket stock-music subscriptions, but all collected funds must be periodically swept to the WFOE's Tier-1 domestic bank account through compliant repatriation filings. EMIs cannot handle FDI capital, profit remittance, or formal trade settlement, and they are legally barred from servicing the WFOE as a stand-alone treasury counterparty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Public Notice No. 42 of 2016, Local File is mandatory when annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, intangible/financial transfers exceed RMB 10 million, service fee payments exceed RMB 40 million, or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is required when group aggregate related-party revenue exceeds RMB 4 billion; the Annual Related-Party Transaction Report (Form 12/13) must always be filed when intercompany transactions exist

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form under EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR) and the beneficial ownership test in the China-HK DTA; the Chinese WFOE must retain substantive core income-generating activities (CIGA) such as licensing infrastructure, staff, and risk management; the Hong Kong entity must maintain physical office, employees, and board functions; a shell HK entity without commercial rationality will be denied treaty relief and recharacterized as a conduit

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion via Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 300% of the underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge; Article 123 provides additional fine on preparers; enterprise added to tax blacklist

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil leaves directors jointly and severally liable for unpaid corporate tax; personal assets subject to restitution; exit/travel ban (出境限制) enforced; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment of up to 7 years where evaded tax exceeds RMB 50,000 and is above 10% of tax payable
DTA Abuse and Beneficial Ownership Fraud

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 permits the tax authority to reassess under GAAR; denial of treaty relief causes a retroactive 10% WHT and additional 20% interest; Tax Collection Law Article 63 penalty of 50%-300% of underpaid tax applies to the enterprise as the withholding agent

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations incur personal civil liability for the State Revenue losses; passport blacklisting and travel restrictions; Criminal Law Article 201 or Article 205 may apply for fraud, with imprisonment up to 7 years and unlimited confiscation of personal assets used in the concealment
Thin Capitalization and Interest Base Erosion

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 46 disallows excessive interest, recharacterizing it as deemed dividends; this triggers an additional 25% CIT assessment and 10% WHT on the deemed dividend; Tax Collection Law Article 63 penalty of 50%-300% of underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily surcharge

Director personal liability: Directors approving the sham loan structure are liable for the corporate tax deficiency in civil asset restitution; court-mandated travel and exit blacklisting follows non-payment; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 169 for breach of fiduciary duty and fraud, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines
