Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA), acting through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) online portal (login available via chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE: incorporation with the State Administration for Market Regulation automatically completes tax registration under the multi-license integration reform; the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code on the business license constitutes the taxpayer identification number for both Corporate Income Tax and VAT; no separate VAT ID is issued. For a foreign non-resident entity without a Chinese permanent establishment: the entity must register through the Electronic Tax Bureau at the competent STA office governing the China-source income, upload a notarised certificate of incorporation, a board resolution appointing a local tax agent or withholding agent, and Chinese bank details, after which the STA issues a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number. If the non-resident entity only earns income subject to withholding by the WFOE, the WFOE is the statutory withholding agent and a separate non-resident registration is generally unnecessary unless the non-resident entity claims treaty benefits or invoices Chinese customers directly without a permanent establishment.

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; all enterprises must complete tax registration at formation regardless of turnover. Small-scale taxpayers are exempt from VAT on monthly taxable sales up to RMB 100,000.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration Circular No. 36 of 2016 (Cai Shui [2016] 36) and subsequent cross-border service measures, software services, information system services and B2B SaaS supplied by a Chinese resident entity to a customer based outside mainland China - including Hong Kong SAR - can be VAT zero-rated (0%) only if the service is completely consumed outside mainland China. The WFOE must evidence that the customer is a non-resident, that the service is not rendered to or consumed by a party in mainland China, and that consideration is received from overseas and repatriated through licensed Chinese banking channels. Where software is embodied in physical media and physically exported, the transaction is treated as an export of goods and must satisfy the exported-goods VAT rebate regime.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export declaration for exported goods) - only necessary when physical software media or devices are exported; pure cloud SaaS has no customs clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill - evidencing physical movement where goods are exported
  • Written cross-border service contract defining the software/information system services and confirming consumption outside mainland China
  • Commercial invoice and service settlement statement
  • Bank foreign exchange income proof issued by a licensed Chinese bank showing receipt from the overseas customer
  • Offshore customer registration certificate or tax residency certificate supporting non-resident status
  • VAT return export zero-rating schedule filed with the State Taxation Administration

China mandates real-time reporting of B2B invoices through the fully digitalised electronic invoice system operated by the Electronic Tax Bureau and connected to the STA Golden Tax Phase IV platform. Invoices are created on the national unified e-invoice platform, with a 20-digit invoice number, an encrypted anti-fraud code, and immediate electronic transmission of the invoice dataset to the STA. Taxpayers manage issuance, red-letter reversals, acceptance, and retention through the tax digital account in the e-Tax Bureau; no physical fiscal stamp or third-party clearance device is required for fully digital e-invoices.

The STA may audit zero-rating claims against contracts, bank receipts, customer overseas status, and consumption indicators. If documentation is missing or contradictory, the claimed zero rate is reversed, VAT at 6% becomes payable, daily late-payment interest at 0.05% accrues from the original filing date, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the tax shortfall may be imposed under the Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. False contracts, forged bank receipts, fraudulent foreign-exchange repatriation, or deliberate tax evasion expose the entity and responsible officers to Chinese criminal liability, and the STA may disqualify the exporter from zero-rating or export-rebate treatment and retrospectively claw back rebates for prior periods.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which the renminbi is fully convertible for current-account transactions such as cross-border trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties, and software licensing fees, provided the supporting contract, invoice, and proof of transaction authenticity pass the bank's genuineness review under SAFE rules. Capital-account items remain restricted and require specific SAFE registration, quota approval, or clearance under the Foreign Investment Negative List. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise engaged in B2B enterprise SaaS is classified as a foreign-invested information service company, which is not on the negative list and therefore may receive paid-in capital from its Hong Kong shareholder through a designated capital escrow account at a tier-1 commercial bank. The injected foreign exchange is not freely usable until the onshore invested enterprise completes capital verification through a certified public accountant and obtains a capital verification report. After the WFOE is operational, its recurring SaaS export receipts from overseas customers can be credited to a current-account foreign-currency collection account without any mandatory conversion, but each credit and subsequent settlement into renminbi must be justified with software usage agreements, invoices, service receipts, and complete transaction records. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permissible only after the WFOE produces an audited statutory financial report, proves that corporate income tax and withholding tax obligations have been satisfied, approves a board distribution resolution, and demonstrates that the cumulative paid-in capital and undistributed profits support the distribution. Any intercompany shareholder loan, offshore cash pooling, or cross-border guarantee would be treated as a capital-account matter requiring separate SAFE approval, external debt ratio compliance, and additional financial integrity evidence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds from B2B software services must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the contractual billing date or service completion date; goods exporters face the same 180-day window from customs declaration, and an extension of up to 210 days may be available with SAFE approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE framework; the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign-currency receipts in a current-account foreign-currency collection account. However, whenever the enterprise wishes to convert those receipts into renminbi, transfer funds to domestic suppliers, or remit dividends offshore, the bank will require underlying trade and service documentation such as signed SaaS agreements, invoices, payment receipts, and a completed Foreign Exchange Declaration for Export of Services. Cryptocurrency settlement is prohibited, and receipts routed through non-bank conduits must be supported by an approved cross-border payment institution settlement report before the bank will accept them as legitimate export proceeds.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low for the mainland WFOE as a statutory account-holding entity. Wise and Stripe are not licensed as deposit-taking banks or payment institutions with a full PRC domestic settlement licence, and PRC foreign-exchange regulations require the WFOE's foreign-currency receipts to enter its onshore regulated banking system through a SAFE-supervised bank with genuine transaction backing. The WFOE cannot legally operate a Wise B2B account or Stripe treasury balance as its principal corporate account. Such digital EMIs can only be considered by the Hong Kong parent as an offshore collection wallet to receive global SaaS subscription payments, with intercompany settlement subsequently routed to the mainland WFOE through compliant SWIFT payments and SAFE reporting. For the Chinese entity, the only bank-recognised route is a tier-1 onshore bank account paired with explicit trade purpose codes.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Master File threshold: aggregate related-party transactions of RMB 1 billion or more in the preceding tax year. Local File thresholds: tangible asset transactions of RMB 200 million or more; intangible asset transactions of RMB 100 million or more; financial asset transactions of RMB 100 million or more; service transactions of RMB 40 million or more in a single tax year. An annual related-party transaction reporting schedule is mandatory irrespective of the threshold.

Economic substance scope: The PRC does not impose Caribbean-style Economic Substance Regulations, but enforces OECD substance-over-form principles through CIT Law Article 41, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, SAT Announcement 2015 No.7 on income attribution, and the Principal Purpose Test (PPT) under the BEPS Multilateral Convention. The WFOE must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities for SaaS, including software development, server and data hosting, IP enhancement, customer contracting and risk management, are performed by its own personnel in China; the Hong Kong shareholder must demonstrate real commercial activity in Hong Kong to preserve treaty benefits and avoid being recharacterised as a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Offshore Revenue Diversion

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63: recovery of the underpaid CIT, a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount, and daily late interest at 0.05%; Criminal Law Article 201: for tax evasion above RMB 50,000 and more than 10% of tax payable, imprisonment of three to seven years and a criminal fine for serious cases

Director personal liability: Under Company Law Article 20 the corporate veil may be pierced if the WFOE functions as an instrument of tax fraud; directors and the controlling Hong Kong shareholder become jointly and severally liable with their personal assets; the legal representative may be put on the Tax Dishonesty List, receive a court-ordered exit ban from mainland China, suffer asset freezing, and face imprisonment upon criminal conviction.
Transfer Pricing Misstatement on Intercompany Royalties and Management Fees

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and Implementation Regulations Articles 111-123 allow the tax authority to make an arm's-length adjustment and impose a 15% annual penalty interest on the resulting CIT deficiency; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 adds an administrative fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; intentional mischaracterisation is referred under Criminal Law Article 201

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false intercompany agreements or knowingly adopt pricing that lacks economic substance may be held personally liable for the adjusted tax under the corporate-veil-piercing doctrine; civil restitution, freezing of personal bank accounts, entry bans for the legal representative, and criminal imprisonment for fraudulent concealment may follow.
Abusive DTA Relief / Shell Beneficial Ownership / False Treaty Declaration

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement 2009 No.124 and Announcement 2015 No.60, treaty benefits are denied and the full 10% WHT shortfall is recovered plus a daily 0.05% surcharge; the false under-declaration is penalised under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 with a fine of 50%-500% of the unpaid WHT; if the shortfall exceeds RMB 50,000 and 10% of tax due, criminal referral for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 follows

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false beneficial ownership declarations are civilly liable for the under-withheld WHT under the corporate-veil-piercing rule; they can be blacklisted, denied exit from mainland China, and individually fined; if forged official documents or invoices are used, prosecution under Criminal Law Articles 203, 205, or 280 can result in imprisonment of up to ten years.
