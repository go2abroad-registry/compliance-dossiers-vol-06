No statutory minimum paid-up capital is required for a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) under the PRC Company Law (2023 Revision) for the proposed consumer micro-SaaS software operation. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the unified Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn, and the local competent tax bureau responsible for the WFOE's licensed address.

E-government filing portal

For a non-resident foreign investor establishing a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) in mainland China: Step 1 - incorporate the WFOE with the local State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining Business License containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which legally operates as the tax Identification Number (TIN) / VAT registration number. Step 2 - within 30 calendar days of license issuance, perform the 'post-filing tax information confirmation' via the e-Tax portal or in person at the competent tax bureau, binding the USCC to tax records. Step 3 - submit legalised investor documents, articles of association, business licence, and address lease, and appoint the legal representative and financial responsible person. Step 4 - complete real-name authentication and set up electronic tax account under the USCC. Step 5 - register for VAT (if eligible), payroll taxes and social insurance. The foreign parent entity does not obtain a separate Chinese TIN unless it creates a Permanent Establishment; the WFOE is the domestic taxable entity.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim 0% VAT on exports of goods or cross-border software services (including SaaS subscriptions for overseas consumers/businesses), the WFOE must satisfy the 'completely consumed outside China' test under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and supplementary rules. For SaaS, the recipient must be an overseas entity/person, the service must be performed on servers located outside China or, if inside China, the economic use must be outside China; there must be a written cross-border service contract, the payment must be received from abroad in foreign currency, and the relevant service must be classified under modern services tax code. Eligible revenue is then zero-rated, entitling the taxpayer to refund of input VAT credits attributable to that supply. Pure domestic digital services remain subject to 6% VAT.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - required for physical goods; not applicable for pure SaaS digital services unless embedded in goods
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - for physical exports
  • Cross-border SaaS agreement or service contract clearly identifying the overseas customer and scope
  • Commercial invoice issued by the WFOE to the foreign buyer
  • Foreign Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank-issued receipt/statement proving actual receipt of foreign currency payment from overseas
  • E-Tax export zero-rating filing / VAT exemption application
  • Foreign exchange income declaration (if applicable)
  • Transfer pricing form (if intercompany) and beneficial ownership statement where required

China mandates electronic invoice issuance through the national e-Invoice platform (fully digital e-invoice), integrated with the e-Tax portal. All VAT taxable sales must be reported in real time and invoices must be transmitted via API to the STA's tax control system. Anti-fraud digital stamping is applied via the tax authority's unified validation; no physical stamps are required for fully digital invoices. Users require enterprise tax software or the e-Tax interface for red-letter (credit note) issuance, buyer verification, and automatic data reconciliation. For export-related zero-rated supplies, separate electronic export declaration records and foreign exchange income vouchers must be linked to the VAT filing.

Claims are subject to strict post-verification by tax authorities. If an export zero-rating claim is found to be unsubstantiated, fraudulent, or if foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days, the STA will claw back the zero-rated treatment and levy 6% output VAT (or 13% on goods) on the supply plus surcharge for late payment (0.05% per day on the overdue VAT) and a fine of up to 50%-500% (typically 0.5-5 times the amount of tax evaded). False filings of services may be treated as false export refunds, attracting a criminal penalty under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (fraudulent export tax refund) punishable by imprisonment (up to life) and fines. Additionally, the entity is barred from future export zero-rating for up to 3 years.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB convertibility fully permitted on the current account for trade in goods, trade in services, dividends, interest, royalties, and ordinary operational remittances, subject to documentary verification and SAFE registration. Capital account items, including foreign direct investment, cross-border intercompany loans, and portfolio flows, are strictly controlled and require specific approvals, filings, or quotas. For a Hong Kong-owned WFOE, initial share capital injection is processed under the FDI regime and must be wired into a designated capital account at a PBOC-licensed commercial bank. Any increase in registered capital requires an amended FDI registration, and the capital contribution cannot be freely swept for non-operational use such as onshore securities investment. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is lawful after the WFOE completes statutory annual audit, corporate income tax filing and payment, and dividend withholding tax settlement. The bank will demand board resolutions, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificates, and a certificate from the tax authority confirming that the distributed profits are after-tax. Export proceeds must be repatriated to China within the statutory period, but no mandatory conversion percentage is imposed; the company may maintain foreign currency in a settlement account or convert to RMB. Every cross-border flow must be reconciled through SAFE's digital monitoring platforms, and failure to document the economic substance of a transaction can trigger capital penalty, FX verification downgrades, and frozen settlement capacity. Overall, the RMB is effectively convertible for genuine operations but remains tightly regulated on the capital account, meaning treasury planning must emphasize real transaction documentation and pre-approved capital routes.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE rules, all export proceeds for goods must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of customs export declaration. There is no statutory compulsory conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain the proceeds in a foreign currency current account through a licensed bank or voluntarily convert them into RMB. Every receipt must be reported and verified through SAFE's cross-border receipt verification system with supporting customs declarations, commercial invoices, packing lists, and bills of lading. Any overdue or unrepatriated export proceeds without an approved extension will trigger administrative penalties, the downgrading of the company's trade foreign exchange facilitation status, and heightened audit scrutiny on all subsequent banking flows.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Not viable for the China-domiciled WFOE. China's banking licensing framework, administered by the PBOC and the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA), permits only licensed commercial banks, foreign-invested banks operating with Chinese banking licenses, and recognized deposit-taking institutions to hold corporate RMB deposits, provide clearing accounts, and execute capital account settlement. Foreign fintech EMI providers, digital-only challenger banks, and payment aggregators such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are not financial institutions in China and cannot act as the WFOE's primary treasury or cross-border clearing bank. They may be used downstream strictly as subscription and micro-SaaS payment gateways, but all proceeds must ultimately settle through an approved Chinese licensed bank or licensed third-party payment institution. Treating an offshore EMI as the corporate operating account creates an immediate regulatory breach and would be rejected in any KYC review by a Chinese account-opening bank.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The compliant capital injection route begins with an SWIFT MT103 wire from the Hong Kong parent's bank to the WFOE's FDI capital account at a PBOC-licensed Chinese commercial bank. The MT103 must state the beneficiary WFOE name, the registered FDI project number, and a SAFE-compliant remittance purpose code for direct investment equity capital. All inbound equity remittances must be first credited to the special capital account and cannot be paid directly to suppliers. For each injection, the WFOE must submit the signed verification certificate to the bank, which then reports to SAFE through the Foreign Exchange Digital Regulatory Platform. The bank will execute the restricted settlement from the capital account into RMB, in accordance with the declared investment purpose. A Chinese certified public accountant (CPA) must issue an FDI capital verification report confirming the subscribed capital has been paid in accordance with the articles of association, and this report is mandatory before the funds can be deployed for rent, salaries, cloud hosting, or other operating expenditures. Inbound capital payments cannot be used for non-business purposes such as loans to shareholders or unauthorized securities trading.For day-to-day operational costs and SaaS licensing fees, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for USD, EUR, or cross-border RMB through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) when the counterparty is a foreign vendor. The payment must carry a valid SAFE purpose code and be backed by an invoice or service contract. For physical goods distribution, the WFOE should rely on an irrevocable documentary Letter of Credit under UCP600 for high-value purchases, with the domestic commercial bank acting as the issuing bank, and, for lower-value transactions, a Documentary Collection under URC522. Subscription revenues collected from Paddle and Lemon Squeezy will be remitted to the WFOE's foreign currency settlement account or converted to RMB via the bank; each inbound settlement must be reconciled with the aggregator's payout report and submitted to SAFE in the quarterly foreign exchange reporting cycle.Deliverable CNY forward contracts with an onshore licensed bank, supported by a genuine underlying trade or investment exposure and a signed master agreement between the WFOE and the clearing bankCross-currency interest rate swaps (CCIRS) for the purpose of matching foreign-currency liabilities or capital contributions with RMB operational cash flows, provided the notional amount is consistent with audited underlying exposuresFX swap agreements for temporary conversion between RMB and a foreign currency to cover short-term liquidity gaps or intercompany funding timing, executed only through a licensed onshore counterpartyOffshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) restricted to the Hong Kong parent as an overlay hedge on its RMB exposure, not permitted inside the WFOE's onshore banking structure because mainland banks require deliverable onshore hedging contracts with documented real exposure

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Not viable for the China-domiciled WFOE. China's banking licensing framework, administered by the PBOC and the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA), permits only licensed commercial banks, foreign-invested banks operating with Chinese banking licenses, and recognized deposit-taking institutions to hold corporate RMB deposits, provide clearing accounts, and execute capital account settlement. Foreign fintech EMI providers, digital-only challenger banks, and payment aggregators such as Paddle and Lemon Squeezy are not financial institutions in China and cannot act as the WFOE's primary treasury or cross-border clearing bank. They may be used downstream strictly as subscription and micro-SaaS payment gateways, but all proceeds must ultimately settle through an approved Chinese licensed bank or licensed third-party payment institution. Treating an offshore EMI as the corporate operating account creates an immediate regulatory breach and would be rejected in any KYC review by a Chinese account-opening bank.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion; Country-by-Country Reporting is triggered for MNE groups with consolidated revenue of at least RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: PRC GAAR and substance-over-form principles under CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulation Article 120 require the WFOE to own the core functions, assets, and risks related to its Micro-SaaS business. Core Income-Generating Activities must be performed in mainland China: software development, code maintenance, data storage, customer contracting, subscription pricing, user support, and risk management. A Hong Kong parent used only as a passive holding vehicle with no Hong Kong office or personnel will fail the beneficial ownership test and may be disregarded for treaty purposes. Hong Kong ESR and CIGA principles apply to the HK parent separately, but they do not cure a lack of substance in China; transfer pricing adjustments, treaty denial, and penalties may follow.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 41 requires related-party transactions to be priced at arm's length; Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily late-payment surcharges; State Taxation Administration may reopen prior years, make transfer pricing adjustments, and suspend preferential rate claims.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false financial statements or direct fraudulent revenue suppression may be held jointly and severally liable for restitution; they can be placed on the State Taxation Administration joint disciplinary 'Dishonest Taxpayer' blacklist, triggering travel bans, exclusion from senior management positions, and restrictions on high-consumption activities; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment of up to three years for substantial evasion, or three to seven years where the amount is huge and the evasion exceeds 30% of the tax due.
Withholding Tax Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 69 provides that a withholding agent that fails to withhold and remit tax is liable to a penalty of 50% to 300% of the amount that should have been withheld; the WFOE may also be ordered to pay the unwithheld tax where recovery from the non-resident is impossible; late-payment surcharges apply from the due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the WFOE to disregard withholding obligations may be personally liable under the corporate veil-piercing doctrine of PRC Company Law Article 20 (2023 revision Article 23); they may be disqualified as legal representatives or directors, have assets frozen through tax enforcement, and face criminal prosecution for falsely concealing taxable matters where fraudulent intent is established.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulation Article 120 empower the tax authority to apply the general anti-abuse rule and recharacterise the structure; the China-Hong Kong DTA beneficial ownership clause, supported by State Taxation Administration anti-treaty-shopping rules and OECD BEPS Action 6, allows denial of treaty relief, recalculation at the 10% statutory rate, recovery of unpaid withholding tax, and daily interest; where evasion is found, Article 63 penalties of 50% to 500% apply.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil-piercing may be invoked where the corporate form is abused to harm creditors or evade taxes: directors and shareholders of both the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent may become jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax debts; serious cases trigger criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201, cross-border travel bans under joint disciplinary mechanisms for tax dishonesty, blacklisting in the PRC Social Credit system, and confiscation of personal assets derived from the abusive structure.
