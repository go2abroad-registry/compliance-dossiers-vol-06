Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the local tax service bureau in the WFOE's registered place; electronic filing through the STA's Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB/electronic tax filing portal) at www.chinatax.gov.cn.

E-government filing portal

On incorporation, the WFOE obtains a Business License from the Market Regulation Administration bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which automatically constitutes the PRC Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. Within 30 days after the Business License is issued, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax registration ('tax information collection') in the Electronic Tax Bureau, uploading the Business License, Articles of Association, Hong Kong shareholder's Certificate of Incorporation and Business Registration certificate, director/legal representative identification documents, registered office lease/property title, basic bank account opening certificate, and appointment of financial/accounting personnel. The ETB then activates the tax types, assigns the entity's VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), and issues an electronic tax registration record. A foreign non-resident entity (for example, a Hong Kong parent) that has no separate PRC establishment does not need a domestic TIN merely to own the WFOE; however, if it independently earns PRC-source income or engages in taxable PRC activities, it must apply to the competent tax bureau for a non-resident enterprise tax number by submitting its Hong Kong incorporation and Business Registration documents, the relevant income contract, and a PRC tax representative appointment, after which the STA assigns a non-resident TIN for withholding tax and reporting purposes.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - the CN WFOE is a PRC resident limited liability company taxed at entity level, subject to Enterprise Income Tax at 25% on worldwide income and VAT on taxable turnover; it is not pass-through eligible.

Available election forms

None for entity classification; PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid-mismatch entity election. Under VAT, a small-scale taxpayer may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer, and certain small-scale taxpayers may elect simplified taxation; general VAT taxpayer status becomes mandatory once annual taxable sales exceed the statutory threshold.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All PRC legal-person companies must complete VAT registration upon business-license issuance. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises when cumulative annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. Below that threshold, the entity remains a small-scale taxpayer; monthly taxable sales up to CNY 100,000 are currently exempt from VAT under the applicable small-scale taxpayer concession.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Cross-border AI/LLM API platform services qualify for 0% VAT zero-rating if: (1) the services are supplied by the PRC WFOE to an overseas non-resident customer; (2) the services are wholly consumed outside mainland China; (3) the service falls within the statutory zero-rated cross-border software services / information system services / business process management services / offshore outsourcing categories; and (4) the taxpayer has completed the one-time cross-border taxable services zero-rating/exemption recordation in the Electronic Tax Bureau and retains supporting evidence. Physical export of goods containing or reproducing software is zero-rated upon electronic export declaration and customs release under the export tax rebate system. The WFOE may claim input VAT refund/credit attributable to zero-rated export supplies only to the extent the zero-rating is substantiated.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration / Bill of Entry issued by China Customs with export customs release code
  • Signed Bill of Lading (sea), Air Waybill (air) or courier export proof (for physical goods or media containing software)
  • Export sales contract and commercial invoice identifying the overseas buyer and stating that the AI/LLM API service or software is supplied from the PRC
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt voucher / Cross-Border Income Declaration / Inward Remittance Certificate evidencing payment received from the overseas customer
  • Electronic Tax Bureau recordation certificate confirming cross-border taxable services zero-rating or exemption
  • Service delivery and consumption evidence, including API token usage logs, server access records, system management reports, or an overseas customer attestation confirming no mainland China consumption

The WFOE must issue VAT invoices through the State Taxation Administration's Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Full digital electronic invoices (FDEI) are generated and transmitted to the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system in real time; each invoice carries a digital anti-counterfeiting QR code and tax classification code. General VAT taxpayers must use tax-control UKey software, integrate ERP/accounting systems through the ETB's API reporting interface, and submit invoice data for automatic reconciliation with VAT returns, bank payment flows and customs declarations. There is no physical fiscal stamp for FDEIs; tax clearance is achieved through real-time digital invoice validation in the ETB.

STA tax risk teams under the Golden Tax Phase IV system will match zero-rated VAT filings with customs electronic declarations, bank foreign-exchange receipts and service consumption evidence. If zero-rating is not substantiated, the tax authority recovers output VAT at the applicable rate (6% for AI/LLM API services, 13% for goods) plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the unpaid tax, and may impose an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the underpaid or over-refunded tax under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law. Falsifying export documents, using forged Inward Remittance Certificates, or fraudulently claiming export refunds can trigger criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds (Criminal Law Article 204) and issuance of false special VAT invoices (Criminal Law Article 205), with imprisonment and criminal fines for responsible individuals.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed floating RMB regime with asymmetric convertibility. Current account items are substantially liberalised: export receipts can be retained in foreign currency or settled into RMB, import and service payments are processed by designated banks against underlying contracts, invoices and customs declarations, and after-tax profits may be remitted to overseas shareholders once the bank verifies audited financials, tax payment stamps and the board resolution. Capital account items remain tightly controlled. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise cannot freely convert equity capital into RMB for prohibited uses; SAFE registration, a dedicated FDI capital account, and post-circulation monitoring are required. Equity injections may not be used to on-lend funds to related parties, to purchase domestic securities without authorisation, or to pay for assets unrelated to the declared business scope. Because the parent is a Hong Kong entity, the WFOE must demonstrate that the HK investor is the genuine ultimate beneficiary, that inbound wires have proper FDI purpose codes, and that any dividend outflow matches after-tax distributable profits. In practice, banks apply authenticity testing on every current-account remittance; any non-genuine or circular flow is declined. Convertibility therefore exists for real business transactions but not as a right for arbitrary capital flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China no longer imposes a fixed mandatory export surrender ratio. Under the current trade FX administration framework administered through SAFE's 'ASOne' trade monitoring system, export proceeds must be repatriated directly into a domestic corporate foreign exchange settlement account and must arrive by the contractual payment date agreed with the overseas buyer; where the contract does not define a date, banks conventionally treat 90 days after customs declaration or service completion as the statutory collection benchmark. Upon receipt, the WFOE may keep the foreign currency balance, or sell it to the bank for RMB at the market rate. The bank will require an export declaration matched with an invoice, a bill of lading or electronic service confirmation, and a balance of payments indirect reporting entry; only then are the funds released for use. There is no permissible channel for keeping export proceeds offshore without registration.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC); delegated to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for all foreign exchange administration

25% (PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law, Article 4); 15% preferential rate available only for qualified High and New Technology Enterprises or encouraged software/IC enterprises.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File threshold: controlled tangible-asset transfers above RMB 200 million, financial-asset transfers above RMB 100 million, intangible-asset transfers above RMB 100 million, and other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. Master File threshold: aggregate related-party revenue/expenses above RMB 1 billion. Annual related-party transaction reporting (Schedule G100000) is required regardless of amount, and contemporaneous documentation must be prepared by the filing date.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47) and special tax adjustments; a WFOE must have real personnel, servers/license assets, and management functions in China. For Hong Kong, the Economic Substance Regulations require entities claiming Hong Kong tax benefits and DTA access to demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong; a functional, IT, or financing entity must have adequate CIGA, premises, and employees. Failure to prove substance denies treaty benefits and may create employment/WFOE permanent-establishment exposure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Invoicing (Artificial Inflation of Deductions)

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of unpaid tax, a daily surcharge of 0.05%, and a monetary penalty of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; Criminal Law Articles 205 and 210 subject the issuer and user of false special VAT invoices to criminal fines and imprisonment, with aggravated penalties for large-scale invoice fraud.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve the false invoices or sign fraudulent returns can be held solidarily liable for unpaid corporate taxes where the corporate veil is pierced for abuse of corporate personality; tax authorities may impose exit restrictions/travel bans under Article 44, and individual prosecutors may pursue criminal liability leading to imprisonment up to seven years for tax evasion and asset forfeiture/restitution.
Treaty Shopping and Shell Company Abuse (Improper DTA Claim)

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules permit the tax authority to make a special tax adjustment and recharacterize the arrangement as an aggressive avoidance manipulation; the under-withheld tax becomes immediately due with interest and a penalty of 50%-500% of the tax avoided; the DTA benefit is denied under the principal purpose test and applicable OECD MLI provisions.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE who knowingly under-withheld by filing a false treaty claim may be jointly and severally liable for the uncollected withholding tax. Persons identified as tax evaders may be added to the serious-honesty-tax-offender list (blacklist), subjected to exit restrictions/travel bans, and if the scheme is executed through fraudulent instruments, criminally prosecuted as accomplices, resulting in personal fines and imprisonment up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Profit Shifting (AI API/LLM Royalty Manipulation)

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41, 44, 48 and 49 and SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42 authorize a transfer-pricing primary adjustment, secondary adjustments, interest on the adjusted tax, and penalties of 50%-150% where documentation is deficient or incomplete; the understated income is reallocated to China and the royalty payment is recharacterized as a constructive dividend subject to non-resident withholding tax.

Director personal liability: If directors caused the WFOE to enter into fictitious or abusive related-party contracts, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law (abuse of independent corporate personality causing loss to creditors or tax collection) to seize personal assets; exit restrictions may be imposed for intentional tax evasion; criminal liability under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 203 can result in imprisonment of up to seven years for the responsible legal representative and finance director.
