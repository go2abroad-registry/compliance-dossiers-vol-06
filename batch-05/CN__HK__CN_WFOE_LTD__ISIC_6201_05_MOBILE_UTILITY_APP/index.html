Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong imposes no minimum paid-up share capital, no paid-up capital deposit requirement, and no blocked escrow account rule for a private company limited by shares. The sole subscriber may declare one or more shares with no par value, or a nominal par value, and the issued shares may remain unpaid until a lawful call is made. A nominal capital structure of US$1,000 divided into 1,000 ordinary shares of US$1.00 each is recommended for bankability and operational clarity.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administered through the local competent tax service bureau with filings on the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal; Hong Kong SAR obligations, if any, are administered separately by the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) through the eTAX portal.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: The investor incorporates the wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and receives a business license containing the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which becomes the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID). Step 2: Within 30 calendar days of business license issuance, the WFOE must perform tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau by activating the USCC in the taxpayer master file. Step 3: Submit the foreign investor's Hong Kong incorporation certificate and proof of address, ultimate beneficial owner identity documents, WFOE articles of association, registered address lease, bank account opening certificate, and the foreign investment information report (FIIR) filed with SAMR/MOFCOM systems. Step 4: Receive electronic tax registration confirmation; no physical tax registration certificate is issued. Step 5: Register for VAT by electing general taxpayer status if annual taxable sales are expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or remain a small-scale taxpayer with the option to elect general taxpayer status to enable input-VAT crediting and export zero-rating. Step 6: Complete the export tax refund/exemption registration with the tax bureau, and for physical goods export, complete the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) foreign trade registration and customs declaration setup. Step 7: Any foreign non-resident entity without an establishment in China must separately obtain a non-resident taxpayer ID through the withholding agent when claiming treaty benefits or when receiving dividend, interest, royalty, or service fees from the WFOE.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

Mainland China has no US-style check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Available elections include: (a) voluntary VAT general taxpayer election below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold; (b) small-scale simplified VAT levy; (c) High and New Technology Enterprise (HNTE) election for the 15% corporate income tax rate; (d) small-low-profit enterprise preferential CIT rates where eligible; and (e) for cross-border digital services, an election to claim VAT zero-rating/refund via the export-tax-refund filing rather than simple exemption. Hong Kong SAR (if relevant) permits territorial-source offshore profit claims and group loss relief, but no hybrid check-the-box election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold for VAT registration: every WFOE must register for VAT within 30 days of incorporation; mandatory general taxpayer registration is triggered by annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000, with digital app services generally subject to 6% VAT when supplied domestically if characterized as modern/IT services.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim the 0% VAT rate with input-VAT refund for qualifying cross-border software and digital/information-technology services if: (1) the service is consumed wholly or substantially outside the PRC; (2) the recipient is an overseas enterprise or individual, including Apple/Google app-store channels where the app store operator separately remits foreign-currency proceeds; (3) a written export or marketplace distribution agreement is maintained; (4) the foreign-currency consideration is received through a licensed bank in China within the required repatriation period; and (5) the export zero-rating/exemption registration and the associated declaration are completed before the refund claim. Domestic app-store sales in China remain subject to PRC VAT at the applicable domestic rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Goods) and customs valuation/packing documents, required only for physical goods or software embedded in physical media
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill, required only for physical export shipments
  • Export contract or app-store marketplace agreement with Apple/Google
  • Bank foreign-exchange income declaration and settlement evidence evidencing receipt of export proceeds from outside the PRC
  • Apple App Store Connect payment report or Google Play financial report identifying the WFOE as developer and overseas net sales
  • Cross-border taxable service zero-rating/exemption filing record and the VAT invoice issued to the overseas customer
  • Foreign exchange repatriation records demonstrating receipt within 180 days

Mainland China requires all VAT invoices to be issued through STA-approved channels. General taxpayers use the fully digitalized electronic invoice platform integrated with Golden Tax Phase IV; invoice data is transmitted in real time to the STA Electronic Invoice Service Platform, and every VAT special invoice carries a digital stamp/fingerprint code for anti-fraud clearance. Bulk and ERP-based billing must integrate through STA-certified API interfaces. There is no physical fiscal stamping, but zero-rated export sales must be flagged in the export-tax-refund system with the substantiation bundle. Hong Kong SAR has no real-time fiscal clearance or electronic invoicing mandate beyond ordinary invoicing under the Inland Revenue Ordinance.

Export zero-rating claims are subject to documentary and field audits by the STA. If the tax authority finds the service was not genuinely consumed outside China, the foreign-exchange proceeds were not repatriated within the 180-day period, or the supporting documents are false, it will disallow the zero rating, claw back all input-VAT refunds, assess the underpaid VAT with a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded under the Tax Administration and Collection Law, and refer willful cases to criminal prosecution for export-tax-refund fraud under the Criminal Law. In fraud cases, the assessment period is unlimited and responsible officers/beneficial owners may be held personally liable.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates an administrative, license-based foreign exchange regime. The Renminbi is convertible on the current account for trade and service transactions, but the capital account remains heavily controlled. Equity capital injected into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise must be routed through a dedicated foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-designated commercial bank. Once settled into RMB, funds are subject to a negative list prohibiting investment in securities, real estate, and non-core business uses. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong shareholder require an audited financial statement, tax clearance certificate, board resolution, and proof that underlying foreign exchange registration is valid. The so-called 'voluntary surrender' framework abolished mandatory conversion quotas on export proceeds, but receipts from overseas app store sales must still be repatriated and lodged in an export proceeds account. SAFE requires the Customer/User Account, the Enterprise Account, and the bank reconciliation trail to match exactly so that the profit sharing with Apple, Google, or local distribution partners is transparent. Any mismatch in the purpose of payment is likely to trigger stoppage and administrative investigation. Overall, the currency is convertible in practice for operating WFOEs only if documentary compliance is flawless; treasury officers must anticipate long settlement times, mandatory pre-registration, and refusal of same-day onward FX trades without a verified underlying trade contract.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's prevailing framework, export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated through a designated export proceeds account within 180 days after the date of receipt. There is no fixed statutory surrender ratio; the mandatory conversion quota was abolished. Exporters may retain the foreign currency in a domestic account indefinitely, provided the proceeds are backed by verified trade documents. However, for mobile utility app companies, cross-border digital receipts from app stores must be evidenced by quarterly sales reports, licensing agreements, and tax invoices before the bank will allow any onward RMB conversion or offset against operating expenses.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China

Airwallex Fit: High

Low - Not viable for a mainland China WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, etc.) are not licensed by SAFE or the PBOC to maintain domestic RMB accounts or to act as FX settlement agents for foreign-invested enterprises in mainland China. Such EMIs cannot provide legal capital verification, tax withholding, or SAFE registration services. Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from the Hong Kong parent must be remitted through SWIFT MT103 to a SAFE-registered capital account at a licenced domestic commercial bank. The remittance purpose code for equity injection is 'Foreign Direct Investment into an Operating Enterprise' (SAFE code 622). The sending bank in Hong Kong must receive the prior SAFE 'Foreign Exchange Registration Notice' issued for the WFOE. After the funds are credited and confirmed against the original certificate of capital contribution, a registered Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report before the bank can release RMB working capital. Each disbursement from the capital settled account must be linked to the approved business scope; any expense outside the negative list requires a supplementary SAFE filing.Outbound trade and service disbursements, including license fees, marketing expenses, cloud server fees, or developer service fees, should settle through taxable service trade rails using SWIFT MT103 with full attached commercial contracts and bank-issued tax payment clearance letters. For documentary trade settlement, the bank can support Commercial Letters of Credit and Documentary Collections only if underlying physical goods or service deliverables are clearly evidenced by bill of lading or signed delivery reports. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 100 million for aggregate annual related-party transactions triggering Local File contemporaneous documentation; RMB 200 million for tangible-property-related transactions; RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers and licences, financing, and service-related transactions; Master File is required where the Hong Kong or ultimate parent group consolidated revenue for the preceding fiscal year exceeds RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies general anti-avoidance rules, substance-over-form analysis, and beneficial ownership requirements under SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018. The Hong Kong shareholder must demonstrate Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, including active strategic decision-making, accounting, finance, asset management, and employment, rather than acting as a mailbox or conduit. The PRC WFOE itself must maintain operational substance in China, including office premises, employees, decision-making functions, bank accounts, and records supporting its mobile app business; a mere contractual title or artificial IP holding arrangement will be disregarded for tax purposes, and the GAAR may recharacterise profit flows to taxable Chinese source.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate tax evasion and false revenue reporting

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-paid tax plus late payment interest at 0.05% per day; Criminal Law Article 201 criminalises tax evasion exceeding 10% of the tax due and the statutory amount threshold, with imprisonment up to three years for ordinary amounts and three to seven years plus fines for large-amount evasion.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil can be pierced under the PRC Company Law where the WFOE is used as an instrument to evade tax liabilities; the legal representative and responsible directors may be jointly liable to restore diverted revenue, face criminal prosecution as directly responsible persons under Criminal Law Article 211, and be blacklisted under exit-control procedures preventing departure from China until tax liabilities and penalties are settled.
DTA treaty abuse and artificial Hong Kong shell company

Statutory sanction: The China-HK DTA benefit is denied under beneficial ownership doctrine; the withholding difference to the 10% statutory rate becomes payable, late surcharge accrues at 0.05% per day, and the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47 and Article 48 authorise the tax authority to make a general anti-avoidance adjustment with mandatory interest and possible penalties of 50% to 500% under Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 63 if false declarations were made.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure the WFOE with a sham HK shareholder may be personally liable for unpaid withholding where they were the controlling minds of the arrangement; they may be subjected to civil restitution claims by the company or creditors, criminal tax liability if the structure is used to conceal evasion, and travel bans under tax enforcement blacklisting while the investigation and adjustment are pending.
Transfer-pricing documentation failure and non-arm's-length profit stripping

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 requires related-party transactions to reflect arm's-length pricing; Articles 43 and 44 allow the tax authority to adjust taxable income by deemed methods where documentation is absent or unreliable; Tax Collection and Administration Law Article 62 imposes late filing or record-keeping penalties up to RMB 10,000, Article 64 imposes fines of 50% to 500% of the under-paid tax for false or low tax declarations, and Article 44 permits carry-over reassessment and adjustment with daily interest at 0.05%.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign fraudulent TP reports, authorize inflated intercompany charges, or allow distribution of profits derived from non-arm's-length arrangements can be sued for breach of fiduciary duty and may be required to repay unlawful distributions; Chinese tax authorities may lift the corporate veil in serious fraud, and responsible directors face criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 as well as exit-control restrictions preventing travel abroad until full tax restitution and penalties are paid.
