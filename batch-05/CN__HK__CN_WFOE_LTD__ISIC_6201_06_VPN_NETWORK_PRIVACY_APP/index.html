Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic administration is conducted through the National Unified Electronic Tax Bureau online portal and the provincial-level e-tax platforms

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the China WFOE, the State Administration for Market Regulation issues a business license bearing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which operates as the sole statutory TIN and VAT registration identifier. Tax registration is completed automatically under the multi-license integration reform. The WFOE must then activate its e-tax account, confirm its VAT taxpayer type, and complete payroll and social insurance registration with the local competent tax authority. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment or place of business that derives China-source income must file a non-resident tax registration and withholding profile with the competent tax authority; the STA allocates a Non-Resident Taxpayer Identification Number, which is also required before treaty relief under the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement can be claimed.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No registration threshold for a WFOE with a Chinese business license; VAT registration is triggered by the first taxable supply. For mandatory general VAT taxpayer status, the annual taxable turnover threshold is RMB 5,000,000; a WFOE may elect general taxpayer status below this threshold where input VAT recovery and export zero-rating are required.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A China WFOE may apply the 0% VAT rate to exported physical goods only where the goods physically depart China Customs territory under customs supervision, the sale is made to an overseas buyer, and consideration is received from outside China. For qualifying cross-border digital services, 0% applies only when services are supplied to an overseas entity, are wholly consumed outside China, and fall within the statutory zero-rated categories, which include software services, information system services, R&D services, and offshore outsourcing services. A VPN network privacy app marketed as Network-Security SaaS will be accepted as zero-rated only if the contractual and technical documentation characterizes the service as a software or information system service. If the STA recharacterizes the supply as a value-added telecommunications service, the supply falls under the cross-border service exemption rather than zero-rating, meaning input VAT is not refundable and no 0% entitlement exists.

  • China Customs Export Goods Declaration (export customs declaration) for physical goods
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or other transport document evidencing export departure
  • Export sales contract or purchase order
  • Commercial export invoice for the transaction
  • Bank foreign exchange collection voucher or Cross-Border RMB receipt confirmation from the settlement bank
  • SAFE foreign exchange income declaration report relating to the export proceeds
  • For cross-border SaaS/software services: offshore service contract, evidence of complete consumption outside China such as IP geolocation or customer-server usage records, service acceptance certificate, and foreign exchange receipt proof

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system and the unified VAT invoice management system. All-eletronic invoices are issued through the national electronic invoice service platform, which transmits invoice data in near real-time to the STA using a state-controlled XML data package with a digital encryption stamp and unique invoice code. Taxpayers must connect to the platform through approved API channels or web-based issuance. Customs declarations, export logistics data, foreign exchange receipt records, and VAT invoice data are cross-matched through the China International Trade Single Window and the STA's tax information systems to detect mismatches and fraudulent invoices.

The STA and customs authorities may disallow an export zero-rating claim if the supporting customs, logistics, invoicing, or foreign exchange repatriation records are missing, inconsistent, or unverifiable. In such cases, the tax authority will reassess VAT at the applicable domestic rate, normally 6% for the SaaS/software service or 13% for goods, and will collect late payment interest from the original due date. Under the Tax Collection Administration Law, penalties for underpaid VAT range from 50% to 500% of the assessed amount. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims carry specific penalties of 100% to 500% of the refunded amount, downgrade of the enterprise's export tax refund credit rating under the classified management system, and potential criminal referral for fraudulent export tax refund under the Criminal Law.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The currency convertibility regime in mainland China is a controlled managed-float system, where the Renminbi (CNY) is fully convertible only on the current account, including trade in goods and services, but remains heavily regulated on the capital account. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) is permitted to receive foreign direct investment (FDI) proceeds and cross-border operational remittances, but every inbound and outbound transfer must be routed through a designated domestic commercial bank that acts as a SAFE-licensed settlement bank. Export proceeds derived from the sale of VPN software subscriptions or digital privacy applications must be repatriated to China within the statutory timeline and either settled into CNY or retained in a foreign currency account, subject to SAFE reporting. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is allowed only after audited financial statements are filed, corporate income tax is settled, and supporting documentation is presented to the bank for post-verification. Dividends are considered current-account items and can be transferred out without pre-approval, but the bank will require tax clearance certificates and board resolutions. Capital account conversion, such as injecting loan principal, purchasing overseas M&A targets, or making outbound portfolio investments, requires individual approvals and registration with SAFE. The WFOE will encounter meaningful friction when attempting to convert CNY into foreign exchange for capital outflows that are not clearly tied to genuine trade or service transactions. Surrender-of-export-proceeds rules apply to all receipts from cross-border SaaS contracts, and the company must maintain meticulous records to demonstrate that the invoice, the amount, and the service delivery are consistent with the declared purpose code. In practice, the bank will enforce a look-through review on each conversion, checking that the underlying network security product does not violate content-control regulations or encryption export restrictions. Consequently, the treasury must allocate lead time of at least ten business days for non-standard cross-border payments and maintain parallel accounting in CNY and USD to reconcile with bank records.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under current SAFE regulations, all export proceeds and service-based foreign currency receipts must be repatriated to mainland China within 180 days from the date of customs declaration or from the date the service invoice falls due. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio for export proceeds, but the WFOE must either keep the funds in an approved foreign currency settlement account or sell them to a designated bank for CNY. Any retention of more than 30 days beyond the payment due date requires a written justification and SAFE filing, while any failure to repatriate or unreasonable delay can trigger audit penalties. For VPN and digital privacy software subscriptions, the enterprise is treated as an exporter of software services under ISIC/NAICS classification, and therefore must quote the correct service item code, such as 'Bank Service Code 7261' or SAFE-approved equivalent for network security software, on all inbound wires. If the buyer is a related Hong Kong entity, the WFOE must also file an intra-group service transfer pricing documentation to satisfy both SAFE and the State Taxation Administration.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore fintech EMI and digital banking platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and Revolut Business have no legal standing to provide primary transaction accounts for a mainland China WFOE. PBOC and SAFE regulations mandate that all domestic foreign exchange receipts and payments for a resident WFOE must clear through a licensed Chinese commercial bank with a valid foreign exchange settlement and sales license. EMIs do not hold such licenses; their virtual accounts, even if branded as Chinese entity accounts operated from Hong Kong, are not recognized as 'bank accounts' by Chinese regulators and cannot be used for capital verification, tax refunds, employee payroll denominated in CNY, or statutory customs declarations. In practice, an EMIs account may be used only as a secondary collection tool for overseas merchant settlement, provided the funds are immediately swept to a domestic Tier-1 bank and declared through the proper purpose code. However, the compliance risk is high because one audit finding of unlicensed cross-border settlement could invalidate the WFOEs foreign exchange filing and trigger reputational sanctions. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should mandate a domestic Tier-1 bank as the operational spine and use offshore EMIs solely for acquiring international SaaS payments, never as a liquidity storefront.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong parent into the mainland WFOE must be executed through a SWIFT MT103 wire from the parent accounts in Hong Kong or another international financial center to the WFOEs SAFE-designated FDI capital deposit account held at one of the Tier-1 Chinese banks. The remitter must use SAFE's foreign exchange purpose code 'Investment - Equities - Foreign Direct Investment' corresponding to the Chinese national statistics classification, and the narrative field must reference the FDI annual inspection registration number obtained from the National Enterprise Credit Information System. Upon receipt, the bank will perform a foreign exchange capital registration, update the SAFE digital system, and request the WFOE to submit the foreign investment legal entity certificate, the approval certificate from the Ministry of Commerce or local bureau, and a capital transfer confirmation letter signed by the statutory director. The HKD or USD wire is then converted into CNY at the banks market-regulated rate, or maintained in USD in the capital account if retained for future procurement of overseas servers. Within six months of injection, the WFOE must engage a Chinese certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report confirming that the paid-in capital matches the approved registered capital; this report is the gateway for the WFOE to use the funds for payroll, rent, software development, and daily operations. To avoid regulatory delays, the treasury should pre-arrange the precise booking date with the receiving bank, as CIPS or SWIFT wires may be suspended if the purpose code or the beneficiary account name does not match the SAFE profile.For trade settlements involving the VPN network privacy software, the WFOE will find that the dominant clearing rail is SWIFT MT103 for high-value service fees, license renewals, and dividends to the Hong Kong parent. Because the underlying product is a digital service, there is no physical bill of lading, so the bank requires download certificates, digital delivery logs, and invoice stamps from the global customer. For procurement of submarine cable capacity, server hardware, or colocation services where the counterparty is a foreign cloud provider such as AWS or Oracle Cloud, the WFOE may also utilize commercial letters of credit (LCs) or documentary collection (D/C) if the vendor is based in a restricted jurisdiction, but most cloud contracts are settled by direct wire transfer under the service trade entry. In practice, the WFOE should establish a dedicated 'CNY/USD operational sweep structure' where all incoming USD from Stripe and Paymentwall is netted and settled to RMB through the designated Tier-1 bank upon each business day, while a standing instruction bank account is reserved for approved outbound wire payments. The bank will apply a 'P-procedure' for large outbound wires above USD 300,000, requiring strong evidence of the commercial relationship, the delivery report, and the tax withholding certificate, if the payment is a royalty or dividend. Since the VPN software may be classified as a restricted export to certain sanctioned countries, the bank will screen all beneficiaries against the OFAC, EU, and UN sanctions lists, and the WFOE must provide end-user certificates from the counterparties. A compliant outbound payment timing would be three to five business days for standard service fees, and ten to fifteen business days if the transaction requires a special purpose code or SAFE approval.Mainland China onshore RMB/USD deliverable forward contracts up to one year tenor, executed through the Tier-1 bank using a documented trade exposure, specifically eligible for hedging the WFOEs USD subscription revenue that is converted to CNY on a rolling monthly basis.Offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) on USD/CNH, available to the Hong Kong parent in Hong Kong, which can hedge the expected dividend flows from the WFOE but cannot be settled in CNY onshore; the hedge must be structured in a way that does not interfere with the WFOEs SAFE-registered capital flows.Cross-currency swaps (CCS) between CNY and USD for long-term capital expenditure, allowed only when the underlying is an investment in fixed assets, the notional aligns with the capital profile, and the swap counterparty is the same PBOC-registered bank that holds the WFOEs capital account.Options on USD/CNY purchased onshore but not sold, with a zero-cost collar strategy permitted for the WFOE, provided the bank completes the risk suitability assessment and the total premium is expensed in accordance with the foreign exchange derivatives self-regulatory rules. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore fintech EMI and digital banking platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, Stripe Treasury, and Revolut Business have no legal standing to provide primary transaction accounts for a mainland China WFOE. PBOC and SAFE regulations mandate that all domestic foreign exchange receipts and payments for a resident WFOE must clear through a licensed Chinese commercial bank with a valid foreign exchange settlement and sales license. EMIs do not hold such licenses; their virtual accounts, even if branded as Chinese entity accounts operated from Hong Kong, are not recognized as 'bank accounts' by Chinese regulators and cannot be used for capital verification, tax refunds, employee payroll denominated in CNY, or statutory customs declarations. In practice, an EMIs account may be used only as a secondary collection tool for overseas merchant settlement, provided the funds are immediately swept to a domestic Tier-1 bank and declared through the proper purpose code. However, the compliance risk is high because one audit finding of unlicensed cross-border settlement could invalidate the WFOEs foreign exchange filing and trigger reputational sanctions. Therefore, the treasury blueprint should mandate a domestic Tier-1 bank as the operational spine and use offshore EMIs solely for acquiring international SaaS payments, never as a liquidity storefront.
