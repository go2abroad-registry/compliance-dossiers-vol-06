Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None - no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement for an ISIC 6201 software and cloud WFOE. The registered capital is fixed in the Articles of Association and subscribed by the Hong Kong shareholder; under the 2023 PRC Company Law the subscription may be paid in installments but must be fully paid within five years from formation. Capital contribution may be cash or non-cash property, but non-cash property must be appraised and transferred to the Company. The capital amount must be reasonably related to actual business needs.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China; tax filings and registrations are performed through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal and the nationwide Electronic Invoice Service Platform operated by the STA's provincial/municipal tax service branches at the WFOE's domicile.

E-government filing portal

Stage A: Incorporate the WFOE with the Administration for Market Regulation; the AMR issues an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code, which automatically functions as the WFOE's taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes. Stage B: Within 30 calendar days of the business license issuance, the WFOE's legal representative or authorized tax agent must complete the supplementary tax registration through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau, confirm the resident enterprise tax status, designate a resident financial/accounting contact, and link the Unified Social Credit Code to the entity's tax account. Stage C: If the WFOE intends to issue special VAT invoices and claim input VAT credits, it must complete VAT general taxpayer registration under the VAT Implementing Rules; below the statutory turnover threshold the entity may elect small-scale taxpayer status. Stage D: For the non-resident Hong Kong parent, no separate standalone TIN is required if it has no permanent establishment in China; instead, the WFOE acts as withholding agent and files the non-resident enterprise withholding registration with the STA, and the Hong Kong parent obtains a non-resident enterprise taxpayer identity number through that withholding registration procedure when applying for treaty benefits or withholding tax reductions. Stage E: The Unified Social Credit Code must be integrated with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) records and, if physical exports are added to the ERP/CRM business, with the customs export registration code to support cross-border foreign exchange receipts and export VAT zero-rating substantiation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The PRC WFOE is a Chinese resident enterprise taxable on its worldwide income as a separate entity at the 25% corporate income tax rate; the Hong Kong parent is a separate non-resident corporate investor. For VAT purposes the WFOE defaults to general VAT taxpayer status when standardized annual taxable sales exceed the statutory threshold, or may elect small-scale taxpayer status below the threshold.

Available election forms

None for entity classification: PRC tax law does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections. The only material election is VAT taxpayer status: an enterprise with annual standardized taxable sales not exceeding RMB 5,000,000 may elect small-scale VAT taxpayer treatment, but registration as a general VAT taxpayer is mandatory once the threshold is exceeded and, once elected or required, the general taxpayer status remains effective for the statutory period. There are no CIT pass-through elections available to a WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual standardized taxable sales; exceeding this threshold triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration. A WFOE may voluntarily register as a general VAT taxpayer below the threshold in order to recover input VAT on ERP/CRM cloud software development and operating costs.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods, zero-rated export VAT applies when goods physically leave mainland China through a customs clearance point, the exporter files an Export Goods Declaration Form, the goods are exported under the WFOE's customs registration, full transportation evidence is retained, and export proceeds are repatriated through a SAFE-monitored channel. For the WFOE's vertical ERP/CRM cloud services, cross-border software as a service, software maintenance and related information system services supplied to a Hong Kong or other non-resident overseas recipient are eligible for zero-rating or VAT exemption under the STA's cross-border taxable services measures when the service is provided to an overseas enterprise, the service is wholly consumed outside mainland China, the service contract is executed in writing, and payment is received in foreign currency from outside China. Services rendered to the overseas company's permanent establishment or branch located in mainland China, or services that relate to real estate, goods, or taxable activities physically located or performed in mainland China, are ineligible and remain subject to 6% VAT.

  • Goods: Customs Export Goods Declaration Form with electronic customs clearance record evidencing physical export from mainland China.
  • Goods: transport document proving physical movement, namely Bill of Lading, multimodal waybill, or air waybill.
  • Goods and services: bank-issued foreign exchange receipt certificate, including the Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank/SAFE export proceeds collection confirmation, showing the overseas purchaser remitted the export/service consideration.
  • Services: signed cross-border software subscription/SaaS agreement identifying the Hong Kong or other overseas recipient and the ERP/CRM services to be consumed outside mainland China.
  • Services: overseas service acceptance certificate, usage/server access logs, or the overseas client's written confirmation evidencing that the cloud software services were wholly consumed offshore.
  • Registration files: customs registration/export-record code if physical goods are exported, together with the SAFE export collection and payment verification records supporting the 180-day foreign exchange repatriation requirement.
  • Invoice evidence: the VAT invoice issued for the zero-rated or exempt transaction as generated through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform, with the approved zero-rating/exemption filing reference.

The WFOE must issue fully digitalized electronic invoices through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform of the State Taxation Administration, which is integrated with the Golden Tax System Phase IV. Each invoice is assigned a unified national invoice number and cryptographic digital signature in near real-time; there is no separate physical anti-fraud stamp. For special VAT invoices used to support input VAT credits, the buyer, invoicing party, amount and VAT amount must be validated/cleared through the platform. For cross-border zero-rated or VAT-exempt software services, the taxpayer must file the cross-border taxable service zero-rating/exemption record with transaction contracts, foreign exchange receipt evidence and offshore consumption documents attached through the Electronic Tax Bureau before or at the time of issuing the relevant invoice.

The STA has authority to conduct retrospective audits, including opening prior years where export zero-rating, VAT exemption, or input credit claims are suspect. If the zero-rated export claim or VAT exemption is denied due to false documentation, missing foreign exchange repatriation, diversion to domestic consumption, or lack of offshore substance, the taxpayer must repay the full VAT avoided or refunded, with daily late-payment surcharges of 0.05% of the overdue tax, plus administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection. Where forged Customs export declarations, fabricated service contracts, counterfeit foreign exchange receipts, or circular offshore schemes are used to obtain export VAT refunds, the case will be referred to Customs and Public Security authorities for criminal investigation, with fines and terms of imprisonment authorized under the Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud. In addition, SAFE may impose monetary penalties for failure to repatriate export proceeds within the statutory 180-day verification window, and the tax authority will claw back any related export tax refunds and VAT exemption benefits together with interest.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate system under which current-account payments and receipts are convertible in principle, while capital-account transactions remain subject to prior registration, approval, or quota controls. For the WFOE, the distinction is operationally decisive. Software export and CRM-service invoices denominated in foreign currency are current-account proceeds and can be collected into a designated bank foreign-exchange settlement account without case-by-case approval, provided the transactions are accurately declared under SAFE trade-monitoring codes. Surplus foreign exchange may be retained or converted to RMB at the onshore market rate. Dividend and profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent also flow through the current account, but banks require audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificates, and a board resolution declaring and allocating profits; any mismatch in these documents will halt payment. Registered capital injection from the Hong Kong parent is a capital-account inflow and requires an SAFE Foreign Direct Investment registration, a dedicated foreign-currency capital account, and compliance with the bank's capital-account payment rules. Conversion is permitted only against verified payment purposes, and arbitrary settlement or speculative conversion is prohibited. The Hong Kong jurisdiction imposes no exchange controls, but it cannot create a compliance shortcut for the mainland WFOE's SAFE-regulated treasury operations.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to China within not more than 180 calendar days from the date of customs export declaration, unless SAFE approves an extension for documented exceptional circumstances. Mandatory conversion ratio: none; the WFOE may retain 100% of export proceeds in a designated foreign-currency settlement account or voluntarily convert any portion into RMB at the onshore exchange rate. Full remittance, bank crediting, and underlying trade-inspection records must be preserved for SAFE audit.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with supervisory delegation to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for trade and balance-of-payments controls; the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) regulates the Hong Kong parent but does not grant convertibility relief to the mainland WFOE.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI solutions such as programmatic wallet platforms, virtual IBAN issuers, and fintech payment gateways are not viable for the mainland WFOE's onshore treasury operations. They generally lack a PBOC banking license, do not hold CIPS/CNAPS clearing membership, and are legally prohibited from accepting deposits or maintaining RMB settlement accounts for a China-registered entity. The WFOE must therefore use a licensed domestic bank for all collection, payroll, tax, and SAFE-visible capital flows. An offshore EMI, if licensed in Hong Kong, may support the Hong Kong parent's ancillary receivables or FX conversion, but should never be used as a pass-through account for WFOE registered capital injection because the transaction would be stripped of its SAFE compliance trail. Viability rating: Low for WFOE onshore clearing; Limited, and strictly auxiliary, for the Hong Kong parent.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound funds from the Hong Kong parent must be remitted by SWIFT MT103 or equivalent ISO 20022 message with a clearly stated purpose code restricted to the SAFE Foreign Direct Investment capital-injection scope, such as equity registered capital contribution or the corresponding SAFE transaction-code category; under no circumstances may the remittance be characterized as a general trade or services payment. Prior to execution, the WFOE must complete the FDI Foreign-Investment Information Report with the local market-regulation authority and SAFE, obtain the capital-account opening confirmation from the designated domestic bank, and verify that the remitted amount does not exceed the registered capital schedule. The funds must be credited to the dedicated FDI Foreign Currency Capital Account, after which the bank performs foreign-exchange settlement under the payment-first conversion-later capital-account monitoring protocol. Once credited, a Chinese certified public accountant issues a capital verification certificate, which forms the basis for the bank to release the funds to operating RMB accounts and for the market-regulation authority to update the equity contribution records.For outbound service and license payments, the primary rail is SWIFT MT103 with full contract, invoice, withholding-tax certificate, and SAFE transaction declaration. For physical hardware purchases or on-premises software consignments, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) or Documentary Collection (DP/DA) can be used, although the cloud vertical will mostly rely on clean payments under direct agreements. CNY-denominated settlements to mainland or Hong Kong counterparts may clear through CIPS; in all outbound cases, the bank will verify tax payment before release and the WFOE should maintain a rolling SAFE trade-inspection file.Onshore deliverable foreign-exchange forward contracts for genuine current-account and foreign-debt flows, with back-to-back underlying documents and maturity matched to the exposure; speculative forwards are prohibited.Onshore foreign-exchange swap transactions combining spot and forward legs, used to manage short-term RMB/USD/HKD liquidity mismatch while maintaining SAFE documentation.Cross-currency swap agreements for registered foreign-currency loans, capped by the approved foreign-debt quota and requiring SAFE registration before drawdown.Offshore RMB non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) solely at the Hong Kong parent treasury level; not permissible as a hedging vehicle for the mainland WFOE's onshore books because the WFOE has no offshore NDF access under current SAFE rules.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI solutions such as programmatic wallet platforms, virtual IBAN issuers, and fintech payment gateways are not viable for the mainland WFOE's onshore treasury operations. They generally lack a PBOC banking license, do not hold CIPS/CNAPS clearing membership, and are legally prohibited from accepting deposits or maintaining RMB settlement accounts for a China-registered entity. The WFOE must therefore use a licensed domestic bank for all collection, payroll, tax, and SAFE-visible capital flows. An offshore EMI, if licensed in Hong Kong, may support the Hong Kong parent's ancillary receivables or FX conversion, but should never be used as a pass-through account for WFOE registered capital injection because the transaction would be stripped of its SAFE compliance trail. Viability rating: Low for WFOE onshore clearing; Limited, and strictly auxiliary, for the Hong Kong parent.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB-denominated contemporaneous documentation thresholds under Public Notice 2016 No.42: Local File preparation is mandatory where annual related-party tangible-property transactions exceed RMB 200 million; intangible-property transfers exceed RMB 100 million; related-party service transactions exceed RMB 100 million; related-party financial transactions (loans, guarantees, advances) exceed RMB 100 million; or total other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is required where the consolidated group's annual related-party revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion or the group otherwise meets the published threshold. Documentation must be prepared in Chinese and submitted to the tax authority upon request or within the prescribed period after the annual CIT filing.

Economic substance scope: The structure must satisfy the Economic Substance Regulations doctrine and the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) test. The Hong Kong parent cannot simply hold legal title to Chinese-developed software, ring-fence invoices, or collect dividends without local decision-making, employees, offices, bank accounts and risk management in Hong Kong. The Chinese WFOE must retain or compensate for the functions actually performed in China: software development, cloud hosting, customer contracting, implementation and support. If the Hong Kong company is a shell or the arrangement has no genuine business purpose, the PRC tax authority may disregard the legal form, recharacterise the Hong Kong entity as a hybrid branch, assert a permanent establishment in China, or apply the Principal Purpose Test to deny treaty benefits. For Hong Kong tax purposes, extraterritorially sourced income may be non-taxable, but only where substantive economic activities are carried on in Hong Kong; passive royalty or dividend holding without CIGA undermines both treaty claims and Hong Kong residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law: recovery of the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Article 201 of the PRC Criminal Law: criminal tax-evasion liability where the amount evaded exceeds RMB 100,000 and exceeds 10% of the tax payable, carrying up to three years' imprisonment for ordinary cases and up to seven years plus fines for large amounts with a high proportion of evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false returns or authorise offshore routing can be made personally liable through veil-piercing orders for the recovery of unpaid tax, interest and penalties, with civil asset restitution from personal and family assets. They will also be placed on the exit-control/blacklisting system, preventing departure from China until the tax debt is settled; in fraud cases, criminal imprisonment of up to seven years may be imposed on the responsible director or de facto controller.
AML / Money Laundering via Payment Rails

Statutory sanction: Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law criminalises money laundering of the proceeds of tax crimes, with imprisonment from five up to ten years and confiscation of assets for aggravated cases. The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law obligates the payment institution, the WFOE and its directors to implement customer due diligence and suspicious-transaction reporting; violations attract administrative fines up to RMB 5 million for institutions and personal fines for responsible individuals, together with suspension of payment licences or disqualification of directors.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto officers who design or benefit from offshore collection schemes are personally exposed to frozen bank accounts, seizure of property, joint and several civil restitution to the tax authority, exit-control blacklisting and travel bans while proceedings are pending. In criminal AML cases, imprisonment of up to ten years may be imposed, and the corporate veil will be pierced if the WFOE is shown to be a façade used to carry out illicit money flows.
