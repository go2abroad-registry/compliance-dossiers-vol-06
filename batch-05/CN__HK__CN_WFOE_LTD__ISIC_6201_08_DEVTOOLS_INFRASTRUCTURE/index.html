Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through local tax service bureaus; online electronic portal: China Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains a business license from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the one-license-one-code reform; the license carries an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) that functions as the mandatory taxpayer identification number for both corporate income tax and VAT. Tax registration is effected automatically through SAMR/STA data sharing, after which the WFOE must activate its tax profile on the local STA e-Tax portal and file supplementary registrations, including VAT taxpayer status, legal representative, bank account, and accounting-policy details. A foreign non-resident entity with no Chinese establishment that needs a Chinese TIN must separately file a non-resident tax registration with the competent local tax office, providing notarized/apostilled incorporation documents (e.g., Hong Kong SAR certificate of incorporation, where applicable), appointment of a tax agent and local address for service, bank account proof, and treaty/PE analysis; the STA then assigns a non-resident taxpayer identification number. The WFOE is not eligible for pass-through status and is automatically a resident enterprise.

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 of annual VAT-taxable turnover for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; every newly incorporated WFOE must complete VAT registration immediately upon business-license issuance, regardless of turnover. Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies only when the goods are sold to an overseas customer, physically exported from the PRC customs territory, and evidenced by an electronic customs export declaration, transport documents, and foreign-exchange repatriation. For qualifying cross-border digital/cloud services, including software development, database, API monitoring, and DevOps platform services, 0% VAT applies when the recipient is an overseas enterprise, the service is delivered and wholly consumed outside mainland China, there is no domestic taxable consumption, and the WFOE retains the contract, service invoice, overseas recipient registration evidence, delivery/access records, and bank FX collection record. The WFOE must be a VAT general taxpayer to claim zero-rating or export rebate; the domestic cloud-service rate, when not exported, is 6%.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a multifaceted currency control regime administered by the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE). The renminbi is fully convertible for current account items, including trade in goods and services, dividends, interest, royalties, and wages, subject to genuine transaction verification. Capital account items, in contrast, remain strictly controlled and require case-by-case approval, filing, or registration with SAFE. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) funded from Hong Kong must inject its registered capital through a dedicated Renminbi or foreign-currency capital account established at a domestic bank under a Foreign Investment Information Reporting (FIIR) confirmation, and the paid-in capital must be verified through the SAFE system before operational use. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted on a current account basis only after audited financial statements, tax clearance, and bank documentary review; no separate SAFE approval is required, but the commercial bank must validate the distribution resolution, prior-year statutory audit, and withholding tax payment certificates. Surrender requirements apply to export proceeds: SAFE regulations require trade-related foreign currency receipts to be repatriated to China and, depending on the currency balance position, converted into renminbi or retained in a foreign currency settlement account. Since 2019, the 100% mandatory surrender requirement has been largely removed, but banks retain monitoring obligations and large or unusually timed receipts may trigger explanations. Overall, the regime favors trade-linked liquidity orchestration through legitimate banking channels, while capital market investments, offshore loans, and outbound guarantees remain tightly monitored and demand robust documentary defense.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into China through designated banks; the current SAFE settlement framework does not impose a compulsory conversion ratio or fixed surrender window for ordinary trade receipts, but funds must be credited to a domestic foreign-currency settlement account and reported through the As-One system, and enterprises may voluntarily convert to renminbi at prevailing interbank rates. For large receipts, trade credit reporting, and advance payment registrations, banks may require underlying contracts, customs declaration records, VAT invoices, and payment justifications within the statutory trade-receipts collection period.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Stripe Treasury, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and other non-licensed payment institutions, have very low to negligible viability as primary banking rails for a mainland China WFOE. China's licencing regime under the PBOC and SAFE restricts depository, foreign exchange settlement, and capital account services to domestic licensed banks and qualified payment institutions holding a Payment Business License. Foreign EMIs are legally barred from maintaining demand deposit or capital injection accounts for WFOEs, and capital account credits must flow through domestic SAFE-designated banks. Offshore EMIs may only serve as secondary invoicing or collection tools for overseas sales, provided the underlying transaction is legitimately routed and the funds are later repatriated through compliant trade settlement channels. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks as the primary liquidity and clearing rails, while EMIs may be used only for non-China receivables collection in jurisdictions where they hold local licences.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the injection amount to the WFOE's SAFE-designated capital foreign exchange account at a Tier-1 domestic bank. The application must indicate the remittance purpose under the Foreign Exchange Current and Capital Account Transaction Classification as 'Foreign Direct Investment' with the relevant SA code for equity injection or round-trip investment, typically '622' for investment in an enterprise in the customs territory. The bank will require the Foreign Investment Information Reporting (FIIR) confirmation, the WFOE's business license, the investment agreement, and proof of parent company creditworthiness. SAFE's capital verification must align the RMB equivalent at the buying rate on the transaction date, after which funds may be transferred to the RMB basic deposit account. Documentary Collections are appropriate for recurring, low-risk trade flows where the seller and buyer maintain a long-standing relationship. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMI platforms, including Stripe Treasury, Mercury, Airwallex, Currencycloud, and other non-licensed payment institutions, have very low to negligible viability as primary banking rails for a mainland China WFOE. China's licencing regime under the PBOC and SAFE restricts depository, foreign exchange settlement, and capital account services to domestic licensed banks and qualified payment institutions holding a Payment Business License. Foreign EMIs are legally barred from maintaining demand deposit or capital injection accounts for WFOEs, and capital account credits must flow through domestic SAFE-designated banks. Offshore EMIs may only serve as secondary invoicing or collection tools for overseas sales, provided the underlying transaction is legitimately routed and the funds are later repatriated through compliant trade settlement channels. Therefore, the treasury blueprint must rely on Tier-1 domestic commercial banks as the primary liquidity and clearing rails, while EMIs may be used only for non-China receivables collection in jurisdictions where they hold local licences.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A Local File is mandatory when related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceed CNY 200 million for tangible property transfers, CNY 100 million for intangible property transfers, CNY 100 million for financial asset transfers, or CNY 100 million for other related-party transactions. A Master File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach CNY 1 billion in a single fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance over form through Articles 41 and 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules; the WFOE must conduct its core income-generating activities, including software development, contract acceptance, risk management, and strategic decision-making, in China. The Hong Kong shareholder must also satisfy Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations and demonstrate that it performs its claimed functions with adequate staff and premises in Hong Kong. Any arrangement created mainly to obtain treaty benefits without commercial substance will be recharacterized by the tax authority.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Corporate Income Tax Filing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of the underpaid tax, late-payment interest, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid; PRC Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes deliberate tax evasion and provides criminal fines and imprisonment for controlling officers.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false financial statements, direct round-tripping payments, or conceal revenue can be personally liable under the corporate veil piercing doctrine; they may be ordered to restore diverted corporate assets to the company, placed on the serious tax violation blacklist, and subjected to exit restrictions or travel bans; criminal tax evasion can lead to imprisonment for up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Documentation Failure

Statutory sanction: Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 41 and 44 authorize transfer pricing adjustments plus interest; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 adds a penalty of 50% to 500% of concealed tax; failure to maintain documentation under SAT Announcement 2016 No. 64 allows the tax authority to use reasonable estimation and increases the risk of penalty assessment.

Director personal liability: If a director creates a sham Hong Kong profit center to avoid Chinese tax, the corporate form may be disregarded and the director held jointly liable for the adjusted tax, interest, and penalties; personal assets may be frozen for civil restitution; the director may be blacklisted with travel bans; directors who fabricate transfer pricing documents face criminal prosecution as accomplices to tax evasion.
Treaty Shopping and Shell Company Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The treaty benefit is denied and the full 10% statutory dividend WHT is recaptured, plus a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underwithheld tax under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law; failure to withhold under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 37 gives rise to separate administrative penalties, and the China-HK Arrangement contains entitlement-to-benefits limits that deny relief to abusive shell arrangements.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced when the Hong Kong shareholder is a mere instrumentality; directors and controlling shareholders are personally liable for restitution of unpaid withholding tax and improperly stripped dividends, may be blacklisted and subject to passport revocation or travel bans, and may face imprisonment for money laundering if the profits are routed through Hong Kong to conceal criminal proceeds.
