Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up registered capital is imposed for a service WFOE under the PRC Company Law or the Foreign Investment Negative List. The sole shareholder must subscribe a registered capital amount reasonable and adequate for the intended business scope and operating costs. Under Article 47 of the revised Company Law, all subscribed capital must be fully paid in within five years from the date of issuance of the business license, unless a shorter statutory period applies. No blocked escrow account or pre-registration capital verification is required for an ordinary WFOE; however, any capital remitted from Hong Kong must be settled and recorded through a RMB capital account opened with a SAFE-designated bank, and the foreign exchange source of funds evidence must be retained.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; administered through local STA bureaux with jurisdiction over the WFOE; online portal is the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) service operated by provincial STA branches.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its tax identity automatically upon incorporation. Step 1: the WFOE is registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation and receives an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which under the One License One Code system is also the taxpayer identification number for PRC VAT and enterprise income tax; no separate tax registration certificate is issued. Step 2: within 30 days of the business license, the WFOE legal representative and financial controller complete real-name authentication on the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau e-Tax portal, file the supplementary tax registration, and request determination of applicable taxes including VAT, corporate income tax, stamp duty, urban construction tax, education surcharges, and individual income tax withholding for employees. Step 3: the WFOE selects VAT status: small-scale taxpayer below RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT taxable sales or general VAT taxpayer with input VAT credit; general VAT taxpayer registration is mandatory above that threshold. Step 4: for a foreign non-resident parent, including a Hong Kong holding company, that has no permanent establishment in China, the WFOE as withholding agent must register the non-resident parent with the competent STA office before paying dividends, interest, royalties, service fees, or making cross-border foreign-exchange remittances; the parent must provide its Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate, certificate of Hong Kong tax/resident status, and related identity and residence evidence for double tax arrangement treatment and for the STA to complete the withholding profile. The non-resident parent is not required to obtain a PRC Unified Social Credit Code unless it itself creates a taxable establishment or permanent establishment in China.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box election or hybrid mismatch classification election; a WFOE is automatically a resident enterprise taxable at entity level. The only available election is the VAT status choice between small-scale taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer, which is a collection-status election and not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual VAT taxable sales in any 12-month rolling period for mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration; no turnover threshold for initial tax registration because the WFOE is tax-registered from incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the PRC VAT zero-rating regime set out in the Ministry of Finance and STA Notice No. 36 of 2016 Annex 4 and subsequent cross-border service rules, a WFOE may claim 0% VAT on supplies of software services, information technology services, and offshore outsourcing services where the services are supplied to an overseas recipient and are wholly consumed outside China. For cybersecurity SaaS, the WFOE must show the cloud platform is delivered and consumed outside China, the contract is with an overseas counterparty, the consideration is received in foreign currency, and no domestic Chinese taxable supply or domestic user benefit is involved; otherwise the standard 6% VAT rate applies. General VAT taxpayers may claim a refund of input VAT attributable to zero-rated export services after filing the relevant e-Tax export refund declaration.

The STA operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the nationwide fully digitalised electronic invoice (e-invoice) platform. Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China embraces current-account convertibility for trade in goods and services; the PRC WFOE can settle incoming cybersecurity SaaS subscription fees, licence payments, and ordinary operating expenses to foreign exchange through a designated bank without SAFE pre-approval. Capital-account transactions, however, are non-convertible without specific regulatory authorization. An inbound equity injection from the Hong Kong parent will be treated as foreign direct investment; the WFOE must first complete SAFE foreign-exchange registration, open a dedicated capital account at a designated commercial bank, and receive the wired funds through the SWIFT network. The foreign capital can be converted into RMB only for bona fide operating uses approved in the registration, and cannot be redeployed for overseas investments, lending, or settlement of domestic loans without further approval. Dividend and profit remittance is classified under current account; after annual audit, board resolution, tax clearance, and verification of past service exports, the bank can remit to the Hong Kong shareholder. Practical hurdles include stricter four-eyes review for cross-border SaaS transactions, requirements to evidence trade-in-service authenticity, and bank scrutiny of the ultimate beneficial ownership chain. The central bank enforcement is data-driven via the Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and violations can lead to freeze of payments and administrative penalties.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the Administration of Export Proceeds and Import Payments and foreign exchange verification measures, goods exporters must repatriate export proceeds into a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 180 days after customs export declaration; a mandatory conversion ratio is no longer applied, as fixed surrender requirements were abolished in 2012. For cross-border service exports such as SaaS, revenue must be repatriated via normal bank settlement, evidenced by contracts, invoices, and trade-in-service declarations, and banks will match receipts with the corporate business license and SAFE registration.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), acting under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to open foreign currency settlement accounts for an onshore PRC WFOE. Any entity-level money flow must pass through a licensed Chinese bank under the nationwide foreign exchange monitoring system. EMIs may support front-end invoicing or acquiring, such as Stripe merchant aggregation, but settlement of SaaS proceeds into mainland China must be executed via an approved commercial bank and compliant SAFE declarations. Using an unlicensed EMI as a treasury account holder would violate PBOC payment rules and preclude AML/KYC acceptance by business banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: HKD 0 (i.e., all controlled transactions are subject to the arm's length principle under the Inland Revenue Ordinance (IRO) sections 50AAC–50AAJ; mandatory documentation is triggered where the Hong Kong entity 's gross income exceeds HKD 6,800,000 and the amount of controlled transactions exceeds HKD 6,800,000 (for other categories, e.g., financial transactions exceeding HKD 56,000,000), or whenever a taxpayer claims an offshore exclusion; the IRD may also demand a Master File and Local File under section 50AAB, regardless of thresholds, when abnormal pricing is found).

Economic substance scope: Hong Kong has separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) for certain offshore-type entities (e.g., pure equity holding companies, finance, IP, and shipping) under the Inland Revenue (Amendment) (No. 2) Ordinance 2018 and the Economic Substance (Requirements) Notice 2021, requiring such entities to demonstrate adequate physical presence, qualified employees, and core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong. For a standard WFOE engaged in cybersecurity SaaS, the IRD's source and substance analysis applies under the broad anti-avoidance provisions of section 61A; the company must maintain its commercial substance in Hong Kong (office, staff, decision-making, and treasury functions) to support any claim that its profits are offshore. If the WFOE is only a letterbox conduit, the IRD may assess it on a look-through basis on profits economically attributable to the principal in China, and the ESR will impose compliance notifications and penalties up to HKD 100,000 for non-compliance.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Understatement

Statutory sanction: IRO section 82A(1) (evasion) allows the District Court to impose a fine of up to HKD 50,000 plus an additional tax of three times the amount of tax undercharged; upon conviction on indictment, imprisonment for up to 3 years (section 82A(2)); for a second or subsequent offence, imprisonment for up to 7 years and a further penalty of three times the tax undercharged; IRD may also issue an estimated assessment and surcharge of 5% plus 10% on the outstanding tax.

Director personal liability: Directors and shadow directors are personally liable to pay the additional tax and penalty, and the IRD may make an assessment on the individual responsible under section 82A(4). The director's personal assets are subject to civil recovery through the District Court; a director may be blacklisted in the Hong Kong travel ban system (restriction order issued by the Commissioner of Inland Revenue under section 76A where tax has become final and conclusive and the director is a control person), preventing departure from Hong Kong. Imprisonment terms can reach 3 years and, for aggravated repeated offences, 7 years; the director also remains jointly and severally liable for the company's unpaid tax under the refund/asset seizure procedures.
Transfer Pricing and Sham Foreign Enterprise (Piercing the Corporate Veil)

Statutory sanction: IRO section 61A (transaction to reduce liability) and section 75 (accommodation bills) permit the IRD to disregard the sham structure and assess the company on a reasonable basis; PRC EIT Law article 41 and the Special Tax Adjustments (SAT Notice 2017 No. 6) may make a corresponding adjustment or challenge the anPRC PE through the General Anti-Abuse Rule (article 47) and Circular 19 (2019 No.7) for the PE of a foreign investor; the IRD can also impose transfer pricing penalties under section 82A(1), including 3 times the undercharged tax, and in China the SAT may impose an additional 5% interest per annum on underpaid tax and a penalty of 50%–5 times of the underpaid tax (Tax Collection Administration Law article 63).

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced where the WFOE is a mere façade: the parent or its actual controlling director is treated as the taxpayer, and the director's personal Chinese/hong kong bank accounts may be garnished to recover PRC tax arrears under the PRC Tax Collection and Administration Law article 48; directors face exit restrictions from China under the Customs authority's enforcement list and blacklisting by the SAT as seriously dishonest persons (失信名单); in Hong Kong, the director can be subject to disqualification proceedings under the Companies Ordinance if the veil-piercing reveals fraudulent trading (Companies Ordinance section 713, which allows the court to hold a director personally liable for the company's debts), and the director's Hong Kong assets can be frozen pursuant to an IRD restriction notice. Criminal exposure for fraudulent trading is 15 years' imprisonment in Hong Kong, and in China evasion of tax above RMB 50,000 (or 10% of payable tax) may lead to imprisonment of up to 7 years plus fines.
ECONOMIC SUBSTANCE AND ARBITRAGE ABUSE UNDER THE CHINA–HK DTA

Statutory sanction: Under the China–HK DTA article 10(2), the 5% beneficial ownership requirement (holding 25%+ of capital and being the beneficial owner) fails; the PRC source WHT will be reverted to 10% with a 5% late payment surcharge per month on the under-withheld amount (PRC Tax Collection Administration Law articles 32 and 52); the SAT may apply the general anti-avoidance rule in EIT Law article 47 and impose an additional tax adjustment with an interest charge on underpaid tax; in Hong Kong, the IRD may disallow offshore claims and recharacterize the dividends as assessable offshore profits, or apply section 61A where the shell has no business purpose; the ESR (for any deemed Hong Kong finance/IP/equity-holding entity) can impose a penalty of HKD 100,000 for failure to comply with the notification and a further HKD 20,000 for each successive day on non-compliance.

Director personal liability: The director of the shell Hong Kong parent can be held personally liable for unpaid PRC WHT as the de facto controlling mind of the entity (the SAT will issue a collection notice to the actual controllers and may freeze personal bank accounts; exit bans are imposed for unpaid tax); in Hong Kong, directors are subject to mandatory disqualification for up to 15 years for breach of the Companies Ordinance when the company is found to be used as a vehicle for international tax fraud, and criminal penalties apply for making false declarations to the IRD (section 82A, up to 3 years' imprisonment); where the court finds the director acquiesced in the sham arrangement, the court may order restitution of the tax advantage with interest, and the director may be individually assessed under the IRD's anti-avoidance powers.
