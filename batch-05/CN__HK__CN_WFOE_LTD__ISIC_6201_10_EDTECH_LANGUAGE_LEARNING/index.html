Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime No Capital Lockup Account Required

None. Under the Foreign Investment Law, there is no statutory minimum paid-up capital for a WFOE. However, the registered capital must be subscribed in the Articles of Association and paid in within the time period specified therein (typically within 30 years for service companies). The capital must be injected into a designated capital contribution account in the company's bank. No escrow account is required, but the capital must be verifiable and traceable.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; all tax registrations, filings, and VAT/export treatment applications for the PRC-incorporated WFOE are processed through the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal and the national Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice platform. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department is not a registration authority for the WFOE itself, but Hong Kong tax obligations, if any, arise only at the shareholder level.

E-government filing portal

A Chinese WFOE obtains its statutory tax identification number automatically from the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on its business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Within 30 days of incorporation, the WFOE must activate the USCC as its taxpayer identification number in the e-Tax Bureau, register its legal representative, financial officer, and tax accountant, confirm VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), file bank account details, and enrol in the electronic invoice service platform. A foreign non-resident entity without a permanent establishment in China that receives China-source income must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number through the withholding agent by submitting the non-resident tax registration and treaty benefit paperwork in the e-Tax Bureau; no Chinese business license is required for that non-resident registration.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — A Chinese WFOE obtains its statutory tax identification number automatically from the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on its business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). Within 30 days of incorporation, the WFOE must activate the USCC as its taxpayer identification number in the e-Tax Bureau, register its legal representative, financial officer, and tax accountant, confirm VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), file bank account details, and enrol in the electronic invoice service platform. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None - China does not provide a US-style check-the-box election or a hybrid mismatch election. A WFOE is automatically a Chinese resident enterprise subject to enterprise income tax at 25% on worldwide income. For VAT only, a taxpayer with annual taxable sales not exceeding CNY 5,000,000 may elect simplified small-scale taxpayer treatment at 3% (temporarily 1% during relief windows), while a taxpayer exceeding that threshold must register as a general VAT taxpayer; small-scale taxpayers may voluntarily opt into general VAT taxpayer status. The WFOE may also elect cross-border service zero-rating or exemption treatment if the statutory export substance requirements are satisfied.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

An entity engaged in taxable transactions must register for VAT within 30 days of obtaining its business licence. Mandatory registration as a general VAT taxpayer is triggered when cumulative annual taxable sales exceed CNY 5,000,000. The WFOE's EdTech subscription and software services are generally subject to the 6% modern-services VAT rate, while domestic sales of physical goods, if any, are subject to the 13% goods headline rate.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero per cent VAT applies to (1) physical exports of goods that are actually exported from China and recorded by Customs, and (2) cross-border supplies of software services to overseas customers where the services are wholly consumed outside China, the overseas recipient is the contracting party, the services are not supplied to a Chinese resident or connected with domestic goods, labour, or real estate, and payment is received in foreign currency from outside China. For the EdTech subscription software, the WFOE must demonstrate offshore use through overseas IP addresses, server logs, user geography, and platform session data, and the sale must qualify as a cross-border taxable service under the VAT zero-rating/exemption rules for software services, information system services, and business process services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) with verified customs release record, for physical goods exports
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing transport of goods out of China, for physical goods exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or SAFE bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing collection and settlement of export proceeds
  • Cross-border service agreement identifying the overseas entity, service scope, and consideration, for digital EdTech subscription sales
  • Offshore consumption evidence, including overseas IP addresses, server logs, device and user location data, and platform session records, together with the e-Tax zero-rating/exemption filing reference for cross-border services

The WFOE must issue Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (FDEI) through the STA's national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. Each invoice is assigned a unique tax-control invoice number and authenticated in real time by the Golden Tax IV system, with a digital stamp and electronic delivery to the buyer. Invoice issuance is quota-controlled and monitored by invoice amount, item type, and counterparty risk classification. ERP-connected taxpayers may use the platform's APIs for automated invoice creation, delivery, and status retrieval. All invoice data is pushed to the tax authority immediately upon issuance, and traditional tax-control disk and paper invoice processes are being phased out.

Tax authorities will require the full export documentation bundle before approving zero-rating or an export VAT refund. If zero-rating is claimed without actual export, without offshore consumption of digital services, without foreign exchange repatriation within the prescribed period, or with forged documentation, the tax authority will reverse the zero-rating, recover the underpaid VAT plus daily late-payment interest, and impose a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax under the Tax Collection Administration Law. Fraudulent refunds, false export declarations, and false VAT special invoices are criminal offences under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China and the Customs Law of the People's Republic of China, with no limitation period for recovery in tax fraud cases and personal criminal liability for the legal representative and responsible officers.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's RMB is convertible on the current account: fees for software licensing, app store commission, and employee-related service payments can be settled through domestic banks with supporting documentation, without SAFE quota approval. The capital account remains restricted: FDI equity injections into a WFOE require prior SAFE registration, a dedicated Foreign Exchange Capital Account, and bank-level authenticity checks before conversion into RMB. Once converted, RMB must be used for permitted business expenses and enterprise operations; it may not be directed to securities or entrusted loans without additional qualifications. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted as a current-account dividend distribution after annual audit, but the remitting bank must verify audited financial statements, tax payment proof, board resolution, the SAFE registration, and a legal-person payout signature. The practical hurdle is not a fixed cap; it is documentary consistency. Any mismatch in invoice description, customs data, or FX purpose code can freeze the payment for additional due diligence. No exchange control approval is needed for bona fide profits, but the trail from registered business scope to signed agreements and tax records must be immaculate.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's current framework, there is no fixed statutory mandatory conversion ratio; however, 100% of export proceeds from goods and services must be repatriated into a domestic foreign exchange account. The bank expects settlement (conversion to RMB) within 30 days after the credit date in the absence of a qualifying retention; any retained foreign exchange balance must be justified by future current-account obligations and must remain within the enterprise's registered current-account quota. Proceeds must be credited no later than 30 days after the underlying customs declaration date or invoice due date, and each receipt must be matched in the Trade Monitoring System before it can be used.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Standalone offshore digital EMI platforms, including major cross-border online business account providers, are not legally permitted to open domestic RMB corporate accounts or to access China's CIPS clearing system directly. They are neither licensed deposit-taking institutions nor SAFE-designated foreign exchange banks in mainland China. The WFOE should not use an offshore EMI as its primary treasury rail. At most, the Hong Kong parent may maintain an EMI account for low-value ancillary receipts or contractor payments outside China, but all RMB liquidity, FDI conversion, dividend remittance, and tax-related payments must flow through a domestically licensed tier-1 commercial bank with SAFE qualifications.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the registered FDI capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the chosen mainland tier-1 bank. The remittance field must specify 'FDI Capital Injection' and include the SAFE Foreign Exchange Business Registration number; the purpose code must match the registered investment category for software development and EdTech subscription services. Upon receipt, the onshore bank will perform electronic capital verification with SAFE, convert the foreign currency into RMB at the spot rate through the interbank market, and credit the converted funds to a RMB capital settlement account. Drawdown from that account is monitored against the negative list and permitted business expenses. Any excess remittance above the registered amount must be returned by the same SWIFT rail, and a statutory capital verification report prepared by a Chinese certified public accountant should be filed within 30 days after receipt.For service-trade obligations such as software royalties, technology licensing fees, management fees, cross-border marketing costs, and purchases of cloud infrastructure, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 current-account remittances with full supporting contracts, tax payment certificates, and the correct SAFE service-trade purpose code. For physical goods procurement, documentary collections or commercial letters of credit can be used through the advising bank in Hong Kong, with the WFOE as importer and the overseas seller as beneficiary; sight and usance LC structures are acceptable. For routine app-store disbursements from Apple and Google to the WFOE, the platform payout rail is used as the initial settlement channel, and subsequent conversion or repatriation to mainland China must be reported as trade-in-service foreign exchange receipts with the corresponding app-store payout statements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a Chinese WFOE to a non-resident enterprise shareholder under PRC EIT Law Article 37 and Implementation Regulation Article 91, reduced to 5% under the China-HK Double Taxation Arrangement when the Hong Kong shareholder is the beneficial owner of the dividend and directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE at the time of distribution.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year WFOE, the annual EIT return must be filed within 5 months after fiscal year-end (i.e., by 31 May of the subsequent year). Quarterly provisional returns must be filed within 15 days after the end of each calendar quarter. Any dividend withholding tax must be remitted within 15 days of the actual dividend distribution.

Box-by-Box Return Breakdown (non-resident focus)

BoxField Non-resident guidanceTax-evasion pitfall
Box 1 Operating revenue (营业收入) — total gross revenue from EdTech language-learning subscriptions, Apple App Store / Google Play in-app purchases, institutional training fees, and licensing income. Report all gross revenue sourced in and outside China; do not net out platform commissions or payment processing fees (these are expenses in Box 2). For revenue attributable to non-resident related parties, ensure the amount is at arm's length and properly identified in the related-party transaction disclosure schedules. In-app purchase flows via Apple or Google should be fully recognised in Chinese accounts, with the platform commission supported by valid VAT/FTT invoices.
Box 2 Operating expense (营业成本) — direct costs of delivering the online language education service, including software development, server hosting, teacher compensation, content creation, royalties, and platform fees. Cross-border royalties paid to a Hong Kong parent for proprietary language-learning software or content are deductible only if the payment is bona fide, arm's length, and beneficiated by actual IP. Deductibility additionally requires satisfaction of the 7% treaty withholding tax documentation, or the statutory 10% withholding tax where treaty relief is unavailable. Related-party management fees must satisfy the Economic Substance Doctrine and be backed by actual services, not passive allocations.
Box 3 Total accounting profit (利润总额) — the standalone financial statement profit calculated under China Accounting Standards. This figure must reflect the WFOE's own functional and risk profile. Do not consolidate with the Hong Kong parent's financial statements. Any transfer pricing adjustments to revenue or expense are made subsequently in Boxes 4 and 5, not directly in this Box. Ensure all related-party transactions are disclosed to permit the tax authority to test economic substance.
Box 4 Tax adjustment additions (纳税调整增加额) — items that increase taxable income: non-deductible entertainment expenses, excess advertising expenses, non-arm's-length related-party charges, excessive interest deductions, and other permanent/temporary differences. Add back disallowed intercompany royalties or management fees paid to the Hong Kong parent where the WFOE cannot prove actual benefit, arm's-length pricing, or economic substance. Also add back interest deductions exceeding the arm's-length debt/equity ratio under the thin capitalisation rule. If the Hong Kong parent is a shell or conduit, the tax authority may recharacterise a portion of the royalty as a deemed distribution taxable as a dividend.
Box 5 Tax adjustment deductions (纳税调整减少额) — items that reduce taxable income: tax-exempt dividends received, R&D super-deductions, additional deductions for disabled employee wages, and carry-forward realisation. Qualifying R&D expenses incurred in developing the WFOE's EdTech platform may be deducted at 100% additional deduction (i.e., 200% total deduction) subject to the R&D project filing. Tax-exempt investment income from a resident subsidiary, if any, is excluded. Do not deduct exclusions or exemptions available only to the Hong Kong parent; treaty reductions are addressed at the withholding layer, not in the EIT return.
Box 6 Loss carryforward offset (弥补以前年度亏损) — current-year taxable income offset by prior-year unrelieved tax losses. For ordinary WFOEs, the carryforward period is 5 years from the loss year. If the WFOE qualifies as a High-and-New Technology Enterprise, an extended 10-year carryforward may apply. Losses cannot be transferred to the Hong Kong parent; separate tax-loss computation under Chinese rules is mandatory. A WFOE cannot offset a loss against the foreign investor's income.
Box 7 Taxable income (应纳税所得额) — amount after Box 4 additions and Box 5 reductions, then applying the Box 6 loss offset. This is the final base for EIT. It must reconcile with the audited financial statements and the transfer pricing report. If the tax authority has made a TP adjustment in prior years, such adjustment is reflected here consistently with the final assessment. Cross-border digital service revenue must be sourced under Chinese PE/deemed source rules.
Box 8 EIT payable before reductions and credits (应纳所得税额) — taxable income multiplied by the applicable statutory or preferential CIT rate. Use 25% statutory rate for the WFOE, or 15% if the WFOE is approved as an HNTE. For qualifying Small Low-Profit Enterprises, apply the applicable reduced rate formula. No credit is allowed for WHT that will be withheld on future distributions of profit; that is a shareholder-level tax.
Box 9 Tax reduction and exemption amounts (减免所得税额) — preferential reductions such as HNTE rate differential, small-enterprise concessions, and regional tax incentives. Any claimed incentive must be evidenced by an approved qualification certificate or self-filing record, e.g., HNTE certificate, software enterprise filing, or free-zone registration. The WFOE must maintain contemporaneous documentation of the eligibility and economic substance of the activity. If a preferential rate is improperly claimed, the entire differential is recoverable with 0.05% daily late surcharge and penalties.
Box 10 Tax credits (抵免所得税额) — foreign tax credits on overseas sourced income that is taxed in China, and equipment/energy-saving credits where applicable. If the WFOE derives income from a permanent establishment abroad, credit must be computed globally under the PRC foreign tax credit rules, limited to China EIT attributable to the foreign-sourced income. For an EdTech WFOE, most foreign revenue is sourced in China, so foreign tax credits are rarely applicable. Do not claim credits for amounts withheld in Hong Kong on dividends from China; that is not a foreign tax.
Box 11 Actual EIT payable for the year (实际应纳所得税额) — Box 8 minus Box 9 and Box 10, net of quarterly prepayments, producing the final settled tax liability or refund. After the EIT return is settled and any refund is applied, the remaining distributable profit (including accumulated retained earnings) may be distributed. At the time of distribution, the WFOE as withholding agent must withhold Chinese dividend WHT at 10% (or 5% under the China-HK DTA if treaty conditions and TRC are met) and remit the WHT within 15 days. No EIT credit is available against this downstream withholding.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File thresholds under SAT Announcement No. 42 (2016): annual related-party tangible-asset transfers equal to or exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible-asset transfers equal to or exceeding RMB 100 million; financial-asset transfers equal to or exceeding RMB 100 million; and related-party services (including royalty and management fees) equal to or exceeding RMB 40 million. Master File preparation is mandatory when the aggregate amount of all related-party transactions equals or exceeds RMB 1 billion in the annual financial year. A Country-by-Country Report is required for a multinational group whose consolidated global annual revenue is at least RMB 5.5 billion in the preceding fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: Although China does not have a formal Economic Substance Regulation comparable to offshore jurisdictions, the PRC enforces substance-over-form through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and Article 112 of the Implementing Regulations. A WFOE must demonstrate real business operations, decision-making, employees, and risk-bearing functions that correspond to its EdTech income-generating activities (content production, platform maintenance, user support, and subscription pricing). In the cross-border context, a Hong Kong shareholder claiming the benefit of the China-HK DTA must prove economic substance in Hong Kong under the beneficial ownership test of SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018): it must possess legal and economic control over dividends, royalties, and interest, and its functions, assets, and risks cannot be absorbed by the Chinese WFOE. Pure shell arrangements, mailbox companies, or entities whose only activity is to receive Chinese-sourced income will be denied treaty relief and re-attributed as taxable in China.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Profit Shifting / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Article 41 and Article 48, the SAT may make a discretionary transfer pricing adjustment and impose late interest on the underpaid tax. Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law penalises fraudulent tax evasion with 50%-500% of the underpaid tax. Where the evaded tax amount is substantial (generally exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax payable), PRC Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability: fines, seizure of property, and imprisonment for up to 7 years for the natural persons involved.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil will be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20(3) if the directors or shareholders abuse the limited liability structure to evade taxes or commit fraud. Directors, the legal representative, and the CFO may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties from their personal assets. The court may impose an exit ban (travel restriction), blacklist the individual in the tax enforcement system, and pursue criminal proceedings that can result in 3 to 7 years' imprisonment, with restitution of the full underpaid amount.
Withholding Tax Failure and DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 37 and 39 require the WFOE as withholding agent to withhold and remit the tax within 15 days of the cross-border payment. Failure to withhold triggers a penalty of 50%-300% of the un-withheld amount under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, plus the required tax itself and daily surcharges. If treaty benefit is obtained by false declarations, the SAT will revoke the relief, impose the 10% statutory WHT differential, add late penalties, and proceed under criminal provisions where fraudulent intent is proven.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve cash distributions or cross-border payments without withholding, or who counsel the creation of a shell Hong Kong entity for treaty shopping, become personally responsible for the withheld tax, penalties, and interest as guaranty or joint liability under the veil-piercing rules. Their personal bank accounts may be frozen, travel bans imposed, and they are exposed to prosecution under PRC Criminal Law Art. 201 and Art. 195 (fraud), leading to imprisonment up to 7 years and confiscation of assets.
Shell Company / Economic Substance Deficiency

Statutory sanction: The SAT invokes the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47) to disregard the shell entity, re-characterise the arrangement, and assess the true taxable owner in China. All treaty benefits are denied under SAT Announcement No. 9 (2018), and the WFOE is subject to additional 10% WHT on payments plus interest. In serious cases, Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law applies, yielding a 50%-500% penalty; under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, the shell arrangement may be treated as disguising the origin of taxable profits, leading to administrative fines and account freezes.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil is pierced because the separate legal personality is abused to obstruct tax claims, making directors and the beneficial owner personally liable for all unpaid corporate tax, penalties, and interest. Courts may order personal asset attachment, travel bans, and criminal sanctions for tax evasion and money laundering (PRC Criminal Law Art. 191) with imprisonment up to 10 years. Directors are additionally barred from holding any director or senior management position and their personal foreign assets may be subject to cross-border enforcement under judicial assistance agreements.
