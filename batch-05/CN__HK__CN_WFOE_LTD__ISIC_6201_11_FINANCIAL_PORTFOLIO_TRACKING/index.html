Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; digital filing and issuance through the Electronic Tax Bureau / e-Taxation portal and the integrated Golden Tax III (Jin San) tax control and electronic invoice platform.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) registered in mainland China is automatically assigned an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) at the moment its Business License is issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). This USCC constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and eliminates any separate TIN application. Within 30 days of obtaining the Business License, the WFOE must complete the supplementary tax registration with the local office of the STA by electronically filing through the e-Taxation portal, attaching the articles of association, certificate of incorporation/Business License, registered address proof, legal representative identity documents, bank account opening notice, and investor identification including the notarized or apostilled documents of the foreign/HK shareholder. The STA will confirm the tax authority jurisdiction, register the WFOE as an Enterprise Income Tax (EIT) taxpayer, activate VAT status, and approve the VAT invoice quota and UKey digital tax-control certificate required for issuing VAT invoices. The same USCC is used for customs registration and foreign exchange filing. For a foreign non-resident entity without a mainland establishment, a separate non-resident enterprise tax registration with a fixed place or a withholding tax record must be created under the e-Taxation portal using a tax agent appointed by the WFOE or the non-resident entity itself; this procedure is ancillary to the WFOE's own registration.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. PRC tax law does not provide a check-the-box or pass-through election. A WFOE is mandatorily classified as a resident enterprise and is taxed on worldwide income at the entity level under the Enterprise Income Tax Law. No hybrid mismatch election is available; relief is limited to foreign tax credits and double-taxation treaty measures.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Every WFOE must register for VAT from the date of business establishment irrespective of turnover. The mandatory general-taxpayer threshold is an annual taxable turnover exceeding RMB 5,000,000, after which the taxpayer must adopt general VAT accounting and use Special VAT Invoices; a WFOE conducting cross-border zero-rated activities may voluntarily opt to become a general taxpayer even below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT law, exported goods that physically leave mainland China through a customs supervised port are eligible for the VAT export zero-rate (0% VAT and corresponding input VAT refund). For qualifying cross-border services, including software services, information technology services, and cloud-based financial analytics provided wholly and actually consumed outside mainland China by a non-resident business or individual, the service may be zero-rated if the contract is with an overseas counterparty, the service is performed outside the PRC, and the consideration is received in foreign exchange via an approved banking channel. The WFOE's fintech wealth-tracking SaaS must satisfy the entirely-consumed-overseas substance test and must register as a VAT general taxpayer to claim the input VAT refund.

  • Export Goods Customs Declaration Form (China Customs export declaration with the completed customs clearance identifier and electronic customs data)
  • Original or electronic transport document: sea Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or express/courier waybill as applicable to the delivery mode
  • Inward remittance certificate (FIRC) or PRC bank foreign-exchange receipt and settlement voucher evidencing payment in foreign currency for the exported goods or services
  • Official commercial invoice, export sales contract or purchase order, and packing list
  • VAT zero-rating declaration and refund application record submitted through the STA Export Tax Rebate Declaration System and e-Taxation portal

The WFOE must use the Golden Tax III (Jin San) electronic tax-control system. VAT invoice issuance, quota management, and document verification are performed online via the e-Taxation portal using an STA-issued UKey digital certificate. All invoices are transmitted to the STA in near real-time; every invoice has a unique nationwide invoice number and a digitally encrypted anti-counterfeit tax-control signature (fiscal stamp) generated by the UKey. Fully digital VAT invoices are accepted and preferred across the jurisdiction. Any manual or non-compliant invoice issue will trigger automatic real-time risk screening.

The STA and customs jointly audit export VAT zero-rating records. If the zero-rate is claimed without full customs departure, overseas consumption substance, or foreign-exchange repatriation within the 180-day deadline, the export refund is denied and the VAT is clawed back; an additional late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day accrues. Fraudulent or deliberately unverifiable zero-rating claims are penalized under the PRC Tax Collection Administration Law at 50% to five times the amount of tax evaded, and serious cases trigger criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under the PRC Criminal Law, as well as potential restrictions on the taxpayer's automatic clearance status and tax credit rating downgrade.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi (RMB) is convertible for current account transactions, including trade in goods and services, subject to genuine transaction verification through the SAFE/Foreign Exchange Monitoring System. Capital account convertibility remains strictly controlled; all foreign direct investment into the Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must be injected through a capital account under SAFE registration, deposited in a capital escrow account, and used in accordance with the negative list and operational purpose. Convertibility for profit repatriation is permitted only after annual audit and tax clearance, with dividends distributed in RMB from the RMB basic operating account and then converted into foreign currency at a bank after submitting board resolutions, audited financial statements, and tax payment proof. No blanket surrender or mandatory conversion ratio applies to current account export receipts; however, all foreign currency receipts under goods trade must be repatriated to China or held in a properly designated overseas account with SAFE approval, and the enterprise must complete trade verification within the statutory time frame. Deployment of capital raised through inbound cross-border loans, mergers, or VIE structures is subject to negative list restrictions, macroprudential cross-border financing limits, and bank monitoring of the final use of funds. Full free convertibility of the RMB for portfolio capital flows is not available; therefore treasury planning must segregate current account payment flows from capital account movements and maintain separate bank accounts to avoid regulatory re-characterization.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into China within 180 days from the date of the customs declaration, unless a SAFE-approved offshore account arrangement exists. Since the 2012 reform, there is no mandatory percentage surrender requirement; the WFOE may retain export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily convert them into RMB. All receivables must be verified against customs data, invoice evidence, and the Banking Administration System within 30 days of collection, and the exporter must complete the trade credit reporting and foreign exchange receipt memo processes. Failure to repatriate within the statutory window triggers SAFE investigation, penalty, and downgrade of the enterprise's interim classification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and its local branches

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital banks, payment EMI platforms, and virtual account providers such as Airwallex, Revolut Business, and Currenxie are not legally permitted to open primary domestic account relationships or provide local RMB IBAN-equivalent accounts for a mainland China WFOE, because only licensed Chinese banks regulated by PBOC and the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission may operate clearing settlement accounts within the PRC. Such EMIs can serve as supplementary treasury orchestration rails for cross-border disbursements, forex conversion, and virtual sub-ledger visibility, but they must channel actual RMB settlement through a Chinese partner bank under PBOC clearance. The WFOE must therefore maintain a domestic Tier-1 bank as the sole legal owner of the settlement relationship for SAFE and tax purposes. Using fintech EMIs for primary fund custody or capital account injection is non-viable and creates an AML underwriting red flag due to the absence of direct PBOC regulatory visibility.

Turnover: No quantitative turnover, total-asset, or headcount threshold exempts a non-dormant Hong Kong incorporated company from the statutory audit mandate. Under Sections 405 and 407 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), every non-dormant company must obtain a statutory audit prepared by a certified public accountant (practising) registered under the Professional Accountants Ordinance, regardless of the small size of the WFOE. A dormant private company may become audit-exempt only after filing a certificate of dormancy with the Companies Registry and remaining free of significant transactions; the dormant status must be renewed each financial year.
