Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, operating through the Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB) online portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform; auxiliary customs and FX authorities are the General Administration of Customs (GACC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE).

E-government filing portal

A non-resident foreign entity may not obtain a PRC tax ID in isolation; it must first incorporate a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) via the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The issued business license contains the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which constitutes the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the WFOE under the 'three certificates in one' reform. Within 30 days of license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau by filing the full registration dossier (legal representative, financial officer, tax officer, registered address, accounting software, bank account details), after which the system activates the VAT, enterprise income tax, and surcharge accounts under the USCC. For export trade, the WFOE must additionally obtain the Customs Registration Code from GACC, complete the Foreign Trade Operator filing, and undertake SAFE foreign exchange business opening (receipt and payment ledger under the goods trade monitoring system) so that the future export VAT zero-rating/recovery claims can be matched against customs, FX, and tax datasets.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None in the sense of a US check-the-box pass-through election or hybrid mismatch election; PRC law mandates entity-level CIT for the WFOE. The only relevant elective mechanism is the VAT taxpayer status election: until annual taxable turnover reaches RMB 5,000,000, the WFOE may elect general VAT taxpayer status (to recover input VAT) instead of defaulting to small-scale taxpayer status; the election is exercised through the ETB and becomes binding for a fixed accounting period. No income tax consolidation or pass-through election exists for a single-entity WFOE.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Unconditional VAT registration duty for all newly established WFOEs within 30 days of business license issuance; mandatory general taxpayer classification when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan) in a rolling 12-month period. Below the threshold the WFOE defaults to small-scale taxpayer status (simplified collection, generally 3%, with temporary preferential collection at 1%). For the health-fitness app revenue supplied as onshore digital services, the applicable domestic VAT rate is 6% (modern services); goods sales are at the headline rate of 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0%) is available for (i) exported physical goods leaving China, and (ii) cross-border supplies of digital and information technology services, including software licensing and application distribution for health-fitness tracking apps, provided the service recipient is an offshore entity (e.g., the Apple/Google platform opco outside China), the service is wholly consumed outside the PRC, and the consideration is settled in foreign currency (or eligible offshore RMB) through an onshore designated bank. The WFOE must satisfy the statutory test that the app services are not supplied or used within China, evidenced by the offshore distributor contracts, app store storefront data, download reporting, and the absence of domestic users for the zero-rated tranche. Where the full zero-rating test is not met, the supply may qualify for the VAT exemption schedule for cross-border services, but exempt status does not carry an input-VAT refund entitlement.

China operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV integrally linking tax, customs, banking, and SAMR data. Since the nationwide rollout of fully digitised electronic invoices (FDEIs), the WFOE must issue all VAT invoices, including zero-rated export invoices, through the STA Electronic Tax Bureau; each FDEI is created from the enterprise Tax Digital Account, transmitted electronically to the STA in near-real time at issuance, signed with the official digital anti-counterfeit cipher, and verifiable by the counterparty through the National VAT Invoice Verification Platform. There is no third-party API pre-clearance for ordinary enterprises; instead the STA performs automated machine-level matching of invoice data, customs export declarations, and FX settlement reports on a real-time basis. Export-oriented WFOEs must therefore maintain complete digital data alignment when issuing zero-rated invoices.

All zero-rated export claims are screened by the Golden Tax export-recovery module, which cross-matches the GACC customs declaration, the bank FX receipt, the FDEI, and the service contract. If the export proceeds have not been collected within 180 days from the customs declaration date or the service zero-rating evidence fails, the STA will issue a supplementary VAT assessment, deny the export recovery, and demand repayment of the refunded input VAT plus daily overdue surcharges of 0.05%; administrative fines range from 50% to five times the tax understated, or up to the tax amount for documentary errors. For fraudulent or forged export documentation, the WFOE and the responsible persons are subject to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China for fraudulent export tax refund, carrying penalties from fixed-term imprisonment to life imprisonment in extremely serious cases along with fines and confiscation; collateral consequences include downgrade or cancellation of the export-tax-refund registration category, a three-year bar on zero-rating, and inclusion on the joint-discipline tax dishonesty blacklist.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the PRC Foreign Exchange Administration Regulations, RMB is largely convertible on the current account for trade, service, and profit-remittance transactions, subject to bank truthfulness review and document verification. Capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment into the WFOE, remain controlled by SAFE through a registration-based system. The Hong Kong parent must complete FDI foreign exchange registration at a designated bank before remitting registered capital, which is credited to a dedicated foreign exchange capital account. Settlement of that capital into RMB is permitted only on a payment-by-payment basis against genuine commercial use, with the bank performing authenticity checks under the negative-list principle. Profit remittance is treated as an outbound current account transaction after the annual audit and tax clearance, but banks will require audited financial statements, board resolution, tax identity or tax-clearance certificate, and up-to-date SAFE registration records. While current account convertibility is substantially liberalised, offshore arbitrage, round-tripping, disguised equity conversion, and rapid repatriation of capital ahead of approved schedules remain prohibited. Additional reporting applies to any cross-border data processing in the health app business, especially to the Hong Kong parent, given PRC data security and personal information protection law.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has abolished mandatory surrender ratios for export foreign currency receipts. Export proceeds must be repatriated into the Chinese banking system through a licensed bank and may be retained in a current-account foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily sold to the bank for RMB. Under SAFE trade monitoring rules, the exporter must report export collection through the bank's electronic system, with the underlying trade receivable normally expected to be received within the usual 30-day reporting window after bank credit and with general trade receivables reconciled within the statutory monitoring cycle; any collection extending beyond the norm requires an explanation and documentary support. No fixed percentage conversion ratio applies.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low direct viability. A foreign fintech EMI is not licensed as a deposit-taking institution under PRC banking law and is legally barred from opening domestic RMB settlement accounts, receiving FDI capital, clearing payroll, or executing VAT payments for a Chinese WFOE. Offshore EMIs cannot interact directly with PBOC/SAFE payment and monitoring systems and therefore cannot serve as the primary treasury rail. An EMI may be used only as an ancillary billing or international collection front-end for the Hong Kong parent, provided all compliance-critical cash flows and tax functions remain in tier-1 domestic PRC banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first complete FDI foreign exchange registration with SAFE through a designated domestic bank, obtaining the required FDI registration code. The capital injection is then executed by SWIFT MT103 from the Hong Kong parent's bank to the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account at a tier-1 PRC bank, with the remittance remarks clearly stating direct investment and registered capital contribution and using the SAFE purpose code for FDI capital injection. The receiving bank performs compliance screening, verifies the registration code, and credits the foreign exchange capital account. RMB settlement is carried out into a settlement-pending account on a payment-by-payment basis against invoices, contracts, and use-of-funds documents. Within a defined period after receipt, a licensed PRC CPA issues a capital verification report, which is filed with the bank and retained for tax and SAFE inspections.For digital health app activities, the primary clearing rails are SWIFT MT103 telegraphic transfers for service fees, IP royalties, cloud infrastructure costs, and profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent. Commercial letters of credit and documentary collections are generally inapplicable to the intangible service and app-license model, but if hardware wearables are later sold domestically or exported, MT103 for settlement and documentary collections for cross-border physical goods may become relevant. Outbound payments for royalties and technology services require the underlying contract, a valid tax invoice, and proof of withholding tax declaration and payment to the PRC tax authority. Dividends are remitted only after audited financial statements, board resolution, tax clearance, and SAFE-verified current account treatment.Deliverable RMB forward contracts are permissible for the WFOE to hedge genuine current-account foreign exchange exposures, such as future service fee payments, software licensing royalties, or dividend remittances, provided the bank verifies an underlying commercial transaction.FX swaps and FX options are permitted onshore for hedging real exposures only and cannot be speculatively priced or documented as synthetic financing structures.Cross-currency interest-rate swaps are permissible solely for genuine balance-sheet hedging of external loans that have been registered with SAFE, and the resulting cash flows must align with the underlying debt schedule.Onshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted for a domestic WFOE; the Hong Kong parent may use offshore NDFs to hedge its RMB exposure, but the mainland entity cannot conclude NDFs with onshore banks or offshore counterparties as part of its own treasury operations.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low direct viability. A foreign fintech EMI is not licensed as a deposit-taking institution under PRC banking law and is legally barred from opening domestic RMB settlement accounts, receiving FDI capital, clearing payroll, or executing VAT payments for a Chinese WFOE. Offshore EMIs cannot interact directly with PBOC/SAFE payment and monitoring systems and therefore cannot serve as the primary treasury rail. An EMI may be used only as an ancillary billing or international collection front-end for the Hong Kong parent, provided all compliance-critical cash flows and tax functions remain in tier-1 domestic PRC banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared if the enterprise's aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 40 million (for other related transactions such as services or royalties) or RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers; RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers. Master File must be prepared if the enterprise's related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion or if the enterprise's transaction is with a related party controlled by a foreign entity and the aggregate amount exceeds the respective thresholds. The related-party transaction report (annual basis) must be filed with the annual corporate income tax return; the Local File and Master File must be submitted within 15 days after the tax authority asks for them, and the Local File must be completed by 30 June of the following year.

Economic substance scope: China does not have a separate Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) regime equivalent to Cayman Islands or UAE, but the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (GAAR) under Article 47 of the EIT Law and the Special Tax Adjustment provisions recharacterize arrangements without reasonable commercial substance. The WFOE must demonstrate that it carries on real business operations, employs staff in China, makes strategic decisions, and manages its own risks. If the Hong Kong parent merely holds shares and provides no substantive services, the Chinese tax authority may pierce the parent's structure and allocate income to the Chinese WFOE under the 'beneficial ownership' and 'economic substance' doctrines. Additionally, the new 'country-by-country reporting' (CBCR) rules apply to multinational groups with consolidated revenue above RMB 5.5 billion; the WFOE must serve as a notification channel to report its ultimate parent entity, and if the group fails to file CBCR, penalties and transfer pricing audits may occur.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Falsification of Accounts

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law: penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; a criminal fine and forfeiture of assets if tax evasion exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes 10% of the unpaid tax; Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes up to 7 years' imprisonment and an unlimited fine. The Inland Revenue Department of Hong Kong may also prosecute tax evasion where the Hong Kong parent is involved.

Director personal liability: Under Article 202 (Criminal Law) and the Tax Collection Administration Law, directors who directly plan or approve the fraudulent scheme bear personal criminal liability, including imprisonment of 3 to 7 years for serious fraud. Civil asset restitution may be ordered by the court to recover the underpaid tax. Directors may also be blacklisted from travelling abroad by the Chinese tax authority under Article 12 of the 'Measures for Controlling Tax-Related Persons' (blacklisting and exit control).
Transfer Pricing Misreporting and Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Article 41 and Article 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law: the tax authority may recharacterize the transactions, make a transfer pricing adjustment, reduce the WFOE's deduction, and impose a 5% per annum interest surcharge on the additional tax (under Article 121 of the Implementation Regulations) plus a penalty of up to 50% of the underpaid tax if the documentation is inadequate. Failure to file related-party transaction form (Form A100000) incurs a penalty of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 per year, and serious violations may lead to a 10% fine on the tax adjustment amount.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign false transfer pricing certificates or instruct the finance team to hide related-party transactions may be held personally liable for the tax adjustment amount under the doctrine of piercing the corporate veil (Article 20 of the Company Law) if sham transactions are used to defraud creditors and the state. They face a travel ban (blacklisting) and are prohibited from leaving mainland China until the tax deficiency is settled. Criminal liability for tax evasion may attach if the transfer pricing manipulation is proven to be intentional and material.
Shell Company / Beneficial Ownership Abuse and Illegal Profit Repatriation

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rules (Article 47 of EIT Law) and Circular 9/2018 (Tax Bureau's Bulletin on Beneficial Ownership), the tax authority may deny the treaty benefits, impose the statutory 10% withholding tax on the dividend, and additionally charge a 15% to 30% fine under Article 42 of the Tax Collection Administration Law. Unlawful outbound remittance may violate the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules, leading to fines up to 5 times the illegal amount for the entity; the enterprise may also have its import/export and foreign exchange records blacklisted. The WFOE's failure to withhold the dividend WHT creates a primary liability on the WFOE under Article 69 of the Tax Collection Administration Law, with a penalty of 50% to 3 times the amount of the tax that should have been withheld.

Director personal liability: Directors who execute or approve the sham dividend arrangement can be personally sued for the unpaid tax and penalties under the 'piercing the corporate veil' doctrine if the court finds that the shell entity structure was used to evade state revenue. Personal assets may be frozen and sold to satisfy the tax debt (under Art. 49 of the Tax Collection Administration Law). Directors may face indictment for 'tax evasion' under criminal law, leading to imprisonment of up to 7 years, and may be jointly and severally liable for the foreign exchange violation, facing a personal fine and harm to their credit record. Additionally, directors of Hong Kong shell companies may violate the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) s. 373 (false statements in documents) and face imprisonment in Hong Kong, as well as a cross-border travel ban from mainland China.
