Competent tax authority

E-government filing portal

For the resident WFOE: (1) incorporate with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the unified social credit code (USCC) which constitutes the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN); (2) tax registration is automatically completed through the multi-department integration system; (3) file supplemental tax profile information (legal representative, financial controller, accounting system, business scope) via the electronic tax service portal of the competent local tax bureau; (4) elect VAT taxpayer status - small-scale if projected annual turnover is less than or equal to CNY 5,000,000, otherwise mandatory general taxpayer status; (5) apply for digital VAT invoice quota and electronic invoice issuance permissions. For a foreign non-resident entity (e.g., a Hong Kong parent or service provider) that derives China-source income subject to withholding tax, the non-resident entity must obtain a non-resident TIN (prefixed 'W') by submitting a registration application with the competent tax authority, supported by its incorporation certificate, board resolution appointing a PRC tax agent or representative, and evidence of the China-source income arrangement. Non-residents without a physical presence must also file through the withholding agent and may need to complete the non-resident enterprise registration via the electronic portal.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to: (a) exported goods departing from PRC customs territory, and (b) specified cross-border services supplied to overseas entities and wholly consumed outside China, including software service, information system service, business-process management (BPO) service, and other designated modern services under Caishui [2016] No. 36 Annex IV. Eligibility requires: the supplier is a VAT general taxpayer; export-related administrative filings (e.g., export tax refund/exemption filing) have been completed; the recipient is resident abroad; the service is not physically rendered within China; payment is received from outside China; and for goods, the export is documented through customs clearance.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Goods Customs Declaration Form) - mandatory for physical export clearance
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - evidencing cross-border movement of goods
  • Bank-Issued Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Bank Cross-Border Payment Receipt - evidencing FX repatriation of export proceeds

Mandatory real-time fiscal clearance under the Golden Tax System Phase IV. All VAT invoices (including the fully digitalised e-invoice) must be issued via the State Taxation Administration's unified electronic invoice platform, which affixes an encrypted digital tax stamp and transmits invoice data to the STA database in near-real time. High-volume issuers must integrate through the official open API (system-to-system interface). Legacy manual invoice books without electronic fiscal clearance are prohibited; vouchers lacking a digital tax stamp are null for VAT input credit.

The PRC tax authority scrutinises zero-rating claims retrospectively. If the export/zero-rated service transaction fails audit, all VAT exemptions/zero-rated refunds are clawed back with interest (0.05% per day on unpaid tax) and a penalty from 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid. Fraudulent claims of export VAT zero-rating constitute tax evasion punishable under the Tax Collection Administration Law and criminal liability under the PRC Criminal Law for export tax refund fraud, with imprisonment up to life imprisonment plus fines and forfeiture of the refunded amount. Administrative consequences include downgrading the taxpayer credit rating and revoking the export tax exemption/zero-rating qualification.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under the People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) supervised managed float, China's RMB is only conditionally convertible on the current account and strictly controlled on the capital account. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must open an FDI capital account for inward investment, a current account for service-trade settlements, and maintains separate domestic RMB settlement accounts. Current account items such as payroll operating costs, license fees, royalties and SaaS subscription receipts are remitted after proof of an underlying commercial contract, tax payment vouchers and purpose-code declaration. Capital account transactions, specifically the injection of equity by the Hong Kong parent, require SAFE's FDI registration, a bank-verified capital account, and a 30-day window to convert into RMB for operating use only, not to purchase securities or real estate. Profit remittance to HK is treated as a current account item: after audited accounts are filed with MOFCOM and the bank confirms corporate income tax has been paid, the exact dividend can be converted into foreign currency without any absolute quantitative cap, but never before statutory audited financials. Surrender obligations are not mandatory in ratio terms; rather, the WFOE must repatriate all export or service-trade proceeds into its domestic FX settlement account within 90 days (or shorter, if the underlying invoice states earlier credit terms), then may choose to settle into RMB with a genuine background. Any deviation from this requires SAFE approval.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For the payroll automation and SaaS business, export/service-income receipts from overseas clients are required to be repatriated into the WFOE's foreign currency settlement account at a Tier-1 bank in mainland China within 90 days of the due date shown on the commercial invoice (or 120 days if the contract specifies service acceptance milestones). Since the 2012 SAFE reform, there is no mandatory percentage of foreign exchange surrender to the central bank; the WFOE can retain up to the bank-approved macro-prudential cap of its average monthly FX receipts or convert 100% into RMB on the spot using a foreign currency settlement form. For goods exports routed through the bonded warehouse, the bank carries a dual verification against the customs electronic declaration; for software and payroll services, the paperwork is based on the service trade certificate, the underlying contract, and a written declaration of the actual period in which services were rendered. All proceeds must be booked using a genuine purpose-code; once misreported, the bank will halt approvals.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its designated foreign exchange administration arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low. Mainland China does not license any offshore fintech EMI (Wise, Revolut, Airwallex, Currencycloud) to open local clearing accounts or provide RMB/foreign exchange conversion for a WFOE. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: A PRC Local File is mandatory where annual related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200 million, intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million; a Master File is required where the ultimate parent group's audited consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 2 billion and the enterprise has cross-border related-party transactions or significant domestic related-party transactions, per SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: The PRC tax authorities apply a substance-over-form doctrine under EIT Law Article 47 and the SAT beneficial ownership rules. The WFOE must demonstrate that it performs the core income-generating activities for its payroll automation software, including development, customization, cloud infrastructure management, client contracting and risk assumption in China. The Hong Kong parent must also show real economic substance in Hong Kong for any treaty claim; a mailbox or shell company with no employees, no offices, no active board decisions and no substantive risk management will not qualify for DTA benefits and may be treated as an agent or conduit, resulting in denial of treaty relief, domestic WHT recovery and transfer pricing adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and Fraudulent Preferential Rate Claims

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes recovery of the evaded tax plus interest and a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. PRC Criminal Law Article 201 criminalizes intentional concealment or false declaration of taxable income; where the evaded amount exceeds RMB 100,000 and constitutes more than 10% of taxable income, directors and other responsible persons can face criminal prosecution, with imprisonment of up to three years for ordinary cases and up to seven years for large amounts or repeat offenses.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false returns, direct the creation of fake R&D records, or participate in fraudulent incentive claims may have the corporate veil pierced. They are personally liable for the company's tax losses and creditor damage under PRC Company Law; their assets are subject to civil restitution; they are placed on the severely dishonest taxpayer blacklist; travel bans are imposed and exit from China is denied; and criminal imprisonment is imposed under the tax evasion provisions.
Transfer Pricing Mispricing and Shell Company Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: PRC EIT Law Articles 41, 44 and 47 authorize the tax authority to make a transfer pricing adjustment, disallow deductions, characterize the payments as constructive dividends, impose interest, and reallocate profits. Failure to maintain Local File or Master File documentation attracts fines under TCAL Article 70 of RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000; where the mispricing constitutes deliberate concealment, the 50% to 500% evasion penalty applies.

Director personal liability: Directors of the WFOE who approve disguised payments to the shell can be held jointly and severally liable for the restitution of shifted profits and statutory penalties. The tax authority may blacklist them, freeze personal assets, restrict overseas travel, and refer the case to criminal authorities for tax evasion. If funds are laundered through Hong Kong shell entities, PRC Criminal Law Article 191 imposes liability for money laundering, including fines and imprisonment of up to 10 years, and the corporate veil is pierced to reach the controllers.
Withholding Tax and Treaty Relief Abuse on Outbound Dividends and Cross-Border Payments

Statutory sanction: TCAL Article 69 requires the withholding agent to pay the un-withheld tax, late payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 300% of the amount not withheld. Under the beneficial ownership rules and the GAAR, treaty relief is denied, the full 10% statutory WHT is recovered from the WFOE or the Hong Kong parent, and interest runs from the date the tax should have been paid. Repeated or intentional non-withholding can lead to criminal prosecution under Criminal Law Article 201 for aiding tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly cause the WFOE to remit dividends, interest or royalties without proper withholding face personal civil liability for the tax loss; personal bank accounts and assets may be seized; the State Administration of Foreign Exchange and tax authorities impose blacklisting and travel bans; and criminal liability includes imprisonment for tax evasion or for money laundering where the outbound remittance stream conceals the origin of the profits. The corporate veil is pierced where the distribution cannot be distinguished from unlawful shareholder stripping of the WFOE's assets.
