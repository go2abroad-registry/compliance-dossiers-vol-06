Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the local competent tax office where the WFOE is registered; electronic portal: National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) via www.chinatax.gov.cn

        
E-government filing portal
Upon incorporation, the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues the WFOE a business license with a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax filings. The WFOE must complete tax registration on the National Electronic Tax Bureau within 30 days of the business license date, submitting the business license, articles of association, registered address proof, legal representative identification, equity structure, and bank account details; the STA then activates the USCC for Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, withholding tax and invoice issuance. A foreign non-resident enterprise (e.g. the Hong Kong parent) with no PRC establishment but deriving China-source income is not separately VAT-registered; the Chinese payer or withholding agent must file the contract withholding registration on the e-Tax platform, where the non-resident is assigned a non-resident tax reference number and the Hong Kong Business Registration number is recorded as the foreign TIN.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
Tax registration is mandatory upon issuance of the SAMR business license. The turnover threshold requiring general VAT taxpayer registration is RMB 5,000,000 in a consecutive 12-month period; below this threshold the WFOE may be treated as a small-scale taxpayer, but general VAT taxpayer status is necessary for the WFOE to issue 0% export VAT invoices for cross-border data/API services and to claim input VAT credits.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
The WFOE may claim 0% VAT on export goods and on qualifying cross-border taxable services, including information technology, software, and data-processing services such as commercial web scraping and proxy API gateways, if the recipient is an overseas enterprise or individual, the service is wholly consumed outside Mainland China, the contract and commercial documentation clearly demonstrate offshore supply, and the consideration is received from outside China. The claim is supported by the STA cross-border taxable service zero-rating/exemption framework; the transaction must be recorded as a zero-rated export in the VAT filing and the WFOE must not issue a domestic taxable VAT invoice for the offshore supply.

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system on the National Electronic Tax Bureau. The WFOE must issue Special VAT Invoices or fully digitalised electronic invoices through the official e-invoice platform; each invoice carries a unique invoice code and is transmitted in near real time to the STA. Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
RMB is fully convertible for current account transactions including trade in goods and services, but remains heavily regulated on capital account transactions. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) operating in China, foreign currency capital injections from the Hong Kong shareholder must flow through a designated domestic bank under a SAFE foreign exchange registration, and once the funds are credited to the capital escrow account, conversion to RMB is permissible for expenditure within the registered business scope. Profit remittances are permitted after annual audits, tax clearance, and proof that the relevant foreign exchange capital has been paid-up and verified, but require a board resolution and the submission of a Certificate of Payment and Foreign Exchange Verification Form. The WFOE must also comply with the Foreign Exchange Control Regulation and the Administration of Settlement and Sales of Foreign Exchange Provisions, in particular using the correct payment purpose codes. In practice, capital account convertibility is subject to a case-by-case SAFE review, making pre-deal regulatory consultation essential. The strategic use of the Hong Kong jurisdiction as a treasury hub mitigates some restrictions because it provides access to offshore liquidity and hedging markets while the onshore WFOE maintains its own legal capital and operational FX accounts.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Pursuant to SAFE's Export Proceeds Repatriation Administration Provisions, export-related foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into China through a designated bank within 90 days of the export customs declaration date. The mandatory conversion ratio is effectively zero—exporters may retain the foreign exchange in a Foreign Exchange Settlement Account or convert to RMB at will, provided the balance remains within the approved current account quota. A foreign exchange verification, based on the customs declaration, commercial invoice and bill of lading, is required to close the export collection record. Administrative penalties and potential KYC downgrade apply if repatriation deadlines are missed.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) via the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Airwallex
            Fit: High
          

          
Cross-border fintech electronic money institutions (EMIs) such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Revolut Business are legally barred from providing onshore banking services or domestic currency accounts to PRC-domiciled entities without a Chinese commercial banking license. PBOC and SAFE regulations require any foreign-invested payment institution to obtain a Payment Business License and SAFE registration to clear RMB and foreign exchange in China. Consequently, an offshore EMI can only act as a supplementary multi-currency wallet for Hong Kong operating companies, not as the primary treasury rail for a Chinese WFOE. Using an unauthorised EMI as the main transaction account will trigger AML downgrade and possible administrative sanction. The viable approach is a Hong Kong treasury entity with the foreign-funded EMI for offshore disbursements, while all onshore RMB obligations are settled via Tier-1 Chinese banks.

Core KYC Rejection Triggers & Defense Dossier

      
      

        Concealed beneficial ownership and unaudited Hong Kong interposing holding layers
        
Root cause: Concealed beneficial ownership and unaudited Hong Kong interposing holding layers

        
Defense evidence: Complete corporate organigram of the ultimate beneficial owners (UBOs) with passport copies; Hong Kong Companies Registry certificate of incorporation, annual return and register of members; notarised shareholders' resolution designating the WFOE's authorized signatories; a Hong Kong tax representative letter confirming economic substance; and audited financial statements of the intermediate holding company for two years.

      

      
      

        Non-verifiable data sourcing and intellectual property risk in public web scraping operations
        
Root cause: Non-verifiable data sourcing and intellectual property risk in public web scraping operations

        
Defense evidence: PRC litigation lawyer opinion confirming compliance with Criminal Law Article 285, Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law and Personal Information Protection Law; signed contracts with data suppliers along with updated API terms of service; robots.txt and scraping permission audit reports; records showing consent and opt-out mechanisms for individual data; and GDPR/CCPA compliance attestation from a third-party security auditor.

      

      
      

        Cryptocurrency (USDC) payment rails implying insufficient anti-money laundering control
        
Root cause: Cryptocurrency (USDC) payment rails implying insufficient anti-money laundering control

        
Defense evidence: A documented policy prohibiting third-party or anonymous crypto funding; whitelisted digital wallet addresses signed by the UBO; on-chain KYC/AML attestation from the issuing exchange (e.g., Circle or regulated exchange); concurrent transaction monitoring log with linkage between on-chain transactions and corresponding invoices; and a corporate resolution clearly stating that USDC settlement will only be used for verified customer subscriptions.

      

      
      

        Benchmark mismatch between projected turnover and requested transactional limits
        
Root cause: Benchmark mismatch between projected turnover and requested transactional limits

          Mandatory Audit Threshold
          
            Mandatory Audit for All Entities
          
        

        
Turnover: All Hong Kong private companies, except wholly dormant companies, are legally required to appoint an independent certified public accountant and file audited financial statements; there are no balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount exemptions under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622). Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File: annual related-party transactions exceeding CNY 200 million for tangible asset transfers, CNY 100 million for financial asset transfers, CNY 100 million for intangible asset transfers, or CNY 40 million for other transactions; Master File: annual related-party transactions exceeding CNY 1 billion; Country-by-Country Report: MNE group consolidated revenue of CNY 5.5 billion or more in the prior fiscal year

      
Economic substance scope: PRC substance-over-form and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under EIT Law Articles 47 and 120 allow the tax authority to disregard or recharacterize a transaction lacking reasonable commercial purpose; a H.K. parent claiming treaty benefits must satisfy the Beneficial Ownership requirements of SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9 and must pass the Principal Purpose Test incorporated through the MLI; under Hong Kong law, the H.K. resident must perform Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, including decision-making, management, staff, premises, and risk assumption, to support offshore claims and treaty access; a WFOE whose H.K. parent is a nominee vehicle with no real substance will lose the 5% dividend rate and may trigger CFC and anti-treaty-abuse adjustments.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Documentation Failure and Thin Capitalization Abuse
        
Statutory sanction: EIT Law Articles 41 and 46 and SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 authorize transfer pricing recharacterization and thin capitalization adjustment; Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes additional tax, daily interest surcharges, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax; the tax authority may also apply a 5% annual adjustment interest under special tax adjustment rules.

        
Director personal liability: Where the WFOE is found to be a mere conduit and the H.K. parent controls all decisions, the corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law; directors and actual controllers may be jointly and severally liable for the restored taxable income, unpaid tax, and penalties; the legal representative may be blacklisted by the State Taxation Administration, subjected to an exit ban under the Exit-Entry Administration Law, and face criminal investigation under Criminal Law Article 201 for fraudulent tax avoidance if the conduct was intentional.

      

      
      

        Tax Evasion Through Fabricated Costs and False Special VAT Invoices
        
Statutory sanction: Criminal Law Article 205 criminalizes the issuance or use of false special VAT invoices, with fines and imprisonment of three to ten years for large amounts and up to life imprisonment for especially huge amounts; Tax Collection Law Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax plus full recovery of the evaded tax; the WFOE's deductions are disallowed in their entirety.

        
Director personal liability: Directors, the finance director, and the actual controlling shareholder face criminal imprisonment for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201, civil restitution of the avoided tax, frozen assets, and personal joint liability under Company Law; the legal representative is blacklisted from leaving China until tax debts are settled; any director who knowingly signed false invoices is treated as a principal offender rather than protected by limited liability.

      

      
      

        DTA and Beneficial Ownership Fraud Through a Shell H.K. Parent
        
Statutory sanction: The tax authority may deny treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test, SAT Public Notice 2018 No. 9, EIT Law Article 47, and the China-H.K. Arrangement's Limitation of Benefits provisions; the WFOE is liable for the additional 10% withholding tax difference, daily late surcharges, and a penalty of 50% to 5 times the under-withheld tax under Tax Collection Law Article 63 or Article 64; false declarations may also trigger criminal fraud liability.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly signed the treaty benefit declaration and dividend resolution may be personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax as withholding-agent responsible persons; the corporate veil may be pierced because the H.K. shareholder is a sham entity used to evade PRC tax, exposing shareholders to civil asset restitution; the legal representative and beneficial owner face a travel ban, blacklisting by tax and customs authorities, and criminal imprisonment for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201; H.K. authorities may also object to the H.K. company's registration and strike it off for lack of economic substance.
