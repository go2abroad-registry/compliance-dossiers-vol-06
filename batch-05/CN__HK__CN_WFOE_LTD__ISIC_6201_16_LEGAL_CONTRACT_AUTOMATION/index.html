Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing and compliance are processed via the China Electronic Tax Administration platform (e-Tax) at https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn (for provincial access, the respective local bureau e-tax portal is used). The WFOE is registered as a mainland China resident enterprise; its Hong Kong ultimate parent does not require a separate mainland TIN for the WFOE, but may itself be subject to Chinese withholding tax on dividends if repatriated.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: After approval by the Ministry of Commerce (or local MOFCOM delegated authority) and registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the WFOE obtains a business license containing a unified social credit code (USCC). Step 2: Within 30 days of the business license issue date, the legal representative, finance officer, or an authorized agent must log onto the local e-Tax portal and complete the Tax Information Report. The STA will activate the USCC as the taxpayer identification number (TIN). Step 3: File supplementary records including the articles of association, registered address lease, bank account information, investment verification, and financial officer appointment. Step 4: For foreign non-resident enterprises that are not registered as Chinese entities (e.g., the Hong Kong parent) but have taxable income from China (e.g., dividends, interest, royalties), a temporary TIN can be obtained by the withholding agent (the WFOE) when filing withholding tax declarations, or the non-resident enterprise can self-register under Article 106 of the Tax Collection Law. Step 5: The WFOE must apply for invoice quotas and register for VAT within the same procedure.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory registration as a General VAT taxpayer applies when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000 (approx. USD 700,000). If annual sales are below this threshold, the taxpayer may elect either Small-Scale taxpayer status (currently applying a 1% levy rate until 2027, previously 3%) or General VAT taxpayer status. Once registered as General, the taxpayer must remain as General for 12 months.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The export of physical goods from China is zero-rated under the VAT system, with a full refund of eligible input VAT. For the WFOE's digital SaaS business (cloud document automation and e-signature authentication), the cross-border supply of software services to overseas customers may be treated as zero-rated under the cross-border services regulations if (a) the customer is located outside mainland China, (b) the services are actually rendered and consumed wholly outside mainland China, (c) the contract and payment evidence demonstrate an offshore supply chain, and (d) the WFOE has filed a declaration of zero-rated cross-border taxable services on the e-Tax platform. Where a service cannot meet the zero-rating criteria, it may still qualify for an exempt (VAT-free) treatment, but with no input VAT credit.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) - for goods; for services, the e-Tax Cross-Border Taxable Services Registration filing with the Customs-verified service code is required
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill - for physical consignments; for services, the international service contract and the invoice issued by the WFOE to the overseas client
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (IRC / Bank Foreign Exchange Receipt) - issued by a licensed mainland bank evidencing receipt of the export proceeds in foreign currency
  • Supporting documents: overseas client's certificate of registration (e.g., Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate), a statement confirming services consumed outside mainland China, and detailed service delivery logs (for digital services) to evidence non-domestic consumption

All VAT invoices, including zero-rated export invoices, must be issued through the national unified digital invoice platform established by the STA. The platform requires real-time transmission of invoice data via API or an approved invoicing software interface. Each invoice is automatically stamped with a digital signature and QR code for verification. No paper invoicing is permitted for newly registered enterprises in the pilot regions. The system automatically matches invoice data with Customs declaration data and foreign exchange settlement records to detect inconsistencies. For cross-border services, an electronic certificate of zero-rated export of cross-border taxable services is generated and must be maintained for audit purposes.

The WFOE's export zero-rating claims are subject to joint audit by the State Taxation Administration and Customs. Under Article 63 of the Tax Collection Law, any false export declaration resulting in a VAT refund triggers clawback of the refunded amount plus interest at 0.05% per day (annualized 18.25%) from the date of refund. An administrative penalty of 0.5 to 5 times the amount of tax evaded can be imposed. If the case is classified as a crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax refund under Article 204 of the Criminal Law, the responsible persons face criminal liability including imprisonment and confiscation. Moreover, the WFOE will be placed on a high-risk watchlist, have its tax credit rating downgraded, and be barred from e-invoice issuance in serious cases.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Agreement, but the capital account remains heavily regulated. For the WFOE, the paid-in equity capital injected from Hong Kong is a capital account flow requiring prior FDI registration with SAFE, and the funds must be held in a Base Currency Capital Account from which only documented business expenditures and permitted current account payments can be settled. Service trade receipts, software license fees, and dividend remittances qualify as current account transfers that are free of conversion taxes and surrender, but require documentary evidence and post-verification. Profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent face hurdles: the WFOE must complete the annual audit, obtain the corporate income tax clearance certificate, file the dividend distribution resolution, and present the SAFE-registered information; however, a further complication is that the accumulated profits must be sourced from genuine business and subject to a 5% or 10% withholding tax under the China-Hong Kong DTA if the parent is the beneficial owner. Capital account outflows such as intercompany principal, offshore loan repayment, and foreign-exchange forward swaps are not freely permitted and require an underlying trade or direct investment, strict SAFE registration, and the approval of the settlement bank. In practice, the WFOE may retain foreign currency receipts in a foreign currency account or convert them at the spot rate, provided each transaction is matched against the customs declaration or service trade agreement. The key constraint is that 100% of RMB converted from foreign currency capital must be used only for the WFOE's operating purposes; it cannot be recycled into domestic equity investments, illegal loans, or property purchases outside the registered business scope.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a WFOE exporting software services, foreign exchange receipts under current account must be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of the service invoice or the date stated in the underlying contract, and the enterprise retains the option to hold the proceeds in a designated foreign currency account or convert them at the prevailing market rate. The mandatory surrender ratio is zero (0%); China abolished mandatory conversion in 2012, but the proceeds must still be routed through a licensed domestic bank and recorded in the SAFE goods/services trade supervision system. Advance receipts for future services above USD 300,000 are reportable within 30 days of receipt and must be matched with the subsequent delivery.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is low to nil for an onshore Chinese WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) that are regulated in Hong Kong or the EEA do not hold a comprehensive foreign exchange bank license in mainland China and cannot open multi-currency IBANs or provide FX conversion directly to a mainland WFOE. SAFE regulations require all cross-border FX settlements to be cleared through a domestic bank with a counter-party bank licence or a designated clearing network, and the funds must travel through the SWIFT system under a valid trade/investment purpose code. An EMI may only assist as a payment processor for the parent entity in Hong Kong, not for the mainland WFOE itself; therefore the WFOE's treasury wire routing must use its own Tier-1 Chinese bank account as the ultimate receiving and disbursement node, with the EMI used solely as a technical gateway for Stripe payouts if the payout is first received by the Hong Kong parent. Any arrangement where the WFOE's operational revenues are held outside mainland China without a SAFE-approved offshore retention waiver would breach the overall current-account repatriation requirement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The equity injection from the Hong Kong parent will be structured as an SWIFT MT103 wire to the WFOE's FDI Foreign Currency Capital Account opened with the chosen Tier-1 commercial bank. The parent must use the remittance purpose code corresponding to 'Direct Investment - Equity Capital' under SAFE's classification (code 621011 in the China trade-in-services statistics) and must reference the contract or the board resolution in the payment narrative. The bank checks the amount against the outstanding registered capital and credits the capital account. The WFOE then executes a one-time foreign exchange conversion at the onshore spot rate, transferring the converted RMB into a Base RMB Settlement Account dedicated to the enterprise's actual operating expenditures. A licensed Chinese CPA may be engaged to issue a capital verification report where the bank's internal policy requests it; though not a statutory requirement for all WFOEs, the report satisfies the compliance committee if the amount exceeds the company's stated annual operating budget. The above process is only lawful after the bank has completed the online reporting of the FDI Base Information through the SAFE digital registry; the precise timeline is no more than two business days before or after the receipt of the capital, and the bank must also register the final settlement by the fifth working day after conversion under the Currency Settlement Control mechanism.For a cloud/document automation SaaS company, the outbound flow will primarily be SWIFT MT103 remittances for foreign cloud infrastructure (e.g., Azure/AWS sub-accounts), patent royalties, and the dividend distribution to the HK parent. The bank will require each MT103 to carry a valid service-trade transaction number (e.g., SE number generated in the Service Trade Portal) and the relevant contract/invoice. Commercial LCs and documentary collections are not applicable to the intangible SaaS business, but if the WFOE later purchases integration hardware for resale, the company can use a standard import LC. For dividend distribution to the HK parent, the formal rail is still an MT103 under capital account with the 'dividend' purpose code, preceded by the annual audit, a certificate of tax withholding (the 5% or 10% WHT) and the SAFE-registered 'Profit Remittance' filing. The financing rail for urgent cash needs is an intra-group loan under the Cross-Border Refinance Facility: the parent can lend CNY via a the cross-border RMB bridge under SAFE's FTZ flexible policy, or borrow USD with an off-balance-sheet guarantee; the loan principal must be repaid through the same bank's outbound wire.Deliverable CNY forward contract (up to 12 months) with the onshore bank, exclusively for hedging the WFOE's current-account exposure evidenced by signed contracts and past three-months of Stripe settlement statementsCNY FX Swap (USD/CNY) for temporary liquidity management, settled fully onshore and subject to the bank's internal risk limitCross-currency basis swap prohibited for purely speculative purposes; permissible only with SAFE's prior consent and an underlying long-term intercompany loan or infrastructure project registrationNon-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are generally prohibited for onshore corporate entities, as all onshore CNY hedging requires the physical delivery of CNY under the existing capital-account management framework

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Feasibility is low to nil for an onshore Chinese WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, Wise Business, Airwallex) that are regulated in Hong Kong or the EEA do not hold a comprehensive foreign exchange bank license in mainland China and cannot open multi-currency IBANs or provide FX conversion directly to a mainland WFOE. SAFE regulations require all cross-border FX settlements to be cleared through a domestic bank with a counter-party bank licence or a designated clearing network, and the funds must travel through the SWIFT system under a valid trade/investment purpose code. An EMI may only assist as a payment processor for the parent entity in Hong Kong, not for the mainland WFOE itself; therefore the WFOE's treasury wire routing must use its own Tier-1 Chinese bank account as the ultimate receiving and disbursement node, with the EMI used solely as a technical gateway for Stripe payouts if the payout is first received by the Hong Kong parent. Any arrangement where the WFOE's operational revenues are held outside mainland China without a SAFE-approved offshore retention waiver would breach the overall current-account repatriation requirement.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: There are no quantitative balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount thresholds exempting an active Hong Kong private company from statutory audit. Every company incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) as auditor and cause accounts to be audited, unless the company is dormant and applies for audit exemption. Dormant status requires the company to have no significant accounting transactions during the financial year and to satisfy the statutory conditions under sections 359 to 362 of the Companies Ordinance. In practical terms, any active Hong Kong private company with even one transaction, asset, liability, or payroll item is legally mandated to undergo an independent CPA audit.
