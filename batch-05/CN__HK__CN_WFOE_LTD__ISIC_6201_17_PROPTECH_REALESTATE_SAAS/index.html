Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); electronic filings are submitted through the STA National Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal. Export-related customs and tax information is exchanged through the China International Trade Single Window operated by the General Administration of Customs.

E-government filing portal

For a newly incorporated Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) issues an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the Business Licence; this USCC is the sole statutory tax identification number for VAT, corporate income tax, and local surtaxes. Within 30 calendar days after business licence issuance, the WFOE must complete post-registration tax formalities through the e-Tax portal: authenticate the legal representative and shareholders, upload the articles of association, choose the accounting/fiscal year and filing frequency, register its bank deposit account with the PRC tax authority, declare its taxpayer classification (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer), and apply for the electronic invoice quota and tax-control e-invoice system. No separate physical TIN card is issued. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a mainland permanent establishment (for example the HK parent) must additionally complete non-resident enterprise tax registration in the e-Tax system using its home-jurisdiction tax ID and register a PRC withholding agent or payer for China-source dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; China does not provide check-the-box or hybrid mismatch entity elections. A WFOE is mandatorily treated as a PRC resident enterprise subject to enterprise income tax on worldwide income. Available elections are limited to preferential tax treatments (e.g., small and low-profit enterprise rate, R&D super deduction, accelerated depreciation) and do not alter the entity-level tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when the previous 12-month taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000; a newly established WFOE must obtain tax registration immediately upon formation regardless of turnover and will be treated as a small-scale taxpayer until the threshold is exceeded unless it elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

A 0% VAT rate applies to goods actually exported from mainland China after customs clearance and to qualifying cross-border services, including software, information technology, and related technology/SaaS services, where the service recipient is located outside mainland China, the service is fully consumed outside mainland China, payment is received from overseas, and the WFOE retains evidence satisfying the STA's cross-border taxable services rules. For goods, zero-rating is claimed on the export VAT return with the customs export declaration; for services, the taxpayer may claim zero-rating or VAT exemption and must maintain a separate input VAT allocation for the zero-rated supply chain.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Goods Declaration for Exportation) generated through the China International Trade Single Window and matched with the exporter's VAT filing; mandatory for physical goods exports.
  • Transport document proving actual export movement from mainland China: Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or international courier/cross-border postal consignment note.
  • Bank foreign-exchange inward remittance notice or cross-border RMB settlement receipt matched with SAFE's Trade Foreign Exchange Monitoring System (the PRC equivalent of an export collection/payment verification record).
  • For zero-rated SaaS services: signed cross-border service contract, overseas recipient business registration evidence, service delivery/usage logs demonstrating consumption outside mainland China, and the overseas payment receipt; no customs goods declaration is applicable to pure services.

China mandates the use of the STA unified national electronic invoice platform and tax-control invoice system. VAT invoices (including fully digitalised electronic invoices) must be issued through STA-approved tax-control software, are transmitted in real time to the tax authority, carry an anti-counterfeiting tax-control code, and are reconciled electronically with the enterprise's VAT filing. No private third-party clearance API is recognised for statutory VAT invoicing; integration with the e-Tax portal and the digital tax-control environment is mandatory.

The STA cross-audits zero-rating claims against Customs and SAFE data, bank receipts, service contracts, and technology logs. If the required documentation is missing, fraudulent, or untimely, the tax authority reassesses the supply at the applicable VAT rate (6% for SaaS services or 13% for goods), charges a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and may impose administrative penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. Deliberate export zero-rating/invoice fraud triggers unlimited retroactive clawback, suspension or revocation of export-tax-refund qualification, and criminal liability for tax fraud. Negligent documentary defects are generally assessable within three to five years, while fraudulent claims have no statute-of-limitations protection.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Under mainland China's FX architecture, current account items enjoy managed convertibility: bona fide trade in services and goods can be settled via designated banks on an authenticity-review basis, without central approval, provided contracts, invoices, customs records or service performance evidence are retained. Capital account items remain highly controlled: each injection, M&A, and investment exit must be registered with SAFE and routed through a dedicated capital account. FDI proceeds in a WFOE are treated as registered capital and can be converted to RMB at spot, but the RMB cannot be deployed for speculative stock-market investment, wealth-management products, or unauthorized intercompany loans; it must be applied to the entity's actual operating needs. Export receipts are not subject to an across-the-board mandatory conversion ratio, but they must come onshore and be reconciled with customs/SAFE trade monitoring. Profit remittance is permitted only after statutory annual audit, final corporate income tax settlement, and payment of dividends; banks require audited financials, a board dividend resolution confirming after-tax profits, and proof that the tax bureau has filed the withholding of 10% dividend income tax, making the actual remittance timely and lawful but administratively heavy.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export receipts must be collected through a designated FX bank within 180 days of the customs export declaration and matched to SAFE's goods-trade monitoring system. There is no mandatory percentage surrender ratio under normal circumstances; the exporter may retain 100% of export FX in a settlement account, convert to RMB at spot, or use those balances for approved current-account obligations, but every credit and debit must be backed by customs declarations and authentic transaction documents. A/B/C trade classification ratings shape the documentary threshold: B- and C-rated exporters face stricter scrutiny, pre-approval requirements, and reduced timelines.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) through the delegated State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Feasibility is low for a primary onshore treasury rail. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Airwallex, Payoneer, or Stripe Treasury are not licensed domestic commercial banks in the PRC and are legally barred from maintaining a WFOE's sole settlement ledger. SAFE permits only licensed cross-border payment institutions to collect foreign SaaS revenue and remit net funds in RMB after authenticating the underlying software-export contracts. However, domestic payroll, VAT, social insurance, withholding tax, and FX hedging must all flow through a PBOC-regulated commercial bank. Therefore, EMIs should be positioned only as an ancillary receivable-collection accelerator, never as the corporate banking spine.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong law imposes no size-based revenue, asset, or headcount threshold for audit exemption. Every Hong Kong-incorporated private company that is not dormant is legally required to appoint one or more certified public accountants (practising) to audit its financial statements annually. An exemption is available only to a dormant company that has applied to the Companies Registry and has had no significant accounting transactions; an operating PropTech SaaS company cannot rely on the exemption. Accordingly, the audit obligation is mandatory for the target entity irrespective of turnover, balance sheet size, employee count, or number of members.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File must be prepared when total intercompany transactions exceed RMB 400 million in a tax year, or when category-specific thresholds are met: tangible property transfers over RMB 200 million, intangible or financial asset transfers over RMB 100 million, or service transactions over RMB 40 million. Master File is required when the multinational group's prior-year consolidated operating revenue exceeds RMB 5.5 billion, or the taxpayer is selected by the tax authority for a transfer pricing investigation.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must conduct its PropTech development, cloud hosting, client contracting, risk management, and credit screening in China with actual staff and assets. Under SAT Notice 2017 No. 6, DEMPE functions and core income-generating activities (CIGA) must be assessed for intangible-related returns. The Hong Kong parent must have real economic substance in Hong Kong to qualify as beneficial owner; otherwise the Principal Purpose Test under the DTA can deny treaty benefits and Chinese GAAR under CIT Law Article 47 can recharacterise the structure.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Deliberate Under-declaration of Chinese-source SaaS Revenue

Statutory sanction: Article 63 of the Tax Collection Administration Law and Article 201 of the Criminal Law of PRC; recovery of underpaid tax plus 0.05% daily surcharge, monetary penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax, and criminal fine; imprisonment up to 3 years for ordinary tax evasion or up to 7 years where the amount is huge and accounts for a high percentage of tax payable.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and financial officers who direct or approve the evasion may be jointly and severally liable for the tax and penalties by piercing the corporate veil under PRC Company Law; their personal assets may be used for restitution; exit bans and tax blacklisting are imposed under Article 44 TAL; criminal prosecution can result in imprisonment.
Treaty Abuse / Shell Company Used to Claim the 5% Dividend WHT Rate

Statutory sanction: Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (General Anti-Avoidance Rule), SAT Public Notice 2019 No. 35, and the Principal Purpose Test in the DTA; the tax authority may deny treaty benefits, reimpose 10% WHT, assess underpaid tax, add daily surcharge, and impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who certify false Tax Residency Certificates or beneficial ownership declarations can be personally liable for the uncollected WHT; they face civil restitution, blacklisting and travel bans, fines, and criminal liability for fraudulent documents and tax evasion, with imprisonment up to 7 years.
Transfer Pricing Compliance Failure / Unsupportable Intercompany Charges

Statutory sanction: Articles 41 and 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law, SAT Public Notice 2017 No. 6, and Article 63 TAL; tax authorities may make a transfer pricing adjustment, recharacterize payments, disallow deductions, assess additional corporate income tax, and impose daily late-payment surcharge plus documentation penalties.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally destroy records, fail to maintain TP documentation, or cause the WFOE to underpay taxes expose themselves to piercing of the corporate veil; personal restitution demands, exit bans and blacklisting, personal fines, and criminal prosecution for evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may follow, including imprisonment terms of up to 7 years.
