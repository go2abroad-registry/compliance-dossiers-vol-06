Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital exists under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance. A private company limited by shares may incorporate with one ordinary share of HK$1.00 or such nominal amount as the subscriber determines. No escrow, blocked capital account, Government capital deposit, or minimum capital threshold is required; the liability of each member is limited to the unpaid amount, if any, on the shares subscribed. Banks and counterparties may impose their own operating-balance expectations but the State does not mandate any capital contribution.

Procedure: (1) Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation; (2) the registration data is electronically shared with the State Taxation Administration, automatically completing tax registration; (3) the WFOE logs into the Electronic Tax Bureau and completes the initial tax registration formalities, with the USCC serving as the taxpayer identification number; (4) if the WFOE exceeds the VAT general taxpayer threshold, it must apply for VAT general taxpayer status. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All incorporated enterprises are required to register for VAT upon formation. A taxpayer becomes a VAT general taxpayer once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000; below that amount, small-scale taxpayer status applies with reduced compliance obligations and applicable small-scale taxpayer VAT relief.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For physical goods exported from China, the supplier may apply VAT zero-rating and claim a refund of input VAT. For the WFOE's cross-border logistics-fleet SaaS and telematics services, zero-rating is available for qualifying software services and information technology services supplied to non-resident enterprises or individuals where the services are effectively consumed wholly outside the PRC, the counterparty is outside China, and consideration is received from outside China. The statutory basis is the cross-border taxable services zero-rating and VAT-exemption policy of the Ministry of Finance and the State Taxation Administration (Cai Shui [2016] No. 36, as subsequently amended). The WFOE must submit a cross-border zero-rated/VAT-exempt service declaration through the Electronic Tax Bureau, execute a qualifying written contract with the overseas customer, retain evidence of actual offshore consumption and use of the SaaS platform, and receive payment in foreign exchange. Domestic supplies of SaaS remain subject to VAT at 6%, which is below the standard headline rate.

  • Bill of Lading, Sea Waybill or Air Waybill - required only for physical export shipments.

China operates the State Taxation Administration Golden Tax System (Phase IV) and the nationwide Fully Digital Electronic Invoice platform. VAT invoices, including special VAT invoices, must be issued through the E-Tax/e-invoice system; issuance data is transmitted to the STA at the time of invoice creation. Fully digital electronic invoices carry a nationally unique invoice number and anti-fraud QR dynamic stamp. High-volume taxpayers may integrate their accounting/ERP systems with the STA-compliant e-invoice API for real-time issuance and transmission. There is no transaction-by-transaction pre-clearance by a tax authority, but the STA performs system-side verification, quota management, and real-time anti-fraud monitoring of invoice issuance and declaration data.

The STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit zero-rating and export-refund claims. If supporting documents are falsified, incomplete, or the services are found to have been consumed in China, the STA disallows the zero-rating claim, claws back the VAT refunded, charges statutory late-payment interest, and may impose fines under the PRC Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export VAT refund claims are subject to recovery of the fraudulently obtained refund and a penalty of one to five times the refund amount; criminal liability may apply under the PRC Criminal Law. The foreign-exchange authority may also impose penalties for failure to repatriate export proceeds within the stipulated period and for submission of false foreign-exchange receipt documentation.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a fundamentally bifurcated convertibility framework: current account transactions are legally convertible on the basis of genuine, verified trade or service flows, while capital account transactions remain subject to stringent prior approval, registration, and quota discipline. For the CN_WFOE_LTD, the Hong Kong parent shareholder will inject registered capital into the onshore entity, which is categorised as a capital account inflow and therefore requires SAFE foreign-invested enterprise (FIE) registration, a dedicated capital foreign exchange settlement account, and downstream monitoring of cheque-based or direct debit-based usage. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current account transfer only after the WFOE has completed annual audit, filed tax returns, cleared all PRC tax liabilities (including withholding on dividends), and obtained the required board resolution for distribution; the bank will then verify the audited financial statements, the foreign exchange payment application, and the completed SAFE Form F/FTN or equivalent electronic tax clearance certificate. Surrender requirements under current SAFE rules are calibrated around repatriation within 180 days for export service receipts, but mandatory conversion ratios have been substantially relaxed; the entity may elect to retain foreign currency in a ratified onshore settlement account or convert voluntarily at market rate. However, capital account receipts from the HK parent cannot be freely converted or disbursed for purposes outside the approved business scope, and the WFOE must file capital verification reports with the local MOC/AMR system. In practical terms, the WFOE faces no physical surrender of 100% of export proceeds, but it must demonstrate full chain of receipt, counterparty authenticity, and consistent invoice matching to avoid clawbacks during annual SAFE entity examination. Hedging access is likewise tied to real exposure documents, meaning only onshore deliverable FX forwards for current account needs can be executed without special SAFE circular approvals.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All export service receipts, including software subscription and telematics-related cross-border payments, must be repatriated into a Chinese domestic bank account within 180 days from the date of service completion or invoice issuance. No mandatory conversion ratio is currently imposed on current account export proceeds; the WFOE may retain such foreign exchange in its foreign exchange settlement account or sell it to the bank at market rate. For capital account receipts such as HK parent equity injections, SAFE registration must be completed before wiring, and the proceeds must be credited only to the capital account settlement account with use controlled by the approved FIE charter.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI providers (e.g., Airwallex, Currenxie, Revolut Business) are categorically non-viable as a primary or statutory banking rail for the CN_WFOE_LTD. Chinese domestic regulations under PBOC and SAFE require all foreign exchange receipts and payments relating to WFOE business activities to flow through licensed onshore banks with full reporting to SAFE. Offshore EMIs cannot hold SAFE registrations nor issue RMB clearing accounts; using an EMI as the principal treasury node would trigger a compliance void in the capital verification, annual audit, and tax reconciliation chain. The only permissible use is to support the Hong Kong parent's treasury or overseas payroll tasks, but such usage must never receive WFOE export proceeds or inject capital on behalf of the WFOE without traversing a licensed onshore correspondent bank and a documented SAFE fee-based transfer. Therefore, the viable architecture is to maintain standard domestic account rails for all regulatory flows and to use a fintech multi-currency wallet solely for the Hong Kong parent's operating expenses.

Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Corporate Income Tax rate under Article 4 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law)

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% (under PRC CIT Law Articles 3(3), 27(2) and 37); reduced to 5% under Article 10(2) of the China-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement (DTA) where the Hong Kong resident beneficial owner holds at least 25% of the capital of the WFOE and satisfies the anti-conduit and substantive business conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under PRC SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42, contemporaneous Local File documentation is required where annual related-party transactions exceed: RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, RMB 100 million for provision of services, or RMB 40 million for 'other' related-party transactions. A Master File is required where the enterprise's annual total related-party transaction amount exceeds RMB 1 billion, or the enterprise is part of a multinational group that exceeds the aggregate revenue threshold corresponding to the Chinese filing requirement.

Economic substance scope: A PRC WFOE is not an offshore entity, but it is nonetheless subject to PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47), general anti-abuse provisions, and the Beneficial Ownership Doctrine under the China-HK DTA. The logistical tech SaaS WFOE must demonstrate actual functions, assets, employees, and risks in mainland China, while the Hong Kong parent must show substantive CIGA in Hong Kong, including board meetings, strategic commercial decisions, treasury holding capacity, and real operational oversight. Arrangements structured solely to avoid or reduce dividend WHT, or to route royalty fees through a shell Hong Kong entity, will be recharacterised under the substance-over-form and economic substance doctrines, with the resulting tax, interest, and penalties assessed against the WFOE and the shareholders.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Shell-Company Treaty Abuse and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: The taxpayer would lose DTA protection under Article 10(2) and be treated as deriving a China-sourced dividend subject to 10% WHT, plus late surcharges and a penalty of 50% to 500% of the under-withheld tax under Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law. Under PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47), the arrangement may be disregarded and the dividend recharacterised as a taxable distribution to the ultimate direct shareholder.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial-ownership declarations or approved the dividend stripping structure are exposed to civil restitution of the shortpaid withholding tax and corporate losses under Company Law Article 20 (piercing the corporate veil); travel bans and exit restrictions are imposed on the legal representative and responsible finance officer under Article 66 TAL and the PRC social credit blacklist. Criminal exposure under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion carries up to 7 years imprisonment and fines; if forged Hong Kong certificates are used, additional fraud offences may add 10 years or more.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Cost-Stripping

Statutory sanction: PRC CIT Law Article 41 requires arm's length pricing; Article 44 permits the tax authority to deem taxable income when documentation is not submitted; Article 48 imposes a special tax adjustment surcharge on underpaid tax for the period from the due date to the adjustment date, and Article 63 TAL penalties of 50% to 500% apply where the mispricing is fraudulent. The tax authority may also deny deductibility of the entire fee and reclassify it as a non-tax-deductible distribution.

Director personal liability: Directors and the chief financial officer who knowingly approve fictitious cross-border services are jointly and severally liable for the resulting Chinese tax deficiency under Company Law Article 20. They may be blacklisted and prohibited from leaving China under Article 66 TAL. If the fraudulent payments are supported by false invoices, the responsible persons are exposed to Criminal Law Article 205 (false special VAT invoices) with sentences reaching life imprisonment in very serious cases; ordinary tax evasion under Article 201 carries up to 7 years.
Non-Filing of the Annual CIT Return, Related-Party Schedule, and Outbound Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: Late filing to Article 62 TAL incurs RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 fines; non-payment of the tax itself incurs a 0.05% daily late-interest charge under Article 32; Article 63 TAL penalises tax evasion at 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; illegal issuance of refund or concealment of income is treated as criminal tax fraud under Criminal Law Article 201 if the evaded tax exceeds RMB 100,000 and 10% of the total tax payable.

Director personal liability: The legal representative and the finance director are personally liable for civil and criminal consequences when they intentionally or recklessly cause the company to fail the filing and withholding duties. Directors face civil asset recovery under Company Law Article 20, freeze and confiscation orders, travel bans/exit blacklisting under Article 66 TAL, and imprisonment terms of up to 7 years for criminal tax evasion; in cases where the non-filing is part of a deliberate scheme to expatriate profits abroad, the courts may impose heavier sentences for damaging order or insider laundering under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and Criminal Law Articles 191 and 201.
