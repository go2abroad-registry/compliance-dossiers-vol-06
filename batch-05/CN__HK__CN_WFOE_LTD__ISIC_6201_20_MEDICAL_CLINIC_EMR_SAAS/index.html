Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), operating through the National Electronic Tax Bureau portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn and the China Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

A foreign non-resident investor establishing a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) in China must first obtain a business license from the local Market Regulation Administration, which issues the WFOE a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). The USCC is the statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN) and VAT identification number for the entity under the multi-license integration system. No separate TIN application is required; the tax authority completes the national tax registration automatically within 30 days of the issuance of the business license via inter-agency data sharing. Thereafter, the WFOE legal representative and financial officer must complete biometric real-name authentication on the Electronic Tax Bureau portal, register the entity's bank settlement account for tax payment purposes, file the accounting system and cost-allocation methods, and submit the required anti-fraud compliance declarations for issuance and receipt of electronic invoices.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The EMR SaaS and related software services qualify for zero-rated VAT if they are provided to overseas entities and are wholly consumed outside mainland China, meaning the offshore recipient is the party that receives and uses the service, the service is not connected to any domestic sale of goods or transfer of real property, and the intangible service is not used within mainland China. The WFOE must be registered as a VAT general taxpayer, maintain proper records demonstrating the cross-border nature of the transaction, file the zero-rating declaration through the Electronic Tax Bureau, and retain service contracts, invoices, proof of foreign exchange receipt, and if applicable, the offshore service filing record with the tax authority.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) in the qualified form required for physical goods or software media that cross the PRC border, if any part of the EMR SaaS deliverable is shipped as an embedded device or server medium
  • Cross-Border Service Contract expressly identifying the overseas service recipient, the nature of the EMR SaaS, payment terms, and the place of consumption
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice issued through the Electronic Invoice Service Platform
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (Bank Telegraphic Transfer / FIRC equivalent) evidencing receipt of foreign currency from outside mainland China
  • Bill of Lading or airway bill issued by the carrier for any physical component of the export, where applicable
  • Proof of login, delivery, or cloud usage by the offshore entity demonstrating that the software service is wholly consumed outside mainland China

Fully digitalized electronic invoices (shu dian piao) are mandatory and must be issued through the national Electronic Invoice Service Platform. The platform transmits complete invoice data to the State Taxation Administration in real time, assigns a unique invoice number, and applies an automatic tax-control stamp or anti-fraud marker. Traditional tax-control disk-based invoicing is no longer required. Every sales invoice, credit note, and related reversal transaction must be cleared through the platform before it is legally valid for input VAT deduction and export zero-rating substantiation.

The State Taxation Administration may retrospectively disallow export zero-rating claims and claw back all input VAT credits previously refunded, together with statutory late-payment surcharges. Where zero-rating is supported by false documentation, fictitious cross-border service contracts, or unverified foreign exchange receipts, the taxpayer is subject to penalties under Article 63 of the Tax Administration and Collection Law ranging from 50 percent to 500 percent of the unpaid or refunded tax amount. In egregious cases involving fraudulent issuance of zero-rated invoices, the taxpayer may face criminal liability for tax evasion or issuance of false special VAT invoices, including imprisonment, forfeiture of the right to export tax refunds, and public blacklisting in tax integrity records.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with current-account convertibility and strict capital-account controls. For a mainland China WFOE with a Hong Kong parent, ordinary operational foreign exchange receipts from SaaS subscriptions are treated as service-trade current-account income and can be credited to a foreign-currency current account or converted into RMB at a designated bank once genuine commercial documents, contracts, and invoices are verified. Export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China; however, there is no mandatory percentage surrender since SAFE's current-account liberalization reforms. Capital-account transactions, including the injection of registered capital from the Hong Kong parent, require SAFE foreign direct investment registration and the use of a dedicated capital account. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is permitted only after the WFOE has completed statutory annual audit, paid all due taxes, and obtained the bank's verification of distributable reserves; the remittance itself is classified as investment income and enjoys current-account convertibility but still requires the tax payment certificate and audited financial report. The practical treasury design must therefore separate current-account flows, which can be cleared through normal operational accounts, from capital-account flows, which are locked into SAFE-monitored escrow-like accounts until the registered capital is fully verified. Cash-pooling between the WFOE and the Hong Kong parent is not automatic; cross-border intra-group financing must be documented and registered under SAFE rules, and any offshore RMB clearing must comply with the cross-border RMB management framework administered by PBOC.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring regime, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated to mainland China within 180 days after the customs declaration date and must be either credited to the enterprise's foreign-currency current account or settled into RMB at a SAFE-designated bank. There is no statutory percentage of mandatory conversion for export receipts; the converting enterprise may elect to retain the full amount in a foreign-currency account. The settlement bank must verify the export customs declaration, invoice, logistics documents, and the enterprise's trade-facilitation classification before accepting the conversion or credit. For cross-border SaaS and software export proceeds treated as service trade, similar repatriation and declaration duties apply under the International Balance of Payments statistics and SAFE's service-trade monitoring.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Direct viability is low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Revolut Business are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as domestic banks and are not permitted to open onshore CNY or foreign-currency clearing accounts, provide SAFE-registered FX settlement, or perform statutory capital verification for a mainland China WFOE. Indirect viability is moderate only when the fintech acts as a front-end receivables or payables facilitator that clears through a fully licensed mainland partner bank or payment institution. Legal constraints require all cross-border FX transactions to be handled by licensed designated forex banks, and foreign EMIs have no standing to register a WFOE's capital-account transactions. The WFOE should therefore maintain its primary treasury and clearing relationships with an onshore Tier-1 commercial bank and use fintech solutions only for invoicing, customer-api orchestration, and reconciliation, never as the legal clearing rail for capital inflows or SAFE-regulated FX settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital from the Hong Kong parent must first be booked under SAFE's FDI information registration. The Hong Kong parent should execute a SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated FDI capital account at a Tier-1 Chinese commercial bank; field 50K identifies the remitter, field 59 identifies the WFOE beneficiary, and field 70 states FDI registered capital injection with the SAFE registration serial number. The receiving bank performs a compliance check and reports the payment in the international balance of payments statistics. After funds arrive, the WFOE obtains a capital-account confirmation and may apply for foreign-exchange capital settlement into RMB under the capital account for payment of local operating expenses. Each injection is verified by a Chinese certified public accountant, who issues a capital verification report, and the bank will not fully release account controls until capital verification and SAFE filing are closed.For EMR software licenses, subscriptions, and third-party cloud services, the standard rail is SWIFT MT103 with full remitter and beneficiary details and a valid SAFE service-trade purpose code. For larger clinical IT hardware procurement, a commercial letter of credit or documentary collection can be used through the bank's trade finance desk. Any service-trade outward remittance above the USD 50,000 equivalent must be preceded by a tax filing certificate issued by the tax authority for withholding income tax or VAT on the service fee, unless a tax treaty exemption has been approved. The bank will require the underlying contract, invoice, and proof of the recipient's non-resident status. CIPS can also clear outbound RMB payments to the Hong Kong parent or vendors, and the selected clearing rail depends on the invoice currency and the vendor's settlement capability.Onshore deliverable RMB/USD forward contracts with a SAFE-designated bank, permitted only where there is a proven current-account or capital-account underlying exposure and no speculative net positions.Cross-currency swaps for hedging medium-term foreign-currency debt or registered capital repayment exposure, subject to SAFE registration and real underlying transaction documentation.Onshore foreign-exchange swaps for temporary liquidity matching between the WFOE's RMB and USD cash flows, supported by cash-flow forecasts and underlying invoices.Offshore non-deliverable forwards on USD/CNH are not an onshore-clearing vehicle for the mainland WFOE; if the Hong Kong parent needs to hedge CNH/USD balance-sheet exposure, the NDF must be executed outside mainland China by the Hong Kong entity and ring-fenced from the WFOE's operational accounts.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Direct viability is low. Offshore fintech EMIs such as Airwallex, Currencycloud, or Revolut Business are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE as domestic banks and are not permitted to open onshore CNY or foreign-currency clearing accounts, provide SAFE-registered FX settlement, or perform statutory capital verification for a mainland China WFOE. Indirect viability is moderate only when the fintech acts as a front-end receivables or payables facilitator that clears through a fully licensed mainland partner bank or payment institution. Legal constraints require all cross-border FX transactions to be handled by licensed designated forex banks, and foreign EMIs have no standing to register a WFOE's capital-account transactions. The WFOE should therefore maintain its primary treasury and clearing relationships with an onshore Tier-1 commercial bank and use fintech solutions only for invoicing, customer-api orchestration, and reconciliation, never as the legal clearing rail for capital inflows or SAFE-regulated FX settlements.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB. A Local File is required for the PRC WFOE if annual related-party transactions meet any of these thresholds: tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million; intangible asset transfers above RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million; or other related-party transactions above RMB 40 million. A Master File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or when the tax authority specifically issues a notification requiring it.

Economic substance scope: Although the PRC WFOE is a substantive onshore entity and not an offshore vehicle, the anti-abuse doctrines under EIT Law Articles 47 and 48, SAT Public Notice 2017 No. 6, and the China-Hong Kong DTA beneficial ownership tests require that any Hong Kong shareholder, IP holder or service provider actually performs functions, assumes risks and employs personnel for the Core Income-Generating Activities. For an EMR SaaS business, CIGA include software engineering, clinical workflow configuration, data hosting, cybersecurity, contract negotiation, pricing and risk assumption. If those activities are in fact managed and controlled in mainland China while only passive paper documents exist in Hong Kong, the tax authorities will disregard the Hong Kong entity, deny treaty benefits and impose withholding and penal adjustments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion and deliberate under-declaration of China-source income

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 and Article 47 authorise the tax authority to recharacterise the arrangement and make transfer pricing or general anti-avoidance adjustments. TCAL Article 63 classifies deliberate under-declaration as tax evasion, requiring repayment of evaded tax, daily 0.05% late payment surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes imprisonment and fines for large fraudulent tax evasion.

Director personal liability: If the directors or de facto controllers abuse the corporate form to divert revenue and evade tax, the PRC Company Law permits piercing the corporate veil, making shareholders and responsible directors jointly and severally liable for the adjusted tax, penalties and unpaid corporate creditors. The legal representative can be blacklisted by the tax authority, restricted from leaving China, subjected to asset tracing and civil restitution, and criminally prosecuted with imprisonment up to 7 years for serious evasion.
Shell company DTA abuse and improper treaty relief claim

Statutory sanction: SAT Public Notice 2019 No. 35 and the beneficial ownership provisions of the China-Hong Kong Tax Arrangement permit the tax authority to deny treaty benefits and to apply the statutory 10% withholding tax plus late payment surcharge. A false treaty claim may be treated as tax evasion under TCAL Article 63, with penalties of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax. If the WFOE fails to withhold, EIT Law Article 107 makes it liable as the withholding agent.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign false treaty declarations and direct the WFOE to remit dividends without correct withholding expose themselves to personal liability for the unremitted withholding tax, civil asset restitution, travel bans and blacklisting. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may reach 7 years' imprisonment if the false DTA claim forms part of a larger fraudulent underpayment scheme.
Transfer pricing documentation failure and non-arm's length intercompany intellectual property charges

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 41 disallows non-arm's length related-party pricing, and Article 44 permits the tax authority to assess taxable income by deemed methods when the taxpayer fails to provide related-party documentation. Interest and late payment surcharges apply under TCAL Article 32 and EIT Law Article 48. TCAL Article 62 imposes administrative fines of RMB 2,000 to RMB 20,000 for failure to maintain records and documentation. In aggravated cases the scheme can be recharacterised as tax evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly implement the fictitious IP structure and execute inflated intercompany royalty payments may be found to have siphoned corporate assets through abuse of limited liability, allowing piercing of the corporate veil and joint and several civil liability. Personal consequences include court-ordered restitution, seizure of personal assets, tax blacklisting, travel bans and imprisonment for aiding tax evasion, with sentence length tied to the amount of tax and penalties evaded.
