Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, acting through the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal and local STA tax service halls. For the Hong Kong holding entity, the separate competent authority is the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department (IRD), but the mainland WFOE's tax registration and compliance sit exclusively with the STA.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: The foreign investor incorporates the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtains the Business License carrying an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is accepted by the STA as the taxpayer identification number for CIT, VAT, and local taxes. Step 2: Within 30 calendar days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration in the e-Tax portal, submitting the USCC, entity articles, legal representative identity, registered address, shareholding and capital schedule, bank account details, accounting/fiscal-year election, and the expected VAT taxpayer qualification (small-scale vs general taxpayer). Step 3: The STA tax office opens the taxpayer master file and, if applicable, VAT general taxpayer registration, issuing the e-tax taxpayer profile with the tax classification codes. Step 4: Where the non-resident parent provides cross-border services or receives dividends, interest, or royalties from the WFOE, the WFOE performs withholding tax registration; the STA generates a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number for the parent. A non-resident with no Chinese establishment is not required to undergo full resident tax registration, but a non-resident that creates a permanent establishment must register that establishment separately with the competent STA office. Step 5: For Hong Kong tax purposes, the parent entity must separately obtain or update its Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate and the relevant IRD profiles, but that registration is not part of the Chinese national tax ID process and does not replace the mainland WFOE's TIN.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
China does not operate a check-the-box regime and has no elective hybrid mismatch entity classification. The WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise subject to entity-level CIT at 25% on worldwide income. The only statutory election relevant here is the VAT general taxpayer election for enterprises below the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales threshold; above the threshold, general taxpayer registration is compulsory. China also applies statutory anti-avoidance regimes, including thin capitalisation, controlled foreign corporation rules, and the general anti-abuse rule, none of which are elective.

RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales excluding VAT is the statutory turnover threshold for mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration.         Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Under MOF/STA Notice No. 36 of 2016, Annex 4, being the Provisions on Zero VAT Rate and Exemption for Cross-Border Taxable Services, zero-rating to 0% VAT is available to a Chinese general VAT taxpayer that supplies software services, information system services, business process management services, offshore outsourcing services, or other listed services to an overseas customer, provided the service is completely consumed outside China. The WFOE must evidence the overseas residence of the customer, the absence of any Chinese establishment through which the customer receives the service, the fact that the service is not connected to goods or real property located in China, and the existence of a wholly cross-border contractual, invoicing, delivery, and payment circuit. SaaS analytics subscriptions can qualify as software or information-system services under the catalogue. Where a service is not in the zero-rated list but is supplied to an overseas customer and wholly consumed offshore, it may still qualify for the VAT exemption without input VAT refund.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with RMB partially convertible. The PBOC and SAFE jointly administer a legal framework that distinguishes between current account and capital account convertibility. For a Hong Kong-incorporated parent funding a mainland Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), remittances under current account items (trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties) can be handled in the onshore banking system with invoice, contract and tax-proof documentation and are effectively freely convertible once the bank performs detail verification. However, capital account transactions, including foreign direct investment capital injections, foreign loans and equity transfers, require separate SAFE registration or approval before any bank will release funds. Dividends from the WFOE to its foreign parent must pass through the profit remittance process, requiring an audited annual financial statement, tax clearance certificate or corporate income tax declaration, board resolution declaring distributable profits, and a bank-operated verification under the Foreign Exchange Business Guidelines. The practical hurdles include delayed audit in high-risk periods, documentary mismatches, and the tax administration requirement to prove or withhold tax under the tax treaty between the Chinese Mainland and Hong Kong SAR. Overall, no full capital account convertibility exists; treasury managers should budget for 7-15 business days for each compliant cross-border transaction and maintain a paper trail from the underlying commercial contract.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Export foreign currency receipts must be repatriated into the domestic banking system within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration unless SAFE approves an extension. There is no mandatory conversion ratio: the WFOE may keep 100% of export proceeds in a foreign currency settlement account or sell them to a domestic bank at the prevailing market rate. However, proceeds must be matched to customs declarations and the bank's verification system, and any discrepancy beyond the tolerance ratio will block future payments.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Master / Local File threshold: Local File preparation triggers under SAT Bulletin No. 42 (2016): annual related-party tangible property transactions exceeding RMB 200 million; intangible property transfers or licenses exceeding RMB 100 million; financial asset transfers exceeding RMB 100 million; and service transactions exceeding RMB 40 million. Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion by Falsified Revenue Reporting
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 TCAL, the taxpayer is ordered to pay tax evaded, a daily overdue surcharge equal to 0.05% of the unpaid tax, and a monetary penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the evaded amount. In criminal cases, the misdeclared tax amount exceeding RMB 50,000 and constituting over 10% of the tax payable triggers Article 201 of the Criminal Law, with imprisonment up to 3 years for amounts relatively large and 3–7 years for enormous amounts.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who sign falsified returns or authorize the concealment are jointly and severally liable to the extent assets were stripped; PRC tax authorities may impose freezing of personal assets, siezure of bank accounts, travel bans and blacklisting. Criminal conviction leads to direct imprisonment, fines, and disgorgement of unlawfully retained funds; the corporate veil is pierced by proof of personal wrongdoing.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation and Missing TP Documentation
        
Statutory sanction: The SAT may adjust taxable income under Article 41 of the CIT Law and impose a five-year retroactive adjustment for TP purposes, adding a 0.05% daily late surcharge on the additional tax. Failure to submit contemporaneous TP documentation under Article 44 TCAL gives rise to a fixed fine of RMB 10,000–50,000 and a 50% to 5 times penalty on the under-reported tax if the adjustment is linked to an insufficiency in submission.

        
Director personal liability: Director officers who negotiate the intercompany contracts without substance are exposed to monetary liability for the sham arrangement; a travel ban may be imposed for non-payment against their personal assets. If TP adjustments are followed by criminal tax evasion, directors can be prosecuted under Article 201, and the controlling person of the Hong Kong affiliate may be named as architect of the arrangement with personal penalties.

      

      
      

        Shell Hong Kong Company and Abuse of DTA Beneficial Ownership
        
Statutory sanction: Under Article 122 CIT Law and SAT Bulletin No. 15 of 2019, the beneficial ownership test and principal purpose test deny the treaty rate. The dividend is then re-assessed at the 10% statutory WHT, and the WFOE, as withholding agent, is responsible for the difference plus daily late surcharge and penalties up to 5 times the under-withheld amount. In egregious cases, the WFOE and the Hong Kong shell are jointly examined as a tax evasion arrangement.

        
Director personal liability: The concept of piercing the corporate veil applies where the shell is a sham: the shareholders and directors of the Hong Kong company are exposed to civil asset recovery, PRC travel bans, and criminal prosecution under Articles 201 and 205 of the Criminal Law. The PRC tax authority may issue a restriction order on fund transfers from Hong Kong bank accounts, and the HK IRD exchange of information could lead to criminal disclosure proceedings against the responsible persons.
