Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic registration and filing are conducted through the State Taxation Administration Electronic Tax Bureau portal coordinated with provincial and municipal tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a mainland China tax resident entity. Its statutory tax identification number is the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) imprinted on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); no separate VAT registration number is issued to enterprises. Procedure: (1) complete company formation with SAMR and obtain the business license; (2) complete tax registration with the competent tax authority within 30 calendar days after establishment; (3) activate the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau account; (4) elect VAT taxpayer classification as small-scale taxpayer or general taxpayer based on expected annual taxable turnover; (5) apply for electronic invoice issuance authorization and connect the accounting/invoicing system to STA digital tax control infrastructure. A foreign non-resident parent without China-source income does not require a Chinese TIN; if the Hong Kong parent later earns China-source income, it must separately file for non-resident enterprise tax registration with the STA and obtain a non-resident tax identifier for withholding and compliance purposes.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must complete VAT registration upon incorporation irrespective of turnover; annual taxable sales below RMB 5,000,000 may remain small-scale taxpayers, while annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 make general taxpayer VAT registration mandatory.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rated VAT treatment applies to goods physically exported from China and to qualifying cross-border services consumed entirely outside China. For the remote desktop access, screen-sharing, and unattended machine telemetry software supplied by the WFOE, zero-rating requires (1) the service to be provided to a non-resident enterprise or non-resident individual; (2) the service to be wholly or predominantly consumed outside China; (3) a valid cross-border service contract and zero-rate invoice; (4) foreign exchange receipt from the offshore customer; and (5) prior zero-rate filing in the Unified Electronic Tax Bureau. Services supplied or used within China, or domestic on-premises support, remain taxable at the applicable domestic rate.

  • Customs Export Declaration (customs export clearance certificate issued through the China International Trade Single Window)
  • Original Bill of Lading (B/L) or airway bill (AWB) evidencing physical export movement to the offshore buyer
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank foreign exchange settlement/collection voucher evidencing receipt of export proceeds from the overseas customer
  • Commercial invoice, packing list, purchase order/sale contract, and the export VAT zero-rating/rebate application package held for tax and customs audit
  • For pure software/IoT services without physical goods: signed cross-border service contract, VAT zero-rate invoice, overseas payment receipt, bank foreign exchange settlement proof, and detailed technical records (server access logs, device telemetry, IP and geolocation evidence) demonstrating that the remote screen-share and telemetry support were delivered to customers outside China

China requires API-connected electronic invoicing through the State Taxation Administration Golden Tax Project Phase IV and the Electronic Invoice Service Platform. VAT invoices and digitalized electronic invoices (DEI) are transmitted to the tax authority in real time for fiscal clearance, are digitally stamped after validation, and are subject to automated anti-fraud risk checks; buyers cannot claim input VAT credit on unvalidated invoices.

The STA and customs jointly audit export zero-rating claims. Any false declaration, missing customs or forex document, failure to repatriate export proceeds within 180 days, or inability to evidence offshore service consumption reverses the zero rate, requires VAT to be recharged at the applicable domestic rate (6% for software/IT services or 13% for goods), and triggers a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day plus an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. In fraudulent export-refund or fake-invoice cases, the claimed VAT refund is clawed back with interest, zero-rating privileges are suspended, and criminal liability for tax evasion or export fraud may be referred.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC operates a controlled but progressively liberalised foreign-exchange regime. For the mainland WFOE, current-account items (trade in goods, software licensing fees, royalties, profit distributions and services) are generally convertible on a real-transaction basis: the enterprise must present a genuine underlying contract, invoice, customs or tax document, and the bank will settle or remit at the market exchange rate. Capital-account items are not freely convertible; inbound equity capital for the WFOE requires a SAFE Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) registration, a dedicated capital account with a licensed bank, and capital verification by an accountant before the funds can be used. The capital account is further subject to payment-by-use restrictions: converted RMB from registered capital can be applied to wages, rent, raw materials, and ordinary operating expenses, but cannot be lent to related parties or used for equity investments or real estate unless separately authorised. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current-account outflow but is operationally conditional upon audited financial statements, tax payment certificates, board approval, and confirmation that the WFOE has completed all dividend withholding taxes. Full RMB convertibility is not available; offshore CNH/USD/NDF markets are separate from the onshore CNY market, and conversion boundaries are enforced through bank Real Name systems and SAFE reporting.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration rules, there is no fixed percentage surrender ratio. All export receipts must be repatriated to the mainland China banking system and matched against export customs declarations within the prescribed monitoring period (generally within 90 days for unrelated-party goods trade, unless extended trade credit terms are registered with the bank). The WFOE may choose to convert the foreign currency into RMB at the market exchange rate or retain the foreign currency in its current-account foreign exchange account, but the bank must hold the underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration and bill of lading or similar shipping evidence on file. Missing or mismatched export collection will trigger a SAFE compliance investigation and may result in the suspension of trade remittance facilities.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) for the mainland China WFOE; Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for the Hong Kong holding/treasury bank layer.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Non-viable for the PRC WFOE as a regulated primary account. PRC law requires any entity that opens bank accounts, receives foreign direct investment, converts capital-account funds, or executes cross-border RMB/FX payments to use a licensed commercial bank supervised by PBOC and SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise are not licensed by the Chinese authorities to provide core deposit or clearing services to a mainland-incorporated WFOE. They cannot file SAFE FDI registrations, perform capital verification, provide formal tax-clearance routing for profit remittance, or issue the bank confirmation documents that the Chinese tax authority expects. Stripe and Paddle may be used by the Hong Kong parent as payment aggregator rails for overseas software subscription revenue, but the mainland WFOE must keep its statutory corporate treasury and capital-account flows inside SAFE-licensed onshore banks.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit registered capital through SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's dedicated FDI capital account at the designated domestic bank. The remittance instruction must identify the beneficiary precisely, reference the SAFE FDI Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate number, and state the purpose as equity injection / registered capital contribution under the direct investment balance-of-payments category. The receiving bank will perform capital-account monitoring, verify the remitter against the shareholder registry, and after receipt the WFOE must complete external capital verification through a licensed accounting firm and report the receipt to SAFE through the bank's capital project information system. The funds may then be settled into RMB only for permitted operating expenditures such as wages, rent, procurement of materials and software development costs; the bank will assign the appropriate balance-of-payments transaction code and maintain the payment-by-use audit trail.Outbound disbursements from the WFOE will be cleared through SWIFT MT103 for software licence fees, subscription services, cloud infrastructure costs, professional fees and intercompany service charges. If the WFOE procures physical IoT hardware or devices, a Commercial Letter of Credit (MT700) or documentary collection may be used for goods trade, supported by invoices, packing lists, bills of lading, inspection certificates and SAFE trade-credit registrations. For outbound royalty or software-related remittances, the WFOE must first complete non-resident enterprise income tax withholding, VAT and any applicable surcharges, obtain the tax clearance certificate, and present the approved contract to the bank before the RMB account is debited and converted into foreign currency for the outward wire. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Non-viable for the PRC WFOE as a regulated primary account. PRC law requires any entity that opens bank accounts, receives foreign direct investment, converts capital-account funds, or executes cross-border RMB/FX payments to use a licensed commercial bank supervised by PBOC and SAFE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, Currenxie, or Wise are not licensed by the Chinese authorities to provide core deposit or clearing services to a mainland-incorporated WFOE. They cannot file SAFE FDI registrations, perform capital verification, provide formal tax-clearance routing for profit remittance, or issue the bank confirmation documents that the Chinese tax authority expects. Stripe and Paddle may be used by the Hong Kong parent as payment aggregator rails for overseas software subscription revenue, but the mainland WFOE must keep its statutory corporate treasury and capital-account flows inside SAFE-licensed onshore banks.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is mandatory when aggregate related-party transactions in a single fiscal year exceed RMB 40 million for transactions other than tangible assets; RMB 100 million each for intangible asset transfers or financial asset transfers; and RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers. Master File and Country-by-Country Report are mandatory when the ultimate parent's annual consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and the Chinese filing entity is required to participate in the group reporting.

Economic substance scope: The PRC applies a substance-over-form principle through the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and the beneficial owner test in treaty contexts. For a Hong Kong intermediate holding company, Hong Kong's Inland Revenue Ordinance and the Economic Substance Requirements applicable to foreign-source income require the entity to carry on core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong, including premises, staff, and risk management. The Chinese WFOE must not be used as a shell to divert profits; profit attribution must be consistent with the actual functions performed, assets used, and risks assumed by each entity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: Under PRC EIT Law Articles 41 and 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (STA Announcement 2017 No. 6), the tax authority may re-determine taxable income through transfer pricing adjustments and impose interest at the benchmark lending rate plus 5 percentage points. If the arrangement is treated as intentional concealment, Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly approve the sham pricing can be held personally liable under PRC Company Law Articles 20 and 21 for abuse of corporate limited liability causing losses to the state and other creditors. For tax evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and representing more than 10% of the amount payable, persons directly responsible face criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, punishable by up to 3 years' imprisonment (3 to 7 years for large sums) and fines, plus travel bans and blacklisting as serious tax violators.
Failure to File Related-Party Disclosures and Country-by-Country Report

Statutory sanction: Under EIT Law Article 44 and STA Announcement 2016 No. 34, the tax authority may assess taxable income by reasonable estimation and impose late interest at the base rate plus 5 points. Tax Collection Administration Law Article 62 permits fines up to RMB 10,000 for failure to file; Article 64 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% for false under-reporting of taxable income.

Director personal liability: Directors who intentionally conceal related-party transactions or destroy documents may face criminal liability for tax evasion under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, with personal fines of up to RMB 100,000 and imprisonment of up to 7 years. Their personal assets may be pursued in civil recovery where the corporate veil is pierced under Company Law Article 20 for using the company to evade tax obligations.
Abuse of Tax Treaty / Shell Company Conduit

Statutory sanction: Under STA Announcement 2018 No. 9, the tax authority may deny beneficial ownership status and recapture the 5% differential tax, increasing the rate to the domestic 10%, plus late payment interest. The General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 allows re-characterisation of the conduit arrangement, and Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 may apply a penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax for fraudulent avoidance.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the dividend declarations and treaty relief filings may be jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax and penalties if they made false representations. Where the arrangement constitutes intentional tax evasion, criminal liability under PRC Criminal Law Article 201 attaches, with imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines. Directors may suffer asset freezes, travel restrictions, blacklisting, and potential personal bankruptcy proceedings where the company cannot satisfy the tax debt and the veil is pierced under Company Law Articles 20 and 21.
