Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital is imposed for an ordinary software WFOE, and no blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Law or the Foreign Investment Law. VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

No turnover threshold triggers VAT registration; all business entities must register for VAT upon incorporation. However, the distinction between small-scale taxpayer and general taxpayer is based on annual taxable sales: RMB 5,000,000 per year. Small-scale taxpayers may benefit from lower rates and exemptions, while general taxpayers use input-output VAT mechanisms.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under PRC VAT rules, exported goods and qualifying cross-border services are zero-rated or VAT-exempt. To claim 0% VAT on the export of gaming anti-cheat software/middleware supplied as digital goods or services: (1) The recipient must be a non-resident enterprise or individual located outside mainland China; (2) The service must be rendered entirely outside China or in connection with goods exported outside China; (3) For physical goods, the exported goods must be lawfully declared at a Chinese customs port and leave mainland China; (4) The full export sales amount must be received in foreign exchange from overseas; (5) The taxpayer must make an application for export VAT zero-rating (exemption/refund) through the export tax refund system. For software delivered electronically with no customs physical export, zero-rating is available only for qualifying cross-border services and requires documentation proving the overseas recipient and actual consumption outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export Bill of Entry/出口货物报关单) generated through China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial invoice with export sales details in a foreign currency
  • Packing list
  • Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing the physical export movement
  • Foreign Exchange Receipt Confirmation (FIRC/出口收汇核销单) or bank receipt voucher from the designated foreign exchange bank
  • Export VAT refund application form (出口退税申报表)
  • Cross-border service contract or license agreement for digital exports, with the customer's overseas business registration or tax ID
  • Bank SWIFT/receipt evidence matching the declared export amount and deadlines
  • Dispatched export verification data matched between customs, tax, and SAFE systems

Since the nationwide implementation of the Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoices (数电票) reform, VAT taxpayers must issue electronic invoices through the State Taxation Administration's unified e-invoice service platform. The system performs real-time fiscal clearance by transmitting invoice data to STA servers, automatically assigning unique invoice numbers, performing anti-fraud validation, and applying digital stamping/verification. Enterprises must integrate their billing systems with the national platform either via API or by using the e-Tax taxpayer portal; no unauthorized offline invoice systems are permitted for general taxpayers. There are mandatory requirements for invoice authenticity verification, quota control, and real-time reporting of all VAT invoices.

Export zero-rating and VAT refund claims are subject to rigorous scrutiny by the STA and customs through the export tax refund audit system. Tax authorities may verify the authenticity of customs declarations, physical movement, payment receipts, and the foreign exchange repatriation. If the export is not substantiated, or if foreign exchange is not repatriated within 180 days (unless an approved extension is obtained), the taxpayer must reverse the zero-rating and pay the standard VAT on the deemed domestic sale, plus late payment penalties (0.05% per day). For fraudulent export refund or zero-rating claims, the taxpayer must repay the refunded tax and pay a fine of 50% to 5 times the tax amount involved. Serious cases involving forged documents or fictitious exports constitute the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates (骗取出口退税罪), punishable by imprisonment up to life, criminal fines, and confiscation. In addition, administrative sanctions may include downgrading the taxpayer's export tax refund classification and revocation of export management qualifications.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is convertible on current account for trade and service transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, but the capital account remains heavily controlled by SAFE. The WFOE is a resident enterprise for foreign-exchange purposes. Its equity injection from Hong Kong is classified as foreign direct investment and must be supported by an SAFE foreign-exchange registration certificate, an FDI capital account at a designated bank, and a capital verification process before full deployment. RMB cannot be freely converted into offshore CNH through the WFOE's treasury; every purchase or settlement of foreign exchange must occur at a licensed domestic bank at the daily PBOC central parity with reasonable commercial documentation. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong parent is treated as a current-account dividend remittance: the WFOE must first close its annual statutory audit, pay enterprise income tax and relevant dividend withholding tax, submit the tax payment certificate, and then present board resolutions and financial statements to the bank. The presence of a Hong Kong shareholder does not exempt the transactions from SAFE registration. Because the WFOE exports software-as-a-service-like anti-cheat middleware, service revenue is current-account and can be received onshore if the underlying contract, invoices, and proof of service delivery are verifiable.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China has no mandatory foreign-exchange surrender percentage for export proceeds today. The statutory requirement is repatriation rather than surrender: export receipts must be collected onshore in a current-account foreign-exchange settlement account and registered with SAFE's monitoring system using the underlying sale/supply documentation. For cross-border service exports by the WFOE, the general bank verification expectation is that service fees are collected within 180 days from invoice date; if not, the WFOE must file a written explanation and supporting evidence with SAFE. Once received, the WFOE may retain 100% of the foreign currency in the settlement account or convert it into RMB at the prevailing market rate. No minimum conversion ratio is imposed.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) have primary authority over the mainland-operating WFOE. The Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) supervises the Hong Kong parent entity only as a separate offshore jurisdiction.

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% for Chinese resident enterprises under Article 4 of the PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law; non-resident enterprises without a Chinese permanent establishment or with passive income not effectively connected to a Chinese PE are subject to a 20% statutory rate reduced to 10% by State Council implementation rules.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% on gross dividends paid by the Chinese WFOE to a non-resident Hong Kong shareholder, pursuant to PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 3 and Article 37 as implemented by the State Council at a 10% effective rate.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (contemporaneous TP documentation) is mandatory if annual related-party tangible asset transactions equal or exceed RMB 200 million, annual related-party intangible asset transactions equal or exceed RMB 100 million, annual related-party service transactions equal or exceed RMB 100 million, or other related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 40 million; Master File is mandatory if aggregate annual related-party transaction value equals or exceeds RMB 1 billion; thresholds are set by SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42.

Economic substance scope: China does not apply UAE-style Economic Substance Regulations, but the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 imposes a strict beneficial ownership and economic substance test for treaty relief; the WFOE must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities, including kernel engineering, anti-cheat algorithm development, data security, gaming client integration and technical contract management, are actually performed in China, while the Hong Kong entity must perform substantive activities in Hong Kong or it will be treated as a conduit that cannot access treaty benefits.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion through Fraudulent E-Filing and False Expense Invoices

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 imposes a monetary penalty of 50% to 500% of the unpaid tax for tax evasion; PRC Criminal Law Article 201 provides criminal liability for fraudulent tax declarations where the amount of tax evaded exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of taxable income, with imprisonment of up to seven years and an additional fine for false invoicing.

Director personal liability: Civil liability extends to directors for repayment of the evaded tax, interest and surcharges; courts may pierce the corporate veil where the WFOE is operated as an instrument of the ultimate shareholder, exposing directors' personal assets; the responsible director will be blacklisted under China's joint punishment mechanism for tax integrity, which imposes travel bans, high-consumption restrictions, exit control at the border and disqualification from holding senior management positions in any enterprise.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation on Software Royalties, Technical Services and Intercompany Licences

Statutory sanction: PRC Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Implementation Regulation Articles 110 to 114 authorise the State Taxation Administration to re-determine taxable income by applying the arm's length standard; SAT Public Notice 2016 No. 42 imposes contemporaneous documentation penalties and the unpaid tax may be subject to a 50% to 500% evasion penalty, daily interest at the Chinese benchmark rate plus surcharge, and an additional 100% penalty for failure to file the TP report; deliberate concealment of TP facts constitutes tax evasion under Article 63.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the related-party contracts are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax deficiency when the subsidiary is found to lack independent commerciality; personal bank accounts may be frozen and assets attached; offending directors face exit bans and international travel restrictions through the tax blacklist; where the artificial pricing was part of a conspiracy to evade income tax, criminal prosecution under Article 201 can result in imprisonment of up to seven years for the director or de facto manager.
Shell Company and Conduit Arrangement Abusing the 5% Treaty Dividend Withholding Tax

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 denies treaty benefits where the recipient is not the beneficial owner because the entity has no substance, no power to use the dividends, or is the recipient only for tax avoidance purposes; EIT Law Article 47 and Article 120 of the Implementation Regulations permit the tax authority to re-characterise the arrangement as abusive, impose the full 10% withholding tax retroactively, assess interest and add penalties of 50% to 500% of the underwithheld amount; if the conduit was intentionally created to defraud the Chinese revenue, criminal tax evasion sanctions under Article 201 may also apply.

Director personal liability: Courts and the State Taxation Administration can pierce the corporate veil of both the Hong Kong shell and the Chinese WFOE, making the ultimate controlling director personally liable for the unpaid withholding tax and statutory surcharges; directors may be subjected to passport denials and blacklisting preventing overseas travel; criminal prosecution can lead to imprisonment of up to seven years for the individuals who executed the sham dividend stripping and treaty shopping arrangement, in addition to civil restitution of all unpaid taxes and interest.
