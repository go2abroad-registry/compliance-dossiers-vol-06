Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), accessible through the Electronic Tax Bureau portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn) and the local tax service desks; for the Hong Kong SAR investor layer, the Inland Revenue Department of the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region (IRD) applies for Profits Tax matters.

        
E-government filing portal
For the China WFOE, the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the business license issued by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number and VAT registration number. The procedure is: (1) complete SAMR company incorporation and obtain the business license; (2) rely on the statutory data sharing between SAMR and the STA to generate initial tax registration status, then activate the Electronic Tax Bureau account using legal representative real-name authentication and the USCC; (3) confirm VAT taxpayer status as general or small-scale taxpayer and activate the STA-certified invoicing environment using a tax control device or digital tax identity certificate; (4) complete social insurance, statistics and foreign exchange registration as applicable; and (5) maintain bank account and corporate records for tax filing. A foreign non-resident entity without an establishment in China that earns China-source income is registered through the payer's non-resident enterprise withholding tax registration at the competent tax authority and does not require a separate VAT ID. If the foreign entity forms a branch or permanent establishment in China, it must apply for tax registration at the local tax authority within 30 calendar days of establishment using certified formation documents and the local business license.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. The PRC tax system has no check-the-box entity classification election and no hybrid mismatch election. The only relevant status election is the VAT registration election between small-scale VAT taxpayer and general VAT taxpayer under the Interim Regulations of the People's Republic of China on Value-Added Tax, subject to the RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales threshold.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
All WFOEs must complete VAT/tax registration when the SAMR business license is issued; no monetary exemption threshold applies. Mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration arises once annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. Businesses below that threshold may elect small-scale taxpayer status, which carries a standard 3% levy rate (temporary 1% preferential rate where in force) and eligibility for the monthly RMB 100,000 / quarterly RMB 300,000 small-scale taxpayer exemption.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported from China under customs supervision qualify for 0% VAT, with input VAT refund, where the exporter holds the customs export declaration, transport documents, commercial invoices and evidence of foreign exchange receipt. Qualifying cross-border services, including software services, information system services, research and development services and business process management services supplied by the WFOE to overseas recipients, are zero-rated if the service is provided to an overseas entity, is wholly consumed outside China, and the exporter maintains the service contract, overseas recipient identity, delivery and acceptance records, and foreign currency remittance evidence. Software/IT services that do not meet the offshore consumption test are taxable at the standard service VAT rate of 6% and do not support zero-rating or input VAT refund.

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV electronic invoice system. VAT special invoices and fully digitalized e-invoices must be issued through STA-certified e-invoicing platforms using a tax control device or the taxpayer's digital tax identity. Each invoice carries a tax-authority-assigned invoice code, serial number, digital signature and anti-counterfeit QR code. Invoice data is transmitted to the STA in near real-time for validation before counterparties may claim input VAT credit or before exporters may apply for zero-rating refund. Exporters should integrate accounting/ERP systems with the STA electronic invoice service APIs for invoice metadata submission, status retrieval and red/blue invoice processing; no separate paper fiscal stamp is required.

      
The STA and customs may jointly audit export VAT refunds and zero-rating claims. If the substantiation bundle is incomplete, inaccurate or fraudulent, the refund is clawed back, late payment surcharge accrues at 0.05% per day from the refund payment date, and administrative penalties under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection range from 50% to 500% of the tax involved. False export refund claims may constitute export tax refund fraud under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, exposing responsible individuals to fines and imprisonment. Retrospective assessment and recovery follow the statutory limitation periods, and tax evasion or fraudulent refund claims are not time-barred under PRC law.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
The PRC maintains a fundamentally asymmetric convertibility regime. RMB is fully convertible on the current account for bonafide trade in goods and services, so the WFOE may receive and pay software-development service fees, license fees, and operating expenses through designated banks without case-by-case SAFE approval, provided that authenticity documents are maintained. The capital account, however, remains managed: paid-in capital, shareholder loans, cross-border guarantees, equity transfers, and liquidation proceeds require SAFE registration, bank verification, and purpose-code controls. The WFOE must first complete foreign-investment information reporting and obtain an FDI registration certificate; the onshore bank then verifies each inbound capital remittance and tracks the subsequent RMB settlement against permissible business-use documentation. Profit repatriation to the Hong Kong shareholder is treated as a current-account dividend remittance only after statutory audit, finalised corporate income tax settlement, and a legally effective board resolution; the bank will check the tax payment certificate, audited financial statements, and SAFE remittance purpose code. Foreign-currency capital held in the FDI capital account cannot be deployed into equity investment, entrusted loans, real estate, or securities unless expressly permitted, and the actual-use settlement principle applies. Therefore, the WFOE should treat RMB as fully convertible for operational trade flows and as restricted-but-administrable for capital-driven flows.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Since the 2012 goods-trade FX reform, China does not impose a universal mandatory surrender ratio. An A-class WFOE may retain export receipts in a designated current-account FX settlement account up to the SAFE-approved maximum balance; any excess above the ceiling must be converted to RMB through the bank. Export proceeds must still be repatriated through the onshore banking system and reported on the SAFE trade monitoring platform. For open-account service export receivables, the standard verification window is generally aligned with the invoice or contract maturity and the bank's monitoring period; SAFE expects completion of export payment verification no later than 90 days from the export date for ordinary transactions. The effective mandatory conversion ratio is therefore 0% for balances retained within the approved cap and 100% for balances exceeding the cap. Late collection obliges the WFOE to submit a written explanation, supporting contract, and proof of collection efforts; repeated breaches can downgrade the company from A-class to B or C class and trigger enhanced monitoring.

      

      
Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), acting under the authority of the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The Hong Kong parent must remit paid-in capital exclusively by direct SWIFT MT103 from its corporate bank account to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI foreign-exchange capital account in China. The wire must reference the full WFOE legal name, the FDI registration certificate number, and the SAFE payment purpose code for foreign-invested enterprise capital contribution. The WFOE must first complete the foreign-investment information reporting required under the PRC Foreign Investment Law and obtain the SAFE FDI registration file; the domestic bank will then pre-screen the remittance against the capital contribution schedule. Upon receipt, the bank credits the foreign-currency capital account and, when the WFOE applies to convert into RMB for operations, verifies actual-use documents such as payroll, rent, procurement contracts, and invoices. A Chinese certified public accountant must issue the statutory capital-verification report within the agreed investment completion window, which is then uploaded to the SAFE Foreign Investment system. The WFOE should not aggregate capital injection with other receipts; mixing trade proceeds and capital flows will cause hard failure in the bank's post-transaction monitoring.Outbound payments for operating expenses, vendor fees, and intercompany service charges should be initiated as SWIFT MT103 wire transfers with complete 50A and 59A beneficiary fields and SAFE purpose codes. For physical hardware integrated into an ODC engagement, settle through a commercial letter of credit issued by the bank of the Hong Kong parent, with documents comprising commercial invoice, packing list, bill of lading or airway bill, inspection certificates, and insurance policy; documentary collections with documents against payment or documents against acceptance may be used for low-risk repetitive trade with established counterparties. For RMB-denominated flows, route through the Cross-Border Interbank Payment System (CIPS) to reduce correspondent charges and speed settlement. All outbound disbursements must be supported by the underlying contract, invoice, tax invoice where applicable, and a board-approved treasury payment policy.Deliverable RMB foreign-exchange forward contracts executed with an onshore licensed bank, strictly supported by a current-account trade invoice or contract or a registered capital-account transaction, with a maturity matching the projected settlement date and no net open speculative position.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permissible onshore for the PRC WFOE; they may be executed only by the offshore Hong Kong parent with an offshore bank to hedge the RMB value of anticipated dividends or capital repatriation, and must never be booked or settled inside China.FX swaps and cross-currency swaps are permissible for managing currency mismatches in refinancing or intercompany funding flows, provided the underlying debt or trade exposure is documented, the swap meets the bank's real-demand underwriting test, and the WFOE maintains full SAFE reporting.
      

      
E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Low to essentially non-existent as a primary treasury rail for a PRC WFOE. Domestic RMB current accounts, FDI capital accounts, payroll, tax remittance, and SAFE filings must all be executed through licensed onshore banks. Foreign fintech EMIs such as Wise Business or Payoneer are lawfully unable to maintain onshore RMB accounts, obtain PBOC bank codes, or perform SAFE capital-account verification for a WFOE. The Hong Kong parent may use EMIs for receivable collection or vendor payments outside China, but any attempt to route WFOE capital injection, export revenue, or dividend remittance through an offshore EMI will be rejected by Chinese bank compliance and may expose the entity to administrative penalties for illegal arbitrage. Therefore, assign EMIs a maximum auxiliary role for offshore non-China-side invoicing, never as a settlement rail for WFOE liquidity or FX.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Master File must be assembled when total related-party transactions reach RMB 1,000,000,000 or more in a fiscal year. Local File preparation is mandatory at lower thresholds: tangible-asset related-party transactions of RMB 200,000,000 or more, intangible-asset transactions of RMB 100,000,000 or more, financial-asset transactions of RMB 100,000,000 or more, or any other related-party transactions (including service fees from software development) of RMB 40,000,000 or more. In this ODC structure, the outsourcing service fees paid to Hong Kong will almost always trigger Local File requirements.

      
Economic substance scope: The Chinese WFOE must maintain actual operational substance: qualified software engineers, premises, project management, internal IT infrastructure and profit-and-loss risk. China's GAAR under CIT Law Article 47 and DTA anti-abuse rules require the Hong Kong parent to perform genuine core income-generating activities (CIGA) in Hong Kong—such as strategic decision-making, IP ownership control and risk assumption—before treaty relief can be claimed. An offshore development centre is not a tax shelter; it is taxable in China, and the Hong Kong holding entity cannot be a pure shell.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Tax Evasion through Fabricated Intercompany Fees
        
Statutory sanction: Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 63: the underpaid CIT is recovered with 0.05% daily interest and a fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded tax. PRC Criminal Law Article 201: if the amount of evasion exceeds the criminal threshold, the responsible entity and its management are subject to up to seven years' fixed-term imprisonment and a fine.

        
Director personal liability: Under Company Law Article 20(3), courts will pierce the corporate veil when the shareholder abuses limited liability to evade tax, allowing the tax authority to attach directors' personal assets and order civil restitution. Directors and the financial officer face travel bans and blacklisting as Dishonest Judgment Debtors until restitution is made; a criminal conviction for tax evasion carries actual imprisonment.

      

      
      

        Abusive Treaty Shell / Lack of Economic Substance in Hong Kong
        
Statutory sanction: China CIT Law Article 47 and the DTA's limitation-of-benefits/principal-purpose rules authorise the tax bureau to deny treaty benefits, reassess the unpaid 10% WHT (or 10% statutory rate on interest/royalties) and impose late-payment surcharge and penalties. False beneficial ownership declarations may also constitute tax fraud under Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 63.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who caused the shell entity to hold the WFOE's equity are personally exposed to piercing because the corporate form is being used to avoid tax obligations; they may be ordered to restore the underpaid tax from their personal assets. Directors who sign false Tax Residency Certificates or fake beneficial ownership declarations commit criminal forgery under Criminal Law Article 280, punishable by up to ten years' imprisonment, and will be subject to asset seizure, travel bans and entry/exit blacklisting.

      

      
      

        AML and Money Laundering through Offshore Payment Routes
        
Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with forfeiture of proceeds and a fine; serious cases attract imprisonment of five to ten years. Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, the offending institution or its responsible persons may be fined up to RMB 5,000,000, and the underlying tax evasion is separately pursued under Law on Administration of Tax Collection Article 63 with 50%-500% penalties.

        
Director personal liability: Veil-piercing will reach the personal assets of directors who knowingly structure round-trip payments to evade withholding and AML controls; directors may be ordered to make civil restitution to the State, face criminal prosecution as principals or accomplices in money laundering, and serve imprisonment terms applicable to Article 191. The court can simultaneously blacklist the directors and impose travel bans preventing departure from China until all tax, fines and proceeds are repatriated.
