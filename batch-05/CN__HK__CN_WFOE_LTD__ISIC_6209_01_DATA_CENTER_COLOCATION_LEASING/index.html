Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up share capital. Under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a private company limited by shares may be incorporated with nominal capital selected by the subscribers, provided at least one share is issued. There is no blocked escrow account requirement for share capital in Hong Kong; the initial capital position and subscriber details are declared in Form NNC1 and the statement of compliance.

Funding & unlock path: Before commissioning, the Fire Services Department must issue a Certificate of Fire Service Installations and Equipment confirming that automatic fire detection, alarm, emergency lighting, signage, and fire suppression comply with the Code of Practice for Minimum Fire Service Installations and Equipment. An Authorised Person must certify structural floor load bearing capacity for raised floor rack systems. The electrical installation must be certified under the Electricity Ordinance (Cap. 406). The Labour Department requires a safety policy, risk assessment, confined-space entry protocols, high-voltage electrical work procedures, and registered safety officers where the workforce threshold is exceeded.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online portal: Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) at https://tpass.chinatax.gov.cn/ (official e-tax interface for tax registration, filing, and invoice issuance)

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation of the WFOE as a Chinese limited liability company, the company must complete industrial and commercial registration at the local Administration for Market Regulation and obtain a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC). Within 30 days after business registration, the WFOE must file for tax registration with the competent local branch of the State Taxation Administration (STA), using the USCC as the taxpayer identification number (TIN) for all tax purposes. For foreign non-resident entities (e.g., the Hong Kong shareholder) that derive Chinese-source income without a permanent establishment, a separate non-resident taxpayer identification number must be obtained via the withholding agent or directly through the e-Tax portal, accompanied by a Certificate of Tax Residence issued by the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not provide a check-the-box election for entity classification; the WFOE is statutorily treated as a Chinese resident enterprise. For VAT purposes, the WFOE may elect to register as a general taxpayer once annual turnover exceeds the threshold, but this is not an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

9%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 (five million yuan) in annual taxable sales turnover; exceeding this threshold compels VAT general taxpayer registration. Below this threshold, simplified 3% assessment may apply, with voluntary registration permitted.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under Finance and Taxation Circular [2016] No. 36, Annex 4, zero-rating (0% VAT) applies to cross-border services supplied by Chinese residents to overseas recipients where the services are wholly consumed outside China. Data center colocation and hosted IT infrastructure services fall within the category of information systems services. The WFOE must evidence that: (i) the recipient is an overseas entity; (ii) the services are rendered in relation to infrastructure located inside China but the beneficial use and consumption occur outside China; and (iii) no goods or services are sold to domestic Chinese consumers. Alternatively, VAT exemption (not zero-rating) may apply to certain offshore outsourcing services.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry) – only if any hardware or equipment physically crosses the border
  • Bill of Lading or Airway Bill (for physical exports)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank SWIFT credit advice evidencing receipt of foreign currency
  • Signed cross-border service contract with the overseas client
  • Certificate of Residency of the overseas client issued by its local tax authority
  • If applicable, the STA export tax refund/zero-rating declaration form with transaction-level supporting schedules

Under the STA's Golden Tax Phase IV system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) program, all VAT invoices must be issued through the national integrated e-invoice platform using real-time API transmission. Each invoice contains a dynamically generated anti-fraud QR code and tax control watermark. The platform performs real-time clearing, and any missing or mismatched invoice data triggers automatic tax audit alerts. Fapiao (official VAT invoices) must be issued for every taxable transaction.

False or unsubstantiated zero-rating claims are penalized under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection and the Criminal Law. The STA may claw back all previously refunded/zero-rated input VAT within 10 years, impose a penalty of 50%–500% of the evaded tax, and levy daily interest of 0.05% on underpaid tax. Criminal liability for fraudulently obtaining export tax refunds can result in imprisonment and fines. In practice, any missing FIRC or customs document in the audit file triggers a full red-flag review and suspension of the zero-rating treatment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed-float foreign exchange regime under which the RMB is convertible on current-account transactions but remains strictly controlled on capital-account transactions. SAFE is the central administrative authority for cross-border receipts and payments, while the PBOC determines monetary policy and exchange-rate management. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) may perform routine current-account settlements, including trade in services such as colocation leasing, management consulting, and cloud infrastructure charges, through a licensed domestic bank with a valid FX purpose code and underlying contract. Capital-account transactions, including inbound equity injections, external debt, dividend remittances, and equity transfers, require separate SAFE registration, a dedicated capital escrow account, and CPA verification of the paid-in registered capital. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is legally permitted only after corporate income tax, book closure, annual audit, and submission of distributable-reserve documentation. The bank must confirm the remittance does not exceed the WFOE's distributable profits. Failure to maintain the capital account's intended use can trigger administrative penalties, delays in fund repatriation, and retrospective verification by SAFE. Overall, convertibility is achieved by compliance architecture rather than by free-market convertibility.

Export proceeds surrender rules

All current-account export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated into the PRC through a SAFE-designated domestic bank and reported in the SAFE trade monitoring system. The statutory settlement reporting window is normally within 180 days of the customs or service-trade declaration date, with extensions available for contractual payment terms. There is currently no universal mandatory conversion ratio; exporters may retain foreign currency in an onshore current-account FX ledger or convert into RMB at the market rate. For data-center colocation and leasing services, recurring foreign-currency income from overseas tenants must be credited to the WFOE's onshore operating account and matched to the applicable service-trade purpose code on the payment confirmation.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Rating: Not viable as a primary or statutory banking rail for the PRC WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise, Airwallex, Revolut, and similar platforms are not licensed deposit-taking institutions in Mainland China and cannot open the SAFE-registered capital escrow accounts required for inbound FDI from Hong Kong. They also cannot access China's domestic payment and clearing infrastructure, issue qualified tax/customs reconciliation documentation, or perform CPA capital verification. Such fintech platforms may be useful only as supplementary collection tools in non-PRC jurisdictions, but they are legally barred from servicing the WFOE's local treasury mandates and must not be used for statutory capital, tax, or SAFE-controlled current-account settlements.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound equity funds from Hong Kong must be transmitted via SWIFT MT103 from the HK parent's own bank account directly to the WFOE's SAFE-registered Foreign Exchange Capital Account at the licensed Chinese bank. The remittance must carry the FDI registration cell number, exact names of both parties, the stated purpose of registered capital contribution, and the applicable SAFE purpose code for inbound direct investment. Upon receipt, the bank posts the funds into the capital escrow ledger; the WFOE appoints a certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in contribution against the business scope and registered capital schedule. Following verification and tax filing, the bank may convert the funds into RMB and transfer them to the general operating account for data-center build-out, colocation capacity leasing, and day-to-day operations. No disbursement, investment, or intercompany clearing is permitted directly from the capital escrow account before verification.Outbound trade and service disbursements should be executed through SWIFT MT103 for direct foreign-currency payments when the underlying contract and invoice amount are below letter-of-credit thresholds. For high-value imported servers, network gear, cooling equipment, and power infrastructure, the WFOE should use an irrevocable commercial letter of credit issued by the PRC bank and advised through the Hong Kong or international correspondent bank. Documentary collections are acceptable for repeat shipments with a consistent documentation set. Every outbound payment must be matched to a valid SAFE purpose code, supported by the original contract, commercial invoice, customs declaration or service-trade filing, tax withholding certificate where applicable, and recipient bank beneficiary-name confirmation. Where the counterparty accepts RMB, CIPS clearing can be used under the same purpose-code and document-retention obligations.Deliverable onshore RMB forwards (USD/CNY, HKD/CNY, EUR/CNY) with maturities aligned to invoiced trade, service, and capital flow schedules, executed through a licensed domestic bank under SAFE real-need verification.Cross-currency swaps matched to the amount and maturity of an approved external loan or long-term capital expenditure plan, limited to the notional of the registered foreign debt or committed cash-flow exposure.FX option structures including collars and zero-cost combinations for committed currency cash flows, provided no speculative leverage is taken and offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not used onshore by the PRC-resident WFOE; any NDF overlay must reside only at the Hong Kong parent level.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million for tangible-property related-party transfers; RMB 100 million for intangible-property and financial-asset transfers; RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (services), per SAT Bulletin [2016] No.42; Master File generally required when MNE group consolidated revenue reaches RMB 2 billion

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must have real staff, premises, decisions, and functions in China; arrangements lacking reasonable commercial purpose are recharacterised under CIT Law Article 47 GAAR. The Hong Kong parent must also satisfy Hong Kong Economic Substance Requirements (ESR) by carrying Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong, maintaining employees and offices there, and not acting as a paper conduit for the Chinese operating company.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer-Pricing Profit Stripping

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 41 and SAT Bulletin No.42 permit tax re-determination plus 5% special interest, late surcharge, and penalty; Tax Collection Administration Law Article 62-63 may impose 50%-5x penalty if deliberate concealment is established

Director personal liability: Directors and officer who approved sham pricing may face joint and several civil liability under PRC Company Law Article 20 (veil piercing); personal assets may be seized; the legal representative is subject to exit bans and tax blacklisting; criminal tax evasion liability under Criminal Law Article 201 can result in imprisonment up to 7 years.
Non-Withholding of Outbound Dividends, Interest or Royalties

Statutory sanction: Tax Collection Administration Law Article 69 imposes a penalty of 50%-3x the under-withheld amount on the WFOE as withholding agent; the tax itself remains collectible from the non-resident, plus daily late surcharges at 0.05%

Director personal liability: Responsible directors are personally liable for unlawful distributions that prejudice creditors or for failing to withhold statutory taxes; civil restitution and asset freezes; travel bans; criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201-203 with imprisonment for the officers who orchestrated the remittance.
Shell Company and Treaty Abuse / Beneficial-Owner Fraud

Statutory sanction: PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47) and the principal-purposes test in the Mainland-HK DTA Protocol deny treaty benefits; full 10% domestic WHT is reimposed, with interest and 50%-5x penalties; Hong Kong IRD may apply IRO Article 61/61A to reconstruct the HK entity's profit and assess Hong Kong profits tax

Director personal liability: Directors/shareholders who design or perpetuate the sham face veil-piercing liability for diverted assets; civil restitution to the Chinese company; judicial travel blacklisting and property preservation; criminal liability for tax evasion or false certification with imprisonment up to 10 years where the defrauded tax amount is extremely large.
