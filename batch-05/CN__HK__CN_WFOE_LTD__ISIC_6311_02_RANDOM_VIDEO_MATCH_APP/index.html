Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None imposed by statute; the registered capital is determined by the shareholders in the Articles of Association and must be reasonably matched to the business scope and operating scale. For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise using a Hong Kong SAR shareholder, the capital is remitted from outside mainland China through the foreign direct investment (FDI) mechanism. There is no mandatory blocked escrow account under PRC company law; however, after the bank opens a foreign currency capital account and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) completes FDI registration, the paid-in capital must enter the PRC through the designated capital account and remain subject to capital account settlement rules before funds may be applied to operating expenses.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and the local tax service bureau with jurisdiction over the registered address of the WFOE; official electronic portal: The STA website at www.chinatax.gov.cn and the Electronic Tax Administration platform at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, supplemented by provincial e-tax bureaus and the China International Trade Single Window for customs-related tax data.

E-government filing portal

For the Chinese WFOE: Step 1 - Obtain an 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation during company establishment; the USCC is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN). Step 2 - Complete tax registration at the competent local STA tax service bureau within 30 days after business license issuance as required by the Law of the PRC on Tax Collection and Administration; in practice, data is shared from the enterprise registration system, but the WFOE must verify its taxpayer registration, select VAT taxpayer type, and activate the e-tax account. Step 3 - Apply for general VAT taxpayer status if annual taxable turnover is expected to exceed RMB 5,000,000, or elect small-scale taxpayer treatment if below the threshold. Step 4 - Register as a withholding tax agent for any non-resident shareholder to whom China-source dividends or royalties will be paid, and file withholding declarations through the e-taxation platform. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a Chinese establishment: complete non-resident enterprise tax registration at the tax bureau of the place where the China-source income arises or where the withholding agent is located; submit the foreign certificate of incorporation, overseas tax residence certificate, underlying contract, authorized representative passport, and notarized or apostilled translations as required; the tax authority will assign a TIN or use the withholding agent's TIN for reporting obligations.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation - The Chinese WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise taxed at entity level on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law at the standard 25% rate; no pass-through treatment is available. The Hong Kong parent is a separate non-resident enterprise and is taxable only on China-source income, subject to withholding taxes on dividends, interest, royalties, and service fees.

Available election forms

None - Chinese income tax law does not provide a check-the-box election, hybrid mismatch election, or pass-through/flow-through election. The WFOE may voluntarily apply for VAT general taxpayer status before the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, and may choose simplified VAT accounting only if classified as a small-scale taxpayer; neither choice changes the default entity-level corporate income tax classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 annual VAT taxable turnover is the mandatory threshold for registration as a VAT general taxpayer; taxpayers with annual service or sales turnover below RMB 5,000,000 are small-scale taxpayers and may be subject to the simplified levy rate. All entities engaged in taxable activities are required to register for VAT upon commencement of business; a non-resident supplier with no Chinese establishment but deriving China-source taxable services must register or be registered through a withholding agent in China regardless of the turnover threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under the Interim Regulations on VAT and Ministry of Finance/State Taxation Administration Circular Cai Shui [2016] No. 36, zero-rating is available for exported goods that are physically exported from China and for qualifying cross-border services supplied to non-resident customers and completely consumed outside China, including eligible software, data processing, internet information services, and telecommunication services where the service recipient is outside China and no Chinese domestic consumption occurs. The WFOE must declare the zero rate on its VAT return, retain a valid export contract, evidence receipt of payment in foreign currency, and for goods, present a verified Customs Export Declaration. Zero-rating is not automatic and requires substantive documentation demonstrating that the goods left the customs territory or that the service is consumed entirely outside China.

  • Customs Export Declaration Form issued by the General Administration of Customs of China via the China International Trade Single Window
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent transport document, such as airway bill, sea waybill, or road/rail consignment note
  • Commercial invoice and packing list evidencing exported goods or cross-border service supply
  • Export contract or cross-border service agreement with the non-resident counterparty
  • Bank-issued export proceeds receipt, Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC), or foreign exchange collection confirmation evidencing repatriation of the export proceeds
  • Export sales invoice issued under the VAT invoice management system, annotated with the zero-rated/vat-exempt basis if required

China operates the Golden Tax System Phase IV and the national fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) platform. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices using STA-authorised electronic invoicing systems, transmit invoice data in real time, and comply with anti-fraud verification through tax-control codes, invoice pooling, and blockchain-backed data comparison. There is no independent third-party fiscal stamping mandate; instead, authorized offline invoices carry the national anti-counterfeit tax-control code and all invoice data is cleared through the STA cloud platform. Export-related invoice data is cross-matched with customs declarations and foreign exchange receipts through the China International Trade Single Window.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange jointly audit export zero-rating claims through automated matching of customs declarations, foreign exchange receipt data, and VAT invoice data. If the claimed zero-rated export is unsubstantiated or fraudulent, the VAT refund is clawed back, a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day is imposed, and an administrative penalty of 50% to five times the evaded tax may be levied under Article 63 of the Law of the PRC on Tax Collection and Administration. Serious cases constitute the crime of fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the PRC, punishable by heavy fines and imprisonment; the taxpayer may also be blacklisted under the taxation credit system and lose export rebate eligibility for a statutory period.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions under the receipt and payment verification regime, but convertibility on the capital account remains regulated and restricted in a negative-list manner. Current-account items such as service-export revenue, licensing fees, and operational expense remittances can be converted and paid abroad through a designated domestic bank upon presentation of authentic commercial documents; banks perform a three-way authenticity verification against contracts, invoices, and tax payment proofs. Capital-account items, including equity subscriptions, shareholder loans, and foreign direct investment round-tripping, require SAFE registration or approval before funds can be remitted. Export proceeds are subject to a repatriation obligation and must be brought into a domestic foreign-exchange account within the statutory window; after repatriation, the enterprise is free to settle into RMB or retain the foreign currency within permitted limits. Profit remittances fall under current-account administration: once audited annual accounts show distributable profits, all applicable PRC taxes are settled, and the foreign exchange purchase and remittance documents are declared, the bank will process the SWIFT payment without additional SAFE approval. The main hurdles are authentic documentation, avoidance of duplicate VAT and income-tax filings, and ensuring beneficial ownership remains transparent enough to clear enhanced due diligence.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under PBC and SAFE current-account administration, export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to an approved domestic foreign-exchange account within 30 calendar days of receipt. There is no mandatory percentage conversion ratio; the WFOE may retain eligible foreign currency up to its permitted account limit or settle it into RMB through a designated bank. All retention, conversion, and settlement must be supported by export customs declarations, sales contracts, commercial invoices, and SAFE digital monitoring declarations, and any offshore retention requires explicit written approval from SAFE.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Very Low and legally barred as the primary treasury counterparty for the PRC WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed banks in China and cannot open domestic corporate settlement accounts, receive FDI equity capital, perform SAFE registration verification, or execute statutory profit remittance clearance. They may only serve as third-party payment collection rails for app-store receipts, such as Apple IAP or Paymentwall, but those proceeds remain foreign-currency receipts that must be declared and ultimately integrated into the onshore PBC-licensed commercial banking system before permissible conversion or disbursement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must route the equity injection via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated FDI foreign-exchange capital account at a PBC-licensed domestic bank, referencing the SAFE foreign-exchange registration certificate and the applicable SAFE purpose code for foreign direct investment share subscription. The receiving bank must verify that the remitter name, remittance currency, purpose declaration, and amount satisfy the SAFE registration; it must then lodge the funds in the capital escrow account and, once compliant, settle into RMB under SAFE's capital-account settlement rules. A registered Chinese CPA must issue the capital verification report, and the WFOE must update its business licence and public registry filings. The capital account may not be used for prohibited domestic equity investments, entrusted loans, wealth-management purchases, or unauthorized cross-border guarantees without a discrete SAFE approval.Outbound disbursements for bandwidth, cloud hosting, licensing fees, and trade services should use SWIFT MT103 for direct service payments, while goods-based settlements may use Commercial Letters of Credit or Documentary Collections. Every outbound instruction requires a genuine underlying contract, commercial invoice, tax payment proof, cross-border payment declaration, and SAFE payment reservation number. For RMB-accepting counterparties, CIPS is the preferred clearing rail to reduce USD concentration and FX conversion friction, but the documentary verification remains identical. All clearing rails are subject to the WFOE's internal-payment policy, maker-checker controls, and post-transaction AML monitoring.Deliverable RMB/USD forward foreign exchange contracts, where maturity and amount match verified current-account or capital-account exposure, are permissible only through a PBC-licensed domestic bank.Cross-currency swaps are permissible to hedge foreign-currency debt service, trade disbursements, or cross-border operational exposure, but cannot be used as standalone speculative positions.RMB foreign-exchange options are permissible when the option is linked to a real, documented commercial cash flow.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are prohibited onshore for the PRC WFOE; the offshore Hong Kong holding entity may access offshore RMB NDF markets under HKMA rules only for hedging genuine exposure, and must demonstrate that the underlying risk is not purely speculative.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Very Low and legally barred as the primary treasury counterparty for the PRC WFOE. Offshore digital EMIs are not licensed banks in China and cannot open domestic corporate settlement accounts, receive FDI equity capital, perform SAFE registration verification, or execute statutory profit remittance clearance. They may only serve as third-party payment collection rails for app-store receipts, such as Apple IAP or Paymentwall, but those proceeds remain foreign-currency receipts that must be declared and ultimately integrated into the onshore PBC-licensed commercial banking system before permissible conversion or disbursement.
