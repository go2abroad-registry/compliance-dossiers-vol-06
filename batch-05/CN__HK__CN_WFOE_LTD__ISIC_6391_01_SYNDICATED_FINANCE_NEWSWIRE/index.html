Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, administering national tax registration through the China Electronic Tax Administration portal (e-Tax), with corresponding local tax bureau registrations; for Hong Kong SAR, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) and its electronic tax filing system (eTax) may apply to any Hong Kong-resident or Hong Kong-source profit exposure.

E-government filing portal

A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) first obtains its business license with the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The USCC serves as the tax identification number (TIN) for enterprise income tax, VAT, and other national taxes. Tax registration must be completed online through the e-Tax portal promptly after license issuance; the WFOE then files an enterprise income tax registration, VAT taxpayer qualification (small-scale or general taxpayer), and electronic invoice issuance registration. For foreign non-resident enterprises without a Chinese establishment but engaged in taxable activities in China, a non-resident tax registration can be applied through the STA e-Tax portal or the competent local tax bureau; the authority issues a non-resident taxpayer identification number and may require a withholding agent if a Chinese payer exists. For a Hong Kong-incorporated entity, the Inland Revenue Department requires an IRD tax file number and profits tax registration once a business is carried on in Hong Kong or Hong Kong-source profits arise.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The renminbi is convertible on the current account for bona fide payments such as subscription fees, royalties, dividends from audited profits, wages, rent, and ordinary trade in services, provided the underlying contract and documentation are filed with the bank. Capital account flows are regulated and require SAFE registration and a designated onshore bank as the settlement agent. A mainland China WFOE owned by a Hong Kong SAR parent may receive foreign direct investment (FDI) as paid-in equity through the SAFE/FDI registration system; the funds are credited to a supervised capital account with a licensed domestic bank. Conversion of FX capital into RMB follows the voluntary settlement with payment verification regime: the bank credits the FX and converts to a RMB pending-payment account from which disbursements are allowed only after review of the underlying supporting documents. Current account export proceeds, including financial news wire API subscription fees from overseas customers, may be retained in foreign exchange or settled into RMB, but they must be declared under the correct cross-border payment/receipt purpose code and reconciled to service contracts and API usage records. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong parent is permitted after payment of corporate income tax and any withholding tax; the outward dividend remittance requires a board resolution, audited financial statements, tax clearance certificate, and a bank AML/sanctions review. Macroprudential limits apply to short-term foreign debts and cross-border lending; the newswire's cross-border data transfers must further comply with the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law and PIPL data export security assessment of PRC, which may affect the cash conversion of data-related proceeds. Overall, the RMB is not fully free internationally; the WFOE must operate through banking channels authorized by SAFE and cannot treat offshore CNH markets as available onshore settlement rails.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio for current account export proceeds in the PRC after the SAFE reforms; a WFOE may retain 100% of its eligible foreign currency proceeds in a current account foreign exchange account or elect to settle into RMB. The mandatory obligation is repatriation and truthful declaration: goods and service trade proceeds must be collected through licensed banks and matched to the underlying contract, customs/e-port data (for goods) or service invoice/contract performance period (for services) within the applicable trade-monitoring window. Goods exports generally require collection within the SAFE verification period (normally 90 days from customs declaration, extendable upon application); service export receipts should be reported upon receipt under the relevant transaction purpose code. Failure to repatriate on schedule triggers a bank compliance report, downgrade of the enterprise's trade classification, and possible SAFE penalty.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) exercising delegated foreign exchange administration through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Rating: LOW as a primary account; SUPPLEMENTARY-ONLY as a front-end collection aggregator. Offshore digital EMIs such as Revolut Business, Payoneer, Airwallex and Stripe Treasury are not licensed as deposit-taking institutions under the PRC Banking Law and are not registered with SAFE/NFRA. They cannot lawfully open a mainland RMB/FX current account or a SAFE-registered capital account for a WFOE, cannot clear RMB through CIPS or China Union Pay, and cannot execute the mandatory payment-verification controls around FDI settlement. They may be used by the WFOE only to collect API subscription or card payments from non-China customers in USD, after which the EMI master account sends an MT103 remittance to the WFOE's Bank of China or ICBC local account. Treating an EMI as the principal settlement rail for capital injection, RMB conversion, dividends, or tax payments would violate SAFE regulations and would raise an immediate red flag in the bank's AML underwriting. Any EMI arrangement must be disclosed as a payment collection sub-processor, not as the WFOE's banker.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must first complete the FDI foreign exchange registration through the designated mainland bank in the SAFE system. The parent then remits the equity contribution by SWIFT MT103 in USD or HKD, or through CIPS in RMB, to the WFOE's FDI capital account at Bank of China or ICBC. The remittance instruction must name the beneficiary as CN_WFOE_LTD and state the purpose as equity contribution to a wholly foreign-owned enterprise. The mainland bank assigns the appropriate SAFE cross-border receipt transaction purpose code for FDI capital inflow; the bank does not treat the capital injection as escrowed funds but as regulated registered capital on its capital account ledger. After receipt, the WFOE obtains a capital verification certificate from a licensed PRC CPA if required by the shareholders or bank. Foreign currency capital can be settled into RMB under the voluntary settlement with payment verification system, and the RMB is held in a pending-payment account from which disbursements must be matched to salaries, rent, vendor invoices, and other operating expenses. The initial injection cannot be used for equity investments, on-lending to third parties, speculative securities purchases, or real estate outside permitted business purposes. Any later increase in registered capital requires an amended SAFE registration and fresh board approval from the Hong Kong parent.The WFOE will make most outbound operating payments by SWIFT MT103 for intangible service purchases such as data feeds, software licensing, cloud hosting, content syndication, and subcontractor fees. The bank will require a signed contract, invoice or proforma, evidence of actual service delivery, and a tax withholding certificate where the payment is subject to PRC withholding tax on cross-border services. For physical hardware or equipment purchased in bulk from a Hong Kong supplier, a commercial letter of credit (MT700) can be used; documentary collections are acceptable but generally slower and less suitable for the newswire business model. RMB-denominated cross-border payments should be routed through CIPS directly to the beneficiary's offshore bank, with the beneficiary receiving CNH or CNY depending on the correspondent bank arrangement; the WFOE must avoid using unlicensed internal netting or so-called underground clearing arrangements. They cannot lawfully open a mainland RMB/FX current account or a SAFE-registered capital account for a WFOE, cannot clear RMB through CIPS or China Union Pay, and cannot execute the mandatory payment-verification controls around FDI settlement. They may be used by the WFOE only to collect API subscription or card payments from non-China customers in USD, after which the EMI master account sends an MT103 remittance to the WFOE's Bank of China or ICBC local account. Treating an EMI as the principal settlement rail for capital injection, RMB conversion, dividends, or tax payments would violate SAFE regulations and would raise an immediate red flag in the bank's AML underwriting. Any EMI arrangement must be disclosed as a payment collection sub-processor, not as the WFOE's banker.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File thresholds in RMB: 200 million for tangible property, 100 million for intangible property, 100 million for financial assets, 40 million for other related-party transactions; Master File required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: PRC GAAR (CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulation Article 120) recharacterises tax-driven arrangements lacking commercial substance. Hong Kong treaty benefits require actual economic substance, TRC, and beneficial-owner status under STA Announcement [2018] No. 9; CIGA analysis for the WFOE and Hong Kong parent is mandatory for defending DTA relief and avoiding sham-conduit reclassification.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Fabricated Intercompany Service Fees

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 63: 50%-5x penalty on underpaid tax plus daily 0.05% late surcharge; CIT Law Article 41 allows recharacterisation; Criminal Law Article 201 for evasive amounts above RMB 100,000 and 10% of tax due.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: personal restitution from directors' assets, freezing orders, exit bans and travel blacklisting, and imprisonment of up to 3-7 years for severe tax evasion.
Failure to Withhold Outbound Dividend and Cross-Border Interest/Royalty WHT

Statutory sanction: TAL Article 69: withholding agent liable for unwithheld tax plus penalty of 50%-3x; CIT Law Article 37 imposes under-withholding collection; late payment surcharge accrues until full settlement.

Director personal liability: Directors and financial officers can be personally liable if they approved reckless or deliberate non-withholding; civil asset restitution, travel restriction, blacklisting, and criminal liability for tax evasion are attachable.
Shell Conduit Treaty Abuse and Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: Denial of beneficial owner status under STA Announcement [2018] No. 9; reassessment at 10% with interest; PRC GAAR recharacterisation; TAL Article 63 penalty of 50%-5x; HK IRO Sections 61/61A voidance.

Director personal liability: Corporate veil piercing where sham structure exists: personal and joint liability for restitution of taxes and penalties, freezing of personal assets, travel bans, and imprisonment for fraudulent tax avoidance of up to 10 years.
