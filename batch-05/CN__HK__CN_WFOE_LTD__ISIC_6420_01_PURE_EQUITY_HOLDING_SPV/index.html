Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), China National Unified E-Tax Bureau (electronic portal: https://etax.chinatax.gov.cn). The competent local tax office is the district-level STA service branch for the WFOE's registered address; for Hong Kong parent entities, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) remains separately responsible.

E-government filing portal

For a mainland China WFOE formed by non-resident shareholders: (1) incorporate with SAMR and obtain the business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes; (2) complete tax registration and taxpayer onboarding in the China National Unified E-Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days of the business license date, with SAMR data synchronizing to STA; (3) complete tax type determination covering corporate income tax, VAT, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge, and stamp duty, and choose small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status; (4) register the basic bank account in the E-Tax portal; and (5) apply for VAT invoice issuance qualification and the UKey digital certificate before issuing electronic invoices. A non-resident or HK shareholder generally does not need a separate Chinese TIN absent a permanent establishment or a taxable supply into China.

Tax ID type & application path: Entity_Level_Taxation — For a mainland China WFOE formed by non-resident shareholders: (1) incorporate with SAMR and obtain the business license containing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory taxpayer identification number for PRC tax purposes; (2) complete tax registration and taxpayer onboarding in the China National Unified E-Tax Bureau within 30 calendar days of the business license date, with SAMR data synchronizing to STA; (3) complete tax type determination covering corporate income tax, VAT, urban maintenance and construction tax, education surcharge, and stamp duty, and choose small-scale or general VAT taxpayer status; (4) register the basic bank account in the E-Tax portal; and (5) apply for VAT invoice issuance qualification and the UKey digital certificate before issuing electronic invoices. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC does not have a check-the-box regime or hybrid mismatch election. Hybrid mismatch outcomes are controlled by the Enterprise Income Tax Law's general anti-avoidance rule, special tax adjustments, controlled foreign corporation rules, and BEPS Action 2 implementing guidance issued by MOF and STA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual aggregate taxable turnover; below this amount the WFOE can be registered as a small-scale VAT taxpayer, while the threshold triggers mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration and full input VAT credit entitlement. All established WFOEs must complete tax registration irrespective of turnover.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT export zero-rating is available only if the goods are physically exported from mainland China under a customs export declaration in the WFOE's name, title/risk pass to an overseas buyer, an export contract and export commercial invoice exist, and the related input VAT is substantiated. For cross-border services, qualifying services supplied by the SPV to an overseas recipient (including certain group administration and treasury services to the HK affiliate) can be zero-rated or VAT-exempt under Caishui [2016] No. 36 for services consumed entirely outside China, while passive dividend receipts and pure equity-holding income are outside the scope of VAT and therefore not zero-rated or refundable. Offshore digital services additionally require verifiable evidence that digital service delivery is consumed outside the PRC.

  • Customs export declaration form for goods (export customs bill of entry)
  • Bill of lading, air waybill, or other independent transport document evidencing export movement
  • Export commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bank-issued foreign exchange receipt certificate (inward remittance certificate or equivalent export proceeds record)
  • Export tax rebate declaration record filed through the STA Export Tax Refund Administration System

Mandatory use of the STA fully digitalized electronic invoice (all-digital e-invoice) system through the China National Unified E-Tax Bureau. Taxpayers must open a STA tax digital account and use a UKey/electronic certificate. All invoice data are transmitted to STA in real or near-real time, and each special VAT invoice receives a unique 20-digit invoice code and machine-verified anti-counterfeit seal issued by the STA platform. There is no separate physical fiscal stamp; the central platform rejects non-compliant or unauthorized invoices, and invoice data are pre-filled into VAT returns and cross-matched with customs and bank records.

STA and customs cross-match the export declaration, VAT invoice data, logistics documentation, and foreign exchange repatriation records. If a 0% export rating or refund is claimed but the export is unverified, inflated, fraudulent, or if export proceeds are not repatriated within the statutory monitoring period, the refunded VAT is clawed back with a daily late-payment surcharge; administrative penalties may range from 50% to five times the tax involved, the taxpayer may be stripped of export tax rebate status and downgraded in tax credit rating, and fraudulent refund claims are criminally sanctionable under Article 204 of the PRC Criminal Law, with fines and imprisonment for the WFOE's responsible officers.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese renminbi is a managed floating currency subject to a regulated convertibility regime. The current account is effectively and largely convertible for trade in goods and services, but banks must obtain and retain authentic underlying documents including customs declarations, invoices, shipping documents, and contracts under SAFE's delegated verification framework. The capital account is not freely convertible; a PRC WFOE requires SAFE foreign investment registration, a dedicated capital-project foreign-currency or RMB capital account, and post-transaction matching before any parent-country equity injection, shareholder loan, or remittance can be executed. Profit repatriation to a Hong Kong parent is allowed only if prior-year audited financial statements confirm real distributable profits, tax has been settled and cleared, and the outward remittance aligns with the approved purpose. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds today, but all foreign-exchange receipts from current-account transactions must be repatriated within statutory timelines, can be retained in an onshore FX deposit account, or sold to a designated bank at market rates. A WFOE holding shares in operating subsidiaries is carefully scrutinized because its passive balance-sheet profile can give rise to shell-like indicators, requiring robust proof of economic substance and clean fund flows.

Export proceeds surrender rules

PRC exporters must repatriate export foreign exchange receipts to China within 180 days of the customs export declaration, with extension possible only under SAFE approval. There is no mandatory conversion ratio to RMB; a company may retain 100% of export receipts in a current-account foreign-currency deposit account or voluntarily sell them to a designated bank, provided the trade contract, customs declaration, commercial invoice, and payment receipt are electronically matched in the SAFE trade-monitoring system. All export receipts must be credited through a licensed Chinese bank and cannot remain offshore beyond the prescribed timeline.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Rating: Low and not viable as the WFOE's primary onshore treasury banking layer. Offshore digital EMIs and virtual banks do not hold a mainland banking licence nor a SAFE foreign-exchange business qualification; they cannot open a Chinese domestic account, cannot receive export proceeds as statutory designated banks, and cannot execute FDI capital verification. The PRC National Financial Regulatory Administration and PBOC prohibit non-bank payment institutions from providing capital-account settlement and SAFE-mandated trade verification services for a WFOE. An offshore EMI may only support ancillary cross-border collection or disbursement where a tier-1 onshore bank remains the direct account-holder and regulatory reporter, and the EMI sponsor bank has no independent ability to satisfy PRC AML reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The HK parent must send the equity injection via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-registered foreign-exchange capital account at a tier-1 designated Chinese bank. Field-70 remittance information must include the WFOE's SAFE foreign-investment registration number and the business purpose / purpose code for FDI equity injection, e.g. 'Equity investment into WFOE'. On crediting, the bank performs an automatic cross-check against the SAFE capital-project registration and issues a capital-item income confirmation. A certified public accountant will issue a formal capital verification report after receipt, confirming paid-in capital, and the bank must obtain that report for final registration. If the injection is in Renminbi, the same capital-account notice applies and clearing can be done via CIPS or SWIFT-linked banks for a corresponding RMB message.For supplier and operational payments, the WFOE should use a tier-1 bank SWIFT MT103 for direct wire payments or MT202 for high-value interbank transfers. For documentary trade settlements, a Commercial Letter of Credit (L/C) is preferred where a foreign beneficiary needs a bank undertaking, while Documentary Collections (D/P or D/A) are acceptable for lower-risk transactions with clean transport, invoice, insurance, and contract documents. Cross-border RMB payments can also clear directly through CIPS to overseas participating banks. Every outward payment must carry the correct SAFE/current-account purpose code and be supported by underlying contract, invoice, customs declaration, and logistics evidence to pass AML and trade-authenticity review.Permissible forward contracts: onshore deliverable RMB/HKD and RMB/USD forwards with genuine trade or FDI underlying exposure; speculative and naked contracts are prohibited.Non-deliverable forwards (NDFs): only the HK parent treasury may use offshore CNH NDFs as a macro hedge; the PRC WFOE cannot directly execute an onshore NDF contract.Currency swap boundaries: onshore FX swaps and cross-currency swaps are allowed only to hedge actual capital, interest, and trade cash-flow exposures, subject to SAFE registration and no arbitrage layer.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File must be prepared if annual related-party transactions equal or exceed RMB 200 million for tangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible asset transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related-party transactions (e.g., management fees, interest, services). Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions reach RMB 1 billion or the taxpayer is part of a multinational group satisfying the Master File criteria under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must have real economic substance in China and be tax-resident on effective management. Its dividends from Chinese subsidiaries are exempt only if the holding activity is genuine. The Hong Kong parent must have sufficient substance in Hong Kong (registered office, local bank account, minute books, director meetings, employment or external management, and active exercise of shareholding rights) to qualify as beneficial owner under the Mainland-HK DTA. PRC GAAR and the Principal Purpose Test under the BEPS MLI allow the State Taxation Administration to deny treaty benefits to shell or letter-box companies. The Core Income-Generating Activity (CIGA) of a pure equity holding SPV is the active management and control of the shareholding, receipt of dividends, and maintenance of corporate records in the jurisdiction of residence.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax evasion through sham beneficial ownership and treaty shopping

Statutory sanction: The DTA claim is denied ab initio; the under-withheld 10% dividend WHT becomes payable with daily late-payment surcharge; the taxpayer is assessed under Tax Administration Law Article 63 as tax evasion, leading to a fine of 50%-500% of the underpaid tax; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes monetary fines and imprisonment for up to 7 years for directors or directly responsible persons.

Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20(3), holding directors or shareholders jointly and severally liable for tax debts if the corporate form is abused to evade tax and injure creditors; directors who sign false DTA forms face personal criminal exposure, travel bans under Tax Administration Law Article 44, and inclusion on the STA serious-tax-violation blacklist with restrictions on credit, financing, and exit from China.
Failure to file related-party transaction disclosures and transfer pricing documentation

Statutory sanction: Under Tax Administration Law Article 62, fines of RMB 2,000-10,000 apply for documentation failure; under EIT Law Article 44 and SAT Announcement [2017] No. 6, the tax authority may recompute taxable income using deemed methods and impose special tax adjustment interest; under Article 63, concealed related-party income is tax evasion with a penalty of 50%-500% and criminal liability for material amounts.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve or conceal false TP documentation are treated as directly responsible persons under Criminal Law Article 211 and can be criminally prosecuted even if the company pays the fine; civil liability and asset restitution may be imposed against directors who cause the company to enter sham contracts with the HK parent; exit restrictions and blacklisting apply to legal representatives for unpaid special tax adjustments.
Thin capitalization and disguised dividend repatriation

Statutory sanction: Excess interest is non-deductible under EIT Law Article 46 and reapportioned as equity under the thin-capitalization rules; the disguised payment is retroactively reclassified as a deemed dividend or deemed royalty or interest, triggering 10% (or treaty-denied 10%) withholding tax plus penalty of 50%-500% for under-withholding under Tax Administration Law Articles 69 and 63; daily surcharges run from the original due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorize back-to-back loans or fake service agreements can be held personally liable for unjust enrichment and recovery under Company Law Article 20; if the vehicle is proven to be a facade, courts may pierce the veil and hold the HK shareholder and directors jointly liable; criminal liability for fraud or tax evasion under Criminal Law Articles 201 and 205 can result in imprisonment, alongside travel bans, exit denial, and inclusion on the public blacklist.
