Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China — the national income tax and VAT authority — acting through the local competent in-charge tax authority where the WFOE is domiciled; all registration, filing, invoice clearance and export-refund processes are performed electronically through the official Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax Bureau) taxpayer service platform.

        
E-government filing portal
Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) under the PRC Foreign Investment Law; the Business License is issued bearing a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) which becomes the permanent TIN and VAT identification number; no minimum registered capital is required for this family-office WFOE. Step 2: SAMR automatically shares incorporation data with the STA under the one-stop government integration mechanism, creating the taxpayer master file; the WFOE must complete the Newly-Established Taxpayer onboarding flow in the e-Tax Bureau within 30 days of Business License issuance. Step 3: For the foreign/Hong Kong non-resident investor, upload certified copies of: the Hong Kong Certificate of Incorporation, Hong Kong Business Registration Certificate, board resolution authorising incorporation, the ultimate beneficial owner's passport, the WFOE's articles of association, the registered-office lease or title document, and the PRC bank basic-deposit-account certificate. Step 4: After STA validation, the taxpayer status is activated, electronic VAT invoice quotas are granted, and the WFOE must file its accounting-system registration and confirm its VAT taxpayer type (small-scale vs general VAT taxpayer) and business scope (investment holding and family-office services). Step 5: The USCC is thereafter used for all Enterprise Income Tax, VAT, withholding tax and monthly/quarterly filings; the Business License bearing the USCC doubles as the statutory tax registration certificate, and no separate paper tax registration certificate is issued.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation — The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, taxable on worldwide income at the statutory 25% rate, with no pass-through or check-the-box relief. Passive investment income (dividends, interest and capital gains) is outside the VAT scope; fee-based family-office investment advisory services are chargeable to VAT at 6% (or 0% if qualifying as exported offshore services). Dividends paid by the WFOE to a qualifying Hong Kong corporate shareholder may obtain the 5% preferential withholding tax rate under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement, subject to the 25% direct shareholding and beneficial-ownership condition.

        
Available election forms
None for entity-level classification: PRC law does not provide check-the-box, pass-through, or hybrid-mismatch elections for WFOEs. Available statutory elections: (i) VAT taxpayer-type election — a taxpayer with annual taxable turnover at or below RMB 5,000,000 may elect to become a general VAT taxpayer (voluntary full-taxpayer registration) or remain a small-scale taxpayer; (ii) Small Low-Profit Enterprise CIT election — if the WFOE satisfies the headcount (no more than 300 employees), asset-size and annual-taxable-income (no more than RMB 3,000,000) caps, it may elect the preferential effective 5% CIT rate in lieu of 25%; (iii) cross-border services VAT election — the taxpayer may apply to the STA for zero-rating or exemption of qualifying services supplied to overseas or Hong Kong clients; and (iv) general VAT taxpayer status allows the option to claim input VAT credits on purchases.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime with Renminbi convertibility that is complete for current-account transactions but tightly gatekept for capital-account items. A WFOE controlled through a Hong Kong parent must treat every equity injection, shareholder loan, and investment disbursement as a capital-account movement requiring SAFE registration, a designated FX capital account at a licensed onshore bank, and an underlying transaction dossier. Dividend remittances to the Hong Kong parent are current-account items after tax settlement, but the bank will require an annual audit, a board resolution, and proof that corporate income tax and withholding tax have been paid; any abnormal distribution to a family office vehicle will trigger enhanced due diligence and possible tax review by the local tax bureau. Export proceeds, if any, are subject to the goods-trade foreign-exchange framework: they must be repatriated to China within 90 days of customs export declaration, yet there is no mandatory conversion ratio because SAFE abolished compulsory surrender in 2012; the exporter may retain the FX in a domestic trade income account or convert it into RMB at a licensed bank. The decisive compliance constraint for this SFO structure is therefore demonstrating genuine business purpose for each capital-account flow, especially where funds move between Hong Kong family entities and the mainland WFOE, and ensuring that all FX conversion and profit repatriation follow the exact SAFE filing and bank verification workflow.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
Under SAFE Circular 38 of 2012 and the Goods Trade Foreign Exchange Administration Operational Rules, export foreign-currency receipts must be repatriated to an onshore bank account within 90 days of the date of customs export declaration. There is no statutory mandatory conversion ratio; the exporter may keep 100% of the proceeds in a foreign-currency account or convert the full amount into RMB at the licensed bank's market rate. SAFE requires documentary support including the export contract, customs declaration form, full-value commercial invoice, packing list, transport documents, and bank credit confirmation. If the WFOE elects to convert, the bank processes an immediate spot settlement under the CFETS reference rate spread. Failure to repatriate within 90 days attracts late reconciliation, write-off penalties, or downgrade of trade FX classification.

      

      
Central bank supervision: The State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

    

    

      
Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

      

        
        

          

            Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails
            Fit: High
          

          
Non-viable for the onshore WFOE treasury layer. Offshore digital EMIs, virtual banks, and Hong Kong-licensed fintech platforms have no statutory authority to hold RMB deposits for a PRC WFOE, to open a SAFE-registered capital account, or to execute onshore current and capital account FX settlements. PBOC and SAFE permit only licensed Chinese commercial banks, finance companies, or qualified designated foreign-exchange banks to handle corporate FX and capital account payments. An offshore EMI can be used by the Hong Kong parent for its own liquidity and cross-currency treasury activity, but it cannot replace the Chinese tier-1 bank for receiving inbound FDI, paying dividends onshore, or managing SAFE-relevant trade settlements. The feasibility rating is therefore low for any proposed EMI-led banking structure; the only bankable architecture is a domestic tier-1 commercial bank as the principal account bank and the EMI as a supportive non-regulated treasury tool in Hong Kong.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

      

        The compliant routing for an inbound capital injection from Hong Kong into the mainland WFOE is a SWIFT MT103 or channeled telegraphic transfer from the HK parent's bank to the WFOE's SAFE-registered FDI capital FX account at a Chinese tier-1 bank. The wire instruction must reference the SAFE FDI registration code and the underlying equity investment agreement. Before the transfer, the WFOE must complete company file registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation, open the capital account, and file the FDI investment with the local SAFE branch. After the funds are credited, the bank verifies the purpose code, converts to RMB if required under the capital-FX settlement regime, and the WFOE must engage a PRC certified public accountant to issue a capital verification report. Any use of the injected capital for securities trading, property, or lending must be checked against the WFOE's business scope and the negative list; an SFO WFOE with ISIC 6430 will usually need a specific investment-management business scope and may require additional regulatory approval.For trade-related outflows, the WFOE should use SWIFT MT103 for direct documentary invoice payments, commercial documentary letters of credit issued by the Chinese bank and advised through a Hong Kong issuing bank, and documentary collections for contractually staged settlements. All outbound wires must be supported by the customs declaration, invoice, transport document, inspection certificates, and tax payment confirmations where applicable. For family-office investment disbursements, the settlement rail is not a pure MT103 but rather securities custody and clearing via the custodian bank and China Securities Depository and Clearing Corporation Limited for domestic instruments, while offshore investment remittances must comply with QDLP, QDIE, or other SAFE-approved quota schemes if the WFOE itself is the investing entity. Dividend withholding tax (WHT)
10% statutory WHT on dividends paid by the Chinese WFOE to a non-resident enterprise, reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement when the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company directly holding at least 25% of the WFOE capital and no conduit arrangement exists.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: RMB 100 million in annual related-party transaction value triggers Local File preparation; RMB 10 billion in related-party transaction value triggers Master File preparation; RMB 100 million for financial or equity/asset transfer transactions and RMB 40 million for other specified related-party transactions also require contemporaneous documentation; even below these thresholds, the WFOE must maintain arm's length evidence if it is selected for a TP risk review.

      
Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Abuse Rules (GAAR) under CIT Law Article 47. The WFOE must have real employees, premises, books, board decisions, risk control, and decision-making functions in mainland China and cannot be a bare title-holding vehicle. For the Hong Kong parent, Economic Substance Regulations and Core Income-Generating Activities principles require that investment strategy, risk assumption, financing, and ownership decisions be performed in Hong Kong; absent such substance, treaty relief may be denied and transactions recharacterized with punitive adjustments.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Corporate Income Tax Evasion by Fraudulent Expense Inflation
        
Statutory sanction: Law on the Administration of Tax Levying and Collection of the PRC (LTAC) Article 63 orders recovery of unpaid tax and imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded amount; late-payment surcharge applies at 0.05% per day under Article 32; Criminal Law Article 201 adds a criminal fine and imprisonment up to seven years where the fraudulent under-declaration exceeds RMB 50,000 and represents more than 10% of tax due.

        
Director personal liability: Under PRC Company Law Article 23, directors who abuse the limited liability structure and knowingly cause false filings may be personally liable for unpaid corporate taxes and creditor losses. The tax authority may blacklist them as seriously dishonest taxpayers, impose exit-control travel bans, freeze personal assets, and pursue criminal prosecution leading to imprisonment and confiscation of personal estate.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing and Thin Capitalization Violations
        
Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41, 46 and 47, together with Implementation Regulations Articles 119 and 121, authorize the tax authority to make special tax adjustments and charge enhanced interest at the benchmark lending rate plus five percentage points; if the adjustment creates a tax underpayment, LTAC Article 63 imposes a 50% to 5 times evaded-tax penalty; refusal to provide documentation allows the tax authority to deem income using reasonable methods.

        
Director personal liability: Directors are exposed to personal asset recovery and unlimited liability where the corporate veil is pierced for willful transfer pricing manipulation; travel bans and blacklisting may be imposed as serious tax dishonesty measures; directors who certify false TP filings can be criminally prosecuted for aiding tax evasion, with custodial sentences available.

      

      
      

        Shell Company, Treaty Shopping and Money Laundering
        
Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 47 and Implementation Regulation Article 120 deny treaty benefits, recharacterize the arrangement, and impose the statutory 10% dividend/interest/royalty WHT; LTAC Article 63 penalizes tax evasion with 50% to 5 times the deficient tax; PRC Anti-Money Laundering laws permit substantial administrative fines; Criminal Law Article 191 punishes money laundering with fines and imprisonment, generally from five to ten years for serious cases.

        
Director personal liability: The corporate veil may be pierced for sham transactions, allowing civil enforcement against directors' personal assets; directors and beneficial owners face exit restrictions and blacklisting by tax and anti-money-laundering authorities; where laundering of family assets through the shell is proven, imprisonment and confiscation of personal estate are legally available.
