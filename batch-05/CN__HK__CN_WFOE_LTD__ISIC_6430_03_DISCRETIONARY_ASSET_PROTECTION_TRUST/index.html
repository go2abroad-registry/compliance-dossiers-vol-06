Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (PRC STA) and its Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) online portal; for this Hong Kong-parented WFOE, the primary national tax registration is with the PRC STA, not the Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department.

E-government filing portal

Upon incorporation, the Administration for Market Regulation issues a Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) which constitutes the WFOE's statutory Tax Identification Number (TIN); tax registration is completed automatically through inter-agency data sharing, but the WFOE must within 30 days confirm its taxpayer profile in the e-Tax portal, elect VAT taxpayer status, register for corporate income tax and VAT, and file bank account details. For a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment, the in-charge tax authority of the withholding agent or the place where income arises may issue a non-resident TIN after submission of the enterprise registration certificate, notarised and legalised proof of existence, a written application, and the underlying cross-border contract.

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 annual taxable sales; exceeding this threshold makes general VAT taxpayer registration mandatory, while all taxable entities must complete tax registration within 30 days of business licence issuance.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Exported goods are zero-rated when physically removed from the PRC customs territory and supported by a customs export declaration. Cross-border services, including trust administration, are zero-rated only if they fall within the statutory cross-border zero-rated service catalogue and are wholly supplied to and consumed by an overseas beneficiary; otherwise they may be VAT-exempt without input-credit refund. The WFOE must establish that the service is performed outside the PRC, consideration is received in foreign exchange from offshore, and no onshore-related benefit is conferred.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document for physical exports
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Chinese bank foreign-exchange receipt voucher evidencing repatriation of export/service proceeds
  • Signed cross-border service contract and VAT invoice (for trust administration services, which do not require customs clearance)

VAT invoices must be issued through the State Taxation Administration's Golden Tax System Phase IV / national electronic invoice platform; fully digitalised electronic invoices with encrypted QR codes are transmitted to the STA in real time, and special VAT invoices require tax-control tray integration and can be cancelled only by red-letter reversal. Anti-fraud digital stamping and e-invoice repository matching are mandatory.

A VAT zero-rating or refund claim is subject to documentary audit by the STA and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange. Failure to repatriate proceeds within 180 days, or submission of false customs declarations or remittance certificates, results in repayment of the refunded VAT, late-payment interest at 0.05% per day, administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax undercharged, and potential criminal liability for fraudulent export tax rebate.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a managed float regime with strict capital account controls while maintaining current account convertibility under the 1996 IMF Article VIII obligations. For a WFOE (Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise) established in mainland China with Hong Kong SAR ownership, inward direct investment constitutes a capital account item requiring prior SAFE registration and the opening of a designated foreign currency capital account at a domestic bank. Capital injections via the HK shareholder must be routed using the FDI (Foreign Direct Investment) registration code, and conversion of foreign currency into RMB is permitted only for genuine business uses backed by underlying contracts, invoices, payroll, tax payments, or other verifiable operational expenses. Repatriation of after-tax profits to the Hong Kong parent is treated as current account remittance, but the WFOE must present audited financial statements, corporate income tax settlement certificates, and a board resolution declaring dividends. The SAFE system verifies the transaction against the FDI account and the declared purpose; remittance without a solid paper trail is blocked. Export proceeds from the WFOE's trading activities are subject to the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, but there is no mandatory surrender ratio, it may retain foreign exchange or settle at market rates. The key hurdles are the 'real needs' principle under the Measures for the Administration of the Foreign Exchange Business of Financial Institutions, which requires each conversion or remittance to be supported by documentary evidence, and the prohibition of using capital-account FX for speculative investments or domestic equity purchases without special approval. Convertibility is therefore asymmetric: current account flows are largely free, while capital account flows, including shareholder loans, equity swaps, and other balance-sheet movements, require case-by-case SAFE approval or post-event filing.

Export proceeds surrender rules

No mandatory surrender ratio and no fixed statutory conversion timeline are imposed under the current SAFE framework. However, all export foreign-currency proceeds must be repatriated to China through bank settlement channels, entered into the Trade in Goods Foreign Exchange Monitoring System, and fully matched against customs declarations. In practice, export receipts must be credited to the WFOE's domestic foreign currency settlement account within the stated trade credit period or, at the latest, within 180 days after the export customs clearance date. Failure to meet this timing without a valid SAFE filing (e.g., extension due to shipping delays or contractual payment terms) will trigger an 'abnormal' compliance flag and may suspend the enterprise's convenient FX receipt and payment status.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its administrative arm, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Unviable. The People's Republic of China does not permit foreign fintech electronic money institutions or offshore EMI platforms to open corporate bank accounts or provide FX clearing services to onshore WFOEs. Only domestically licensed commercial banks authorised by the PBOC and SAFE may lawfully handle corporate foreign exchange receipts and payments. Attempts to channel WFOE business through an EMI would violate the Administrative Measures for the Settlement and Sale of Foreign Exchange and be rejected by both the banking system and tax authorities.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit capital via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital account, referencing the SAFE-issued Foreign Exchange Registration Form code. The remittance purpose must be 'Foreign Direct Investment – Capital Contributions' and the payment instructions must include the WFOE name, the bank account number, and the FDI registration number. Upon receipt, the funds are credited to the capital escrow account. Conversion into RMB requires the WFOE to provide a payment instruction with underlying contracts and invoices; a certified public accountant must later complete a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital, which is then filed with the State Administration for Market Regulation and SAFE.Operational trade settlements from the WFOE can be executed through SWIFT MT103 for current account payments such as subcontracting fees, royalties, and operating expenses. For physical goods trade, the standard clearing rails include irrevocable documentary letters of credit (L/C) and documentary collections (D/P and D/A) opened through the WFOE's domestic bank, with all shipping documents routed through the banking channel. Turnover: Under the Hong Kong Companies Ordinance, Cap 622, every company must appoint an independent certified public accountant auditor unless it qualifies for statutory audit exemption. A private company is exempt as a small private company for a financial year if it satisfies any two of the following three quantitative conditions: (a) total revenue does not exceed HKD 10,000,000; (b) total assets do not exceed HKD 10,000,000; and (c) the company has not more than 50 employees. A subsidiary member of a group may be exempt only if the group qualifies as a small private company group under sections 359 and 360 of the Companies Ordinance. The exemption is unavailable where any member holding at least 5% of the voting rights requests an audit or where another statute or regulator, including the trust and company service provider licensing regime under Cap 615 or a trust company licence condition, imposes a mandatory independent audit. Licensed fiduciary and asset-protection trust entities should confirm with their prudential supervisor whether an audit is contractually or regulatorily required irrespective of the Companies Ordinance thresholds.
