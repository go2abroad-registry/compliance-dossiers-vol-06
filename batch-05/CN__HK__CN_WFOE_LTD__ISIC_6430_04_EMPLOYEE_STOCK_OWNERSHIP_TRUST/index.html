Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum registered capital applies to a limited liability WFOE under current PRC law. The registered capital must be a genuine amount subscribed by the shareholder and recorded in the Articles of Association, and the paid-in portion must be remitted to a domestic RMB or foreign-exchange capital account within the legally permitted contribution period. No blocked escrow account is required for the ESOP administration business; however, capital remitted from the Hong Kong shareholder will be subject to foreign-exchange verification by the receiving bank under SAFE capital item supervision.

Funding & unlock path: Not applicable to heavy industry. The WFOE must nonetheless obtain a fire safety inspection certificate for its leased office under the PRC Fire Protection Law before occupation, maintain an evacuation plan, and register the workplace with the local emergency management bureau for occupational safety, with fire drills conducted annually.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) for Mainland China, including the provincial Electronic Tax Bureau (ETB) online filing platforms and the STA official website at www.chinatax.gov.cn; for the Hong Kong SAR leg, the Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong, including the eTAX online filing portal and the Business Registration Office operating under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310).

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the China WFOE with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR); the issued business license carries the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) for the WFOE and its VAT taxpayer number. Step 2: SAMR automatically transmits registration data to the STA under the multi-certificate integration procedure, so a separate tax registration application is not required; the WFOE is entered in the STA taxpayer register and obtains an Electronic Tax Bureau filing profile. Step 3: Complete supplemental tax registration data in the ETB, including bank account information, accounting and tax officer details, payroll and social security registrations if employees exist, and the registered address. Step 4: If annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000, apply for VAT general taxpayer status; voluntary general taxpayer registration is available below that threshold. The WFOE also applies for e-invoice issuance capability through the STA VAT invoice management platform. Step 5: For any Hong Kong SAR branch, subsidiary, or qualifying ESOP trust administrator in Hong Kong, separately incorporate or register with the Hong Kong Companies Registry where applicable, and obtain a Business Registration Certificate from the IRD Business Registration Office under the Business Registration Ordinance (Cap. 310). The Business Registration Number is the Hong Kong tax identifier for Profits Tax, employer obligations, and stamp duty; Hong Kong has no VAT or GST, so no indirect tax registration is required in Hong Kong. Step 6: For foreign non-resident entities that do not create a permanent establishment in Mainland China, the WFOE as payer must complete withholding registration in the ETB; the non-resident does not receive a Chinese VAT ID and may separately apply for a China tax resident certificate for treaty purposes where relevant.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation for the Mainland China WFOE: it is a Chinese resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law, subject to 25% enterprise income tax on worldwide income, with the small-and-low-profit enterprise preferential rates available where statutory conditions are met. The Hong Kong SAR component or trust is on Territorial_Scope: Profits Tax is imposed by the Inland Revenue Ordinance at 16.5% only on profits arising in or derived from Hong Kong; a Hong Kong-resident administrator or trustee is not automatically taxable on non-Hong Kong source fees. The ESOP trust itself is not a pass-through entity for China corporate income tax unless separately structured as a PRC partnership; no check-the-box transparency applies.

Available election forms

None resembling the US check-the-box election. China determines tax classification by legal form under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and partnership taxation rules. Available administrative elections include: (i) election to register as a VAT general taxpayer where turnover is below the RMB 5,000,000 threshold, (ii) election to use the simplified VAT collection method where lawfully available, and (iii) filing elections for VAT exemption or zero-rating of qualifying cross-border taxable services in the Electronic Tax Bureau. Hong Kong has no check-the-box election and no hybrid-mismatch election; taxpayers rely on IRD source-of-profits determinations and must consider anti-hybrid and general anti-avoidance provisions under the Inland Revenue Ordinance if mismatches arise.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

China VAT general taxpayer registration is mandatory when annual taxable sales exceed RMB 5,000,000. The WFOE must register as a taxpayer immediately upon obtaining its business license even with zero turnover; the RMB 5,000,000 amount determines small-scale taxpayer versus general taxpayer status rather than whether a taxpayer exists. ESOP administration and related cross-border or domestic services are generally subject to the 6% VAT rate, or permissible reduced and simplified rates, unless an exemption or zero-rating applies. Hong Kong has no turnover-based indirect tax registration threshold because it imposes no VAT or GST.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Mainland China zero-rates the export of goods under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and the current VAT export-rebate framework. For the WFOE's cross-border services, zero-rating or VAT exemption is available only for listed cross-border taxable services supplied to a non-resident recipient, used entirely outside Mainland China, and supported by contractual, payment, and performance evidence. ESOP administration services supplied to an offshore plan sponsor or foreign trustee may qualify if the service is not connected with Chinese land or goods in China and the provider files the cross-border taxable service zero-rating or exemption application in the Electronic Tax Bureau. Goods exported from Mainland China to Hong Kong SAR qualify as exports for VAT purposes because Hong Kong is a separate customs territory; the goods must physically leave Mainland China through a customs-supervised port and be recorded in the China Customs export system.

  • Customs Export Goods Declaration with customs release endorsement
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bill of Lading, Air Waybill, or combined transport document evidencing that the goods left China
  • Bank Export Proceeds Credit Notice, Inward Remittance Certificate, or bank foreign exchange receipt evidencing collection from the overseas buyer
  • Sales contract or purchase order for the exported goods
  • Export Tax Rebate (Exemption) Declaration filed through the STA Export Tax Rebate Integrated Service Platform; for cross-border services, the cross-border service contract, invoice, foreign-exchange receipt evidence, and service performance documentation are substituted for the customs declaration

Mainland China uses the STA Golden Tax Phase IV integrated VAT invoice system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) platform. Taxpayers must issue VAT invoices through the STA-approved online platform; the system performs real-time data reception, assigns a unique invoice number and anti-fraud QR code, and files invoice data to the STA automatically at issuance. There is no separate physical fiscal device stamp; fiscal clearance is embedded in the central invoice database. Export tax rebate claims and cross-border service zero-rating or exemption filings are documented and transmitted through the STA export tax rebate integrated service platform and through the customs-STA data exchange. Hong Kong does not require electronic fiscal clearance for any GST or VAT and uses the IRD eTAX portal for tax returns and business registration.

The STA and China Customs operate integrated risk-monitoring and data-matching systems. If export proceeds are not repatriated to China within approximately 180 days of export and no deferred-collection registration has been made with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), the zero-rated export treatment is subject to reversal; the output VAT becomes payable with late-payment surcharge and interest. Fraudulent or unsubstantiated export zero-rating or rebate claims are penalized under Article 66 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Tax Collection and Administration: recovery of the full amount obtained plus a fine of one to five times the amount fraudulently obtained, and criminal liability where smuggling or fraudulent export-tax-refund offences are established. Cross-border service zero-rating claims are audited for substance, payment traceability, and overseas consumption; the tax authority may claw back exempted or zero-rated input credit and impose penalties if the offshore-service conditions are not independently verified. Hong Kong Profits Tax penalties apply to false or incomplete tax returns filed with the IRD, including additional tax assessments of up to three times the tax undercharged and criminal sanctions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a strictly managed floating regime under the People's Bank of China and SAFE. The current account is essentially convertible for genuine trade in goods and services, but all foreign exchange payments require the collecting bank to verify trade documents, transaction codes, and the enterprise's customs declaration. The capital account is far more rigid: cross-border equity injections, capital increases, profit remittances, and transfers of WFOE ownership are permitted only after SAFE registration under the Foreign Investment Information Reporting system. A WFOE whose commercial purpose is the administration of an employee stock ownership trust must demonstrate that any inbound funding corresponds to a registered equity incentive plan; otherwise the bank cannot credit the proceeds. Profit remittance follows a verification-first principle: the enterprise must file audited financial statements, a board dividend resolution, tax clearance, and the SAFE remittance e-notice. Conversion of foreign currency into RMB is allowed at the central parity rate with a daily band, but reverse conversion of RMB for offshore distribution is scrutinized as if it were a capital outflow. There are no freely floating, market-determined current account volumes; use of offshore fintech EMIs is not recognized by the onshore banking system. In practice, treasury structuring must treat every cross-border ring as a separate, registered flow with traceable underlying proof.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The SAFE requires all export proceeds to be repatriated to the WFOE's bank accounts within the period agreed in the sales contract, subject to a maximum monitoring tolerance of 180 days from the customs declaration date, with longer periods only on prior bank application and SAFE-approved justification. There is no mandatory conversion ratio in normal circumstances; an exporter may retain foreign currency in a foreign exchange settlement account or have the bank convert it into RMB under its active foreign exchange account policy. However, receipt must be linked to a matched customs declaration and invoice; if the amount exceeds USD 500,000 equivalent, the bank will insist on loading the transaction in the SAFE monitoring system before crediting the settlement account. Undisputed surrender duties apply only to certain strategic commodities or where the enterprise has outstanding foreign exchange liabilities; otherwise the surrender is not a forced sale but a repatriation obligation.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the core treasury rail for a mainland WFOE. Under PRC foreign exchange administration regulations, only SAFE-approved domestic banks holding a foreign exchange business license may open RMB current accounts and foreign exchange accounts for resident enterprises. Offshore EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex generally operate under licenses from other jurisdictions and have no mainland banking branch; they cannot connect to CNAPS or the SAFE cross-border post-verification system. They also cannot perform capital account registration, export/import document matching, or the SAFE e-node reporting required for a WFOE. The only acceptable use is as an auxiliary non-resident account for funds held outside China before a compliant SAFE-registered inbound injection, but the onshore WFOE must never use an EMI as its primary clearing house. Doing so would cause bank refusal, accounting qualification issues, and regulatory citations for unsanctioned cross-border fund movement.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be routed through SWIFT MT103 with a clearly stated foreign exchange purpose code. The remitter must be the registered HK shareholder, not a third party. The beneficiary bank must verify the SAFE Foreign Investment Information Reporting code before crediting the WFOE capital account. Once credited, the WFOE applies to the bank to settle the foreign currency into RMB under the capital settlement rules by submitting a capital fund settlement use schedule, board resolution, and the underlying ESOP administration expense plan. A licensed Chinese certified public accountant then issues a capital verification report for registered capital. Working capital overheads cannot be paid directly from the capital account before the SAFE settlement filing is completed.For domestic operating expenses, the WFOE uses RMB clearing through CNAPS to local vendors. For non-trade cross-border service payments, the WFOE initiates SWIFT MT103 with the relevant current account purpose code and supporting contract. For actual trade settlements, a commercial letter of credit issued on behalf of the WFOE is cleared through the issuing bank's SWIFT network, with the beneficiary presenting shipping documents against the LC terms. Documentary collections are permissible for low-risk, repetitive import or export transactions. Outbound dividends to the Hong Kong shareholder must be remitted by MT103 after board approval, audited financial statements, tax clearance, and SAFE e-registration; ESOP-related repurchases from offshore beneficiaries require an approved employee share incentive plan registration file with SAFE.Onshore CNY deliverable forward contracts with a licensed commercial bank are permissible for hedging real FX exposure arising from trade receivables, payables, or committed service fees.Cross-currency swaps are available for current account trade-related cash flows, but any capital account-linked cross-currency swap requires prior SAFE approval and a documented real exposure.NDFs are not a permissible onshore hedging instrument for a China resident WFOE; hedging must be executed as a deliverable forward settled onshore under SAFE rules.FX options are allowed only when the WFOE has a genuine underlying obligation and the option is used to hedge committed foreign currency payment or receipt, never for speculative leverage.

E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as the core treasury rail for a mainland WFOE. Under PRC foreign exchange administration regulations, only SAFE-approved domestic banks holding a foreign exchange business license may open RMB current accounts and foreign exchange accounts for resident enterprises. Offshore EMI platforms such as Wise, Revolut Business, or Airwallex generally operate under licenses from other jurisdictions and have no mainland banking branch; they cannot connect to CNAPS or the SAFE cross-border post-verification system. They also cannot perform capital account registration, export/import document matching, or the SAFE e-node reporting required for a WFOE. The only acceptable use is as an auxiliary non-resident account for funds held outside China before a compliant SAFE-registered inbound injection, but the onshore WFOE must never use an EMI as its primary clearing house. Doing so would cause bank refusal, accounting qualification issues, and regulatory citations for unsanctioned cross-border fund movement.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File is required for a year in which related-party transfers of tangible assets equal or exceed CNY 400,000,000, transfers of financial assets equal or exceed CNY 100,000,000, intangible asset transfers or licenses equal or exceed CNY 200,000,000, or other related-party service transactions equal or exceed CNY 100,000,000; a Master File is required where aggregate related-party transactions exceed CNY 1,000,000,000 in a single fiscal year.

Economic substance scope: China is not an Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) territory, but it applies the substance-over-form principle and General Anti-Avoidance Rules under EIT Law Article 47. The HK shareholder and ESOP trust must demonstrate core income-generating activities in Hong Kong; a letterbox company with no employees, premises, or board decisions will be denied treaty benefits and may be recharacterized as a conduit subject to PRC withholding and GAAR penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion through Shell Conduit and Dividend Stripping

Statutory sanction: Violation of the Enterprise Income Tax Law Articles 3, 37 and 39; underpayment must be settled with interest; the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 can recharacterize the conduit transaction. Criminal Law Article 201 imposes a fine of one to five times the tax evaded and imprisonment up to seven years for large-scale evasion; the taxpayer is added to the State Taxation Administration serious tax dishonesty list (blacklist).

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized the dividend payment without withholding are jointly and severally liable for the uncollected tax, interest and penalties under tax collection law. The legal representative may be subjected to an exit ban or travel restriction due to blacklisting; if the directors knowingly used the shell to evade taxes, they face civil restitution of diverted corporate assets and criminal imprisonment of up to seven years.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance and Artificial Intercompany Fee Shifting

Statutory sanction: Violation of Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Article 44 and SAT Public Notice [2016] No. 42; the tax authority may make a transfer pricing adjustment to taxable income, impose a late-payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, add penalty interest after the first year of adjustment, and fine the company under the Tax Collection Administration Law for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed fabricated service agreements or caused false transfer pricing files to be submitted are personally exposed to piercing of the corporate veil; they can be held jointly liable for the adjusted tax and penalties, blacklisted by the tax authority, subjected to exit bans, and prosecuted for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 with imprisonment up to seven years.
AML Beneficial Ownership Concealment in the Employee Stock Ownership Trust

Statutory sanction: Violation of the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and Beneficial Ownership Information Management Measures; regulatory fines may reach RMB 5,000,000 for serious violations, with suspension of business and removal of responsible officers; the entity is also placed on the anti-money laundering and tax integrity blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors and senior officers who knowingly concealed beneficial ownership face civil liability for restitution of gains, travel bans and blacklisting, and criminal liability for money-laundering-related offences, including imprisonment. Corporate personality is pierced when the trust is used as a sham to evade tax or conceal ownership, making directors personally liable for unpaid taxes and fines.
