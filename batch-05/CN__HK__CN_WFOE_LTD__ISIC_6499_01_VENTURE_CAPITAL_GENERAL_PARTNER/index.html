Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (mainland tax authority) through its provincial and municipal offices; online filings are made via the Electronic Tax Service Platform (e-Tax) operated by the State Taxation Administration. The Hong Kong SAR Inland Revenue Department is not the registration authority for this mainland-incorporated WFOE and is relevant only to the Hong Kong shareholder or any Hong Kong-resident fund entity.

E-government filing portal

For the WFOE, the statutory tax identifier is the 18-character Unified Social Credit Code obtained from the Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation; this code is the Taxpayer Identification Number (TIN) and VAT registration number. The WFOE must complete combined tax registration through the e-Tax portal within 30 calendar days of business licence issuance, which simultaneously registers the entity for corporate income tax, VAT, payroll-related withholding, and local surtaxes. Next, the WFOE must apply in e-Tax for VAT taxpayer qualification (small-scale or general VAT taxpayer) and for electronic invoice issuance permissions. If export or import activities are planned, the WFOE must separately complete customs registration with the General Administration of Customs through the China International Trade Single Window and obtain the customs registration code used on export declarations. A foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China does not receive a separate Chinese TIN; the Chinese payer or withholding agent, including the WFOE when making payments, handles non-resident contract recordation and withholding declarations under its own TIN. A non-resident enterprise that establishes a permanent establishment must complete ordinary tax registration and obtain a TIN under the same e-Tax procedure.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation; the WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise under the Enterprise Income Tax Law and is taxed on worldwide income at the standard 25% corporate income tax rate. No pass-through treatment is available for a limited liability WFOE.

Available election forms

None available for income tax; there is no check-the-box entity classification election and no general hybrid mismatch election under PRC CIT. For VAT only, a newly incorporated WFOE may elect small-scale taxpayer treatment at registration where projected annual taxable turnover does not exceed RMB 5 million; at or above that amount, or voluntarily with a sound accounting system, general VAT taxpayer registration applies. The Mainland and Hong Kong SAR Tax Arrangement provides reduced withholding possibilities but does not operate as an entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable sales is the mandatory turnover threshold for general VAT taxpayer registration; however, all WFOEs must complete tax and VAT registration within 30 days of incorporation even with zero turnover. Below RMB 5 million the WFOE will be classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, export zero-rating is available where goods physically leave PRC customs territory under a customs export declaration, the sale is made to an overseas buyer, and export proceeds are repatriated to China; refund of input VAT is then linked to the export declaration. For cross-border services, zero-rating is reserved to the statutory list of zero-rated cross-border taxable services in the VAT zero-rating and exemption catalogue, including specified offshore R&D, design, software, information-system and business-process services, supplied to an offshore recipient and wholly consumed outside China. The WFOE VC fund-management services are not automatically zero-rated; routine management fees will generally be taxable at 6%, or may qualify for VAT exemption only where the services are provided to an offshore fund and the full-offshore-consumption test is documented. Cross-border e-commerce export goods must file a customs export declaration under supervision codes 9610, 9710 or 9810, as applicable, to access the zero-rating and refund mechanism.

  • Customs Export Declaration (export bill of entry / export declaration form) filed through the China International Trade Single Window
  • Commercial invoice and packing list
  • Bill of Lading for sea freight, Air Waybill for air freight, or equivalent cargo receipt for road or rail export evidencing physical departure from PRC customs territory
  • Foreign exchange receipt voucher from the receiving bank, also referred to in international trade practice as an Inward Remittance Certificate, evidencing repatriation and settlement of export proceeds in China
  • Export sales contract and, where applicable, the cross-border e-commerce export manifest or declaration under customs supervision code 9610, 9710 or 9810
  • Electronic export refund and zero-rating declaration data and supporting evidence set submitted through the e-Tax export tax refund module; no separate paper form is prescribed
  • For zero-rated or exempt cross-border services: written service agreement, evidence that the recipient is an offshore entity and the services are wholly consumed outside China, plus bank receipt of offshore payment

The WFOE must use the State Taxation Administration fully digitalised e-invoice platform inside the e-Tax system. Each fully digitalised e-invoice receives a unique electronic invoice number and an anti-counterfeit QR code at the moment of issuance, and the complete invoice data set is transmitted in real time to the Golden Tax System Phase IV. There is no pre-printed fiscal stamping or physical pre-clearance voucher; paper-invoice issuers must use the Tax UKey and transmit each invoice record at the time of issue. VAT special invoices may only be issued by general VAT taxpayers; small-scale taxpayers issue general VAT invoices or apply for special-invoice issuance through the State Taxation Administration process.

The State Taxation Administration, Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange cross-check export declarations, logistics records, VAT invoice data, and bank foreign-exchange receipts through the Golden Tax System Phase IV. If zero-rating is claimed without the required documents, or the documents are false or internally inconsistent, the tax authority will deny zero-rating, recompute VAT at the applicable rate, and claw back any input VAT refund already settled, with late-payment surcharge. Under Article 63 of the Law of the People's Republic of China on Administration of Tax Collection, fraudulent or deceptive underpayment of tax is subject to a penalty of 50% up to five times the tax shortfall. Fraudulent export tax refund claims additionally expose the WFOE and responsible officers to criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, suspension of export tax-refund qualifications, and customs penalties under the Customs Law. The 180-day foreign-exchange repatriation benchmark is separately monitored by SAFE; exports not repatriated within the required window may lose refund eligibility and trigger corrective compliance measures.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime with convertibility for current account transactions under Article VIII of the IMF Articles of Agreement, while capital account transactions remain heavily regulated. A WFOE must register all cross-border foreign exchange flows with the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) through its designated bank. Capital injections from the Hong Kong parent are classified as foreign direct investment (FDI) and are subject to repatriation restrictions, meaning the funds cannot be used for purposes outside the approved business scope without subsequent filing. Surrender requirements are applied to current account receipts, not capital injections; export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days of customs declaration, and the full amount may be retained in a foreign currency account or voluntarily converted. Profit remittances require an audited financial statement, tax settlement certificate, and a resolution of the board of directors, and are remitted through a SAFE-designated bank under the current account, though bank scrutiny reviews the authenticity of the underlying earnings. Thus, while RMB is fully convertible for trade and service payments, capital account items such as overseas loans, derivatives, and portfolio investment remain constrained, requiring a robust treasury function to navigate approval pipelines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under SAFE current account administration, export proceeds must be repatriated into China within 180 days of the export customs declaration date; there is no mandatory conversion ratio, and enterprises may retain 100% of the foreign currency or settle to RMB at a licensed designated bank, provided the transaction is documented and matches the customs manifest.

Central bank supervision: The People's Bank of China (PBOC) and its statutory delegate, the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Offshore digital EMI platforms (e.g., Revolut, Airwallex, Multifri) are not licensed by the PBOC/SAFE to conduct onshore RMB/FX settlement or hold WFOE domestic corporate accounts. They may be used only for offshore non-RMB treasury if the Hong Kong parent uses them, but the China-domiciled WFOE must operate through a SAFE-designated bank. Therefore, the viability is very low and legally barred for domestic clearing and capital account flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's SAFE-designated foreign currency capital account at Bank of China, using FIFX code 'FDI Capital Injection' and the WFOE's FDI registration code. Upon receipt, the bank verifies the harmonized consistency of the remitter, the registration, and the amount, then permits conversion into RMB via the capital settlement system (CAPI) in tranches strictly for approved business expenditures such as payroll, rent, legal fees, and technology acquisition. A Chinese certified public accountant must issue a capital verification report within 30 days of the first injection; the report is filed with SAFE.For trade settlement, use standard SWIFT MT103 for direct payments, commercial letters of credit (MT700/MT400) for documentary trade, and documentary collections (D/C) for open-account risk. All outbound remittances must be enrolled with the tax bureau for withholding or dividend taxes, and an 'e-tax certificate' must be uploaded to the bank. For RMB-denominated outbound trades, use CIPS (Cross-Border RMB Payment System) to avoid USD clearing delays. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Enterprise Income Tax on net taxable income; 15% preferential rate for qualified High/New-Technology Enterprises; effective 5% rate for Small Low-Profit Enterprises within applicable thresholds

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax under EIT Law Article 37 on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise. Reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement where the Hong Kong resident corporate beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the PRC payer and satisfies all beneficial-ownership conditions.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42, a PRC resident enterprise must prepare the contemporaneous Local File if its annual related-party transactions exceed the statutory category thresholds in RMB, including approximately RMB 200 million for tangible-property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible-property and financial-asset transfers, and RMB 100 million for related-party services; the Master File is required where aggregate annual related-party transactions reach the much higher statutory threshold associated with Form G100000, and all related-party transactions must be disclosed in the Annual Related-Party Transaction Report filed with the annual CIT return.

Economic substance scope: Although the Hong Kong offshore Economic Substance Regulations do not directly apply to a mainland PRC resident WFOE, the PRC General Anti-Avoidance Rule under EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 64 will disregard structures without economic substance. The WFOE must demonstrate that Core Income-Generating Activities, including fund-management oversight, investment diligence, asset management decisions, risk control, and corporate governance, are performed where the people, premises, and decision-making are located. A pair of Hong Kong paper entities carrying no substance will not obtain treaty benefits and will be recharacterised as agents of the mainland operation, causing the mainland tax authority to reallocate income, impose 10% or 5% withholding on deemed distributions, and assess late-payment penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Thin Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 46 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures (State Council Decree 512) disallow the excessive interest and recast the excess as a deemed dividend. Tax Administration Law Article 63 imposes a fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax plus daily surcharges. The withholding on the recast distribution is 10%, with DTA 5% available only if the Hong Kong parent meets the 25%-holding beneficial-ownership test and has substantive activity.

Director personal liability: Directors who approve false loan agreements or ignore debt-equity limits face piercing of the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 (2023 Company Law Article 23) where corporate assets are commingled or the company is used as a vehicle for tax fraud. Directors may be personally liable for unpaid tax, penalties, and interest; the legal representative can be blacklisted and subjected to exit-control travel bans by the tax authority; criminal tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 is punishable by imprisonment of up to 7 years and fines.
Shell Company / Base Erosion and Profit Shifting

Statutory sanction: EIT Law Article 47 and SAT Announcement [2016] No. 64 authorise the tax authority to recharacterise the entire arrangement, deny treaty benefits under the beneficial-ownership provisions of SAT Announcement [2015] No. 60, and reallocate income to the mainland. The resulting adjustment attracts 10% statutory withholding on royalties and interest and 10% or 5% on deemed dividends, plus daily late-payment interest and Tax Administration Law Article 63 penalties of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who structure and sign the sham cross-border service agreements are liable for restitution of diverted profits and joint and several tax obligations where the corporate form is abused. The tax authority may impose personal travel bans, seize personal assets through enforcement proceedings, and refer the matter for criminal prosecution for tax evasion or fraudulent treaty-benefit claims. Criminal Law Article 201 carries up to 7 years' imprisonment for large-scale evasion, and the individual can be fined up to 5 times the evaded amount.
Transfer Pricing Documentation / Related-Party Disclosure Failure

Statutory sanction: SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 imposes contemporaneous documentation obligations; failure to substantiate related-party pricing may lead to a special tax adjustment under the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, reallocation of taxable income, deemed dividends, and additional withholding. Tax Administration Law Article 63 provides penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid amount; the tax authority may also issue a RMB 2,000 to RMB 10,000 administrative penalty for non-filing and collect late-payment surcharges on the full revised tax.

Director personal liability: Directors who cause the company to conceal related-party transactions or destroy transfer-pricing records can be held personally accountable for the resulting corporate tax liability if the company is dissolved, emptied, or used for personal benefit. Under the Company Law pierce-the-veil rule and tax enforcement practice, directors may face civil asset recovery, exit-control blacklisting as legal representatives, and criminal liability for fraudulent tax returns under Criminal Law Article 201 where the concealed tax exceeds the criminal threshold, exposing them to imprisonment for up to 7 years and criminal fines.
