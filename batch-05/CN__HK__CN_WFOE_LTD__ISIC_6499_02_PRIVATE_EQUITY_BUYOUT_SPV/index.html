Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; local enforcement through the provincial and municipal tax service bureaus; online portal is the STA e-Tax / Electronic Tax Bureau digital tax platform, which integrates taxpayer registration, VAT invoicing, export tax rebate filing and daily tax declaration.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE: after the State Administration for Market Regulation (AMR) issues the business license, the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) is automatically generated and adopted as the tax identification number; tax registration is established simultaneously through the multi-license integration procedure, followed by activation of the e-tax account, selection of the local competent tax jurisdiction, registration of legal representative, financial accounting and bank accounts, and VAT invoice system certification. For a foreign non-resident parent or offshore investor (including the Hong Kong SAR entity), no separate TIN is required unless it derives China-source income; in such case, the non-resident enterprise must obtain a non-resident taxpayer identification number from the competent STA office or appoint the WFOE/withholding agent to complete non-resident tax registration and withholding filing, supported by contract, beneficial-ownership declaration and, where applicable, the China-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement residence certificate.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None; the PRC does not operate a check-the-box entity classification regime. A WFOE is automatically treated as a resident enterprise subject to entity-level corporate income tax. The only relevant elections are the VAT general taxpayer election on prospective turnover and the small-scale VAT taxpayer status, neither of which is a hybrid mismatch or entity classification election.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

Annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5,000,000 (RMB 5 million) triggers mandatory registration as a VAT general taxpayer. Below this threshold, the WFOE is treated as a small-scale VAT taxpayer unless it elects general taxpayer status; a newly formed WFOE must monitor prospective taxable turnover to determine registration on incorporation.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

To claim PRC 0% export VAT treatment, the WFOE must be registered as a VAT general taxpayer, the goods must be physically exported from the PRC customs territory and matched to a valid customs export declaration, the sale must be evidenced by contract, commercial invoice, carriage documents, and the export proceeds must be repatriated into the WFOE's domestic bank account in foreign currency (or RMB settlement authorized by SAFE). Qualifying offshore services under the cross-border service zero-rating framework must be rendered to an overseas recipient, consumed entirely outside China, and evidenced by an offshore contract and overseas payment receipt. Pure equity holding or buyout SPV income (dividends, capital gains on unlisted equity) is not automatically export zero-rated and must follow the PRC equity-transfer and non-resident withholding rules.

  • Customs Export Declaration / Export Goods Customs Declaration (Bill of Entry) with release seal, HS code, export port, destination and transaction value
  • Original Bill of Lading or air/sea waybill evidencing shipment out of the PRC customs territory and named consignee
  • Commercial invoice and packing list in English matching the customs declaration
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) / Export Foreign Exchange Collection Confirmation / bank credit slip showing receipt of the inward remittance
  • Foreign exchange verification and trade data submission records generated by the SAFE banking system
  • Input VAT special invoices and procurement/production records substantiating the refundable VAT credit, plus the STA export tax rebate declaration submitted through the e-tax platform

The PRC mandates tax-control digital invoicing through the STA unified electronic invoice platform and the VAT invoice management system. Taxpayers must issue fully digitalized electronic VAT invoices in real time using the e-tax account; each invoice is cleared/verified by the tax authority with an anti-fraud digital certificate, must bear the exact national tax classification code, and is automatically transmitted to the STA database for reconciliation. Paper invoices are being phased out, and any manual invoice generator without STA integration is non-compliant.

If the export declaration, bill of lading, FIRC, invoice chain, and SAFE receivables records are inconsistent, the STA will reverse the zero-rating, re-assess output VAT at the applicable PRC rate (13% for goods or 6% for taxable financial/modern services), add late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day from the original due date, impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax amount, and recover any VAT refunds previously paid. Serious instances can lead to cancellation of export tax rebate qualification, B/C risk-classification by SAFE, fines for non-repatriation, and criminal referral for fraudulently obtaining export tax rebates under the Criminal Law.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The PRC maintains asymmetric convertibility. Current account items are generally convertible after the bank has verified the underlying genuine transaction, but capital account transactions remain subject to a strict registration and approval regime. A China WFOE may purchase or sell foreign exchange for current account payments such as dividends, royalties, license fees and profit remittances once it presents the underlying contract, tax settlement certificate, audited financial statements and a valid exchange purpose code. Foreign exchange received as FDI equity capital is credited to the capital-account foreign currency settlement account and can only be used within the scope approved by SAFE and the bank, usually after completing the foreign investment information report and capital verification. Since 2012, no mandatory export surrender ratio applies; exporters maintain proceeds either in foreign currency accounts or convert at their discretion, while SAFE monitors trade authenticity through the goods trade verification system. Profit remittance is legally permissible but practically blocked unless the annual financial audit, corporate income tax final settlement, withholding tax declarations and SAFE current-account verification are clean. Therefore, RMB is liberalised for trade and normal services, but capital flows such as intercompany loans, downstream equity investments and acquisition payments require SAFE registrations, pre-approvals or post-event reporting, creating meaningful friction for a private equity buyout SPV.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Goods exporters must repatriate export foreign exchange proceeds to China within 90 days from the date of customs export declaration unless SAFE has approved offshore retention. After repatriation, no mandatory conversion ratio is imposed; the exporter may retain 0-100% in a foreign currency settlement account or convert to RMB at the prevailing market rate. The collecting bank must perform receipt verification against the customs declaration data before crediting. Any retention beyond 90 days requires a SAFE-compliant overseas retention registration and annual review.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Not legally viable for onboarding the WFOE as a principal account provider. Offshore digital EMIs lack PRC banking licences and are not accepted by SAFE as regulated FX clearing institutions; PBOC rules prohibit unlicensed electronic money institutions from taking deposits or conducting RMB and foreign currency clearing for resident entities. A WFOE must use a licensed Tier-1 Chinese bank for all onshore and cross-border flows. At most, an EMI can offer a read-only dashboard after a licensed bank account is opened, but no transactional reliance is permitted.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must wire the registered capital to the WFOE's Foreign Exchange Capital Settlement Account using SWIFT MT103. The message must identify the transfer as FDI equity injection and include the correct SAFE capital account purpose code for investment in foreign-invested enterprises plus the WFOE's foreign investment filing code. Upon arrival, the bank credits the capital account; foreign currency may be settled into RMB under SAFE settlement requirements, but the settled RMB must be monitored for permitted uses only. A certified Chinese CPA must issue a capital verification report for each injection. The bank must also validate the board resolution, the foreign investment negative-list status, the SAFE registration and the MOFCOM foreign investment information reporting evidence before the funds become available for acquisition disbursements.For goods trade, outbound settlements should use SWIFT MT103 with invoice, transport document and customs declaration matched through the bank's goods trade archive; where contractual deferred payment is preferred, an irrevocable letter of credit or documentary collection issued through the same Tier-1 bank is acceptable. For a PE buyout SPV, the majority of outbound flows will be acquisition consideration, upstream dividends or professional fees; these require tax clearance certificates, withholding tax evidence, SAFE registration and the governing transaction agreements. Mandatory Audit Threshold Small Company Audit Exemption Regime

Turnover: Hong Kong imposes no quantitative turnover, total-asset, or headcount threshold above which an audit is triggered because all active non-dormant companies must be audited. A wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) used as a private-equity buyout SPV must appoint a certified public accountant (practising) under Cap. 622 s.403 and must deliver audited financial statements to the Inland Revenue Department with its Profits Tax Return. There is no private-company size exemption. The only statutory alternative is the dormant company regime under Cap. 622 s.373, which requires a formal application and is available only where the company has had no accounting transactions during the relevant period and is not a member of a group with transactions; a PE SPV that holds investments, maintains a bank account, receives distributions, or incurs professional fees will not qualify for dormancy and must be audited regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

Statutory withholding tax on outbound dividends paid by a Chinese resident WFOE to a non-resident corporate shareholder is 10% under the Enterprise Income Tax Law; the PRC-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement reduces the rate to 5% where the Hong Kong beneficial owner directly holds at least 25% of the capital of the Chinese company for a continuous 12-month period preceding the dividend distribution.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File documentation is required for a Chinese WFOE if the annual amount of related party transactions exceeds RMB 200 million for tangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for intangible property transfers, RMB 100 million for financial asset transfers, or RMB 40 million for other related party transactions; the Annual Related Party Transaction Reporting Form is mandatory whenever any related party transaction occurs, regardless of amount, and a Master File is generally required for an enterprise with aggregate related party transactions above RMB 100 million or whose total revenue exceeds RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies arm's length and substance-over-form principles, and the tax authorities may deny treaty benefits or recharacterize transactions where the WFOE or the Hong Kong shareholder lacks real economic substance; the Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) of the private equity buyout SPV, including investment sourcing, deal evaluation, financing decisions, equity monitoring, and disposal decisions, must be performed with actual personnel, premises, and decision-making authority in China for the WFOE and in Hong Kong for the DTA claimant; a Hong Kong shell or conduit company with no CIGA is exposed to denial of the 5% dividend rate, remeasurement of withholding at 10%, and Chinese General Anti-Avoidance Rule challenges under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation and False Related-Party Transactions

Statutory sanction: Under Articles 41 and 46 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and Article 63 of the Tax Administration Law, the tax authority may adjust taxable income to arm's length, disallow excessive interest under the 2:1 debt-to-equity thin capitalization rule, impose 0.05% daily late payment interest, and levy a penalty equal to 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax; criminal liability for tax evasion attaches when the underpaid tax and the evasion ratio exceed the statutory thresholds.

Director personal liability: Directors and de facto controllers who instigate or knowingly permit the false documentation are jointly and severally liable for the tax restitution and penalties; tax authorities may apply exit controls and travel bans; under PRC Criminal Law Article 201, conviction for tax evasion is punishable by imprisonment of up to seven years and fines, with personal assets subject to seizure for unpaid tax and penalties.
Shell Company and Double Tax Agreement Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under Article 47 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law and the General Anti-Avoidance Rules, the tax authority may recharacterize the Hong Kong entity, deny treaty benefits, impose the statutory 10% withholding rate, and assess underpaid tax penalties of 50% to 500% plus interest; the entity may also be included in the State Taxation Administration serious tax dishonesty blacklist.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or submit false beneficial ownership declarations and tax residency certificates face personal civil liability for the under-withheld tax and penalties; criminal exposure for fraudulent tax filing can lead to imprisonment of up to seven years; administrative travel bans or blacklisting can be imposed to enforce collection from the individual directors as responsible persons.
Thin Capitalization, Unlawful Profit Repatriation, and Failure to Withhold

Statutory sanction: Under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 46 and Tax Administration Law Article 69, the excessive interest is recharacterized as a deemed dividend subject to 10% withholding, the non-complying distributor is ordered to recover the unlawful distribution, the withholding agent is liable for a penalty of 50% to 300% of the uncollected withholding tax, and the tax authority may additionally impose late payment interest and criminal tax evasion penalties where deliberate.

Director personal liability: Directors are personally liable to repay unlawful dividends to the WFOE and compensate creditors under PRC Company Law; if the unlawful distribution and false withholding declarations constitute fraud, directors face civil asset restitution, exit bans and travel blacklisting, and criminal prosecution under the PRC Criminal Law with imprisonment of up to seven years, fines, and confiscation of personal assets.
