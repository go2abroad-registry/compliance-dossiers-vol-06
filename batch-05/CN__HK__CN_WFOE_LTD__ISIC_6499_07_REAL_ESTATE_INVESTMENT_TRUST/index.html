Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None for an ordinary WFOE under the PRC Company Law. The registered capital is a subscribed amount fixed in the Articles of Association; the sole foreign shareholder must contribute it in accordance with a written contribution schedule, which may not exceed five years unless a special sectoral rule applies. There is no statutory requirement for an escrow or blocked account at incorporation; however, after establishment, all inbound capital contributions must be remitted through a SAFE-registered foreign exchange capital account opened at a licensed PRC bank and the capital must be used in accordance with the approved business scope and foreign exchange rules.

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing and compliance portal: the National Electronic Tax Administration (e-Tax) platform operated by the STA, including provincial e-Tax systems and the Electronic Invoice Service Platform.

E-government filing portal

For a wholly foreign-owned enterprise (WFOE) incorporated in mainland China: (1) the foreign investor must notarize, legalize or apostille corporate documents and complete any required foreign investment filing or approval under the Foreign Investment Law; (2) the WFOE must be registered with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), obtaining the business license bearing the 18-digit Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which functions as the statutory taxpayer identification number for all tax purposes; (3) within 30 days of business license issuance, the WFOE must complete tax registration with the competent STA tax office through the e-Tax platform, declaring the legal representative, registered address, shareholding structure, business scope, accounting standards, and bank account details; (4) the STA will activate the VAT taxpayer status (small-scale VAT taxpayer or VAT general taxpayer), payroll and individual income tax registration, stamp tax registration, property tax files, and land use tax files; (5) for the real estate investment portfolio activity, each taxable real estate holding must be separately reported to the local tax authority for real estate related taxes; (6) if the WFOE is treated as a resident enterprise, no separate non-resident enterprise tax registration is required, but a non-resident enterprise deriving China-source income without a permanent establishment must obtain a non-resident tax registration identifier through the withholding agent or directly with the STA.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 of annual aggregate taxable turnover. An entity with annual taxable sales exceeding RMB 5 million is mandatory to register as a VAT general taxpayer; below this threshold the entity may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer under simplified taxation, but all persons making taxable supplies within mainland China must complete VAT registration.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods qualify for 0% VAT export treatment where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods are physically exported from mainland China through a customs port, the exporter possesses valid export customs clearance records, the sale is made to an overseas purchaser, and the export proceeds are repatriated into China in compliance with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) rules. Cross-border services, including certain qualifying offshore financial or fund-management services, may qualify for 0% VAT or VAT exemption where the recipient is located outside mainland China and the service benefit is entirely consumed outside mainland China, subject to the STA cross-border service zero-rating filing and supporting-document requirements. Real estate rental income from properties located in mainland China remains taxable at the applicable VAT rate and does not qualify for export zero-rating.

  • Bill of Lading or equivalent international transport document (air waybill, truck manifest, or multimodal transport document)
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or bank export-proceeds receipt issued by a SAFE-designated foreign exchange bank

China mandates issuance and clearance of VAT invoices through the STA Golden Tax System (currently Golden Tax Phase III/IV). Special VAT invoices and electronic VAT invoices must be generated using tax control equipment, U-key, or the STA electronic invoice service, with real-time or near-real-time encrypted upload of invoice data to STA servers. The electronic invoices carry anti-fraud QR codes, digital signatures, and tax-control stamps; fully digitalized all-electronic invoices are progressively mandatory in pilot zones. ERP-to-STA application programming interfaces are available in the e-Tax platform for invoice reconciliation, VAT return filing, and fiscal clearance. Non-compliant issuance can result in invoice invalidation, blocked invoicing, and penalty assessments.

The STA and China Customs jointly audit export VAT refund and zero-rating claims. The exporter must retain all supporting documentation for at least five years. If a zero-rating claim is false, unsupported, or involves counterfeit customs declarations, or if export foreign exchange proceeds are not repatriated as required, the STA will retrospectively claw back input VAT credits refunded, assess interest at 0.05% per day on tax underpaid, and impose administrative penalties ranging from 50% to 5 times the tax evaded or falsely refunded under the Tax Collection and Administration Law and its implementing rules. Serious cases may result in disqualification from export VAT refund treatment and criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange rate regime and a partially convertible capital account. For the Hong Kong-owned WFOE REIT, current account transactions such as property management fees, service imports, and tax payments are fully convertible in principle: once the bank validates the transaction documents and the tax payment certificate, RMB or foreign currency can be converted and remitted through the bank's settlement system. However, capital account transactions remain subject to SAFE's permit and registration architecture. Inbound equity contributions from Hong Kong require an FDI registration certificate, an entity-level FX registration, and a domestic capital account before any foreign currency is converted into RMB for land or property investment. Profit remittance, which is a current account item under Chinese FX law, nevertheless creates friction because the outbound dividend must be backed by audited financial statements, a tax settlement proof, and a board resolution, and the bank screens the distribution ratio against the enterprise's statutory capital to avoid disguised capital outflows. Surplus funds held in foreign currency by the WFOE are not subject to mandatory surrender, but the enterprise must maintain FX balances within the SAFE-designated account ceiling. The overall assessment is: strong current account convertibility, strict capital account screening, moderate profit remittance administrative friction, with no convertibility of RMB through offshore EMIs.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE Rules on Foreign Exchange for Current Accounts, export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days from the date of export declaration or receipt of payment. There is no mandatory fixed conversion ratio; the exporter may choose to retain the full amount in a foreign exchange settlement account or convert all or part at market price. The bank must reconcile the export declaration, foreign exchange receipt form, and customs data through the trade single window, and proceeds must be fully absorbed into the cross-border receipt verification system before any onward disbursement.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), with delegated foreign exchange regulatory authority to the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low. Offshore digital EMI institutions are legally barred from opening domestic RMB or foreign currency accounts for a China-registered WFOE because China's banking regulatory perimeter requires all RMB settlements and capital account transactions to be processed through licensed domestic financial institutions supervised by PBOC and SAFE. A fintech EMI cannot receive or verify FDI capital injections, cannot provide a compliant capital verification platform, and is not recognized by Chinese tax bureaus for statutory tax payment collections. At best, an offshore EMI may assist the Hong Kong parent with treasury cash visibility outside mainland China, but it cannot serve as a substitute for the WFOE's onshore Tier-1 bank account. Any representation that a foreign EMI can handle China-side clearing, FX conversion, or dividend distribution would be legally non-viable and AMLD non-compliant.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must remit the equity injection via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's FDI capital account at a designated Tier-1 domestic bank. Turnover: Hong Kong has no statutory size-based audit exemption for ordinary companies. Every non-dormant company incorporated under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), irrespective of turnover, balance-sheet total, gross assets or number of employees, must have its financial statements audited by a certified public accountant under Part 9 of the Companies Ordinance. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required when related-party transactions in a single category exceed RMB 100 million or total related-party transactions exceed RMB 400 million; Master File required when group annual consolidated revenue exceeds RMB 5 billion and cross-border related-party transactions exceed RMB 100 million. Annual Related-Party Transaction Reporting Form required when intercompany transactions exceed RMB 100 million or any cross-border related-party payment exceeds RMB 10 million.

Economic substance scope: Mainland China applies GAAR and substance-over-form rather than Hong Kong or BVI ESR. The WFOE must demonstrate substantive local operations, including property management, leasing administration, financial reporting, and board decision-making. If the Hong Kong intermediate entity lacks CIGA functions, PRC authorities may recharacterize income as attributable to a PRC permanent establishment and impose enterprise income tax plus dividend withholding tax.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: PRC Criminal Law Article 201; administrative penalty of 50% to 5 times the evaded tax; daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%; blacklisting as a major tax violation case.

Director personal liability: Directors who authorized falsified filings may be jointly and severally liable for unpaid tax and penalties if corporate assets are depleted. Criminal liability includes imprisonment up to 7 years and fines; travel bans and exit controls apply under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 44.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the Principal Purpose Test; recharacterization as a conduit; imposition of 10% withholding or 25% permanent establishment taxation plus surcharges; penalty up to 5 times underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63.

Director personal liability: Veil-piercing may attach to directors who signed beneficial ownership declarations for substance-less entities; civil restitution, travel ban, and criminal fraud conviction under Criminal Law Articles 205 or 210 with imprisonment up to 12 years.
Disguised Profit Repatriation / Thin Capitalization

Statutory sanction: Disallowance of deductions, reclassification as dividend subject to 10% withholding, penalty of 50% to 500% of underpaid tax under Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63, plus daily interest.

Director personal liability: Court may pierce the corporate veil under PRC Company Law Article 20 for abuse of corporate form to evade tax; personal restitution, travel bans, and imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines.
