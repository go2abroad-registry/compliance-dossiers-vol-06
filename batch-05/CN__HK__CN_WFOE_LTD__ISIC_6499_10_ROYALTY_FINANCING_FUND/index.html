Stage 03
Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

      
Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

    

    

      
Tax ID Application Infrastructure

      

        
Competent tax authority
State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA), acting through the applicable local competent tax offices and the national Electronic Tax Bureau online portal for corporate income tax, value-added tax, invoice issuance, and export tax refund/exemption administration.

        
E-government filing portal
For the China WFOE: (1) incorporate the entity with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) and obtain the business license containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which serves as the statutory tax identification number in China; (2) rely on the one-license-one-code and multi-certificate integration reform, under which the STA receives incorporation data from SAMR and completes the taxpayer registration file automatically, without a separate VAT identification number application; (3) complete post-registration tax formalities at the competent tax authority, including filing the legal representative details, financial and accounting system, bank account numbers, invoicing control equipment or digital invoicing registration, and any required tax-filing schedule; (4) if the WFOE requires VAT general taxpayer status to issue special VAT invoices or recover input VAT, file the general VAT taxpayer qualification registration through the Electronic Tax Bureau. For a foreign non-resident entity such as the Hong Kong holding shareholder with no Chinese establishment, the non-resident must obtain/update a non-resident tax registration file with the competent tax authority where the Chinese payer is located; China-source income is then brought within the tax net through payer withholding, and the non-resident may claim relief under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement by submitting a Hong Kong residence certificate and the applicable treaty relief documentation.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

      

        
Default tax classification
Entity_Level_Taxation

        
Available election forms
None. The PRC tax system does not provide a check-the-box pass-through election; the WFOE is mandatorily a Chinese resident enterprise taxed at entity level. The Hong Kong shareholder may elect treaty benefits under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement where available, but no hybrid mismatch or check-the-box classification election can alter the WFOE's domestic entity-level taxation.

      

    

    

      
VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

      

        
Standard VAT / GST rate
13%

        
Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)
No economic turnover threshold triggers initial tax registration for an incorporated Chinese WFOE; registration is mandatory and automatic upon SAMR business license issuance. For VAT classification, annual taxable sales above CNY 5,000,000 require mandatory VAT general taxpayer registration; an entity with annual taxable sales at or below CNY 5,000,000 may remain a small-scale taxpayer, subject to the STA rules and voluntary registration options.

      

      
      

        Export 0% zero-rating conditions
        
Goods exported from China qualify for the 0% VAT rate if they are actually declared for export and leave the customs territory under a valid export declaration, the seller is the VAT-registered WFOE, the buyer is an overseas customer, and the export proceeds are received and repatriated within the prescribed period. Qualifying offshore services, including eligible cross-border digital and professional services, may be zero-rated or exempt only when supplied to an overseas recipient, consumed entirely outside China, and listed in or falling under the STA cross-border taxable service catalogue; the WFOE must substantiate the offshore recipient, offshore consumption, and absence of China-source use. General revenue-based financing/royalty income must be carefully analysed, as financial service income is generally within the 6% VAT band unless a specific cross-border exemption or zero-rating rule applies.

China mandates invoice issuance through STA-approved channels, including the nationwide fully digitalized electronic invoice system. Issuance data is transmitted to the Golden Tax platform on a near-real-time basis for validation and monitoring; special VAT invoices used for input VAT credits and export zero-rating must contain the statutory encrypted tax control code, digital stamp/QR code, and unique invoice number, and must be reported electronically through the Electronic Tax Bureau or approved API/ERP interfaces. There is no pre-clearance paper stamp, but the anti-fraud framework automatically matches invoice, customs declaration, and foreign exchange receipt data before export refunds are released.

      
The STA, customs, and SAFE data-matching audit framework cross-checks export declarations, logistics documents, VAT invoices, and inward foreign exchange receipts. If zero-rating is claimed without genuine physical export, without offshore consumption of services, or without timely repatriation of export proceeds, the tax authority will disallow the zero-rating, recover the refunded output VAT, impose late-payment surcharge from the original due date, and may levy an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the underpaid or refunded tax under the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export refund schemes involving false invoices, fabricated customs declarations, or fictitious export transactions are subject to criminal prosecution under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, including imprisonment and monetary fines, with retrospective clawbacks extending to the full refund amount plus statutory interest and penalties.

Stage 04
Banking & Treasury Architecture

      
Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

    

    

      
Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

      

        
Current-account convertibility
China maintains a hybrid current-account convertible and capital-account controlled regime. For a WFOE engaged in royalty-based revenue financing, imports of services and repatriation of after-tax profits are treated as current account items and are generally freely convertible with real transactional documents, including contracts, invoices, and tax certificates. Capital account operations, including inbound equity investment and foreign debt, require SAFE registration, a dedicated foreign exchange capital account, and post-settlement verification. The RMB and foreign currency are not freely convertible on the market for capital purposes; every cross-border capital movement must flow through the bank's supervision system. Profit remittance requires an annual audit, tax declaration, tax clearance, and board resolution. The central bank monitors the use of capital via payment-after-verification systems. Consequently, treasury planning must anchor on SAFE pre-registration, careful use of investment purpose codes, and verified underlying trade documentation. Foreign exchange hedging is permitted only with a genuine underlying exposure.

        
Export proceeds surrender rules
The law requires physical repatriation of export receipts to a designated domestic foreign exchange account within 180 days of customs declaration or service performance; enterprises may then retain the foreign exchange or convert to RMB at their commercial discretion. There is no mandatory conversion ratio under the current SAFE scheme, but all export receipts must appear in the bank's reconciliation system, and any shortfall must be explained with supporting trade or service documentation. Service export proceeds for royalty financing must be supported by contract, tax certificate, and payment evidence, and all such receipts must flow through a licensed Chinese commercial bank.

      

      
Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with SAFE as the implementing enforcement agency

            Airwallex
            Fit: High

        Inbound equity capital is wired from the Hong Kong parent to the WFOE's SAFE-designated Foreign Exchange Capital Account at a licensed Chinese bank using SWIFT MT103. After arrival, funds are credited to the capital account, verified by the bank against SAFE's system, and a CPA issues a capital verification report. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

      
Master / Local File threshold: Local File documentation is compulsory under SAT Announcement [2016] No. 42 if related-party transfers of tangible assets exceed RMB 200 million, intangible property transfers exceed RMB 100 million, financial asset transfers exceed RMB 100 million, or related-party services exceed RMB 40 million annually. Master File is required if the total consolidated related-party transaction amount equals or exceeds RMB 1 billion. Annual Chapter 15 forms and contemporaneous documentation must be filed with the annual CIT return.

      
Economic substance scope: China's Economic Substance Doctrine is embodied in the General Anti-Abuse Rule (Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47) and the special anti-avoidance rules on beneficial ownership and treaty abuse. For the Hong Kong affiliate, the tax authorities assess Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) to verify that royalty, financing, and holding functions are actually performed in Hong Kong and not routed solely for treaty or tax avoidance. If the Hong Kong entity lacks the necessary personnel, office, decision-making autonomy, and risk management, any DTA claim is disallowed. For the Chinese WFOE, the entity must maintain substantive operational presence in China to support the revenue-based financing model and related-party contracts; the absence of such substance triggers GAAR recharacterization and potential denial of deductions.

      

    

    

      
Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

      
      

        Treaty Abuse / Shell Company Arrangement
        
Statutory sanction: Denial of treaty benefits under the PRC-HK Double Taxation Arrangement Article 8 and the Principal Purpose Test under BEPS measures; imposition of the statutory 10% withholding tax instead of the treaty rate; a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge; and recharacterization of royalty or interest payments as taxable dividends under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 47. The tax authority may further impose a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or cause the filing of false beneficial-ownership documentation may be personally liable for unpaid taxes and penalties if the corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20. They may be subject to travel bans and blacklisting under the tax credit system, and criminal prosecution for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 may lead to imprisonment of up to seven years plus fines.

      

      
      

        Transfer Pricing Manipulation of Royalties and Service Fees
        
Statutory sanction: Tax authority adjustment under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Implementation Regulations Article 109 to the arm's-length price; recovery of underpaid tax plus a 5% annual interest adjustment on the shortfall under special tax adjustment rules; an additional penalty of up to 5 times the tax evaded if the transfer pricing arrangement is deemed fraudulent or intentionally misleading; and public disclosure as an avoidance scheme.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who are officers of the WFOE and who knowingly execute excessive royalty contracts may face personal civil restitution for damages caused to minority shareholders or creditors under PRC Company Law. If the tax authority finds that the directors used the corporate structure to evade tax, they may be disqualified from directorship, restricted from leaving the country, and criminally liable under Criminal Law Article 201 for tax evasion, with penalties ranging from fines to imprisonment of up to seven years, depending on the amount involved.

      

      
      

        Thin Capitalization / Artificial Debt Financing
        
Statutory sanction: Disallowance of the excessive interest deduction under Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 46 and Implementation Regulations Article 119; reclassification of the excessive interest as a deemed dividend distribution subject to 10% withholding tax; imposition of a 0.05% daily late-payment surcharge; and further penalties of 50% to 5 times the tax evaded if the financing arrangement is found to be fraudulent under Article 63 of the Law on Tax Collection and Administration.

        
Director personal liability: Directors who approve the artificial financing scheme while knowing there is no genuine repayment capacity may be held personally liable for debt obligations if the court disregards the corporate separation under PRC Company Law Article 20. The tax authority may also issue a blacklisting travel ban preventing the directors from obtaining exit approval until tax liabilities are settled. Criminal liability includes imprisonment for tax evasion under Criminal Law Article 201 and a concurrent fine, especially when the amount of tax evaded is large and the directors orchestrated the arrangement.
