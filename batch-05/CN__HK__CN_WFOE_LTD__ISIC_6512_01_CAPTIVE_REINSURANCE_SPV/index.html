Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up share capital or escrow/blocked account requirement exists under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private company limited by shares; the company may be incorporated with a very small subscription consideration. However, before commencing captive reinsurance business, the Insurance Authority (Cap. 41) will impose a risk-based minimum paid-up share capital requirement, commonly HKD 2,000,000 or more for an authorized captive insurer, together with solvency margin requirements, while no blocked escrow account is required at the Companies Registry stage.

Funding & unlock path: The company is not subject to an industrial Civil Defense fire permit because captive reinsurance services do not involve manufacturing or storage of hazardous materials. However, the office premises must comply with the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95), including means of escape, fire service installations, and fire safety management requirements, and with the Occupational Safety and Occupational Health Ordinance (Cap. 509) for general workplace safety. The landlord or service provider should hold the building’s fire certificate or a letter of compliance for the office unit, and an internal workplace risk assessment must be completed before staff commence operations.

Paid-in capital rule: No minimum paid-up share capital or escrow/blocked account requirement exists under the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for a private company limited by shares; the company may be incorporated with a very small subscription consideration. Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) for the mainland Chinese WFOE, using the China Electronic Tax Bureau (E-Tax) national online portal as the electronic filing and tax-administration channel; for the Hong Kong SAR element, Inland Revenue Department (IRD) of Hong Kong, using the eTAX online platform and the Business Registration Office of the IRD for the Business Registration Certificate and the Business Registration Number.

E-government filing portal

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None for the mainland WFOE; China does not operate a check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election, and the WFOE cannot elect out of entity-level corporate income tax. For the Hong Kong captive/intermediate entity, there is no check-the-box regime; the only statutory election available is the group loss relief election under the Inland Revenue Ordinance where the connected-group and Hong Kong-residence conditions are met, while offshore-profits exemption is asserted through the tax return claim process rather than an elective classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales is the mandatory threshold for compulory general VAT taxpayer registration; basic tax registration is required upon incorporation irrespective of turnover. Below RMB 5,000,000 the WFOE may remain a small-scale VAT taxpayer. Hong Kong SAR has no VAT or GST registration threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The mainland WFOE may claim 0% VAT export zero-rating on goods only when it is a VAT general taxpayer, the goods physically leave China under a customs export declaration, the sale is made to an overseas customer, title and risk transfer outside China, the foreign-currency proceeds are collected and repatriated into China within the statutory period, and the export documentation is fully consistent. For cross-border services and qualifying offshore digital services, zero-rating or exemption is available for services on the STA cross-border taxable services catalogue that are supplied to a recipient outside China, wholly consumed outside China, not used by a mainland establishment of the recipient, and paid for from outside China. For captive reinsurance, the WFOE must document the offshore risk, the non-resident insurer or reinsurer recipient, the absence of mainland-source risk retention, and the actual payment flow in foreign currency; if the captive reinsurance service does not qualify, it remains subject to 6% VAT. Hong Kong does not have an indirect-tax zero-rating mechanism.

China maintains a real-time fiscal clearance and anti-fraud invoice-control system through the Full Digital Electronic Invoice pilot and the National VAT Invoice Management and Monitoring System. Taxpayers must transmit invoice issuance data in real time through the Electronic Tax Bureau, the STA-approved tax-control UKey/digital certificate, or application programming interface (API) integration. Every invoice is automatically assigned a unique invoice code and secured verification code at issuance, and buyer-side input VAT credit on special VAT invoices must be confirmed or reported in the national invoice system before the credit can be taken. Hong Kong SAR does not operate a fiscal invoice clearance or e-invoicing mandate; electronic invoices are accepted under the Electronic Transactions Ordinance.

The STA, China Customs, SAFE, and commercial banks triangulate customs declarations, logistics waybills, VAT invoices, export contracts, and bank receipt/remittance records. A zero-rated export claim that is unsubstantiated, contains inconsistent documentation, or fails the 180-day foreign-exchange receipt rule will be disallowed; the refunded tax is clawed back with late-payment interest and an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent export-invoice or tax-refund schemes are referred to the judiciary under Criminal Law Article 204 and may result in criminal fines and imprisonment up to life imprisonment. The retrospective audit period is unlimited where evasion or fraud is established. For the Hong Kong entity, false profits-tax declarations are subject to IRD civil penalties of up to three times the tax undercharged and criminal liability on summary or indictment.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

Mainland China operates a managed floating exchange-rate regime in which RMB is fully convertible for genuine current account purposes, including reinsurance premiums, claims, and operating expenses, provided the bank obtains authenticated underlying documentation. By contrast, capital account items remain heavily controlled; the WFOE must receive shareholder capital through a SAFE-registered FDI capital account, convert currencies from Hong Kong remittances under a legitimate settlement mechanism, and cannot deploy proceeds outside the approved business scope. The captive reinsurance business creates layered flows: premiums received from group insureds are current account inflows requiring policy schedules and actuarial support, while loss payments and profit remittances to the Hong Kong parent are outflows requiring proof of accrued liability, tax clearance, audited financials, and compliance with SAFE's foreign exchange current account verification rules. Dividends may be remitted annually or after an interim audit, but the bank must ensure no illegal capital flight, thin capitalization, or round-tripping is embedded in the structure. All transactions must maintain robust underlying records because SAFE can retroactively reclassify fraudulent current account entries as capital movements and impose fines.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current Trade in Goods FX Verification Framework, export related foreign exchange receipts must be repatriated into a domestic current account FX settlement account within 180 days of the customs declaration date, unless a prior SAFE extension is granted. There is no statutory fixed conversion ratio; an entity may retain the proceeds in foreign currency or voluntarily convert to RMB at any licensed onshore bank. For service trade and reinsurance premium receipts, no surrender formula applies, but the receipt must be collected on the due date stated in the contract and reported to SAFE through the bank. If the enterprise is not listed in the Goods Trade FX Status list, the bank may reject the receipt until registration is completed.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), acting through the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) and the National Financial Regulatory Administration (NFRA) for captive reinsurance licensing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Viable only as an offshore facilitation layer for the Hong Kong parent, not for the CN WFOE. Chinese banking law prohibits foreign EMI licenses from granting account-hosting or deposit-taking services to PRC legal entities; a WFOE cannot maintain its main liquidity, FX settlement, or capital verification cash flows with a non-PRC electronic money institution. Any attempt to use an unlicensed digital EMI for WFOE disbursements will be treated as a PBOC anti-money laundering red flag. The recommended approach is to use licensed Tier-1 Chinese banks onshore and, if desired, the Hong Kong branch of the same bank as the correspondent and clearing counterparty.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital injections from the HK parent must be routed through SWIFT MT103 or ISO 20022 pacs.008 into the WFOE's FX Capital Account at a licensed SAFE-designated bank. Before any remittance, the WFOE must complete the MOFCOM and AMR foreign investment filing and the SAFE FDI registration, obtaining a unique FDI business registration code. The remittance instruction must cite the purpose code 'FDI capital injection' and reference the FDI filing number; no trade invoices are needed for equity capital, but the bank will require the parent board resolution, share subscription agreement, and the WFOE's new business license. After receipt, the bank will quarantine the funds in a supervised capital account until a Chinese certified public accountant issues the capital verification report; only thereafter may the funds be converted into RMB within the permissible foreign exchange settlement framework and deployed for lawful current account expenses. Any conversion above the self-use threshold requires SAFE pre-approval. The entire loop is reported through SAFE's capital project information system; late reporting or mismatch of purpose codes will result in fines and frozen accounts.For captive reinsurance settlements, the primary clearing rail is an SWIFT MT103 remittance under service trade current account rules. The bank will verify the reinsurance contract, claim statement, actuarial adjustment, and the SAFE service trade tax declaration form before debiting the WFOE's RMB or FX account. Where the claim relates to physical cargo in transit, a Commercial Letter of Credit may be used only if the underlying reinsurance policy expressly incorporates a documentary payment undertaking; otherwise, documentary collections are not a standard rail for claim payments. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Viable only as an offshore facilitation layer for the Hong Kong parent, not for the CN WFOE. Chinese banking law prohibits foreign EMI licenses from granting account-hosting or deposit-taking services to PRC legal entities; a WFOE cannot maintain its main liquidity, FX settlement, or capital verification cash flows with a non-PRC electronic money institution. Any attempt to use an unlicensed digital EMI for WFOE disbursements will be treated as a PBOC anti-money laundering red flag. The recommended approach is to use licensed Tier-1 Chinese banks onshore and, if desired, the Hong Kong branch of the same bank as the correspondent and clearing counterparty.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File required where aggregate controlled transaction exceeds HKD 20 million (including reinsurance premiums, commissions, claims reimbursements, management fees, and financing); Master File required where the MNE group's consolidated revenue exceeds HKD 200 million and the Hong Kong entity undertakes ≥ HKD 20 million of intercompany transactions.

Economic substance scope: For the captive reinsurance company to benefit from the 8.25% concessionary rate under the Inland Revenue Ordinance, it must demonstrate that its Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) are conducted in Hong Kong – specifically underwriting planning, risk evaluation, setting of premiums, investment management of reserves, claims management, and governance meetings. The IRD applies a substance-over-form analysis under anti-avoidance provisions (IRO s61A / s61B) and will deny treaty benefits if the entity has no real office, no locally resident staff, and no substantive decision-making. Although Hong Kong has no separate Economic Substance Regulations legislation, the IRD will look to OECD BEPS recommendations and the 2019 EU list requirements; a letterbox captive insurer would be treated as null and subject to severe penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Offshore Profit Omission

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s80(2) and s82 – upon summary conviction, a fine of HK$100,000 and imprisonment for 3 years; upon indictment, a fine of HK$500,000 and imprisonment for 3 years, plus mandatory additional tax of 3 times the amount of profits tax undercharged. In addition, IRD may raise assessments under s60 with a 100% penalty.

Director personal liability: Directors who knowingly sign or authorize false tax returns are personally liable. The IRD may sue the director in tort for the tax shortfall (corporate veil pierced under companies winding-up principles); the court may make a personal civil recovery order attaching the director's personal assets, impose a travel ban (by court injunction to prevent departure), and order disqualification under the Companies Ordinance for up to 15 years. Criminal imprisonment of up to 3 years applies to individual directors.
Transfer Pricing Non-Compliance

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s50AAS(4) and s50AAS(5) – failure to provide documentation attracts a fine of HK$50,000; where an assessment is made based on errors or omissions, an additional 3 times tax undercharged may be imposed under s82A. IRD also has power to apply the arm's length principle and adjust the assessable profits; the adjustment may be appealed but the underlying sanction remains.

Director personal liability: Directors may be held personally liable for the company's tax assessment if they fail to maintain adequate TP documentation and the IRD establishes that the director had actual control and knowledge of the non-arm's length transaction. Civil liability: restoration of the tax shortfall from personal estate. Criminal liability: prosecution for aiding and abetting an offence under IRO s80 – imprisonment up to 3 years; additionally, in cross-border contexts, the Court of Appeal may issue an asset-restraint order against the director's assets.
Shell Company / Insufficient Economic Substance – DTA Abuse

Statutory sanction: Inland Revenue Ordinance s61A (avoidance transactions) and s61B (artificial transactions) – the IRD may disregard the entire arrangement, compute assessable profits as if the transaction had not been entered into, and charge tax at the full 16.5% rate with a penalty up to 3 times the tax avoided (s82A). The Hong Kong Insurance Authority (HKIA) may revoke the captive reinsurance licence under the Insurance Ordinance (Cap. 41) and impose a financial penalty not exceeding HK$5 million for breach of the insurance licensing condition requiring adequate substance.

Director personal liability: Directors are treated as 'shadow directors' if they act on instructions from the parent (Companies Ordinance s2). The corporate veil is pierced under s538 of the Companies Ordinance for fraudulent trading, exposing directors to personal civil liability for the whole amount of tax evaded, plus damages. Criminal consequences: imprisonment for up to 2 years for knowingly carrying on business with intent to defraud creditors; in addition, the court may make a disqualification order (2-15 years), bar the director from managing any company, and freeze personal assets pending trial. Travel bans can be issued by the High Court to preserve jurisdiction.
