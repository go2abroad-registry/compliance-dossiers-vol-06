Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No minimum paid-up capital is required by the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622) for incorporation; a Hong Kong private company may adopt HKD 1 as issued capital. However, for the SFC Type 1 regulated activity (dealing in securities), the licensed corporation must maintain minimum paid-up capital of HKD 5,000,000 and liquid capital of HKD 3,000,000 (or such higher amount imposed by licence conditions) under the Securities and Futures (Financial Resources) Rules (Cap. 571N). No blocked escrow account is required by the Companies Registry, but the paid-up capital must be subscribed in cash, deposited in a licensed bank in Hong Kong, and evidenced by a certified accountant's report.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for industrial fire/civil-defense permits; standard commercial office fire safety compliance under the Fire Services Ordinance (Cap. 95) and Buildings Ordinance (Cap. 123) is required. Employers must carry mandatory employees' compensation insurance and display the Business Registration Certificate; no Dangerous Goods Ordinance storage licence applies.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA) and its Electronic Tax Bureau online portal (etax.chinatax.gov.cn)

E-government filing portal

For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), tax registration is effected automatically through the 'multi-certification integration' business registration process with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR). The Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) granted upon business license issuance serves as the statutory taxpayer identification number (TIN). The entity must complete supplementary tax filing information, including accounting standards, legal representative particulars, and banking details, within 30 days of business license issuance via the Electronic Tax Bureau portal. No separate TIN certificate is issued; the USCC is the single taxpayer identification credential for corporate income tax, VAT, and other taxes. Non-resident enterprises without a permanent establishment in China must separately register for withholding tax purposes with the tax authority where the source income arises.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All entities with taxable business activities in China must register for VAT from commencement of business; mandatory general taxpayer status is triggered when annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000, with voluntary registration available below this threshold.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Zero-rating for exported goods applies where the goods are physically exported under a valid customs export declaration and the taxpayer is a general VAT taxpayer. Cross-border services qualify for zero-rating or VAT exemption where the services are supplied to overseas customers and wholly consumed outside China, supported by written contracts, invoices, and evidence of receipt of foreign currency. Financial and securities brokerage services supplied to non-residents are generally VAT-exempt rather than zero-rated under Caishui [2016] No. 36 where the prescribed conditions are satisfied.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Bill of Entry)
  • Bill of Lading
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC)

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV system requiring real-time reporting of all VAT invoices. Fully digitalised electronic invoices are issued through the STA Unified Invoice Service Platform, with API-based real-time data submission, compulsory cryptographic digital seals, and automatic comparison of invoice data against declared VAT returns.

Fraudulent export zero-rating or VAT refund claims may result in retrospective clawback of the refunded amount, interest, late-payment surcharges, and a penalty of 50% to five times the tax evaded. Criminal liability for fraudulent export tax refunds under Chinese criminal law can attract up to life imprisonment, and the offending taxpayer may be placed on an export tax refund blacklist with suspension of future zero-rating eligibility.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

RMB is fully convertible for current-account transactions: the WFOE may buy or sell foreign exchange for trade-related and operational payments through a designated bank subject to authenticity verification. However, capital-account convertibility remains strictly controlled. Inbound equity capital from the Hong Kong shareholder must be registered with SAFE under the FDI regime, paid into a dedicated foreign currency capital account, and settled into RMB on a payment-contingent basis. The WFOE must comply with the Negative List and use the converted RMB only for permitted operating or investment purposes; any change of use requires a SAFE re-filing. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted as an eligible dividend, but requires audited financial statements, a duly passed board or shareholder resolution, proof of paid-in capital, corporate income tax filing, withholding tax payment certificates, and a bank authenticity check. The HKMA supervises the Hong Kong clearing node without exchange controls; HKD, USD, and RMB clearing is unrestricted but must be executed through an authorised RMB clearing bank or CIPS for cross-border RMB payments. Net-net, the liquidity friction lies in documentation and genuine-underlying inspection, not in legal inconvertibility; current-account flows are largely free, while capital-account flows are gatekept by SAFE.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the SAFE current-account administration regime, there is no mandatory conversion ratio: the WFOE may retain 100% of export foreign currency receipts in its designated foreign exchange account or convert them at a licensed bank. The statutory requirement is that export proceeds be repatriated into China and filed through the bank's electronic system within 30 days after the customs export declaration for goods, or within the contractually documented settlement term for services, not exceeding 90 days without a justified SAFE filing. The receiving bank must complete authenticity verification and system filing within five business days. Late or non-repatriation triggers SAFE investigation, administrative penalties, and loss of fast-track green-channel status for future cross-border transactions.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC), State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) as the complementary clearing and banking supervisor for the Hong Kong treasury node

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Not viable as principal treasury rail and expressly not recommended for this regulated broker entity. Offshore digital EMIs such as Airwallex, multi-currency online account providers, or unlicensed payment processors cannot obtain a PBOC payment institution licence and are legally barred from opening domestic RMB or foreign exchange settlement accounts for a mainland China WFOE. They are also not licensed deposit-taking institutions and cannot hold segregated retail client money under the securities brokerage AML framework. Feasibility rating is Low for inbound FDI capital, client money segregation, and outbound brokerage settlement; a fintech EMI may at best serve as a minor ancillary expense payment processor in a jurisdiction where it holds a valid local licence, but it must never be used for regulated capital flows.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound FDI capital from the Hong Kong shareholder to the mainland WFOE must be routed by SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign currency capital account at a SAFE-licensed domestic bank. The remittance must clearly state the SAFE FDI registration number, the beneficiary's tax identification number, and the foreign exchange purpose code corresponding to equity injection under direct investment. Before the wire, the WFOE must complete SAFE FDI foreign exchange registration and open the foreign currency capital account; after receipt, the bank performs a purpose and authenticity check, converts the capital into RMB only against documented payment needs, and the WFOE must obtain a CPA capital verification report. For the Hong Kong leg, HKD and USD transfers can clear through CHATS or the HK RMB clearing system, while cross-border RMB should be settled through CIPS or via Bank of China (Hong Kong) as the authorised RMB clearing bank.Outbound settlement flows for brokerage-related trade and operating disbursements should be executed on standard corporate banking rails: SWIFT MT103 for telegraphic transfers of commissions, settlement obligations, and client redemptions; irrevocable Commercial Letters of Credit where physical goods trade finance is intermediated; and Documentary Collections such as documents against payment or documents against acceptance for lower-value trade disbursements. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% (standard PRC Corporate Income Tax rate under Article 4 of the PRC Corporate Income Tax Law; applicable to the WFOE as a PRC resident enterprise earning worldwide brokerage income, including offshore CFDs and equities).

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory withholding tax on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident corporate shareholder under Article 37 of the CIT Law; reduced to 5% under the Mainland-China/Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement if the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident company that directly holds at least 25% of the WFOE's share capital and satisfies the look-through/beneficial ownership conditions.
