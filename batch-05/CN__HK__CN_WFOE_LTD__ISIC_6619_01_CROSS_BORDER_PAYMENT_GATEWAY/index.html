Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online filing through the STA's official electronic tax service platform (e-Tax) and the nationwide taxpayer registry; business registration and Unified Social Credit Code records are administered jointly with the Administration for Market Regulation.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Incorporate the WFOE and obtain the business license; the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) on the license is the statutory taxpayer identification number for all PRC taxes. Step 2: Complete tax registration for tax affairs through the e-Tax platform within 30 days of license issuance, filing articles of association, legal representative identity, ultimate beneficial owner information, registered address, accounting standards and bank settlement account. Step 3: Await the STA's confirmation of taxpayer status, VAT type (general or small-scale taxpayer) and filing period. Step 4: For a foreign non-resident parent company with PRC-source income, the WFOE must act as withholding agent and, where necessary, apply for or record the parent entity's non-resident tax identification at the same authority before remitting dividends, interest, royalties or offshore service fees. A WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise subject to entity-level taxation on worldwide income at the 25% corporate income tax rate; there is no pass-through or ring-fencing election.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None. The PRC has no check-the-box election for entity classification and no hybrid mismatch election. The only tax-status election is the VAT small-scale taxpayer election (available below RMB 5,000,000 annual taxable sales) versus general VAT taxpayer status; this is an indirect-tax election and does not change CIT entity classification.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

0.13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for basic VAT registration: every WFOE must register for VAT from commencement. The mandatory general VAT taxpayer threshold is RMB 5,000,000 in annual taxable sales (exclusive of VAT); below that level, small-scale taxpayer treatment is available, and the small-scale simplified VAT rate for payment gateway services is 3% subject to temporary reliefs. Above RMB 5,000,000, general taxpayer registration is compulsory and domestic payment services are charged at 6%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% VAT applies only where the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, the exported goods are declared in its own name at Chinese customs, the goods physically leave China, and the export proceeds are received and repatriated through a Chinese bank. For cross-border services, zero-rating is limited to the statutory list of exported services in the STA cross-border taxable services rules, including international transportation, research and development, design, software, information system services, circuit design and testing, and specified technology services; the service must be supplied to an overseas unit, consumed outside China, properly invoiced, and supported by foreign-exchange receipt. A cross-border payment gateway service is ordinarily a financial or payment service that falls into the VAT exemption category rather than the zero-rated category. Therefore, the WFOE should apply the correct 0% rate only if the payment gateway can be documented as an enumerated software or information-system service; otherwise, the compliant indirect tax treatment for the exported payment service is VAT exemption, supported by contract, delivery evidence, invoice and inward remittance documentation.

  • China Customs Export Declaration generated through the China Customs Single Window (the export customs declaration is the Chinese equivalent of a Bill of Entry; it shows the declared goods, HS codes and value matching the VAT invoice)
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill, multimodal or rail/road consignment note evidencing physical export of the goods
  • Bank inward foreign-exchange proceeds certificate or export proceeds repatriation statement from a Chinese bank (the China FIRC-equivalent) evidencing receipt of payment from the overseas buyer
  • For cross-border services: signed cross-border service agreement, service completion report, VAT e-invoice or receipt, and bank credit advice confirming payment from the overseas recipient
  • Electronic zero-rating or export rebate claim submitted through the STA e-Tax export module, matched to customs and foreign-exchange data

The STA requires issuance of VAT invoices through its certified VAT invoice control system and the Golden Tax System, with real-time upload of invoice data for anti-fraud verification. Special VAT invoices must be matched on the national invoice verification platform before input VAT is creditable. Fully digitalized electronic invoices are generated through the STA e-invoice platform with an official digital fiscal identification code, anti-counterfeit QR code and automatic real-time reporting. Export zero-rating and exemption claims are processed through the STA e-Tax export tax rebate subsystem, cross-checked against China Customs Single Window export data; there is no paper fiscal stamp, but the digital clearance and invoice reconciliation are mandatory.

The STA, China Customs and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange perform joint data cross-checks on export declarations, VAT invoice data, foreign-exchange receipts and financial statements. Failure to substantiate the export or repatriation entitles the tax authority to claw back the zero-rated benefit or refund, impose late-payment surcharge at 0.05% per day, and assess a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax involved under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection. Fraudulent export zero-rating or false customs/foreign-exchange documentation may trigger criminal liability for export tax fraud and false VAT invoice offences; for fraudulent conduct there is no limitation period for reassessment, and the responsible officers may also be pursued personally.

Stage 04

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The Chinese RMB is fully convertible on the current account; trade in goods and services, dividends, royalties, and interest can be settled without prior approval, but banks must perform genuineness verification of supporting documents. The capital account remains administratively restricted: foreign direct investment, portfolio flows, cross-border loans, and inter-company financing require SAFE registration, or PBOC approval in certain debt cases. For a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE), equity contribution from the Hong Kong parent must be injected through a designated foreign exchange capital account and released only after capital verification and SAFE filing. Profit remittance is permissible after annual statutory audit, tax settlement, and submission of board resolution, audited financials, and tax payment certificates to the bank; no separate central bank approval is required for current account profit transfers. However, capital account conversion of the paid-in capital into RMB follows the 'payment-forward' negative list regime, meaning settled RMB cannot be used for prohibited on-lending, securities investment, or real estate speculation. The practical hurdles include cumulative foreign exchange filing requirements, bank-level document pre-screening, and periodic SAFE verification exercises. Since 2012, the mandatory export surrender requirement has been abolished; exporters may retain all foreign exchange receipts in their settlement accounts or convert to RMB at any time based on market rates, subject to transaction authenticity checks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory timeline requires export proceeds to be repatriated to China within 90 days from the date of export customs declaration, or within an extended period approved by SAFE in justified cases (usually up to 180 days). The mandatory conversion ratio is 0% – there is no requirement to surrender a fixed percentage of export foreign currency earnings to the central bank. All proceeds must be received into an onshore foreign exchange settlement account at a licensed domestic bank; the bank will match the incoming SWIFT with the customs declaration and tax export rebate records before allowing the company to credit the amount to its operational account or convert to RMB.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Airwallex Fit: High

Low viability. Offshore digital EMI platforms such as Revolut Business, Airwallex, and Payoneer are not licensed by PBOC or SAFE to provide domestic payment accounts, clearing, or corporate treasury services directly to Chinese legal entities. A WFOE operating as a cross-border payment gateway must onshore its collection and disbursement through a domestic Tier-1 bank connected to CNAPS, CIPS, and SWIFT. Foreign EMIs can be used only as ancillary receivable channels for overseas merchant collections, but funds must be repatriated into the domestic bank account through a licensed money service route. The regulatory boundaries are strict: any entity providing payment aggregation or acquiring services within mainland China must obtain a Payment Business License from PBOC, and foreign unlicensed providers are barred from offering local settlement in RMB.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent remits equity funding via SWIFT MT103 to the WFOE's designated foreign exchange capital account at a SAFE-licensed domestic bank. The remittance must clearly state the transaction purpose code for FDI capital injection and reference the 'Foreign Exchange Registration Certificate' issued by SAFE. Upon receipt, the bank validates the remitter's identity against the registered overseas investor, confirms the incoming amount against the approved FDI table of contributions, and performs capital verification through SAFE's Capital Project Information System. The capital may be settled into RMB only after submission of the Payment Confirmation and the 'Negative List Compliance Statement'. Subsequent working capital disbursements from the capital account must be traced via the bank's payment-forward system; funds cannot be used for inter-company loans, securities investments, or real estate purchases unless explicitly approved.The WFOE will use SWIFT MT103 for cross-border service fees, royalties, and technology licensing payments; Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700) for verified import trade; and Documentary Collections (MT400/MT422) for invoice-based disbursements where LC terms are not required. Every outbound transfer must be supported by operating contracts, original invoices, customs declarations (for goods), and tax withholding vouchers filed with the local tax bureau. The bank will screen each beneficiary against sanctions lists and verify the purpose code (e.g., 'Trade in Services', 'Payment of Royalties', 'Freight Charges'). For RMB-denominated cross-border payments, the clearing route will use CIPS or CNAPS to avoid SWIFT-dependent USD conversion; for USD/EUR payments, SWIFT MT103 is mandatory. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Local File (TP documentation) under Announcement [2016] No. 42 must be prepared where the aggregate annual amount of cross-border related-party transactions entered into by the WFOE is greater than RMB 100 million, or where domestic related-party transactions exceed RMB 200 million. A Master File is required where the aggregate annual related-party turnover reaches RMB 1 billion, or where the WFOE executes transactions involving a foreign related party of significant size. A contemporaneous TP report with a benchmarking study and ICAMA/settlement flow analysis is expected for a payment gateway, especially for intra-group transaction fees, technology royalties, and financing costs.

Economic substance scope: China applies an economic-substance doctrine through Article 10 of the Enterprise Income Tax Law (arm’s length principle) and Articles 47–48 (GAAR), so a WFOE with no real personnel, premises, or decision-making functions may have profits adjusted or transactions restructured. If the HK parent holds only a passive investment or licenses IP without substantive headquarters, financing, or risk-management functions, Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) may also apply to the HK entity and require an adequate economic presence in Hong Kong; a WFOE itself is not subject to Hong Kong ESR, but its contracts and actual operations must be consistent with the residence, control, and purpose of the HK shareholder.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Corporate Income Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Additional tax at 25% on the under-assessed amount, daily late surcharge of 0.05%, and a penalty of 50%–500% of the evaded tax. Under the Criminal Law, evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 is punishable by imprisonment for up to seven years, depending on gravity; the company may also be blacklisted and lose its payment institution license.

Director personal liability: Directors, supervisors, and actual controllers are personally liable for restitution costs of the company’s underpaid tax, face exit bans order of the tax and customs authorities, may be disqualified as directors of licensed entities, and may be sentenced to imprisonment under Article 201 (tax evasion) and Article 31 (unit crime) of the PRC Criminal Law.
Shell Company / Lack of Economic Substance

Statutory sanction: The Chinese tax authorities may disregard the HK entity under GAAR, treat profits as Chinese-source and taxable to the WFOE, and reject treaty relief, thus levying CIT at 25% and statutory dividend WHT at 10%, together with interest and penalty of 50%–500%. In Hong Kong, the Inland Revenue and the Financial Secretary may reject the HB/HT entity status or apply a denial of tax benefits.

Director personal liability: The directors and beneficial owners of the shell are jointly and severally liable to the WFOE’s tax debt if they intentionally created the substance shell to avoid tax; travel bans and an order to enter personal appearances before the State Taxation Administration may be imposed, and fraudulent abuse can constitute criminal tax evasion punishable by up to seven years’ imprisonment.
Transfer Pricing Manipulation Involving HK Parent

Statutory sanction: Adjustment under Article 41 CIT Law: the tax authority recalculates WFOE’s taxable income using TNMM or profit split and imposes a penalty of 50%–500% on the additional tax, plus 0.05% per-day interest; for cross-border related-party transactions, the statutory adjustment period is five years but may extend under fraud; failure to provide documentation also triggers administrative fines under Announcement [2016] No. 42.

Director personal liability: Directors who approved false TP documentation risk civil liability for the tax adjustment; serious manipulation with intent to evade tax invokes personal joint liability, prohibition from leaving mainland China, and imprisonment for tax evasion.
AML / Money Laundering Through Payment Gateway

Statutory sanction: Under the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law and measures of PBoC and SAFE, the WFOE may be fined up to RMB 5 million, have its payment license revoked, and the responsible personnel may be imposed personal fines. Funds can be confiscated; criminal money laundering is punishable at sentences of up to ten years’ imprisonment; Payment service liabilities may be enforced against the WFOE’s dedicated bank settlement account and merchant security deposit.

Director personal liability: Directors and compliance officers are personally criminally liable for knowing participation in money laundering; they are jointly and severally punished by travel bans, asset freezing, civil restitution to affected merchants or funds, and prison terms of up to ten years under Article 191 of the PRC Criminal Law.
