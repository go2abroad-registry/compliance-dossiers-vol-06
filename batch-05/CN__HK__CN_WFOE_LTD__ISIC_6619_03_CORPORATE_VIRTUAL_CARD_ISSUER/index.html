Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China, accessed through the Electronic Tax Administration (E-Tax) portal at etax.chinatax.gov.cn, with provincial STA e-tax bureaus processing corporate registrations, VAT filings, invoice issuance, and export refunds.

E-government filing portal

Step 2: The AMR business license bears the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the STATUTORY TAXPAYER IDENTIFICATION NUMBER for the WFOE. Step 3: Within 30 calendar days of obtaining the business license, log onto the Electronic Tax Administration portal and complete the initial tax registration by submitting certificate of incorporation, articles of association, registered office lease, legal representative identity documents, shareholder structure, and local bank account details. Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All established WFOEs must complete tax registration with the STA. For VAT classification, an enterprise mainly engaged in financial or other modern services with annual taxable turnover above RMB 5,000,000 must register as a general VAT taxpayer; below that threshold it may remain a small-scale taxpayer. Small-scale taxpayers currently benefit from a monthly sales exemption of RMB 100,000, but a licensed corporate virtual card issuer engaged in financial services will normally be required to be a general VAT taxpayer due to the high-risk licensed nature and cross-border card scheme flows.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods zero-rating: VAT-registered exporters must physically export goods from the PRC customs territory under customs supervision and retain valid export customs declarations; the overseas buyer must be a non-resident party and the sale must be evidenced by contract, commercial invoice, and foreign exchange collection. Cross-border services zero-rating or exemption: qualifying services, including certain financial and payment-adjacent services supplied to recipients outside mainland China and consumed entirely outside mainland China, may qualify under Caishui [2016] No. 36 and related STA announcements; the entity must evidence the cross-border nature of the service, the overseas status of the service recipient, actual offshore consumption, and collection of the service consideration in foreign exchange. For a virtual card issuer, only card services genuinely supplied to overseas cardholders or offshore card schemes may assert zero-rating, while domestic onshore card issuing and spend-management services remain subject to 6% VAT.

Mandatory use of the State Taxation Administration's all-digital electronic invoice platform, accessed through the E-Tax portal and operating under the Golden Tax Phase IV supervision architecture. Every invoice must be issued through the tax-control/e-invoice platform, carry a unique electronic invoice number and QR anti-fraud stamp, and be reported to the STA in real time at issuance. The tax authority automatically cross-matches output invoices, input VAT deduction certificates, corporate income tax filings, customs declarations, and bank settlement data. Any invoice issued outside the official electronic clearance channel is invalid for input VAT credit and export zero-rating substantiation.

The STA, General Administration of Customs, and State Administration of Foreign Exchange conduct cross-agency data matching on all zero-rated transactions. If export proceeds are not repatriated within the 180-day statutory period without a valid SAFE extension, or if the customs and foreign exchange documentation bundle fails audit, the claimed zero-rating is nullified and the output VAT is clawed back together with input VAT refunds previously paid. Late payment surcharges accumulate daily at 0.05% on the underpaid tax. Administrative penalties for fraudulent or unverified zero-rating range from 50% to five times the tax amount under the Law on Administration of Tax Collection; serious cases of false export declarations and export VAT fraud constitute criminal tax crimes under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China, punishable by imprisonment, fines, and confiscation of property.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is convertible for current account items, including trade settlements, service payments, and dividend remittances, subject to the presentation of underlying documents and bank verification through SAFE's cross-border income and expense monitoring system. Capital account items, however, remain subject to regulatory approval or registration mandates: an inbound foreign direct investment from the Hong Kong parent into the mainland WFOE requires prior registration on SAFE's FDI information system, the opening of a dedicated foreign currency capital account, and completion of a capital verification before any conversion into RMB for operational purposes. Profit remittance to Hong Kong is normally permitted after the annual statutory audit, comprehensive tax clearance, and a formal equity distribution resolution; the bank executes the remittance without mandatory conversion, but it must record the appropriate payment purpose code and file board minutes, audited financial statements, and the foreign exchange registration certificate. In practice, the WFOE cannot freely convert RMB for offshore investment, related-party lending, unsubstantiated royalty outflows, or settlement of crypto-asset obligations, and any such plan would either be rejected by the bank or require a specific PBOC/SAFE authorization. Treasury management must therefore keep current account flows fully documented and segregate capital injections into strictly monitored capital account usage in line with the registered business purpose.

Export proceeds surrender rules

The statutory repatriation regime for export receipts requires a mainland exporter to bring eligible foreign currency earnings onshore through a designated bank within 180 days from the date of customs declaration, unless a special SAFE exemption or longer collection period applies. The former mandatory surrender ratio has been abolished and replaced by voluntary settlement, so the WFOE may retain 100% of verified export proceeds in its foreign currency settlement account or sell the foreign currency to the bank at the prevailing market rate. For single disbursements above an equivalent of USD 1 million, additional supporting documents, customs records, and cargo tracking details may be required to complete the cross-border receipt verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and the State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are neither licensed nor chartered by the PBOC or SAFE to act as a banking or payment settlement account provider for a mainland-registered WFOE. A Chinese legal entity may not lawfully keep its primary treasury or virtual-card clearing balances with an unlicensed foreign EMI because such an arrangement would circumvent the PBOC Payment Institution Administrative Rules and the cross-border foreign exchange verification system. A WFOE may use such EMIs only as very limited ancillary interfaces supported by a licensed Chinese third-party payment institution, and even then the funds must ultimately pass through a domestic bank account holding the master settlement position. Feasibility rating is therefore not viable for primary treasury settlement; legal and operational risk is high, and account closure risk persists.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent should originate the capital injection through a SWIFT MT103 wire into the WFOE's foreign currency capital account, with the beneficiary field showing the WFOE's name, SAFE FDI registration code, and the explicit purpose code for foreign investor equity contribution. The payment message must state that the transfer is an equity injection into a wholly foreign-owned enterprise and must not use vague narration. After the funds are credited, the WFOE must arrange a capital verification in accordance with the joint verification rules and submit the CPA confirmation to the bank before converting the foreign currency into RMB for operating expenses. If the injection is denominated in RMB, the remittance can be settled through CIPS using the corresponding RMB investment code; however, SAFE monitoring still applies and the bank will require a clear equity contract and board resolution.Trade payments and operational settlements from the WFOE should be routed through the domestic bank's trade finance desk. For direct cash disbursements, use SWIFT MT103 with the appropriate tax withholding and payment purpose code, attaching commercial invoice, contract, and service verification documents. For physical goods, issue a Commercial Letter of Credit under UCP600 through the sponsoring bank, or use Documentary Collections under URC522. E-commerce / gateway settlement fit: Offshore digital EMIs such as Wise Business, Revolut Business, or Payoneer are neither licensed nor chartered by the PBOC or SAFE to act as a banking or payment settlement account provider for a mainland-registered WFOE. A Chinese legal entity may not lawfully keep its primary treasury or virtual-card clearing balances with an unlicensed foreign EMI because such an arrangement would circumvent the PBOC Payment Institution Administrative Rules and the cross-border foreign exchange verification system. A WFOE may use such EMIs only as very limited ancillary interfaces supported by a licensed Chinese third-party payment institution, and even then the funds must ultimately pass through a domestic bank account holding the master settlement position. Feasibility rating is therefore not viable for primary treasury settlement; legal and operational risk is high, and account closure risk persists.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Contemporaneous Local File must be prepared when annual related-party transfers of tangible assets exceed RMB 200 million, transfers of financial assets or loans exceed RMB 100 million, transfers of intangible assets exceed RMB 20 million, or related-party service transactions exceed RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when aggregate annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 1 billion.

Economic substance scope: The WFOE must demonstrate real economic activity in China, including core value-creation functions such as software development, card issuing platform management, compliance and AML controls, card network connectivity, risk management, and client servicing. The Hong Kong entity seeking treaty benefits must separately satisfy beneficial ownership substance standards and, to the extent applicable, Hong Kong Economic Substance Regulations with Core Income-Generating Activities, including active management and operational supervision of its shareholding, intellectual property, and financing functions.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Transfer Pricing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Administration Law Article 63 authorises recovery of underpaid tax, daily late surcharge, and a fine of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded; special tax adjustments may recharacterise the transactions and impute taxable income with a ten-year look-back; Criminal Law Article 201 criminalises the conduct where the amount of tax evaded is material.

Director personal liability: Directors and the legal representative who knowingly directed the fraudulent filing are 'directly responsible persons' and may be sentenced to up to seven years' imprisonment with criminal fines. Civil asset recovery may attach to directors who commingled corporate and personal assets; social credit blacklisting imposes a travel ban, restriction on leaving China, denial of bank credit, and suspension of professional licences.
Shell Company Structure and Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: The Principal Purpose Test in the China-HK Double Tax Arrangement and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule under Article 47 of the Tax Administration Law deny treaty benefits and recharacterise the transaction as if the Hong Kong shell did not exist. The WFOE will be assessed for unpreserved withholding tax, interest, and penalties; the Hong Kong entity may lose its Hong Kong tax resident benefits in the relevant years.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed false beneficial-ownership declarations or created sham substance documentation are jointly and severally liable for the additional withholding tax and penalties; in egregious cases they may be prosecuted for tax evasion and face imprisonment, travel bans, and asset freezing; civil piercing of the veil is available where the directors used the corporate shell for personal gain.
Unlicensed Payment Operations and AML Compliance Failure

Statutory sanction: Unlicensed payment activity is treated as illegal business operation under Criminal Law Article 225 and may trigger confiscation of illegal income and fines; money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191 attracts severe custodial penalties; regulatory enforcement by the People's Bank of China can result in suspension of operations, revocation of licences, administrative fines, and prohibition on future financial activity.

Director personal liability: Directors, legal representatives, and compliance officers who knowingly engaged in the unlawful payment scheme may be prosecuted for illegal business operation with imprisonment up to ten years and criminal fines; money laundering offences carry imprisonment and personal confiscation; additional sanctions include social credit blacklisting, travel bans, and personal asset seizure through the corporate-veil piercing doctrine where corporate accounts were used as personal instruments.
