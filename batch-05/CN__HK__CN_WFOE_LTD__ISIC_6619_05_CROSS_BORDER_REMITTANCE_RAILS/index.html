Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

None. Hong Kong private companies limited by shares have no statutory minimum paid-up capital and no blocked escrow account requirement. The subscribers may set the nominal capital in the Articles of Association, and each allotted share must be fully paid or treated as fully paid by cash or non-cash consideration. The Company must have at least one shareholder, at least one natural-person director aged 18 or above, and a company secretary who is an individual resident in Hong Kong or a corporate entity having a registered office or place of business in Hong Kong.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable to industrial factories, warehouses or assembly plants. Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic taxpayer services through the STA e-Tax platform (chinatax.gov.cn) and the in-charge local tax authority where the WFOE is registered

E-government filing portal

The WFOE obtains its Unified Social Credit Code (USCC) from the State Administration for Market Regulation at incorporation; the USCC is automatically transmitted to the STA and serves as the statutory Taxpayer Identification Number for CIT, VAT, withholding and all other national taxes. Tax registration is completed through the one-stop licence issuance procedure within 30 days of the business licence date; no separate TIN application is required for the WFOE. The legal representative and appointed tax officer must activate the STA e-Tax account using real-name identity verification, then confirm filing periods, tax payment methods and VAT taxpayer status. A foreign non-resident parent or service provider with no PRC establishment is not required to obtain a local TIN for remittance settlement; however, if that non-resident derives China-source income, the WFOE as payer must complete non-resident withholding registration with the in-charge tax authority and secure a withholding identification code for the non-resident recipient.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All enterprises must register for tax within 30 days of business licence issuance regardless of turnover; mandatory general VAT taxpayer registration applies once annual taxable turnover exceeds RMB 5,000,000 in any rolling 12-month period. Below that threshold, the VAT small-scale taxpayer simplified-levy regime applies.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

For goods, 0% export VAT applies where the WFOE physically exports goods from the PRC under an accepted electronic customs export declaration, transfers title to a non-PRC buyer, and collects export proceeds in foreign currency through a designated foreign exchange bank. For services, China zero-rates only cross-border taxable services expressly listed in the STA zero-rating catalogue, such as international transport, research and development, design, software and certain information technology services supplied to overseas clients and wholly consumed outside the PRC. This money transmission and remittance agency service is an auxiliary financial service and, absent an STA confirmation of zero-rating catalogue inclusion, is generally classified under the cross-border VAT exemption catalogue rather than the 0% rate; consequently, no export VAT refund is claimable unless the specific service is confirmed as zero-rated.

  • Customs Export Declaration (Export Bill of Entry) or approved electronic customs export manifest
  • Bill of Lading, air waybill or other transport document evidencing physical export from the PRC
  • Inward Remittance Certificate / bank-issued foreign exchange receipt evidencing repatriation of export proceeds
  • Cross-border service contract, original VAT invoice, and overseas-party consumption confirmation for qualifying offshore services
  • SAFE export collection verification/write-off record proving receipt of foreign currency within the statutory deadline

China operates the Golden Tax Phase IV electronic invoicing and fiscal clearance system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA electronic invoice service platform, with near-real-time transmission of invoice issuance, red-letter reversal and voidance data to the tax authority. Full Digital Electronic Invoices carry an STA-issued unique invoice number, QR code and encrypted digital stamp; API integration with the e-invoice platform is required for high-volume issuers. No third-party invoice clearing, manual stamping or paper fiscalisation is recognised as an alternative.

Where the STA determines that export zero-rating or VAT exemption was claimed without actual export, without qualifying service classification, or without timely foreign-currency repatriation within the 180-day window, the STA will disallow the zero-rating, retrospectively restore output VAT, impose daily late-payment surcharge, and may treat the matter as export-tax fraud if the declaration is false. Penalties include fines ranging from 50% up to five times the tax involved (one to five times for fraudulent export VAT refunds), suspension or revocation of export tax refund registration, joint liability for directors and beneficial owners where wrongdoing is established, and referral to criminal authorities under the PRC Criminal Law export-fraud provisions.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China's FX regime distinguishes rigorously between current account and capital account convertibility. Current account items, such as trade-related settlement and service fees, enjoy liberal convertibility subject to genuine transaction verification. By contrast, capital account items—including FDI, cross-border loans, and portfolio investments—require case-by-case SAFE registration and quota approvals. For a WFOE engaged in cross-border money transmission, the underlying transaction is a fee-generating service to migrant workers and hence falls within current account service payments, but the creation of a floating RMB flash account, pooling of aggregated settlement funds, and any intercompany treasury operations may trigger capital account treatment. Moreover, all FX receipts must be settled through designated foreign currency bank accounts under the Foreign Exchange Accounts and Security Statement basis, and no automatic conversion is allowed without a verified trade or payment document. Profit remittance to the HK parent requires audited financial statements, a tax clearance certificate from the State Taxation Administration, and evidence that the source of foreign exchange is realized from genuine operational earnings. Therefore, practical convertibility is high for trade-related flows but low for unrestricted treasury movement without supporting documentation.

Export proceeds surrender rules

China abolished the mandatory export surrender ratio in 2012. Nevertheless, SAFE regulations impose a 90-day repatriation deadline for export proceeds, counting from the date of export customs declaration. For remittance service enterprises that aggregate small individual flows, the proceeds are classified as trade-in-services receipts, which must be settled in RMB or retained in foreign exchange only if the institution can prove an equivalent future foreign currency payment need. The mandatory conversion ratio for high-risk Expatriate Worker Remittance agencies is effectively 100% where a bank has insufficient confidence in the company's foreign currency cash flow plan; such a policy is enforced via the bank's internal compliance unit under a safe harbour framework using the applied current-account conversion rate.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) of the People's Republic of China, operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Revolut Fit: High

Not legally viable. Offshore digital EMIs (e.g., Revolut Business, N26) are not licensed banking institutions in China and cannot open a resident foreign-exchange account or access CNAPS (China National Advanced Payment System). A WFOE remittance licensee must maintain all settlement positions in a licensed domestic bank and clear through the PBOC's second-generation payment system. Offshore EMIs may provide ancillary reconciliation tools only, and any contractual arrangement to hold client payouts on-chain or in non-PBOC rails is a supervisory violation.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound registered capital from HK parent is classified as FDI. The process: 1) Register with MOFCOM and obtain a Foreign Investment Enterprise Certificate; 2) Open a foreign exchange capital project account with the tier-1 bank, obtaining the SAFE FDI Control Information Verification number; 3) The inbound wire must be denominated in HKD or USD, identifiable with purpose code FDI-01 (establishment of enterprises); 4) After the wire is received, the bank downloads the SWIFT MT103, performs a post-booking verification against the SAFE registration, and issues a Foreign Exchange Registration & Reporting Certificate; 5) The inbound money is credited into the Capital Settlement Account; then the company's accountant must instruct a payment to the Basic Deposit Account after providing an invoice and M/T documentation. Subsequently, CNY conversion is permitted only after a tax certificate is provided, and a certified public accountant (CPA) issues a capital verification report to be filed with State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR).For trade-related payments (e.g., license fees, sub-contracted services abroad), use SWIFT MT103 with purpose code 223022 for service trade. For physical goods settlements, documentary collections (DP/DA) or Commercial Letters of Credit (L/C) with confirmation by HSBC or Bank of China (Hong Kong) are used. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard Corporate Income Tax rate

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% withholding under PRC CIT law on dividends paid by a PRC enterprise to a non-resident enterprise; the China-Hong Kong tax arrangement provides a 5% rate subject to 25% shareholding, a valid Tax Residency Certificate, and satisfaction of beneficial ownership and anti-abuse criteria.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

For a calendar-year WFOE, annual CIT return must be filed and paid by 31 May of the following year; quarterly provisional returns are due by 15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January. Non-calendar fiscal years follow the same 15-day quarterly rule and 5-month annual rule measured from the fiscal year-end.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: The WFOE must prepare a Local File / contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation if in a single fiscal year it has related-party tangible asset transfers above RMB 200 million, intangible asset or financial asset transfers above RMB 100 million, or intercompany services and other payments above RMB 40 million. A Master File is required when the group satisfies the consolidated revenue and related-party transaction thresholds under STA Announcement No. 42 of 2016, and the annual related-party transaction reporting form must be filed when any threshold is met.

Economic substance scope: China applies substance-over-form and the General Anti-Avoidance Rule: the WFOE must maintain physical office, actual operation, decision-making, employees and core income-generating activities in China. The Hong Kong parent must also demonstrate economic substance to obtain treaty benefits; if the Hong Kong entity is a shell or fails the principal purpose test / limitation on benefits analysis, China will deny the 5% treaty dividend rate and reimpose 10% withholding. Hong Kong’s Economic Substance Regulations similarly require relevant offshore entities to perform their Core Income-Generating Activities in Hong Kong and maintain people, premises and expenditures proportionate to the activity.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion / Fraudulent Filing

Statutory sanction: PRC Tax Collection Administration Law Article 63 requires repayment of the underpaid tax, a 0.05% daily surcharge, and a monetary fine of 50% to 500% of the evaded amount; Criminal Law Article 201 imposes a fine and up to three years’ imprisonment when the evaded amount is large and exceeds 10% of taxable income, or three to seven years when the amount is huge and exceeds 30% of taxable income, with possible confiscation of property.

Director personal liability: Piercing the corporate veil: directors who caused the false filing are jointly and severally liable for civil restitution of unpaid tax and corporate assets; personal bank accounts and assets may be restrained; a travel ban and exit restriction blacklist is enforced by tax authorities; criminal conviction can lead to imprisonment of up to seven years and disqualification from acting as a director.
AML / Unlicensed Money Transmission

Statutory sanction: The PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law imposes administrative fines up to CNY 5 million on institutions and up to CNY 500,000 on responsible directors in serious cases; Criminal Law Article 225 on illegal business operations carries up to ten years’ imprisonment for serious circumvention; Criminal Law Article 191 on money laundering carries fines, imprisonment up to ten years, and confiscation of laundering proceeds.

Director personal liability: Directors approving or directing the unlicensed remittance channel face personal fines, PBOC blacklisting, prohibition from regulated payment industries, freezing of personal assets, travel restrictions, and criminal imprisonment with confiscation of proceeds; civil asset restitution is also available to affected customers and regulators.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse and Transfer Pricing Misstatement

Statutory sanction: Under the General Anti-Avoidance Rule and STA Announcement No. 6 of 2017, the tax authority may recharacterize the shell, deny treaty benefits, impose 10% withholding on the full dividend, assess additional CIT, and levy a penalty up to 50% of the additional tax plus daily late surcharges; fraudulent abuse can be referred for criminal investigation.

Director personal liability: Directors who caused the WFOE to route dividends, royalties or interest into a Hong Kong shell are jointly and severally liable for the resulting tax after veil piercing; civil asset restitution is ordered, personal assets are attached, exit bans and blacklisting are imposed, and criminal tax evasion convictions can result in imprisonment of up to seven years.
