Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

For this services WFOE, PRC law prescribes no minimum registered capital and no paid-up capital floor. The Articles of Association must state the registered capital subscribed by the sole Hong Kong shareholder. Under Article 47 of the 2023 Company Law, all subscribed capital contributions must be paid in full within five years from the date of incorporation, unless laws, administrative regulations or State Council decisions prescribe a shorter period. No blocked escrow account is required for a general services WFOE; however, paid-in capital must be remitted through a licensed PRC bank into the WFOE foreign exchange capital account or basic deposit account and cannot be withdrawn except through lawful dividend distribution or a lawful capital reduction procedure.

Tax Identity & Indirect Tax

Tax authority e-portal, entity tax classification elections, VAT thresholds, and 0% export documentation chain.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration of the People's Republic of China (STA); online portal: the Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax), the national unified electronic tax service platform operated by the STA through its in-charge provincial tax service bureaus.

E-government filing portal

The WFOE is a PRC resident enterprise; its statutory tax identification number (TIN) and VAT registration number is the Uniform Social Credit Code (USCC) printed on the Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) Business License. Procedure: (1) the foreign/HK shareholder incorporates the WFOE with the local SAMR; no minimum paid-in capital is mandatory for a service WFOE; (2) after approval, SAMR issues the Business License with the USCC and, through the multi-certificate integration system, automatically transfers the new entity record to the STA, completing a preliminary tax registration; (3) the legal representative completes real-name authentication and activates the Electronic Tax Bureau account using the USCC and the initial tax authority password; (4) within 30 calendar days of the Business License, the WFOE must complete the taxpayer information profile, including expected taxable activities (financial management consulting and accounting services), accounting standards, bank account filing, finance officer details, and payroll/social-insurance registration; (5) the WFOE applies for VAT taxpayer status, either as a general taxpayer for input-VAT recovery and cross-border zero-rating, or as a small-scale taxpayer if annual taxable turnover is RMB 5,000,000 or below, and applies for special VAT invoice issuance authorization, including fully digitalized electronic invoices; (6) a foreign non-resident entity without a place of establishment in China that receives China-source income obtains a separate PRC non-resident taxpayer identification through withholding declarations filed by the payer or by self-registration on the Electronic Tax Bureau; this non-resident ID is distinct from the WFOE's USCC.

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

There is no turnover threshold for VAT registration: every WFOE must complete tax registration upon incorporation. The classification threshold is RMB 5,000,000 of annual taxable turnover; at or above this amount the WFOE must register as a VAT general taxpayer; below this amount it may remain a small-scale taxpayer (simplified collection method, no input-VAT credit) or voluntarily register as a general taxpayer.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

The WFOE may claim the 0% VAT export rate for its virtual CFO, accounting, bookkeeping, and financial-management consulting services only if: (1) the recipient is an entity or individual located outside mainland China; (2) the service is consumed wholly outside mainland China; (3) the services do not relate to goods or real property located in mainland China and do not create a direct taxable benefit within China; (4) the WFOE is a VAT general taxpayer, because a small-scale taxpayer cannot access input-VAT refunds and may only claim exemption; and (5) the contract, invoice, overseas-consumption evidence, and foreign-exchange repatriation records are all retained to prove the cross-border nature of the supply.

  • Cross-border service agreement (bilingual) executed with the overseas client, specifying deliverables, place of performance, overseas consumption, and payment terms.
  • Zero-rated VAT invoice, issued as a special VAT invoice or fully digitalized electronic invoice through the STA electronic invoice platform with a 0% tax rate line.
  • Bank foreign-exchange receipt credit notice and the Cross-border Receipt and Payment Statement issued by a SAFE-designated bank, the current functional equivalent of the former Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC).
  • Certificate of overseas consumption / overseas service completion statement signed by the overseas client, supported by business emails, project reports, timesheets, or management dashboards demonstrating that the CFO services were delivered from and used wholly outside mainland China.
  • Customs export declaration, bill of lading, or airway bill is not required for a pure service supply; if the WFOE also exports physical deliverables or documentation, a Customs Export Declaration and a Bill of Lading/Airway Bill would be required to substantiate the goods component.

China's Golden Tax System Phase IV requires real-time, machine-readable transmission of all VAT invoice data to the STA. General taxpayers must issue special VAT invoices through the STA-approved electronic invoicing platform; fully digitalized electronic invoices are issued via the STA's digital invoice platform and carry a unique tax authority-assigned invoice number and anti-counterfeiting QR code, constituting the digital fiscal marking. Enterprise systems integrate with the tax platform through STA-authorized APIs or web interfaces, and invoice issuance data is automatically matched against VAT declarations, input invoices, bank settlement records, and cross-border FX receipts. Zero-rated service export invoices must be reported through the export tax refund module of the Electronic Tax Bureau before any VAT refund can be discharged.

The STA and tax inspection bureaus can retroactively disallow a zero-rated export claim and claw back the VAT refund if the documentation bundle is incomplete, the overseas consumption statement is false, or the foreign-currency consideration is not received and repatriated to the WFOE's Chinese bank account within the required 180-day window. Under the PRC Tax Administration Law, underdeclaring output tax or fraudulently over-crediting input VAT through false zero-rating is tax evasion, subject to a penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax underpaid, a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and full recovery of the refund. Serious cases involving forged contracts, fabricated bank receipts, or dummy overseas consumers can be referred for criminal tax fraud prosecution under the PRC Criminal Law and result in blacklisting under the STA's tax integrity and credit discipline system.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The RMB is not fully convertible on capital account, although current-account items are broadly convertible once genuine supporting documents are provided to the bank. For a CNY/HKD-based WFOE owned through Hong Kong, all equity capital introduced from outside the People's Republic of China must be registered with SAFE under the FDI direct-registration procedure before inbound remittances. The registered capital must be released into a dedicated capital account, and each drawdown is subject to the receiving bank's desktop review of the remitter, contract purpose, and compliance filings. Once paid in, the funds cannot be freely moved into intercompany loans without additional SAFE registration and custody rules. Profit remittance to the Hong Kong shareholder is permitted only after the annual corporate income tax filing, an audit by a PRC accountant, and issuance of a shareholders' dividend resolution; the remitting bank verifies the audited financial statements, tax clearance certificate, dividend distribution resolution, and proof that the profit is after-tax under PRC generally accepted accounting principles. A dividend withholding certificate often triggers a 10 percent statutory withholding, which can be reduced to 5 percent where the Hong Kong direct shareholder qualifies as beneficial owner under the Mainland and Hong Kong Double Taxation Arrangement. Total turnaround from board decision to completed wire commonly spans two to four weeks.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export proceeds must be repatriated to a domestic foreign-exchange settlement account within 90 days from the customs declaration date, and unrepatriated amounts require a prior SAFE extension. There is no fixed mandatory conversion ratio under the current managed floating regime: a WFOE may either retain the foreign currency in its settlement account up to the volume justified by trade receipts or convert it to RMB at the prevailing market rate. Receipts, retention, and settlements must be reported in the SAFE goods-trade monitoring system, and outstanding receivables over the statutory window will trigger bank inquiries and possible balance freezing.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) together with State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under PBOC

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Stripe Fit: High

Low as a primary domestic account, and legally prohibited in substance. Wise, Stripe, or other offshore digital EMI platforms are not licensed in China to open RMB accounts, hold deposit-taking licences, or perform SAFE reporting for a WFOE. They can be used only as non-licensed front-end billing/invoicing or payment-collection gateways for non-PRC clients, with the final settlement cleared through a PRC Tier-1 bank. Any attempt to make a domestic operational account with such EMI the main treasury rail would fail compliance, disrupt tax filing, and violate PBOC closure rules.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

The Hong Kong parent must initiate a SWIFT MT103 wire to the WFOE-beneficiary FDI capital account at a PRC Tier-1 bank, using the WFOE registered name and a reference line reading 'FDI paid-in capital / capital contribution' plus the SAFE FDI registration number. The receiving bank captures the transfer in the SAFE direct-recording and confirmation system; the funds are credited as restricted capital and cannot be disbursed at will. After receipt, a PRC licensed CPA issues a Capital Verification Report confirming the paid-in amount and bank confirmation for the registered capital. The WFOE then uploads the report, remittance advice, and board resolution to the bank, and expenditures from the capital account are made only for the declared purposes (rent, payroll, procurement, taxes) under SAFE-approved purpose codes.Outbound payments from the WFOE to overseas trade or service counterparties are cleared through SWIFT MT103 for lower-value service payments and through Commercial Letters of Credit (MT700/MT707) for goods-based transactions requiring presentation of bills of lading, commercial invoice, packing list, and certificate of origin. Documentary Collections (documents against payment and documents against acceptance) are available for recurring open-account relationships under a master agreement and periodic settlement schedule. Every transfer must include the service/business SAFE purpose code, the underlying contract and invoice, and a clean tax-payment statement for relevant invoices; payments to personal accounts are accepted only for statutory payroll and disallowed for trade settlement.Onshore deliverable forward foreign-exchange contracts with domestic authorized banks for genuine current-account or capital-account exposure, evidenced by underlying invoices/contracts and limited to ordinary trade tenors.Onshore FX swaps for short-term liquidity management when the WFOE has matching RMB and foreign-currency cash flows within the same value cycle.Cross-currency interest rate swaps for USD/HKD/RMB denominated loan liabilities, subject to SAFE registration and real-demand backing under non-speculative hedging policy.Plain-vanilla FX options purchased by the WFOE for hedging purposes; offshore non-deliverable forwards (NDFs) are not permitted as speculative onshore instruments and cannot substitute for SAFE-authorized domestic hedging.

Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Under sections 403-405 of the Companies Ordinance (Cap. 622), a private company is exempt from statutory audit only if it qualifies as a small private company or a member of a small private company group. The exemption is available where the company satisfies any two of the following three criteria in both the current and the immediately preceding financial year: (i) total annual revenue does not exceed HK$10 million; (ii) total assets do not exceed HK$10 million; and (iii) average number of employees does not exceed 50. A mandatory independent certified public accountant (CPA) audit is therefore legally required if the company fails any two of these thresholds for two consecutive years. In addition, a company that is incorporated under any other statute or is a public company, a company limited by guarantee, or a subsidiary of a public or overseas company is always subject to statutory audit regardless of size. For the purposes of the Inland Revenue Ordinance, the IRD may require audited financial statements even where the Companies Ordinance exemption applies, particularly where the company's total turnover exceeds HK$5 million, has related-party transactions, or where the IRD issues a notice requiring audited accounts.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: In CNY, contemporaneous Local File documentation is mandatory for the Chinese WFOE when related-party tangible asset transfers exceed RMB 200,000,000 annually; financial asset or intangible asset transfers exceed RMB 100,000,000 annually; related-party loans and other financial transactions exceed RMB 50,000,000 annually; or other related-party transactions exceed RMB 40,000,000 annually. A Master File is required if the controlled group prepares one under the Chinese filing hierarchy or if the group consolidated revenue meets the STA master file threshold, generally RMB 5,500,000,000 or above, with additional condition that the Chinese entity is engaged in covered related-party transactions.

Economic substance scope: Although mainland China does not apply an offshore Economic Substance Regulations regime in the same manner as Hong Kong, Chinese transfer pricing and anti-avoidance rules require that the Chinese WFOE possess functional and economic substance commensurate with its revenue; the Hong Kong shareholder claiming DTA benefits must satisfy the Hong Kong ESR and Core Income-Generating Activities (CIGA) tests, particularly for pure equity holding companies deriving dividend income. If the Hong Kong entity lacks employees, offices, expenditure, and decision-making substance in Hong Kong, the Chinese tax authority may invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rules under CIT Law Article 47 and recharacterize the structure as abusive, deny treaty benefits, attribute income to the Hong Kong shareholder or the mainland economic substance, and impose penalties.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under Article 63 of the PRC Tax Administration Law, the STA shall recover the underpaid tax, impose a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and impose an administrative penalty of 50% to 500% of the tax evaded. If the amount of tax evaded exceeds 10% of tax payable and exceeds RMB 50,000, the case is referred under Article 201 of the Criminal Law as tax evasion, with fines and imprisonment.

Director personal liability: Where a director participates in or approves the fraudulent return, the corporate veil is pierced under PRC Company Law principles; the director is jointly and severally liable for the unpaid tax, penalties, and interest, may be subject to travel bans under the joint dishonesty blacklisting mechanism, asset freezes, prohibitions on being appointed as a director, and criminal conviction carrying up to 3 years' imprisonment for larger evasion amounts and 3 to 7 years where the amount is enormously large.
Shell Company / Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: Under CIT Law Article 47 and the Special Tax Adjustment Measures, the tax authority may disregard the Hong Kong conduit, reattribute the dividend income to the actual beneficial owner, impose the full 10% statutory withholding tax on the grossed-up dividend, charge 0.05% daily interest, and levy penalties of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax. The Hong Kong Inland Revenue Department may also refuse or revoke the TRC and penalize the entity under the Inland Revenue Ordinance for providing false information or assisting in tax evasion, with potential fines and imprisonment upon conviction.

Director personal liability: Directors who sign or approve the false TRC application and treaty benefit declaration are personally liable for the resulting tax shortfall and may be named in the tax administration blacklist with exit-control restrictions; in Hong Kong, a knowingly false declaration can lead to fines and imprisonment of up to 3 years; on the mainland side, treaty abuse constituting tax evasion may trigger criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 for the controlling shareholder and directors, including asset confiscation, personal fines, and imprisonment up to 7 years for especially serious cases.
AML / Foreign Exchange Violation

Statutory sanction: Under Article 39 of the Foreign Exchange Administrative Regulations, illegal foreign exchange receipt or unauthorized cross-border payment exposes the entity to a fine of not less than 30% and up to 500% of the illegal amount; failure to repatriate offshore revenue may also attract the same penalty. Under Article 32 of the PRC Anti-Money Laundering Law, institutions failing to perform KYC, transaction monitoring, and record-keeping obligations may be fined up to RMB 2,000,000 and responsible persons may be fined and banned from relevant positions.

Director personal liability: Directors and actual controllers who orchestrate offshore collection structures to evade SAFE scrutiny may be held civilly liable for the unpaid foreign exchange penalties and may suffer exit bans, arrest and asset freezing, and blacklisting in civil enforcement; knowingly using the financial system to transfer illicit proceeds or to conceal the origin of funds constitutes money laundering under Criminal Law Article 191, punishable by up to 10 years' imprisonment plus fines, and the corporate limited liability shield will not protect directors whose conduct is fraudulent, grossly negligent, or in violation of peremptory financial regulations.
