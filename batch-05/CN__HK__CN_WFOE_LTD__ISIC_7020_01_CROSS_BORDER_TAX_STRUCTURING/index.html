Registered Agent & Physical Address Licensed Agent Required

For a WFOE in the tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory industry, a virtual office or commercial mailbox is virtually always rejected by the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR), the State Taxation Administration, and licensed banks. These regulators require a genuine physical place of business evidenced by a registered lease agreement, utility bills, and the actual presence of staff, because the business involves issuing professional tax opinions and invoice transactions. Banks conducting mandatory KYC and UBO verification under PBOC Order No. 3 and the 2024 Anti-Money Laundering Law will decline to open a corporate account for a company with only a virtual address, and the tax bureau will refuse normal VAT invoice administration. A serviced office with a real lease, office equipment, and staff may be accepted, but a mere smart mailbox or virtual desk address will not pass post-incorporation verification.

Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up capital for a consulting WFOE under PRC law. The registered capital must be subscribed by the shareholder as set forth in the Articles of Association; the term and schedule of capital contribution are fixed at the board’s discretion (subject to the fact that capital contributions must be completed within the registration period stipulated in the Articles of Association, usually no later than 2032-2060 under the transitional arrangements of the new Company Law). No blocked escrow account is required. However, for tax structuring, the registered capital must be proportionate to the business operations and may be scrutinized under transfer pricing rules.

Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; online administrative compliance is conducted through the China Electronic Tax Bureau (e-Tax) portal under the Golden Tax-IV national tax information system.

E-government filing portal

For the China WFOE (Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise): (1) the foreign shareholder(s) incorporate the WFOE with the Administration for Market Regulation (AMR), which issues the Business License containing the Unified Social Credit Code (USCC); (2) the USCC functions automatically as the WFOE's national taxpayer identification number for all PRC tax purposes, and the tax registration is completed via the AMR-STA one-window integration without a standalone tax registration application; (3) within 30 days of the Business License issuance, the WFOE must complete supplementary tax formalities with the competent local STA office, including, where relevant, an application for VAT general taxpayer status, payroll and fund registrations, and the configuration of the e-Tax portal account; (4) a foreign non-resident enterprise without a permanent establishment in China that derives Chinese-source income must separately complete non-resident enterprise tax registration to obtain a Non-Resident Enterprise Taxpayer Identification Number (NRE TIN) used for withholding tax declaration; (5) the WFOE's USCC must appear on all VAT invoices, customs export declarations, income tax returns, and cross-border foreign exchange payment/tax filings. Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation

Available election forms

None under Chinese corporate income tax: China applies a statutory legal-entity classification for limited liability companies and offers no check-the-box or comparable pass-through election. Hybrid mismatch elections are likewise unavailable; the STA instead invokes anti-avoidance rules, including the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR), to recharacterize arrangements creating double non-taxation. The only material statutory election available to the WFOE is the indirect tax status election between small-scale taxpayer status (simplified levy, generally 3% or current preferential rates) and general VAT taxpayer status (full input-output VAT credit), which is elected based on projected annual taxable turnover and accounting capability.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

13%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

CNY 5,000,000 in annual taxable turnover (rolling 12-month basis) triggering mandatory registration as a VAT general taxpayer. Below this threshold, the WFOE is classified as a small-scale taxpayer unless it voluntarily elects general taxpayer status. The applicable VAT rate for tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services is 6% for general taxpayers, while the country-standard headline rate on goods is 13%.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Goods: 0% VAT export zero-rating applies where the WFOE, as a registered VAT general taxpayer, physically exports goods with a China Customs export declaration, the goods leave the customs territory, and the export proceeds are repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank. Services: zero per cent VAT with input-tax refund is limited to the statutory catalogue of cross-border services (including international transport, R&D, design, software, and integrated circuit design). International tax structuring and transfer pricing advisory services are not enumerated in the zero-rating catalogue; accordingly, such services are VAT-exempt (not zero-rated) only if the service is supplied to a non-resident enterprise engaged wholly outside China, the service is consumed entirely outside China, payment is received in foreign exchange, and the WFOE files the cross-border exemption (not zero-rating) procedure with the STA while maintaining the supporting contract, performance evidence, and tax filing records. Because exemption (rather than zero-rating) is applied, input VAT on costs attributable to the exempt service is generally non-creditable and must be apportioned and excluded.

  • Ocean Bill of Lading or Air Waybill evidencing physical export movement
  • Commercial Invoice and Packing List
  • Bank Inward Remittance Certificate / Foreign Exchange Collection Verification Record evidencing export proceeds repatriation
  • Export Tax Rebate (Exemption) Declaration Form filed through the STA e-Tax export rebate module
  • Cross-border Service Trade Tax Payment / Tax Filing Record Form (for cross-border services subject to the VAT exemption or zero-rating procedure)

China mandates real-time fiscal electronic invoicing under the Golden Tax-IV (GT-IV) system and the fully digitalized electronic invoice (FDEI) framework. All VAT invoices (special and ordinary) are issued via the STA's official electronic invoice service platform, with mandatory real-time transmission of invoice data to STA servers at issuance; there is no physical fiscal stamping, but a unique national invoice number and digital anti-counterfeiting code are applied to each invoice. The platform automatically reconciles issued invoices against VAT returns and customs export declarations. Approved ERP and accounting platforms may integrate via STA-certified standard APIs for machine-to-machine (M2M) invoice submission and clearance.

Export zero-rating and cross-border VAT exemption claims are subject to ex-post audit by the STA and China Customs. If a claim is unsubstantiated, missing the mandatory documentation, or fraudulent, the STA will disallow the zero-rating/exemption, claw back the VAT refunded or exempted, charge a daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05% of the overdue tax amount, and impose an administrative penalty ranging from 50% to five times the tax evaded under Article 63 of the Law on the Administration of Tax Collection. China Customs may additionally impose fines of 10% to 50% of the customs value for false export declarations. Where fraudulent export tax refund activity involves a significant amount, criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law (crime of fraudulent export tax refund) attaches to the WFOE and its responsible officers, carrying imprisonment and substantial monetary penalties.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China operates a managed floating exchange rate regime with strict capital account controls. Current account transactions, including trade in services and cross-border consultancy fees, are generally freely convertible once genuine commercial documentation is submitted through designated banks. Capital account transactions, such as foreign direct investment into a Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise, require SAFE registration, an FDI capital account, and post-verification of the injection before the funds can be converted to RMB and deployed. Profit remittances from a WFOE are permissible only after annual audited financial statements, corporate income tax return filings, and tax clearance certificates are presented to the remitting bank; a board resolution and the original foreign exchange registration certificate must accompany the payment. There is no blanket surrender requirement for export proceeds, but every receipt must be reconciled through the SAFE digital monitoring system. A WFOE engaged in tax structuring advisory services must ensure that all inbound share capital is traceable to the declared Hong Kong shareholder and that cross-border payments are mapped to proper SAFE purpose codes; failure to do so triggers enhanced review or rejection.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Under the current SAFE regime, exporters are not subject to mandatory conversion quotas. Export proceeds in foreign currency may be retained in an export foreign exchange settlement account or voluntarily sold to a designated bank. The exporter must complete customs declaration and export collection verification, and all export proceeds must be repatriated and logged in the SAFE monitoring system within 180 days of the customs declaration, unless an extension is granted by the local SAFE branch. Any conversion of retained foreign exchange to RMB must use the onshore market rate and be supported by underlying trade or service contracts.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE) under the People's Bank of China, coordinating with the Hong Kong Monetary Authority (HKMA) for cross-border CNY/HKD clearing

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Cross-border financial technology EMIs and offshore digital banks have low viability for servicing a mainland China WFOE. The PRC legal framework requires any entity providing payment, settlement, or foreign exchange services to be licensed by the PBOC and SAFE or operate through a qualified domestic bank. Offshore EMIs cannot lawfully hold a WFOE's onshore currency accounts, process RMB clearing, or perform capital account verification. They may only serve as ancillary invoicing or communication tools, and no treasury-critical function should rely on them.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the Hong Kong parent to the mainland WFOE must be routed as a direct SWIFT wire into the FDI capital account at a designated onshore bank. The remittance reference must include the SAFE acquisition registration code and the purpose code for foreign direct investment. After the bank receives the funds, the WFOE must complete a capital verification report through a licensed Chinese accounting firm. RMB conversion of the foreign currency capital requires submission of the bank's capital settlement form, the FDI registration certificate, and a written capital use plan. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: No quantitative size-based exemption exists. Under the PRC Company Law (2023 revision), every limited liability WFOE must prepare annual financial reports and have them audited by an independent Chinese CPA firm, regardless of balance-sheet size, turnover, or headcount. The mandatory audit is triggered by the legal form itself: even a WFOE with zero revenue, assets below RMB 50 million, or fewer than 300 employees is subject to the annual statutory audit. The audit must be conducted under the China Standards on Auditing (CSA) by a qualified accounting firm licensed by the PRC Ministry of Finance and the Chinese Institute of Certified Public Accountants.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 40 million for related-party service transactions; RMB 200 million for tangible goods; RMB 100 million for intangible assets and financial assets (per SAT Announcement 2016 No. 42); master file required where the multinational group's consolidated revenue in the previous fiscal year exceeded RMB 5.5 billion.

Economic substance scope: China applies a substance-over-form doctrine through the beneficial ownership tests in SAT Announcement 2018 No. 9 and the GAAR under EIT Law Article 48; the Hong Kong parent must demonstrate real economic substance (employees, premises, decision-making) in Hong Kong for the relevant business; otherwise treaty benefits are denied and the transaction is recharacterized; if the WFOE is deemed a pure shell, the HK intermediary is taxed as a dummy and Chinese-source income is adjusted.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Mispricing / Tax Evasion

Statutory sanction: Under the Enterprise Income Tax Law Article 41 and Tax Administration Law Article 63, the tax authorities may make an upward adjustment to taxable income, impose a 50% up to 300% penalty on unpaid tax, and charge daily late interest of 0.05% from the due date; Article 201 of the Criminal Law imposes criminal liability for intentional evasion exceeding RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax due.

Director personal liability: Civil restitution: directors are jointly and severally liable under Company Law Article 20 if the evasion was orchestrated for personal benefit; travel restrictions are imposed via exit control measures by the tax authority; criminal imprisonment up to 7 years plus fines for individuals; personal property may be attached.
Shell Company / Abuse of Treaty

Statutory sanction: The tax authority will invoke the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 48) to recharacterize the transaction; the DTA benefit is denied, retroactive tax is imposed with 50%-300% penalty under TAL Article 63; under SAT Announcement 2015 No. 7, even an indirect transfer of the WFOE's shares by a third-country parent is recharacterized as a Chinese taxable transfer.

Director personal liability: Directors may face personal fines up to RMB 100,000 under TAL Article 107 for providing false certificates; criminal liability for aiding tax evasion carries up to 3 years imprisonment (Criminal Law Article 202); nominee directors are blacklisted under the AEOI/CRS framework and denied re-entry to the Mainland; withdrawal of signature documents by the HK Companies Registry triggers personal disqualification.
Withholding Tax and Reporting Failure

Statutory sanction: Failure to withhold triggers a penalty ranging from 50% to 200% of the unwithheld amount under TAL Article 69; failure to file a return attracts a fine of up to RMB 10,000 and payment of overdue interest at 0.05% daily under TAL Article 32 and Article 62.

Director personal liability: The legal representative is placed on a 'no-departure' blacklist subject to court order, restricting the use of passports and airplane travel; the tax authority may seize the director's bank accounts and shares; intentional non-remittance of more than RMB 100,000 or omission of tax due triggers criminal imprisonment for 7 years and confiscation of personal assets (Criminal Law Article 203).
