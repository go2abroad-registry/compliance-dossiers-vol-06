Tax ID Application Infrastructure

Competent tax authority

State Taxation Administration (STA) of the People's Republic of China; electronic filing is conducted through the 'Electronic Tax Bureau' enterprise portal of the provincial-level STA, operating on the unified national e-tax platform.

E-government filing portal

Step 1: Foreign parent and/or ultimate shareholder notarises and legalises incorporation/identity documents and appoints a legal representative. Step 2: Registration with the State Administration for Market Regulation (SAMR) through the one-window multi-department platform; the newly created Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise receives an 18-character Unified Social Credit Code (USCC), which is the taxpayer identification number (TIN/VAT ID) for all PRC tax purposes. Step 3: Tax registration is completed automatically when SAMR transmits the incorporation record to the local STA; the WFOE must then log into the electronic tax bureau, complete its taxpayer profile, select VAT taxpayer status (small-scale or general), and activate digital e-invoice issuance. Step 4: The WFOE must file the enterprise income tax registration/filing, appoint finance/accounting staff or an agent, and report foreign investment information. Step 5: If the non-resident Hong Kong shareholder derives China-source dividends, interest, royalties or service fees without a permanent establishment, the WFOE as withholding agent must register the non-resident's tax record with STA and file withholding returns; a reduced withholding rate may be claimed under the Mainland-HK Double Tax Arrangement.

Default Tax Classification & Elections

Default tax classification

Entity_Level_Taxation: The WFOE is a China resident enterprise taxed on worldwide income under the Enterprise Income Tax Law (headline CIT rate 25%, with reduced rates for qualifying small low-profit enterprises); there is no pass-through classification. It is separately a VAT taxpayer and an employer subject to withholding obligations.

Available election forms

None: the PRC does not operate an entity-level check-the-box or hybrid-mismatch election. Available administrative elections: (a) a small-scale VAT taxpayer may elect general taxpayer status if it files clean tax/accounting records and meets STA conditions; (b) a qualifying small low-profit enterprise may elect the reduced enterprise income tax rate through its annual return; (c) non-resident taxpayers may elect treaty relief under the Mainland/Hong Kong double taxation arrangement by filing a withholding-reduction application.

VAT / GST & 0% Export Zero-Rating

Standard VAT / GST rate

6%

Mandatory registration threshold (Nexus)

All WFOEs must obtain VAT registration upon incorporation; compulsory general VAT taxpayer registration is triggered when assessable turnover for the 12-month rolling period exceeds CNY 5,000,000. A taxpayer with annual turnover at or below CNY 5,000,000 is classified as a small-scale VAT taxpayer (3% levy, with period-specific reliefs) and may elect general taxpayer status only when the STA conditions on accounting and control systems are satisfied.

Export 0% zero-rating conditions

China 0% VAT is available to exported goods that physically leave China under customs supervision. For services, the zero rate is reserved for specifically enumerated cross-border services (such as international transport, research and development, design, software, information technology and related export-type services). ISIC 7020 overseas market-entry consultancy is not in the mandatory zero-rate list; however, it can be VAT-exempt (not zero-rated) if the service is supplied to a foreign enterprise/individual, the service and results are consumed wholly outside China, contract and invoice evidence identify the overseas recipient, and the foreign-exchange receipt is bank-verified. To claim zero-rating or exemption the WFOE must file the cross-border-taxable-services declaration, retain the underlying contract, issue a compliant e-invoice, and hold the prescribed service-export documents. For goods exports, the WFOE must be a customs-registered exporter, use an approved export declaration method, receive export proceeds through a SAFE-designated bank, and reconcile the export declaration with bank receipts and VAT return filings.

  • GACC Customs Export Declaration (Customs Goods Declaration) with verified export manifest and the WFOE exporter customs registration number
  • Bill of Lading (sea/land/air waybill or multimodal transport document) showing the WFOE as shipper and the overseas consignee as destination
  • Original commercial invoice, packing list and export/sales contract describing the goods or services and price basis
  • Foreign-exchange receipt proof: bank credit advice/inward remittance receipt and SAFE service-trade or goods-trade foreign-exchange declaration showing receipt of overseas funds from the foreign buyer/service recipient
  • VAT special electronic invoice or ordinary invoice issued with export remarks, plus the VAT declaration file supporting zero-rated or exempt treatment
  • For cross-border consultancy or digital services: signed cross-border service agreement, service-consumption-outside-China statement, offshore recipient business registration/identity proof, and service delivery evidence (deliverable, time log, overseas usage assessment) supporting the zero-rate or exemption claim

China operates mandatory electronic invoicing and real-time invoice clearance through the STA's Fully Digitalized Electronic Invoice (FDEI) platform and the Golden Tax III end-to-end system. VAT invoices must be issued through the STA e-invoice system; each invoice receives a national uniform e-invoice number and anti-fraud QR code/ciphertext, and invoice data is transmitted to the STA in real time. Business platforms integrate via STA-approved application programming interfaces (APIs) or through the electronic tax bureau browser-based issuer; no physical fiscal stamp is used for fully digitalized invoices. Input VAT credits on special VAT invoices must be confirmed/checked in the online special-invoice verification system before claim.

STA and GACC jointly monitor export declarations, VAT refund/exemption claims, logistics events and SAFE foreign-exchange receipts through the Golden Tax system. If a zero-rated or exempt status is not substantiated, the WFOE must reverse the 0% treatment and charge output VAT at the applicable rate (normally 6% or 13%), pay late-payment interest/surcharge, and re-file returns. Mis-declaration or fraudulent export refund claims are clawed back with penalties from 50% to 5x the tax involved under the Law of the People's Republic of China on the Administration of Tax Collection; serious export-refund fraud is subject to criminal liability under the Criminal Law of the People's Republic of China. The normal recovery period for underpayment is 3 years, extended to 5 years for negligent under-declaration, and no time limit applies where the underpayment results from fraud, evasion or unauthorised claims.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

The People's Republic of China maintains a managed floating exchange-rate regime with key features of partial currency convertibility. All current-account transactions are generally convertible, subject to underlying-documentation and authenticity checks by designated banks. Capital-account transactions, however, remain subject to comprehensive regulatory oversight by SAFE, requiring case-by-case filings, approvals, and post-event reporting. A WFOE receiving registered-capital injections from its Hong Kong parent must complete SAFE foreign-investment (FDI) registration and open an FDI capital account before any inbound remittance. Following payment entry, capital verification by a Chinese certified public accountant is mandatory, and the RMB settlement proceeds can only be applied toward operating expenses consistent with the registered business scope. Dividend repatriation to the Hong Kong parent is permitted after annual audited financial statements are prepared and corporate income tax is settled; the remittance must be accompanied by a board resolution, tax clearance certificate, and audit report. Current foreign-exchange surrender requirements no longer impose a forced conversion ratio; exporters may retain proceeds in foreign currency or convert to RMB at their own discretion, subject to repatriation timelines and verification.

Export proceeds surrender rules

Export earnings must be repatriated into a domestic foreign-exchange account within 180 days of the export customs declaration date. There is no statutory minimum conversion ratio; enterprises may choose to keep export receipts as foreign currency or settle into RMB at designated commercial banks. All receipts are monitored through SAFE's trade-in-goods monitoring system, and any exception or discrepancy triggers post-shipment verification.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBoC) / State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Wise Fit: High

Low feasibility. China's FX regulatory framework mandates that all cross-border payment and foreign-exchange settlement services be executed by licensed domestic banks operating under SAFE and PBoC oversight. Offshore digital EMIs (including Wise B2B) do not hold a PRC banking or cross-border payout license and are legally barred from maintaining CNY-denominated accounts or clearing RMB for mainland WFOEs. Such EMIs may only be used by the Hong Kong parent as a treasury overlay for foreign-to-foreign transactions, but they cannot serve as a primary liquidity management or clearing rail for the WFOE's onshore operations.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injection from the HK parent requires a prior SAFE FDI registration. The HK parent initiates a SWIFT MT103 from its own corporate account to the WFOE's dedicated foreign-currency capital account opened with a designated Tier-1 Chinese bank. The payment must carry a purpose code consistent with 'Direct investment capital' and the WFOE must present its FDI registration certificate at the receiving bank. Upon arrival, the funds are converted to RMB at the prevailing CFETS central parity rate, subject to real-use certification; a PRC CPA must issue a capital verification report within six months of incorporation, and the RMB funds are then moved to the operational RMB account for disbursements strictly within the stated business scope.For consulting and licensing services abroad, the WFOE uses SWIFT MT103 remittances for service-trade settlements, after withholding and remitting tax on services; the bank requires evidence of a valid commercial contract and a tax-payment certificate. For physical goods transactions, the preferred clearing rail is a commercial letter of credit (L/C) opened by the WFOE's bank, or a documentary collection (D/P or D/A) for lower-value shipments. Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: RMB 200 million aggregate related-party transactions in a single year requiring the contemporaneous Master File and Local File under Announcement 2016 No. 42; the Country-by-Country Report threshold is RMB 1 billion consolidated revenue in the previous year. Related-party transaction disclosure forms are required when annual related-party transactions exceed RMB 10 million for financing, RMB 10 million for tangible property, RMB 10 million for intangible property, and RMB 10 million for services (thresholds are not aggregated across categories).

Economic substance scope: China does not apply the offshore Economic Substance Regulations (ESR) in Hong Kong; instead the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (GAAR) under CIT Law Articles 47-48 and the OECD BEPS substance-over-form principles apply. The WFOE must demonstrate substance in China as the revenue-generating entity. Under the China-Hong Kong DTA and BEPS MLI principal-purpose test, the Hong Kong parent must maintain real economic substance in Hong Kong, including own office premises, qualified employees making strategic decisions, conducting core income-generating activities (CIGA) relevant to the shareholding, and not performing conduits for treaty shopping. The State Taxation Administration may recharacterize a Hong Kong shell company's dividend or royalty income as Chinese-source if the WFOE's core functions, people, and risks are located in mainland China and the Hong Kong entity lacks the capacity to own and manage the IP or investments.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Tax Evasion and False Filing (Tax Law)

Statutory sanction: Articles 63 and 64 of the CIT Law and Article 35 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law: confiscation of illegal gains, a penalty from 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax, daily late-payment surcharge of 0.05%, and blocking of the WFOE's export-tax rebates or administrative licenses. If tax evasion exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes more than 10% of the tax payable, it is a crime under Article 201 of the Criminal Law of the PRC: offender subject to up to 3 years' imprisonment and a criminal fine; for extraordinarily large amounts, 3-7 years' imprisonment.

Director personal liability: A court may pierce the corporate veil under the Company Law of the PRC Article 20 if the sole shareholder or director abused the WFOE's separate legal personality, commingled funds, or stripped assets, causing severe creditor or tax loss; the director's personal assets may be attached for unpaid taxes and restitution. The tax authority may impose a personal fine up to RMB 100,000 for the legal representative where the entity intentionally evades tax. In egregious cross-border money-laundering-driven evasion, directors face exit bans (出境限制) under the Exit-Entry Administration Law and blacklisting by the Supreme People's Court enforcement list; criminal imprisonment applies to directly responsible supervisors and other responsible persons, not merely the entity.
Transfer Pricing and Thin-Capitalization Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Articles 41 and 46 and Implementation Regulations Article 119: tax authority may make an upward transfer-pricing adjustment, disallow interest exceeding the 2:1 debt-to-equity thin-capitalization ratio, and impose a penalty of up to 5 times the underpaid tax under Article 44 of the Tax Collection and Administration Law for failure to provide contemporaneous documentation. For serious tax evasion, criminal penalties under Article 201 apply with fines and imprisonment up to 7 years.

Director personal liability: Personal liability may attach under the General Anti-Abuse Rule if the director arranged the transaction without business purpose; the director can be named as the responsible person for the tax shortfall under a demand letter and can be subject to civil asset recovery. Cross-border evidence requests and mutual assistance procedures under the OECD Multilateral Mutual Assistance Convention allow Chinese authorities to obtain Hong Kong bank records, then pursue the director in Hong Kong courts for fraudulent misrepresentation; a travel ban and passport revocation can be issued by the Chinese court where the director is the legal representative of the WFOE.
Artificial Shell towards Hong Kong Treaty Abuse

Statutory sanction: CIT Law Article 48 allows the tax authority to make a special tax adjustment and impose a penalty from 50% to 5 times underpaid tax; Announcement 2015 No. 7 may recharacterize an offshore indirect transfer of shares in the WFOE as a Chinese-source taxable capital gain at 10%. The DTA relief is denied, triggering the full statutory 10% WHT on all outbound distributions, plus interest. If the improper purpose was to defraud the tax administration, the General Anti-Avoidance Rule may be coupled with the Criminal Law Article 201 for evasion.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed the shell-company resolutions, structured the offshore chain, and instructed the dividend flow are exposed to a civil claim for unpaid withholding tax plus interest; the court can pierce the veil and attach directly held properties, freeze bank accounts, and impose an exit-restriction order (限高令). Under Articles 201-202 of the Criminal Law, a responsible director can receive 3-7 years' imprisonment for tax evasion of a large amount (more than RMB 50,000 and 10% of the tax payable) and a fine; the same penalty applies to instigators under the doctrine of joint crime. Travel bans and blacklisting are automatic where tax arrears are enforced by the court.
