Paid-up Capital & Blocked Account Regime Mandatory Capital Deposit & Lockup

No statutory minimum paid-up registered capital is imposed for a consulting and carbon advisory WFOE under PRC law, and there is no blocked escrow account rule. The registered capital must, however, be stated in the Articles of Association at a level consistent with the expected operating scale, and every subscribed contribution must be paid in within the time period stated in the Articles. The capital is remitted by the Hong Kong parent as foreign direct investment into a designated SAFE-regulated capital contribution account, supported by bank FDI settlement vouchers, and the funds remain subject to PRC capital controls until the capital contribution registration, verification and lawful expenditure procedures are completed.

Funding & unlock path: Not Applicable for manufacturing or industrial civil defense permits; however, the WFOE office premises must comply with the standard Fire Protection Law of the People's Republic of China, obtain the fire safety acceptance or occupancy inspection certificate for the leased office space, maintain accessible emergency exits and fire extinguishers, and file the mandatory workplace safety and emergency response records with the local emergency management authority before commencing employee occupancy.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China maintains a managed floating exchange rate with strict current and capital account differentiation. Current account transactions, including cross-border trade in services and dividend remittances, are generally convertible and can be settled through designated foreign exchange banks after verification. Capital account transactions, such as FDI equity injections, cross-border shareholder loans, offshore guarantees, and foreign exchange derivatives involving principal, remain heavily regulated and require SAFE registration, filing, or approval. A Wholly Foreign-Owned Enterprise (WFOE) must open a foreign exchange capital account or RMB capital account to receive inbound investment, and the funds cannot be arbitrarily converted into RMB without a declared, bona fide purpose. Profit repatriation requires audited financial statements, a Chinese tax clearance certificate, proof of paid-in capital completion, and an underlying board resolution approving the dividend distribution. The remittance is further screened by the bank against sanctions, negative news, and consistency with prior transaction history. In practice, RMB is fully convertible for current account receipts and payments, but capital account flows remain subject to layered documentation requirements, macro-prudential management, and SAFE monitoring. This bifurcation creates a moderate operational timeline for cross-border treasury planning and requires disciplined regulatory filing before capital injections or loan repayments are executed.

Export proceeds surrender rules

For a mainland WFOE exporting goods or services, export proceeds in foreign currency must be repatriated through a designated foreign exchange bank using the trade in goods verification system. The statutory verification timeline for export receipts is generally 90 days from the date of the export customs declaration, unless the enterprise has obtained a SAFE-approved extension for open account terms or delayed receipts. There is no mandatory surrender ratio and the mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent; the WFOE may retain the foreign currency in a SAFE-approved foreign currency settlement account or voluntarily convert to RMB. Every receipt must be reported through the cross-border receipt and payment direct reporting system and reconciled with the corresponding China Customs declaration, invoice, and transportation documents before settlement is completed.

Central bank supervision: State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE), operating under the People's Bank of China (PBOC)

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Offshore digital EMIs are not viable as a primary banking or clearing solution for a mainland China WFOE. Mainland China does not recognize overseas electronic money institution licenses as substitutes for licensed commercial banking accounts, and no non-bank fintech EMI can provide SAFE-compliant capital account custody, RMB settlement, or foreign exchange verification. A Hong Kong-based EMI may support ancillary cross-border collection or provide a multi-currency wallet in Hong Kong, but all regulated inbound capital and RMB disbursements must clear through a domestically licensed Chinese commercial bank. The recommended architecture is direct onboarding with a tier-1 Chinese bank, with fintech platforms used only as a visible payment interface or reconciliation layer over licensed SWIFT and CNAPS rails.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

Inbound capital injections from the Hong Kong parent must follow the SAFE FDI process. The WFOE must first complete foreign direct investment registration through the local SAFE branch or its designated bank to obtain a unique FDI business registration code. The parent then remits the equity injection or shareholder loan via SWIFT using the correct cross-border transaction purpose code, denominated in RMB or a permitted foreign currency, into the WFOE's foreign exchange capital account. The receiving bank verifies the remittance against the SAFE registration, the equity subscription agreement, the board resolution, and the capital verification schedule. After receipt, the WFOE must engage a Chinese CPA to issue a capital verification report confirming the paid-in capital. Conversion from foreign currency to RMB is executed only against approved business purposes, and all funds are transferred into the RMB capital account for restricted operational use. Any subsequent increase in investment amount, profit distribution, or shareholder loan repayment requires an updated SAFE registration or filing before execution.Outbound trade disbursements for ESG carbon accounting and offset brokerage services should use SWIFT MT103 for direct service fees, consulting payments, and similar current account transfers; commercial letters of credit or documentary collections for any physical or third-party offset procurement that requires shipment-based settlement. Each disbursement must be matched with a written contract, a commercial invoice, a tax payment or withholding certificate where applicable, and, if goods are involved, a China Customs declaration. Corporate Tax & Transfer Pricing

Corporate income tax (CIT), box-by-box return breakdown, dividend WHT, and director personal liability.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25% standard PRC Corporate Income Tax (CIT) rate; 15% for High and New Technology Enterprise; 5% effective for a Small Low-Profit Enterprise with annual taxable income not exceeding RMB 3 million; 15% for eligible encouraged industries in designated western regions and the Hainan Free Trade Port.

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory WHT on dividends paid by a PRC resident enterprise to a non-resident enterprise (CIT Law Art. 3 and 37); reduced to 5% under Article 10 of the Mainland-HK Double Taxation Arrangement if the HK shareholder is the beneficial owner and directly holds at least 25% of the WFOE's paid-in capital for the 12 months immediately before the dividend distribution; otherwise 10% applies.

Annual CIT return must be filed by 31 May following the tax year; quarterly prepayment returns are due by 15 April, 15 July, 15 October and 15 January for the preceding quarter; any due date falling on a non-working day is postponed to the next working day under the official SAT tax calendar.
