Export 0% zero-rating conditions

Under CaiShui [2016] No. 36 Annex 4, a Chinese resident entity providing services to an overseas party (including Hong Kong SAR) may apply the zero VAT rate if the services are completely consumed outside mainland China and fall within prescribed categories, including consulting services rendered as offshore service outsourcing (knowledge process outsourcing). The service recipient must be a non-resident enterprise, the service must have no direct connection with mainland Chinese goods or real estate, and deemed wholly performed and consumed abroad. The supplier must possess a written cross-border service contract, receive payment in foreign currency or RMB from abroad, and retain evidence of the offshore service outsourcing qualification, if any, to substantiate zero-rating.

  • Cross-Border Service Contract / Agreement with the overseas counterparty
  • Commercial Invoice issued for the service fee
  • Inward Remittance Certificate (FIRC) or Bank Credit Advice evidencing repatriation of foreign exchange
  • Offshore Service Outsourcing / Technology-Advanced Service Enterprise certification (if applicable)
  • Statement of Complete Consumption Outside Mainland China signed by the service provider

China mandates the use of the national electronic invoice system ('digital fully-assigned e-invoice' system) for all VAT invoices. All invoices must be issued through the e-Tax Portal or authorized third-party systems integrated via API with the STA. Real-time transmission of invoice data to the tax authority's fiscal database occurs upon issuance; the system generates a unique invoice code and a dynamic anti-counterfeiting QR code. Monthly VAT returns must reconcile all invoiced output and input amounts against the STA electronic records. The legacy physical VAT special invoice with paper tax control stamps is being phased out in favor of fully digitalized e-invoices.

The STA may conduct post-export audits to verify that the foreign-currency receipt occurred within the statutory 180-day period (extendable under specific rules). If zero-rating is improperly claimed, the tax authority claws back the output VAT, imposes a late payment surcharge of 0.05% per day, and levies an administrative fine of 50% to 500% of the underpaid tax under the Tax Collection and Administration Law. Fraudulent claims may trigger criminal liability under Article 204 of the Criminal Law for tax fraud.

Banking & Treasury Architecture

Central-bank FX diagnosis, tier-1 local banks vs offshore digital EMI matrix, and KYC rejection defense guide.

Foreign Exchange Regime & Central Bank Policy

Current-account convertibility

China operates a current-account-convertible but capital-account-managed FX system. The CN WFOE LTD, although owned through Hong Kong, is a mainland China legal entity; therefore its RMB and foreign-exchange flows are governed by PBOC/SAFE. Current account items, including retainer fees, consulting bills, salaries, rent, and ordinary operating expenses, are freely convertible if the bank's authenticity review is satisfied. Capital account items, such as the Hong Kong parent's equity injection, foreign debt, and profit distributions, are not freely convertible; they require SAFE registration, a designated capital account, and documentary proof of real underlying transactions. A capital injection can be subscribed in RMB or foreign currency, but the funds are subject to an account-use negative list: they cannot flow into securities, entrusted loans, property speculation, or wealth-management products. Dividend remittance to Hong Kong is achieved through the current-account profit remittance route only after annual audit, enterprise income tax settlement, and SAFE verification; the bank must see after-tax retained earnings and evidence of no unresolved tax liabilities. Without these checks, the WFOE will be blocked from settlement and outbound remittance.

Export proceeds surrender rules

There is no mandatory percentage surrender for current account proceeds; the mandatory conversion ratio is zero percent. The WFOE may retain 100% of export/service receipts in a domestic foreign-exchange account or convert them into RMB at market rates through a designated bank. However, export proceeds must be repatriated into mainland China within 180 days from the date of export customs declaration or the contractual service completion date, unless SAFE has granted an extension in advance. For the WFOE's government-affairs consulting scope, retainer wires from offshore clients should be reported as service-trade income under the correct SAFE purpose code, and the bank must match each receipt to a signed retainer agreement, an invoice, and evidence that the consulting/lobbying service was actually performed. The WFOE must retain all underlying documents for at least five years.

Central bank supervision: People's Bank of China (PBOC) and State Administration of Foreign Exchange (SAFE); SAFE is the full foreign-exchange administration authority within the PBOC framework for mainland China operations of the CN WFOE LTD.

Offshore Digital Bank / EMI Whitelist

Cross-border EMI / Fintech rails Fit: High

Low to nil as a regulated settlement rail. Offshore digital banking platforms and multi-currency EMIs cannot open the required RMB CNAPS settlement account, cannot accept SAFE foreign-exchange registrations, cannot complete the Chinese tax/VAT invoice chain, and cannot issue capital verification reports. The CN WFOE LTD is legally required to maintain its primary treasury and regulatory clearing books with a PBOC-licensed domestic bank. At absolute best, an offshore EMI can serve as a supplementary non-RMB collection wallet for an offshore affiliate, but all funds must eventually clear into a PBOC-regulated bank for the WFOE's statutory capital, tax, and SAFE compliance. Use of a fintech EMI as the principal account would fail SAFE audits, capital verification, and corporate income tax reporting.

Cross-Border Clearing Corridors & Settlement Fit

All inbound capital from the Hong Kong parent must first be registered with SAFE. For foreign-currency equity injection, the parent sends a SWIFT MT103 with purpose FDI capital contribution to the WFOE's SAFE-designated capital account. For RMB injection, use the CIPS or SWIFT RMB FDI route; the bank must report the payment with the appropriate cross-border RMB transaction code. After receipt, a Chinese CPA issues a capital verification report and the bank matches the funds with the foreign investor's identity. No third-party remitter is allowed; the remitter must be the registered Hong Kong shareholder. The capital account can be settled into RMB only for approved working-capital uses, including rent, salaries, professional fees, and ordinary operating expenses. The WFOE must file annual FDI monitoring reports through SAFE, and any future capital increase must be preceded by an updated SAFE registration before funds arrive.For current-account outflows, the WFOE will use CIPS or SWIFT MT103 for service and consulting fees, retainer payments, taxes, duties, licensing costs, and other operational disbursements; every payment requires a supporting contract, invoice, tax withholding certificate, and SAFE service-trade declaration. If the WFOE imports physical goods in support of its trade-policy practice, an irrevocable commercial letter of credit or documentary collection through the same tier-1 bank is acceptable; because the registered activity is ISIC 7020 advisory, goods-based trade financing should be rare. For profit distributions to the Hong Kong parent, the outbound remittance is classified as a current-account dividend and requires audited financial statements, tax clearance, a profit-distribution resolution, and SAFE-related bank review. Mandatory Audit Threshold Mandatory Audit for All Entities

Turnover: Hong Kong does not impose asset, turnover, or headcount audit exemptions: every private limited company incorporated under Companies Ordinance Cap. 622 must appoint a certified public accountant practising in Hong Kong and have its financial statements audited annually, regardless of whether revenue, total assets, or employee count is zero. The only exemption is a genuinely dormant private company that has delivered a statutory dormancy declaration to the Companies Registry and has had no significant accounting transactions in the period. For the mainland China WFOE, the foreign-invested enterprise status itself is the legal trigger for an annual independent audit by a PRC CPA firm, with no balance-sheet, turnover, or headcount de minimis; the audited financial statements support the Corporate Income Tax annual settlement, the Foreign Investment Annual Information Report, customs and foreign exchange compliance, and bank covenant review. Public-issue companies, financial institutions, state-owned enterprises, and regulated licence-holders are additionally subject to mandatory audit irrespective of size.

Statutory corporate income tax (CIT)

25%

Dividend withholding tax (WHT)

10% statutory under Articles 3(3) and 37 of the EIT Law. Reduced to 5% under the Mainland-Hong Kong Double Tax Arrangement (DTA) when the beneficial owner is a Hong Kong resident enterprise holding directly at least 25% of the WFOE's capital and satisfying the anti-abuse and substance conditions.

Mandatory Return Breakdown

Deadline: · Frequency:

The annual return must be filed and the balance of tax paid within five months after the end of the taxable year. For a calendar-year entity, the deadline is May 31 of the following year.

Deadline: · Frequency:

Withholding returns are generally due within 7 days after the date on which the payment obligation arises, i.e., the earlier of the actual payment date or the date the amount becomes legally due. The tax must be remitted to the State Treasury within the same time frame.

Transfer Pricing & Economic Substance

Master / Local File threshold: Under SAT Announcement No. 42 of 2016, a Local File is required if any of the following annual related-party transaction thresholds are exceeded: (i) tangible asset transfers over RMB 200 million; (ii) financial asset transfers over RMB 100 million; (iii) intangible asset transfers over RMB 100 million; (iv) other related-party transactions (including service fees, interest, and royalties) over RMB 40 million. A Master File must be prepared if the ultimate parent's consolidated turnover exceeds RMB 5.5 billion and the entity's annual related-party transaction volume exceeds RMB 1 billion, or if the entity is designated as a significant enterprise. For a government affairs advisory WFOE, cross-border service fees and membership/licensing arrangements typically fall within the 'other related-party transactions' category; even if below the threshold, contemporaneous documentation is prudent to substantiate the arm's length nature of payments.

Economic substance scope: China does not have an offshore-style Economic Substance Regulation (ESR) label, but the tax authorities apply equivalent substance doctrines: (i) the General Anti-Avoidance Rule (EIT Law Article 47 and Implementing Regulation Article 120) permits recharacterising arrangements lacking business purpose; (ii) the Beneficial Ownership test (SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018) requires the Hong Kong parent to demonstrate real economic activities – employees, office premises, management decisions, and risk assumption – to obtain treaty benefits; (iii) the transfer pricing rules under Articles 41–44 of the EIT Law require the WFOE to prove that its intercompany terms are not determined purely by tax motives. A government-affairs lobbying WFOE must therefore maintain physical substance in China, execute substantive lobbying functions, employ qualified policy analysts, and document each engagement to avoid a finding that the Chinese entity is a shell or the Hong Kong owner is a conduit.

Fatal Compliance Red Lines & Director Liability

Transfer Pricing Manipulation / Thin Substance

Statutory sanction: Under Article 44 of the EIT Law, the STA may make a deemed adjustment based on comparable margins or industry benchmarks. Under Article 121 of the Implementing Regulations, the tax authority can also levy a 50% surcharge on the underpaid tax attributable to the transfer pricing adjustment. TCAL Article 63 imposes a penalty of 50% to 5 times the underpaid tax if the arrangement is deemed fraud. A daily surcharge of 0.05% applies to the unpaid tax from the statutory due date.

Director personal liability: Directors who signed or approved the artificial service contracts may have the corporate veil pierced under PRC Company Law Article 20, leading to joint and several personal liability for the unpaid tax, penalties, and fines. The directors may also be subject to exit bans (travel restriction) under TCAL Article 44. If the understatement of income exceeds RMB 50,000 and constitutes deliberate tax evasion, Criminal Law Article 201 imposes criminal liability with imprisonment up to 7 years, fines, and confiscation of property derived from the crime.
Non-Resident Withholding Tax Failure

Statutory sanction: Under TCAL Article 69, a withholding agent that fails to withhold shall be fined 50% to 3 times the unwithheld tax; the tax itself is collected from the payee. Under TCAL Article 32, the unpaid withholding tax also accrues a daily surcharge of 0.05% from the default date. If the failure is part of a fraudulent scheme (e.g., false contracts), Article 63 applies with penalties up to 5 times the underpaid tax. Criminal liability under Criminal Law Article 201 may attach if the amount evaded is substantial and the directors knowingly participated.

Director personal liability: Directors who are responsible for financial management or who instructed the withholding agent to conceal the payment may be held personally liable for the unwithheld WHT and penalties, based on the principle of piercing the corporate veil (Company Law Article 20). They may be individually blacklisted by the STA, subjected to travel bans, and denied exit from China. In egregious cases, criminal courts have imposed prison sentences of 3 to 7 years for tax evasion involving withholding obligations, together with fines on the individuals and the company.
Tax Treaty Abuse / Conduit Arrangement (Beneficial Ownership Denial)

Statutory sanction: Under SAT Announcement No. 9 of 2018, the beneficial ownership claim is denied and treaty relief is reversed to the 10% statutory rate. Under EIT Law Article 47 (GAAR), the STA may recharacterise the transaction and impose an additional 50% adjustment surcharge. TCAL Article 63 provides for a 50% to 5 times penalty for any resulting underpayment. Additionally, the Anti-Money Laundering Law may be invoked if the conduit arrangement is used to launder the proceeds of lobbying-related bribery or corruption, resulting in fines and seizure of assets.

Director personal liability: Directors who created or approved the conduit arrangement are personally liable for the restitution of the underpaid tax and interest under Company Law Article 20, as they abused the limited liability form for tax evasion. The STA may impose an exit ban on each director and place the company on the 'serious dishonesty' blacklist, affecting their credit and ability to do business. Criminal consequences include tax evasion charges under Criminal Law Article 201 (up to 7 years' imprisonment) and money laundering charges under Article 191 (up to 10 years' imprisonment in serious cases), plus fines and confiscation of the assets routed through the conduit.
